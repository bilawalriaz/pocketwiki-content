# Svalbard

## Overview
Svalbard is a Norwegian archipelago in the Arctic Ocean, midway between mainland Norway and the North Pole. It is a unique territory governed by the 1920 Svalbard Treaty, which grants Norwegian sovereignty while allowing citizens of all signatory nations equal rights to reside and conduct commercial activities. Its strategic location, fragile Arctic environment, and special legal status make it a focal point for international research, tourism, and geopolitical interest.

## Timeline
- **1596** — Dutch explorer Willem Barentsz discovers the archipelago, naming it Spitsbergen.
- **1619** — Dutch establish Smeerenburg, one of the first settlements, for whaling.
- **1899** — First coal mining begins along Isfjorden by Norwegians.
- **9 Feb 1920** — Svalbard Treaty is signed, granting sovereignty to Norway.
- **14 Aug 1925** — Treaty enters into force; the Svalbard Act makes it part of Norway.
- **1941** — Norwegian and Soviet settlements are evacuated and facilities destroyed (Operation Gauntlet).
- **1943** — German battleship *Tirpitz* attacks and destroys settlements at Longyearbyen and Barentsburg (Operation Zitronella).
- **1962** — The Kings Bay mining accident kills 21 workers, causing a political crisis in Norway.
- **1993** — University Centre in Svalbard (UNIS) is established in Longyearbyen.
- **2002** — Longyearbyen is incorporated, adopting a community council.
- **2017** — Svalbard Global Seed Vault and Arctic World Archive open.
- **30 Jun 2025** — Mine 7, the last Norwegian coal mine in Svalbard, closes.

## Body

### Geography and Climate
Svalbard's land area is 61,022 km², dominated by the island of Spitsbergen. Approximately 60% of the archipelago is covered by glaciers, including Austfonna, one of the world's largest. The climate is Arctic, but moderated by the West Spitsbergen Current, making winter temperatures up to 20°C higher than at similar latitudes elsewhere. The region experiences midnight sun and polar night. Global warming has caused significant temperature increases, with a 4°C rise between 1970 and 2020.

### History and Settlement
Whalers from several nations used Svalbard as a base in the 17th and 18th centuries before it was largely abandoned. Permanent settlement began with early 20th-century coal mining. The 1920 Treaty established Norwegian sovereignty and a demilitarized zone. During World War II, settlements were evacuated and later attacked by German forces. Post-war, the Soviet Union maintained a significant presence, but this has diminished. Today, Longyearbyen is the main settlement, alongside the Russian mining community of Barentsburg and research stations like Ny-Ålesund.

### Politics and Economy
The Svalbard Treaty creates a unique legal regime: Norway holds sovereignty, but all treaty nationals have equal rights to live and work. The territory is outside the Schengen Area and EEA, with its own tax system. The economy has shifted from coal mining (now minimal) to tourism and research. The Governor of Svalbard administers the archipelago directly. Norway claims an exclusive economic zone around Svalbard, a claim disputed by other nations like Russia.

### Nature and Environment
Svalbard is home to iconic Arctic wildlife, including polar bears, Svalbard reindeer, and Arctic foxes. The Barents Sea hosts millions of seabirds. The fragile ecosystem is protected by seven national parks and 23 nature reserves covering two-thirds of the archipelago. In 2024, Norway announced new tourism regulations, including ship passenger limits, to protect flora and fauna.

## Terms
- **Svalbard Treaty (1920):** International agreement granting Norway sovereignty over Svalbard while giving all signatory nations equal rights to commercial activity and residence.
- **Svalbard Act (1925):** Norwegian law that formally annexed Svalbard as part of the Kingdom of Norway.
- **Governor of Svalbard (Sysselmester):** The appointed official responsible for administration, law enforcement, and environmental policy on the archipelago.
- **Midnight Sun:** Natural phenomenon where the sun remains visible for 24 hours at high latitudes during summer.
- **Polar Night:** Period of continuous darkness when the sun does not rise above the horizon in winter.
- **Permafrost:** Ground that remains frozen for two or more consecutive years.
- **Exclusive Economic Zone (EEZ):** A maritime zone where a state has special rights regarding resource exploration and use, extending up to 200 nautical miles from its coast.
- **Svalbard Global Seed Vault:** A secure seed bank near Longyearbyen storing duplicates of crop seeds from worldwide gene banks as a safeguard against loss.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Maritime Sovereignty:** Norway claims an exclusive economic zone around Svalbard, but other treaty parties, notably Russia, dispute the extent of these rights, arguing the treaty provides them broader access.
- **Geopolitical Tensions:** Increased Russian investment in Barentsburg and Pyramiden, alongside Norwegian military modernization and concerns over dual-use research facilities (e.g., China's Yellow River Station), highlight ongoing strategic competition in the Arctic.
- **Environmental Protection vs. Activity:** Balancing the growth of tourism and research with the need to protect Svalbard's fragile Arctic ecosystem is a persistent challenge, leading to evolving regulations.

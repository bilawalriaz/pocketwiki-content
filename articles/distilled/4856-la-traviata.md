# La traviata

## Overview
*La traviata* is a three-act opera by Giuseppe Verdi, with a libretto by Francesco Maria Piave, based on Alexandre Dumas fils's play *La Dame aux camélias*. It tells the story of Violetta, a Parisian courtesan who sacrifices her happiness for the sake of her lover's family and dies of tuberculosis. The opera is significant for its contemporary setting (at the composer's wish), its intimate psychological focus, and its enduring popularity as one of the most frequently performed operas worldwide.

## Body

### Composition and Premiere
Verdi conceived *La traviata* after seeing Dumas's play in 1852, wanting a "subject for our own age." He and librettist Piave worked on it for a commission from La Fenice opera house in Venice. However, the theatre's authorities insisted the contemporary story be set in the past, "c. 1700," against the creators' wishes. Verdi was also unhappy with the casting of the lead soprano, Fanny Salvini-Donatelli, whom he felt was too old and overweight for the role of the young, consumptive Violetta. The opera premiered on 6 March 1853 and was a failure, which Verdi attributed to the singers.

### Success and Spread
A revised version, with changes mainly to acts 2 and 3, premiered successfully in Venice in 1854, with Maria Spezia-Aldighieri as Violetta. This success led to rapid international productions in Madrid, Vienna, Barcelona, London, and New York by 1856. Early performances in England and the United States were sometimes deemed morally questionable due to the plot's focus on a courtesan. The opera was first performed in French in Paris in 1864. It quickly became one of the most popular operas of Verdi's lifetime.

### Plot and Music
The opera is set in Paris and its vicinity in the early 19th century. It follows Violetta, a famed courtesan, who falls in love with the sincere Alfredo Germont. They live happily in the country until Alfredo's father, Giorgio, forces Violetta to sacrifice her relationship to protect his daughter's social standing. Heartbroken, Violetta returns to her old life and a gambling party, where Alfredo publicly humiliates her. In the final act, a dying Violetta is reunited with a repentant Alfredo, but dies in his arms. The music is intimate and lyrically focused, with Violetta's character developed through her evolving vocal style—from hectic coloratura in act 1 to spiritual lyricism in act 3.

## Terms
- **Brindisi**: A drinking song; the famous "Libiamo ne' lieti calici" opens the opera.
- **Coloratura**: Elaborate, florid vocal ornamentation, used for Violetta in act 1.
- **Courtesan**: A high-class prostitute or mistress; Violetta's profession.
- **Libretto**: The text or script of an opera.
- **Prelude**: An orchestral introduction to the opera.
- **Tuberculosis**: The fatal lung disease from which Violetta suffers.

## Debates and Open Questions
The source text highlights a central historical debate: the conflict between Verdi's desire for a contemporary setting and the theatre's insistence on a historical one. This delayed the "realistic" productions the composer envisioned until the 1880s. The initial failure of the premiere is also debated, with Verdi himself questioning whether the fault was his or the singers'.

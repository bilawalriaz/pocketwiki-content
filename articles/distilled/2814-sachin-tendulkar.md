# Sachin Tendulkar

## Overview
Sachin Tendulkar is a former Indian international cricketer widely regarded as one of the greatest batsmen in the history of the sport. He holds numerous world records, including being the all-time highest run-scorer in both Test and One Day International (ODI) cricket, and is the only player to have scored 100 international centuries. His career spanned over 24 years, during which he became a symbol of national pride in India and received the country's highest civilian award, the Bharat Ratna.

- **15 November 1989** — Makes Test debut for India against Pakistan at age 16.
- **1992** — Becomes the first overseas-born player to represent Yorkshire in county cricket.
- **1994** — Opens batting in ODIs for the first time; scores first ODI century.
- **1998** — Dominates Australian bowler Shane Warne; scores "Desert Storm" innings in Sharjah.

- **2008** — Breaks Brian Lara's record for most Test runs; scores 100th Test century.
- **10 February 2010** — Becomes the first batsman to score a double century in ODI cricket (200*).
- **2 April 2011** — Part of the Indian team that wins the Cricket World Cup.
- **16 March 2012** — Scores his 100th international century against Bangladesh.
- **23 December 2012** — Announces retirement from ODI cricket.
- **16 November 2013** — Plays his 200th and final Test match, retiring from all forms of cricket.
- **2014** — Awarded the Bharat Ratna, India's highest civilian honour.

## Body

### Early Life and Domestic Career
Born into a Marathi family, Tendulkar was introduced to cricket at age 11 by his brother Ajit to curb his bullying tendencies. He trained under coach Ramakant Achrekar, who advised him to focus on batting after a stint at the MRF Pace Foundation. Tendulkar made his first-class debut for Bombay in 1988, scoring a century. He later became the first overseas-born player to represent Yorkshire in English county cricket in 1992.

### International Career: Rise and Records
Tendulkar debuted for India in 1989 at age 16, the youngest Test player for the country. He quickly established himself, scoring his first Test century in 1990. His career was marked by consistent excellence and record-breaking achievements. He became the first batsman to score 10,000 ODI runs and the first to reach every subsequent 1,000-run milestone in that format. In 2010, he became the first to score a double century in ODIs. He surpassed Brian Lara's record for most Test runs in 2008 and scored his 100th international century in 2012.

### Captaincy and Later Career
Tendulkar captained India in two stints (1996-1997 and 1999-2000), but his tenure was not statistically successful. He later played a key role in recommending Mahendra Singh Dhoni as captain in 2007. His later career included a dip in form around 2005-06 and a return to consistency. He was part of India's 2011 World Cup-winning team and retired from all cricket in 2013 after his 200th Test.

### Playing Style and Legacy
Described as a "wholesome batsman," Tendulkar was known for his technical perfection, balance, and a vast array of shots, particularly his straight drive. He was cross-dominant, batting right-handed but writing left-handed. While not a regular bowler, he took 201 international wickets. He is revered globally, with fans often calling him the "God of Cricket." He was inducted into the ICC Cricket Hall of Fame in 2019.

## Terms
- **Test Cricket:** The longest format of the game, played over five days between two national teams.
- **One Day International (ODI):** A limited-overs format where each team bats for a maximum of 50 overs.
- **Century:** A score of 100 or more runs by a single batsman in one innings.
- **Ranji Trophy:** India's premier domestic first-class cricket competition.
- **Border–Gavaskar Trophy:** A Test cricket series contested between India and Australia.
- **Bharat Ratna:** India's highest civilian award, given for exceptional service in any field.
- **Rajya Sabha:** The upper house of India's Parliament.
- **IPL (Indian Premier League):** A professional Twenty20 cricket league in India.
- **Wisden:** A reference annual for cricket, often called the "bible of cricket."

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Captaincy Record:** Tendulkar's two tenures as Indian captain are widely considered unsuccessful, leading to debate about whether his individual brilliance did not translate to leadership success.
- **The "Nervous Nineties":** Tendulkar was dismissed in the 90s 27 times in his career, leading to occasional discussion about a specific mental hurdle during that phase of an innings.
- **Monkeygate Incident:** During the 2007-08 tour of Australia, Tendulkar's changing testimony in the hearing regarding alleged racist comments by Harbhajan Singh towards Andrew Symonds caused controversy and strained relations with some Australian players.
- **Post-Retirement Political Career:** His tenure as a nominated Member of Parliament (2012-2018) was criticized for extremely low attendance (8% overall), raising questions about the role of celebrity nominations in politics.

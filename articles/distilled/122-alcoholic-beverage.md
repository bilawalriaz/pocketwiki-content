# Alcoholic beverage

An alcoholic beverage is any drink containing alcohol, typically divided into beers, wines, and spirits, with alcohol content usually between 3% and 50%. These drinks are primarily consumed for their psychoactive effects, making alcohol one of the most widely used recreational drugs globally, with about 33% of all humans currently drinking. Alcohol is a depressant that slows the central nervous system; while low doses cause euphoria and sociability, higher doses cause drunkenness or death, and long-term use leads to alcoholism, cancer, and cardiovascular disease. The global alcoholic drink industry exceeded $1.5 trillion in 2017, and most countries regulate production, sale, and consumption through laws, while some ban it entirely.

## Body

### History
Intentionally fermented drinks existed at least as early as the Neolithic period, with late Stone Age jugs providing evidence. The oldest verifiable brewery was found in a prehistoric burial site in a cave near Haifa, Israel, containing 13,000-year-old beer residue in stone mortars, possibly used for ritual feasts to honor the dead.

In the ancient period, beer was likely brewed from barley as early as 13,000 years ago in the Middle East. At Jiahu in China, chemical analysis of pottery jars confirmed a fermented drink of grape and hawthorn fruit wine, honey mead, and rice beer was produced in 7000–5600 BC. The earliest winemaking evidence dates to 6000–5800 BCE in Georgia, where grape pips differed from wild varieties, pointing to deliberate cultivation. The earliest clear chemical evidence of barley beer dates to about 3500–3100 BCE at Godin Tepe in Iran. Celtic people made alcoholic cider as early as 3000 BCE. In Sumerian culture, alcohol was regulated, consumed by the social elite, and used as sacrificial offerings; the Epic of Gilgamesh describes Enkidu drinking seven jugs of beer and becoming elated. In Egypt, a brewery at Hierakonpolis dating to circa 3400 BCE had an estimated yield of up to 1135 litres daily; workers building the Giza pyramids received nearly 4 litres of beer daily, with modern reproductions at 5% ABV. Wine was reserved for royalty and elites, while beer was the common drink.

In the medieval period, Muslim chemists like Jābir ibn Ḥayyān (ninth century) and Abū Bakr al-Rāzī (c. 865–925) experimented with distillation. Distillation of wine is attested in Arabic works attributed to al-Kindī (c. 801–873 CE) and al-Fārābī (c. 872–950). The process spread to Italy in the 12th century, where Taddeo Alderotti (1223–1296) described repeated fractional distillation through a water-cooled still. By the early 14th century, distilled drinks had spread across Europe, reaching Ireland and Scotland by the 15th century, primarily for medicinal purposes.

In the early modern period, alcoholic beverages were safer than water in the Thirteen Colonies, since water sources were often contaminated with sewage. The Whiskey Rebellion (1791–1794) was a violent tax protest against the first federal tax on a domestic product. The Rum Rebellion of 1808 was Australia's first and only military coup, staged by the New South Wales Corps to depose Governor William Bligh, named after the illicit rum trade monopoly. The alcohol monopoly system began in Falun, Sweden in 1850 to prevent overconsumption, spreading nationwide in 1905; Russia established a state vodka monopoly in 1894. The temperance movement grew in the late 19th century, leading to national prohibitions in Canada (1918–1920), Norway (spirits only, 1919–1926), Finland (1919–1932), and the United States (1920–1933).

### Fermented drinks
Beer is fermented from grain mash, typically barley or a blend of grains, flavored with hops, and naturally carbonated; it is the most consumed alcoholic beverage worldwide. Cider is fermented from fruit juice, most commonly apples, with alcohol content from 1.2% to 8.5% ABV or more. Mead, also called hydromel, is made by fermenting honey with water, ranging from 3% to over 20% ABV, with honey as the majority of fermentable sugar. Pulque is a Mesoamerican drink from maguey (Agave americana) that can be distilled into tequila or mezcal. Rice wine is fermented and possibly distilled from rice, consumed in East, Southeast, and South Asia, including sake, huangjiu, mijiu, and cheongju. Wine is most commonly produced from grapes, involving longer fermentation and aging, resulting in 9%–16% ABV; sparkling wines like Champagne undergo secondary fermentation, and fruit wines use other fruits.

### Fermentation and distillation
Wine, beer, and spirits are produced through yeasts, roughly 160 species of single-celled fungi. The most common genus, Saccharomyces ("sugar fungus"), produces alcohol because it can function with minimal oxygen; in the absence of oxygen, sugars are only partially broken down, following the equation: glucose → alcohol + carbon dioxide + energy. Distilled beverages (liquors or spirits) are produced by concentrating ethanol through distillation, with spirits having at least 20% ABV; common drinks like whisky and vodka are around 40%. Fortified wine, such as port or sherry, has a distilled beverage (usually brandy) added, distinguishing it from spirits made by distillation. Rectified spirit (neutral grain spirit) is purified by repeated distillation, containing up to 95% ABV, and lacks flavor. Congeners are substances produced during fermentation, responsible for most taste and aroma, and possibly contributing to hangovers; tannins in wine add bitterness and structure, aiding aging.

### Uses and culture
Alcoholic beverages are integrated into many social activities, including drinking games (evidence dates to antiquity), drinking songs, and various establishments: beer gardens (outdoor areas), beer halls (large pubs), cider houses, ouzeris (Greek taverns serving ouzo), pulquerías (Mexican taverns serving pulque), tiki bars (themed with Polynesian décor), toddy shops (Indian establishments serving palm toddy), and wine bars. Festivals include beer festivals and annual wine festivals, which occur after the grape harvest in late September. Beer and wine tasting involve sensory examination, with various rating scales. Tourism includes craft beer tourism and enotourism (wine tourism). In food, apéritifs are served before meals to stimulate appetite, while digestifs are served after for digestion. Ethanol is a solvent for fatty substances and essential oils, facilitating flavoring; some spirits are aged in oak barrels. Alcohol has been used to preserve food, and vinegar is produced by double fermentation (sugars to ethanol, then ethanol to acetic acid). Wine and food matching pairs dishes with wine to enhance dining, though taste is subjective. Alcoholic drinks are also used as offerings: libations (ritual pouring) to deities or ancestors, common in ancient Egypt and the Andes (challa to Pachamama), and sacramental wine in Christianity, which must contain alcohol per Catholic Canon 924. Tinctures are extracts dissolved in ethanol, with at least 20% alcohol for preservation.

### Laws and health
Alcohol laws regulate manufacture, packaging, labelling, distribution, sale, consumption, and drunk driving, seeking to reduce adverse health and social impacts. Legal drinking ages usually vary between 15 and 21 years, with most countries setting 18; the U.S. sets 21 in all 50 states. Some jurisdictions prohibit alcohol entirely for religious reasons (e.g., Islamic countries with sharia law) or public health (e.g., U.S. Prohibition 1920–1933). Alcohol-related crimes include rum-running (smuggling), wine fraud (adulteration with cheaper products), moonshine (illegal distillation), and drunk driving (DUI/DWI). Short-term health effects range from decreased anxiety and euphoria to stupor, unconsciousness, and memory blackouts at higher doses. Long-term use leads to alcohol use disorder, physical dependence, cardiovascular disease, and several cancers. A 2023 meta-analysis concluded low daily intake provides no health benefits, while increased consumption raises mortality risks. The International Agency for Research on Cancer lists ethanol as a carcinogen, and the World Health Organization considers alcohol a Group 1 carcinogen with no risk-free quantity; in 2025, U.S. Surgeon General Vivek Murthy supported cancer warning labels on alcohol products.

## Terms
- **ABV (alcohol by volume)**: Standard measure of alcohol concentration in beverages, ranging from less than 0.1% in fruit juices to up to 98% in rare spirits.
- **Congeners**: Substances produced during fermentation, including alcohols like propanol and compounds like acetone, responsible for taste and aroma and possibly hangovers.
- **Depressant**: A psychoactive drug class that slows central nervous system activity; alcohol is one.
- **Distillation**: Process of concentrating ethanol by heating and condensing fermented liquid, producing spirits with at least 20% ABV.
- **Fermentation**: Process where yeasts break down sugars into alcohol and carbon dioxide in the absence of oxygen.
- **Fortified wine**: Wine with a distilled beverage (usually brandy) added, such as port or sherry.
- **Libation**: Ritual pouring of liquid as an offering to a deity, spirit, or in memory of the dead.
- **Rectified spirit**: Alcohol purified by repeated distillation, containing up to 95% ABV, also called neutral grain spirit.
- **Temperance movement**: Social movement advocating against consumption of alcoholic beverages, leading to national prohibitions in several countries.
- **Tincture**: Extract of plant or animal material dissolved in ethanol, typically 25–60% concentration.

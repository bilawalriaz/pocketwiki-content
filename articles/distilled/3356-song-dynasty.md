# Song dynasty

## Overview
The Song dynasty (960–1279) was an imperial Chinese dynasty that reunified much of China after the Five Dynasties and Ten Kingdoms period. It is renowned for its unprecedented economic growth, technological innovation, and cultural flourishing, but its history is marked by constant military pressure from northern rivals, ultimately leading to its conquest by the Mongol-led Yuan dynasty. The dynasty is divided into the Northern Song (960–1127), based in Kaifeng, and the Southern Song (1127–1279), which retreated south after losing the north to the Jurchen Jin dynasty.

## Timeline
- **960** — Emperor Taizu of Song founds the dynasty, usurping the Later Zhou.
- **1005** — Chanyuan Treaty ends major warfare with the Liao dynasty; Song pays tribute.
- **1044** — *Wujing Zongyao* military manual compiled, first to list gunpowder formulas.
- **1069–1085** — Wang Anshi's New Policies reforms create political factions at court.
- **1127** — Jingkang Incident: Jurchen Jin capture Kaifeng and the Song court.
- **1132** — Song establishes its first permanent navy headquarters at Dinghai.
- **1234** — Mongols conquer the Jin dynasty, becoming Song's new northern neighbor.
- **1271** — Kublai Khan founds the Yuan dynasty.
- **1279** — Battle of Yamen: Yuan forces conquer the last Southern Song resistance.

## Body

### Political History and Military Conflicts
The Northern Song was founded by Emperor Taizu, who reunified China and established a strong central government in Kaifeng, promoting a civil service based on merit. The dynasty's primary foreign policy challenge was managing relations with northern powers: the Khitan Liao dynasty and the Tangut Western Xia. After initial conflicts, the Song-Liao relationship was stabilized by the Chanyuan Treaty (1005), which established peace in exchange for Song tribute. Internal political stability was undermined by factional disputes over reforms, most notably those of Chancellor Wang Anshi.

The alliance with the Jurchen Jin to defeat the Liao backfired when the Jin turned on the Song. The Jingkang Incident (1127) saw the Jin capture the capital, forcing the Song court to flee south and establish the Southern Song at Lin'an (modern Hangzhou). The Southern Song maintained a robust economy and developed a powerful navy to defend the Yangtze River. The Mongols, after conquering the Jin in 1234, became the new threat. After decades of warfare, including the death of Möngke Khan during a siege in 1259, Kublai Khan's forces completed the conquest in 1279.

### Economy and Society
The Song economy was among the most advanced in the medieval world, with a GDP estimated to be three times that of Europe in the 12th century. Population doubled between the 10th and 11th centuries, fueled by expanded rice cultivation using early-ripening Champa rice from Vietnam. This growth spurred urbanization, with cities like Kaifeng and Hangzhou exceeding a million residents. The government withdrew from direct economic intervention, allowing a vibrant private sector, guilds, and long-distance maritime trade.

Society was characterized by a growing scholar-official class selected through competitive civil service examinations, which promoted social mobility. The gentry class took on greater local administrative roles. Cities had lively entertainment quarters, and social clubs for poetry, tea, and art were popular. Women had certain legal rights, including property inheritance under specific conditions, and notable women like the poet Li Qingzhao emerged.

### Culture, Philosophy, and Technology
The Song era saw a renaissance in arts and philosophy. Landscape painting and the *ci* poetry form reached new heights. Neo-Confucianism, synthesized by thinkers like Zhu Xi, became the dominant philosophy, integrating Buddhist and Daoist ideas. The civil service examination curriculum was standardized around Zhu Xi's commentaries on the Four Books.

Technological innovation was prolific. The Song was the first government to issue paper money (*jiaozi*) and to establish a permanent standing navy. Key inventions included gunpowder weapons (fire arrows, bombs, the fire lance), the magnetic compass for navigation, movable type printing (invented by Bi Sheng), and advanced mechanical devices like Su Song's astronomical clock tower. Polymaths like Shen Kuo made groundbreaking contributions to science, mathematics, and engineering.

## Terms
*   **Northern Song (960–1127):** The first period of the Song dynasty, with its capital at Bianjing (Kaifeng), controlling most of East China.
*   **Southern Song (1127–1279):** The second period after the court retreated south of the Yangtze, establishing a new capital at Lin'an (Hangzhou).
*   **Neo-Confucianism:** A philosophical movement that reinvigorated Confucianism with ideas from Buddhism and Daoism, emphasizing ethical universalism and the study of the Four Books.
*   **Civil Service Examination:** A system of recruiting government officials through competitive exams based on knowledge of Confucian classics, promoting meritocracy.
*   **Gentry:** The educated elite class, including scholar-officials, degree-holders, and local leaders, who managed local affairs and cultural life.
*   **Jiaozi:** The world's first government-issued paper money, established during the Song dynasty.
*   **Wujing Zongyao:** A 1044 military compendium that is the first known book to list formulas for gunpowder and describe gunpowder weapons.
*   **Pound Lock:** A canal lock invention that allowed boats to be raised or lowered between different water levels, improving inland navigation.
*   **Movable Type Printing:** An innovation attributed to Bi Sheng (c. 1040) using individual clay characters, which enhanced the spread of literature and knowledge.

## Debates and Open Questions
Scholars debate the extent of social mobility enabled by the civil service examination system. While some historians argue it created a meritocratic society, others contend that wealthy, land-owning families maintained a disproportionate advantage in affording the education needed to pass the exams, limiting true equality of opportunity. The role and influence of the gentry class in local governance versus the central state's authority is also a subject of ongoing analysis.

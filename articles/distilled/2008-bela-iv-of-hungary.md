# Béla IV of Hungary

## Overview
Béla IV (1206–1270) was King of Hungary and Croatia whose reign was defined by the catastrophic Mongol invasion of 1241–42 and his subsequent, radical reforms to rebuild and fortify the kingdom. His efforts to repopulate the land, promote urbanization, and decentralize military power earned him the posthumous epithet "second founder of the state," though his reign also saw civil war with his son and costly foreign conflicts.

## Timeline
- **1206** — Béla born, oldest son of King Andrew II.
- **1214** — Crowned king in his father's lifetime, against Andrew's wishes.
- **1220** — Appointed Duke of Slavonia, Croatia, and Dalmatia; marries Maria Laskarina.
- **1226** — Becomes Duke of Transylvania; begins reclaiming royal land grants.
- **1235** — Succeeds his father as King of Hungary.
- **1239** — Adopts title "King of Cumania"; settles fleeing Cumans in Hungary.
- **1241** — Mongols invade; Hungarians annihilated at Battle of Mohi (11 April).
- **1242** — Mongols withdraw; Béla returns to a devastated kingdom.
- **1246** — Battle of the Leitha River; Duke Frederick II of Austria killed.
- **1254** — Gains Duchy of Styria from Ottokar II of Bohemia.
- **1260** — Loses Styria to Ottokar II after Battle of Kressenbrunn.
- **1266** — Ends civil war with son Stephen; divides kingdom along the Danube.
- **1270** — Dies; succeeded by his son Stephen V.

## Body

### Early Life and Reign as Duke
Béla was crowned as a child in 1214, but his father, Andrew II, opposed this and did not grant him a province until 1220. As Duke of Slavonia and later Transylvania, Béla began asserting royal authority by reclaiming the extensive land grants his father had made to nobles and the Church. He also pursued an expansionist policy east of the Carpathians, supporting Christian missions among the pagan Cumans and adopting the title "King of Cumania" in 1233.

### The Mongol Invasion and Aftermath
Upon becoming king in 1235, Béla intensified efforts to restore royal rights, alienating the nobility. In 1239, he settled a large Cuman population fleeing the Mongols, which caused internal strife. The Mongol invasion began in 1241. After the Hungarian army was destroyed at Mohi, Béla fled, pursued by Mongols as far as Trogir in Dalmatia. The Mongols withdrew in 1242 following the death of their Great Khan, leaving Hungary devastated.

### "Second Founder of the State"
Béla's post-invasion reforms were transformative. He abandoned the royal monopoly on castle-building, allowing nobles and the Church to erect stone fortresses. He promoted the development of fortified towns, moving the population of Pest to found Buda in 1248. To repopulate the kingdom, he encouraged colonization by Germans, Poles, and others, granting them favorable privileges. He also rebuilt the army and established a defensive alliance with neighboring princes like Daniil Romanovich of Halych.

### Foreign Policy and Civil War
Béla's foreign policy involved complex alliances and conflicts. He gained and then lost the Duchy of Styria to King Ottokar II of Bohemia. He established a southern buffer zone in Bosnia and Serbia. In his later years, a bitter civil war erupted with his eldest son and heir, Stephen, over favoritism toward younger children. The conflict was resolved in 1266 by dividing the kingdom along the Danube, with Béla ruling the west and Stephen the east.

## Terms
*   **Rex iunior (Junior King):** A title for the heir apparent, often ruling a designated portion of the kingdom during the senior king's lifetime.
*   **Golden Bull of 1222:** A charter of rights for the Hungarian nobility, issued by Béla's father, which Béla was later forced to confirm.
*   **Banderium:** The private armed retinue of a baron or prelate, which Béla allowed them to maintain.
*   **Banate:** A province ruled by a Ban, a high-ranking royal official, often on a frontier region.
*   **Franciscan tertiary:** A layperson affiliated with the Franciscan order, living by its principles without taking monastic vows.
*   **Suzerainty:** The right of a superior lord to whom a vassal or state is subordinate.
*   **Magna Hungaria:** The legendary ancestral homeland of the Hungarians, east of the Ural Mountains.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   The exact number of casualties and the scale of depopulation caused by the Mongol invasion and subsequent famine are debated, though sources agree it was catastrophic.
*   The degree to which Béla's pre-invasion policies, particularly the settlement of the Cumans, directly contributed to the social unrest and military failure at Mohi is a point of historical analysis.
*   The long-term effectiveness of his decentralizing reforms (allowing private fortresses and armies) is debated, as they strengthened the nobility at the expense of future royal authority.

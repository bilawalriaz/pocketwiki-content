# Kandahar

## Overview
Kandahar is a major city in southern Afghanistan and the capital of Kandahar Province. It is one of the oldest continuously inhabited sites in the region, serving as a strategic crossroads for empires and trade for millennia. While Kabul is the official capital, Kandahar is the spiritual and de facto seat of power for the Taliban and a traditional center of Pashtun culture and power.

## Body

### Name and Geography
The name "Kandahar" is most likely derived from the ancient region of Gandhara, as early Arab writers used "Kandahar" for Gandhara due to the lack of a 'G' in Arabic. A less plausible theory links it to Alexander the Great's city, Alexandria Arachosia ("Iskandar"). The city is located between the Arghandab and Tarnak rivers at an elevation of about 1,010 m (3,310 ft). It is the center of the cultural region of Loy Kandahar and is divided into 15 municipal districts.

### Ancient and Medieval History
The region shows evidence of human settlement from ca. 5000 BC. A fortified city at the site was an important Achaemenid outpost. After Alexander's conquest, the territory passed through the Seleucid, Mauryan (which introduced Buddhism), Greco-Bactrian, Indo-Greek, Kushan, and other empires. Arab conquests began in the 7th century, with Islam becoming the official religion under the Saffarid dynasty in 870. The city was later ruled by the Ghaznavids, Mongols, Timurids, and was a contested prize between the Mughal and Safavid empires.

### Formation of the Modern City
In 1709, Mirwais Hotak revolted against Safavid rule, establishing an independent Hotak dynasty with Kandahar as its capital. After the Hotaks were overthrown by Nader Shah, who destroyed Old Kandahar in 1738, Ahmad Shah Durrani founded a new city nearby in 1761. He made Kandahar the capital of his new Afghan Empire in 1747. The capital was later moved to Kabul in 1776 by his son Timur Shah.

### Modern Conflicts and Taliban Rule
Kandahar was occupied by British forces in 1839 and 1879. It remained relatively peaceful until the 1980s Soviet–Afghan War, when it was heavily besieged and its population plummeted from 200,000 to 25,000. After the Soviet withdrawal, the city descended into lawlessness, which fueled the rise of the Taliban. They captured Kandahar in 1994, making it their headquarters and enforcing a strict interpretation of Islamic law. Following the 2001 US invasion, the city was taken by anti-Taliban forces. It saw intense fighting during the NATO era, notably Operation Medusa in 2006. The Taliban recaptured Kandahar in August 2021.

## Terms
- **Loy Kandahar**: The larger cultural region centered on the city of Kandahar.
- **Nahia**: An administrative city district; Kandahar has 15.
- **Hotak dynasty**: An Afghan dynasty founded by Mirwais Hotak in 1709 with Kandahar as its capital.
- **Durrani Empire**: The Afghan empire founded by Ahmad Shah Durrani in 1747, with Kandahar as its initial capital.
- **Pashtunwali**: The traditional code of conduct of the Pashtun people.
- **Mujahideen**: Islamic guerrilla fighters, notably against the Soviet invasion in the 1980s.
- **Taliban**: A Pashtun Islamist movement that captured Kandahar in 1994 and recaptured it in 2021.
- **ISAF**: The NATO-led International Security Assistance Force in Afghanistan (2001–2014).
- **Operation Medusa**: A major 2006 battle in Kandahar Province between ISAF/Afghan forces and the Taliban.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The exact etymology of "Kandahar" is debated, with derivation from Gandhara being the most accepted scholarly view, while links to Alexander the Great or the Indo-Parthian king Gondophares are considered less plausible.
- The city's role as the "de facto capital" of Afghanistan under the Taliban is noted, as the supreme leader and spiritual advisers are based there, even though Kabul remains the official capital.

# History of games

## Overview
Games are formalized expressions of play, integral to all human cultures and among the oldest forms of social interaction. They capture and transmit cultural worldviews, serving as tools for teaching, social bonding, and markers of status. The history of games spans from prehistoric bone implements to modern electronic games, reflecting evolving technology, social structures, and beliefs.

## Timeline
- **c. 7000–9000 BC** — Stones possibly used for a mancala-like game in the Middle East.
- **c. 3500 BC** — Senet appears in Predynastic Egyptian burial sites.
- **c. 2600–2400 BC** — Royal Game of Ur played in Mesopotamia; a complete set found at Shahr-i Sokhta, Iran.
- **c. 2300 BC** — Cubical and oblong dice used in the Indus Valley Harappan civilization.
- **c. 1400 BC** — Mesoamerican ballgame (Ōllamaliztli) begins.
- **c. 4th century BC** — Go (Weiqi) first mentioned in Chinese historical annals.
- **822 AD** — Chess introduced to the Iberian emirate of Cordoba.
- **1283** — Alfonso X of Castile commissions the *Libro de los juegos*.
- **1497** — First chess theory text published in Spain.
- **1851** — First international chess tournament held in London.
- **1972** — First home video game console, Magnavox Odyssey, released.

## Body

### Pre-modern Games
Early gaming tools were often made from bone, shells, or stones. Board games likely originated in the ancient Near East, with finds like the 5,000-year-old pieces at Başur Höyük. Games like Senet (c. 3500 BC) and the Royal Game of Ur (c. 2600 BC) were elite pastimes that evolved to incorporate religious and astronomical significance. In India, dice games were common, and Chaturanga, the predecessor of chess, possibly developed during the Kushan or Gupta periods. East Asia saw the invention of Go (c. 4th century BC) and later Chinese Chess (Xiangqi). In the Americas, the Mesoamerican ballgame held major religious and ritual importance. African Mancala games are among the oldest, with evidence dating to the 6th–7th century CE, though some theories suggest earlier origins.

### The Spread and Evolution of Chess
Chess evolved from the Indian game Chaturanga. After the Muslim conquest of Persia, it became Shatranj, with Islamic prohibition on images leading to abstract piece designs. It spread from the Arab world to Europe by the 10th century. Modern chess rules, including the powerful Queen and Bishop, emerged in 15th-century Spain and Italy. The first world championship was held in 1886, and the World Chess Federation (FIDE) was founded in 1924.

### Commercial and Modern Board Games
The 19th century saw the commercialization of board games. Adaptations of ancient games like Pachisi became Parcheesi (1869) and Ludo (1896). Moralistic games like *The Mansion of Happiness* (1800) were followed by secular ones like *The Checkered Game of Life* (1860). *The Landlord's Game* (1904), designed to illustrate economic theory, evolved into Monopoly by 1933. The 20th century produced classics like Scrabble (1938), Diplomacy (1954), and Risk (1957). A German-style board game (Eurogame) movement began in the late 1970s, leading to titles like *Carcassonne* and *The Settlers of Catan*.

### Card, Role-Playing, and Electronic Games
Playing cards arrived in Europe from Mamluk Egypt in the 14th century. Poker was documented in 1833, and Contract bridge rules were published in 1925. Collectible card games like *Magic: The Gathering* gained popularity in the 1990s. Role-playing games (RPGs) evolved from miniature wargames, with *Dungeons & Dragons* released in 1974. Electronic games began with a 1947 patent, leading to arcade games in 1971, home consoles in 1972, and the golden age of arcades in the late 1970s.

## Terms
- **Senet**: An ancient Egyptian board game (c. 3500 BC) where players moved pieces on a 30-square board, later imbued with religious meaning about the soul's journey.
- **Chaturanga**: The ancient Indian predecessor to chess, possibly developed between the 4th and 6th centuries AD, featuring pieces representing army divisions.
- **Shatranj**: The Persian version of chess after the Muslim conquest, characterized by abstract piece designs due to Islamic prohibitions on figurative art.
- **Mancala**: A family of "sowing" or "count-and-capture" board games with ancient origins, widespread across Africa and beyond.
- **Eurogame**: A style of modern board game originating in Germany, emphasizing strategy, indirect player interaction, and high-quality components.
- **Role-Playing Game (RPG)**: A game where players assume the roles of characters in a fictional setting, often involving narrative development and rule-based mechanics.
- **Collectible Card Game (CCG)**: A game where players build custom decks from a pool of unique cards, often acquired through randomized packs.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The precise origins and dating of some games are debated, such as whether mancala dates to 6000 BC in Jordan or if the holes at Tlacuachero, Mexico (c. 3000 BC) are dice-game scoreboards.
- The development of chess is complex; while Chaturanga is the likely precursor, some historians suggest the Greek game *petteia* may have influenced its evolution.
- The original rules of many ancient games, like the Chinese game Liubo, are lost and subject to scholarly reconstruction.

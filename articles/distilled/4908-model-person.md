# Model (person)

## Overview
A model is a person who displays commercial products or serves as an artist's model. Modelling involves using one's body to represent another's body or artistic imagination, distinct from posing for portraits or other performances. The profession encompasses various types, including fashion, glamour, fitness, and commercial modelling, and has evolved from a part-time activity into a global industry with significant cultural and economic impact.

## Timeline
- **1853** — Charles Frederick Worth establishes the first "house model" profession.
- **1946** — Ford Models is founded in New York.
- **1960s** — Modelling agencies are established globally; models begin to become household names.
- **1967** — Association of London Model Agents is formed, legitimising the industry.
- **1974** — Beverly Johnson becomes the first Black woman on the cover of U.S. Vogue.
- **1980** — Ford Models Supermodel of the World competition is established.
- **1990s** — The "supermodel" era peaks; the "heroin chic" trend emerges.
- **2000s** — Health controversies lead to age and BMI restrictions for models.
- **2012** — Vogue bans models under 16 and those appearing to have eating disorders.
- **2013** — New York passes enhanced child labour protections for underage models.

## Body

### History and Professionalization
Modelling as a profession began in 1853 with Charles Frederick Worth, who used his wife as a "house model." The industry expanded with fashion photography in the early 20th century. Models remained anonymous until the late 1940s, when supermodels like Lisa Fonssagrives gained fame. The 1960s saw the rise of agencies and the first household-name models like Twiggy. The 1970s and 1980s brought globalisation, scouting, and lucrative cosmetic contracts, making modelling a full-time career. The 1990s cemented the "supermodel" phenomenon, while the 2000s introduced health regulations and digital-age changes.

### Types of Modelling
**Fashion Modelling** includes runway, magazine, and editorial work. Runway models must meet strict height and weight criteria (e.g., 5'8"–5'11" for women). The industry has faced criticism for promoting unhealthy body images, leading to bans on underage and underweight models in several countries. **Glamour Modelling** focuses on sexuality and varies by region; it became prominent in the UK with "Page 3" in 1969. **Fitness Modelling** displays a toned physique. **Commercial Modelling** includes promotional, spokesmodel, and trade show roles, where models interact directly with consumers. **Plus-size** and **petite models** cater to non-standard fashion sizes. **Parts models** specialise in specific body parts like hands or feet.

### Diversity and Representation
Black models faced significant barriers until the postwar era. Pioneers like Donyale Luna (first Black model on British Vogue, 1966) and Naomi Sims gained prominence in the 1960s–70s. The 1974 "Battle of Versailles" showcased Black models and advanced their visibility. Supermodels like Naomi Campbell and Tyra Banks achieved global fame in the 1990s. However, representation declined in the mid-1990s due to a preference for a uniform aesthetic, prompting activism from figures like Campbell and Iman to address industry racism.

### Digital Age and Instagram Models
The rise of social media created "Instagram models," who gain followings through lifestyle photos and are hired for influencer marketing. This platform has allowed unsigned models to be discovered and has blurred lines between traditional modelling and celebrity. Some Instagram models have become celebrities, while others use the platform to parody or critique the industry.

## Terms
- **House Model**: The first professional modelling role, coined in 1853, where a model exclusively represents a fashion house.
- **Supermodel**: A highly paid, globally recognised model, a term popularised in the 1990s.
- **Heroin Chic**: A 1990s fashion trend favouring pale, thin, and androgynous models.
- **Plus-size Model**: A model with larger measurements than standard editorial models, used in advertising for larger clothing sizes.
- **Fit Model**: A model used by designers to check garment fit and drape.
- **Promotional Model**: A model hired to interact with consumers and drive demand for a product at events.
- **Spokesmodel**: A model employed to be the face of a brand in advertisements.
- **Gravure Idol**: A Japanese female model who appears provocatively in magazines and photobooks.
- **Instagram Model**: A person who gains a large social media following and is hired for influencer marketing.

## Debates and Open Questions
The industry has ongoing debates about **health standards**, prompted by model deaths from eating disorders, leading to bans on underage and underweight models. **Racial diversity** remains a contentious issue, with periods of "whitewashing" on runways and activism demanding inclusion. The **impact of social media** is debated, as Instagram models challenge traditional agency structures and raise questions about authenticity and exploitation. The **ethics of child modelling** are addressed by new labour laws, but concerns about pressure and exploitation persist.

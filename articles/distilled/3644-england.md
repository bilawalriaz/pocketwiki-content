# England

## Overview
England is a country that is part of the United Kingdom, located on the island of Great Britain. It is the origin of the English language, the common law system, association football, and the parliamentary system of government, which have been adopted worldwide. As the most populous country in the UK, it has a profound historical and cultural impact, from the Industrial Revolution to its global legal and linguistic legacy.

## Timeline
- **c. 780,000 years ago** — Earliest known human presence (Homo antecessor).
- **43 AD** — Roman invasion and conquest, creating the province of Britannia.
- **5th–6th centuries** — Settlement by Germanic tribes (Angles, Saxons, Jutes).
- **597** — Reintroduction of Christianity by missionaries from Rome.
- **927** — Political unification of England first accomplished under Æthelstan.
- **1066** — Norman Conquest; William the Conqueror becomes king.
- **1215** — Signing of Magna Carta, limiting sovereign power.
- **1534** — Henry VIII breaks from the Catholic Church, establishing the Church of England.
- **1603** — Personal union of the English and Scottish crowns under James VI/I.
- **1707** — Acts of Union create the Kingdom of Great Britain, ending England as a sovereign state.
- **18th century** — The Industrial Revolution begins, transforming society.
- **1948** — The National Health Service (NHS) is established.

## Body

### History
The area was first inhabited by modern humans during the Upper Paleolithic. Its name derives from the Angles, a Germanic tribe who settled in the 5th and 6th centuries. After Roman rule ended in 410, Anglo-Saxon kingdoms formed, which were later unified under Wessex. The Norman Conquest of 1066 replaced the English elite with a French-speaking aristocracy, profoundly influencing the English language. The Plantagenet dynasty followed, a period marked by the signing of Magna Carta and the founding of Oxford and Cambridge universities. The Tudor period saw the break with Rome and the start of the Age of Discovery. The Kingdom of England ceased to be a separate sovereign state in 1707 when it joined with Scotland to form Great Britain.

### Governance
England is part of the United Kingdom, a constitutional monarchy. It has not had its own government since 1707 and is governed directly by the UK Parliament. There is no devolved English parliament, though a three-tier system of governance was established in 2026. The legal system of England and Wales is based on common law, where judges apply legal precedent (*stare decisis*). The court system is headed by the Senior Courts of England and Wales, with the UK Supreme Court as the highest court.

### Geography and Climate
England covers about 62% of the island of Great Britain. Its terrain consists chiefly of low hills and plains in the centre and south, with upland and mountainous terrain in the north and west, including the Pennines and the Lake District. The highest point is Scafell Pike (978 m). The longest river entirely in England is the Thames (215 miles). It has a temperate maritime climate, influenced by the Atlantic Ocean and the Gulf Stream, with mild winters and cool summers.

### Economy
England has one of the world's largest economies, with a GDP per capita of £37,852 in 2022. It is a leader in sectors like pharmaceuticals, aerospace, and software. London is a global financial centre. Since the 1970s, the economy has shifted from manufacturing to services. Agriculture is highly mechanised, producing 60% of the nation's food needs with only 2% of the labour force.

### Demographics and Culture
With over 56 million inhabitants, England is 84% of the UK's population. English is the principal language. Christianity is the most widely practised religion (46.3% in 2021), with the Church of England as the established church. English culture has global influence, particularly in literature (Shakespeare), music (The Beatles), and sport. England codified many modern sports, including association football, cricket, and rugby.

## Terms
- **Common Law**: A legal system where law is developed by judges through decisions of courts and similar tribunals, rather than through legislative statutes alone.
- **Acts of Union 1707**: The acts of the Parliament of England and the Parliament of Scotland that united the two kingdoms to form the Kingdom of Great Britain.
- **Magna Carta**: A 1215 charter of rights agreed to by King John, establishing the principle that everyone, including the king, was subject to the law.
- **Industrial Revolution**: The transition to new manufacturing processes in Great Britain, continental Europe, and the United States, in the period from about 1760 to sometime between 1820 and 1840.
- **Devolution**: The statutory delegation of powers from the central government of a sovereign state to govern at a subnational level, such as a regional or local level.
- **Stare Decisis**: The legal principle of determining points in litigation according to precedent.
- **Temperate Maritime Climate**: A climate with mild temperatures and relatively even rainfall throughout the year, moderated by the ocean.
- **Anglican Communion**: The worldwide association of churches that are in communion with the Archbishop of Canterbury.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The scale and nature of the Anglo-Saxon settlement of Britain are subject to considerable disagreement, with debate over whether it was a large-scale migration or a more limited elite takeover.
- There is ongoing debate about how to counterbalance the lack of a devolved government for England, given that Scotland, Wales, and Northern Ireland have their own parliaments or assemblies.
- The etymology of the tribal name "Angles" is disputed by scholars.

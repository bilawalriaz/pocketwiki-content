# Taylor Swift

## Overview
Taylor Alison Swift (born December 13, 1989) is an American singer-songwriter known for autobiographical songwriting and artistic reinventions. She is the highest-grossing live music artist, the wealthiest female musician, and one of the best-selling music artists of all time. Swift began as a country artist, transitioned to pop, and later explored indie folk, becoming a defining cultural figure of the 21st century.

## Body

### Early life and career beginnings
Swift was born in West Reading, Pennsylvania, named after singer-songwriter James Taylor. Her father was a Merrill Lynch stockbroker; her mother a mutual fund marketing executive. Her maternal grandmother, Marjorie Finlay, was an opera singer. At age nine, Swift aspired to musical theater, but after watching a documentary about Faith Hill, she pursued country music. At 11, she traveled to Nashville with demo tapes but was rejected by all labels, leading her to focus on songwriting. At 14, her family relocated to Hendersonville, Tennessee, to help her break into country music.

### 2004–2014: Country career
Swift signed with Sony/ATV in 2004, becoming its youngest signee. She left RCA Records after a year because they planned to keep her in development until 18; she wanted to release songs immediately. After a showcase at the Bluebird Cafe, she signed with Scott Borchetta's new label Big Machine Records. Her debut album *Taylor Swift* (2006) made her the first female country artist to write or co-write every track on a platinum-certified debut. *Fearless* (2008) won Album of the Year at the Grammys and became the most-awarded country album of all time. *Speak Now* (2010), written entirely by herself, debuted with over one million first-week sales. *Red* (2012) featured new producers including Max Martin and Shellback, and became the fastest-selling country album in US history.

### 2014–2018: Pop transition and controversy
Swift relocated to New York City in March 2014 and transformed her image from country to pop with *1989* (2014), inspired by 1980s synth-pop. It became her best-selling album with 14 million copies worldwide. During this period, she publicly opposed free streaming services, removing her catalog from Spotify in November 2014 and criticizing Apple Music's free trial in 2015. A 2016 dispute with Kanye West over the song "Famous" made Swift a subject of an online "cancel" movement. She also countersued and won a case against a former radio jockey she had accused of sexual assault. These controversies influenced *Reputation* (2017), which explored themes of fame and finding love amidst turmoil.

### 2018–2023: Republic Records, folk era, and re-recordings
Swift signed with Republic Records in 2018 with a provision allowing her to maintain ownership of her masters. *Lover* (2019) was the global best-selling album by a solo artist that year. After Scooter Braun purchased Big Machine Records, including her masters, Swift began re-recording her back catalog in November 2020 to control licensing. Amidst the COVID-19 pandemic, she surprise-released the indie folk albums *Folklore* and *Evermore* (2020), making her the first woman to win Album of the Year three times. She released four re-recorded albums (2021–2023) and *Midnights* (2022), which made her the first artist to occupy the entire top 10 of the Billboard Hot 100.

### 2023–present: Eras Tour and later albums
The Eras Tour (2023–2024) became the highest-grossing tour in history with $2 billion in revenue. Its events inspired *The Tortured Poets Department* (2024), which debuted with 2.6 million first-week units, and *The Life of a Showgirl* (2025), which broke the record for the biggest debut week in US history. In May 2025, Swift finalized the purchase of the masters to her first six albums. She married Travis Kelce on July 3, 2026.

### Artistry
Swift's discography spans country, pop, and folk, incorporating subgenres like country pop, synth-pop, electropop, and indie rock. She possesses a mezzo-soprano voice but mostly sings in her alto range. Her early vocals were criticized as weak, but have received more positive assessments since *Folklore*. Swift is credited as lead songwriter on every song she has released and sole songwriter on over 60 tracks. She divides her lyrics into three types: "quill lyrics" (antiquated poeticism), "fountain pen lyrics" (modern storylines), and "glitter gel pen lyrics" (lively and frivolous). Her concerts feature Broadway-influenced elements, and she plays four instruments live. She has directed 15 of her music videos.

### Achievements and public image
Swift has won 14 Grammy Awards, including a record four Album of the Year wins. She is the most-awarded artist of the American Music Awards (40 wins), Billboard Music Awards (49), and MTV Video Music Awards (30). The IFPI has named her Global Recording Artist of the Year a record six times. She is the first individual from the arts named *Time* Person of the Year (2023). Swift's career trajectory from country to pop was extensively commented on; she was deemed "America's Sweetheart" early on, and her 2020s reinventions turned her into an acclaimed singer-songwriter. She is a known triskaidekaphile, using the number 13 in her works.

### Cultural impact, wealth, and activism
Academics have described Swift as "the last pop superstar." Her success popularized country music beyond the US and her pop transition is credited as a catalyst for poptimism. She has filed over 300 trademark applications in the US. As of July 2026, Forbes estimated her net worth at $2.1 billion. Her private jet use has drawn scrutiny for carbon emissions. Since 2018, Swift has been public about her political views, endorsing Democratic candidates and criticizing President Donald Trump. She is a pro-choice feminist, supports LGBTQ rights, and has donated extensively to disaster relief, cancer research, and the arts.

## Terms
- **Poptimism**: Critical perspective that takes pop music seriously as art
- **Master recordings**: Original recordings from which all copies are made; owning them controls licensing
- **Re-recording**: Recording new versions of old songs to gain control over licensing
- **Synth-pop**: Pop music genre characterized by synthesizers and drum machines
- **Indie folk**: Folk music style with independent, stripped-back production
- **Trap-pop**: Pop music incorporating trap beats and hip-hop elements
- **Mezzo-soprano**: Female voice range between soprano and contralto
- **Triskaidekaphile**: Person with a fondness for the number 13
- **Swifties**: Swift's global fanbase

## Debates and open questions
- Critics debated whether Swift's country identity signified her songwriting more than her musical style, with some accusing her of causing mainstream country to stray from its roots.
- Her transition to pop led rockist critics to view it as an erosion of authenticity, while others defended it as necessary for artistic evolution.
- Some critics have dismissed her "confessional" songwriting as tabloid material; scholars attribute this criticism to sexism, noting male artists with similar themes receive more serious merit.
- Her political engagements have been questioned as potentially strategic career moves, with some viewing her influence as sizable but overstated.

# Karakoram

## Overview
The Karakoram is a major mountain range in Asia, spanning Pakistan, China, and India. It is the second-highest mountain range on Earth and the most glaciated place outside the polar regions, containing four of the world's fourteen 8,000-meter peaks, including K2.

## Timeline
- **c. 3000 BC** — Name "Karakoram" (Turkic for "black gravel") originally applied to a pass by Central Asian traders.
- **1850s** — Thomas Montgomerie of the Survey of India labels high peaks K1 to K6 from Mount Haramukh.
- **1887** — Colonel Francis Younghusband's expedition crosses the Muztagh Pass.
- **1892** — General Sir George K. Cockerill explores the valleys above the Hunza River.
- **1910s-1920s** — Explorations establish most of the region's geography.
- **1936** — French expedition to the range is chronicled in the film *Karakoram*.
- **1999-2000** — Floral surveys conducted in the Shyok River catchment area.

## Body

### Geography and Glaciation
The Karakoram is approximately 500 km (311 mi) long. It is bounded by the Aksai Chin plateau to the east, the Tibetan Plateau to the northeast, the Kunlun Mountains to the north, the Pamir Mountains to the northwest, and the Hindu Kush to the west. Its southern boundary is formed by the Gilgit, Indus, and Shyok rivers, separating it from the Himalayas. The range is the most glaciated place outside the polar regions, with 28-50% of its area covered by glaciers, including the Siachen and Biafo, the second- and third-longest glaciers outside the poles.

### Geology and Climate
The Karakoram lies at the active plate boundary between the Indo-Australian and Eurasian plates. Its glaciers are a key indicator of climate change. Unlike the Himalayas, where glaciers are losing mass rapidly, many Karakoram glaciers are stable or slightly retreating, a phenomenon termed the "Karakoram Anomaly." This is partly because many are insulated by a layer of rubble. During the last Ice Age, connected glaciers stretched across the region, with snowlines about 1,300 m (4,300 ft) lower than today.

### Exploration and Naming
The name "Karakoram" was applied to the entire range by early 20th-century explorers like Kenneth Mason. Before this, the term "Muztagh" (Ice Mountain) was also used. The range is less inhabited than the Himalayas due to its altitude and ruggedness. Major explorations in the late 19th and early 20th centuries mapped its geography.

### Passes and Subranges
The range is divided into numerous subranges, including the Baltoro Muztagh and Siachen Muztagh, though their exact division is debated. Key passes include the Khunjerab Pass (4,693 m), the highest paved international border crossing, and the historic Karakoram Pass. The Khunjerab and Shimshal passes are the only ones still in regular use.

## Terms
- **Eight-thousander**: A mountain peak with an elevation of 8,000 meters (26,247 ft) or more.
- **Glacier**: A persistent body of dense ice that is constantly moving under its own weight.
- **Karakoram Anomaly**: The observed stability or slight mass gain of some glaciers in the central and western Karakoram, contrasting with global glacial retreat.
- **Muztagh**: A term meaning "Ice Mountain," historically used for parts of the range.
- **Pass**: A navigable route through a mountain range.
- **Plate boundary**: The edge of two of the Earth's tectonic plates, often a zone of high geological activity.
- **Snowline**: The edge of the persistent snow cover, below which snow does not remain year-round.

## Debates and Open questions
The naming and division of the Karakoram's various subranges are not universally agreed upon among geographers and scholars.

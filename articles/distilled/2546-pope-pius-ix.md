# Pope Pius IX

## Overview
Pope Pius IX (1792–1878) was the head of the Catholic Church from 1846 to 1878, with the longest verified papal reign in history. His pontificate was transformative, marked by a dramatic shift from liberal reformer to staunch conservative, the loss of the Papal States' temporal power, and the centralization of church authority through the definition of papal infallibility.

## Timeline
- **13 May 1792** — Born Giovanni Maria Mastai-Ferretti in Senigallia.
- **16 June 1846** — Elected Pope, taking the name Pius IX.
- **1848** — Flees Rome after revolution; declares himself a "prisoner in the Vatican."
- **8 Dec 1854** — Promulgates the dogma of the Immaculate Conception.
- **1858** — The Mortara case: a Jewish boy is seized from his family by papal police.
- **1864** — Issues the *Syllabus of Errors*, condemning liberalism and modernism.
- **1869-1870** — Convenes the First Vatican Council.
- **1870** — Council defines papal infallibility; Rome is captured by Italy.
- **7 Feb 1878** — Dies in Vatican City, having refused to leave after Rome's capture.
- **3 Sept 2000** — Beatified by Pope John Paul II.

## Body

### Early Life and Election
Giovanni Mastai-Ferretti was born into nobility and ordained a priest in 1819 after overcoming epilepsy. He gained a reputation as a liberal reformer during his time as Archbishop of Spoleto and Imola. Elected pope in 1846 amid high hopes for reform, he initially enacted liberal measures, including freeing political prisoners and easing restrictions on Jews.

### The Turn to Conservatism
The Revolutions of 1848 were a turning point. After his prime minister was assassinated and he was made a prisoner, Pius IX fled Rome. Following his return in 1850, his policies became increasingly conservative and anti-liberal. He reversed religious freedom laws, re-instituted the Roman ghetto, and in 1858 oversaw the controversial seizure of Edgardo Mortara, a Jewish boy secretly baptized by a servant, from his family.

### Doctrinal Centralization
Pius IX strengthened papal authority. He defined the dogma of the Immaculate Conception in 1854. His 1864 *Syllabus of Errors* condemned liberalism, modernism, and the separation of church and state. He convened the First Vatican Council (1869–1870), which defined the dogma of papal infallibility, asserting the pope's supreme doctrinal authority.

### Loss of Temporal Power
As the last sovereign ruler of the Papal States, Pius IX faced the unification of Italy. After French troops withdrew in 1870, Italian forces captured Rome. Pius IX refused to recognize the new Italian kingdom, excommunicated its leaders, and declared himself a "prisoner in the Vatican," a status that persisted until the 1929 Lateran Treaty.

### Global Church and Relations
His pontificate saw the centralization of church governance in Rome. He re-established Catholic hierarchies in England, Wales, and the Netherlands, provoking anti-Catholic sentiment. He maintained complex relations with nations like Mexico (opposing Emperor Maximilian's religious freedom), Russia (protesting the persecution of Poles), and Germany (facing the *Kulturkampf*). He also elevated the first American cardinal, John McCloskey.

## Terms
*   **Papal Infallibility:** Dogma defined in 1870 stating that the pope is preserved from error when he speaks *ex cathedra* (from the chair of Peter) on matters of faith and morals.
*   **Immaculate Conception:** Dogma defined in 1854 stating that the Virgin Mary, from the first moment of her conception, was preserved free from all stain of original sin.
*   **Syllabus of Errors:** An 1864 document listing 80 propositions condemned as errors, including liberalism, rationalism, and the separation of church and state.
*   **Papal States:** The sovereign territories in central Italy ruled directly by the Pope, which were annexed by the Kingdom of Italy in 1870.
*   **Prisoner in the Vatican:** The title Pius IX adopted after 1870, refusing to leave the Vatican or recognize the Italian state that had seized Rome.
*   **First Vatican Council:** Ecumenical council (1869–1870) convened by Pius IX that defined papal infallibility.
*   **Kulturkampf:** The struggle in the 1870s between the German Empire (under Bismarck) and the Catholic Church, aimed at reducing the Church's political and social influence.
*   **Mortara Case:** The 1858 incident in which papal police seized a Jewish boy, Edgardo Mortara, from his family because he had been secretly baptized, sparking international outrage.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Political Legacy:** Scholars debate whether his early liberalism was genuine or a tactical stance, and how his later conservatism shaped the modern papacy's spiritual, rather than temporal, authority.
*   **Impact of Infallibility:** The definition of papal infallibility remains a point of theological and historical discussion regarding its effect on episcopal collegiality and the Church's relationship with the modern world.
*   **Beatification Controversy:** His 2000 beatification was criticized by some Jewish and Christian groups due to his authoritarian politics, the Mortara case, and the reinstitution of the ghetto.

# Kosovo

## Overview
Kosovo is a landlocked country in Southeast Europe with a population of nearly 1.6 million, predominantly ethnic Albanians. It declared independence from Serbia in 2008, a move recognized by over 110 UN member states but not by Serbia or the UN Security Council. Its modern history is defined by a struggle for self-determination, culminating in the 1998-99 Kosovo War and subsequent international administration.

## Timeline
- **1389** — Battle of Kosovo between a Serbian-led coalition and the Ottoman Empire.
- **1455** — Ottoman Empire fully conquers Kosovo after the Second Battle of Kosovo.
- **1878** — League of Prizren forms to advocate for Albanian autonomy within the Ottoman Empire.
- **1912** — Kosovo ceded to the Kingdom of Serbia after the Balkan Wars.
- **1945** — Becomes the Autonomous Region of Kosovo and Metohija within Yugoslavia.
- **1989** — Serbian President Milošević revokes Kosovo's autonomy.
- **1998-99** — Kosovo War between Yugoslav/Serb forces and the Kosovo Liberation Army (KLA).
- **1999** — UN Security Council Resolution 1244 places Kosovo under UN administration (UNMIK).
- **2008** — Kosovo unilaterally declares independence from Serbia.
- **2010** — International Court of Justice rules Kosovo's declaration of independence did not violate international law.
- **2013** — Brussels Agreement reached between Serbia and Kosovo to normalize relations.

## Body

### History and Conflict
The territory of modern Kosovo was the core of the ancient Dardani kingdom and later a Roman province. It became a political and cultural center of the medieval Serbian state in the 13th and 14th centuries. Ottoman rule lasted nearly five centuries after the 1389 Battle of Kosovo. In the 19th century, Kosovo became the center of Albanian nationalism, leading to tensions with Serbian ambitions. After the Balkan Wars (1912–13), it was incorporated into Serbia. Within Yugoslavia, Kosovo was an autonomous province, but ethnic tensions persisted, leading to the revocation of its autonomy by Serbia in 1989. This sparked a non-violent resistance movement, which was later overtaken by the armed Kosovo Liberation Army (KLA), leading to the 1998-99 Kosovo War. The war ended with a NATO intervention and the withdrawal of Yugoslav forces, placing Kosovo under UN administration.

### Independence and Status
Kosovo declared independence from Serbia on 17 February 2008. Its independence is recognized by 110 UN member states, including the US and most EU members, but not by Serbia, Russia, or China. The International Court of Justice's 2010 advisory opinion held the declaration did not violate international law. The 2013 Brussels Agreement, brokered by the EU, established a framework for normalizing relations, including the creation of a Community of Serb-majority municipalities. Serbia does not recognize Kosovo's sovereignty but accepts the authority of its institutions under this agreement.

### Geography and Climate
Kosovo is landlocked, covering 10,887 km². Its terrain is varied, featuring the Accursed Mountains in the west and the Šar Mountains in the southeast, with two major plains: the Kosovo Plain and the Metohija Plain. The climate is mainly continental with Mediterranean and Alpine influences. The average annual temperature is 9.5°C, with precipitation ranging from 600 to 1,300 mm annually.

### Demographics and Society
The 2024 census recorded a population of 1,585,566. Ethnic Albanians constitute approximately 92% of the population. Ethnic Serbs (2.3%) are concentrated in the north and some eastern municipalities. Other minorities include Bosniaks, Ashkali, Egyptians, Turks, Romani, and Gorani. Albanian and Serbian are official languages at the national level. The society is secular, with 93.5% of the population identifying as Sunni Muslim in the 2024 census.

### Government and Economy
Kosovo is a parliamentary republic. Its economy is a developing, upper-middle-income one, heavily reliant on remittances, foreign aid, and the services sector. The euro is the official currency. Unemployment and poverty remain significant challenges. Kosovo is a member of the IMF and World Bank and has applied for EU membership.

## Terms
*   **UNMIK:** The United Nations Interim Administration Mission in Kosovo, established by UN Security Council Resolution 1244 in 1999 to administer the territory.
*   **KFOR:** The NATO-led international peacekeeping force in Kosovo, deployed in 1999 to maintain security after the war.
*   **KLA:** The Kosovo Liberation Army, an ethnic Albanian paramilitary group that fought Yugoslav/Serb forces in the late 1990s.
*   **Brussels Agreement:** A 2013 EU-brokered deal between Serbia and Kosovo aimed at normalizing relations, including provisions for Serb-majority municipalities.
*   **Metohija:** The western part of Kosovo, historically associated with Serbian Orthodox monasteries; the name is used in Serbian to distinguish it from the eastern Kosovo region.
*   **Dardania:** The name of an ancient kingdom and Roman province covering the territory of modern Kosovo, sometimes used as a national identity by some Albanians.
*   **EULEX:** The European Union Rule of Law Mission to Kosovo, a civilian mission assisting Kosovo's rule-of-law institutions.

## Debates and open questions
*   **Status:** Kosovo's final political status remains contested. Serbia continues to claim it as an autonomous province, while Kosovo functions as an independent state.
*   **International Recognition:** The lack of recognition from UN Security Council permanent members Russia and China prevents Kosovo from joining the UN.
*   **Community of Serb-Majority Municipalities:** The implementation of the 2013 Brussels Agreement, particularly the creation of this association, is a major point of contention and delay in normalization.
*   **Historical Narratives:** The significance of the 1389 Battle of Kosovo and the medieval period are interpreted differently by Serbian and Albanian historiographies.

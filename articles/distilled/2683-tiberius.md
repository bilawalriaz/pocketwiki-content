# Tiberius

## Overview
Tiberius was the second Roman emperor, ruling from AD 14 to 37 as the successor to his stepfather, Augustus. He was a capable administrator and general who consolidated the empire's frontiers but became a reclusive and suspicious ruler, leaving power to his prefect Sejanus. His reign is marked by a transition from the stability of Augustus to the later excesses of the Julio-Claudian dynasty.

## Timeline
- **16 Nov 42 BC** — Born in Rome to Tiberius Claudius Nero and Livia Drusilla.
- **38 BC** — Mother Livia divorces Nero and marries Octavian (later Augustus).
- **20 BC** — Leads forces in the East; helps recover Roman legionary standards from Parthia.
- **11 BC** — Divorces his beloved wife Vipsania and marries Augustus's daughter Julia.
- **6 BC** — Retires from public life to Rhodes, creating a succession crisis.
- **AD 4** — Recalled; adopted as Augustus's heir after the deaths of Augustus's grandsons.
- **AD 14** — Becomes emperor upon Augustus's death.
- **AD 19** — His nephew and adopted son, Germanicus, dies under suspicious circumstances.
- **AD 23** — His son Drusus Julius Caesar dies.
- **AD 26** — Withdraws permanently to the island of Capri.
- **AD 31** — Executes his powerful prefect Sejanus for treason.
- **16 Mar AD 37** — Dies at Misenum; succeeded by his grand-nephew Caligula.

## Body

**Early Life and Path to Succession**
Tiberius was born into the ancient patrician gens Claudia. His mother, Livia, divorced his father and married Octavian (Augustus) in 38 BC. Tiberius began his political career in 24 BC and proved himself a skilled general, conquering Raetia and campaigning in Pannonia and Germania. His personal life was marked by a happy first marriage to Vipsania, which was ended at Augustus's command so he could marry Augustus's scandalous daughter, Julia. After a self-imposed retirement to Rhodes (6 BC–AD 2), he was forced back into public life and adopted as Augustus's heir in AD 4 following the deaths of Augustus's grandsons, Gaius and Lucius Caesar.

**Early Reign and the Rise of Germanicus**
Tiberius became emperor reluctantly in AD 14 at age 55. He initially sought to share power with the Senate but was perceived as arrogant and hypocritical. His early reign was dominated by the brilliant campaigns of his adopted son, Germanicus, who recovered Roman standards lost in the Teutoburg Forest and celebrated a triumph in AD 17. Germanicus's death in AD 19, which he blamed on the governor Piso, created a rift between Tiberius and the Senate. After the death of his own son, Drusus, in AD 23, Tiberius grew increasingly reclusive.

**Sejanus and the Capri Years**
In AD 26, Tiberius withdrew to Capri, leaving administration in Rome to his ambitious Praetorian Prefect, Lucius Aelius Sejanus. Sejanus consolidated power, purged potential rivals (including the family of Germanicus), and controlled information to and from the emperor. In AD 31, Tiberius uncovered a plot by Sejanus to seize power and had him executed. This was followed by a wave of treason trials. Tiberius spent his final years in isolation, leaving the succession unresolved.

**Legacy and Historiography**
Tiberius left the imperial treasury with nearly 3 billion sesterces, having strengthened the empire through consolidation rather than costly expansion. However, his reputation was destroyed by ancient historians like Tacitus and Suetonius, who portrayed him as a paranoid, cruel, and debauched tyrant. Modern scholarship notes that the number of treason trials during his reign was relatively limited. His death in AD 37 was surrounded by rumors of murder by his successor, Caligula.

## Terms
*   **Princeps:** The official title for the Roman emperor, meaning "first citizen," maintaining a facade of republican government.
*   **Praetorian Prefect:** Commander of the Praetorian Guard, the emperor's elite bodyguard, who became a powerful political figure under Tiberius.
*   **Tribunicia Potestas (Tribunician Power):** A key power held by the emperor, granting veto authority and sacrosanctity, essential for ruling without the traditional republican offices.
*   **Maius Imperium (Supreme Command):** The supreme military command over the provinces, which Tiberius held jointly with Augustus before becoming sole emperor.
*   **Maiestas (Treason):** A legal charge used to prosecute perceived threats to the emperor's dignity and the state, revived and frequently used during Tiberius's reign.
*   **Sodales Augustales:** A priesthood established by Tiberius to administer the imperial cult to the deified Augustus.
*   **Gens Claudia:** The ancient patrician family into which Tiberius was born, one of the most prominent in the early Roman Republic.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Motives for Retirement:** The reasons for Tiberius's sudden withdrawal to Rhodes in 6 BC are unclear. Theories include frustration with his unhappy marriage to Julia, a belief that he was only a placeholder for Augustus's grandsons, or a genuine desire for private life.
*   **Character and Tyranny:** Historians debate whether Tiberius was a reluctant, competent administrator who became paranoid after personal tragedies, or a hypocritical and cruel tyrant from the start. The ancient sources are overwhelmingly negative, but some modern scholars argue this is exaggerated.
*   **Responsibility for Sejanus:** The extent of Tiberius's knowledge and complicity in Sejanus's early purges is uncertain. It is debated whether he was manipulated or initially condoned the prefect's actions before turning on him.
*   **Cause of Death:** Ancient accounts differ on whether Tiberius died naturally or was murdered by Caligula and the prefect Macro. No definitive evidence exists.

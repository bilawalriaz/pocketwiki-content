# Kampala

## Overview
Kampala is the capital and largest city of Uganda, serving as the nation's primary economic, political, and cultural hub. Its metropolitan area generates over half of Uganda's GDP, making it vital to the country's economy. The city's history is deeply intertwined with the Buganda Kingdom and British colonialism, shaping its unique geography and development.

## Timeline
- **Pre-colonial** — Area is core of Buganda Kingdom; capital (Kibuga) shifts between hills.
- **1890** — Frederick Lugard of Imperial British East Africa Company arrives; builds Fort Lugard on "Hill of the Impala" (Kampala).
- **1900** — Buganda Agreement formalizes British rule, allocating land on hills to missions, government, and the Kabaka.
- **1906** — Kampala Township boundary set; first sanitary board created.
- **1922** — Makerere University founded as Uganda Technical College.
- **1931** — Uganda Railway reaches Kampala, connecting it to Mombasa.
- **1938** — First electric street lights switched on.
- **1962** — Uganda gains independence; capital transferred from Entebbe to Kampala, which gains city status.
- **1968** — Kampala's boundaries expanded to current size (189 km²).
- **1986** — Battle of Kampala during Ugandan Bush War; National Resistance Movement captures the city.
- **2010** — Kampala Capital City Authority Act enacted, increasing central government control.
- **2024** — City proper population reaches 1,797,722 (2024 census).

## Body

### Etymology and Early History
The name "Kampala" derives from "Akasozi k’empala" (Hill of the Impala), referring to Old Kampala Hill, a former hunting ground for the Kabaka (King) of Buganda. The British colonial settlement established there in 1890 gave its name to the expanding city. The area was historically the site of the shifting Kibuga (capital) of the Buganda Kingdom, with each king establishing his capital on a different hill.

### Colonial Development and Planning
British colonial rule, formalized by the 1900 Buganda Agreement, structured Kampala's development by allocating specific hills to religious missions (e.g., Lubaga for Catholics, Namirembe for Anglicans) and the government. The city's first land-use plan was created in 1912. Subsequent plans, like Ernst May's 1947 design and Henry Kendall's 1951 plan, aimed to expand and modernize the city but were often only partially implemented. Kampala became the capital upon Uganda's independence in 1962.

### Geography and Climate
Kampala is built on numerous hills and valleys filled with swamps, covering 15% of its land area. The original seven hills include Old Kampala, Mengo, Kibuli, Namirembe, Lubaga, Nsambya, and Nakasero. The city has a tropical rainforest climate with two wetter seasons, heaviest from February to June. Its highest point is Kololo Hill (1,311 m).

### Demographics and Economy
The city proper's population has grown rapidly, reaching nearly 1.8 million in 2024. Its population is ethnically diverse, drawing from all of Uganda and neighboring countries. Kampala is Uganda's economic engine, hosting all 25 licensed commercial banks and major media companies. The informal sector, including urban agriculture in wetlands, is a significant contributor to the economy.

### Transport and Infrastructure
Transport includes boda-bodas (motorcycle taxis), minibuses ("taxis"), and a developing bus system. Plans for a light rail system exist. The city is served by Entebbe International Airport. Major roads include the Northern Bypass. A passenger rail service between Namanve and Kampala was restored in 2018.

### Health and Education
Kampala is Uganda's healthcare hub, with Mulago National Referral Hospital as the largest public facility and several private hospitals. Education ranges from private pre-primary institutions to major universities like Makerere University (founded 1922) and Kyambogo University.

### Culture and Sport
Cultural institutions include the Uganda Museum, the National Theatre, and the UNESCO World Heritage Kasubi Tombs (burial grounds of Buganda kings). The Ndere Cultural Centre promotes Ugandan arts. Kampala hosts major sports facilities like Mandela National Stadium and is home to football clubs in the Uganda Premier League. It will co-host the 2027 Africa Cup of Nations.

## Terms
- **Kabaka**: The king of the Buganda Kingdom.
- **Kibuga**: The historical capital of the Buganda Kingdom, which shifted between hills.
- **Mailo**: A land tenure system created by the 1900 Buganda Agreement, granting freehold ownership.
- **Boda-boda**: A popular motorcycle taxi service.
- **Taxi**: In Kampala, refers to a 15-seater minibus used for public transport.
- **Kampala Capital City Authority (KCCA)**: The governing body for Kampala, created by the 2010 Act.
- **Mulago**: Uganda's national referral hospital and a teaching hospital for Makerere University.
- **Kasubi Tombs**: A UNESCO World Heritage Site and the burial ground for Buganda kings.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The effectiveness of Kampala's urban planning is debated, as multiple physical plans (1947, 1951, 1972, 1994) were never fully implemented due to political and economic turmoil.
- The city faces ongoing challenges with traffic congestion, informal settlements, and the management of its wetlands, which are used for urban agriculture but are ecologically sensitive.
- The balance of control between the central government (via KCCA) and local authorities remains a point of administrative discussion.

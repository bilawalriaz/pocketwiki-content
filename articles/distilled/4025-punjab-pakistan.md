# Punjab, Pakistan

## Overview
Punjab is the most populous and economically dominant province of Pakistan, forming the bulk of the transnational Punjab region partitioned in 1947. Known as the "breadbasket" of the nation, its fertile plains and major rivers have made it a cradle of civilizations and a center of cultural and political history in South Asia.

## Timeline
- **c. 3000 BCE** — Bronze Age Harrapan civilisation flourishes in the region.
- **1500–500 BCE** — Indo-Aryan migrations lead to the Vedic civilisation; the Rigveda is composed.
- **518 BCE** — Achaemenid emperor Darius the Great annexes the region up to the Jhelum River.
- **326 BCE** — King Porus fights Alexander the Great at the Battle of the Hydaspes.
- **8th century CE** — Arab armies of the Umayyad Caliphate introduce Islam into Punjab.
- **11th century** — Punjab is annexed by the Ghaznavid dynasty; Lahore becomes its capital.
- **1206** — The Delhi Sultanate is established, beginning three centuries of rule over Punjab.
- **1526** — Mughal emperor Babur defeats the Lodis, establishing Mughal rule.
- **1799–1849** — Ranjit Singh establishes the Sikh Empire, which rules until British annexation.
- **1947** — The province is partitioned between India and Pakistan, causing massive migration and violence.

## Body

### Etymology and Geography
The name "Punjab" derives from Persian words meaning "Land of Five Waters," referring to the Jhelum, Chenab, Ravi, Sutlej, and Beas rivers. The province is Pakistan's second-largest by area, characterized by fertile alluvial plains, the Salt Range, and parts of the Thar and Thal deserts. It borders all other Pakistani provinces and shares an international border with India.

### Ancient and Medieval History
The region shows evidence of early human habitation in the Soan Valley. It was a core area of the Harrapan civilisation and later the Vedic civilisation. After invasions by the Achaemenids, Greeks (including Alexander the Great), and Mauryas, the region saw the rise of the Indo-Greek Kingdom. From the 8th century, Arab conquests introduced Islam. Subsequent rule passed through the Ghaznavid, Ghurid, and Delhi Sultanate dynasties, with the Mongol invasions of the 13th century causing significant disruption.

### Mughal, Sikh, and British Rule
The Mughal Empire established control in 1526, dividing the region into the Lahore and Multan provinces. The 18th century saw the decline of Mughal authority, the rise of Sikh principalities (misls), and the eventual establishment of the Sikh Empire by Ranjit Singh. The British annexed Punjab in 1849 after the Anglo-Sikh Wars. Under British rule, Punjab became a key colonial asset and a center of anti-colonial movements. The Jallianwala massacre of 1919 in Amritsar was a pivotal event.

### Partition and Modern Province
The 1947 partition split Punjab between India and Pakistan, leading to massive communal violence and migration that resulted in religious homogeneity on both sides. Post-independence, Pakistani Punjab has been the country's economic powerhouse, contributing over half of its GDP. It is the most industrialized and urbanized province, though a development gap exists between its more prosperous north and poorer south.

## Terms
- **Doab**: A tract of land lying between two rivers; Punjab has four major doabs.
- **Misl**: A sovereign confederacy of Sikh chieftains that ruled parts of Punjab in the 18th century.
- **Subah**: A province of the Mughal Empire.
- **Sufism**: A mystical branch of Islam that has heavily influenced Punjabi culture.
- **Green Revolution**: A period of agricultural transformation in the mid-20th century that increased Punjab's productivity.
- **Basant**: A seasonal festival celebrating the arrival of spring, historically associated with kite flying.

## Debates and open questions
- The status of the Punjabi language is a social issue, with activists demanding its promotion as a medium of instruction against the dominance of Urdu and English.
- The economic and developmental divide between northern and southern Punjab remains a significant challenge.

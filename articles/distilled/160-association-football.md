# Association football

## Overview

Association football, commonly known as football or soccer, is a team sport played between two teams of 11 players who use their feet to kick an inflated ball, aiming to score more goals than the opposing team. Governed by the Laws of the Game, it is the world's most popular sport, with an estimated 250 million players active in over 200 countries. The sport is organized globally by FIFA, with the FIFA World Cup being its most prestigious and most-viewed international competition.

## Timeline

- **c. 8th century** — FIFA states soccer originated in Britain; no historical connection to ancient games outside Europe.
- **1848** — Cambridge rules drawn up at University of Cambridge, influential in developing subsequent codes.
- **1863** — Football Association (FA) founded in London; first comprehensive Laws of the Game published.
- **1872** — First official international match: Scotland vs. England in Glasgow; FA Cup founded.
- **1886** — International Football Association Board (IFAB) formed in Manchester.
- **1888** — World's first football league founded in Birmingham by William McGregor.
- **1904** — FIFA formed in Paris, adhering to the FA's Laws of the Game.
- **1930** — First FIFA World Cup inaugurated.
- **1991** — First FIFA Women's World Cup held in China.
- **2023** — Women's World Cup expands to 32 teams; Spain wins first title.

## Body

### Name and Origins

Association football is part of a family of football games. The term "association" refers to the Football Association (FA), founded in London in 1863, which published the first rules. The term "soccer" comes from Oxford "-er" slang from about 1875, initially spelt "assoccer" (shortening of "association"). In Great Britain, the sport is usually called "football," while "soccer" is common in Australia, Canada, South Africa, most of Ireland, and the United States.

Kicking ball games arose independently across cultures. The Chinese game cuju (蹴鞠) is the earliest form of a kicking game with historical evidence, first recorded in the Zhan Guo Ce, a military history from the Han dynasty. During the Han dynasty (206 BCE – 220 CE), cuju games were standardised. The Silk Road facilitated its transmission; the Tang dynasty invented the inflatable ball. Other East Asian games include kemari in Japan and chuk-guk in Korea. In North America, the Algonquians played pasuckuakohowog, described as "almost identical" to European folk football.

### Modern Codification

The Cambridge rules (1848), written at Trinity College with representatives from Eton, Harrow, Rugby, Winchester, and Shrewsbury, were influential but not universally adopted. The FA first met on 26 October 1863 at the Freemasons' Tavern in London. The laws banned running with the ball in hand and hacking (kicking an opponent in the shins). Blackheath FC withdrew over the hacking ban and later helped form the Rugby Football Union in 1871. The Sheffield FA played by its own rules until the 1870s, with the FA absorbing some rules until little difference remained.

The FA Cup (from 1872) is the world's oldest football competition. The first international match (Scotland vs. England, 1872) was instigated by Charles W. Alcock. The first league was founded in Birmingham in 1888 by William McGregor with 12 clubs. The IFAB was formed in 1886 by the four British associations; FIFA joined in 1913.

### Contemporary Era

For most of the 20th century, Europe and South America dominated. The FIFA World Cup (1930) became the main stage. The European Cup and Copa Libertadores were created, with champions contesting the Intercontinental Cup. In the 21st century, European clubs remain dominant, often signing the best players from Latin America. Football has improved in Africa, Asia, and North America. No team from outside Europe or South America has reached a men's World Cup final. Football plays an important role in life; the Ivory Coast national team helped secure a truce in its civil war (2006–2007), while football was the proximate cause of the 1969 Football War between El Salvador and Honduras.

### Women's Association Football

Women's football has historically faced opposition, with national associations curbing or outlawing it. Frescoes from the Han dynasty (25–220 CE) depict female cuju players. The first women's match recorded by the Scottish FA took place in 1892 in Glasgow. The British Ladies' Football Club was founded by Nettie Honeyball in 1894. Women's football grew during the First World War with female employment in heavy industry. Dick, Kerr Ladies F.C. was the most successful team of the era. In 1921, the FA outlawed the game on association members' pitches, stating the game was "quite unsuitable for females." Bans also occurred in Brazil (1941–1979), France (1941–1970), and West Germany (1955–1970). Restrictions began to be reduced in the late 1960s and 1970s. The FA rescinded its ban in 1971. The FIFA Women's World Cup was inaugurated in 1991; by 2019 it had expanded to 24 teams with 1.12 billion viewers. North America is the dominant region, with the United States winning the most World Cups and Olympic tournaments.

### Gameplay and Laws

The game is played with a spherical ball of 68–70 cm circumference. Players other than goalkeepers may not deliberately handle the ball with their hands or arms. The primary law is that only goalkeepers may use hands, within their penalty area. Play stops only when the ball leaves the field or the referee stops play. Most matches produce few goals; the 2022–23 English Premier League averaged 2.85 goals per match. Positions (strikers, defenders, midfielders) are not specified by the Laws but have evolved; the layout is known as a formation.

There are seventeen laws, maintained by IFAB and published by FIFA. Each team has a maximum of eleven players (minimum usually seven). Goalkeepers must wear distinguishable clothing. Substitutions are typically limited to five in 90 minutes. A referee has full authority, assisted by two assistant referees and often a fourth official. Goal line technology determines if the whole ball crossed the goal-line; VAR (video assistant referees) corrects "clear and obvious" mistakes in four call types: goals, red cards, penalties, and mistaken identity.

The pitch for international matches is 100–110 m long and 64–75 m wide. Goals are 7.32 m wide and 2.44 m high. The penalty area marks where the goalkeeper may handle the ball. A standard match is two 45-minute halves, running continuously; the referee may add stoppage time. Added time was introduced after an 1891 incident where a goalkeeper deliberately kicked the ball out of play to prevent a penalty. Tie-breaking methods include extra time (two 15-minute periods) and penalty shoot-outs. Restart methods include kick-off, throw-in, goal kick, corner kick, indirect and direct free kicks, penalty kick, and dropped-ball. Misconduct is punished by yellow (caution) or red (dismissal) cards; a second yellow leads to a red. Off-field sanctions include fines, point deductions, and expulsion.

### Governance and Competitions

FIFA, headquartered in Zürich, is the international governing body, with six continental confederations: AFC, CAF, UEFA, CONCACAF, OFC, and CONMEBOL. The IFAB sets the Laws, with the four UK associations holding one vote each and FIFA holding four. The World Cup has been held every four years since 1930 (except 1942 and 1946 due to World War II), expanding to 48 teams in 2026. The 2022 tournament was watched by an estimated 5 billion people. The current men's champions are Spain, who won their second title at the 2026 tournament in the United States; the current women's champions are also Spain, after winning their first title in the 2023 tournament. A football tournament has been at every Summer Olympics since 1900 except 1932. Since 1992, male competitors must be under 23 (with three over-age players allowed since 1996); the women's tournament has no age restrictions. Continental championships include the European Championship, Copa América, Africa Cup of Nations, Asian Cup, Gold Cup, and Nations Cup. The FIFA Confederations Cup was discontinued after 2017.

Domestic competitions vary. Most European leagues use a round-robin format with promotion and relegation, awarding three points for a win. Latin American leagues often split into Apertura and Clausura. Major League Soccer (US/Canada) and the A-League (Australia) use closed league systems without promotion/relegation. The top five European leagues (Premier League, Bundesliga, La Liga, Serie A, Ligue 1) generated €19.6 billion in combined revenue in 2022–23.

## Terms

- **Laws of the Game**: The official rules of association football, maintained by IFAB since 1886.
- **IFAB**: International Football Association Board; sets the Laws, with four UK associations and FIFA as members.
- **FIFA**: Fédération Internationale de Football Association; the international governing body, formed in 1904.
- **Cuju**: Ancient Chinese kicking game, the earliest form of a kicking game with historical evidence.
- **Hacking**: Kicking an opponent in the shins; banned in the 1863 FA rules.
- **VAR**: Video assistant referees; used to correct "clear and obvious" mistakes in goals, red cards, penalties, and mistaken identity.
- **Penalty area**: Marked area in front of each goal where the goalkeeper may handle the ball and where certain fouls result in a penalty kick.
- **Stoppage time**: Additional time added by the referee for stoppages; also called injury time or added time.
- **Offside**: A position in which a player may not receive a pass; a goal scored from an offside position can be disallowed.

## Debates and open questions

FIFA has stated that soccer originated in Britain around the eighth century and that no historical connection exists with any game played in antiquity outside Europe, though kicking ball games arose independently across multiple cultures. The 1921 FA ban on women's football has been argued by players and writers to have been motivated by envy of large crowds and lack of control over money, rather than the stated reason that the game was "quite unsuitable for females." The IFAB's 2008 decision to fix international pitch dimensions at 105 m by 68 m was later put on hold and never implemented.

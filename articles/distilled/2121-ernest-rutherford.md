# Ernest Rutherford

## Overview
Ernest Rutherford (1871–1937) was a New Zealand-born physicist and chemist, widely regarded as "the father of nuclear physics." His pioneering experiments revealed the structure of the atom, defined the nature of radioactive decay, and led to the discovery of the proton and the theoretical prediction of the neutron, fundamentally shaping modern physics and chemistry.

## Body

### Early Life and Education
Rutherford was born in New Zealand and educated at local schools before winning a scholarship to Nelson College in 1887. He later attended Canterbury College, University of New Zealand, earning degrees in arts, science, and mathematics. In 1895, he won an 1851 Research Fellowship to study at the Cavendish Laboratory in Cambridge, where he began his research under J. J. Thomson.

### Radioactivity and the Nobel Prize
At Cambridge, Rutherford began studying uranium's radioactivity, discovering two distinct types of penetrating radiation. In 1899, he named them alpha and beta rays. After moving to McGill University in Canada, he collaborated with Frederick Soddy to study radioactive gases from thorium. They identified a new substance, later found to be an isotope of radon, and formulated the "Law of Radioactive Change," introducing the concept of radioactive half-life. This work demonstrated that radioactivity involves the spontaneous disintegration of atoms, a radical departure from the idea of atoms as indestructible. In 1903, he named a third, highly penetrating emission the gamma ray. For this research, he was awarded the 1908 Nobel Prize in Chemistry.

### The Nuclear Atom
In 1907, Rutherford moved to the University of Manchester. There, he and his colleagues developed methods to count alpha particles and proved they were helium nuclei. The pivotal moment came in 1909 when, under his direction, Hans Geiger and Ernest Marsden performed the gold foil experiment. They observed that some alpha particles were deflected at very large angles when fired at gold foil. Rutherford's interpretation of this data led him to propose in 1911 that an atom's positive charge and most of its mass are concentrated in a tiny, central nucleus. In 1912, he invited Niels Bohr to his lab; Bohr adapted this nuclear model with quantum theory, creating the Bohr model of the atom.

### Discovery of the Proton and Prediction of the Neutron
In 1913, Rutherford and Henry Moseley developed the atomic numbering system. In 1917, while at Manchester, Rutherford performed experiments bombarding nitrogen with alpha particles. He discovered that this reaction ejected a particle he initially called a "hydrogen atom," which he later identified as a fundamental particle and renamed the proton. This was the first artificially induced nuclear reaction. In 1919, he returned to Cambridge as Director of the Cavendish Laboratory. In 1920, he postulated the existence of a neutral particle, which he called the neutron, to explain why atomic nuclei did not fly apart due to proton repulsion. His associate James Chadwick experimentally confirmed the neutron's existence in 1932.

### Leadership and Later Work
As Cavendish Director, Rutherford fostered an environment that led to major discoveries. Under his leadership, Chadwick discovered the neutron (1932), and John Cockcroft and Ernest Walton performed the first controlled nuclear disintegration using a particle accelerator (1932). During World War I, Rutherford worked on submarine detection, contributing to the development of piezoelectricity for ultrasound technology. He died in 1937 from complications following surgery for a strangulated hernia.

## Terms
*   **Radioactivity:** The spontaneous emission of radiation from the disintegration of atomic nuclei.
*   **Alpha/Beta/Gamma Rays:** The three main types of radiation emitted during radioactive decay, identified by Rutherford. Alpha particles are helium nuclei, beta particles are electrons, and gamma rays are high-energy photons.
*   **Half-life:** The time required for half of the atoms in a radioactive sample to decay.
*   **Nucleus:** The small, dense, positively charged core of an atom, containing most of its mass.
*   **Proton:** A subatomic particle with a positive electric charge, found in the nucleus.
*   **Neutron:** A neutral subatomic particle found in the nucleus, with a mass similar to a proton.
*   **Piezoelectricity:** The electric charge that accumulates in certain solid materials in response to applied mechanical stress, used in ultrasound technology.

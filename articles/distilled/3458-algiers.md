# Algiers

## Overview
Algiers is the capital and largest city of Algeria, located on the Mediterranean coast. Its strategic location has made it a center of diverse cultural and political influences for millennia, from Phoenician traders to Ottoman corsairs and French colonizers. Today, it is a major metropolis and the political, economic, and cultural heart of modern Algeria.

## Timeline
- **c. 1200–250 BC** — Phoenician trading settlement established (ancient Ikosion/Icosium).
- **972 AD** — Modern city formally founded by Buluggin ibn Ziri of the Zirid dynasty.
- **1516** — Oruç Reis seizes the city, beginning Ottoman rule; it becomes a base for Barbary pirates.
- **1830** — French invasion; Algiers becomes the capital of French Algeria.
- **1942–1944** — Serves as the administrative center of Free France during World War II.
- **1957** — Battle of Algiers during the Algerian War of Independence.
- **1962** — Algeria gains independence; Algiers remains the capital.
- **1988** — "Spring of Algiers" protests demand democratic reforms.
- **1992** — The Casbah of Algiers is designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

## Body

### Name and Origins
The city's Arabic name, *al-Jazāʾir*, means "the Islands," referencing four main islands off the coast that were later connected to the mainland in 1525. This name derives from the earlier *Jazāʾir Banī Mazghanna* ("Islands of the Banu Mazghanna"), used when Buluggin ibn Ziri refounded the city in 972 AD on the ruins of the Punic settlement Icosium. The local Berber name is *Dzayer*. In classical antiquity, it was known as *Ikósion* to the Greeks and *Icosium* to the Romans.

### Early and Medieval History
The site has been inhabited since at least 1200 BC. It was a Phoenician trading port, then part of Numidia, the Roman Empire, and the Vandal and Byzantine Empires. Following the Muslim conquest of the Maghreb in the 7th century, the area came under various Islamic caliphates. The modern city was established in 972 AD by the Zirid dynasty. It later became part of the Hammadid dynasty and then the Almohad Caliphate, experiencing periods of relative independence under local amirs.

### Ottoman Rule and Piracy
In 1516, the corsair Oruç Reis was invited to oust the Spanish, seized power, and later placed the city under Ottoman sovereignty. Algiers became the chief seat of the Barbary pirates, whose raids and slave-taking were a major economic activity. The city was a cosmopolitan hub with a significant population of renegade Christians. A failed Spanish siege in 1541 and a major Anglo-Dutch bombardment in 1816 marked the era.

### French Colonial Period
Tensions culminated in the 1830 French invasion, following the "fly-whisk incident" with the ruling Dey. Algiers became the capital of French Algeria, which was declared an integral part of France. The French demolished parts of the old Casbah to build a European-style city with boulevards and Haussmannian architecture. The colonial period was marked by racial segregation under the *Code de l'indigénat* and the displacement of the native population. During World War II, Algiers was seized by the Allies in 1942 and briefly served as the capital of Free France.

### Independence and Modern Era
Algiers was the epicenter of the brutal Algerian War (1954–1962), notably the 1957 Battle of Algiers. After independence in 1962, the city expanded massively. It became a symbol of anti-colonialism and a member of the Non-Aligned Movement. The 1988 "Spring of Algiers" protests led to democratic reforms and a new constitution in 1989. The city was later scarred by the Algerian Civil War of the 1990s and a 2007 terrorist attack.

### Geography and Climate
Algiers is located on the Bay of Algiers, backed by the Mitidja Plain and the Bouzaréah massif. It has a Mediterranean climate with mild, rainy winters and warm, dry summers. The city is considered highly vulnerable to future sea-level rise due to climate change.

### Administration and Economy
The city is administered as part of Algiers Province and is divided into numerous communes. It is the economic engine of Algeria, contributing about 20% of the national GDP and hosting major companies like Sonatrach and Air Algérie. The economy is supported by trade, finance, and growing tourism centered on its historic Casbah and modern attractions.

### Architecture and Landmarks
Algiers blends architectural styles. The historic Casbah is a UNESCO site of traditional Algerian, Ottoman, and Andalusian architecture. Notable landmarks include the 11th-century Djamaa el Kebir mosque, the 17th-century New Mosque, the Ketchaoua Mosque (converted from a cathedral), the neo-Moorish Grand Post Office, and the modern Martyrs' Memorial (Maqam Echahid).

## Terms
- **Casbah**: The historic citadel or old quarter of Algiers, a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
- **Barbary pirates**: Privateers operating from North African ports, particularly Algiers, who raided European shipping and coasts from the 16th to early 19th centuries.
- **Pieds-noirs**: French citizens of European descent who lived in Algeria before its independence in 1962.
- **Code de l'indigénat**: A French colonial legal code that enforced discriminatory status on the indigenous Muslim population.
- **Dey**: The title of the ruler of the Ottoman Regency of Algiers from 1671 to 1818.
- **Zirid dynasty**: A Berber Sunni Muslim dynasty that ruled parts of North Africa from the 10th to 12th centuries.
- **Mitidja**: A fertile plain in northern Algeria where Algiers is located.
- **Maqam Echahid**: The Martyrs' Memorial, an iconic concrete monument in Algiers commemorating the Algerian War of Independence.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The exact origin of the Berber name *Dzayer* is debated, with theories linking it to the Zirid founders or a contraction of a term meaning "those of Ziri."
- The authorship of the primary 17th-century source *Topografía e historia general de Argel*, which details life in Ottoman Algiers, is disputed.
- The human and material cost of the Algerian War (1954–1962) remains a subject of significant scholarly debate, with death toll estimates ranging widely from 350,000 to 1,500,000.

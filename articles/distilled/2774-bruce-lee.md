# Bruce Lee

## Overview
Bruce Lee (1940–1973) was a Hong Kong and American martial artist, actor, and filmmaker who became a global cultural icon. He is credited with popularizing martial arts films in the 1970s, founding the hybrid martial arts philosophy Jeet Kune Do, and breaking racial stereotypes in Western cinema. His influence extends to modern combat sports, film, and popular culture worldwide.

## Body

### Early Life and Martial Arts Training
Born to Cantonese opera singer Lee Hoi-chuen and Grace Ho, Lee was introduced to film as a child. He began learning tai chi at age seven and later studied Wing Chun under Ip Man, though he faced prejudice due to his mixed ancestry. He also trained in boxing, winning a Hong Kong tournament in 1958. After a series of street fights, his parents sent him to the United States in 1959 to claim his citizenship.

### Education and Early Career in the U.S.
Lee settled in Seattle, working as a waiter and opening his first martial arts school. He enrolled at the University of Washington in 1961, where he studied drama and philosophy. In 1964, he moved to Oakland, California, and gained attention at the Long Beach International Karate Championships with demonstrations like the "one-inch punch." His role as Kato in *The Green Hornet* (1966–1967) introduced him to American audiences.

### Development of Jeet Kune Do and Hong Kong Stardom
A controversial 1964 match with Wong Jack-man influenced Lee's philosophy, leading him to reject rigid traditional styles. In 1967, he founded Jeet Kune Do ("way of the intercepting fist"), a hybrid system emphasizing practicality and flexibility. After limited success in Hollywood, he returned to Hong Kong in 1971. His films *The Big Boss* (1971), *Fist of Fury* (1972), and *The Way of the Dragon* (1972) made him an international star and revolutionized martial arts cinema with realistic choreography.

### Final Films and Death
Lee's American-Hong Kong co-production *Enter the Dragon* (1973) became a global hit. He died on July 20, 1973, at age 32. The official cause was cerebral edema, attributed to a reaction to medication, but theories include heat stroke and hyponatremia (low blood sodium). His unfinished film *Game of Death* was completed and released posthumously in 1978.

### Legacy and Cultural Impact
Lee is regarded as the most influential martial artist of all time and a pop culture icon. He inspired the "kung fu craze" of the 1970s and is credited as the "father of mixed martial arts" (MMA) for his cross-training philosophy. His films helped change the portrayal of Asians in Western media, defying emasculated stereotypes. His influence persists in film, television, comics, video games, and combat sports.

## Terms
- **Jeet Kune Do**: The hybrid martial arts philosophy founded by Bruce Lee, meaning "way of the intercepting fist." It emphasizes practicality, flexibility, and absorbing techniques from various styles.
- **Wing Chun**: A close-range Chinese kung fu style focusing on efficiency and directness, which Lee studied under Ip Man.
- **Cerebral Edema**: Swelling of the brain, the official cause of Lee's death.
- **Hyponatremia**: A condition of insufficient sodium concentration in the blood, a proposed cause of Lee's fatal cerebral edema.
- **Kung Fu Craze**: The surge of Western interest in Chinese martial arts and films sparked by Lee's work in the 1970s.
- **One-Inch Punch**: A demonstration of power developed by Lee, where a punch delivered from a very short distance can forcefully push back an opponent.
- **Rooftop Fights**: Organized bare-knuckle fights between rival martial arts school gangs in 1950s–60s Hong Kong, which Lee participated in.
- **Jun Fan Gung Fu**: The name for Lee's initial martial arts system, meaning "Bruce Lee's Kung Fu," which later evolved into Jeet Kune Do.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Cause of Death**: While the official verdict was "death by misadventure" from cerebral edema due to medication, alternative theories include heat stroke (due to removed sweat glands) and hyponatremia from excessive water intake.
- **Match with Wong Jack-man**: Accounts differ on the fight's duration (3 minutes vs. 20–25 minutes) and outcome. Lee's dissatisfaction with the fight reportedly spurred his philosophical evolution.
- **Role in *Kung Fu* TV Series**: Lee pitched a series called *The Warrior*, which Warner Bros. redeveloped as *Kung Fu* and cast David Carradine, citing Lee's accent and ethnicity as barriers.
- **Grace Ho's Parentage**: Lee's mother's ancestry is contested, with theories involving German, Dutch-Jewish, English, or Chinese heritage.

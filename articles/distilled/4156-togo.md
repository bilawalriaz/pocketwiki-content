# Togo

## Overview
Togo is a small, narrow country in West Africa, bordered by Ghana, Benin, and Burkina Faso. It is one of the world's least developed nations, with an economy heavily reliant on agriculture and phosphate mining. Its modern history is defined by European colonialism and a long period of authoritarian rule by the Gnassingbé family, which began with a 1967 coup and continues today under a reformed parliamentary system.

## Body

### History and Politics
Various peoples, including the Ewe, Mina, and Gun, settled the area between the 11th and 16th centuries. The coast later became a major European slave-trading hub, earning the region the name "The Slave Coast." In 1884, Germany established a protectorate named Togoland. After Germany's defeat in World War I, the territory was split into British and French mandates. The British portion voted to join Ghana in 1957, while French Togoland became autonomous and gained full independence in 1960.

The post-independence period was marked by instability. The first president, Sylvanus Olympio, was assassinated in a 1963 coup. In 1967, Gnassingbé Eyadéma overthrew the interim government and established a single-party state. He ruled for 38 years until his death in 2005, making him Africa's longest-serving leader at the time. His son, Faure Gnassingbé, was then installed as president by the military, a move condemned internationally as a coup. After protests and elections, Faure Gnassingbé has remained in power, winning contested elections in 2005, 2010, 2015, and 2020. In 2024, a constitutional reform shifted Togo from a presidential to a parliamentary system. The reform took effect in May 2025, making Faure Gnassingbé the head of government (President of the Council of Ministers) and installing a largely ceremonial president.

### Economy and Geography
Togo's economy is based on agriculture, which employs about half the workforce, and mining, particularly phosphate. It is a member of the Economic Community of West African States (ECOWAS) and serves as a regional trade center. The country has faced challenges from political instability, depressed commodity prices, and stalled economic reforms.

Geographically, Togo is a narrow strip of land extending from the Gulf of Guinea northward. Its landscape varies from coastal lagoons and marshes to a hilly central plateau and rolling savannah in the north. The highest point is Mont Agou (986 m), and the longest river is the Mono River (400 km). The climate is generally tropical.

### Society and Culture
Togo is home to about 40 ethnic groups. The largest are the Ewe (32%) in the south and the Kabye (22%) in the north. French is the official language, while Ewe and Kabiye are designated national languages. The population is religiously diverse, with Christians (49.2%), Muslims (18.6%), and adherents of traditional religions (17.0%) as of the 2022 census. Cultural traditions include Ewe vodun practices, Kabye wrestling rites, and the weaving of ceremonial garments. The "Nana Benz" are famous Togolese women traders who historically dominated the wax-print cloth market.

## Terms
- **Protectorate:** A territory that is nominally under the control of a foreign power but retains some local governance.
- **Mandate:** A territory administered on behalf of the League of Nations (later UN) by a member state after World War I.
- **Coup d'état:** A sudden, illegal seizure of power from a government.
- **Single-party state:** A system of government where one political party dominates and other parties are banned.
- **Gbe family:** A group of related languages spoken in West Africa, including Ewe and Fon.
- **Vodun:** A traditional religion practiced in West Africa, involving the worship of spirits and deities.
- **Commonwealth of Nations:** A voluntary association of 56 independent countries, mostly former territories of the British Empire.
- **ECOWAS:** The Economic Community of West African States, a regional political and economic union.

## Debates and Open Questions
The legitimacy of Faure Gnassingbé's succession in 2005 and his subsequent election victories remain contested, with opposition groups and international observers alleging fraud and irregularities. The 2024 constitutional reform, which shifted power to the head of government (a position held by Gnassingbé), has been criticized as a maneuver to extend his family's dynastic rule under a new system. The long-term impact of joining the Commonwealth on Togo's political and economic alignment is also an open question.

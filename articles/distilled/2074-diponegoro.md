# Diponegoro

## Overview
Prince Diponegoro (1785–1855) was a Javanese prince and devout Muslim who led a major five-year rebellion (the Java War, 1825–1830) against Dutch colonial rule. His struggle, rooted in political succession disputes and opposition to Dutch influence, became a foundational symbol of Indonesian nationalism and resistance, earning him status as a National Hero of Indonesia.

## Timeline
- **11 November 1785** — Born in Yogyakarta as Bendara Raden Mas Mustahar, eldest son of Sultan Hamengkubuwono III.
- **1812** — Acts as adviser to his father during British invasion; declines offer of Sultan title from Stamford Raffles.
- **1814** — Passed over for succession after father's death in favor of younger half-brother, Hamengkubuwono IV.
- **1822** — Mount Merapi erupts; Hamengkubuwono IV dies, leading to a dispute over guardianship of infant heir.
- **1825** — Java War begins; Diponegoro leads guerrilla warfare against Dutch forces.
- **1829** — Dutch General De Kock's strategy of fortified camps and mobile forces turns the tide against Diponegoro.
- **28 March 1830** — Captured under a flag of truce at Magelang, ending the war.
- **1830** — Exiled first to Manado, Sulawesi.
- **July 1833** — Moved to Fort Rotterdam in Makassar.
- **8 January 1855** — Dies in exile in Makassar at age 69.
- **1973** — Declared a National Hero of Indonesia.
- **March 2020** — Dutch King Willem-Alexander returns Diponegoro's kris (dagger) to Indonesia.

## Body

### Early Life and Succession Dispute
Born into the Yogyakarta Sultanate, Diponegoro's youth was marked by political upheaval, including the end of VOC rule, a British invasion, and the return of Dutch control. He advised his father, Sultan Hamengkubuwono III, during these events. After his father's death in 1814, Diponegoro was passed over for the throne in favor of his younger half-brother, who was supported by the Dutch. Diponegoro, a devout Muslim, opposed the court's pro-Dutch policies and perceived relaxation of religious observance. A further succession dispute in 1822, following the death of his half-brother and the appointment of an infant heir, solidified his opposition.

### The Java War (1825–1830)
Diponegoro's rebellion drew support from Javanese farmers and nobles angered by Dutch taxes, land policies, and crop failures. He was widely believed to be the prophesied "Ratu Adil" (just ruler). Natural disasters like the 1822 Merapi eruption and a 1824 cholera epidemic fueled belief in an imminent cataclysm, bolstering his cause. The war began with Dutch losses due to Diponegoro's effective guerrilla tactics. The Dutch eventually committed more troops and General De Kock implemented a strategy of fortified camps (*benteng*) and mobile forces, which by 1829 regained the initiative. The conflict also had a racial dimension, with Diponegoro's forces targeting the Chinese minority, whom they viewed as foreign infidels.

### Capture, Exile, and Death
By 1830, Diponegoro's military position was untenable. During negotiations, he demanded a free state and the role of caliph for Java. On 28 March 1830, he was captured at Magelang under a flag of truce—an act he considered a Dutch betrayal, while the Dutch claimed he surrendered. He was exiled, first to Manado and then to Makassar's Fort Rotterdam in 1833. In exile, he wrote his autobiography, the *Babad Diponegoro*, and received visitors. He died in 1855 and was buried in a neighborhood then inhabited by Chinese and Dutch residents.

### Legacy
Diponegoro's resistance inspired fighters in the Indonesian National Revolution and modern nationalism. He was declared a National Hero in 1973. His legacy is commemorated in numerous institutions, including the Kodam IV/Diponegoro army command, naval ships, Diponegoro University, and major roads. His story is depicted in Javanese arts and his own *Babad Diponegoro*. In 2020, the Netherlands returned his kris (dagger) to Indonesia, though its authenticity is debated.

## Terms
- **Java War (1825–1830):** A major armed conflict in Java between Diponegoro's Javanese forces and the Dutch colonial army.
- **Ratu Adil:** A Javanese prophesied "just ruler" whose coming is often associated with times of crisis.
- **Benteng:** A strategy of fortified camps used by the Dutch to limit rebel movement and secure key areas.
- **Kris:** A traditional Javanese asymmetrical dagger, often considered to possess spiritual power.
- **Babad Diponegoro:** The autobiography of Prince Diponegoro, written during his exile.
- **Caliph:** A political and religious leader considered a successor to the Prophet Muhammad; Diponegoro demanded this title for all of Java.
- **VOC:** The Dutch East India Company (*Vereenigde Oostindische Compagnie*), which preceded direct Dutch colonial rule.
- **Sultanate of Yogyakarta:** A Javanese princely state under Dutch suzerainty, whose rulers held both traditional and administrative power.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Legitimacy of Succession:** Diponegoro believed he was promised the right to succeed his half-brother, though such a succession was illegal under Islamic rules.
- **Circumstances of Capture:** The Dutch claimed Diponegoro surrendered, while he and his supporters viewed his arrest under a flag of truce as a betrayal.
- **Authenticity of the Kris:** There is doubt whether the kris returned by the Netherlands in 2020 is the original one belonging to Diponegoro.
- **Racial Violence:** The extent and motivations behind the targeted violence against the Chinese minority during the war remain a noted aspect of the conflict.

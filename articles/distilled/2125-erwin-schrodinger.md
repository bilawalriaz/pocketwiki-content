# Erwin Schrödinger

## Overview
Erwin Schrödinger (1887–1961) was an Austrian theoretical physicist who was a central figure in the development of quantum mechanics. He is most famous for the Schrödinger equation, which describes how the quantum state of a physical system changes over time, and for the "Schrödinger's cat" thought experiment. His work, along with that of Werner Heisenberg and others, revolutionized physics and chemistry in the 20th century.

## Body

### Early Life and Career
Schrödinger was born in Vienna to a Catholic father and a Lutheran mother of English descent. He studied at the University of Vienna, receiving his Ph.D. in 1910 and completing his habilitation in 1914. After serving in World War I, he held academic positions in Jena, Stuttgart, Breslau, and Zurich. In 1927, he succeeded Max Planck in Berlin.

### Quantum Mechanics and Wave Mechanics
In the early 1920s, Schrödinger engaged with the "old quantum theory" of Bohr and Sommerfeld. In January 1926, he published his landmark paper introducing the Schrödinger equation, which provided a wave-based description of quantum phenomena. This "wave mechanics" was shown to be equivalent to Werner Heisenberg's matrix mechanics. Schrödinger was uncomfortable with the probabilistic interpretation of his theory, famously stating, "I don't like it, and I'm sorry I ever had anything to do with it." In 1935, he published a paper codifying "quantum entanglement," a phenomenon he called a departure from classical thought. To highlight the paradoxical implications of quantum superposition, he devised the "Schrödinger's cat" thought experiment.

### Exile and Later Work
Schrödinger left Germany in 1933 in protest of Nazi policies. After a brief, unsuccessful tenure at Oxford and a position in Graz, he fled Austria following the 1938 Anschluss. In 1939, he moved to Dublin at the invitation of Éamon de Valera, becoming director of the School of Theoretical Physics at the Dublin Institute for Advanced Studies. There, he wrote *What Is Life?* (1944), which explored genetics from a physical perspective and inspired James Watson and Francis Crick to research the gene. He also pursued a unified field theory with Albert Einstein, but abandoned the effort after criticism from Einstein. He returned to Vienna in 1956.

### Philosophy and Personal Life
Schrödinger was deeply interested in philosophy, particularly the works of Schopenhauer and Spinoza, and Eastern concepts like Vedanta. He saw consciousness as fundamental and anticipated ideas later associated with the many-worlds interpretation of quantum mechanics. He lived in an open marriage with his wife, Anny, and his mistress, Hildegunde March, with whom he had a daughter. His unconventional domestic arrangements contributed to difficulties in his career. Allegations of sexual abuse of minors emerged in a 1989 biography, though a 2026 paper challenged the evidence for these claims.

## Terms
- **Wave function:** A mathematical description of the quantum state of a system, whose evolution is governed by the Schrödinger equation.
- **Schrödinger equation:** The fundamental equation of wave mechanics that describes how the wave function of a physical system changes over time.
- **Quantum entanglement:** A phenomenon where the quantum states of two or more particles are linked, such that the state of one instantly influences the state of the other, regardless of distance.
- **Superposition:** The principle that a quantum system can exist in multiple states simultaneously until it is measured.
- **Unified field theory:** A theoretical framework that seeks to describe all fundamental forces and particles in terms of a single, coherent field.
- **Negentropy:** A concept from Schrödinger's *What Is Life?* referring to the ability of living organisms to maintain order by consuming negative entropy (or "free energy") from their environment.
- **Anschluss:** The annexation of Austria into Nazi Germany in March 1938.
- **Habilitation (venia legendi):** A post-doctoral qualification in some European countries that grants the right to teach at a university.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Interpretation of Quantum Mechanics:** Schrödinger was deeply dissatisfied with the Copenhagen interpretation's probabilistic nature and the role of the observer. His "cat" paradox was designed to critique this view.
- **Consciousness and Reality:** Schrödinger's philosophical writings, influenced by Vedanta, proposed a monist view where individual consciousness is an illusion, a perspective that remains a topic of debate in philosophy of mind.
- **Historical Allegations:** The nature and validity of allegations regarding Schrödinger's personal conduct, particularly those presented in Walter Moore's 1989 biography, are contested. A 2026 paper by Magdalena and Martin Gronau argues the claims are not substantiated.

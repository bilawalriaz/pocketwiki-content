# Ivory Coast

## Overview
Ivory Coast (officially Côte d'Ivoire) is a West African nation that was a French colony until 1960. It is a major economic power in the region, historically driven by cocoa and coffee production, but experienced severe political instability and civil wars in the early 21st century. Since 2011, it has returned to peace and strong economic growth, though challenges like poverty and ethnic tensions remain.

## Timeline
- **1843–44** — French protectorate established via treaties with local kings.
- **1893** — Ivory Coast becomes a formal French colony.
- **1960** — Independence achieved under Félix Houphouët-Boigny.
- **1993** — Death of Houphouët-Boigny; political and economic crisis begins.
- **1999** — Military coup d'état led by General Robert Guéï.
- **2002–07** — First Ivorian Civil War.
- **2010–11** — Second Ivorian Civil War following disputed presidential election.
- **2011** — Alassane Ouattara becomes president; peace and economic recovery begin.
- **2016** — New constitution adopted.
- **2020** — Ouattara wins a controversial third term in boycotted elections.
- **2025** — France officially hands over its last military base to Ivorian control.

## Body

### Etymology and Colonial Establishment
Portuguese merchants named the coast "Costa do Marfim" (Coast of Ivory) for its primary trade. The French later used "Côte d'Ivoire." The modern state's coastline does not exactly match the historical "Ivory Coast," which spanned present-day Ghana and Ivory Coast. The post-independence government in 1986 declared "Côte d'Ivoire" the only official name for diplomatic purposes, refusing translations, though "Ivory Coast" remains common in English media.

French colonization began with protectorates in 1843–44 and was consolidated as a colony in 1893. The French administration used a policy of "association," governing Africans as subjects without political rights, while a small elite could access French citizenship through "assimilation." Forced labor was used for plantations and public works until abolished in 1946.

### Independence and the Houphouët-Boigny Era
Félix Houphouët-Boigny, a Baoulé chief's son and cocoa farmer, led the independence movement. He maintained close ties with France, which benefited the economy. After independence in 1960, his government stimulated agricultural production, leading to the "Ivorian Miracle"—high economic growth fueled by coffee and cocoa exports. The French community in the country doubled by 1980. However, an economic crisis in the 1980s, caused by global recession, drought, and debt, led to social unrest and pressure for multi-party democracy. Houphouët-Boigny ruled until his death in 1993.

### Political Instability and Civil Wars
Houphouët-Boigny's successor, Henri Konan Bédié, promoted the divisive concept of "Ivoirité" to exclude political rivals, straining ethnic relations. A 1999 coup brought General Robert Guéï to power. The 2000 election, won by Laurent Gbagbo after a public uprising, was followed by a 2002 mutiny that split the country, with rebels controlling the north. A fragile peace and unity government collapsed, leading to a 2004 crisis when Gbagbo ordered airstrikes that killed French soldiers, prompting French military intervention.

The 2010 presidential election, which Ouattara won, was disputed by Gbagbo, sparking the Second Civil War. International forces intervened, and Gbagbo was captured in 2011. He was later tried at the International Criminal Court and acquitted in 2019. Ouattara has been president since 2011, overseeing economic recovery but facing controversy over his 2020 third-term election.

### Economy and Demographics
Ivory Coast is the world's largest cocoa exporter and a key economy in West Africa. After post-war recovery, it achieved high growth rates (averaging 7.1% from 2012–2023). However, 46.1% of the population faced multidimensional poverty as of 2016. The economy relies heavily on agriculture, with smallholder farming predominating.

The population is ethnically diverse, with major groups including the Akan, Voltaic, and Mandé peoples. About 22% of residents are foreign workers, primarily from neighboring countries. French is the official language, with Dyula widely used as a trade language. Islam (42.5%) and Christianity (39.8%) are the main religions, with a decline in traditional animist faiths.

## Terms
- **Ivoirité**: A political concept emphasizing Ivorian nationality, used to exclude rivals of foreign descent from political office.
- **Assimilation**: A colonial policy aiming to extend French language, laws, and customs to colonized peoples, granting them French citizenship.
- **Association**: A colonial policy allowing Africans to retain customs compatible with French interests, but governing them as subjects without political rights.
- **Indigénat**: A separate legal system applied to colonized subjects, distinct from French law.
- **Maquis**: A traditional Ivorian open-air restaurant, often serving grilled chicken and fish with attiéké or kedjenou.
- **Attiéké**: A popular side dish made from grated cassava, similar to couscous.
- **Coupé-Décalé**: A popular music genre from Ivory Coast.
- **WASH**: A concept focusing on safe drinking water, hygiene, and sanitation, promoted by international organizations in the region.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The role and impact of French military intervention during the civil wars are disputed, with each side accusing France of supporting the opposition.
- The legitimacy of Alassane Ouattara's 2020 third-term election is contested by opponents who argue it was unconstitutional.
- The effectiveness of the "Ivoirité" policy in addressing national identity versus its role in exacerbating ethnic tensions remains a subject of analysis.

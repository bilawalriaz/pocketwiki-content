# Ottoman wars in Europe

## Overview
The Ottoman–European wars were a series of military conflicts spanning from the late 13th century to the early 20th century, beginning with the Ottoman expansion into the Balkans and culminating in the empire's dissolution after World War I. These wars reshaped the political map of Europe and the Mediterranean, marking the rise, peak, and eventual collapse of Ottoman power in the region.

## Timeline
- **1354** — Ottoman capture of Gallipoli provides a base for European expansion.
- **1389** — Battle of Kosovo leads to Serbian subjugation.
- **1453** — Fall of Constantinople ends the Byzantine Empire.
- **1456** — Siege of Belgrade halts Ottoman advance into Central Europe for 70 years.
- **1526** — Battle of Mohács leads to the conquest of central Hungary.
- **1565** — Great Siege of Malta is an Ottoman failure.
- **1571** — Holy League defeats the Ottoman fleet at the Battle of Lepanto.
- **1683** — Ottoman siege of Vienna is broken, beginning a major reversal.
- **1699** — Treaty of Karlowitz ends the Great Turkish War with major Ottoman losses.
- **1878** — Treaty of Berlin grants independence or autonomy to Balkan Christian nations.
- **1912–13** — Balkan Wars reduce Ottoman European territory to Eastern Thrace.
- **1923** — Treaty of Lausanne concludes the final conflicts and establishes modern Turkey.

## Body

### Ottoman Rise and Expansion (14th–15th Centuries)
The Ottoman Empire entered Europe during Byzantine civil wars, seizing Gallipoli in 1354 as a strategic foothold. Exploiting further Byzantine and Bulgarian weakness, they conquered Adrianople (1369), Sofia (1382), and Tarnovgrad (1393). The Serbian Empire was worn down after the Battle of Kosovo (1389), and the fall of Constantinople in 1453 ended the Byzantine Empire. Ottoman expansion was temporarily checked by Timur's victory at the Battle of Ankara (1402) and by Hungarian resistance, notably at the Siege of Belgrade (1456).

### Mediterranean and Venetian Wars (15th–17th Centuries)
A long series of wars with the Republic of Venice (1423–1718) saw Ottoman conquests of Negroponte (1470), Cyprus (1571), and Crete (1669). The Holy League's victory at Lepanto (1571) was a major naval defeat for the Ottomans but did not reverse the loss of Cyprus. The Ottomans also failed to capture Malta in 1565, a defeat that halted their advance toward Italy.

### Central European Front and the Great Turkish War (16th–17th Centuries)
After conquering Hungary following the Battle of Mohács (1526), the Ottomans besieged Vienna in 1529 and 1547. Their advance was finally reversed by the Holy League after the failed 1683 siege of Vienna. The ensuing Great Turkish War (1683–99) ended with the Treaty of Karlowitz, which transferred Hungary and Transylvania to Habsburg control.

### Decline and Nationalist Uprisings (19th Century)
The 19th century saw successful nationalist revolts by Serbia (1804–17), Greece (1821–32), and Romania (1877–78), often supported by Russia. The Crimean War (1853–56) temporarily preserved Ottoman integrity with British and French aid. However, the Russo-Turkish War of 1877–78 led to the Treaty of Berlin, which recognized the independence of several Balkan states and marked the empire's definitive retreat from most of Europe.

### Dissolution (Early 20th Century)
The Italo-Turkish War (1911) resulted in the loss of Libya. The Balkan Wars (1912–13) further shrank Ottoman territory in Europe. The empire's entry into World War I on the side of the Central Powers led to its defeat and partition. The final conflicts (1919–23) resulted in the establishment of the Republic of Turkey from the empire's Anatolian heartland.

## Terms
- **Rumelia**: The Ottoman name for their European territories, primarily in the Balkans.
- **Janissaries**: Elite infantry units of the Ottoman army, often composed of conscripted Christian boys.
- **Holy League**: A coalition of Christian states formed to oppose the Ottoman Empire, notably in 1571 and 1684.
- **Végvárs**: Hungarian border fortresses built as a defensive line against Ottoman and Mongol invasions.
- **Capitulations**: Agreements granting extraterritorial rights and privileges to foreign powers within the Ottoman Empire.
- **Beylerbeyli**: A major Ottoman administrative province, such as the Beylerbeyli of Rumelia.
- **Sanjak beys**: Ottoman provincial governors.
- **Akinjis**: Light Ottoman cavalry used for raiding.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The exact date of the fall of Gallipoli is disputed due to changes in the Byzantine calendar (1354 or 1356).
- The impact of the Battle of Lepanto is debated: while a major tactical victory for the Holy League, it did not prevent the Ottoman conquest of Cyprus or significantly alter the long-term balance of naval power.
- The role of internal Ottoman weaknesses versus external military pressure in causing the empire's 19th-century decline is a subject of ongoing historical analysis.

# Shijiazhuang

## Overview
Shijiazhuang is the capital and most populous city of China's Hebei Province, a major industrial and transportation hub whose dramatic growth from a small village into a metropolis of over 11 million people exemplifies China's rapid urbanization and industrialization since 1949. Its development was catalyzed by its strategic position at a railway junction and its role as a political center during the Chinese Civil War.

- **Nov 12, 1947** — City captured by Communist forces.

- **Jan 2021** — Major COVID-19 outbreak leads to city-wide lockdown and testing.

## Body
### Name and Origins
The name "Shijiazhuang" (Shi family's village) first appeared in 1535. Its popular use began after the 1907 railway station construction. The origin is disputed; one theory links it to homophones for "ten families" and "stone," while another suggests it was named after an official surnamed Shi. The city was briefly renamed Shimen in 1925 but reverted to Shijiazhuang in 1947 by the CCP.

### Historical Development
The area has ancient roots, but Shijiazhuang's modern growth began with the arrival of railways in 1905 and 1907, which turned it into a national transportation and commercial center. After its capture by Communist forces in 1947, it became a key political and industrial base. Its population tripled between 1948 and 1958 due to state-led industrialization, particularly in textiles and chemicals. It became the provincial capital in 1968. Rapid growth resumed in the 1990s.

### Geography and Climate
Located on the North China Plain east of the Taihang Mountains, the city has a continental, monsoon-influenced semi-arid climate with hot, humid summers and cold, dry winters. It has been frequently listed among the world's most air-polluted cities, with 2020 PM2.5 levels over 11 times the WHO guideline.

### Economy and Industry
Shijiazhuang is Hebei's economic center, with a 2014 GDP of CN¥510.02 billion. It is a major industrial base nicknamed the "medicine hub of China," home to large pharmaceutical companies. The textile industry is also a backbone. The city hosts the Sanlu Group, a dairy giant central to the 2008 Chinese milk scandal. It is part of the Beijing-Tianjin-Shijiazhuang Hi-Tech Industrial Belt.

### Transportation
The city is a major hub where the Beijing–Guangzhou and other railways intersect. The new Shijiazhuang railway station (2012) uniquely serves both conventional and high-speed lines via a 5 km tunnel under the city. It has an international airport, a growing metro system, and is known for its cyclist-friendly roads.

## Debates and Open Questions
The origin of the city's name is "heavily disputed," with competing theories involving homophones, family names, and older historical references. The accuracy of a 2011 survey naming Shijiazhuang residents the "happiest" in China caused "strong doubts from netizens."

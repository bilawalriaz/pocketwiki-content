# Cameroon

## Overview
Cameroon is a Central African nation often called "Africa in miniature" due to its remarkable geographic, linguistic, and cultural diversity. Its strategic location at the crossroads of West and Central Africa has made it a historical meeting point of diverse peoples and colonial powers. The country is currently governed as a unitary presidential republic under long-standing authoritarian rule, facing significant internal conflicts, particularly the Anglophone Crisis.

## Timeline
- **c. 1472** — Portuguese explorers reach the coast, naming the Wouri River "Rio dos Camarões" (River of Shrimps).
- **1884** — Germany establishes the colony of Kamerun.
- **1919** — After WWI, Kamerun is divided into French and British League of Nations mandates.
- **1955** — France outlaws the pro-independence Union of the Peoples of Cameroon (UPC), sparking a guerrilla war.
- **1960** — French Cameroun gains independence as the Republic of Cameroon under President Ahmadou Ahidjo.
- **1961** — The southern part of British Cameroons federates with Cameroon to form the Federal Republic of Cameroon.
- **1972** — The federation is abolished; the country is renamed the United Republic of Cameroon.
- **1982** — Paul Biya succeeds Ahidjo as president, beginning his ongoing rule.
- **1984** — President Biya restores the country's name to the Republic of Cameroon.
- **2006** — A UN-mediated agreement resolves a territorial dispute with Nigeria over the oil-rich Bakassi peninsula, granting it to Cameroon.
- **2016–present** — Protests in English-speaking regions escalate into the Anglophone Crisis, a separatist insurgency.

## Body

### Early History and Colonialism
The territory shows evidence of human occupation dating back 30,000 years. Early societies included the Sao culture near Lake Chad and various Bantu-speaking groups. Portuguese traders arrived in 1472, followed by other European powers who established trade and missionary work. Germany formally colonized the area as Kamerun in 1884, using forced labor for plantations. After Germany's defeat in World War I, the territory was split between France and Britain as League of Nations mandates.

### Path to Independence and Nation-Building
French-administered Cameroon gained independence in 1960. The southern British territory voted to join it in 1961, forming a federation. President Ahidjo centralized power, using the conflict with the outlawed UPC to justify authoritarian rule. In 1972, the federal system was abolished in favor of a unitary state. Paul Biya became president in 1982 and has ruled since, maintaining control through his Cameroon People's Democratic Movement (CPDM).

### Politics and Governance
Cameroon is a unitary presidential republic where the president holds extensive power. The political system is dominated by the CPDM, with the Social Democratic Front (SDF) as the main opposition. The government is widely criticized for corruption, human rights abuses, and suppressing dissent. The Anglophone Crisis, which began as protests in 2016 over the marginalization of English-speaking regions, escalated into a violent separatist conflict in 2017, leading to significant displacement and humanitarian needs.

### Economy and Society
The economy relies on agriculture (employing about 70% of the population), petroleum, and timber. While GDP has grown, poverty and unemployment remain significant challenges. Cameroon is highly diverse, with over 250 languages spoken. French and English are official languages, but their use is a source of tension, contributing to the Anglophone Crisis. Christianity is the majority religion, with Islam prominent in the north. The country faces issues including high fertility rates, child labor, and strained healthcare and education systems.

### Geography and Environment
Cameroon's geography is exceptionally varied, ranging from coastal plains and equatorial rainforests in the south to mountains, savannas, and the arid Sahel in the north. Its highest point is Mount Cameroon (4,095 m). The country is a biodiversity hotspot but faces environmental threats from deforestation and the bushmeat trade. It hosts numerous protected areas, including the Dja Faunal Reserve, a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

## Terms
- **Fondom/Chiefdom:** Traditional systems of local governance led by a fon or chief, recognized by the state.
- **UPC (Union of the Peoples of Cameroon):** A pro-independence political party outlawed by France in 1955, which led a guerrilla war.
- **Anglophone Crisis:** An ongoing conflict stemming from protests in 2016 by English-speaking regions against perceived marginalization, which escalated into a separatist insurgency.
- **Ambazonia:** The name of the self-declared state sought by separatists in the English-speaking Northwest and Southwest regions.
- **CPDM (Cameroon People's Democratic Movement):** The ruling political party of President Paul Biya.
- **CFA franc:** The currency used in Cameroon and several other Central and West African countries.
- **Bakassi Peninsula:** A fertile, oil-rich peninsula on the Gulf of Guinea, the subject of a long territorial dispute with Nigeria, resolved in Cameroon's favor in 2006.
- **Makossa/Bikutsi:** Popular genres of Cameroonian music.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The root causes and potential resolution of the **Anglophone Crisis** are subjects of intense debate, involving questions of federalism, secession, and national unity.
- The **legitimacy of elections** under President Biya's long rule is contested by opposition groups and human rights organizations.
- The **effectiveness of anti-corruption measures** is questioned, as Cameroon consistently ranks poorly on transparency indices despite government initiatives.
- The **impact of climate change and environmental degradation** on Cameroon's diverse ecosystems and agricultural livelihoods is a growing concern.

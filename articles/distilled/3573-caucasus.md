# Caucasus

## Overview
The Caucasus is a geographical and cultural region spanning Eastern Europe and Western Asia, situated between the Black and Caspian Seas. It is defined by the Greater and Lesser Caucasus mountain ranges, which have historically acted as a barrier and a crossroads between empires. The region is renowned for its exceptional linguistic diversity, complex political history, and role as a biodiversity hotspot.

## Timeline
- **c. 1.8 million years ago** — Earliest human fossils outside Africa found at Dmanisi, Georgia.
- **c. 4000–2000 BC** — Kura–Araxes culture flourishes across the Southern Caucasus.
- **301 AD** — Armenia becomes the first nation to adopt Christianity as a state religion.
- **12th–13th centuries** — The Kingdom of Georgia reaches its peak under David the Builder and Queen Tamar.
- **1783** — Georgia signs the Treaty of Georgievsk with the Russian Empire.
- **19th century** — Russian Empire conquers the North Caucasus, leading to the Circassian ethnic cleansing.
- **1918** — The short-lived Transcaucasian Democratic Federative Republic is formed.
- **1922–1936** — The Transcaucasian SFSR exists as a unified Soviet entity.
- **1991** — Georgia, Armenia, and Azerbaijan gain independence after the Soviet collapse.
- **1988–1994** — First Nagorno-Karabakh War.
- **2020** — Second Nagorno-Karabakh War.

## Body

### Geography and Demographics
The region is bisected by the Greater Caucasus range, with the Lesser Caucasus to the south. Mount Elbrus (5,642 m), in the Western Caucasus, is considered Europe's highest peak. The watershed along the Greater Caucasus is a conventional boundary between Europe and Asia, though other definitions exist. Politically, the North Caucasus (Ciscaucasus) is part of Russia, while the South Caucasus (Transcaucasia) comprises Georgia, Armenia, and Azerbaijan. The area is home to over 50 ethnic groups and three indigenous language families (Kartvelian, Northwest Caucasian, Northeast Caucasian), alongside Indo-European and Turkic languages. Religious demographics include Orthodox Christianity, Armenian Apostolic Christianity, and Sunni and Shia Islam.

### History
The Caucasus has been inhabited since the Paleolithic Era, with the Dmanisi site providing the earliest evidence of hominins outside Africa. Ancient kingdoms like Armenia, Colchis, and Iberia were incorporated into Persian and later Roman/Byzantine empires. The region saw a major religious shift with the adoption of Christianity in the 4th century, followed by the Muslim conquests. The medieval period saw the rise of powerful Christian states like Georgia and the spread of Islam. From the 18th century, the Russian Empire gradually expanded into the region, leading to the Caucasian Wars and the displacement of indigenous populations like the Circassians. The 20th century brought Soviet rule, mass deportations of ethnic groups, and, after 1991, independence for the South Caucasus states, which has been followed by numerous territorial conflicts.

### Mythology and Culture
The Caucasus features prominently in Greek mythology as the site of Prometheus's punishment. This theme of a chained culture hero is echoed in local folklore, such as the Georgian Amirani and Circassian Nasran. The region's rich oral tradition includes the Nart sagas, epic stories of a heroic race. These myths show connections to ancient Iranian, Indian, and Norse cultures. The folklore preserves ancient motifs, including giant-slaying tales similar to the Greek story of Polyphemus.

## Terms
*   **Ciscaucasus:** The North Caucasus region, lying between the Greater Caucasus range and the Kuma-Manych Depression.
*   **Transcaucasia:** The South Caucasus region, located south of the Greater Caucasus range; a term considered "Russo-centric" by some.
*   **Kartvelian:** A language family indigenous to the South Caucasus, primarily spoken in Georgia.
*   **Nart sagas:** A body of oral epic tales from the Caucasus about a race of heroes called the Narts.
*   **Endemic:** A species native and restricted to a certain place; the Caucasus has high levels of plant and animal endemism.
*   **Relict:** A species or ecosystem that has survived from an earlier period; the Caucasus forests are a major Ice Age refugium.
*   **Lingua franca:** A language used for communication between groups of people who speak different native languages; Russian serves this role in the North Caucasus.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Europe-Asia Boundary:** There is no single agreed-upon boundary. The Greater Caucasus watershed, the Kuma-Manych Depression, and the Kura and Rioni rivers are all proposed as the dividing line.
*   **Etymology of "Caucasus":** The name's origin is debated. Theories connect it to Scythian words for "snow," Ancient Greek for "seagull mountain," or words for "high" in other Indo-European languages.
*   **Political Status:** The post-Soviet territorial disputes, particularly over Abkhazia, South Ossetia, and Nagorno-Karabakh, remain unresolved and are sources of ongoing conflict.

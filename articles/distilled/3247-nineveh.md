# Nineveh

## Overview
Nineveh was an ancient city in Upper Mesopotamia, located on the eastern bank of the Tigris River in modern-day Mosul, Iraq. It served as the capital and largest city of the Neo-Assyrian Empire, potentially becoming the wealthiest city in the ancient world. Its rise, dramatic fall in 612 BC, and subsequent archaeological rediscovery make it a key site for understanding Near Eastern history and biblical narratives.

## Timeline
- **c. 6000 BC** — Founded as a Neolithic farming village.
- **c. 5000 BC** — Transitions from a Halaf to an Ubaid period village.
- **c. 3000–2500 BC** — The "Ninevite 5" period, defined by characteristic pottery, sees regional influence.
- **c. 1809–1775 BC** — Becomes part of the Kingdom of Upper Mesopotamia under Shamshi-Adad I.
- **1365 BC** — Reclaimed by Assyrian king Ashur-uballit I, beginning the Middle Assyrian Empire.
- **c. 700 BC** — Sennacherib makes Nineveh the magnificent capital of the Neo-Assyrian Empire.
- **612 BC** — Sacked and razed by a coalition of Babylonians, Medes, and Scythians after a civil war.
- **554 AD** — Becomes a bishopric of the Church of the East.
- **641 AD** — Conquered by Arabs; eclipsed by the new administrative center of Mosul.
- **1842–1849** — Major archaeological excavations begin, rediscovering Assyrian palaces and libraries.
- **2014–2017** — Significant destruction of gates and reliefs by the Islamic State (ISIL/Daesh).

## Body

### Early History and Rise
Nineveh was founded as early as 6000 BC. It developed from a farming village into a proto-city during the Ubaid period (c. 5000 BC) and later became a trade colony of Uruk due to its strategic location. The "Ninevite 5" period (c. 3000–2500 BC) marks a time of regional influence defined by its pottery. After periods under Akkadian, Mitanni, and Middle Assyrian control, the city's true prominence came during the Neo-Assyrian Empire.

### Neo-Assyrian Capital
From the reign of Ashurnasirpal II (883–859 BC) onward, Nineveh saw major expansion. King Sennacherib (c. 700 BC) transformed it into a world capital. He built the "palace without a rival," an elaborate water system with canals and aqueducts, and fortified the city with a 12-km wall and fifteen gates. At its height, Nineveh covered about 7 square kilometers and had over 100,000 inhabitants, possibly 150,000, making it one of the largest settlements worldwide. Its palaces were adorned with thousands of meters of stone reliefs depicting military campaigns and royal life.

### Fall and Later History
After the death of Ashurbanipal (c. 627 BC), civil war weakened the Assyrian Empire. In 612 BC, a coalition of former subjects besieged and sacked Nineveh, ending its role as a political center. Though devastated, it was not completely abandoned. It saw revival under Persian and Seleucid rule, and by the 6th century AD, it was a Christian bishopric. After the Arab conquest in 641 AD, it was gradually eclipsed by Mosul and was mostly ruins by the 13th century.

### Archaeology and Modern Threats
The site was rediscovered in the 19th century by archaeologists like Paul-Émile Botta and Austen Henry Layard, who unearthed palaces and Ashurbanipal's library of cuneiform tablets. Modern excavations continue, revealing new structures. The site faces severe threats, including urban encroachment, looting, and deliberate destruction by ISIL, which demolished several gates and damaged reliefs between 2014 and 2017. The nearby Mosul Dam also poses a flood risk.

## Terms
*   **Neo-Assyrian Empire:** The final and most powerful phase of the Assyrian Empire (c. 911–609 BC), with Nineveh as its capital.
*   **Tell:** An archaeological mound formed by the accumulated remains of ancient settlements.
*   **Lamassu:** A celestial guardian figure from Mesopotamian mythology, often depicted as a winged bull or lion with a human head.
*   **Cuneiform:** An ancient system of writing using wedge-shaped marks on clay tablets.
*   **Bas-relief:** A type of sculpture where the figures project slightly from the background.
*   **Ubaid period:** A prehistoric period in Mesopotamia (c. 6500–3800 BC) preceding the Uruk period.
*   **Ninevite 5:** An archaeological period (c. 3000–2500 BC) in Upper Mesopotamia, named for pottery found at Nineveh.
*   **Khosr River:** A tributary of the Tigris that bisects the site of ancient Nineveh.

## Debates and open questions
*   **Hanging Gardens:** Some scholars, like Stephanie Dalley, argue that the gardens built by Sennacherib at Nineveh, not Babylon, were the original "Hanging Gardens of Babylon."
*   **Biblical Founder:** Interpretations of Genesis 10:11 differ on whether the founder of Nineveh was the person Ashur or the country Assyria (via Nimrod).
*   **Post-612 BC Status:** It is unclear whether Nineveh came under the rule of the Medes or the Neo-Babylonian Empire immediately after its sack in 612 BC.

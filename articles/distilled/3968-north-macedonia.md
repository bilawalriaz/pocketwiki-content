# North Macedonia

## Overview
North Macedonia is a landlocked, multi-ethnic country in Southeast Europe, historically contested by empires and neighboring states. Its modern identity and borders were shaped by the dissolution of Yugoslavia and a long-standing naming dispute with Greece, which was resolved in 2018. The country is a parliamentary democracy and a member of NATO, but its path to European Union membership remains complicated by bilateral issues with Bulgaria.

## Timeline
- **c. 356 BC** — Philip II of Macedon incorporates parts of the region into the Kingdom of Macedon.
- **146 BC** — Romans establish the province of Macedonia.
- **6th-9th c. CE** — Slavic tribes settle in the region; Christianization occurs under the First Bulgarian Empire.
- **1014** — Byzantine Emperor Basil II defeats the Bulgarian Empire, reasserting control.
- **14th c.** — Region becomes part of the Serbian Empire, then is conquered by the Ottoman Empire.
- **1903** — Ilinden–Preobrazhenie Uprising against Ottoman rule.
- **1913** — Following the Balkan Wars, most of the territory is annexed by Serbia.
- **1945** — People's Republic of Macedonia established as a constituent state of communist Yugoslavia.
- **1991** — Country declares independence from Yugoslavia.
- **1993** — Admitted to the UN under the provisional reference "the former Yugoslav Republic of Macedonia."
- **2001** — Ohrid Agreement ends an ethnic Albanian insurgency and establishes power-sharing.
- **2019** — Country officially changes its name to North Macedonia under the Prespa agreement.
- **2020** — North Macedonia becomes the 30th member state of NATO.

## Body

### History and Identity
The territory of North Macedonia has been home to various peoples, including the ancient Paeonians, and was incorporated into the Macedonian, Roman, Byzantine, Bulgarian, Serbian, and Ottoman empires. Slavic settlement from the 6th century CE and the subsequent development of a distinct regional culture under the Bulgarian and Serbian empires are key historical layers. The modern Macedonian national identity crystallized in the 20th century, particularly during the Yugoslav period when the Socialist Republic of Macedonia was established. The country's name and national symbols have been sources of contention, most notably with Greece, which objected to the use of "Macedonia" and associated symbols like the Vergina Sun.

### The Naming Dispute and International Recognition
Following independence in 1991, Greece objected to the country's use of the name "Macedonia," fearing territorial claims on its own northern region. This dispute blocked the country's integration into international organizations. The UN admitted it in 1993 under the provisional reference "the former Yugoslav Republic of Macedonia." The long-running dispute was resolved by the 2018 Prespa agreement, in which the country changed its official name to North Macedonia. In return, Greece lifted its veto, allowing North Macedonia to join NATO in 2020 and begin EU accession talks.

### Politics and Society
North Macedonia is a parliamentary democracy with a multi-ethnic population. The 2001 Ohrid Framework Agreement ended a brief conflict between government forces and ethnic Albanian insurgents, granting greater autonomy to municipalities with significant Albanian populations and making Albanian a co-official language. The political landscape is often defined by relations between the ethnic Macedonian majority and the Albanian minority. The country faces challenges including corruption, high unemployment, and a significant informal economy.

### Economy and Development
Since independence, North Macedonia has transitioned to an open market economy, implementing reforms like a flat tax rate to attract foreign investment, particularly in the automotive and electronics sectors. The economy is heavily reliant on trade, with the EU as its largest partner. Despite progress, it remains one of Europe's lower-income countries, with a GDP per capita significantly below the EU average. Tourism, centered on Lake Ohrid and the capital Skopje, is a growing sector.

## Terms
- **Prespa Agreement:** A 2018 treaty between Greece and North Macedonia that resolved their naming dispute, changing the country's name and addressing issues of cultural appropriation.
- **Ohrid Framework Agreement:** A 2001 peace accord that ended an ethnic Albanian insurgency in North Macedonia and established a system of power-sharing and greater local autonomy for ethnic minorities.
- **Ilinden–Preobrazhenie Uprising:** A 1903 revolt against Ottoman rule in Macedonia, a foundational event in Macedonian national history.
- **Antiquisation:** A policy pursued by the VMRO-DPMNE government (2006-2016) involving the erection of statues and renaming of infrastructure after ancient Macedonian figures, which exacerbated tensions with Greece.
- **Vardar Banovina:** The name of the Yugoslav province that encompassed most of present-day North Macedonia from 1929 to 1941.
- **Autocephaly:** The status of a Christian church that is self-governing and independent of other churches. The Macedonian Orthodox Church declared autocephaly in 1967 but was not recognized by other Orthodox churches until 2022.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Historical Narrative:** The interpretation of figures and events from the medieval Bulgarian and Serbian empires remains a point of contention, particularly in relations with Bulgaria, which has blocked EU accession talks over issues of historical recognition and minority rights.
- **Ethnic Relations:** While the Ohrid Agreement established a framework for peace, the balance of power and representation between ethnic Macedonians and Albanians remains a central and sometimes tense aspect of domestic politics.
- **EU Accession:** The path to EU membership is uncertain. While accession talks have begun, progress is contingent on resolving bilateral disputes with Bulgaria and implementing further constitutional changes to recognize a Bulgarian minority.

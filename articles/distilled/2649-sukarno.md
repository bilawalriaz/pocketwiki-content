# Sukarno

## Overview
Sukarno (1901–1970) was the founding father and first president of Indonesia, leading the nation from its declaration of independence in 1945 until his removal from power in 1967. He was the central figure in the Indonesian struggle for independence from Dutch colonial rule and later shaped the country's early political and ideological identity. His legacy is complex, marked by his role in unifying a diverse archipelago, his authoritarian "Guided Democracy," and his dramatic fall following a violent anti-communist purge.

## Timeline
- **17 August 1945** — Proclaims Indonesian independence with Mohammad Hatta.
- **27 December 1949** — Dutch sovereignty is transferred to Indonesia after a revolutionary war.
- **1955** — Organizes the Bandung Conference, a precursor to the Non-Aligned Movement.
- **5 July 1959** — Reinstates the 1945 constitution by decree, establishing "Guided Democracy."
- **1961** — Co-founds the Non-Aligned Movement (NAM) in Belgrade.
- **1 October 1965** — The "30 September Movement" murders senior army generals, triggering a crisis.
- **11 March 1966** — Signs the Supersemar order, transferring key authority to General Suharto.
- **12 March 1967** — Stripped of his presidency by the Provisional People's Consultative Assembly (MPRS).
- **21 June 1970** — Dies under house arrest in Jakarta.

## Body

### Early Life and Nationalist Struggle
Born in Surabaya to a Javanese teacher and a Balinese mother, Sukarno was educated in Dutch colonial schools. He studied civil engineering at the Bandung Institute of Technology, where he developed his political ideology, Marhaenism, which blended nationalism, Islam, and Marxism. In 1927, he co-founded the Indonesian National Party (PNI) to advocate for independence. His activism led to his arrest in 1929, a famous trial where he delivered the "Indonesia Accuses" speech, and subsequent imprisonment and exile by Dutch authorities until the Japanese invasion in 1942.

### Japanese Occupation and Independence
During World War II, Sukarno collaborated with the occupying Japanese forces, believing it a necessary step toward independence. He helped mobilize the population for the Japanese war effort, including the recruitment of forced laborers (*romusha*). In return, the Japanese allowed the formation of preparatory committees for independence. On 1 June 1945, Sukarno introduced the state philosophy of Pancasila. Following Japan's surrender, he was pressured by youth groups to declare independence, which he did on 17 August 1945.

### The Indonesian National Revolution
The proclamation was followed by a four-year armed and diplomatic struggle against the returning Dutch. Sukarno's government fought a guerrilla war while engaging in international diplomacy. Key events included the British-mediated Linggadjati Agreement (1946), the Dutch military "Police Actions" (1947 and 1948), and the Madiun Affair, a communist rebellion Sukarno helped suppress. The Dutch captured Sukarno in 1948, but international pressure, including a U.S. threat to cut Marshall Plan aid, forced them to negotiate. Sovereignty was finally transferred on 27 December 1949.

### Parliamentary Democracy and Guided Democracy
The 1950s were marked by political instability under a parliamentary system, with frequent changes of government and regional rebellions. Sukarno grew disillusioned with this system. In 1959, he dissolved parliament and reinstated the 1945 constitution by decree, establishing his authoritarian "Guided Democracy." He promoted the state ideology of NASAKOM (Nationalism, Religion, Communism) to balance the military, Islamic groups, and the growing Indonesian Communist Party (PKI). His rule was characterized by a cult of personality and ambitious infrastructure projects.

### Foreign Policy and Downfall
Sukarno pursued an assertive, anti-imperialist foreign policy. He was a founding leader of the Non-Aligned Movement but increasingly aligned Indonesia with the communist bloc, receiving substantial Soviet military aid. He confronted the Netherlands over West Papua (Irian Jaya) and later launched the *Konfrontasi* against the formation of Malaysia. Domestically, the economy deteriorated, with hyperinflation exceeding 600% by 1965. The fragile balance of Guided Democracy collapsed after the mysterious "30 September Movement" coup attempt in 1965, which led to the murder of six generals. A subsequent military-led purge killed at least 500,000 people, primarily PKI members. General Suharto gradually assumed power, and Sukarno was formally stripped of his presidency in 1967, dying under house arrest in 1970.

## Terms
*   **Pancasila:** The foundational philosophical theory of the Indonesian state, formulated by Sukarno in 1945. Its five principles are belief in God, humanitarianism, national unity, democracy, and social justice.
*   **Marhaenism:** Sukarno's political ideology, named after an Indonesian peasant, blending elements of nationalism, Islam, and Marxism to advocate for social justice for all Indonesians.
*   **Guided Democracy:** Sukarno's authoritarian political system introduced in 1959, which replaced parliamentary democracy with a presidential system based on consensus under his guidance.
*   **NASAKOM:** An acronym for *Nasionalisme, Agama, Komunisme* (Nationalism, Religion, Communism), the state ideology under Guided Democracy meant to unite Indonesia's three main political pillars.
*   **30 September Movement (G30S):** A failed coup attempt on 1 October 1965, blamed on the PKI, which served as the catalyst for the anti-communist purge and Suharto's rise to power.
*   **Supersemar:** The "Order of 11 March," a document signed by Sukarno in 1966 that transferred significant executive authority to General Suharto.
*   **Konfrontasi:** The Indonesia–Malaysia confrontation (1963–1966), a low-level conflict waged by Sukarno's Indonesia against the newly formed Federation of Malaysia.
*   **Non-Aligned Movement (NAM):** An international organization of states not formally aligned with any major power bloc during the Cold War, co-founded by Sukarno in 1961.

## Debates and open questions
*   **The 30 September Movement:** The full extent of the PKI's involvement and whether Sukarno had any prior knowledge remain subjects of historical debate.
*   **Supersemar's Signing:** Whether Sukarno signed the Supersemar order voluntarily or was coerced, possibly at gunpoint, is disputed.
*   **Sukarno's Legacy:** His reputation has fluctuated; minimized under Suharto's New Order, he has been politically rehabilitated and is widely revered as the founding father of modern Indonesia.

# Harbin

## Overview
Harbin is the capital and largest city of Heilongjiang province in Northeast China. A major political, economic, and cultural hub, it was shaped by its Russian-built railway center, Japanese occupation, and Soviet influence. The city is globally renowned for its winter tourism, particularly the International Ice and Snow Sculpture Festival, and its distinctive European-influenced architecture.

## Timeline
- **c. 2200 BC** — Earliest human settlement in the Harbin area.
- **1115** — Wanyan Aguda establishes the Jin dynasty capital, Shangjing Huiningfu, in present-day Acheng District.
- **1898** — The modern city is founded with the construction of the Russian-built Chinese Eastern Railway.
- **1910–1911** — A major pneumonic plague outbreak kills 1,500 in Harbin, part of a larger pandemic.
- **1920** — Over 100,000 Russian White Guards and refugees flee to Harbin, making it a major center of the Russian diaspora.
- **1932** — Japan captures Harbin, which becomes the largest city in the puppet state of Manchukuo.
- **1935** — The Soviet Union sells the Chinese Eastern Railway to Japan, prompting many Russian émigrés to leave.
- **1945** — The Soviet Army captures Harbin.
- **1946** — The Chinese People's Liberation Army takes control; Harbin becomes the first large city governed by the communists.
- **1985** — The first Harbin International Ice and Snow Sculpture Festival is held.
- **2010** — UNESCO designates Harbin a "City of Music."
- **2025** — Harbin hosts the Asian Winter Games.

## Body

### Early History and Russian Foundation
Human settlement dates to the late Stone Age. The region birthed the Jin dynasty (1115–1234), with its first capital in modern Acheng District. The area remained rural until 1898, when the Russian Empire selected it as the administrative base for the Chinese Eastern Railway, extending the Trans-Siberian Railway. This spurred rapid growth. Russian and European architects planned the city, giving it a distinct European appearance. By 1913, the Chinese Eastern Railway Zone population was 68,549, comprising 53 nationalities, with Russians as the largest group.

### International City and Russian Émigré Hub
After the 1917 Russian Revolution, Harbin became a major center for White Russian émigrés, peaking at around 120,000 in the early 1920s. The community established Russian-language schools, newspapers, and cultural institutions. The Harbin Institute of Technology was founded in 1920. The city was a cosmopolitan hub with consulates from 16 countries. A pneumonic plague outbreak in 1910–1911, investigated by Dr. Wu Lien-teh, was a significant public health event.

### Japanese Occupation and World War II
Japan occupied Harbin in 1932 after the Mukden Incident. The city became the largest in the puppet state of Manchukuo. During this period, the infamous Unit 731 conducted biological warfare experiments on prisoners in Pingfang District. The Russian Fascist Party, supported by the Japanese, played a role. Many Jews and Russians fled. The Soviet Army captured Harbin in August 1945, and it was transferred to the Chinese People's Liberation Army in 1946.

### Post-1949 Development
Harbin was a key construction city during China's First Five-Year Plan (1951–1956), with 13 major Soviet-aided projects establishing it as an industrial base. During the Cultural Revolution, many foreign architectural sites were damaged. Since the reform and opening up in 1979, the city has developed its economy and tourism. It hosted the 1996 Winter Asian Games and the 2009 Winter Universiade. The original Russian community dwindled to about 30 elderly individuals by 1988; modern Russian residents are recent arrivals.

### Economy and Industry
Harbin has the largest economy in Heilongjiang province. Its economy is based on a comprehensive industrial system including power equipment manufacturing (Harbin Electric), pharmaceuticals (Harbin Pharmaceutical Group), automobiles, and food processing. The fertile chernozem soil makes it a base for commodity grain production. The annual China Harbin International Economic and Trade Fair, renamed the "China-Russia EXPO" in 2013, is a major investment event.

### Culture and Tourism
Harbin's culture blends Han, Manchu, and Russian influences. It is famed for its European-style architecture, such as the Saint Sophia Cathedral and Central Street. The city is a UNESCO "City of Music" and hosts the Harbin Summer Music Concert. Cuisine features dishes like Guo Bao Rou and Russian-influenced items like smoked sausage and dalieba bread. The Harbin International Ice and Snow Sculpture Festival, held since 1985, is one of the world's four largest ice and snow festivals, featuring massive illuminated ice structures.

## Terms
*   **Chinese Eastern Railway:** A railway built by the Russian Empire in Manchuria, completed in 1903, which was the catalyst for Harbin's modern founding and growth.
*   **Manchukuo:** The puppet state established by the Empire of Japan in Manchuria from 1932 to 1945.
*   **Unit 731:** A covert biological and chemical warfare research and development unit of the Imperial Japanese Army that conducted lethal human experimentation in Harbin during World War II.
*   **White Guards:** The anti-Bolshevik military forces of the Russian Civil War; many fled to Harbin after their defeat.
*   **Russian Fascist Party:** A far-right political party of Russian émigrés in Harbin that collaborated with the Japanese authorities.
*   **Sino-Soviet split:** The worsening of relations between the People's Republic of China and the Soviet Union in the 1960s, which impacted Harbin's development and led to the closure of the Soviet consulate.
*   **First Five-Year Plan:** China's economic development plan from 1953 to 1957, during which Harbin received major Soviet-aided industrial projects.
*   **Sponge city:** An urban planning concept to manage stormwater, used in Harbin to mitigate climate change impacts, as seen in the Qunli stormwater park.

## Debates and Open questions
*   The etymology of "Harbin" is debated; proposed origins include the Jurchen word for "swan" or a Manchu term meaning "a place for drying fishing nets."
*   The exact death toll of the 1910–1911 pneumonic plague outbreak in Harbin is given as 1,500 residents, but the broader Manchurian plague pandemic claimed 60,000 victims.
*   The number of victims of Unit 731's experiments at the Pingfang camp is estimated between 3,000 and 12,000.

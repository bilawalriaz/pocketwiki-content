# Khosrow I

## Overview
Khosrow I (r. 531–579), also known as Anushirvan ("the Immortal Soul"), was the Sasanian King of Kings of Iran. He is considered the most distinguished Sasanian ruler, renowned for his administrative, military, and cultural reforms that centralized the state and fostered a renaissance of art and science. His reign marked the Sasanian Empire's greatest territorial extent and established a model of the "philosopher king" admired in both the East and West.

## Timeline
- **c. 512–514** — Born at Ardestan, youngest son of Kavad I.
- **c. 520** — Byzantine adoption negotiations for Khosrow fail.
- **531** — Succeeds his father Kavad I.
- **532** — Signs the "Perpetual Peace" with Byzantium; Justinian I pays 11,000 pounds of gold.
- **540** — Violates peace, invades Syria, sacks Antioch.
- **541** — Invades Lazica, initiating the Lazic War.
- **557** — Alliance with Göktürks helps destroy the Hephthalite Empire.
- **562** — Fifty-Year Peace Treaty signed with Byzantium.
- **572** — War with Byzantium resumes under Justin II.
- **573** — Captures the Byzantine fortress-city of Dara.
- **579** — Dies; succeeded by his son Hormizd IV.

## Body

### Accession and Consolidation
Khosrow inherited a reinvigorated empire but faced internal threats. To secure his position, he first signed the "Perpetual Peace" with Byzantium in 532, receiving 11,000 pounds of gold. He then ruthlessly consolidated power, executing his brother Kawus, who claimed the throne, and later his uncle Bawi and other aristocrats involved in a conspiracy. These actions eliminated rival power centers within the royal family and nobility.

### Wars with Byzantium
Khosrow's reign was marked by intermittent warfare with the Byzantine Empire. In 540, he broke the peace, invading Syria and sacking Antioch. This led to the prolonged Lazic War (541–562) over control of the Caucasus region. A truce in 545 was broken, and full war resumed until the Fifty-Year Peace Treaty of 562, which secured an annual Byzantine payment. War resumed in 572 under Justin II, leading to Khosrow's capture of Dara in 573. The conflict continued until after his death.

### Eastern Campaigns and Yemen
To the east, Khosrow allied with the Göktürks to destroy the Hephthalite Empire in 557, ending a long-standing threat. Relations with the Turks later soured over control of Silk Road trade. To the south, he intervened in Yemen, sending the general Wahrez to defeat the Aksumites (Ethiopians) and establish a Sasanian protectorate, partly to secure maritime trade routes.

### Administrative and Military Reforms
Khosrow's most enduring legacy was his internal reforms, which centralized the state. He implemented a new tax system based on land surveys and water rights, directing revenue to the central treasury instead of local nobles. He created a new social class, the *dehqans* (small landowners), who formed a loyal bureaucratic and military backbone. The army was reorganized into four regional commands under *spahbads* (generals), reducing reliance on feudal levies and creating a professional force.

### Cultural and Intellectual Patronage
Khosrow was a renowned patron of philosophy and science, earning the title "Plato's Philosopher King" from Greek refugees he welcomed after Justinian closed the Athenian schools. He expanded the Academy of Gondishapur, a center for Greek, Indian, and Syriac learning. He fostered translations of texts, and his court saw the introduction of chess from India. His reign is considered a cultural renaissance, synthesizing diverse intellectual traditions.

### Religious Policy
A Zoroastrian, Khosrow walked a "fine line" with religious minorities. He maintained good relations with the Christian patriarch Aba I but exiled him when accused of Byzantine collusion. He executed a Mihranid noble for apostasy to Zoroastrianism under pressure from the court. He dealt harshly with any beliefs, like Manichaeism, that threatened the state's religious and political structure.

## Terms
- **Anushirvan**: Khosrow's epithet meaning "the Immortal Soul," referring to his piety and realm's prosperity.
- **Dehqans**: A new class of small landowners created by Khosrow, forming the backbone of his bureaucracy and military.
- **Spahbed**: A general or military commander; Khosrow divided the empire into four military districts, each under a *spahbed*.
- **Aswaran**: The Sasanian cavalry, the main force of the army, which Khosrow expanded by allowing *dehqans* to enlist.
- **Mazdakism**: A religious and social movement advocating communal property and loosened marriage rules, used by Kavad I to curb noble power before being suppressed.
- **Perpetual Peace (532)**: A treaty ending the first war of Khosrow's reign, in which Byzantium paid 11,000 pounds of gold.
- **Fifty-Year Peace Treaty (562)**: A detailed treaty ending the Lazic War, with Byzantium agreeing to annual payments.
- **Academy of Gondishapur**: A major intellectual center in the Sasanian Empire, expanded by Khosrow, where Greek, Indian, and Syriac knowledge was synthesized.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The historicity of the story of Khosrow executing the Mazdakite leader Mazdak is uncertain, as it comes from the later *Shahnameh*.
- The chronological possibility of Khosrow's marriage to a Turkic princess is debated; *Encyclopædia Iranica* states it is "chronologically impossible" given the birth date of his successor.
- The exact motivations for Khosrow's wars with Byzantium are analyzed by historians; Richard Frye argues they were partly to establish Sasanian dominance over the Silk Road trade.

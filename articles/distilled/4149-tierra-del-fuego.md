# Tierra del Fuego

## Overview
Tierra del Fuego is an archipelago at the southern tip of South America, divided between Chile and Argentina. It is significant for its extreme geography, unique subpolar ecosystems, and a history marked by early human settlement, European exploration, and the tragic displacement of its indigenous peoples.

## Timeline
- **c. 8000 BC** — Earliest known human settlement in the archipelago.
- **1520** — Ferdinand Magellan's expedition becomes the first European visit; names the islands "Land of Fire."
- **1525** — Francisco de Hoces first speculates Tierra del Fuego is an island group.
- **1830** — Robert FitzRoy's HMS Beagle voyage brings four Fuegians to England.
- **1879** — Chilean expedition reports gold, triggering a major immigration and gold rush.
- **1881** — Boundary Treaty divides the archipelago between Argentina and Chile.
- **1945** — Oil is discovered in northern Tierra del Fuego.
- **1990** — The Argentine part of Tierra del Fuego becomes a new province.

## Body

### History and Exploration
The earliest human settlement dates to approximately 8,000 BC, with the Yahgan among the first known inhabitants. Portuguese explorer Ferdinand Magellan, sailing for Spain in 1520, was the first European to visit, naming the islands for the fires he saw along the coast. Later expeditions by Drake (1578) and a Dutch company (1616) further mapped the region, with the latter naming Cape Horn. In 1830, the HMS Beagle expedition, which included Charles Darwin, brought four native Fuegians to England, where they became temporary celebrities.

### Colonization and Indigenous Displacement
European settlement and the displacement of native populations began in the second half of the 19th century, driven by a sheep farming boom and gold rushes following an 1879 Chilean expedition's report of placer gold. This led to massive immigration and conflict with the Selkʼnam people. Missionary activity, both Anglican and Salesian Catholic, occurred alongside this period. The indigenous Selkʼnam and Yaghan populations were decimated by disease, violence, and forced transfers to missions like Dawson Island. Today, only a few Selkʼnam remain, while some Yaghan have settled in Villa Ukika.

### Geography and Geology
The archipelago's main island, Isla Grande de Tierra del Fuego, covers 48,100 km², with the western 61% belonging to Chile and the eastern 39% to Argentina. The Beagle Channel divides the main island from southern islands like Hoste and Navarino. The geology is shaped by the Andean orogeny and glaciation, featuring the Cordillera Darwin mountain range and the active Magallanes-Fagnano Fault. The climate is subpolar oceanic, with cool summers, mild winters, and high precipitation in the west, decreasing eastward.

### Flora and Fauna
Only 30% of the islands are forested, classified as Magellanic subpolar forest, which includes six tree species like the southern beech (*Nothofagus*) and the world's southernmost conifer. Strong winds create distinctive "flag-trees." Fauna includes guanacos, Andean condors, king penguins, and the world's southernmost lizard. Introduced North American beavers have caused significant forest damage. The surrounding waters are rich in cetaceans, including southern right whales and various dolphins, as well as pinnipeds like southern elephant seals.

### Economy
Modern economic activities include fishing, oil and natural gas extraction, sheep farming, and growing ecotourism. Tourism often focuses on "southernmost" claims, with both Ushuaia (Argentina) and Puerto Williams (Chile) vying for the title. On the Argentine side, petroleum and natural gas extraction contributed 20% of regional economic output from 2005-2010.

## Terms
*   **Archipelago:** A group of islands.
*   **Subpolar oceanic climate (Köppen Cfc):** A climate with short, cool summers and long, wet, moderately mild winters.
*   **Magellanic subpolar forest:** The specific type of cold-climate forest found in the region.
*   **Placer gold:** Gold particles found in alluvial deposits (river beds and streams).
*   **Sinistral strike-slip fault:** A geological fault where the blocks on either side move horizontally past each other, with the opposite side moving to the left.
*   **Podzols and inceptisols:** Types of soils that develop under the region's forests.

## Debates and open questions
*   **Southernmost City:** Both Ushuaia, Argentina, and Puerto Williams, Chile, claim the title of "southernmost city in the world."
*   **Sovereignty Disputes:** The 1960s and 1970s saw sovereignty claims by Argentina over the Picton, Lennox, and Nueva Islands, bringing the two countries to the brink of war.

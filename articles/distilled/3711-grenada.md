# Grenada

## Overview
Grenada is a small archipelagic country in the eastern Caribbean, known as the "Island of Spice" for its nutmeg production. Its history is marked by colonial struggles between European powers, the legacy of slavery, a post-independence Marxist revolution, and a controversial U.S. invasion, shaping its modern identity as a parliamentary democracy within the Commonwealth.

## Timeline

- **1498** — Christopher Columbus sights the island, naming it "La Concepción."
- **1649** — French expedition founds permanent settlement after conflict with the Kalinago.
- **1763** — Grenada ceded to Britain by the Treaty of Paris.
- **1833** — Slavery abolished in the British Empire, with emancipation completed in Grenada by 1838.
- **1974** — Independence granted under Prime Minister Eric Gairy.
- **1979** — Marxist New JEWEL Movement overthrows Gairy in a bloodless coup.
- **1983** — U.S.-led invasion follows internal coup and execution of Prime Minister Maurice Bishop.
- **2004** — Hurricane Ivan causes severe damage, destroying 90% of homes.
- **2024** — Hurricane Beryl devastates the island of Carriacou.

## Body

### Colonial History and Slavery
Before European arrival, Grenada was inhabited by Indigenous peoples from South America, primarily the Kalinago. Christopher Columbus sighted it in 1498, but the Spanish did not settle. The French established a colony in 1649 after subduing the native population. The economy was built on sugar and indigo worked by enslaved Africans, with the enslaved population growing from about 222 in 1669 to over 15,000 by 1763. Britain gained control in 1762, formalized by the 1763 Treaty of Paris, though the French briefly retook it during the American Revolutionary War. Slavery was abolished in 1833, and to address labor shortages, indentured workers from India were brought starting in 1857.

### Path to Independence and Revolution
Grenada moved toward self-governance in the 20th century, with universal adult suffrage achieved in 1951. It was part of the Federation of the West Indies (1958-1962) and became an Associated State in 1967. Full independence was granted on 7 February 1974 under Prime Minister Eric Gairy. Political unrest led to a 1979 coup by the Marxist-Leninist New JEWEL Movement (NJM), which established a People's Revolutionary Government (PRG) under Maurice Bishop. In 1983, internal power struggles resulted in Bishop's arrest and execution by hardline members of his own party.

### The 1983 Invasion and Aftermath
Following Bishop's murder, the People's Revolutionary Army formed a military government. Citing concerns over a new airport being built with Cuban assistance and regional instability, the U.S. and the Regional Security System invaded on 25 October 1983 in Operation Urgent Fury. The invasion was condemned by the UN General Assembly as a violation of international law. After U.S. withdrawal, democratic elections were held in 1984. The country has since experienced political stability, though it faces economic challenges and natural disasters, including major hurricanes in 2004, 2005, and 2024.

### Geography and Climate
Grenada is a volcanic island with a mountainous interior, its highest point being Mount St. Catherine (840 m). It has a tropical climate with temperatures between 22–32 °C (72–90 °F). The island lies at the southern edge of the Atlantic hurricane belt and has suffered significant damage from storms, most recently Hurricane Beryl in 2024.

### Economy and Society
Tourism is the primary economic driver, alongside the export of spices, especially nutmeg and mace. The population is predominantly of African descent (82%), with smaller communities of Indian (2.2%) and mixed (13%) heritage. English is the official language, but Grenadian Creole English is widely spoken informally. The country is a parliamentary democracy with King Charles III as head of state, represented by a Governor-General.

## Terms
- **Kalinago**: The Indigenous people from South America who inhabited Grenada before European colonization.
- **Associated State**: A status granted to Grenada in 1967, giving it full autonomy over internal affairs while Britain retained control of defense and foreign relations.
- **New JEWEL Movement (NJM)**: The Marxist-Leninist political party that overthrew Eric Gairy's government in 1979.
- **People's Revolutionary Government (PRG)**: The revolutionary government led by Maurice Bishop from 1979 to 1983.
- **Operation Urgent Fury**: The codename for the 1983 U.S.-led invasion of Grenada.
- **Nutmeg**: Grenada's top export crop, introduced in 1843, which earned it the nickname "Island of Spice."
- **Grenadian Creole English**: An English-based creole language used in informal communication across Grenada.
- **Oil down**: Considered the national dish, a stew cooked in coconut milk with various meats and provisions.

## Debates and open questions
- The purpose of the 10,000-foot airstrip built on Grenada in the early 1980s remains debated. The U.S. argued it was for military use by Cuba and the USSR, while the PRG and contractors claimed it was for commercial aviation.
- The 1983 U.S. invasion is a point of contention, condemned by the UN General Assembly as a "flagrant violation of international law" but defended by the U.S. and some regional states as a necessary intervention.
- The fate of Maurice Bishop's body has never been resolved, a subject investigated by a truth and reconciliation commission and student research projects.

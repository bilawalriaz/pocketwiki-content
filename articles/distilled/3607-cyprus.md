# Cyprus

## Overview
Cyprus is an island nation in the eastern Mediterranean, strategically located at the crossroads of Europe, Africa, and Asia. Its history is marked by successive waves of settlement and rule by various empires, leading to a complex modern identity. The island is currently divided between the internationally recognized Republic of Cyprus in the south and the self-declared Turkish Republic of Northern Cyprus in the north, a division stemming from intercommunal conflict and a 1974 military intervention.

## Body

### Ancient and Medieval History
Cyprus was first settled around 13,000 years ago. By the Late Bronze Age (c. 1650 BC), it was an urbanized society known as Alashiya, connected to the wider Mediterranean through copper trade. A major wave of Greek settlement occurred after the Bronze Age collapse (c. 1100–1050 BC), establishing the island's predominantly Greek character. It was subsequently ruled by Assyrians, Egyptians, Persians, and then Alexander the Great in 333 BC. After periods under the Ptolemaic and Roman Empires, Cyprus became part of the Byzantine Empire for about 900 years. It endured Arab raids from 649, leading to a period of shared administration with the Caliphate from 688 until full Byzantine rule was restored in 965. In 1191, Richard I of England captured the island during the Third Crusade, eventually selling it to the Lusignan dynasty, who ruled the Kingdom of Cyprus until 1473. The Republic of Venice then controlled the island until the Ottoman conquest in 1570–1571.

### Ottoman and British Rule
Ottoman rule (1571–1878) brought significant demographic change, with the settlement of Turkish peasants and soldiers creating a Muslim community. The Greek Cypriot population, led by the Orthodox Church, maintained its cultural identity. Following the Russo-Turkish War, Cyprus was leased to Britain in 1878 and formally annexed in 1914. British rule fostered the growth of Greek Cypriot nationalism and the ideal of *enosis* (union with Greece), which became official Greek policy in the 1950s. In response, Turkish Cypriots, initially favoring continued British rule, later advocated for *taksim* (partition) and annexation to Turkey.

### Independence, Conflict, and Division
Cyprus gained independence in 1960, with a constitution allocating power between Greek and Turkish Cypriots. This power-sharing arrangement soon led to political deadlock. Intercommunal violence erupted in 1963–64, displacing over 25,000 Turkish Cypriots and ending their political representation. On 15 July 1974, a coup by Greek Cypriot nationalists seeking *enosis* prompted a Turkish military invasion on 20 July. The intervention resulted in the de facto partition of the island, with Turkey occupying the northern third. Over 150,000 Greek Cypriots and 50,000 Turkish Cypriots were displaced. In 1983, the Turkish Cypriot administration declared independence as the Turkish Republic of Northern Cyprus (TRNC), recognized only by Turkey. The international community considers this occupation illegal. The Annan Plan for reunification was rejected by Greek Cypriots in a 2004 referendum. Cyprus joined the EU in 2004, with EU law suspended in the north. Reunification talks have repeatedly stalled.

### Modern Cyprus
The Republic of Cyprus is a presidential republic and a developed, high-income economy. It joined the eurozone in 2008. The economy, based on services, tourism, and shipping, suffered a major financial crisis in 2012–2013 but has since recovered. Geopolitically, Cyprus faces ongoing disputes with Turkey over maritime boundaries and offshore natural gas exploration. The island's population is predominantly Greek Orthodox in the south and Sunni Muslim in the north, with English widely spoken as a second language.

## Terms
- **Enosis**: The political union of Cyprus with Greece, a goal of Greek Cypriot nationalists.
- **Taksim**: The partition of Cyprus into separate Greek and Turkish polities, a policy advocated by Turkish Cypriots.
- **Millet System**: An Ottoman administrative system where non-Muslim religious communities were governed by their own religious leaders.
- **EOKA (Ethniki Organosis Kyprion Agoniston)**: The National Organisation of Cypriot Fighters, a Greek Cypriot guerrilla group that fought for *enosis* during British rule.
- **TMT (Türk Mukavemet Teşkilatı)**: The Turkish Resistance Organisation, formed by Turkish Cypriots to counter EOKA and advocate for *taksim*.
- **Green Line**: The UN buffer zone dividing Nicosia and, by extension, the island.
- **Annan Plan**: A 2004 UN proposal for a federal solution to the Cyprus dispute, rejected by Greek Cypriots.
- **Sovereign Base Areas**: The British military territories of Akrotiri and Dhekelia, retained by the UK after Cypriot independence.

## Debates and open questions
- The nature of the 1963–64 crisis is controversial, with differing accounts on whether Turkish Cypriots withdrew voluntarily or were forced into enclaves.
- The legality of Turkey's 1974 intervention is disputed; Turkey cites the 1960 Treaty of Guarantee, while the UN and international community deem the subsequent occupation illegal.
- The future political structure of Cyprus remains unresolved, with proposals ranging from a bi-zonal, bi-communal federation to a two-state solution, the latter rejected by the Republic of Cyprus and the international community.
- The destruction of cultural heritage in the north is contested, with Greek Cypriots alleging systematic erasure of their history and some scholars noting shared responsibility for damage.

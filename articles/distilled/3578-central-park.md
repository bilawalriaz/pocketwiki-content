# Central Park

## Overview
Central Park is a 843-acre urban park in Manhattan, New York City, and the first landscaped public park in the United States. Designed by Frederick Law Olmsted and Calvert Vaux, it serves as a vital green space, recreational hub, and cultural icon, attracting an estimated 42 million visitors annually. Its creation and ongoing management reflect evolving ideas about urban planning, public space, and conservation.

## Timeline
- **1853** — New York State Legislature passes the Central Park Act, authorizing purchase of the site.
- **1858** — Frederick Law Olmsted and Calvert Vaux win the design competition with the "Greensward Plan."
- **1857-1876** — Construction of the park, with the first areas opening in late 1858 and completion in 1876.
- **1934** — Robert Moses begins a major rehabilitation program as parks commissioner.
- **1962** — Designated a National Historic Landmark district.
- **1974** — Designated a New York City scenic landmark.
- **1980** — The Central Park Conservancy is founded to combat deterioration.
- **1998** — The Central Park Conservancy assumes management of the park under a public-private partnership.
- **2018** — All loop drives are permanently closed to vehicular traffic.

## Body

### Planning and Creation
The creation of a large park in Manhattan was first proposed in the 1840s as the city's population rapidly expanded. A 778-acre site was approved in 1853. The chosen site was occupied by several settlements, including the majority-Black community of Seneca Village, which were seized through eminent domain. In 1858, landscape architects Frederick Law Olmsted and Calvert Vaux won a design competition with their "Greensward Plan," which emphasized a picturesque, naturalistic landscape separated from the city by sunken transverse roads. Construction began in 1857, requiring massive earth-moving and the importation of topsoil. The park was completed in 1876.

### Governance and Management
Central Park is owned by the New York City Department of Parks and Recreation but has been managed since 1998 by the Central Park Conservancy, a private, non-profit organization, in a public-private partnership. The Conservancy sets the annual operating budget and is responsible for the park's care. The park is patrolled by the NYPD's 22nd (Central Park) Precinct. A volunteer medical emergency service, the Central Park Medical Unit, has operated within the park since 1975.

### Landscape and Features
The park is divided into three sections: the North End, Mid-Park, and South End. Its landscape includes eight artificially created lakes and ponds, several wooded areas like the Ramble and North Woods, and extensive lawns. Key attractions include the Jacqueline Kennedy Onassis Reservoir, the Great Lawn, Bethesda Terrace and Fountain, and the Central Park Zoo. The park contains 21 children's playgrounds, 36 ornamental bridges, and a system of pedestrian walkways, scenic drives, and transverse roads. It is a biologically diverse ecosystem with hundreds of species of flora and fauna.

### History of Use and Restoration
After its completion, the park became a popular destination for all social classes. It experienced a period of decline in the early 20th century, followed by a major rehabilitation in the 1930s under parks commissioner Robert Moses. A second decline in the 1960s and 1970s led to the creation of the Central Park Conservancy in 1980, which has since undertaken extensive restoration projects. The park has been a site for major cultural events, concerts, and protests, and is one of the most filmed locations in the world.

## Terms
- **Eminent domain**: The power of a government to take private property for public use, with compensation.
- **Greensward Plan**: The winning design for Central Park by Olmsted and Vaux, emphasizing a naturalistic, picturesque landscape.
- **Transverse roads**: Sunken roadways that carry crosstown traffic across the park, designed to be visually unobtrusive.
- **Public-private partnership**: A cooperative arrangement between a government agency and a private-sector company to finance, build, and operate projects.
- **Central Park Conservancy**: The non-profit organization that manages Central Park under contract with New York City.
- **National Historic Landmark**: A building, site, or object officially recognized by the U.S. government for its historical significance.
- **Scenic landmark**: A designation by the New York City Landmarks Preservation Commission for sites of special character or aesthetic value.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The ethics and future of the horse-carriage tour industry within the park remain contentious, with calls for its elimination versus reform.
- The impact of new supertall skyscrapers along the park's southern edge on sunlight and the park's environment is a subject of ongoing study and concern.
- The management model of the Central Park Conservancy, as a private entity overseeing a public park, raises questions about equity, access, and public accountability.

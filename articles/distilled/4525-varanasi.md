# Varanasi

## Overview
Varanasi, also known as Benares or Kashi, is one of the world's oldest continually inhabited cities, located on the Ganges River in Uttar Pradesh, India. It holds a central place in Hindu traditions of pilgrimage, death, and mourning, and is a major cultural and religious hub. Its significance is underscored by its association with major Hindu deities, its role in the Bhakti movement, and its status as a center for education, music, and the arts for millennia.

- **2021 CE** — Prime Minister Narendra Modi inaugurates the Kashi Vishwanath Corridor.

## Body

### Sacred Geography and Mythology
Varanasi is considered an extremely holy site in Hinduism, believed to have been founded by the god Shiva. It is one of seven holy cities (Sapta Puri) that can grant liberation (Moksha). The city's ancient name, Kashi, derives from the Sanskrit root *kaś-* meaning "to shine," earning it the epithet "City of Light." Its geography is sacred, bounded by the Varuna and Assi rivers, with the Ganges forming its spiritual core. The ghats—steps leading to the river—are central to ritual life, with Manikarnika and Harishchandra ghats being primary cremation sites.

### Historical Development
Excavations confirm habitation from at least 1800 BCE. The city was part of the Kingdom of Kashi during the time of the Buddha. It endured multiple invasions, including raids in 1033 and 1194 CE that damaged temples. Despite this, it remained a center for intellectuals and theologians. Under Mughal rule, Emperor Aurangzeb ordered the destruction of many temples in 1656, but later Maratha and Bhumihar rulers rebuilt much of the city. The Benares State was established in 1740 and came under British control in 1775. After Indian independence in 1947, it became part of Uttar Pradesh.

### Education and Culture
Varanasi has long been an educational center. The Benares Sanskrit College, founded in 1791, is India's oldest Sanskrit college. The city was a cradle for the Bhakti movement, producing figures like Kabir, Ravidas, and Tulsidas. It is also a major musical center, home to the Benares gharana (a school of tabla playing) and associated with artists like Bismillah Khan. Banaras Hindu University (BHU), founded in 1916, is one of Asia's largest residential universities.

### Economy and Modern Challenges
Silk weaving, particularly for Banarasi saris, is the dominant industry, employing a large Muslim weaver community. Tourism is the second most important industry, driven by religious pilgrimage. The city faces significant infrastructure and environmental pressures, particularly pollution of the Ganges from sewage and industrial chemicals. Major ongoing projects include the Kashi Vishwanath Corridor and proposed metro and road expansions.

## Terms
- **Ghat:** A series of steps leading down to a river, used for bathing, ritual ablutions, and ceremonies.
- **Jyotirlinga:** A sacred shrine where the god Shiva is worshipped as a fiery column of light; Varanasi's Kashi Vishwanath is one of twelve such temples.
- **Bhakti movement:** A medieval Hindu devotional movement emphasizing personal devotion to a deity over ritual.
- **Zamindari:** A historical land-tenure system where a zamindar collected taxes from peasants for the state.
- **Kashi Naresh:** The traditional Hindu king and religious head of Varanasi, based at Ramnagar Fort.
- **Benares gharana:** A specific school or tradition of playing the tabla (Indian percussion instrument).
- **Sapta Puri:** The seven holy cities of Hinduism that can grant liberation (Moksha).
- **Moksha:** Liberation from the cycle of rebirth and suffering in Hindu and Buddhist philosophy.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The precise age and continuous habitation of Varanasi are subjects of archaeological study, with artefacts found dating to 1800 BCE but the city's legendary antiquity often cited as far older.
- The historical narrative of temple destruction and reconstruction under different rulers (e.g., Mughal and Maratha periods) reflects ongoing scholarly debate about cultural and religious syncretism and conflict.
- The severe pollution of the Ganges in Varanasi and the effectiveness of cleanup initiatives like the Namami Gange project remain critical and debated environmental and public health issues.

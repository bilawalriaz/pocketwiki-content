# Dreyfus affair

## Overview
The Dreyfus Affair (1894–1906) was a political scandal that deeply divided the French Third Republic. It began with the wrongful conviction for treason of Captain Alfred Dreyfus, a Jewish artillery officer, and evolved into a national crisis that pitted pro-republican, anticlerical "Dreyfusards" against pro-army, often Catholic "anti-Dreyfusards." The affair became a symbol of modern injustice and antisemitism, profoundly shaping French politics, society, and the role of the press.

## Timeline
- **Dec 1894** — Captain Alfred Dreyfus is convicted of treason and sentenced to life imprisonment.
- **1896** — Lt. Col. Georges Picquart identifies Major Ferdinand Walsin Esterhazy as the real traitor; the Army suppresses the evidence.
- **Jan 1898** — Esterhazy is acquitted by a military court after a two-day trial.
- **13 Jan 1898** — Émile Zola publishes "J'Accuse...!" in *L'Aurore*, accusing the military of a cover-up.
- **1899** — Dreyfus is returned to France for a second trial; convicted again but pardoned and released.
- **3 Jun 1899** — The Supreme Court overturns the 1894 conviction and orders a new trial.
- **12 Jul 1906** — The Court of Cassation fully exonerates Dreyfus, annulling all prior judgments.

## Body

### Origins and the First Trial (1894)
In 1894, the French military's intelligence service (the "Statistics Section") discovered a torn note, the *bordereau*, addressed to the German military attaché and detailing secret French artillery documents. The search for the author was arbitrarily narrowed to officers on the General Staff. Captain Alfred Dreyfus, an Alsatian of Jewish descent, was identified as the suspect. Despite weak evidence—primarily a disputed handwriting analysis by Major du Paty de Clam and a bizarre "autoforgery" theory from criminologist Alphonse Bertillon—Dreyfus was arrested. A secret dossier, containing forged and irrelevant documents, was illegally submitted to the judges during his closed military trial. He was convicted unanimously on 22 December 1894, publicly degraded, and deported to Devil's Island in French Guiana.

### The Truth Emerges and the Affair Divides France (1895–1898)
While Dreyfus languished in prison, his brother Mathieu campaigned tirelessly for a retrial. In 1896, the new head of counter-espionage, Lt. Col. Georges Picquart, discovered evidence pointing to Major Esterhazy as the true author of the *bordereau*. The Army hierarchy suppressed Picquart's findings and forged documents (the "faux Henry") to reinforce Dreyfus's guilt. Public doubt grew, and in January 1898, Émile Zola's open letter "J'Accuse...!" in *L'Aurore* accused the military of a deliberate cover-up, igniting a fierce national debate. The affair split society into two camps: the Dreyfusards (including Zola, Clemenceau, and Poincaré) who demanded justice, and the anti-Dreyfusards (including Drumont and nationalist leagues) who defended the Army and the nation's honor. Antisemitic riots erupted across France.

### The Second Trial, Pardon, and Final Rehabilitation (1899–1906)
Following the exposure of the forgery and the suicide of its author, Major Henry, the Supreme Court annulled the 1894 verdict and ordered a new trial. In 1899, Dreyfus was tried again in Rennes. Despite evidence of his innocence, he was convicted a second time, but with "extenuating circumstances," and sentenced to ten years. To achieve civil peace, President Loubet pardoned Dreyfus on 19 September 1899, though this did not declare him innocent. The final legal rehabilitation came on 12 July 1906, when the Court of Cassation unanimously quashed the 1899 verdict, finding no crime had been committed. Dreyfus was reinstated in the army and served with distinction in World War I.

## Terms
*   **Bordereau:** The unsigned, torn note found in 1894 that detailed secret French military documents and was used to frame Dreyfus.
*   **Dreyfusards:** Supporters of Alfred Dreyfus who campaigned for his exoneration, often aligning with republican, anticlerical, and humanist ideals.
*   **Anti-Dreyfusards:** Opponents of Dreyfus who defended the Army's verdict, often motivated by nationalism, antisemitism, and Catholic conservatism.
*   **Statistics Section (SR):** The French military intelligence service responsible for counter-espionage, which initially pursued Dreyfus and later suppressed evidence of his innocence.
*   **Faux Henry:** A forged document created by Major Hubert-Joseph Henry in 1896 to falsely implicate Dreyfus and discredit evidence pointing to Esterhazy.
*   **Autoforgery:** A discredited theory proposed by Alphonse Bertillon during the first trial, claiming Dreyfus forged the *bordereau* himself to frame himself.
*   **Degradation:** The formal military ceremony where Dreyfus was stripped of his rank and insignia in January 1895.
*   **J'Accuse...!:** The famous open letter published by Émile Zola in 1898, accusing the French military and government of a miscarriage of justice and cover-up.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Role of Antisemitism:** While early accounts downplayed it, modern historians agree that antisemitism was a central, driving force in Dreyfus's conviction and the public hostility he faced.
*   **The Secret Dossier:** The exact contents and purpose of the secret file illegally submitted to the 1894 judges remain debated. Recent research suggests it included homosexual correspondence between German and Italian attachés, which may have been used to inflate the scandal.
*   **Military vs. Civilian Justice:** The affair exposed deep tensions between the autonomous, honor-bound culture of the French Army and the principles of civilian republican justice, a conflict that shaped French civil-military relations for decades.
*   **Herzl and Zionism:** The extent to which the Dreyfus Affair directly inspired Theodor Herzl to found modern political Zionism is debated; some scholars argue he may have exaggerated its personal impact, though it undeniably provided a powerful catalyst.

# Lake Van

## Overview
Lake Van is the largest lake in Turkey and one of the world's few large endorheic (closed-basin) lakes, meaning it has no outlet. Formed by tectonic activity and later isolated by volcanic eruptions, its saline, alkaline waters and dramatic history of level changes make it a key site for studying regional paleoclimate and geology. It has been a central geographic and cultural feature for civilizations from Urartu to the present day.

## Timeline
- **c. 600,000 years ago** — Lake forms as a tectonic basin; later isolated by lava flows from Mount Nemrut.
- **c. 1000 BC** — Lake becomes the center of the Kingdom of Urartu.
- **26 August 1071** — Battle of Manzikert fought north of the lake, leading to Seljuk control of the region.
- **1085–1192** — The Shah-Armen (Sökmenler) dynasty rules the area from Ahlat on the lake's western shore.
- **1974** — First acoustic bathymetric survey of the lake is performed.
- **1989–1990** — International team retrieves sediment cores for paleoclimate study.
- **1990s** — Lake level rises by at least 3 meters, flooding agricultural land.
- **2017** — Archaeologists report discovery of a submerged Urartian fortress.
- **2018** — A new fish species (*Oxynoemacheilus ercisianus*) is discovered in the lake.

## Body

### Hydrology and Geology
Lake Van is a saline soda lake with a strongly alkaline (pH 9.7–9.8) composition, rich in sodium carbonate. It covers 3,755 km², has a volume of 607 km³, and reaches a maximum depth of 451 meters. The lake is primarily tectonic, formed by crustal subsidence. Its current endorheic state was caused by prehistoric lava flows from the dormant Mount Nemrut volcano, which blocked its original outlet to the southwest. The region remains geologically active due to its proximity to the Karlıova triple junction.

### Paleolimnology and Climate
The lake's level has fluctuated dramatically, by as much as 600 meters over the past 600,000 years. Research, including the PALEOVAN project, identifies five major lowstands (periods of low water level) during glacial periods, suggesting reduced regional precipitation was the dominant cause. Notable events include a major lowstand 120 meters below modern levels between 71,000 and 59,000 years ago, a rapid rise to 90 meters above modern levels around 34,000 years ago, and a near-complete desiccation between 16,000 and 15,000 years ago. Recent decades have seen smaller, rapid fluctuations.

### Ecology and Environment
The lake's extreme alkalinity limits its biodiversity. Until 2018, the only known fish was the endemic Pearl Mullet (*Alburnus tarichi*), which migrates to river mouths to spawn. The lake hosts 103 phytoplankton and 36 zooplankton species. A unique feature is the presence of 40-meter-tall microbialites—solid towers formed by cyanobacteria. The region is also home to the rare Van cat breed. The lake's climate is humid continental, with cold winters and mild summers.

### Human History and Culture
The lake has been a historical heartland. The Urartian capital of Tushpa was located on its shores. It was one of the three "seas of Armenia." The 1071 Battle of Manzikert, fought nearby, led to Seljuk conquest and the establishment of local dynasties like the Shah-Armens. The area later saw conflict between the Ottoman and Safavid empires. Architectural heritage includes the 10th-century Cathedral of the Holy Cross on Akdamar Island and historic Ahlatshah tombstones. A submerged fortress, possibly Urartian, was reported in 2017.

## Terms
- **Endorheic**: A closed drainage basin that retains water and allows no outflow to other external bodies of water.
- **Saline soda lake**: A lake with high concentrations of dissolved salts, particularly sodium carbonate.
- **Tectonic lake**: A lake formed by the movement of the Earth's crust, such as faulting or subsidence.
- **Varves**: Annual layers of sediment deposited in a lake, used for dating and climate reconstruction.
- **Paleolimnology**: The study of the history of lakes through their sediment records.
- **Microbialite**: A sedimentary deposit formed by the interaction of microorganisms (like cyanobacteria) with minerals.
- **Lowstand**: A period when a lake's water level is significantly lower than its average or modern level.
- **Tephra**: Fragmented material ejected into the air by a volcanic eruption.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The precise causes of the lake's dramatic historical level changes are debated, with researchers weighing the relative impacts of regional climate (precipitation), volcanic activity (tephra/lahar deposition), and tectonic forcing.
- The existence and nature of the "Lake Van Monster" are subjects of popular myth but lack scientific evidence, with claimed sightings attributed to misidentification.
- The exact age and construction details of the submerged fortress discovered in 2017 are based on visual assessment and await further archaeological confirmation.

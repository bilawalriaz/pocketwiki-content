# Taiwan

## Overview
Taiwan, officially the Republic of China (ROC), is an island nation in East Asia with a complex history and a contentious political status. It is a major global economy, particularly in semiconductor manufacturing, and has transitioned from authoritarian rule to a vibrant democracy. Its political identity and international standing remain central issues, as it is claimed by the People's Republic of China (PRC) and faces significant diplomatic isolation.

## Timeline
- **c. 6000 BC** — Ancestors of Taiwanese indigenous peoples settle the island.
- **1624** — The Dutch East India Company establishes a colonial base on Taiwan.
- **1683** — The Qing dynasty annexes Taiwan.
- **1895** — The Qing cedes Taiwan to the Empire of Japan via the Treaty of Shimonoseki.
- **1945** — Japan surrenders Taiwan to the Republic of China (ROC) after World War II.
- **1949** — The ROC government retreats to Taiwan after losing the Chinese Civil War.
- **1971** — UN Resolution 2758 recognizes the PRC as China's representative, expelling the ROC.
- **1987** — Martial law is lifted, beginning Taiwan's transition to democracy.
- **1996** — The first direct presidential election is held.
- **2019** — Taiwan legalizes same-sex marriage, the first in Asia.
- **2024** — William Lai Ching-te of the DPP wins the presidential election.

## Body

### History and Political Status
Taiwan has been settled for at least 25,000 years, with Austronesian indigenous peoples arriving around 6,000 years ago. Large-scale Han Chinese immigration began under Dutch colonial rule in the 17th century. The island was annexed by the Qing dynasty in 1683 and ceded to Japan in 1895. After World War II, the ROC assumed control. Following the ROC government's retreat to Taiwan in 1949 after losing the Chinese Civil War, the island developed separately from mainland China.

The ROC's international status is highly contested. The PRC claims Taiwan as its territory and insists on the "One China principle," refusing diplomatic ties with states that recognize the ROC. Consequently, only 11 UN member states and the Holy See maintain official relations with Taiwan. The ROC participates in most international forums under the name "Chinese Taipei." Domestically, politics is divided between the Pan-Blue Coalition (favoring eventual unification with China) and the Pan-Green Coalition (favoring eventual independence).

### Economy and Society
Taiwan experienced rapid economic growth from the 1960s, known as the "Taiwan Miracle," becoming one of the Four Asian Tigers. Its export-driven economy is the 21st-largest globally by nominal GDP, with a dominant focus on electronics and semiconductor manufacturing. Companies like TSMC are world leaders in advanced chip production.

The population of about 23.9 million is predominantly Han Chinese (95%), with recognized indigenous Austronesian peoples (2.5%). Mandarin is the primary language, though Taiwanese Hokkien and Hakka are widely spoken. The society is highly urbanized, with a super-aged population and one of the world's lowest fertility rates. Taiwan is a developed country with high rankings in civil liberties, healthcare, and human development.

### Government and Law
The ROC government is based on its 1947 Constitution, significantly revised in the 1990s. It is divided into five branches (Yuan): Executive, Legislative, Judicial, Control, and Examination. The president is the head of state, elected by popular vote. The unicameral Legislative Yuan is the main legislative body.

Taiwan transitioned from a one-party state under martial law (1949–1987) to a multi-party democracy. In 2017, the Constitutional Court ruled that same-sex marriage was constitutional, and parliament legalized it in 2019. The legal system is based on civil law.

## Terms
- **Republic of China (ROC):** The official name of the state governing Taiwan and associated islands since 1912.
- **People's Republic of China (PRC):** The state governing mainland China since 1949, which claims Taiwan as part of its territory.
- **Kuomintang (KMT):** The major political party in the Pan-Blue Coalition, historically favoring eventual unification with China.
- **Democratic Progressive Party (DPP):** The major political party in the Pan-Green Coalition, favoring Taiwanese independence and a distinct Taiwanese identity.
- **1992 Consensus:** An informal understanding between the ROC and PRC that both sides acknowledge there is "one China," but with different interpretations of what that means.
- **Chinese Taipei:** The compromise name under which Taiwan participates in most international organizations, such as the Olympics and the WTO.
- **Martial Law:** A period of authoritarian rule under the KMT from 1949 to 1987, during which political opposition was suppressed.
- **Taiwan Miracle:** The period of rapid economic growth and industrialization in Taiwan from the 1960s to the 1990s.
- **Austronesian:** A major language family and ethnolinguistic group to which Taiwan's indigenous peoples belong.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Political Status:** The core debate is whether Taiwan should pursue formal independence, maintain the status quo, or eventually unify with China. Public opinion strongly favors the status quo, but identification as "Taiwanese" has risen sharply.
- **International Recognition:** Taiwan's exclusion from the UN and most international bodies, and its participation under the name "Chinese Taipei," remains a major point of contention and practical difficulty.
- **Cross-Strait Relations:** The nature of future relations with the PRC is uncertain. The PRC's Anti-Secession Law threatens the use of "non-peaceful means" if Taiwan moves toward formal independence, while the U.S. maintains a policy of "strategic ambiguity" regarding its defense commitments.

# Northern Crusades

## Overview
The Northern Crusades were a series of medieval military campaigns conducted by Catholic kingdoms and military orders to Christianize pagan peoples around the Baltic Sea, primarily Balts, Finns, and West Slavs. These campaigns, spanning from the 12th to the 15th centuries, were driven by religious zeal, economic interests, and political expansion, fundamentally reshaping the region's religious and political landscape.

## Timeline
- **1147** — Wendish Crusade begins against Polabian Slavs (Wends).
- **1195** — Pope Celestine III officially calls for crusades against Baltic pagans.
- **1202** — Livonian Brothers of the Sword military order founded.
- **1219** — Danish conquest of northern Estonia begins after Battle of Lindanisse.
- **1226** — Duke Konrad I of Masovia invites Teutonic Knights to Prussia.
- **1236** — Livonian Brothers of the Sword defeated at Battle of Saule; later merged into Teutonic Order.
- **1237** — Teutonic Order incorporates Livonian Brothers, creating its Livonian branch.
- **1242** — Battle of the Ice: Novgorod defeats Livonian Order, halting eastward expansion.
- **1260** — Battle of Durbe: Samogitians and Curonians defeat united Livonian and Teutonic Orders.
- **1386** — Union of Krewo: Lithuanian Grand Duke Jogaila converts to Christianity, marrying Polish Queen Jadwiga.
- **1410** — Battle of Grunwald: Polish-Lithuanian alliance decisively defeats the Teutonic Order.

## Body

### Background and Motives
The Northern Crusades targeted pagan populations in modern-day Estonia, Finland, Latvia, Lithuania, Poland, and Russia. While officially framed as religious missions authorized by papal bulls (e.g., Pope Alexander III's 1171 bull *Non parum animus noster*), they also served economic and political goals, such as controlling Baltic trade routes and resources. The Teutonic Order, a German military-religious order founded c. 1190, became the primary military force, profiting immensely alongside German merchants.

### Major Campaigns
The **Wendish Crusade (1147)** against Polabian Slavs initiated the conflicts. The **Swedish Crusades (1150–1293)** targeted Finns, Tavastians, and Karelians, often competing with Danish efforts. The **Danish Crusades** to Finland (1191, 1202) were part of a rivalry with Sweden for influence. The **Livonian Crusade (1198–1290)** was the most extensive, involving the conquest of Livonians, Latgallians, Selonians, and Estonians. Bishop Albert of Buxhoeveden secured a constant flow of crusaders and founded the Livonian Brothers of the Sword in 1202. The island of Saaremaa resisted until 1227, and Curonians and Semigallians were subjugated by 1290.

### Teutonic Order and Lithuanian Resistance
The Teutonic Knights were invited to Prussia in 1226 by Duke Konrad I of Masovia to suppress pagan Old Prussians. After the Livonian Brothers' defeat in 1236, their remnants merged into the Teutonic Order, expanding its control. Lithuania, the last pagan state in Europe, resisted conversion until Grand Duke Jogaila's baptism in 1386. The conflict culminated in the 1410 Battle of Grunwald, where a Polish-Lithuanian alliance defeated the Teutonic Order, marking a turning point.

### Conflict with Orthodox Powers
The crusades also involved conflicts with Orthodox Christian states like Novgorod and Pskov. The Livonian Order's eastward expansion was halted at the 1242 Battle of the Ice. Papal efforts, such as trade embargoes against Novgorodians (c. 1229), aimed to protect Catholic interests in Finland and Estonia, but the region remained contested.

## Terms
- **Northern Crusades**: Medieval campaigns to Christianize Baltic pagans, authorized by the papacy.
- **Teutonic Order**: German military-religious order founded c. 1190, key in Baltic conquests.
- **Livonian Brothers of the Sword**: Military order founded in 1202, merged into the Teutonic Order in 1237.
- **Papal Bull**: Official decree from the Pope, often authorizing crusades.
- **Terra Mariana**: Latin name for the Livonian Confederation, meaning "Land of Mary."
- **Old Prussians**: Baltic pagan people conquered by the Teutonic Order.
- **Hillfort**: Fortified settlement, key defensive structure for Baltic tribes.
- **Battle of Grunwald (1410)**: Decisive defeat of the Teutonic Order by Polish-Lithuanian forces.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The historicity of the **First Swedish Crusade (c. 1150)** is debated; it may be a 19th-century romantic nationalist invention.
- The extent of **Danish success and permanent footholds** in Finland before Swedish efforts is uncertain, with sparse attestation for some campaigns (e.g., 1202, 1209).
- The identity of the **"barbarians"** threatening Finland in papal letters (c. 1221) remains unclear, though later documents specify Novgorodians.
- The effectiveness of **peaceful conversion attempts** (e.g., by Adalbert of Bremen, 1045–1072) versus military campaigns is debated.

# Jericho

## Overview
Jericho, located in the Jordan Valley, is among the oldest continuously inhabited cities in the world, with archaeological evidence of settlement dating back to approximately 9000 BCE. Its strategic location near a major spring made it a persistent oasis of habitation, leading to its designation as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2023 as the "oldest fortified city in the world." The city's history spans from prehistoric hunter-gatherer camps to its role in biblical narratives, Hasmonean and Herodian kingdoms, and modern Palestinian governance.

## Timeline
- **c. 10,000 BCE** — Natufian hunter-gatherers camp at the Ein es-Sultan spring.
- **c. 9500–9000 BCE** — First permanent settlement (Pre-Pottery Neolithic A) with mud-brick houses.
- **c. 8000 BCE** — Construction of a massive stone wall and tower at Tell es-Sultan.
- **c. 6800 BCE** — Settlement re-founded (Pre-Pottery Neolithic B) with rectilinear buildings and plastered skulls.
- **c. 3000–2000 BCE** — Growth through the Early Bronze Age, with fortifications and a palace.
- **c. 1700–1550 BCE** — Jericho reaches its greatest Bronze Age extent in the Middle Bronze Age.
- **63 BCE** — Roman general Pompey passes through Jericho.
- **37–4 BCE** — Herod the Great constructs extensive winter palaces and aqueducts.
- **68–70 CE** — Roman forces advance through Jericho during the First Jewish Revolt.
- **614 CE** — Byzantine monasteries are abandoned after the Sasanian invasion.
- **1948** — Jericho comes under Jordanian control after the 1948 Arab–Israeli War.
- **1994** — Administrative control is handed to the Palestinian Authority.

## Body

### Prehistoric and Bronze Age Settlement
The earliest settlement at Tell es-Sultan was a PPNA village featuring circular mud-brick houses, a massive stone wall, and a stone tower built around 8000 BCE. This tower is among the oldest ever discovered. The subsequent PPNB period saw rectilinear buildings, plastered human skulls, and expanded agriculture. After a hiatus, the site was reoccupied and grew through the Chalcolithic and Early Bronze Ages, becoming a fortified town. It reached its greatest extent in the Middle Bronze Age (c. 1700–1550 BCE) with extensive defenses, before being destroyed in the 16th century BCE.

### Hasmonean, Herodian, and Roman Periods
In the 2nd century BCE, the Hasmoneans established Jericho as a winter resort with royal palaces at Tulul Abu el-'Alayiq. Herod the Great later expanded these palaces, built aqueducts, and developed the area into a major agricultural and leisure center. After the Roman conquest in 63 BCE, Jericho became part of the Roman province of Judaea. The city is mentioned in the New Testament as a place visited by Jesus. Following the First Jewish Revolt (66–73 CE), Jericho declined significantly.

### Byzantine, Islamic, and Crusader Eras
A new Byzantine city was built east of Tell es-Sultan in the 4th century CE, featuring churches and monasteries. A Jewish community also thrived, as evidenced by synagogues like the "Shalom 'al Yisrael" synagogue. After the Muslim conquest in the 7th century, Jericho became part of Jund Filastin. The Umayyad period saw the construction of Hisham's Palace. Crusaders later rebuilt monasteries and established a large-scale sugar industry, which continued under Ayyubid and Mamluk rule.

### Modern History
Jericho was part of the Ottoman Empire until World War I, after which it came under British Mandate rule. It was annexed by Jordan in 1950 and occupied by Israel in 1967. Under the 1994 Oslo Accords, it became the first West Bank city transferred to Palestinian Authority control. The city remains an enclave of Area A, surrounded by Area C under full Israeli control.

## Terms
*   **Tell**: An archaeological mound formed by successive layers of human habitation.
*   **PPNA (Pre-Pottery Neolithic A)**: A Neolithic period (c. 9500–8500 BCE) characterized by sedentary villages, mud-brick architecture, and early agriculture, but before the invention of pottery.
*   **PPNB (Pre-Pottery Neolithic B)**: A subsequent Neolithic period (c. 8800–6500 BCE) featuring rectilinear architecture, plastered skulls, and expanded domestication.
*   **Hasmoneans**: A Jewish dynasty that ruled Judea and surrounding regions from 140 to 37 BCE after gaining independence from the Seleucid Empire.
*   **Herod the Great**: Roman client king of Judea (37–4 BCE) known for massive building projects, including expansions at Jericho.
*   **Jund Filastin**: An administrative military district of the early Islamic Caliphate, part of the province of Bilad al-Sham.
*   **Area A**: A designation under the Oslo Accords for Palestinian-administered civil and security control in the West Bank.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Purpose of the Neolithic Wall and Tower**: The function of the massive stone wall and tower at Tell es-Sultan (c. 8000 BCE) is debated; theories include defense against floods or ceremonial use.
*   **Population Estimates**: The number of inhabitants during the PPNA period is uncertain, with estimates ranging from 200–300 to 2,000–3,000.
*   **Historicity of the Biblical Battle of Jericho**: The biblical account of Joshua's conquest is not generally accepted by scholars as historical fact. Archaeologist Lorenzo Nigro suggests the narrative may have developed from local memories of earlier destructions.
*   **Date of the Middle Bronze Age Destruction**: The calibrated carbon date for the destruction of the Middle Bronze Age city (City-IV) is c. 1617–1530 BCE, which aligns with a stratigraphical date of c. 1550 BCE.

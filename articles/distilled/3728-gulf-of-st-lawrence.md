# Gulf of St. Lawrence

## Overview
The Gulf of St. Lawrence is a large, semi-enclosed sea that connects the Great Lakes to the Atlantic Ocean via the St. Lawrence River. It is a vital ecological and economic region, bordering five Canadian provinces and the French territory of Saint-Pierre and Miquelon, and serves as a critical habitat for marine life and a historic hub for fisheries and transportation.

## Timeline
- **c. 1530** — Basque whalers from Saint-Jean-de-Luz begin whaling at Red Bay in the Gulf.
- **1534** — French explorer Jacques Cartier makes the first documented European voyage in the Gulf's waters.
- **1579** — English ports close to Spanish oil imports, leading to the collapse of Basque whaling in the Gulf.
- **1829–1830** — The Pointe-des-Monts Lighthouse is built, marking the traditional boundary between the St. Lawrence River and the Gulf.
- **1919** — Canada's first Migratory Bird Sanctuaries are established on islands in the Gulf.
- **1955** — The Canso Causeway is constructed, blocking water exchange through the Strait of Canso.
- **March 2018** — The Western Honguedo Strait Coral Conservation Area is created via a Canada–Quebec agreement.
- **March 31, 2024** — Quebec's network of protected areas in the Gulf region reaches 274,431 km².

## Body

### Geography and Extent
The Gulf is bounded by the Labrador Peninsula and Quebec to the north, Newfoundland and Saint-Pierre to the east, Nova Scotia and Cape Breton Island to the south, and the Gaspé Peninsula and New Brunswick to the west. It contains major islands like Anticosti, Prince Edward, and Cape Breton. There is no consensus on the exact demarcation between the St. Lawrence River and the Gulf; the Pointe-des-Monts Lighthouse (built 1829–1830) is a historically recognized boundary point. The International Hydrographic Organization defines the Gulf's extent with specific geographic coordinates.

### Hydrography and Outlets
The Gulf's waters flow to the Atlantic Ocean through several straits: the Strait of Belle Isle (15–60 km wide), the Cabot Strait (~56 miles wide), and the Jacques Cartier and Honguedo Straits around Anticosti Island. The Strait of Canso, once a natural outlet, was blocked by the Canso Causeway in 1955. A key undersea feature is the Laurentian Channel, a deep (~290 m), long (~1,250 km) trough formed by glacial erosion. Deep, cold ocean water enters the Gulf via this channel, but its bottom waters in the estuary have become hypoxic (low in oxygen) over the 20th century.

### Ecology and Climate
The Gulf is a highly productive ecosystem. Its "Estuary and Gulf of St. Lawrence bioregion" has some of Atlantic Canada's warmest summer surface waters and most extensive winter sea ice. Ice forms in the river by December and covers the entire Gulf by January/February. Thirteen cetacean species inhabit the area, including the blue whale, humpback whale, sperm whale, and North Atlantic right whale.

### Human History and Activity
The Gulf has supported First Nations fisheries and transportation for millennia. European activity began with Basque whalers (c. 1530) and Jacques Cartier's 1534 voyage, which named the "Country of Canadas." The region's ports, like Havre-Saint-Pierre and Port-Menier, support coastal communities. St. Paul Island is known as the "Graveyard of the Gulf" due to shipwrecks.

### Protected Areas
Conservation efforts include the Western Honguedo Strait Coral Conservation Area (2018) and a network of national parks along the coast, such as Mingan Archipelago, Forillon, and Prince Edward Island National Parks. As of 2024, Quebec's protected network in the region covers 274,431 km², with 12.21% in marine and coastal environments.

## Terms
- **Estuary**: The tidal mouth of a large river, where the sea meets freshwater. The boundary between the St. Lawrence Estuary and the Gulf is debated.
- **Hypoxic**: A condition of low dissolved oxygen in water, which can harm marine life. The bottom waters of the Laurentian Channel have become hypoxic.
- **Laurentian Channel**: A deep, long underwater trough on the Gulf floor, carved by glacial erosion, that allows deep ocean water to enter.
- **Cetaceans**: A group of marine mammals including whales, dolphins, and porpoises. Thirteen species inhabit the Gulf.
- **Migratory Bird Sanctuary (MBS)**: A protected area for birds, established under Canada's Migratory Birds Convention Act. The first were created in the Gulf in 1919.
- **Canso Causeway**: A man-made causeway completed in 1955 that connects Cape Breton Island to mainland Nova Scotia, blocking the Strait of Canso.
- **Bioregion**: A geographically defined area with distinct ecological characteristics. The "Estuary and Gulf of St. Lawrence bioregion" is a key planning area.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Boundary Definition**: There is no consensus on the precise demarcation between the St. Lawrence River and the Gulf of St. Lawrence, nor on whether the Gulf is hydrographically a gulf or an estuary.
- **Ecological Change**: The cause of hypoxia in the bottom waters of the Laurentian Channel over the 20th century is a significant environmental concern.

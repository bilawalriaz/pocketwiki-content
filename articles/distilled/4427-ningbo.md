# Ningbo

## Overview
Ningbo is a major sub-provincial city and port in Zhejiang province, China, with a history spanning over 8,000 years. It is a critical economic hub, home to the world's busiest port by cargo tonnage, and holds a unique administrative status granting it provincial-level autonomy in economic policy.

## Timeline
- **c. 6300 BCE** — Jingtou Mountain Culture relics indicate early human activity.
- **c. 4800 BCE** — Hemudu culture flourishes, evidencing settlement and rice cultivation.
- **636 CE** — Mingzhou (Ningbo's former name) is established during the Tang dynasty.
- **c. 1522** — Portuguese begin trading in Ningbo.
- **1842** — Ningbo is opened as one of the first five treaty ports via the Treaty of Nanjing.
- **1940** — Japanese forces bomb Ningbo with plague-infected fleas during WWII.
- **2006** — Ningbo Port begins expansion to merge with Zhoushan Port.
- **2008** — Hangzhou Bay Bridge opens, significantly reducing travel time to Shanghai.
- **2010** — Ningbo-Zhoushan port becomes the world's busiest by cargo tonnage.
- **2016** — Ningbo is awarded "City of Culture in East Asia" by China, Japan, and Korea.

## Body

### History and Etymology
Ningbo's history dates to the Neolithic Jingtou Mountain and Hemudu cultures. Its name means "serene wave," and it is abbreviated "Yong" for the local Yong River. Formerly known as Mingzhou ("Bright Province"), it was a major trade port on the Maritime Silk Road, hosting Arab and Jewish communities during the Song dynasty. The Portuguese knew it as "Liampó" in the 16th century. It was forcibly opened as a treaty port in 1842 and suffered a Japanese biological attack in 1940.

### Geography and Climate
Ningbo is located on the coast, bordered by Hangzhou Bay and the East China Sea. Its land area is 9,816 km², with a coastline of 1,562 km. The city has a humid subtropical climate with four distinct seasons, hot summers, and cool winters. It is affected by the Asian monsoon and typhoons, and is identified as a city at high risk of flooding due to climate change.

### Administrative Status and Economy
Ningbo is one of China's five separate state-planning cities, giving it provincial-level autonomy in economic and financial policy. Its economy is driven by a strong private sector and exports of electrical products, textiles, and industrial tools. The Ningbo-Zhoushan port is the world's busiest for cargo (1.22 billion tons in 2021) and the third-busiest for containers (31.1 million TEUs in 2021). The city hosts numerous development zones, including a national free trade zone.

### Transportation and Infrastructure
Ningbo's transportation network includes the Hangzhou Bay Bridge, the world's second-longest trans-oceanic bridge. It is served by Ningbo Lishe International Airport and a railway network with high-speed lines to Hangzhou and Wenzhou. The city has an extensive expressway system and a growing metro network.

### Culture and Tourism
Ningbo is a cultural center with attractions like the Tianyi Pavilion, Asia's oldest existing private library (built 1516 CE), and the Hemudu Culture Relics Site. The Ningbo Museum, designed by Pritzker Prize winner Wang Shu, is a notable modern landmark. The city is known for its seafood and Ningbo Tangyuan (sweet glutinous rice balls).

## Terms
- **Sub-provincial city:** An administrative division in China with a status between a province and a prefecture-level city, often with greater autonomy.
- **State-planning city:** One of five Chinese cities (including Ningbo) with separate economic planning status, granting them provincial-level autonomy in economic policy.
- **TEU (Twenty-foot Equivalent Unit):** A standard unit of cargo capacity used to measure container traffic.
- **Maritime Silk Road:** An ancient network of trade routes connecting China to Southeast Asia, India, and beyond via the sea.
- **Treaty port:** A port opened to foreign trade and residence by treaty after a military defeat, a practice common in 19th-century China.
- **Wu Chinese:** A major group of Sinitic languages spoken in the Yangtze Delta region, including the Ningbo dialect.
- **Sponge city:** An urban design concept to manage rainwater and mitigate flooding by using permeable surfaces and natural landscapes.

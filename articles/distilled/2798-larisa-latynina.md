# Larisa Latynina

## Overview
Larisa Latynina is a former Soviet artistic gymnast who dominated the sport from 1956 to 1964. She is credited with establishing the Soviet Union as a gymnastics powerhouse and held the record for the most Olympic medals (18) for 48 years, a testament to her sustained excellence across three Olympic Games.

## Timeline
- **27 December 1934** — Born Larisa Semyonovna Diriy in Kherson, Ukrainian SSR.
- **1954** — Debuts internationally at the Rome World Championships, winning team gold.
- **1956** — Wins first Olympic all-around and team gold at the Melbourne Games.
- **1958** — Wins five titles at the World Championships while four months pregnant.
- **1960** — Defends Olympic all-around and team titles at the Rome Games.
- **1962** — Wins all-around title at the World Championships, beating Věra Čáslavská.
- **1964** — Wins team and floor exercise gold at the Tokyo Games, setting the 18-medal record.
- **1966** — Announces retirement after the World Championships.
- **1968-1976** — Coaches the Soviet women's team to Olympic gold in 1968, 1972, and 1976.
- **1980** — Organizes gymnastics competition at the Moscow Olympics.
- **1998** — Inducted into the International Gymnastics Hall of Fame.

## Body

### Early Life and Career Beginnings
Born Larisa Diriy, she was raised by her mother after her father left and was later killed at the Battle of Stalingrad. She survived the Nazi occupation of Ukraine. After initially practicing ballet, she switched to gymnastics. Her first major success came at the 1954 Rome World Championships, where she won team gold for the Soviet Union at age 19.

### Olympic and World Championship Dominance
Latynina's Olympic career spanned three Games (1956, 1960, 1964), where she won a total of 18 medals: nine gold, five silver, and four bronze. She won the all-around title in 1956 and 1960, and the floor exercise title in all three Olympics. Her 1958 World Championship performance was remarkable, as she won five titles while secretly pregnant. She was the first female gymnast to win team, all-around, and event gold at two consecutive Olympics (1956 and 1960). Her record of 18 Olympic medals stood until 2012.

### Coaching and Later Life
After retiring in 1966, Latynina coached the Soviet national team until 1977, leading them to Olympic team gold in 1968, 1972, and 1976. She later organized gymnastics for the 1980 Moscow Olympics. She has received numerous state honors, including the Olympic Order (1989). In 2023, she publicly opposed Russian athletes competing under a neutral flag at the Olympics due to the 2022 invasion of Ukraine.

## Terms
- **Artistic Gymnastics**: A discipline of gymnastics involving performances on apparatus like the vault, uneven bars, balance beam, and floor exercise.
- **All-around**: A competition where gymnasts perform on all apparatus, with the winner having the highest combined score.
- **Team Event**: A competition where a country's gymnasts perform, and their scores are combined for a team total.
- **Merited Master of Sports of the USSR**: A high honorary title for elite athletes in the Soviet Union.
- **Olympic Order**: A high distinction awarded by the International Olympic Committee for outstanding contributions to the Olympic Movement.

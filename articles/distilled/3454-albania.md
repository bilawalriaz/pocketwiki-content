# Albania

## Overview
Albania is a country in Southeast Europe on the Balkan Peninsula, with a rich history from ancient Illyrian tribes through Ottoman rule to its 1912 independence. It is a parliamentary republic and EU candidate, known for its diverse landscapes, religious diversity, and a 20th-century transition from monarchy to communist dictatorship to democracy. Its modern identity is shaped by a complex history of resistance, cultural revival, and ongoing political and economic reforms.

## Body

### Early History and Antiquity
Albania was inhabited by Illyrian tribes, with Greek colonies founded along its coast. The Illyrian Ardiaean Kingdom, under rulers like Agron and Teuta, clashed with Rome, leading to Roman conquest by 167 BC. The region was later part of the Byzantine Empire. The first undisputed mention of Albanians is in an 11th-century text by Michael Attaliates, referring to a 1079 revolt.

### Medieval Period and Ottoman Rule
After the Byzantine Empire's split, Slavs arrived in the Balkans. The Principality of Arbanon (1190) was an early Albanian entity. The 13th-century Kingdom of Albania was established by Charles I of Anjou. The 15th century saw resistance to Ottoman expansion led by the national hero Skanderbeg, who united Albanian principalities. Despite his victories, Albania fell to the Ottomans after his death. Four centuries of Ottoman rule followed, marked by Islamisation, administrative integration, and the emergence of prominent Albanians in the empire's military and bureaucracy.

### National Awakening and Independence
The 19th-century Albanian Renaissance (Rilindja) fostered cultural and national consciousness. The Treaty of San Stefano (1878), which threatened to partition Albanian lands, spurred the formation of the League of Prizren to defend territorial integrity. Albania declared independence in 1912, but its borders, set by the 1913 Treaty of London, left many ethnic Albanians outside the state. The early 20th century was turbulent, with a brief monarchy, Italian invasion in 1939, and World War II occupation.

### Communist Era and Transition
After WWII, Enver Hoxha established a Marxist-Leninist regime, isolating Albania internationally and banning all religion. The regime fell in 1991, leading to a transition to democracy marked by political instability and a 1997 civil war sparked by pyramid schemes. Since then, Albania has pursued Euro-Atlantic integration, joining NATO in 2009 and opening EU accession talks in 2022. The long tenure of Prime Minister Edi Rama (2013–present) has seen economic growth and EU progress, but also criticism for democratic backsliding, corruption, and state capture.

### Geography and Climate
Albania has a varied landscape, from the snow-capped Albanian Alps and Korab Mountains to fertile coastal plains. Its climate ranges from Mediterranean on the coast to continental in the interior. The country is a biodiversity hotspot, with protected areas like Butrint National Park and the Vjosa, one of Europe's last wild rivers.

### Economy and Society
Albania is an upper-middle-income economy transitioning from central planning. The service sector, including tourism, is dominant. Challenges include corruption, a large informal sector, and regional economic disparities. The population is 2.4 million (2023), with significant emigration. Albanian is the official language, and the society is religiously diverse, with Muslims, Catholics, and Orthodox Christians. Education is free and compulsory, and the healthcare system provides universal access.

## Terms
- **Illyrians**: Indo-European tribes who inhabited the western Balkans, including modern Albania, before Roman conquest.
- **Skanderbeg**: Gjergj Kastrioti Skanderbeg, a 15th-century Albanian lord who led a 25-year resistance against the Ottoman Empire.
- **Ottoman Empire**: A Turkic empire that ruled Albania from the late 14th century until 1912, significantly influencing its demographics and culture.
- **Rilindja**: The Albanian National Awakening, a 19th-century cultural and political movement for independence and national identity.
- **League of Prizren**: An 1878 organization formed to defend Albanian-inhabited territories from partition by neighboring states.
- **Enver Hoxha**: The communist leader of Albania from 1944 until his death in 1985, known for extreme isolationism and repression.
- **SPAK**: The Special Structure against Corruption and Organized Crime, a key judicial body in modern Albania's anti-corruption efforts.
- **EU Candidate Status**: A formal step in the European Union accession process, granted to Albania in 2014.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The precise relationship between the ancient settlement of Albanopolis and the medieval settlement of Albanon/Arbanon is a subject of scholarly debate.
- The ethnogenesis of the Albanian people is uncertain, with historical records providing limited clarity on their origins.
- The motives behind the widespread Islamisation of Albanians during Ottoman rule are debated, with factors ranging from social advancement to coercion.
- Assessments of Albania's current political system are divided, with debates over the extent of democratic backsliding, the effectiveness of anti-corruption measures, and the influence of clientelism and state capture.

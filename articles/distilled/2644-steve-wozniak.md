# Steve Wozniak

## Overview
Steve Wozniak is an American technology entrepreneur and engineer, best known as the co-founder of Apple Inc. and the primary designer of the Apple I and Apple II computers. His work in the 1970s and 1980s was foundational to the personal computer revolution, establishing key technical and commercial precedents for the industry.

## Timeline
- **August 11, 1950** — Born in San Jose, California.
- **1971** — Meets Steve Jobs; they begin their first business partnership building and selling "blue boxes."
- **1975** — Begins designing the Apple I computer.
- **April 1, 1976** — Co-founds Apple Computer Company with Steve Jobs and Ronald Wayne.
- **1977** — Introduces the Apple II, one of the first highly successful mass-produced microcomputers.
- **February 7, 1981** — Suffers a plane crash and temporary amnesia, leading to a leave from Apple.
- **May 1982 & 1983** — Founds Unuson and sponsors the US Festivals.
- **Early 1985** — Permanently leaves Apple's active workforce to found CL 9.
- **1987** — CL 9 releases the first programmable universal remote, the "CORE."
- **2000** — Inducted into the National Inventors Hall of Fame.
- **2017** — Founds Woz U, an online educational technology service.
- **2020** — Launches Efforce, a marketplace for funding ecological projects.

## Body

### Early Life and Pre-Apple Work
Wozniak was born in 1950 to an engineer father. He was inspired by science fiction and engineering books. After being expelled from the University of Colorado Boulder for hacking, he enrolled at UC Berkeley. In 1971, he built his first computer, "Cream Soda," with a friend. He was introduced to Steve Jobs by mutual friend Bill Fernandez. Their first partnership involved building and selling "blue boxes," devices for making free long-distance calls. In 1973, Wozniak designed a circuit board for Atari's *Breakout* game, though he was initially unaware of the full bonus paid to Jobs.

### Founding Apple and Early Success
In 1975, Wozniak began designing the Apple I to impress the Homebrew Computer Club. He offered the design to HP five times but was rejected. With Jobs, he formed Apple Computer on April 1, 1976. They secured an order for 50 assembled units from the Byte Shop, funding the first batch by selling personal items. The Apple I sold for $666.66. After receiving funding from Mike Markkula in late 1976, Wozniak left HP to become Apple's VP of R&D. He then designed the Apple II, which featured color graphics and became a landmark mass-produced personal computer. He also designed the Disk II floppy drive. Apple's 1980 IPO made both founders millionaires.

### Later Apple Work and Departure
Wozniak influenced the early Macintosh project with Jef Raskin. A 1981 plane crash caused severe injuries and amnesia, leading to a temporary leave. He returned to UC Berkeley, enrolling under the name "Rocky Raccoon Clark," and later sponsored the US Festivals. Upon returning to Apple, he found the company culture had shifted, with the Apple II division being marginalized. He left Apple permanently in 1985 to found CL 9, stating he wanted to recapture the fun of developing new technology. He remained on Apple's payroll in a ceremonial capacity.

### Post-Apple Ventures and Philanthropy
After Apple, Wozniak founded CL 9, which created the first programmable universal remote. He has been involved in numerous ventures, including Wheels of Zeus (wireless GPS), Acquicor Technology, and Fusion-io. He is a philanthropist focused on education, helping fund computer initiatives in schools, including a 1990 project in the former Soviet Union. He co-founded the Electronic Frontier Foundation in 1990. In recent years, he has launched Efforce (ecological projects) and Privateer Space (addressing space debris). He continues to represent Apple at events.

## Terms
*   **Microcomputer:** A small, relatively inexpensive computer with a microprocessor as its central processing unit.
*   **Blue Box:** An electronic device that generates the tones used to manipulate telephone network switches, allowing for free long-distance calls.
*   **Homebrew Computer Club:** An early computer hobbyist group in Silicon Valley that was a key center for the home computer era.
*   **Mass-produced:** Manufactured in large quantities using standardized designs and automated processes.
*   **Graphical User Interface (GUI):** A user interface that allows interaction with electronic devices through graphical icons and visual indicators, as opposed to text-based commands.
*   **Prosopagnosia:** A neurological condition characterized by the inability to recognize faces.
*   **Anterograde amnesia:** A condition where an individual is unable to form new memories after the event that caused the amnesia.
*   **Phreaking:** The exploration and exploitation of the telephone network, often involving the use of blue boxes.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   The exact division of credit and financial reward for the *Breakout* circuit board design between Wozniak and Jobs is a point of historical discussion, with Wozniak learning of the full bonus paid by Atari a decade later.
*   Wozniak's views on artificial superintelligence have evolved significantly over time, shifting from foreboding to seeing potential benefits, and later expressing skepticism about its emergence, highlighting ongoing uncertainty about AI's long-term impact.
*   The reasons for Wozniak's final departure from Apple in 1985 have been publicly attributed to both disagreements with management and his personal desire to pursue new, fun engineering projects.

# Mount Elbrus

## Overview
Mount Elbrus is the highest mountain in Russia and Europe, a dormant stratovolcano in the Caucasus Mountains. Its dual summits, glacial systems, and history of human endeavor—from mythology to modern mountaineering—make it a significant geographical and cultural landmark.

## Timeline
- **c. 10 million years ago** — Formation of Mount Elbrus begins.
- **c. 700,000 years ago** — Main caldera forms, marking the end of a major eruptive cycle.
- **c. AD 50** — Last known eruption.
- **c. AD 130** — Greek writer Arrian mentions a peak named Strobilos, later identified by some as Elbrus.
- **10 July 1829** — First recorded ascent of the eastern summit by Khillar Khashirov.
- **1874** — First ascent of the western summit by a British expedition.
- **21 August 1942** — German troops plant a swastika flag on the summit during WWII.
- **13 February 1943** — Soviet mountaineers remove the German flags.
- **1956** — 400 mountaineers climb Elbrus en masse to mark a political anniversary.
- **1986** — Elbrus is incorporated into Prielbrusye National Park.
- **1990** — First recorded speed race on the mountain; Anatoli Boukreev sets a record.
- **1997** — A Land Rover is driven to the East Peak summit.
- **7 May 2017** — Karl Egloff sets the current speed record for the full ascent/descent of the long route.

## Body

### Etymology and Geography
The name "Elbrus" is linked to the Alborz range in Iran, derived from the Avestan *Harā Bərəzaitī*, meaning "high watch." Local names include "Uash-ha Makhua" (Circassian for "mountain of happiness") and "Mingi Taw" (Turkic for "eternal mountain"). Located in the western Caucasus, it is the highest peak of the range, situated on the border of the Russian republics of Kabardino-Balkaria and Karachay-Cherkessia. Its two dormant volcanic domes are separated by a saddle at 5,416 m; the western summit is 5,642 m and the eastern is 5,621 m.

### Geology and Eruptive History
Elbrus formed from the collision of the Arabian and Eurasian plates. Its main caldera formed around 700,000 years ago. The volcano is considered dormant, with its last eruption about AD 50. Evidence of recent activity includes fresh-looking lava flows, the longest extending 24 km, and ongoing solfataric activity and hot springs.

### Climate and Glaciation
The summit has an ice-cap climate. Winters are severe, with temperatures below -50°C and violent winds. Summer offers about 50% sunny days, but blizzard conditions can develop rapidly. The volcano's snowfields cover 138 km² and feed 22 main glaciers, all of which are receding.

### History and First Ascents
Ancient Greek mythology associated the Caucasus with Prometheus, and the peak Strobilos was later identified by some as Elbrus. The eastern summit was first climbed in 1829 by Khillar Khashirov, a guide for a Russian army expedition. The higher western summit was first reached in 1874 by a British-led team. During WWII, German forces occupied the area and briefly planted a flag on the summit in 1942, an act Hitler reportedly condemned as a "stunt." Soviet mountaineers removed the flags in 1943.

### Modern Mountaineering and Infrastructure
The normal route on the southern slope uses a cable car system (built 1959-1976, modernized later) to reach 3,800 m, followed by a climb to the summits. Accommodation includes the "Barrels" huts at 3,800 m and the Diesel refuge at 4,157 m. The climb is physically arduous due to altitude and weather, with an average annual death toll of 15–30, often from unorganized attempts. Speed races are held, with records for the long route (from Azau Meadow) including Karl Egloff's 2017 ascent/descent time of 4:20:45.

### Special Ascents
Elbrus has been climbed by vehicle (a Land Rover in 1997), on ATVs (2016), and multiple times with horses by Karachai riders, first in 1998. The mountain is also a destination for charity climbs.

## Terms
- **Stratovolcano**: A conical volcano built up by many layers of hardened lava and tephra.
- **Dormant volcano**: A volcano that is not currently erupting but is expected to erupt again.
- **Caldera**: A large cauldron-like hollow that forms shortly after the emptying of a magma chamber in a volcanic eruption.
- **Saddle**: The low point between two mountain summits.
- **Solfataric activity**: The emission of sulfurous gases from a volcano.
- **Normal route**: The most common and easiest path to a summit.
- **Crevasse**: A deep open crack, especially one in a glacier.

## Debates and Open Questions
The identification of the ancient Greek peak "Strobilos" with Elbrus is a scholarly interpretation, not a certainty. The exact number of main glaciers is debated (22 or 23 depending on the source). The geopolitical status of the mountain was briefly altered when it was incorporated into the Georgian SSR from 1944–1956.

# Dementia

## Overview
Dementia is an incurable, progressive neurocognitive syndrome characterized by a decline in cognitive processes—memory, thinking, behavior, and motor control—sufficient to impair daily activities. It is not a normal part of aging, though age is the greatest risk factor. Alzheimer's disease accounts for 60–70% of cases, followed by vascular dementia (at least 20%); mixed dementia (commonly Alzheimer's plus vascular) is now acknowledged as the most frequent presentation. As of 2025, dementia is the seventh leading cause of death globally, with 10 million new cases annually and an estimated 57 million people affected in 2021. While no cure exists, acetylcholinesterase inhibitors (e.g., donepezil) offer modest symptomatic benefit in some subtypes, and non-pharmacological interventions, person-centered care, and palliative approaches aim to maintain quality of life.

## Body

### Definition and Core Features
Dementia is a syndrome, not a specific disease, caused by neurodegenerative diseases (e.g., Alzheimer's), brain injuries, stroke, or other conditions. It involves a progressive decline in at least one cognitive domain (memory, language, attention, executive function, visuospatial skills) interfering with independence. Unlike age-related cognitive decline, intelligence remains intact initially. Neuropsychiatric symptoms (NPS)—affecting >90% of cases—include agitation, depression, hallucinations, apathy, and personality changes (increased neuroticism, decreased conscientiousness). Motor symptoms (gait changes, paratonia, parkinsonism) correlate with cognitive decline and drive dependency. Mild Behavioral Impairment (MBI) may precede cognitive symptoms and signal specific subtypes (e.g., disinhibition suggests frontotemporal dementia).

### Stages and Progression
The course is typically divided into three stages: **Early (Mild)**: Symptoms noticeable, interfere with complex tasks (finances, navigation), some help needed; advance care planning recommended. **Middle (Moderate)**: Pronounced memory loss, difficulty recognizing familiar people, repetitive questioning, loss of insight, significant support needed for daily activities. **Late (Severe)**: 24-hour supervision required; loss of speech, mobility, swallowing difficulties (dysphagia), incontinence, failure to recognize family. Feeding tubes are not recommended due to complications. **Prodromal/Preclinical**: Mild Cognitive Impairment (MCI), now termed "mild neurocognitive disorder" (DSM-5/ICD-11), involves subtle deficits not affecting independence; may revert, stabilize, or progress to dementia. Alzheimer's has an asymptomatic preclinical stage (Clinical Stage 1 as of 2024) detectable via biomarkers decades before symptoms. Staging tools include the 7-stage Global Deterioration Scale (GDS), Functional Assessment Staging Tool (FAST), and Clinical Dementia Rating (CDR).

### Major Types and Pathology
*   **Alzheimer's Dementia (60–70%)**: Hallmarks are extracellular amyloid-beta plaques and intracellular hyperphosphorylated tau neurofibrillary tangles. Medial temporal lobe (hippocampus, amygdala) is earliest site of atrophy. TREM2 malfunction impairs microglial debris clearance, triggering chronic inflammation. Diagnosed via CSF/blood biomarkers (p-tau217, flotillin), amyloid PET (Pittsburgh compound B, florbetapir), and tau PET (flortaucipir, approved 2024). Anti-amyloid immunotherapies (donanemab, lecanemab) target mild stages.
*   **Vascular Dementia (≥20%)**: Caused by cerebrovascular disease (mini-strokes, large vessel strokes). Symptoms depend on lesion location. Risk factors: hypertension, diabetes, atrial fibrillation, smoking. Frequently co-occurs with Alzheimer's as mixed dementia.
*   **Lewy Body Dementias**: Includes Dementia with Lewy Bodies (DLB) and Parkinson's Disease Dementia (PDD). Synucleinopathies featuring alpha-synuclein aggregates (Lewy bodies). DLB core features: fluctuating cognition, REM Sleep Behavior Disorder (RBD), parkinsonism, visual hallucinations (80%). RBD often precedes cognitive decline.
*   **Frontotemporal Dementias (FTDs)**: Young-onset (45–65). Behavioral variant (bvFTD): personality change, disinhibition, executive dysfunction from frontal/temporal atrophy. Primary Progressive Aphasia variants: semantic dementia (word meaning loss), nonfluent/agrammatic (speech production), logopenic (word retrieval/repetition). FTD-ALS overlaps with motor neuron disease. Progressive supranuclear palsy and corticobasal degeneration are tauopathy-related FTD disorders.
*   **Mixed Dementia**: Coexistence of ≥2 types (most commonly Alzheimer's + Vascular; second Alzheimer's + Lewy Body). Acknowledged in 2025 as most common type. Progresses faster, onset earlier, diagnosis difficult as one type often predominates.
*   **Other Types**: Hippocampal sclerosis (TDP-43, age >85); Autoimmune dementia (treatable with immunotherapy, contraindicates anti-amyloid mAbs); Childhood dementias (genetic); Early-onset (<65, ~7% of >65 prevalence); LATE (Limbic-predominant Age-related TDP-43 Encephalopathy, autopsy diagnosis, age >80).
*   **Secondary Dementias**: Chronic Traumatic Encephalopathy; Huntington's (HTT gene mutation, chorea, cognitive decline); HIV-associated Neurocognitive Disorder (HAND, motor/speech/behavioral); Prion diseases (rapid progression, weeks-months, e.g., Creutzfeldt-Jakob); Alcohol-related (thiamine deficiency, frontal/temporal/parietal/hippocampal atrophy).

### Diagnosis
Diagnosis relies on history, cognitive testing, blood tests (rule out reversible causes: hypothyroidism, B12 deficiency, neurosyphilis), and imaging. DSM-5 criteria: significant decline in ≥1 cognitive domain interfering with independence, not due to delirium or mental disorder. **Cognitive Tests**: MMSE (most studied), MoCA (better for MCI, free, multilingual), CognICA (5-min iPad app, FDA 2021), RUDAS (cross-cultural), clock drawing, Trail-making. Informant questionnaires: IQCODE, NPI (behavioral), AD-8 (screening only). **Imaging**: MRI/CT (structural: atrophy, strokes, tumors, hydrocephalus); FDG-PET (glucose metabolism: temporoparietal hypometabolism = Alzheimer's; frontal = FTD; occipital = DLB); Amyloid PET (plaques, decades pre-symptom); Tau PET (flortaucipir, 2024). Blood-based biomarkers (p-tau217) increasingly lead diagnosis.

### Prevention and Risk Factors
Lancet Commission (2017, 2020, 2024) identifies 14 modifiable risk factors: low education, hypertension, hearing loss, smoking, obesity, depression, physical inactivity, diabetes, low social contact, excessive alcohol, traumatic brain injury, air pollution, untreated visual impairment, high LDL cholesterol. Addressing all could prevent ~45% of cases. Psychological risks: high neuroticism, low conscientiousness, loneliness (increases risk 1/3 per ELSA), low purpose. Hearing loss (~9% global cases) may mediate social isolation. Sarcopenia/frailty bidirectionally linked to cognitive decline. **Diet**: Mediterranean, DASH, and MIND diets (low saturated fat, low glycemic carbs) associated with less decline; olive oil specifically linked to improved function. **Dental Health**: Poor oral health associated with decline (inflammation, chewing impairment), potentially causative, not just consequential. No medications prevent dementia; statins, antihypertensives, vitamins lack evidence. Population-level interventions (e.g., salt reduction) modeled to save costs and life-years.

### Management
**Pharmacological**: **Cholinesterase Inhibitors (Donepezil, Rivastigmine, Galantamine)**: Modest benefit in mild-moderate Alzheimer's, Lewy Body, Parkinson's dementia (cognition, function, global state); higher doses increase adverse events. **Memantine**: Small consistent benefit in moderate-severe Alzheimer's; combinable with ChEIs; no clear benefit in mild stages. **Antipsychotics**: Last resort for severe aggression/psychosis threatening safety. **Black box warning**: ~50% severe reactions in Lewy Body Dementia (neuroleptic malignant syndrome risk), increased stroke/death risk. **Antidepressants (SSRIs)**: Associated with faster cognitive decline, dose-dependent; complex interplay with severity. **Ginkgo biloba (EGb 761)**: Approved in Europe; Level B evidence (same as ChEIs/memantine); shows benefit in both Alzheimer's and Vascular dementia; neuroprotective (antioxidant, mitochondrial, monoamine modulation). **Sleep**: >40% report problems. Benzodiazepines avoided (falls, delirium, cognitive worsening). Melatonin/ramelteon for RBD in DLB. Low-dose trazodone limited evidence. **Pain**: ~80% in nursing homes experience pain; often expressed as behavioral symptoms. PAINAD scale aids assessment. **Communication**: Nonverbal cues (eye movement, expression) essential as speech declines; hearing loss in care homes often unaddressed.

**Non-Pharmacological**: **DICE Approach** (Describe, Investigate, Create, Evaluate) + caregiver training for neuropsychiatric symptoms. **Psychosocial**: Cognitive stimulation (mild-moderate), reminiscence therapy (quality of life, mood), music therapy (depressive symptoms, behavior), Montessori programs, animal-assisted therapy, massage/touch (agitation), personally tailored activities. **Person-Centered Care**: Focus on individual needs/values/history; reduces agitation, NPS, depression; improves staff burnout. **Exercise**: Improves daily function; optimal type unclear; multicomponent (aerobic, balance, strength) improves independence and reduces falls. **Assistive Tech**: Home automation, wearables, VR (memory/wellbeing), telehealth (assessment non-inferior to in-person), digital caregiver support. **Palliative Care**: Recommended early (diagnosis onward) for goals-of-care planning, symptom management, surrogate decision-making, avoiding crisis hospitalizations. Barriers: prognostic uncertainty, dementia not recognized as terminal, coordination gaps.

### Epidemiology and Inequalities
2021: 57 million cases globally; 10M new cases/year; doubling every 20 years (est. 150M+ by 2050). 60%+ in low/middle-income countries. Women: 2/3 of cases; higher caregiver burden; underrepresented in research. Prevalence: ~7% >65 (up to 10% in high longevity regions). Incidence doubles every 6.3 years. Median survival: 6.7 years (dx 60–69) to 1.9 years (dx 90+). **Inequalities**: Low SES = higher risk, earlier onset, more hospitalizations, inappropriate meds, higher mortality. Rural/regional access disparities. Racial/ethnic minorities: higher risk factor burden (hypertension), lower quality care, less anti-dementia prescribing, lower adherence (distrust, stigma, autonomy concerns). Global policy gap: 2025 target 75% WHO states with plans; actual 23% (45 countries). **Country Data**: UK: ~1M cases (2024), leading cause of death (2022/23), cost £42.5B (2024) → £90.3B (2040). US: Deaths tripled 1999–2020 (450K); 5th leading cause (2024); $384B formal + $413B informal care (2025); stroke doubles dementia risk. Australia: 425K cases (2024); leading cause of death women (2023).

### History and Terminology
Antiquity: Pythagoras (senium), Solon (legal capacity), Chinese texts ("foolish old person"). Aristotle/Plato: decline inevitable. Cicero: decline not inevitable, activity protective (ignored for centuries). Byzantine: emperors with decline; special hospitals. 19th Century: Cerebral atherosclerosis theory. 1907: Alois Alzheimer identifies plaques/tangles in Auguste Deter (age 51). 1910: Kraepelin names "Alzheimer's disease." 1913–1952: "Dementia praecox" = schizophrenia. 1952: Schizophrenia removed from organic causes; vascular dementias re-emerge as multi-infarct. 1976: Katzmann equates senile dementia with Alzheimer's, notes high mortality. 1994: Reagan announcement spikes awareness. 21st Century: Differentiation via pathology, symptomatology, PET/SPECT imaging. Terminology evolution: "Senile dementia" → "SDAT" (age >65) / "Alzheimer's" (age <65) → unified "Alzheimer's disease" regardless of age.

### Society, Culture, and Economics
**Costs**: Global ~$1.3T (2021). US: $384B formal + $413B informal (2025); lifetime $405K. UK: £28.7K (mild) → £80.5K (moderate) per person/year; 60%+ borne by patients/families. Low/middle income: 42% direct, 58% informal (likely underestimated). **Awareness/Stigma**: 2024: 80% public, 65% professionals believe dementia is normal aging (up from 66%/62% in 2019). Only 42% UK public know it is terminal (2022). 88% patients report discrimination (2024). High-profile diagnoses (Reagan, Willis, Williams, Hayworth, Parks, Bennett, Wendy Williams) drive awareness. **Philanthropy**: Atlantic Philanthropies $177M (2015, Global Brain Health Institute); Caretaker album *Everywhere at the End of Time* (2020, TikTok viral); Tom Hunter £1M (2020).

### Research Directions
Co-research standards (Dementia Enquirers Gold Standards) for involving people with impaired capacity. EMPOWER Dementia Network+ (UK) targets inequalities via co-production. 2026 NIA Dementia Care Summit. AI/ML for early diagnosis and planning. Kynurenine pathway (tryptophan metabolite) disruption linked to neuropsychiatric symptoms/cognitive prognosis in mild Alzheimer's (lower kynurenines); further research needed.

## Terms
- ****Neurocognitive Disorder**** — DSM-5 term replacing "dementia" (major) and "MCI" (mild); emphasizes cognitive decline from a previous level.
- ****Mild Behavioral Impairment (MBI)**** — Late-life onset of sustained neuropsychiatric symptoms (apathy, disinhibition, agitation) without cognitive impairment; may precede dementia.
- ****Prodromal Stage**** — Pre-dementia phase (Mild Cognitive Impairment / Mild Neurocognitive Disorder) with subtle deficits not yet affecting independence.
- ****Paratonia**** — Involuntary, variable resistance to passive movement (inability to relax muscles); common motor sign in dementia, distinct from parkinsonian rigidity.
- ****Synucleinopathy**** — Neurodegenerative disease characterized by abnormal aggregation of alpha-synuclein protein (Lewy bodies); includes DLB, PDD, MSA.
- ****Tauopathy**** — Neurodegenerative disease characterized by abnormal aggregation of hyperphosphorylated tau protein (neurofibrillary tangles); includes Alzheimer's, PSP, CBD.
- ****Mixed Dementia**** — Coexistence of two or more dementia pathologies (most commonly Alzheimer's + Vascular); acknowledged 2025 as most common type.
- ****Amyloid PET / Tau PET**** — Positron Emission Tomography using radiotracers (e.g., florbetapir, flortaucipir) to visualize amyloid plaques or tau tangles in vivo.
- ****Person-Centered Care**** — Care model prioritizing individual's history, preferences, and relationships to maintain dignity/identity; distinct from patient participation.
- ****Palliative Care**** — Holistic, team-based care focusing on quality of life, symptom management, and goal-concordant care at all stages, not just end-of-life.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Mixed Dementia Prevalence vs. Diagnosis**: Acknowledged as most common type (2025 NIH), yet clinical diagnosis remains difficult as one pathology often masks others, potentially denying patients subtype-specific treatments.
*   **Anti-Amyloid Immunotherapy Risk-Benefit**: Donanemab/lecanemab show modest slowing in early stages but carry ARIA (amyloid-related imaging abnormalities) risks; long-term clinical meaningfulness and cost-effectiveness debated.
*   **Antidepressant Use**: SSRIs associated with accelerated cognitive decline in a dose-dependent manner; unclear if this reflects confounding by indication (depression severity) or direct neurotoxicity.
*   **Antipsychotic Withdrawal**: Evidence suggests stopping antipsychotics often causes no deterioration, yet they remain widely prescribed for behavioral symptoms despite mortality risks, especially in Lewy Body Dementia.
*   **Palliative Care Timing**: Uncertainty regarding optimal referral triggers; dementia often not recognized as terminal, leading to late referral and crisis-driven hospitalizations.
*   **Kynurenine Pathway**: Lower kynurenine metabolites correlate with Alzheimer's neuropsychiatric symptoms; causal mechanism and therapeutic target validation required.
*   **Health Equity**: Persistent disparities in diagnosis, care access, research inclusion, and outcomes for racial/ethnic minorities, low-SES, rural populations, and women (who bear disproportionate burden as patients and caregivers).
*   **Prevention Translation**: While ~45% of cases theoretically preventable via 14 modifiable factors, population-level implementation (e.g., hearing aid provision, air pollution control, education equity) remains inconsistent globally.

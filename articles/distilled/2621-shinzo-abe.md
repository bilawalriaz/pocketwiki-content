# Shinzo Abe

## Overview
Shinzo Abe (1954–2022) was Japan's longest-serving prime minister, serving nearly nine years across two terms. A staunch conservative, he sought to strengthen Japan's security, revitalize its economy through "Abenomics," and revise its pacifist constitution. His legacy is polarizing, marked by both a more assertive international role and controversial historical revisionism that strained relations with neighboring countries.

## Timeline
- **21 Sep 1954** — Born in Tokyo to a prominent political family.
- **1993** — Elected to the House of Representatives.
- **26 Sep 2006** — Becomes Prime Minister for the first time (age 52).
- **12 Sep 2007** — Resigns as Prime Minister due to illness and political setbacks.
- **26 Dec 2012** — Returns as Prime Minister after leading the LDP to a landslide election victory.
- **Dec 2013** — Visits the controversial Yasukuni Shrine, drawing international criticism.
- **Jul 2014** — Cabinet reinterprets the constitution to allow for "collective self-defense."
- **Sep 2015** — Legislation for Peace and Security passes, allowing overseas deployment of the Self-Defense Forces.
- **Nov 2019** — Becomes Japan's longest-serving prime minister by days in office.
- **28 Aug 2020** — Resigns again, citing a relapse of ulcerative colitis.
- **8 Jul 2022** — Assassinated while delivering a campaign speech in Nara.

## Body

### Early Life and Political Rise
Born into a political dynasty, Abe was the son of Foreign Minister Shintaro Abe and grandson of Prime Minister Nobusuke Kishi, a former "Class-A" war criminal suspect. He entered politics in 1993, quickly gaining prominence for his hawkish stance on North Korea, particularly regarding Japanese abductees. As Chief Cabinet Secretary under Junichiro Koizumi, he gained national popularity for his tough negotiating position, which secured the abductees' permanent return to Japan.

### First Premiership (2006–2007)
Abe became Japan's youngest post-war prime minister in 2006. His first term focused on strengthening the U.S.-Japan alliance, improving ties with India, and initiating the Quadrilateral Security Dialogue (Quad) with the U.S., Australia, and India to counter China's rise. Domestically, he sought to revise the pacifist Article 9 of the constitution and downplay Japanese wartime atrocities, including denying government coercion in the recruitment of "comfort women." His popularity plummeted due to political scandals and poor health, leading to his resignation in 2007.

### Second Premiership and "Abenomics" (2012–2020)
Abe returned to power in 2012, launching "Abenomics," an economic strategy based on three "arrows": aggressive monetary easing, flexible fiscal policy, and structural reforms. The policy initially boosted the stock market and weakened the yen but had mixed long-term success in ending deflation. His second term saw a more assertive foreign policy, including the controversial 2015 Legislation for Peace and Security, which allowed for limited collective self-defense. He also navigated complex relations with China and South Korea, achieving a 2015 agreement with Seoul on the comfort women issue, though ties later deteriorated over trade and historical disputes.

### Assassination and Legacy
In 2022, Abe was assassinated by a man who confessed he targeted Abe due to the former prime minister's perceived ties to the Unification Church. His legacy is complex: supporters credit him with strengthening Japan's security posture and international stature, while critics condemn his historical revisionism and nationalist policies, which they argue damaged regional relations and undermined Japan's post-war pacifist identity.

## Terms
*   **Abenomics:** Shinzo Abe's economic policy framework, consisting of three "arrows": monetary easing, fiscal stimulus, and structural reforms.
*   **Article 9:** The clause in Japan's post-war constitution that renounces war and prohibits the maintenance of military forces, which Abe sought to revise.
*   **Collective Self-Defense:** The right of a country to use military force to defend an ally under attack, even if not directly threatened itself. Japan reinterpreted its constitution to allow this in 2014.
*   **Comfort Women:** A euphemism for women, primarily from Korea and China, who were forced into sexual slavery by the Imperial Japanese Army before and during World War II.
*   **Nippon Kaigi:** A conservative, nationalist organization in Japan that advocates for historical revisionism and a stronger national identity, of which Abe was a member.
*   **Quadrilateral Security Dialogue (Quad):** A strategic forum comprising Japan, the United States, Australia, and India, initiated by Abe in 2007 to counter China's influence.
*   **Yasukuni Shrine:** A Shinto shrine in Tokyo that honors Japan's war dead, including convicted Class-A war criminals. Visits by Japanese leaders are highly controversial with neighboring countries.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Historical Revisionism:** Scholars and neighboring nations debate whether Abe's statements on comfort women, the Nanjing Massacre, and his Yasukuni visits constituted a dangerous denial of historical facts or a legitimate effort to move beyond what he called a "masochistic" view of history.
*   **Effectiveness of Abenomics:** Economists continue to assess whether Abenomics successfully revitalized Japan's economy or merely provided a temporary boost, leaving underlying issues like deflation and demographic decline unresolved.
*   **Constitutional Revision:** Abe's lifelong goal of revising Article 9 remains unfulfilled. The debate continues over whether such a change is necessary for Japan's security or would fundamentally alter its pacifist identity.
*   **Regional Relations:** The long-term impact of Abe's assertive foreign policy on stability in East Asia is debated, with arguments that it either strengthened deterrence against China or unnecessarily escalated tensions.

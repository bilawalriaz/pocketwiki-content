# Patna

## Overview
Patna, historically known as Pataliputra, is the capital and largest city of the Indian state of Bihar. Founded in 490 BCE, it is one of the oldest continuously inhabited places in the world and served as the seat of power for major Indian empires, including the Maurya and Gupta. Today, it is a major riverine city, a center of trade and administration, and a sacred site for Sikhs, Hindus, Buddhists, and Jains.

## Timeline
- **490 BCE** — Founded by King Ajatashatru of Magadha as a strategic fortification.
- **c. 300 BCE** — Capital of the Maurya Empire; described by Megasthenes as a great city.
- **399–414 CE** — Visited by Chinese Buddhist monk Faxian, who noted its prosperity and hospitals.
- **12th century** — Captured by Bakhtiar Khilji, leading to a decline in its prestige.
- **16th century** — Revived by Afghan emperor Sher Shah Suri, who built a fort and town.
- **1620** — Described as the "chiefest mart towne of all Bengala" by the English.
- **1704** — Renamed Azimabad by Mughal Emperor Aurangzeb.
- **1763** — Site of the Patna massacre, where Nawab Mir Qasim killed British East India Company employees.
- **1793** — Annexed by the British East India Company.
- **1912** — Became the capital of the new province of Bihar and Orissa.
- **2000** — Remained Bihar's capital after the state was partitioned, creating Jharkhand.

## Body

### Ancient and Medieval History
Patna was founded in 490 BCE by Ajatashatru of Magadha to combat the Licchavis. It became the capital of successive empires, including the Maurya, Shunga, Gupta, and Pala dynasties. During the Maurya period (c. 300 BCE), its population was about 400,000. It was a renowned seat of learning, home to scholars like Aryabhata and Chanakya. After the fall of the Gupta Empire and the 12th-century invasion by Bakhtiar Khilji, Patna lost its political and cultural preeminence.

### Mughal, Colonial, and Modern Era
Under the Mughals, Patna was revived as a commercial center, especially by Sher Shah Suri. By 1620, it was a major international trade hub for saltpetre, silk, and calico. The British East India Company established a factory there in 1620. After the Battle of Buxar (1764), the Company gained control, and in 1912, Patna became the capital of Bihar and Orissa. Post-independence, its economy experienced fluctuations but has seen sustained growth since 2005, particularly in services and manufacturing.

### Geography and Climate
Patna is located on the southern bank of the Ganges and straddles the Son, Gandak, and Punpun rivers, making it the world's largest riverine city. It covers 250 km² and has a humid subtropical climate with hot summers, a monsoon season, and cool winters. The city is in seismic zone-IV and is vulnerable to floods and cyclones. It faces severe air pollution, with PM-2.5 levels recorded at six times the safe limit in a 2014 WHO survey.

### Economy and Demographics
The economy is driven by agriculture, trade, and a growing service sector. Major exports include grain, sugarcane, and Patna rice. The city is an emerging IT hub. As of the 2011 census, the city population was 1.68 million, with a literacy rate of 83.37%. Hindi and Urdu are official languages, but Magahi is the native tongue. The city has a low percentage of slum dwellers but faces high unemployment, particularly among women.

### Administration and Infrastructure
Patna is governed by the Patna Municipal Corporation (PMC) and is the seat of the Bihar state government and the Patna High Court. The city is connected by major national highways and railways, with Patna Junction being one of India's busiest stations. The Patna Metro is an operational rapid transit system. The Ganges is a key trade route via National Waterway-1.

## Terms
- **Pataliputra**: The ancient name for Patna, capital of the Maurya and Gupta empires.
- **Magadha**: An ancient Indian kingdom, the core of which was in modern-day Bihar, south of the Ganges.
- **Alluvial soil**: Fertile soil deposited by rivers, ideal for agriculture.
- **Humid subtropical climate**: A climate type with hot, humid summers and mild winters.
- **PM-2.5**: Fine particulate matter in the air with a diameter of 2.5 micrometers or less, a key indicator of air pollution.
- **Patna Municipal Corporation (PMC)**: The civic body responsible for the city's infrastructure and public services.
- **National Waterway-1**: The longest inland waterway in India, running along the Ganges from Allahabad to Haldia.
- **Magahi**: The native language of the Patna region, derived from ancient Magadhi Prakrit.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The exact age of Patna is debated; while traditional accounts cite 490 BCE, some scholars suggest its wooden fortifications indicate it may be much older, pending archaeological confirmation.
- The origin of the modern name "Patna" has multiple theories, including derivation from the goddess Patan Devi or the *patli* tree.

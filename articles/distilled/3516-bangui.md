# Bangui

## Overview
Bangui is the capital and largest city of the Central African Republic (CAR), serving as the nation's administrative, commercial, and transport hub. Situated on the Ubangi River, its history as a French colonial outpost evolved into a turbulent post-independence era marked by political instability, coups, and conflict, which have profoundly shaped its development and current challenges.

## Timeline
- **c. 9th century BC** — Archaeological evidence of Iron Age metallurgy found near Bangui.
- **26 June 1889** — Modern Bangui founded by French administrators Michel Dolisie and Alfred Uzac.
- **1906** — Bangui chosen as the administrative headquarters for the French colony.
- **1958** — Ubangi-Shari (CAR) gains autonomy from France.
- **1960** — Central African Republic becomes independent.
- **1966** — Jean-Bédel Bokassa seizes power in a coup.
- **1970** — University of Bangui inaugurated.
- **1979** — Bokassa is overthrown in a French-backed operation (Operation Caban).
- **1996** — Soldiers mutiny in Bangui, leading to widespread violence.
- **2003** — François Bozizé seizes power after a rebellion.
- **2013** — Séléka rebels capture Bangui, forcing Bozizé to flee.
- **2021** — Rebels attack Bangui; one peacekeeper is killed before they are repelled.

## Body

### Founding and Colonial Era
The modern settlement was established in 1889 on the northern bank of the Ubangi River, named for the "rapids" (Bobangi word) that marked the limit of navigation from Brazzaville. Its strategic location led to it becoming the French administrative headquarters in 1906. The city remained a key military and administrative center through World War II, operating what was described in 1932 as "the most remote radio station in Africa."

### Post-Independence and Political Instability
After independence in 1960, Bangui became the stage for the country's political turmoil. President Bokassa's extravagant rule, including the inauguration of the University of Bangui in 1970, led to economic decline and a rupture with France. Following his overthrow in 1979, the city experienced further instability under Presidents Dacko, Kolingba, and Patassé, marked by coups, rebellions, and donor-driven democratic transitions. A 1996 soldier mutiny in Bangui resulted in over 50 deaths.

### 21st Century Conflict
The 2003 coup by François Bozizé brought a brief period of relative stability, but a rebellion by the Séléka coalition in 2012-2013 culminated in the capture of Bangui in March 2013. This triggered severe sectarian violence, displacing nearly half the city's population by early 2014. The city has since faced sporadic attacks, including a 2021 assault by rebels.

### Geography, Climate, and Administration
Bangui lies on the Ubangi River, which forms the border with the Democratic Republic of the Congo. It has a tropical savanna climate (Köppen: Aw) with a rainy season prone to flooding. Administratively, it is an autonomous commune divided into eight arrondissements. The Bangui Magnetic Anomaly, a major geological feature, is centered near the city.

### Economy and Infrastructure
As the national commercial center, Bangui's economy relies on trade, manufacturing (textiles, food, beer), and handling exports like cotton, rubber, and diamonds. The river port is vital for international trade. The city is served by Bangui M'Poko International Airport and a road network, though many routes are impassable during the rainy season. Unemployment was near 23% as of 2001.

### Society and Culture
The population is diverse, including various ethnic groups and foreign traders. French and Sango are official languages. The city's culture features traditional crafts, a vibrant music scene influenced by Congolese rumba, and popular sports like basketball and football. Landmarks include the Boganda Museum, Notre-Dame Cathedral, and the former Bokassa Palace.

## Terms
- **Arrondissement**: An administrative district; Bangui is divided into eight.
- **Bangui Definition**: A 1985 clinical definition of AIDS symptoms (prolonged fever, weight loss, diarrhea) developed in the city, later found problematic.
- **Groupement**: A sub-division of an arrondissement; Bangui has 16.
- **Kodro**: A traditional house in Bangui's suburbs, built of mud bricks with a thatched roof.
- **Séléka**: A coalition of rebel groups that seized power in CAR in 2013.
- **Ubangi River**: A major tributary of the Congo River, forming the border with the DRC and vital for transport.
- **Bangui Magnetic Anomaly**: A large, unexplained geological feature centered near the city.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The origins of the Bangui Magnetic Anomaly are not fully understood.
- The efficacy and long-term impact of the Bangui Definition for AIDS in the African context remain debated, as it can confuse symptoms with those of malnutrition.

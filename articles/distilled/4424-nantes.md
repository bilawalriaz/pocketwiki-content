# Nantes

## Overview
Nantes is a major city in western France on the Loire River, historically the capital of the Duchy of Brittany. Its identity and economy have been shaped by its river port, its role in the Atlantic slave trade, and a modern transition to a service and creative economy. The city is culturally part of Brittany but administratively in the Pays de la Loire region, a source of ongoing debate.

## Timeline
- **c. 100 BC** — Settlement of the Namnetes tribe on the Loire.
- **851** — Captured by the Bretons, becoming part of Brittany.
- **1460** — University of Nantes founded by Duke Francis II.
- **1532** — Union of Brittany and France; Rennes becomes provincial capital.
- **1598** — Edict of Nantes signed by Henry IV, ending French Wars of Religion.
- **18th C.** — Nantes becomes France's largest port and a center of the slave trade.
- **1793-94** — Reign of Terror under Jean-Baptiste Carrier; thousands executed.
- **1851** — Connected to Paris by railway, spurring industrial growth.
- **1943** — Major Allied bombing raids destroy much of the city.
- **1985** — First modern tramway reintroduced in France.
- **2013** — Awarded European Green Capital Award.

## Body

### Historical Development
The city was founded by the Gaulish Namnetes tribe and later became a Roman port. After the fall of Rome, it was contested by Franks and Bretons, eventually becoming the primary residence of the Dukes of Brittany in the 15th century. The 1532 union with France diminished its political status to Rennes, but it prospered as a major port in the 18th century, becoming central to the French Atlantic slave trade. The French Revolution caused severe economic decline and a period of mass executions. The 19th century saw industrialization in shipbuilding and food processing, followed by post-WWII deindustrialization. Since the 1980s, Nantes has reinvented itself as a service-based, creative, and green city.

### Geography and Climate
Nantes is located on the Loire estuary, 50 km from the Atlantic. The city was historically built on numerous islands created by river channels, most of which were filled in during the 20th century. It sits on the Armorican Massif, with the granite Sillon de Bretagne ridge forming cliffs in the west. The climate is oceanic (Köppen: Cfb), with mild, rainy winters and warm summers.

### Governance and the Brittany Question
Nantes is the prefecture of the Loire-Atlantique department and the Pays de la Loire region. However, it is historically and culturally part of Brittany. Its exclusion from the modern Brittany region, created in the 1950s, remains controversial. Polls show majority support in Loire-Atlantique for reunification with Brittany, but political and economic objections persist.

### Economy and Transport
The economy shifted from port and industry to services, aeronautics (Airbus), and creative industries. The Nantes metropolitan area is a major economic hub with strong job growth. Transport is centered on a major railway station, an airport, and an extensive public transit network (trams, buses, river shuttles) managed by Semitan. Nantes was the first French city to reintroduce trams in the modern era (1985).

### Culture and Landmarks
Key landmarks include the Gothic Castle of the Dukes of Brittany and the 15th-century cathedral. The city is known for the Machines of the Isle of Nantes, a contemporary art and mechanical attraction. It hosts major festivals like La Folle Journée (classical music) and has a vibrant cultural scene. The city's cuisine features specialties like *beurre blanc* sauce and Muscadet wine.

## Terms
*   **Namnetes:** The Gaulish tribe that founded the settlement which became Nantes.
*   **Duchy of Brittany:** A historical duchy in northwest France; Nantes was its primary capital in the 15th century.
*   **Edict of Nantes:** A 1598 decree granting rights to Protestants in France, signed in Nantes.
*   **Triangular Trade:** The 18th-century trade system linking Europe, Africa, and the Americas, central to Nantes's port economy.
*   **Reign of Terror:** The radical phase of the French Revolution (1793-94) marked by mass executions, including in Nantes.
*   **Pays de la Loire:** The modern administrative region containing Nantes, created in the 1950s.
*   **Sillon de Bretagne:** A granite ridge forming a natural landmark in western Nantes.
*   **Semitan ("Tan"):** The public transport authority for the Nantes metropolitan area.

## Debates and open questions
The primary ongoing debate concerns the administrative status of Nantes and the Loire-Atlantique department. A significant portion of the population supports reunification with the Brittany region, but this is opposed by officials in the Pays de la Loire region, who argue the region would be economically weakened without Nantes. The city council acknowledges its Breton cultural ties but maintains a position of openness, considering Nantes an independent metropolis.

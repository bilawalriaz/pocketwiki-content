# Eastern Arabia

## Overview
Eastern Arabia is a historical region along the Persian Gulf coast, encompassing parts of modern Bahrain, Iraq, Kuwait, Oman, Qatar, Saudi Arabia, and the UAE. For centuries, its people shared a unified, sea-oriented culture and identity, moving freely across what are now national borders. The region's history is marked by its role as a major trade hub between Mesopotamia and the Indus Valley, and by successive waves of control by Persian, Arab, and Islamic powers.

## Timeline
- **c. 3000 BC** — Kingdom of Dilmun first appears in Sumerian records.
- **c. 2200–1600 BC** — Dilmun's "Golden Age" as a major trade center.
- **6th–3rd century BC** — Bahrain (Tylos) is part of the Achaemenid Persian Empire.
- **3rd century BC–3rd century AD** — Parthian Empire controls the Persian Gulf coast.
- **3rd–7th century AD** — Sasanian Empire rules the region, naming it Mazun.
- **5th century** — Beth Qatraye becomes a major center for Nestorian Christianity.
- **628 CE** — Eastern Arabia embraces Islam.
- **10th century** — Qarmatian Republic, a utopian Ismaili state, dominates the region.
- **1077–1253 AD** — Uyunid dynasty rules eastern Arabia.
- **1253–1320 AD** — Usfurid dynasty gains control.
- **14th century** — Jarwanid dynasty rules from Qatif.
- **15th–16th centuries** — Jabrid dynasty dominates the entire Gulf coast.
- **1670** — Bani Khalid tribe expels the Ottomans and rules the region.
- **1793** — The First Saudi State invades and deposes the Bani Khalid.

## Body

### Ancient Kingdoms and Trade
Eastern Arabia's recorded history begins with the Kingdom of Dilmun, a major trade intermediary between Mesopotamia and the Indus Valley civilization (Meluhha). Archaeological evidence, including Indus Valley seals found in Mesopotamia and Dilmun seals in India, confirms extensive commerce in goods like timber, ivory, and copper. Dilmun held a monopoly on this trade by c. 1900 BC and is mythologized in Sumerian texts as a paradisiacal land. After Dilmun's decline, the ancient city of Gerrha (possibly near modern Al-Aḥsā) became a significant trading post. The island of Bahrain, known to Greeks as Tylos, was part of the Achaemenid and later Hellenistic worlds, noted for cotton production and pearls.

### Persian and Early Islamic Rule
From the 3rd century BC, the region was controlled by Iranian dynasties. The Parthians established garrisons to secure trade routes. The succeeding Sasanians organized the area into the province of Mazun, subdivided into districts like Haggar (Al-Aḥsā) and Mishmahig (Bahrain). By the 5th century, the region, known in Syriac as Beth Qatraye, was a major center for Nestorian Christianity. Following the rise of Islam in the 7th century, Eastern Arabia was among the first regions to convert, in 628 CE. The area's trade-based prosperity continued under the Abbasid Caliphate.

### Islamic Dynasties
The Qarmatian Revolution (late 3rd century AH) established a radical Ismaili republic with its capital in Al-Aḥsā. The Qarmatians created a wealthy, egalitarian society funded by a slave-based economy and tribute, but were defeated by the Abbasids in 976. Their decline led to a succession of Arab dynasties: the Uyunids (11th–13th centuries), the Usfurids (13th–14th centuries), and the Shi'ite Jarwanids (14th century). The Jabrids dominated in the 15th–16th centuries, controlling the entire Gulf coast until their defeat by the Portuguese and internal collapse. Finally, the Bani Khalid tribe expelled the Ottomans in 1670 and ruled until their power was broken by the First Saudi State invasion in 1793.

## Terms
- **Dilmun**: An ancient Bronze Age civilization centered in Bahrain, acting as a key trade hub between Mesopotamia and the Indus Valley.
- **Tylos**: The ancient Greek name for the Bahrain archipelago, known for its pearl fisheries and cotton production.
- **Beth Qatraye**: The Syriac Christian name for northeastern Arabia, a major center for Nestorian Christianity before Islam.
- **Qarmatians**: A messianic Ismaili sect that established a utopian republic in eastern Arabia in the 10th century.
- **Mazun**: The Sasanian Persian name for the province of eastern Arabia.
- **Gulf Arabic (Khaleeji)**: The dialect of Arabic spoken in Eastern Arabia, forming a key part of the regional identity.
- **Bani Khalid**: An Arab tribal confederation that dominated eastern Arabia in the 17th and 18th centuries before being overthrown by the Saudis.

## Debates and open questions
- The exact location of the ancient city of Gerrha is debated, with candidates including sites near Al-Aḥsā or within the Bahrain archipelago.
- The identity of the "Two Seas" referenced in the name *al-Baḥrayn* (Bahrain) is disputed, with theories pointing to the salt and fresh waters of the region, or specific bays.
- The religious affiliation of the Jarwanid dynasty is contested; while some sources describe them as Ismailis or "extreme Shi'ites," modern scholarship often concludes they were Twelver Shi'ites.

# Václav Havel

## Overview
Václav Havel (1936–2011) was a Czech playwright, dissident, and statesman who became a pivotal figure in the peaceful overthrow of communism in Central Europe. He served as the last president of Czechoslovakia (1989–1992) and the first president of the Czech Republic (1993–2003), championing democracy, human rights, and civil society. His life and work embody the transition from authoritarian rule to democratic governance in the post-communist era.

## Timeline
- **5 October 1936** — Born in Prague into a wealthy, entrepreneurial family.
- **1963** — First major play, *The Garden Party*, premiered, gaining international acclaim.
- **1968** — Participated in the Prague Spring; blacklisted after the Warsaw Pact invasion.
- **1978** — Published seminal essay *The Power of the Powerless*.
- **1979–1983** — Longest imprisonment as a political prisoner.
- **1977** — Co-founded Charter 77 dissident initiative.
- **29 December 1989** — Elected President of Czechoslovakia by the Federal Assembly.
- **1990** — Won Czechoslovakia's first free elections in 44 years with Civic Forum.
- **20 July 1992** — Resigned as Czechoslovak president, refusing to preside over the country's dissolution.
- **26 January 1993** — Elected first President of the Czech Republic.
- **2 February 2003** — Left office after his second term as Czech president.
- **18 December 2011** — Died at his country home in Hrádeček, aged 75.

## Body

### Early Life and Literary Career
Havel's educational and professional opportunities were limited by his bourgeois family background under the communist regime. After an apprenticeship and brief economics studies, he entered Prague's theatre world. His absurdist plays, such as *The Garden Party* (1963) and *The Memorandum* (1965), used satire to critique the Communist system, earning him international recognition. Following the 1968 Warsaw Pact invasion, his works were banned in Czechoslovakia, pushing him toward political dissidence.

### Political Dissidence
After 1968, Havel became a leading dissident. He helped found Charter 77, a human rights manifesto, and the Committee for the Defense of the Unjustly Prosecuted. His philosophy, articulated in essays like *The Power of the Powerless* (1978), argued that citizens under communism were forced to "live within a lie." His activism led to multiple imprisonments, the longest from 1979 to 1983, documented in *Letters to Olga*. His dissident reputation was cemented by his support for the persecuted rock band The Plastic People of the Universe.

### Presidency and the Velvet Revolution
Havel's Civic Forum party was instrumental in the 1989 Velvet Revolution that toppled Czechoslovak communism. He became president by a unanimous vote. His presidency focused on dismantling the Warsaw Pact and guiding the country toward democracy. He oversaw the 1990 free elections and the 1993 dissolution of Czechoslovakia, after which he became the Czech Republic's first president. Domestically, his policies were controversial: he granted a general amnesty to political prisoners, which critics linked to a rise in crime, and condemned the post-WWII expulsion of Sudeten Germans. He was a key advocate for NATO enlargement eastward.

### Post-Presidency and Legacy
After leaving office in 2003, Havel remained a public intellectual, launching initiatives like the annual Forum 2000 conference and the VIZE 97 Foundation. He continued writing, premiering his play *Leaving* in 2008. His political philosophy emphasized anti-consumerism, environmentalism, and direct democracy. He received numerous international awards, including the Presidential Medal of Freedom and the Gandhi Peace Prize. The Prague airport was renamed Václav Havel Airport Prague in 2012, and a bust of him was placed in the U.S. Capitol in 2014.

## Terms
- **Velvet Revolution**: The non-violent transition of power in Czechoslovakia in 1989 that ended communist rule.
- **Charter 77**: A dissident human rights initiative launched in 1977, co-founded by Havel, that criticized the government for failing to implement human rights treaties.
- **Samizdat**: The clandestine copying and distribution of banned literature in communist states.
- **StB**: The Czechoslovak State Security secret police, responsible for surveillance and repression of dissidents.
- **Civic Forum**: The anti-communist political movement and party led by Havel that won the 1990 elections.
- **Sudeten Germans**: The ethnic German population expelled from Czechoslovakia after World War II, a historical issue Havel addressed controversially.
- **Warsaw Pact**: The military alliance of communist states in Eastern Europe, dissolved in 1991, which Havel helped dismantle.
- **Absurdist theatre**: A dramatic style using illogical scenarios to critique societal norms, employed by Havel in his early plays.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Amnesty Impact**: Havel's general amnesty for political prisoners was criticized for allegedly causing a significant increase in crime rates, with total crimes and murders reportedly doubling.
- **Sudeten German Expulsion**: His public condemnation of the post-WWII expulsion of Sudeten Germans was highly controversial domestically, challenging a long-held national narrative.
- **Presidential Power**: While the Czech constitution vested most power in the prime minister, Havel's personal prestige expanded the moral authority and practical role of the presidency beyond its intended limits.
- **Political Legacy**: Some, like Communist Party leader Vojtěch Filip, viewed Havel as a controversial figure whose words sometimes conflicted with his deeds, particularly regarding foreign policy like the NATO bombing of Yugoslavia.

# Ferdinand II of Aragon

## Overview
Ferdinand II of Aragon, known as Ferdinand the Catholic, was a foundational monarch of Spain. As co-ruler with his wife Isabella I of Castile, their marriage united the crowns of Aragon and Castile, creating a personal union that formed the basis of modern Spain. Their reign saw the completion of the Reconquista, the sponsorship of Christopher Columbus's voyage, and the establishment of Spain as a major European and global power.

## Timeline
- **10 March 1452** — Born in Sos del Rey Católico, Kingdom of Aragon.
- **19 October 1469** — Marries Isabella of Castile in Valladolid.
- **1474** — Becomes King of Castile (jure uxoris) upon Isabella's accession.
- **1479** — Succeeds his father as King of Aragon, uniting the two crowns in a personal union.
- **1492** — Completes the conquest of Granada; issues the Alhambra Decree expelling Jews; sponsors Columbus's first voyage.
- **1494** — Treaty of Tordesillas divides the non-European world with Portugal.
- **1502** — Forces Muslims in Castile and Aragon to convert to Christianity.
- **26 November 1504** — Isabella I dies; Ferdinand becomes regent of Castile for their daughter Joanna.
- **1506** — Marries Germaine of Foix; Treaty of Villafáfila briefly cedes Castile to Philip the Handsome.
- **1512** — Conquers most of the Kingdom of Navarre.
- **23 January 1516** — Dies in Madrigalejo; succeeded by Joanna, with Charles I (his grandson) soon assuming power.

## Body
**Marriage and the Catholic Monarchs**
Ferdinand's 1469 marriage to Isabella was a dynastic alliance between two members of the House of Trastámara, governed by a prenuptial agreement of shared power. Their joint rule, symbolized by the motto "tanto monta," created a personal union of the Crowns of Castile and Aragon. While legally separate until 1707, they acted as a unified monarchy, centralizing power and completing the Reconquista with the fall of Granada in 1492.

**Religious Policy and the Americas**
The Catholic Monarchs pursued a policy of religious uniformity. In 1492, they issued the Alhambra Decree, expelling unconverted Jews. In 1502, Ferdinand abrogated the Treaty of Granada's protections for Muslims, forcing their conversion or expulsion. Concurrently, their sponsorship of Christopher Columbus in 1492 initiated the Spanish colonization of the Americas. The Treaty of Tordesillas (1494) with Portugal formalized the division of newly discovered lands.

**Italian Wars and Later Reign**
After Isabella's death in 1504, Ferdinand governed Castile as regent for their daughter Joanna, though his authority was contested. He married Germaine of Foix in 1506 to ally with France. The latter part of his reign was dominated by the Italian Wars, where he fought France for control of Naples and Milan, often through shifting alliances. In 1512, he conquered most of Navarre, bringing nearly all of modern Spain under his rule.

**Succession and Legacy**
Ferdinand's death in 1516 left the succession to his daughter Joanna, who was deemed mentally unfit. Power was quickly assumed by their grandson, Charles I (later Holy Roman Emperor Charles V). Ferdinand and Isabella's reign laid the groundwork for the Spanish Empire, centralizing the monarchy, reforming the church, and setting the stage for Spain's global dominance in the 16th century.

## Terms
- **Personal Union**: A constitutional arrangement where a single monarch rules multiple separate states, as Ferdinand did with Aragon and Castile.
- **Reconquista**: The centuries-long process by which Christian kingdoms in the Iberian Peninsula recaptured territory from Muslim rule, culminating in 1492.
- **Alhambra Decree**: The 1492 edict ordering the expulsion of Jews from Castile and Aragon who did not convert to Christianity.
- **Mudéjar/Morisco**: Terms for Muslims living under Christian rule; *Mudéjars* were those allowed to practice their faith, while *Moriscos* were those forcibly converted.
- **Cortes**: The representative assembly of the nobility and clergy in the kingdoms of the Iberian Peninsula.
- **Regent**: A person appointed to administer a state because the monarch is a minor, absent, or incapacitated.
- **Nueva Planta decrees**: A series of 18th-century laws that formally abolished the separate legal systems of the Crown of Aragon, unifying Spain administratively.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The extent of Joanna's mental instability and whether her long confinement was politically motivated by her father and later her son.
- The legal and practical nature of the union between Castile and Aragon during Ferdinand and Isabella's reign, given the kingdoms retained separate laws and institutions.
- The significance of Ferdinand's purchase of the Byzantine imperial title from Andreas Palaiologos, which was never utilized.

# Balkan Mountains

## Overview
The Balkan Mountains, known locally as Stara Planina ("Old Mountain"), are a major mountain range in Southeastern Europe that forms a natural barrier across Bulgaria and extends into Serbia. They are geologically young fold mountains, part of the Alp-Himalayan chain, and are significant for their role as a historical fortress, a biodiversity hotspot, and the source of the name "Balkans."

- **1190** — Battle of Tryavna between Bulgarian and Byzantine Empires.
- **1279** — Battle of Devina between Bulgarian and Byzantine Empires.

- **1876** — Hristo Botev, a national hero, dies in the western mountains.

- **1980s** — Special nature reserves created in Serbia; *Thecodontosaurus* fossils discovered.
- **1997** — Nature Park Stara Planina established in Serbia.
- **2017** — Central Balkan National Park's beech forests included in UNESCO World Heritage Site.

## Body

### Etymology and Mythology
The name "Balkan" likely derives from the Persian *bālkāneh* ("high house") via Ottoman Turkish. In antiquity, the range was known as Haemus, from a Thracian word for "mountain ridge." Greek mythology offers two folk etymologies for Haemus: one links it to *haima* ("blood") from a battle between Zeus and Typhon; the other to a mythical King Haemus, transformed into a mountain for hubris.

### Geography and Hydrology
The range runs about 560 km from the Serbia-Bulgaria border to Cape Emine on the Black Sea. It is divided into Western, Central (containing the highest point, Botev Peak at 2,376 m), and Eastern sections. The mountains form a watershed between Black Sea and Aegean drainage basins. They are crossed by numerous passes and gorges, most notably the Iskar Gorge. The karst landscape features many caves, including Magura, known for post-Palaeolithic paintings.

### History
The Balkan Mountains were a natural fortress for the Bulgarian Empire, site of key battles against the Byzantines. During Ottoman rule, they provided refuge for haiduks (rebels). The mountains are central to Bulgarian national identity, associated with the poet Hristo Botev and the pivotal 1877-78 battles at Shipka Pass.

### Protected Areas and Ecology
The range hosts significant protected areas in both countries. In Bulgaria, the Central Balkan National Park is a UNESCO site with primeval beech forests, diverse flora (including endemics like *Primula frondosa*), and fauna such as brown bears, wolves, and chamois. Vrachanski Balkan Nature Park features extensive karst landscapes and reintroduced vulture species. In Serbia, Nature Park Stara Planina (est. 1997) protects diverse montane ecosystems, including rare plants and the southernmost locality for the northern crested newt. Conservation efforts include vulture reintroduction and monitoring of the Eurasian lynx.

## Terms
- **Fold mountains:** Mountains formed by the compression and folding of rock layers.
- **Karst:** A landscape formed from the dissolution of soluble rocks like limestone, characterized by caves and sinkholes.
- **Watershed:** An area of land that drains all precipitation to a common outlet.
- **Haemus:** The ancient name for the Balkan Mountains.
- **Stara Planina:** The Bulgarian and Serbian name for the mountains, meaning "Old Mountain."
- **Haiduk:** A bandit or rebel, especially one fighting Ottoman rule in the Balkans.
- **Endemic:** A species native to and restricted to a specific geographic area.
- **Primeval Beech Forests:** Ancient, undisturbed beech forests recognized as a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

## Debates and Open Questions
The exact etymology of the ancient name "Haemus" is debated, with competing folk etymologies from Greek mythology. The environmental impact of proposed micro-hydropower plants in Serbia's protected areas has been a major point of contention between developers, authorities, and local communities.

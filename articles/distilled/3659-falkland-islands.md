# Falkland Islands

## Overview
The Falkland Islands are a remote British Overseas Territory in the South Atlantic Ocean, comprising two main islands and hundreds of smaller ones. They are significant due to their strategic location, unique subantarctic ecology, and a long-standing sovereignty dispute between the United Kingdom and Argentina, which culminated in a 1982 war.

## Timeline
- **1690** — First undisputed European landing by English captain John Strong.
- **1764** — French captain Louis Antoine de Bougainville establishes the first settlement, Port Louis.
- **1765** — British captain John Byron claims the islands for King George III.
- **1833** — Britain reasserts rule, establishing a permanent administration.
- **1982** — Argentine military forces invade; British administration is restored after the Falklands War.
- **2013** — A sovereignty referendum shows 99.8% of votes in favor of remaining a UK overseas territory.
- **2020** — Demining of landmines left from the 1982 war is completed.
- **2025** — Final investment decision is taken for the Sea Lion oil field development.

## Body

### History and Sovereignty Dispute
The islands were uninhabited when Europeans first explored them in the 17th century. French and British settlements were established in the 1760s, with Spain later acquiring the French claim. Britain withdrew its garrison in 1774 but left a sovereignty plaque. After periods of Spanish and then nominal Argentine control, Britain reasserted its rule in 1833. Argentina has continuously disputed this, claiming it inherited sovereignty from Spain upon independence in 1816. Tensions escalated in the 20th century, leading to the 1982 Falklands War, which began with an Argentine invasion and ended with a British victory restoring the status quo. The UK has since expanded its military presence. A 2013 referendum overwhelmingly supported remaining a British territory, a result Argentina does not recognize.

### Government and Defence
The Falklands are a self-governing British Overseas Territory under a 2009 constitution. The UK is responsible for defence and foreign affairs. The islands have a unicameral Legislative Assembly, and all politicians are independent. Defence is provided by a UK military garrison, supplemented by the locally funded Falkland Islands Defence Force. The territory claims a 200-nautical-mile exclusive economic zone, which overlaps with Argentina's claim.

### Geography and Climate
The archipelago, with an area of 4,700 sq mi (12,000 km²), lies on the Patagonian Shelf about 300 mi (500 km) east of South America. It consists of two main mountainous islands, East and West Falkland, separated by Falkland Sound. The climate is cold, windy, and humid maritime, with frequent rain and strong westerly winds. The highest point is Mount Usborne at 2,313 ft (705 m).

### Biodiversity and Environment
The islands are biogeographically part of the Antarctic zone. They host large populations of birds, including five penguin species and major albatross colonies. The only endemic bird species are the Falkland steamer duck and Cobb's wren. The native warrah (a type of fox) was hunted to extinction. Introduced species like rabbits and rats have harmed native flora and fauna. The landscape is treeless, dominated by dwarf shrubs, and virtually all land is used as sheep pasture.

### Economy
The economy is based on fishing, tourism, and sheep farming for high-quality wool. Fishing contributes 50-60% of GDP. Tourism has grown, attracting visitors with wildlife and Antarctic connections. Oil exploration is a developing but controversial sector due to maritime disputes with Argentina; the Sea Lion Field project was sanctioned in 2025. The Falkland Islands pound is pegged to the British pound sterling. The government is the largest employer.

### Demographics and Culture
The population was 3,662 in the 2021 census, with about 30% being temporary residents. The community is largely descended from British settlers. English is the official language, and culture is predominantly British with some Hispanic influences. The main settlement is Stanley. Education follows the English system and is free and compulsory for ages 5-16.

## Terms
- **British Overseas Territory**: A territory under the jurisdiction and sovereignty of the United Kingdom, but not part of it.
- **Sovereignty dispute**: The ongoing disagreement between the UK and Argentina over which state has rightful control of the islands.
- **Exclusive Economic Zone (EEZ)**: A maritime zone extending 200 nautical miles from a coast, where a state has special rights regarding resource exploration and use.
- **Camp**: The term for the countryside areas of the Falklands outside the main settlement of Stanley.
- **Warrah**: An extinct native fox-like mammal, the only native terrestrial mammal of the islands.
- **Falkland Islands Company**: A dominant commercial entity that historically controlled much of the islands' trade and economy.
- **Ottawa Treaty**: An international agreement banning anti-personnel landmines, to which the UK is a signatory.

## Debates and Open Questions
The core debate is the sovereignty dispute between the UK and Argentina. The UK bases its position on continuous administration since 1833 and the islanders' right to self-determination. Argentina claims it inherited sovereignty from Spain in 1816 and views the 1833 British takeover as usurpation. The 2013 referendum result is rejected by Argentina as irrelevant. Future negotiations are stalled, with neither side agreeing on terms.

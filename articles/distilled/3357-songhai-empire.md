# Songhai Empire

## Overview
The Songhai Empire was a major West African state that dominated the Sahel region during the 15th and 16th centuries. At its peak, it was one of the largest empires in African history, renowned for its wealth from trans-Saharan trade, its administrative sophistication, and its centers of Islamic learning. Its rise, golden age, and eventual collapse illustrate the dynamics of empire-building, internal decay, and external intervention in pre-colonial West Africa.

## Timeline
- **c. 11th century** — A Songhai state exists in and around Gao.
- **c. 1300** — The Mali Empire conquers Gao.
- **c. 1430s** — The Songhai reassert control of Gao as Mali weakens.
- **1464–1492** — Reign of Sonni Ali; expands the empire, conquers Timbuktu (1468) and Djenné (1475).
- **1493–1528** — Reign of Askia the Great; institutes reforms, promotes Islam, and expands the empire.
- **1528** — Askia the Great is overthrown by his son, beginning a period of instability.
- **1583** — Death of Askia Daoud triggers a war of succession.
- **1591** — Battle of Tondibi; Songhai forces are defeated by a Moroccan army under Judar Pasha.
- **Post-1591** — The Songhai Empire collapses; its nobles found smaller successor kingdoms in the south.

## Body

### Origins and Rise
The Songhai people formed from various groups, including the Sorko, Gao, and Do, who settled along the Niger River. By the 10th century, Songhai chiefs established Gao as a small kingdom controlling regional trade. Around 1300, the expanding Mali Empire conquered Gao. As Mali disintegrated in the late 14th century, the Songhai reasserted independence and began expanding, taking advantage of Mali's weakness.

### The Sonni Dynasty and Expansion
Sonni Ali (r. 1464–1492) transformed the Songhai into a major power. He conquered key trading cities, annexing Timbuktu in 1468 and, after a seven-year siege, Djenné in 1475. His military campaigns and control of trade routes greatly increased the empire's wealth. However, his rule was controversial; Islamic scholars often portrayed him as a tyrant for his treatment of Timbuktu's scholarly elite and his adherence to traditional religion.

### The Askia Dynasty and Golden Age
After Sonni Ali's death, his general Muhammad Ture, known as Askia the Great (r. 1493–1528), usurped power. Askia centralized administration, established a bureaucracy for tax collection and justice, and promoted Islam by building mosques and schools. He professionalized the army, introduced a system of weights and measures, and expanded the empire to its greatest territorial extent. His reign marked the empire's peak in wealth, stability, and cultural influence.

### Decline and Fall
Following Askia the Great's overthrow in 1528, the empire suffered from succession disputes and civil wars. A brief period of stability under Askia Daoud was followed by another war of succession after his death in 1583. In 1591, Sultan Ahmad I al-Mansur of Morocco, seeking to control trans-Saharan trade, sent an army under Judar Pasha. Despite superior numbers, the Songhai army was defeated at the Battle of Tondibi due to Moroccan gunpowder weapons. The invasion destroyed the empire's core cities and led to its fragmentation into smaller kingdoms.

## Terms
*   **Sahel:** The semi-arid region in West Africa south of the Sahara Desert, where the Songhai Empire was located.
*   **Trans-Saharan trade:** The network of trade routes crossing the Sahara, vital for the exchange of goods like gold, salt, and slaves.
*   **Sonni dynasty:** The first ruling dynasty of the Songhai Empire (c. 1464–1493).
*   **Askia dynasty:** The second ruling dynasty of the Songhai Empire (1493–1591), founded by Askia the Great.
*   **Hajj:** The Islamic pilgrimage to Mecca, undertaken by Askia the Great to demonstrate his piety and empire's wealth.
*   **Sharia:** Islamic law, which formed the basis of the Songhai legal system under Askia.
*   **Qadi:** A judge in an Islamic legal system, responsible for administering justice in Songhai towns.
*   **Hikoy:** The commander of the Songhai Empire's naval fleet on the Niger River.
*   **Battle of Tondibi (1591):** The decisive conflict where a Moroccan army defeated the Songhai, leading to the empire's collapse.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   The characterization of Sonni Ali as a "tyrant" is largely based on accounts from Islamic scholars he antagonized; his legacy is debated between this view and that of a brilliant military and political founder.
*   The precise causes and timeline of the Songhai's reassertion of independence from Mali are not fully clear, with sources noting dynastic instability in Mali as a key factor.
*   The extent to which the empire's internal decay, rather than the technological superiority of Moroccan firearms, was the primary cause of its defeat at Tondibi is a point of historical analysis.

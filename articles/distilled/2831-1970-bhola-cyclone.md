# 1970 Bhola cyclone

## Overview
The 1970 Bhola cyclone was the deadliest tropical cyclone on record, striking East Pakistan (now Bangladesh) and India's West Bengal on 12 November 1970. It killed at least 300,000 people, possibly up to 500,000, primarily from a massive storm surge. The disaster and the Pakistani government's poor response fueled political unrest that led to the Bangladesh Liberation War seven months later.

## Timeline
- **8 Nov 1970** — A depression forms in the central Bay of Bengal.
- **10 Nov** — Storm intensifies, reaching peak winds of 185 km/h (115 mph).
- **12 Nov** — Cyclone makes landfall in East Pakistan with a 10-metre storm surge.
- **13 Nov** — Storm weakens over land.
- **Late Nov** — International aid begins to arrive; criticism of the Pakistani government's response grows.
- **7 Dec 1970** — Pakistani general election held; Awami League wins a landslide in East Pakistan.
- **25 Mar 1971** — Bangladesh Liberation War begins.
- **1972** — Cyclone Preparedness Programme established in East Pakistan.

## Body

### Meteorological History
The cyclone developed from remnants of a Pacific storm. It formed on 8 November and intensified as it moved northward. It reached peak intensity on 11 November with sustained winds of 185 km/h (115 mph) and made landfall on 12 November during high tide, which amplified the storm surge.

### Impact and Death Toll
The storm surge devastated the low-lying Ganges Delta, with a 10-metre surge reported. In the worst-hit upazila, Tazumuddin, over 45% of the population died. The exact death toll is uncertain; two medical surveys estimated a minimum of 224,000 to 240,000 deaths, but the total is likely higher. The storm destroyed crops, fishing fleets, and over 3.6 million people were directly affected. It also caused significant damage in India's Andaman Islands and West Bengal.

### Government Response and Criticism
The response from the West Pakistan-led government, under General Yahya Khan, was widely criticized as slow and inadequate. Relief efforts were hampered by poor coordination and a lack of urgency. Local political leaders and international media condemned the "gross neglect." This perceived indifference deepened the divide between East and West Pakistan.

### Political Consequences
The mishandling of the disaster galvanized public anger in East Pakistan. It allowed the opposition Awami League, led by Sheikh Mujibur Rahman, to win a landslide in the December 1970 election. The central government's refusal to transfer power led to the Bangladesh Liberation War in March 1971.

### International Response and Legacy
Numerous countries and organizations, including the US, UK, India, and the UN, provided aid, though some was delayed by the Pakistani government. The disaster led to long-term improvements, including the creation of the Cyclone Preparedness Programme and the construction of over 200 cyclone shelters in Bangladesh by 2000. These measures helped reduce casualties in subsequent cyclones, like the 1991 storm.

## Terms
- **Storm surge:** An abnormal rise in sea level generated by a storm, over and above the predicted tides.
- **Upazila:** A geographic region and administrative unit in Bangladesh, similar to a sub-district.
- **Cyclonic storm:** A tropical cyclone with sustained winds between 63–88 km/h (39–55 mph).
- **Anemometer:** An instrument used to measure wind speed.
- **Cyclone Preparedness Programme:** A joint government and Red Crescent initiative to warn coastal populations of cyclones.
- **Awami League:** A major political party in Bangladesh, which led the independence movement.
- **Ganges Delta:** A vast river delta formed by the Ganges and Brahmaputra rivers, highly vulnerable to flooding.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The exact death toll remains uncertain, with estimates ranging from 300,000 to 500,000.
- There is debate over how much weather information was shared between Indian and Pakistani authorities before the storm, given political tensions.
- The degree to which the government's relief failures directly caused the subsequent political crisis and war is a subject of historical analysis.

# Sogdia

## Overview
Sogdiana was an ancient Iranian civilization and network of city-states in Central Asia, located between the Amu Darya and Syr Darya rivers (modern Uzbekistan, Tajikistan, etc.). It was not a unified empire but a collection of commercially oriented polities centered on Samarkand. The Sogdians became the preeminent middlemen of the Silk Road from roughly the 2nd century BC to the 10th century AD, facilitating trade, cultural exchange, and the spread of religions between China, Persia, and the Byzantine Empire. Their language served as a lingua franca for Asian trade, and their diaspora communities were influential in China, particularly during the Tang dynasty.

- **260 AD** — Sasanian Empire conquers Sogdiana, making it a satrapy.

- **8th century** — Muslim conquest of Transoxiana begins under the Umayyad Caliphate.
- **819–999** — Samanid Empire rules; Sogdian language declines in favor of Persian.
- **999** — Kara-Khanid Khanate conquers the Samanids, completing Turkicization.

## Body

### Geography and Early History
Sogdiana lay north of Bactria and included the fertile Zeravshan valley. Its territory corresponds to modern Samarkand and Bukhara in Uzbekistan and the Sughd region of Tajikistan. The region possessed a Bronze Age urban culture, with settlements like Sarazm (4th millennium BC) and Kök Tepe (15th century BC). The Avestan texts refer to the region as "Gava," likely the land of the Sogdians.

### Achaemenid to Hellenistic Periods
Cyrus the Great conquered Sogdiana around 546–539 BC, incorporating it into the Achaemenid Empire. Darius I introduced Aramaic writing and coinage. Sogdiana was listed on the Behistun Inscription. After Alexander the Great's conquest in 328 BC, he married the Sogdian noblewoman Roxana and encouraged intermarriage to pacify the region. Sogdiana then passed through the Seleucid Empire and the Greco-Bactrian Kingdom.

### Nomadic Rule and Silk Road Prominence
From the 2nd century BC, Sogdiana was ruled successively by Sakas, Yuezhi (predecessors of the Kushans), and Hephthalites. The Hephthalites (c. 479–557 AD) fostered Sogdian prosperity by investing in cities and using them as Silk Road intermediaries. After their defeat, the region was controlled by the Western Turkic Khaganate. Sogdian city-states, though never politically united, dominated Silk Road trade as merchants, using their language as a lingua franca. They established trading networks from Byzantium to China.

### Arab Conquest and Islamicization
The Muslim conquest of Sogdiana began in the early 8th century under the Umayyads. Local rulers like Gurak of Samarkand eventually submitted. The Abbasid victory at the Battle of Talas (751) against the Tang dynasty secured Muslim control. The Samanid Empire (819–999), a Persianate dynasty, ruled the region, promoting Islam and the Persian language. By the end of the Samanid period, Sogdian had largely been supplanted by New Persian, and the population had converted to Islam.

### Sogdians in China
Sogdian merchants and diplomats settled in China, forming influential communities, especially during the Tang dynasty (618–907 AD). They served as soldiers, officials, and translators. The "Ancient Letters" (4th century) provide early evidence of their diaspora. Sogdians were deeply involved in the An Lushan Rebellion (755–763), after which many hid their identity. They blended Chinese and Zoroastrian burial practices and were key translators of Buddhist texts. The "Nine Surnames of Zhaowu" refer to prominent Sogdian clans in China.

### Language and Culture
Sogdian was an Eastern Iranian language, now extinct except for its descendant, Yaghnobi, spoken in Tajikistan. It was written in scripts derived from Aramaic. Sogdian art, exemplified by the Afrasiab murals (7th century) in Samarkand, shows cosmopolitan influences. Their religion was pluralistic: Zoroastrianism was likely dominant, but they also practiced Buddhism, Manichaeism, and Nestorian Christianity. The goddess Nana was widely worshipped.

## Terms
- **Lingua franca**: A language used for communication between people who do not share a native language.
- **Satrapy**: A province of the ancient Achaemenid (Persian) Empire, governed by a satrap.
- **Silk Road**: Ancient network of trade routes connecting East Asia with the Mediterranean world.
- **Sabao**: A title for a leader of a Sogdian merchant community in China, from Sanskrit *sarthavaha*.
- **Jizya**: A tax levied on non-Muslim subjects in an Islamic state.
- **Ikhshid**: A title used by Sogdian rulers, particularly of Samarkand.
- **Zoroastrianism**: An ancient Iranian religion based on the teachings of the prophet Zoroaster, featuring dualism and fire rituals.
- **Manichaeism**: A major Iranian Gnostic religion founded by the prophet Mani in the 3rd century AD.
- **Nestorian Christianity**: A branch of Eastern Christianity that originated in the Church of the East.

## Debates and open questions
- The precise location and meaning of the Avestan toponym "Gava" (associated with Sogdians) is debated; it may refer to Bukhara rather than Samarkand.
- The extent of Hephthalite urban planning and their direct role in Sogdian city-building is inferred from archaeological parallels and not definitively proven.
- The degree to which the Sogdian language and culture were already declining before the Islamic conquest, versus being actively supplanted by it, is a subject of scholarly discussion.

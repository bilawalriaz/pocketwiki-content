# Solomon Islands

## Overview
The Solomon Islands is an archipelagic nation in Melanesia, Oceania, consisting of six major islands and over 1,000 smaller islands. It is a constitutional monarchy with a parliamentary system, and its economy is largely based on subsistence agriculture, fishing, and logging. The country's modern history is marked by European colonization, a pivotal World War II campaign, a period of ethnic conflict following independence, and recent geopolitical shifts, including a security pact with China.

## Timeline
- **c. 30,000–28,000 BC** — First settlement by people from the Bismarck Islands and New Guinea.
- **1568** — Spanish navigator Álvaro de Mendaña is the first European to visit.
- **June 1893** — Captain Herbert Gibson declares the southern Solomon Islands a British protectorate.
- **1942–1945** — Solomon Islands campaign, including the Battle of Guadalcanal, is a major WWII conflict.
- **1975** — Official name changed from "British Solomon Islands Protectorate" to "The Solomon Islands."
- **1978** — Solomon Islands gains independence; name becomes "Solomon Islands."
- **1998–2003** — Ethnic conflict known as "The Tensions" erupts between Guadalcanal and Malaitan groups.
- **July 2003** — Australian-led Regional Assistance Mission to Solomon Islands (RAMSI) arrives to restore order.
- **September 2019** — Solomon Islands switches diplomatic recognition from Taiwan to the People's Republic of China.
- **April 2022** — Solomon Islands and China sign a security co-operation agreement.

## Body

### Prehistory and Early Settlement
The islands were first settled between 30,000 and 28,000 BC. Later, from c. 1200–800 BC, Austronesian Lapita people arrived, mixing with the native population. Their languages became dominant, forming the basis for most of the 60–70 indigenous languages spoken today. Communities lived in small villages practicing subsistence agriculture, supported by extensive inter-island trade networks.

### European Contact and Colonial Era
Spanish navigator Álvaro de Mendaña was the first European to visit in 1568. The name "Solomons" was later applied by others who optimistically linked the islands to the biblical King Solomon. In 1893, Britain declared a protectorate over the southern islands, later expanded to include the northern islands ceded by Germany in 1900. The colonial administration, based first at Tulagi and later Honiara, struggled with limited resources, while the economy developed through plantation agriculture, notably copra.

### World War II and the Path to Independence
During WWII, the Solomon Islands were the site of fierce fighting between Allied and Japanese forces from 1942–1945, most notably the Battle of Guadalcanal. The war caused massive destruction and thousands of deaths but also led to social change, including the development of Honiara and the influence of English-based Pijin. Post-war, the Maasina Rule movement advocated for native rights. Britain gradually prepared the islands for self-governance, achieved in 1976, followed by full independence on 7 July 1978 as a constitutional monarchy.

### Post-Independence Politics and "The Tensions"
The early post-independence years were marked by political instability, with frequent changes of prime minister. In 1998, long-standing tensions between settlers from Malaita and native Guadalcanal islanders erupted into civil conflict known as "The Tensions." The conflict involved militias like the Isatabu Freedom Movement and the Malaita Eagle Force, leading to economic collapse and lawlessness. A peace agreement was signed in 2000, but violence continued until the 2003 intervention by RAMSI, which restored stability.

### Recent Developments and Foreign Relations
Since RAMSI's withdrawal in 2017, politics have remained volatile with frequent no-confidence votes. A major shift occurred in 2019 when Solomon Islands switched diplomatic recognition from Taiwan to China. This was followed by a 2022 security pact with China, allowing for potential Chinese military presence, which has raised concerns in Australia, New Zealand, and the United States. The country continues to face challenges from poverty, limited infrastructure, and the impacts of climate change.

## Terms
*   **Melanesia:** A subregion of Oceania extending from the island of New Guinea to the Fiji islands, where the Solomon Islands is located.
*   **Lapita:** A prehistoric Austronesian people who spread across the Pacific, identifiable by their distinctive pottery, and are ancestors of many modern Polynesian and Melanesian populations.
*   **Blackbirding:** The coercive recruitment or kidnapping of Pacific Islanders for labor on plantations in the 19th and early 20th centuries.
*   **Protectorate:** A state that is controlled and protected by another sovereign state, which manages its foreign affairs while allowing some internal autonomy.
*   **RAMSI:** The Regional Assistance Mission to Solomon Islands, an Australian-led intervention force deployed in 2003 to restore law and order after ethnic conflict.
*   **Pijin:** An English-based creole language that serves as the de facto lingua franca of Solomon Islands.
*   **Wantok:** A concept of kinship and mutual obligation among people who share a common language or come from the same area.
*   **Kastom:** A Pijin term referring to traditional beliefs, customs, and land ownership practices.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **State Failure vs. Unformed State:** Scholars debate whether Solomon Islands is a "failed state" that cannot build a national identity over local loyalties, or an "unformed state" that never fully consolidated its institutions after independence.
*   **Ethnic Conflict Label:** Some academics argue that labeling the 1998–2003 conflict as purely "ethnic" is an oversimplification, as it also involved political and economic factors.
*   **China Security Pact:** The 2022 security agreement with China is a source of regional tension, with debate over its implications for sovereignty, regional stability, and the balance of power in the Pacific.

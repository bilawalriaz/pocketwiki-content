# Czech Republic

## Overview
The Czech Republic, also known as Czechia, is a landlocked country in Central Europe with a rich history rooted in the medieval Kingdom of Bohemia. It is a developed, high-income parliamentary democracy and a member of the European Union and NATO, known for its advanced industrial economy, cultural heritage, and high standard of living.

## Timeline
- **c. 29,000–25,000 BC** — Venus of Dolní Věstonice, the world's oldest ceramic figurine, is created in the region.
- **Late 9th century** — The Duchy of Bohemia is founded under the Přemyslid dynasty.
- **1002** — Bohemia is formally recognized as an Imperial Estate of the Holy Roman Empire.
- **1198** — The Duchy of Bohemia is raised to a Kingdom.
- **1618–1620** — The Defenestration of Prague and Battle of White Mountain lead to Habsburg consolidation of power.
- **1918** — Following World War I, the independent First Czechoslovak Republic is created.
- **1938** — The Munich Agreement leads to Nazi Germany's occupation of the Czech lands.
- **1948** — A communist coup d'état establishes a single-party state in Czechoslovakia.
- **1968** — The Prague Spring liberalization is suppressed by a Warsaw Pact invasion.
- **1989** — The Velvet Revolution ends communist rule and restores democracy.
- **1993** — Czechoslovakia peacefully dissolves into the independent Czech Republic and Slovakia.
- **2004** — The Czech Republic joins the European Union.

## Body

### History
The region's history begins with prehistoric settlements, evidenced by artifacts like the Venus of Dolní Věstonice. Slavic tribes settled in the 6th century, leading to the formation of Great Moravia and later the Duchy of Bohemia. The Kingdom of Bohemia became a key part of the Holy Roman Empire. The 15th-century Hussite Wars, sparked by religious reformer Jan Hus, were a major pre-Reformation conflict. After 1526, the Habsburgs gradually took control, solidifying their rule after crushing the Protestant Bohemian Revolt in 1620. The lands became part of the Austrian Empire in 1806.

The modern Czech state emerged from the collapse of Austria-Hungary after World War I, forming Czechoslovakia in 1918. This parliamentary democracy was dismantled by Nazi occupation in 1938. After liberation in 1945, a communist coup in 1948 brought the country into the Eastern Bloc. A brief period of liberalization, the Prague Spring, was ended by a 1968 Soviet-led invasion. The Velvet Revolution of 1989 peacefully ended communist rule. In 1993, Czechoslovakia dissolved, creating the independent Czech Republic.

### Government and Politics
The Czech Republic is a unitary parliamentary republic. The Parliament is bicameral, consisting of the Chamber of Deputies (200 members) and the Senate (81 members). The President is the head of state, with limited powers, and has been directly elected since 2013. The Prime Minister is the head of government. The country's legal system is based on continental civil law. It is a member of major international organizations, including the EU, NATO, and the OECD, with foreign policy centered on these alliances.

### Economy
The Czech Republic has a developed, high-income social market economy. It is export-oriented, with a strong industrial sector (37.5% of GDP) and a large services sector (60%). Key industries include automotive manufacturing (e.g., Škoda Auto), machinery, and electronics. The country uses its own currency, the Czech koruna, not the euro. It has a low unemployment rate and a high Human Development Index ranking. Major trading partners are Germany and other EU countries.

### Demographics and Society
The population is predominantly Czech (57.3%), with significant minorities including Moravians, Slovaks, and Ukrainians. The country is highly linguistically homogeneous, with Czech as the official language. It is one of the most secular nations in Europe, with about 75-79% of residents declaring no religion. Education is compulsory for nine years, and university education is tuition-free. The healthcare system is universal and compulsory.

### Culture
Czech culture has a long history in art, architecture, literature, and music. Notable figures include composers Bedřich Smetana and Antonín Dvořák, writer Franz Kafka, and filmmaker Miloš Forman. The country is renowned for its Baroque and Gothic architecture, puppetry (a UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage), and a strong tradition in film and animation. Czech cuisine is hearty, featuring meats, dumplings, and world-famous beer, with the pilsner style originating in Plzeň.

## Terms
- **Unitary parliamentary republic:** A state where the central government holds supreme power and is led by a prime minister responsible to a parliament.
- **Social market economy:** An economic system combining a free-market capitalist system with social policies and state regulation to ensure fair competition and a welfare state.
- **Velvet Revolution:** The non-violent transition of power in Czechoslovakia in 1989 that ended communist rule.
- **Hussite Wars:** A series of conflicts in the 15th century between the followers of religious reformer Jan Hus and the forces of the Catholic Church and Holy Roman Empire.
- **Protectorate of Bohemia and Moravia:** The Nazi German-occupied territory of the Czech lands from 1939 to 1945.
- **Schengen Area:** An area comprising 27 European countries that have officially abolished all passport and other types of border control at their mutual borders.
- **Visegrád Group:** A cultural and political alliance of four Central European countries: the Czech Republic, Hungary, Poland, and Slovakia.

## Debates and open questions

The country's relationship with its historical German-speaking minority and the post-WWII expulsion of Sudeten Germans remains a complex historical topic.

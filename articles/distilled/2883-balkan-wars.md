# Balkan Wars

## Overview
The Balkan Wars (1912–1913) were two conflicts that dramatically redrew the map of Southeast Europe. In the First Balkan War, an alliance of Balkan states defeated the Ottoman Empire, stripping it of nearly all its European territory. In the Second Balkan War, the victors fought each other over the spoils. These wars accelerated the decline of the Ottoman Empire, reshaped regional power dynamics, and created tensions that directly set the stage for World War I.

## Timeline
- **8 Oct 1912** — Montenegro declares war on the Ottoman Empire, starting the First Balkan War.
- **17 Oct 1912** — Serbia, Bulgaria, and Greece declare war on the Ottoman Empire.
- **3 Nov 1912** — Greek Navy defeats Ottoman fleet at Battle of Lemnos, securing Aegean dominance.
- **8 Nov 1912** — Greek forces accept the surrender of Thessaloniki from Ottoman forces.
- **30 May 1913** — Treaty of London ends the First Balkan War; creates independent Albania.
- **29 Jun 1913** — Bulgaria attacks Serbia and Greece, starting the Second Balkan War.
- **10 Jul 1913** — Romania declares war on Bulgaria.
- **29 Jul–9 Aug 1913** — Battle of Kresna Gorge: Bulgarian forces halt the Greek advance.
- **10 Aug 1913** — Treaty of Bucharest ends the Second Balkan War, partitioning Macedonia.
- **30 Sep 1913** — Treaty of Constantinople: Bulgaria cedes most of Eastern Thrace back to the Ottoman Empire.

## Background and Causes
By the early 20th century, Bulgaria, Greece, Montenegro, and Serbia were independent but had large ethnic populations under Ottoman rule. The "Eastern Question"—how to manage the declining Ottoman Empire—was complicated by the conflicting interests of the Great Powers (Russia, Austria-Hungary, Britain, France, Germany). The 1908 Young Turk Revolution initially raised hopes for Ottoman reform, but the subsequent rise of Turkish nationalism and Austria-Hungary's annexation of Bosnia-Herzegovina frustrated Serbia and other Balkan states. The Italo-Turkish War (1911–12) and Albanian revolts exposed Ottoman weakness. Secret treaties formed the Balkan League (1912), an alliance of the four Balkan states aimed at seizing Ottoman territories, though they had no agreed plan for dividing them.

## The First Balkan War (1912–1913)
The League's coordinated attack overwhelmed the Ottoman Empire. Bulgaria fought the main Ottoman armies in Thrace, advancing to the outskirts of Constantinople. Serbia conquered Macedonia and pushed into Albania, reaching the Adriatic. Greece captured Thessaloniki and advanced into Epirus and the Aegean islands. Montenegro besieged Shkodra. The Greek Navy's victory at Lemnos prevented Ottoman reinforcements from Asia. The war ended with the Treaty of London (May 1913), which ceded all Ottoman territory west of the Enos-Midia line to the Allies and established an independent Albania, a decision that angered Serbia.

## The Second Balkan War (1913)
Disputes over dividing Macedonia, particularly between Bulgaria and Serbia, led to war. Bulgaria, dissatisfied with its gains and feeling cheated by Serbia's refusal to honor pre-war agreements, attacked its former allies on 29 June 1913. Serbia and Greece, having signed a defensive pact in May, repelled the attack. Romania then invaded Bulgaria from the north, and the Ottoman Empire retook Adrianople and Eastern Thrace. The Treaty of Bucharest (August 1913) partitioned Macedonia: Serbia and Greece gained the largest shares, while Bulgaria retained only a portion of Macedonia and Western Thrace. Romania gained Southern Dobruja.

## Legacy and Consequences
The wars were marked by ethnic cleansing and atrocities by all sides. For the Ottoman Empire, the loss of its European heartland was a catastrophic blow, contributing to its final collapse in 1918. Serbia doubled in size and population, emboldening its push for a South Slavic union, which threatened Austria-Hungary. The wars destabilized the region: Bulgaria, resentful of its losses, later allied with the Central Powers. The power vacuum and heightened tensions, especially between Austria-Hungary and Serbia, directly set the stage for the July Crisis of 1914 and the outbreak of World War I.

## Terms
*   **Balkan League:** A secret alliance formed in 1912 between Bulgaria, Greece, Montenegro, and Serbia to wage war against the Ottoman Empire.
*   **Eastern Question:** The diplomatic and political problem posed by the slow decline of the Ottoman Empire and how the Great Powers should respond.
*   **Young Turk Revolution:** A 1908 uprising that restored the Ottoman constitution and parliament, initially raising hopes for reform but later leading to a nationalist government.
*   **Treaty of London (1913):** Ended the First Balkan War; mandated Ottoman withdrawal from all European territory west of the Enos-Midia line and created an independent Albania.
*   **Treaty of Bucharest (1913):** Ended the Second Balkan War; partitioned Macedonia among Serbia, Greece, and Bulgaria, and awarded Southern Dobruja to Romania.
*   **Ethnic cleansing:** The systematic forced removal or extermination of an ethnic or religious group from a given territory, a feature of both wars.
*   **Macedonian Struggle:** A period of low-intensity conflict (c. 1904–1908) in Ottoman Macedonia between Greek and Bulgarian irregulars and the Ottoman army.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   The exact division of Macedonia was a primary cause of the Second Balkan War, with competing Bulgarian, Serbian, and Greek claims.
*   The role of the Great Powers was contradictory; they officially opposed the wars but unofficially pursued conflicting interests, undermining any unified intervention.
*   Russia's policy was a critical factor: it helped create the Balkan League but then failed to manage the post-war disputes, ultimately losing influence and being drawn into World War I to support Serbia.
*   The wars are considered by some scholars, like Paul Mojzes, to constitute an "unrecognized genocide" due to the scale of ethnic cleansing and civilian atrocities.

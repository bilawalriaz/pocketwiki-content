# Luoyang

## Overview
Luoyang is one of China's oldest cities and a cradle of Chinese civilization, located at the confluence of the Luo and Yellow Rivers in Henan province. It served as the capital for multiple dynasties, making it the earliest of the Four Great Ancient Capitals of China. Its historical significance is reflected in its UNESCO World Heritage sites and its role as a cultural and political center for millennia.

## Body

### Historical Significance and Dynastic Capital
Luoyang's history is defined by its repeated role as a dynastic capital. The Eastern Zhou dynasty established its capital there in 771 BC. After a period of decline, it was made the capital of the Eastern Han in 25 AD, a period marked by significant development and the first recorded contact with the Roman Empire. The city was destroyed in 189 AD but was restored as the capital of the Wei and subsequent Jin dynasties, reaching a population of 600,000. Following centuries of conflict, the Northern Wei dynasty moved its capital to Luoyang in 493 AD, initiating a major building campaign that included the Longmen Grottoes. The Sui and Tang dynasties again made it a primary capital, with Empress Wu Zetian formally establishing it as the "Divine Capital" of her Zhou dynasty. It was damaged during the An Lushan Rebellion but remained a major cultural center into the Northern Song dynasty.

### Cultural and Archaeological Legacy
Luoyang is a foundational site for Chinese culture, described as the root of Confucianism, the birthplace of Taoism, and the point of first transmission for Buddhism. The Longmen Grottoes, a UNESCO site, contain over 30,000 Buddhist statues from the Northern Wei period. The city is also the origin of the compass, papermaking, and printing. Archaeological conservation is actively practiced, with a mobile lab for on-site artifact stabilization. The city's cultural identity is celebrated annually through the National Peony Fair, centered on its famous city flower, and the traditional 24-course "Luoyang Water Banquet."

### Modern Administration and Geography
Luoyang is a prefecture-level city governing 7 districts and 7 counties. Its geography is predominantly mountainous (45.51%) and hilly (40.73%), with a humid subtropical climate. A major administrative reorganization in 2021, which merged Yanshi City and other areas into the urban core, doubled its urban area to facilitate integration with the Zhengzhou Metropolitan Area. The city has a modern transportation network, including a subway system that opened in 2021, making it the first non-provincial capital in central/western China with this infrastructure.

## Terms
- **Prefecture-level city:** An administrative division of China, governing surrounding districts and counties.
- **Sinicization:** The process of cultural assimilation to Han Chinese culture, notably pursued by Northern Wei Emperor Xiaowen.
- **Longmen Grottoes:** A UNESCO World Heritage site of Buddhist rock-cut art near Luoyang, begun in 493 AD.
- **Disaster of Yongjia (311 AD):** The sacking and destruction of Luoyang by Xiongnu-led forces, marking a major collapse of the Western Jin dynasty.
- **An Lushan Rebellion (755-763 AD):** A devastating civil war during the Tang dynasty that damaged Luoyang.
- **National Peony Fair:** An annual festival in Luoyang celebrating the peony, its city flower, since 1983.
- **Luoyang Water Banquet:** A traditional 24-course culinary tradition of the city.

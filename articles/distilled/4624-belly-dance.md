# Belly dance

## Overview
Belly dance, known in Arabic as *Raqs sharqi* ("Eastern Dance"), is a Middle Eastern dance form characterized by isolations and movements of the hips and torso. It has evolved into various regional styles, with the Egyptian form being the most globally recognized due to the influence of Egyptian cinema. The dance is popular worldwide, taught both through formal instruction and community traditions in its countries of origin.

## Timeline
- **1864** — The term "belly dance" first appears in a review of a painting.
- **1893** — The dance gains major Western attention at the Chicago World's Fair.
- **1930s** — Arab immigrants bring professional belly dance to New York nightclubs.
- **1954** — Egyptian dancer Samia Gamal stars in the Hollywood film *Valley of the Kings*.
- **1987** — American Tribal Style (ATS) belly dance is created in the United States.
- **2004** — Egypt temporarily bans foreign-born solo belly dancers.

## Body

### Names and Movements
The English term "belly dance" is a translation of the French *danse du ventre*, first used in 1864. The primary Arabic name is *Raqs sharqi*. The dance is torso-driven, focusing on muscle isolations rather than limb movements. Common movement categories include percussive (staccato hip accents), fluid (sinuous figures of eight and undulations), and shimmies (small, fast vibrations). There is no universal naming scheme for these movements.

### Origins and History in the Middle East
Belly dance has ancient roots, with Greek and Roman sources describing dancers with suggestive movements. It was part of courtly entertainment in the Umayyad, Abbasid, and Fatimid caliphates, often performed by slaves or prostitutes for private male audiences. In the Ottoman Empire, it was performed in the sultan's palace. Historically, professional performers in Egypt were the *Awalim* (musicians/poets) and the *Ghawazi*.

### Modern Social Context and Egyptian Style
In much of the modern Arab world, belly dance is considered disreputable and is sometimes outlawed. In Egypt, it persists in two contexts: as a social folk dance (*Raqs Baladi*) at gatherings, and as a professional performance art. The modern Egyptian style, shaped by 19th-century *Awalim* and later cinema, is noted for its controlled, precise movements. Professional Cairo dance was historically exclusive to Egyptians, though a few non-native dancers have performed there.

### Costume
The most common costume is the *bedlah* ("suit"), featuring a fitted bra, hip belt, and skirt or pants, often heavily decorated. Egyptian law requires dancers to cover their upper bodies, typically with a sheer fabric over the stomach, though enforcement varies. Earlier costumes included full skirts and cropped vests.

### Global Spread and Western Styles
Belly dance was popularized in the West during the 18th-19th century Romantic movement. It gained major notoriety at the 1893 Chicago World's Fair, where it was dubbed the "hoochie coochie." In the U.S., it evolved into distinct styles like American Cabaret and, later, the group improvisational American Tribal Style (ATS) and its fusion offshoots. The dance is also popular in Australia, the UK, and Greece, where it is known as *Tsifteteli*.

### Health Benefits
Belly dance is a low-impact, weight-bearing exercise suitable for all fitness levels. Its isolations improve torso flexibility, and undulations gently strengthen and decompress the spine and abdominal muscles. Using props like veils or finger cymbals builds upper body and finger strength.

## Terms
- **Raqs sharqi**: Arabic for "Eastern Dance"; a broad category of professional belly dance forms.
- **Raqs Baladi**: Arabic for "Folk Dance"; the informal, social form of belly dance.
- **Bedlah**: The typical belly dance costume, consisting of a bra, belt, and skirt/pants.
- **Awalim**: Historical Egyptian professional dancers, primarily musicians and poets.
- **Ghawazi**: Historical Egyptian public street dancers, often from the Dom people.
- **Shimmy**: A small, fast, continuous vibration of the hips or ribcage.
- **Isolations**: The movement of one part of the torso independently from others.
- **American Tribal Style (ATS)**: A modern, group improvisational style created in the U.S. in 1987.
- **Tsifteteli**: The Greek name for belly dance, meaning "double stringed."

## Debates and Open Questions
- The exact origins of belly dance are debated, with theories linking it to ancient fertility rites or social dances, though concrete evidence is lacking.
- The identity of the dancer "Little Egypt" who performed at the 1893 Chicago World's Fair is disputed.
- In Greece, there is ongoing controversy over whether *Tsifteteli* represents Greek culture or is a relic of Ottoman oppression.

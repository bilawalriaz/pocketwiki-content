# Arabian Desert

## Overview
The Arabian Desert is a vast, hyper-arid wilderness covering most of the Arabian Peninsula, making it the world's fourth-largest desert and the largest in Asia. It is a critical biome for specialized flora and fauna, but faces significant conservation challenges due to human activity and climate extremes.

## Timeline
- **c. 3000 BC** — The desert's climate began its current arid phase.
- **1987** — Harrat al-Harrah Reserve established in Saudi Arabia.
- **1995** — Arabian oryx and sand gazelle reintroduced to 'Uruq Bani Ma'arid Reserve.

## Body

### Geography and Climate
The Arabian Desert, an extension of the Sahara, spans 2,330,000 km² across Saudi Arabia and neighboring countries. Its landscape includes the Rub' al-Khali (Empty Quarter), the world's largest continuous sand body, with dunes up to 250 m high. The climate is subtropical and hot, with most areas receiving about 100 mm of rain annually, though some hyperarid zones get as little as 30-40 mm. Temperatures are extreme, with summer highs often exceeding 40°C (104°F) and record temperatures above 50°C (122°F).

### Flora and Fauna
The ecoregion has low biodiversity, with about 900 plant species and 102 native mammal species. The Rub' al-Khali itself has only 37 plant species. Key fauna includes the Arabian oryx, sand gazelle, and sand cat. Historically, species like the Asiatic cheetah and lion lived here but are now extinct in the region. Many species, such as the striped hyena and jackal, have been extirpated due to hunting and habitat loss.

### Human Presence and Resources
The desert is home to Arab cultures, with Islam as the predominant faith. Major cities like Riyadh, Dubai, and Abu Dhabi are located within or on its margins. The region is rich in natural resources, including oil, natural gas, phosphates, and sulfur.

### Conservation and Threats
The conservation status is critical/endangered. Primary threats include overgrazing by livestock, poaching, and vegetation damage from off-road driving. Only 4.37% of the ecoregion is protected. Saudi Arabia and the UAE have established several reserves, such as Harrat al-Harrah and the Arabian Oryx Protected Area, to conserve species like the Nubian ibex and reintroduce others like the Arabian oryx.

## Terms
- **Xeric shrublands**: A biome characterized by plants adapted to very dry conditions.
- **Endemic**: A species native and restricted to a certain place.
- **Hyperarid**: An area with extremely low rainfall, less than 50 mm per year.
- **Silicates**: Minerals that make up the majority of the desert's sand, colored by iron oxide.
- **Escarpment**: A steep slope or long cliff formed by erosion or faulting.
- **Extirpated**: A species that no longer exists in a specific region but lives elsewhere.
- **Reintroduced**: The process of releasing a species back into an area where it once lived.

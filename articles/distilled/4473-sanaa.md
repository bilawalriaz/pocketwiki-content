# Sanaa

## Overview
Sanaa is the capital and largest city of Yemen, situated at a high elevation in the country's western mountains. It is a city of immense historical depth, founded by the Kingdom of Saba in the 1st century CE, and renowned for its UNESCO-listed Old City with distinctive tower houses. Its modern significance is defined by its role as Yemen's political and economic center, a status severely challenged by the ongoing civil war and Houthi control since 2014.

## Body

### Ancient and Islamic History
Sanaa was founded by the Kingdom of Saba in the 1st century CE as a secondary capital, strategically located at a crossroads of trade routes. It served as an important military center and was later the capital of Axumite viceroys. The city's name, attested in 3rd-century CE Sabaean inscriptions, is likely derived from the Sabaic root for "well-fortified." After the rise of Islam, Sanaa remained a governing seat and was praised by the 8th-century jurist al-Shafi'i. It was contested by various dynasties, including the Sulayhids (1062), Ayyubids (1175), and Rasulids, before falling under Ottoman control in 1547.

### Ottoman and North Yemen Periods
The Ottomans conquered Sanaa in 1547 but were expelled by Zaydi imams in 1629. They recaptured the city in 1872, instituting modernization reforms like city planning and schools to counter European influence. After the Ottomans left, Imam Yahya made Sanaa his capital but pursued isolationism, stifling development and making the city a hub for opposition. His son Ahmad moved the capital to Taiz in 1948. Following a 1962 republican coup, Sanaa regained its capital status, leading to the North Yemen Civil War (1962–1970) and subsequent rapid, often unplanned, urban expansion fueled by Gulf remittances.

### 21st Century and Conflict
After Yemeni unification, Sanaa was designated the national capital. It was the epicenter of the 2011 revolution that ousted President Saleh. In September 2014, Houthi forces seized the city. The ensuing Saudi-led intervention caused severe damage, including to the UNESCO-listed Old City. The conflict has devastated infrastructure, with the airport closed to commercial flights since 2016, and created a humanitarian crisis marked by cholera outbreaks and severe shortages of water, electricity, and medicine.

### Architecture and Urban Form
Sanaa's Old City is famous for its multi-story tower houses, built from stone and brick with geometric plaster decorations. These traditionally housed extended families, with the top floor (*mafraj*) used for social gatherings. The city is divided into the ancient Old City and the sprawling modern "new city," which expanded dramatically after the 1960s. New construction often uses concrete blocks, while the Old City faces threats from groundwater damage and conflict.

### Demographics and Society
Sanaa is Yemen's largest city, with a population of approximately 3.3 million (2023) and a very high growth rate. The population is young, with about 60% under 18. Traditionally, society was hierarchically structured into classes like the *sayyids* (descendants of Muhammad) and *akhdam* (lowest class), but this has shifted toward a wealth-based system since the 1960s. The majority are Zaydi Muslims, with Sunni minorities concentrated in newer areas. A historic Jewish community, present for millennia, has nearly vanished due to emigration and Houthi threats.

### Economy and Infrastructure
As the capital, Sanaa's economy is dominated by the public sector and commerce. It generates a disproportionate share of Yemen's formal jobs and businesses but also has high poverty and unemployment. The city faces critical infrastructure failures: its aquifer is being depleted at an unsustainable rate, leading to severe water scarcity; electricity is largely supplied by private generators and solar panels after the public grid collapsed; and waste management is overwhelmed. Transport relies on an informal network of minibuses and taxis, with severe congestion and pollution.

## Terms
- **Tower House**: A traditional multi-story residential building in Sanaa's Old City, housing an extended family.
- **Mafraj**: The uppermost story of a tower house, used as a reception room and for social gatherings.
- **Zaydi Islam**: A branch of Shia Islam that is the majority faith in northern Yemen.
- **Qat**: A stimulant plant whose leaves are chewed socially; a major cash crop and cultural practice.
- **Wadi**: A valley or streambed that is dry except during the rainy season.
- **Akhdam**: The lowest social class in traditional Sanaani society.
- **Tanzimat**: A series of modernization reforms instituted by the Ottoman Empire in the 19th century.
- **Amanat al-Asimah**: The administrative governorate of Sanaa, meaning "the Capital's Secretariat."

## Debates and Open Questions
- The exact founding date and early history of Sanaa are debated, with Islamic sources offering legendary accounts (e.g., founded by Shem, son of Noah) that differ from archaeological and epigraphic evidence.
- The precise location of the ancient Ghumdan Palace, mentioned in Sabaean inscriptions, is not definitively known.
- The long-term viability of Sanaa as a capital is uncertain due to the complete depletion of its primary aquifer, projected around 2030, and the ongoing conflict.

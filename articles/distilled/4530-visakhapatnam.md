# Visakhapatnam

## Overview
Visakhapatnam (also known as Vizag) is the largest city in the Indian state of Andhra Pradesh, situated between the Eastern Ghats and the Bay of Bengal. It is a major economic hub, a key naval base, and a significant historical port city with a legacy stretching back to the 6th century BCE. Its strategic location has made it a center for trade, industry, and defense for centuries.

## Timeline
- **c. 6th century BCE** — Earliest historical references to the region, part of the Kalinga territory.
- **11th–12th centuries CE** — City built around this time, likely by the Chola dynasty king Kulothunga I.
- **1571–1674** — Under the control of the Qutb Shahi kings of Golconda.
- **1711–1724** — Ruled by the Nizam of Hyderabad.
- **1757** — French establish a factory in the city.
- **1804** — British East India Company gains control after the Battle of Visakhapatnam.
- **1947** — Indian independence; city becomes part of Andhra Pradesh.
- **2005** — Greater Visakhapatnam Municipal Corporation (GVMC) is established.
- **2020** — A toxic gas leak at a polymer plant kills at least eleven people.

## Body

### Ancient and Medieval History
Visakhapatnam's history dates to the 6th century BCE, and it was historically part of the Kalinga region. It was ruled by various dynasties including the Andhra Satavahanas, Vengi, Pallava, Eastern Ganga, and Gajapati. Archaeological evidence suggests the present city was developed in the 11th-12th centuries CE. Control fluctuated between the Chola and Gajapati kingdoms until the Vijayanagara Empire's conquest in the 15th century. Later rulers included the Kingdom of Jeypore, Qutb Shahis, Mughals, and the Nizam of Hyderabad.

### Colonial Era and Independence
European powers, including the French and British, established trade operations in the city. The British East India Company gained control in 1804 following the Battle of Visakhapatnam. The city remained under British colonial rule until Indian independence in 1947. During this period, it was part of the Northern Circars and the Madras Presidency. The British developed Visakhapatnam into a prominent harbour on the east coast.

### Buddhist Heritage
The region has a significant Buddhist legacy, evidenced by numerous ancient sites. Key locations include Pavurallakonda (1st century BCE–2nd century CE), Sankaram (a 2,000-year-old site where all three major schools of Buddhism flourished), Bavikonda (with remains of a Buddhist complex from the 3rd century BCE to 3rd century CE), and Thotlakonda. These sites contain stupas, viharas, and artifacts like Roman and Satavahana coins, indicating ancient trade links.

### Economy and Industry
Visakhapatnam is the economic hub of Andhra Pradesh with a diversified economy. It is known as the "shipbuilding capital of India" due to shipyards like Hindustan Shipyard. The city hosts heavy industries, ports (Visakhapatnam Port is India's fifth-busiest cargo port), pharmaceuticals, and a growing IT and fintech sector. Major industrial zones include the Jawaharlal Nehru Pharma City and the Petroleum, Chemical and Petrochemical Investment Region (PCPIR).

### Administration and Governance
The city is administered by the Greater Visakhapatnam Municipal Corporation (GVMC), established in 2005. It is divided into 9 zones and 98 wards. The city is also the headquarters of Visakhapatnam district and the South Coast Railway Zone. The Visakhapatnam Metropolitan Region Development Authority (VMRDA) oversees urban planning for a larger area.

### Defence and Research
Visakhapatnam is the headquarters of the Indian Navy's Eastern Naval Command. It hosts numerous naval bases, including India's first dedicated nuclear submarine base at INS Rambilli. The city is also home to research organizations like the Naval Science and Technological Laboratory (a DRDO lab), the National Institute of Oceanography, and a facility of the Bhabha Atomic Research Centre (BARC).

## Terms
*   **Eastern Ghats:** A discontinuous range of mountains along India's eastern coast, bordering Visakhapatnam.
*   **Northern Circars:** A historical name for a coastal region in eastern India, including Visakhapatnam, that was under French and then British control.
*   **GVMC (Greater Visakhapatnam Municipal Corporation):** The civic body responsible for the administration and infrastructure of Visakhapatnam city.
*   **VMRDA (Visakhapatnam Metropolitan Region Development Authority):** The urban planning agency for the Visakhapatnam metropolitan area.
*   **PCPIR (Petroleum, Chemical and Petrochemical Investment Region):** A large industrial zone proposed between Visakhapatnam and Kakinada.
*   **Eastern Naval Command:** The Indian Navy's command responsible for the east coast of India, headquartered in Visakhapatnam.
*   **Vihara:** A Buddhist monastery or dwelling place for monks.
*   **Stupa:** A hemispherical structure containing relics, used as a place of meditation in Buddhism.

## Debates and open questions
*   The exact origin of the city's name is debated. Local belief ties it to a 4th-century temple dedicated to Vaisakha, while other theories link it to a Chola king (Kulotungapatnam) or a Muslim saint (Ishakapatnam).
*   The precise timeline and builders of the ancient temple to Viśakha, now believed to be submerged, are based on legend and archaeological inference rather than definitive records.

# Maria Theresa

## Overview
Maria Theresa (1717–1780) was the only female ruler of the Habsburg monarchy, reigning in her own right for 40 years. She inherited a weakened state and, through military defense and extensive reforms, transformed it into a modern, centralized power, laying the groundwork for "enlightened absolutism" in Austria.

## Timeline
- **13 May 1717** — Born in Vienna, daughter of Emperor Charles VI.
- **20 Oct 1740** — Accession to the throne upon her father's death.
- **Dec 1740** — Frederick II of Prussia invades Silesia, beginning the War of the Austrian Succession.
- **25 June 1741** — Crowned Queen of Hungary in Pressburg (Bratislava).
- **12 Feb 1736** — Married Francis Stephen of Lorraine.
- **13 Sept 1745** — Her husband, Francis I, elected Holy Roman Emperor.
- **1 May 1756** — First Treaty of Versailles with France, initiating the Diplomatic Revolution.
- **1756–1763** — Seven Years' War; Austria fails to recover Silesia.
- **18 Aug 1765** — Death of her husband, Francis I.
- **1771–1778** — Issues "Robot Patents" to regulate forced peasant labor.
- **29 Nov 1780** — Dies in Vienna; succeeded by her son, Joseph II.

## Body

### Accession and Early Reign
Maria Theresa began her reign in 1740 with a depleted treasury and army, a legacy of her father's costly diplomatic efforts to secure the Pragmatic Sanction of 1713, which guaranteed her right to inherit. Despite this, she immediately faced the repudiation of the sanction by powers like Prussia, which invaded and seized the wealthy province of Silesia. This triggered the eight-year War of the Austrian Succession. A pivotal moment came when she secured crucial military support from the Hungarian Diet through a dramatic personal appeal. While she lost Silesia and minor Italian territories, she successfully defended the core of her Habsburg domains.

### Domestic Reforms
Maria Theresa implemented sweeping reforms to strengthen the state, advised by ministers like Wenzel Anton of Kaunitz-Rietberg and Friedrich Wilhelm von Haugwitz. She centralized administration, created a standing army funded by new taxes on the nobility, and reformed finances, doubling state revenue by 1764. Her government also enacted legal codifications, promoted medical advances like smallpox inoculation, and established secular primary schools for both sexes in the 1770s to combat illiteracy. These reforms modernized Austria's bureaucracy and economy, though they were often driven by the needs of an absolutist state rather than pure Enlightenment ideals.

### Religious and Social Policies
A devout Catholic, Maria Theresa enforced religious unity and rejected toleration. She expelled Jews from Prague and other cities, though later edicts offered some protections, and she actively persecuted Protestants, exiling many to Transylvania. Her policies toward Eastern Orthodox subjects involved confirming old privileges while asserting firmer state control over their church. In social policy, she issued "Robot Patents" to limit the forced labor of peasants, but her more radical plans to abolish serfdom were blocked by the nobility and her own son, Joseph II. Serfdom was only abolished after her death.

### Family and Dynastic Policy
Maria Theresa had sixteen children, using their marriages as tools of foreign policy. Her most famous daughter, Maria Antonia, married the French Dauphin and became Marie Antoinette. The deaths of several children and her husband to smallpox deeply affected her, leading her to promote inoculation. Her relationship with her co-ruler and son, Joseph II, was often tense, marked by ideological clashes over reforms and foreign policy, such as the First Partition of Poland, which she reluctantly accepted.

## Terms
- **Pragmatic Sanction (1713):** A decree by Charles VI securing the right of his daughter, Maria Theresa, to inherit his lands, which was later repudiated by other powers.
- **War of the Austrian Succession (1740–1748):** A conflict triggered by the repudiation of the Pragmatic Sanction, primarily fought over Maria Theresa's inheritance, resulting in the loss of Silesia to Prussia.
- **Seven Years' War (1756–1763):** A global conflict in which Austria, allied with France, fought Prussia in a failed attempt to reclaim Silesia.
- **Diplomatic Revolution:** The 1756 alliance between traditional enemies Austria and France against Prussia, orchestrated by Kaunitz.
- **Robot Patents:** Regulations issued by Maria Theresa to limit and define the forced labor (robota) owed by peasants to their landlords.
- **Raabisation:** A reform project to convert feudal estates into small, heritable farms, implemented on crown lands but resisted by the nobility.
- **Enlightened Absolutism:** A system of government where monarchs applied Enlightenment ideas to strengthen state power and efficiency, as seen in Maria Theresa's centralized reforms.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Enlightened or Absolutist?** Historians debate whether Maria Theresa's reforms were truly "enlightened" or primarily aimed at strengthening absolutist state control. Her support for torture and religious intolerance contrasts with Enlightenment ideals.
- **Role of Co-Rulers:** Scholars analyze the complex power dynamics with her husband Francis I and especially her son Joseph II, questioning the extent of her personal authority versus their influence.
- **Effectiveness of Reforms:** While her centralization and financial reforms were successful, her social policies, like the Robot Patents, had limited impact, and her religious policies are viewed as reactionary by modern standards.

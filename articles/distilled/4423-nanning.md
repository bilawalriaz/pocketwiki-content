# Nanning

## Overview
Nanning is the capital of the Guangxi Zhuang Autonomous Region in southern China, known as the "Green City" for its lush subtropical foliage. It serves as the economic, financial, and cultural center of Guangxi and a key gateway for China-ASEAN cooperation. The city has a long history, evolving from an ancient Baiyue settlement to a modern metropolis with a rapidly developing economy and infrastructure.

## Body

### History and Administration
Nanning's history spans over 1,700 years, beginning as a county in the Eastern Jin Dynasty. It was a significant trade center during the Song and Ming dynasties, earning the nickname "Little Nanjing." After the Communist Party captured the city in 1949, it was established as the capital of Guangxi in 1950. The city is administered as a prefecture-level city with seven districts, one county-level city, four counties, and six development zones.

### Economy and Resources
Nanning's economy shifted toward commerce and administration after 1949. Its 2015 GDP was 341 billion RMB. The city is rich in mineral resources, including gold, iron, manganese, and aluminum, containing a third of China's mineral resource types. It hosts six development zones and industrial parks, which are significant contributors to its GDP. The city is a major hub for trade with ASEAN nations.

### Geography and Climate
Located in a hilly basin in southern Guangxi, 160 km from the Vietnamese border, Nanning has a warm, humid subtropical climate. The annual mean temperature is 22.3 °C, with hot, humid summers and mild, dry winters. The city receives 1,286 mm of rainfall annually, mostly from May to October. It is known for its biodiversity, with over 3,000 plant species.

### Infrastructure and Transportation
Nanning has a modern transportation network. The Nanning Metro system, with four lines operational by 2020, connects key city areas. Nanning Wuxu International Airport serves air travel. The city is a major railway junction, with high-speed rail links to Guangzhou, Kunming, and other cities, and plans for a line to Vietnam. An extensive expressway network, including the G80 and G75, connects Nanning regionally.

### Culture and Education
Nanning is the cultural and educational center of Guangxi, hosting numerous universities such as Guangxi University and Guangxi Medical University. As of 2025, it is ranked among the top 150 global science cities for research output. The city's food culture features local dishes like Laoyou rice noodles and Lemon duck, alongside diverse Chinese and international cuisines. Key attractions include People's Park, Mount Qingxiu, and the Guangxi Museum.

## Terms
- **Zhuang**: The largest ethnic minority group in China, for whom the Guangxi Zhuang Autonomous Region is named.
- **Baiyue**: Ancient ethnic groups that inhabited southern China, including the area of modern Nanning.
- **Prefecture-level city**: An administrative division in China that governs surrounding counties and districts.
- **Development Zone**: A designated area in China with policies to attract investment and promote industrial growth.
- **China-ASEAN Expo (CASEAN)**: An annual trade and investment expo held in Nanning to promote cooperation between China and the Association of Southeast Asian Nations.
- **Laoyou rice noodles**: A famous local Nanning dish of rice noodles in a sour-spicy broth, meaning "old friend rice noodles."

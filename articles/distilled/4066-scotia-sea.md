# Scotia Sea

## Overview
The Scotia Sea is a body of water in the Southern Ocean, bordered by the Drake Passage and the Scotia Arc—a system of undersea ridges and islands. It is ecologically significant for its unique tundra vegetation and abundant marine wildlife, and historically notable for a famous early 20th-century expedition traverse.

## Timeline
- **c. 1902–1904** — Scottish National Antarctic Expedition under William S. Bruce operates in the region.
- **1916** — Sir Ernest Shackleton and five others traverse the sea in the lifeboat *James Caird*.
- **c. 1932** — The sea is named after the expedition ship *Scotia*.

## Body

### Location and Geography
The Scotia Sea covers approximately 900,000 km² and is framed by the Scotia Arc, which includes Tierra del Fuego, South Georgia, the South Sandwich Islands, the South Orkney Islands, and the Antarctic Peninsula. About half of the sea lies above the continental shelf. It sits atop the Scotia Plate and is a region where many icebergs melt.

### History and Naming
The sea was named around 1932 after the ship *Scotia*, used by the Scottish National Antarctic Expedition (1902–1904). Its most famous crossing occurred in 1916 during the Imperial Trans-Antarctic Expedition, when Shackleton and five crew sailed nearly 900 miles from Elephant Island to South Georgia in the *James Caird*, sighting the sun only four times for navigation. Argentina considers the area part of the "Mar Argentino," which includes territories it claims but does not occupy, such as South Georgia and the Falkland Islands.

### Ecology
The bordering islands, part of the Scotia Sea Islands tundra ecoregion, support vegetation like mosses, lichens, and algae despite harsh, icy conditions. The waters are rich in wildlife. Key bird species include four types of albatross, the endemic South Georgia pipit, and large colonies of southern giant petrels. Penguin species include king, chinstrap, macaroni, gentoo, Adelie, and rockhopper penguins. Seal populations include Antarctic and sub-Antarctic fur seals, leopard seals, Weddell seals, southern elephant seals, and crabeater seals.

### Threats and Conservation
Though never permanently inhabited, the islands have been used for fishing and seal hunting. Ecosystems face threats from introduced species, notably on South Georgia, and from overfishing. South Georgia, the South Sandwich Islands, and Bouvet Island are protected as nature reserves, with Bird Island designated a site of special scientific interest. International agreements protect seal populations, which are recovering.

## Terms
- **Scotia Arc**: An undersea ridge and island arc system that frames the Scotia Sea.
- **Scotia Plate**: The tectonic plate upon which the Scotia Sea sits.
- **Antarctic Convergence**: A biological boundary in the Southern Ocean where cold Antarctic waters meet warmer sub-Antarctic waters.
- **Tundra ecoregion**: A biome characterized by low temperatures, short growing seasons, and vegetation like mosses and lichens.
- **Mar Argentino**: An area in the South Atlantic claimed by Argentina, which includes the Scotia Sea region.
- **Continental shelf**: The submerged edge of a continent; about half the Scotia Sea lies above it.

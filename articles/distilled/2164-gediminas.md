# Gediminas

## Overview
Gediminas (c. 1275–1341) was Grand Duke of Lithuania from 1316 until his death. He is a pivotal figure who transformed Lithuania from a regional power into a vast empire stretching from the Baltic to the Black Sea. His reign established the Gediminid dynasty, which later ruled Poland, Hungary, and Bohemia, and he is traditionally credited with founding Vilnius, Lithuania's capital.

## Timeline
- **c. 1275** — Gediminas is born.
- **c. 1316** — Becomes Grand Duke of Lithuania.
- **1319** — Allies with the Tatars against the Teutonic Order.
- **1322–1323** — Engages in diplomatic negotiations with the Papacy, offering conversion in exchange for protection.
- **1323** — Issues circular letters inviting immigrants to Lithuania; signs a compact with papal and other delegates in Vilnius.
- **1323** — Repudiates promises of conversion, dismisses Franciscans, and maintains paganism.
- **1325** — Marries his daughter Aldona to Casimir III of Poland, securing an alliance.
- **1320s–1340s** — Conducts major military campaigns, conquering Ruthenian lands including Kiev (c. 1320s).
- **1338** — Concludes a peace and trade agreement with the Livonian Order, distinguishing Lithuania from Rus'.
- **1341** — Dies, presumably killed in a coup; is cremated in a pagan ceremony.
- **1345** — His son Jaunutis is deposed by Algirdas.

## Body

### Origins and Succession
Gediminas' ancestry and early life are obscure due to scarce sources. Theories about his relationship to his predecessor Vytenis include being his son, brother, cousin, or hostler. Chronicles by the Teutonic Knights claimed he was a hostler who killed Vytenis, while Lithuanian Chronicles claimed he was Vytenis' son, though their similar ages make this unlikely. Recent research suggests his ancestor may have been Skalmantas, with historians debating whether Skalmantas was the father or brother of Vytenis' and Gediminas' father, Butvydas.

### Religious Diplomacy and Paganism
Gediminas' primary goal was to protect Lithuania from the Teutonic and Livonian Orders, who raided under the pretext of conversion. He pursued a complex diplomatic strategy: he negotiated with the Pope, offered privileges to Christians, and even invited immigration, all to gain international support. However, he ultimately refused baptism, likely to avoid angering his pagan subjects in Žemaitija and Aukštaitija and his Orthodox subjects, who threatened him with death if he converted. He maintained paganism while allowing Catholic clergy to minister to existing Catholics, but executed friars who attempted to convert pagans. This strategy allowed him to balance alliances, such as with Poland, while preserving domestic stability.

### Territorial Expansion
From 1316 to 1340, Gediminas aggressively expanded Lithuania south and east into Ruthenian principalities. He defeated Stanislav of Kiev, conquered the city, and installed his relatives as rulers, pushing Lithuania's border near the Black Sea. He avoided war with the powerful Golden Horde and secured alliances through marriage, such as with the Grand Duchy of Moscow. His conquests were often poorly documented, making the exact stages difficult to follow.

### Domestic Rule and Death
Gediminas built fortresses and castles, including in Vilnius, which he established as the permanent capital around 1320. He protected religious minorities and improved the army. He died in 1341, presumably killed in a coup, and was cremated in a pagan ceremony with human sacrifices. He was succeeded by his son Jaunutis, who was later deposed by Algirdas.

## Terms
*   **Grand Duke:** The supreme ruler of Lithuania.
*   **Teutonic Order / Livonian Order:** Military-religious orders that waged constant raids on Lithuania, seeking to conquer and Christianize it.
*   **Ruthenian principalities:** The various principalities in modern-day Belarus and Ukraine, often under Mongol suzerainty, that Gediminas conquered.
*   **Gediminids:** The dynasty founded by Gediminas, which came to rule multiple European kingdoms.
*   **Paganism:** The traditional polytheistic religion of the Lithuanian tribes, which Gediminas maintained despite diplomatic overtures to the Church.
*   **Orthodox Christianity:** The Eastern Christian faith prevalent among Gediminas' Ruthenian subjects.
*   **Catholic Christianity:** The Western Christian faith of the Papacy and the Teutonic Knights, which Gediminas engaged with diplomatically.
*   **Golden Horde:** The Mongol successor state that was a major regional power Gediminas avoided confronting directly.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Gediminas' Origins:** His exact relationship to his predecessor Vytenis (son, brother, cousin, or usurper) remains debated due to conflicting chronicles.
*   **Sincerity of Conversion:** Scholars question whether his letters to the Pope were a genuine request for baptism or solely a diplomatic maneuver to gain protection from the Teutonic Order.
*   **Cause of Death:** The source states he was "presumably killed during a coup d'état," but the exact circumstances are not detailed.
*   **Number of Wives:** Historical sources conflict on whether he had one, two, or three wives, affecting the interpretation of his succession.

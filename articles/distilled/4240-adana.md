# Adana

## Overview
Adana is a major city in southern Turkey, situated on the Seyhan River in the fertile Çukurova plain of the historic Cilicia region. Its strategic location by a Taurus Mountain pass has made it a vital center for trade, agriculture, and administration for over four millennia, shaping its diverse and complex history.

## Timeline
- **c. 1335 BC** — First recorded mention of Adana, ruled by Kizzuwatna under Hittite protection.
- **c. 900 BC** — Neo-Hittite state of Quwê, centered on Adana, is established.
- **612 BC** — Kingdom of Cilicia founded with Adana as a key city.
- **333 BC** — Alexander the Great captures the region after the Battle of Issus.
- **625 AD** — Byzantine Emperor Heraclius defeats Sasanian forces at the Battle of Sarus.
- **704 AD** — Umayyad Caliphate conquers Cilicia, making it a frontier zone.
- **1132 AD** — Armenian Principality of Cilicia captures Adana.
- **1352 AD** — Ramadanid Emirate is established with Adana as its capital.
- **1517 AD** — Ottoman Empire incorporates the Ramadanid Emirate.
- **1832-1840** — Egypt briefly controls Adana under Muhammad Ali Pasha.
- **April 1909** — The Adana massacre results in the deaths of over 20,000 Christians.
- **1921** — France evacuates Cilicia, and Adana is incorporated into modern Turkey.

## Body

### Ancient and Medieval History
Adana's history begins in the Bronze Age, inhabited by Luwians and Hurrians. After the Hittite collapse, it became the capital of the Neo-Hittite state of Quwê. The region was later unified under the Mopsos dynasty. Following the conquests of Alexander the Great, Adana entered the Hellenistic era, briefly adopting the name "Antioch on Sarus" under the Seleucid Empire. It later became part of the Roman and Byzantine Empires, serving as an early Christian bishopric. The city was a contested frontier between Byzantine and Arab forces from the 7th century onward, leading to periods of abandonment and resettlement.

### Armenian and Ottoman Rule
After the Crusades, Adana was captured by the Armenian Kingdom of Cilicia in 1132, becoming a hub for international trade. In the 14th century, the Mamluks established the Ramadanid Emirate, with Adana as its capital, which lasted until Ottoman incorporation in 1517. Direct Ottoman rule began in 1608. In the 19th century, Adana was briefly under Egyptian control (1832-1840), during which modern infrastructure like irrigation canals was built. The city's population grew significantly, becoming a multi-ethnic center of cotton production.

### 20th Century Upheaval and Modernization
The early 20th century saw severe violence against Christian minorities. The Adana massacre of 1909 killed over 20,000 Armenians, Greeks, and Assyrians. During World War I, the Armenian population was systematically deported in 1915. After the Ottoman defeat, France occupied Cilicia (1918-1921) and attempted to establish an autonomous Armenian state, but withdrew under Turkish nationalist pressure. The subsequent "Cilicia Evacuation" led to the mass exodus of most Armenians and Greeks. The 1923 "Law of Abandoned Properties" redistributed their assets, drastically altering the city's demographics. The completion of the Seyhan Dam in 1956 spurred major growth and industrialization.

### Economy and Geography
Adana is the economic heart of the Çukurova plain, one of Turkey's most fertile agricultural regions. It is a leading producer of cotton, corn, soybeans, and citrus fruits. Historically a center for cotton processing, its economy now includes chemical production, food processing, textiles, and automotive manufacturing (e.g., Temsa buses). The city is a major commercial and logistics hub, though it has experienced economic challenges and "brain drain" since the 1990s due to national economic centralization policies. Geographically, it lies on the Seyhan River, which divides the city and provides fertile alluvial soil, though it historically caused floods until embankments were built.

## Terms
- **Çukurova**: The large, fertile plain in southern Turkey where Adana is located; historically known as Cilicia.
- **Cilicia**: A historical region on the Mediterranean coast of Anatolia, significant for its strategic mountain passes and agricultural wealth.
- **Thughūr**: A fortified frontier zone, particularly the border between the Byzantine Empire and the Arab Caliphates.
- **Ramadanid Emirate**: A Turkmen dynasty that ruled Adana and the surrounding plain from 1352 until Ottoman annexation in 1517.
- **Muhacirs**: Muslim immigrants, often from the Balkans or Crete, resettled in Turkey, including in Adana, after population exchanges or conflicts.
- **Crypto-Armenians**: Descendants of Armenians who assimilated into Turkish/Muslim society but may privately maintain Armenian cultural or religious practices.
- **Pavyon**: A traditional Adana entertainment venue, similar to a hostess club, featuring live music and private rooms.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The exact etymology of "Adana" is debated, with theories linking it to an Indo-European river term, a Greek mythological figure, or a local leader.
- The scale of demographic change in the 1920s is complex; while many Armenians and Greeks left, a significant number, including "Crypto-Armenians," remained and assimilated.
- The city's modern economic struggles are attributed to national neo-liberal policies that centralized corporate headquarters in Istanbul, leading to industrial decline and unemployment.

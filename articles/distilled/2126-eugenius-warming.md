# Eugenius Warming

## Overview
Eugen Warming (1841–1924) was a Danish botanist widely regarded as a founder of ecology. His 1895 book *Plantesamfund* is considered the first textbook on plant ecology, establishing the field by explaining how plants adapt to environmental conditions across different regions. His work shifted botanical study from descriptive floristics to functional explanations of plant communities.

## Body

### Early Life and Career
Warming was born on the island of Mandø. After his father's death, he moved to Jutland. He studied natural history at the University of Copenhagen but left for three and a half years (1863–1866) to work with Peter Wilhelm Lund in Brazil. Upon returning, he studied in Munich and Bonn before completing his doctorate in 1871. He held various academic posts, including a professorship in Stockholm (1882–1885), before securing the botany chair at Copenhagen in 1885, a position he held until 1910.

### Major Scientific Works
Warming's early work focused on plant morphology and systematics. His collections from Brazil formed the basis for a monumental 40-volume work on the flora of central Brazil. He later turned to ecology, publishing *Plantesamfund* (1895). This book, based on his lectures, introduced the concept of plant communities and explained how similar environmental problems (e.g., drought, cold) are solved by similar adaptations in plants worldwide, regardless of their species. This functional approach was a major departure from the prevailing descriptive floristics. The book was widely translated and influenced ecologists globally, including Arthur Tansley and Henry Chandler Cowles.

### Teaching and Fieldwork
Warming was a dedicated teacher who authored influential textbooks on systematic and general botany. He believed in taking students into nature, securing government grants for annual excursions to Danish habitats like dunes and salt marshes. His published excursion notes served as practical introductions to plant adaptation. He also led or participated in expeditions to Greenland, Norway, Venezuela, and the Faroe Islands, producing significant works on Arctic and North Atlantic vegetation.

### Later Research and Influence
Warming continued to research plant life-forms, publishing a classification system in 1908 that considered factors beyond wintering, such as drought stress. He was a firm believer in adaptation but a declared Lamarckist, believing inheritance of acquired characters was a primary mechanism of evolution. His influence extended beyond botany; sociologist Robert E. Park used Warming's ecological succession concepts to develop human ecology. In Denmark, he held significant institutional power, including a long tenure on the board of the Carlsberg Foundation.

## Terms
- **Ecology:** The scientific study of the interactions between organisms and their environment. Warming's work helped establish it as a distinct field.
- **Plant community (Plantesamfund):** An assemblage of plant species growing together in a defined area, interacting with each other and their environment.
- **Life-form:** A classification of plants based on their structural and physiological adaptations to their environment, particularly how they survive unfavorable seasons.
- **Floristics:** The study of plant species composition and distribution in a region, often descriptive.
- **Functional ecology:** An approach that explains the structure and dynamics of communities based on the traits and adaptations of organisms.
- **Lamarckism:** A theory of evolution proposing that organisms can pass on characteristics acquired during their lifetime to their offspring.
- **Phenotypic plasticity:** The ability of a single genotype to produce different phenotypes (observable traits) in response to different environmental conditions.
- **Cerrado:** A vast tropical savanna ecoregion of Brazil, a key focus of Warming's ecological studies.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Priority in ecology:** While ecologist Robert J. Goodland called Warming the founder of ecology, the field's development involved multiple contributors. Warming's 1895 book is a landmark, but the German ecologist A.F.W. Schimper published a similar work in 1898 that leaned heavily on Warming's research without proper acknowledgment.
- **Evolutionary mechanisms:** Warming's Lamarckian views placed him at odds with the emerging Darwinian synthesis. He questioned the role of natural selection as the primary driver of evolution, emphasizing inheritance of acquired characters and hybridization instead.
- **Life-form classification:** Warming developed his own complex life-form system, but his student Christen Raunkiær later proposed a simpler, more widely adopted scheme. Warming did not approve of what he saw as Raunkiær's over-simplification.

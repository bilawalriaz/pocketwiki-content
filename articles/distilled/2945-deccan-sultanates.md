# Deccan sultanates

## Overview
The Deccan sultanates were five medieval Persianate Muslim kingdoms on the Deccan Plateau in India, emerging after the collapse of the Bahmani Sultanate. They were significant as rival powers that collectively defeated the Vijayanagara Empire and later fell to the Mughal Empire, while also fostering a unique cultural synthesis in art, architecture, and language.

## Timeline
- **1490** — Ahmadnagar, Bijapur, and Berar declare independence from the Bahmani Sultanate.
- **c. 1492** — Bidar becomes independent.
- **1512** — Golconda declares independence.
- **1565** — The sultanates ally to defeat the Vijayanagara Empire at the Battle of Talikota.
- **1574** — Ahmadnagar invades and annexes Berar.
- **1596** — The Mughal Empire strips Berar from Ahmadnagar.
- **1616–1636** — The Mughal Empire conquers Ahmadnagar.
- **1619** — Bijapur annexes Bidar.
- **1686–1687** — The Mughal Emperor Aurangzeb conquers Golconda and Bijapur.

## Body

### Origins and Rivalry
The five sultanates—Ahmadnagar, Berar, Bidar, Bijapur, and Golconda—were founded by rulers of diverse origins, including Marathi, Kannadiga, Georgian, and Iranian backgrounds. All claimed legitimacy as successors to the Bahmani Sultanate, continuing to use its coins. Despite frequent rivalry, they allied against the Vijayanagara Empire in 1565, destroying its capital and permanently weakening it. The sultanates were later absorbed by the Mughal Empire through a series of conquests and annexations between 1596 and 1687.

### Political History of Individual Sultanates
**Ahmadnagar** was founded by Malik Ahmad Nizam Shah I after defeating a Bahmani army in 1490. It later adopted Nizari Shi'ism as its state religion. The sultanate was eventually conquered by the Mughals, with a final resistance led by Malik Ambar ending in 1636.

**Berar** was founded by Fathullah Imad-ul-Mulk, a Kannadiga Hindu captured and raised as a Muslim. It was annexed by Ahmadnagar in 1574.

**Bidar**, the smallest sultanate, was founded by Qasim Barid I, a Georgian slave. It was annexed by Bijapur in 1619.

**Bijapur** was ruled by the Adil Shahi dynasty, whose founder may have been a Georgian slave. It expanded after the Battle of Talikota and was conquered by the Mughals in 1686.

**Golconda** was founded by Sultan Quli Qutb-ul-Mulk, an Iranian Turkmen migrant. It was conquered by the Mughals in 1687.

### Cultural Contributions
The sultanates fostered a vibrant Persianate culture. They contributed to the development of the Dakhani language, a precursor to Dakhani Urdu, and produced distinctive Deccani miniature painting. Architectural achievements include the Gol Gumbaz in Bijapur and the Charminar in Hyderabad. The rulers generally displayed religious tolerance, employing Hindus in high administrative posts and patronizing local arts and literature.

## Terms
- **Persianate**: A culture influenced by Persian language, arts, and administration.
- **Deccan Plateau**: A large plateau in southern India, south of the Vindhya Range.
- **Bahmani Sultanate**: A medieval Muslim empire in the Deccan that fragmented into the five sultanates.
- **Nizari Shi'ism**: A branch of Shia Islam that was established as the state religion in Ahmadnagar.
- **Deccani Muslim**: Muslims native to the Deccan region, as opposed to foreign-born nobles.
- **Battle of Talikota (1565)**: A decisive battle where the sultanates defeated the Vijayanagara Empire.
- **Dakhani language**: An Indo-Aryan language that developed in the Deccan, blending Persian, Arabic, and local languages.
- **Bidriware**: A type of metalwork from Bidar featuring zinc alloy inlaid with silver and brass.

## Debates and Open Questions
The precise ethnic origins of the founders of some sultanates, particularly Bijapur, are debated, with sources suggesting they may have been Georgian slaves or of Persian/Turkmen origin. The Brahmin lineage claimed for the founder of Ahmadnagar is also noted as possibly being a genealogical topos rather than historical fact.

# Ferdinand Marcos

## Overview
Ferdinand Marcos was the tenth president of the Philippines (1965–1986). He established a dictatorship under martial law in 1972, which lasted until 1981. His regime is condemned as a kleptocracy, marked by massive corruption, human rights abuses, and economic mismanagement, ending with his ouster by the People Power Revolution in 1986.

## Timeline
- **September 11, 1917** — Born in Sarrat, Ilocos Norte.
- **1940** — Convicted (later overturned) for the assassination of Julio Nalundasan.
- **December 30, 1965** — Inaugurated as the 10th President of the Philippines.
- **March 18, 1968** — Jabidah massacre occurs, a catalyst for the Moro insurgency.
- **September 21, 1972** — Declares martial law, beginning his rule as a dictator.
- **January 17, 1981** — Formally lifts martial law.
- **August 21, 1983** — Opposition leader Benigno "Ninoy" Aquino Jr. is assassinated.
- **February 7, 1986** — Holds a snap presidential election, officially declared the winner amid fraud allegations.
- **February 25, 1986** — Flees the Philippines after the People Power Revolution; Corazon Aquino becomes president.
- **September 28, 1989** — Dies in exile in Honolulu, Hawaii.
- **November 18, 2016** — Remains are buried at the Libingan ng mga Bayani (Heroes' Cemetery).

## Body

### Early Life and Political Rise
Born to a lawyer-politician father, Marcos studied law at the University of the Philippines. His early career was marked by the 1935 assassination of political rival Julio Nalundasan, for which he and his father were convicted; the conviction was later overturned. During World War II, he served in the U.S. Army and was a prisoner of war. After the war, he entered politics, serving in the House of Representatives (1949–1959) and the Senate (1959–1965), where he became Senate President. He advanced his career by exaggerating his military record, claiming to be the "most decorated war hero in the Philippines," a claim later debunked.

### Presidency and Declaration of Martial Law
Marcos was elected president in 1965 and re-elected in 1969. His first term focused on massive infrastructure projects funded by foreign loans, boosting popularity but increasing national debt. His second term faced economic crises and social unrest, including the "First Quarter Storm" of student protests in 1970. Amidst this turmoil, and following the Plaza Miranda bombing (1971) and a series of Metro Manila bombings in 1972, Marcos declared martial law on September 23, 1972. He justified this as necessary to create a "Bagong Lipunan" (New Society), suspending Congress, arresting opposition leaders, and ruling by decree.

### The Dictatorship (1972–1986)
Under martial law, Marcos consolidated power, controlling the military and media. His regime was characterized by the "Rolex 12," a group of key advisors. While the formal end of martial law came in 1981, Marcos retained broad powers and won a third term in an election boycotted by the main opposition. The economy, fueled by debt, collapsed in the early 1980s. The 1983 assassination of opposition leader Ninoy Aquino sparked massive public outrage. In 1986, Marcos called a snap election, which was marred by fraud. The resulting People Power Revolution, a mass civilian protest, led to his ouster and exile to Hawaii.

### Human Rights Abuses and Kleptocracy
The Marcos regime committed widespread human rights abuses, including extrajudicial killings, enforced disappearances, torture, and mass arrests of political opponents, activists, and suspected communists and Muslims. Estimates of victims range from 1,500 to 4,000 killed or disappeared, with tens of thousands imprisoned or tortured. The regime is also infamous for its kleptocracy. The Marcos family and their cronies allegedly looted billions of dollars from the Philippine economy. The Presidential Commission on Good Government estimates Ferdinand Marcos stole between $5 billion and $10 billion. This wealth was hidden in overseas bank accounts and real estate, leading to numerous posthumous lawsuits and asset recovery efforts.

## Terms
*   **Kleptocracy:** A government with corrupt leaders who use power to exploit the people and natural resources for personal wealth and political power.
*   **Martial Law:** The imposition of direct military control or suspension of civil law by a government, especially in response to an emergency.
*   **Bagong Lipunan:** Tagalog for "New Society," the name of Marcos's political movement and ideology during martial law.
*   **People Power Revolution:** A series of nonviolent mass protests that peacefully overthrew the Marcos government in 1986.
*   **Edifice Complex:** A term for Marcos's policy of massive, often rushed, infrastructure construction to curry public support.
*   **Conjugal Dictatorship:** A term describing the joint rule of Ferdinand and Imelda Marcos, highlighting her significant political power.
*   **Rolex 12:** The informal name for the group of twelve key advisors who managed the Marcos regime during martial law.
*   **Ill-gotten Wealth:** Assets acquired through illegal or unethical means, specifically referring to the wealth amassed by the Marcos family.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **The Plaza Miranda Bombing (1971):** Responsibility is disputed. The Marcos government blamed the Communist Party, but some historians and declassified U.S. intelligence documents implicate Marcos himself in a false flag operation.
*   **Marcos's War Record:** Marcos claimed to be the most decorated Filipino war hero of World War II. These claims have been widely debunked by researchers and official records.
*   **The Jabidah Massacre (1968):** While the massacre of Moro recruits is documented, the full extent of official involvement and its direct link to Marcos's orders remains a point of historical investigation.
*   **Scale of Stolen Wealth:** While estimates range from $5 billion to $10 billion, the precise total and the full extent of hidden assets remain unknown due to the complexity of the financial networks used.

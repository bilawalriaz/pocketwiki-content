# Women's history

## Overview
Women's history is the study of women's roles, experiences, and contributions throughout history, challenging traditional narratives that have minimized or ignored them. It emerged as a formal field in the 1960s and 1970s, driven by feminist activists and scholars who sought to recover lost stories and integrate women's perspectives into mainstream historical understanding. This field is inherently a form of historical revisionism, aiming to correct the gender bias of conventional history.

## Timeline
- **1801** — Hester Thrale publishes *Retrospection...*, an early attempt at popular history from a female perspective.
- **1865** — The Allgemeiner Deutscher Frauenverein (ADF) is founded in Germany, demanding women's rights to education, employment, and political participation.
- **1907** — Finland becomes the first European country to elect women to its national legislature.
- **1920s** — Women's suffrage is granted in major countries including the United States, Canada, and Great Britain.
- **1928** — Chen Dongyuan publishes *Zhongguo fu nü sheng Huo shi*, the first systematic introduction to women's history in China.
- **1963** — Gerda Lerner offers the first regular college course in women's history in the United States.
- **1972** — Sarah Lawrence College establishes the first American Master of Arts program in Women's History.
- **1980s** — Women's history as a field develops significantly in Japan and Scotland.
- **1985** — Deborah Gray White publishes *'Ar'n't I a Woman?*, pioneering the integration of race and slavery into women's history.
- **1989** — The collapse of Communism in Eastern Europe leads to new, though uneven, developments in women's history scholarship.
- **2017** — The #MeToo movement draws global attention to sexual harassment and assault.

## Body

### Development of the Field
The formal study of women's history began in the United States and Britain, led by second-wave feminist historians influenced by social history. They argued that traditional history, written mainly by and about men, had excluded women's experiences. Early efforts were recuperative, seeking to recover lost stories. Over time, the field evolved to integrate women's experiences into broader historical narratives and became part of interdisciplinary areas like gender studies and feminist theory.

### Regional Scholarship
The development of women's history has varied globally. In France, extensive scholarship exists despite a lack of dedicated university programs. In Eastern Europe, the field was delayed by political atmospheres and institutional resistance, with significant progress only after 1989. In the Middle East, scholarship began in the 1930s-40s, was revitalized by the feminist movement in the 1980s, and now grapples with debates about Orientalism and the applicability of Western feminism. In China, the field was systematized by Chen Dongyuan's 1928 work, and recent research uses the concept of gender to yield new knowledge.

### Key Themes and Debates
Scholars debate the impact of capitalism on women's status, with some arguing it reduced women's economic importance and others that it created conditions for emancipation. The history of women's employment shows they have always worked, though often in different sectors than men. Themes also include the fight for political rights, the social construction of sexuality and reproduction, and the role of women in warfare, particularly on the home front during total war. A major ongoing debate concerns the role of Islam in women's lives in the Middle East, with scholars split between critical and defensive interpretations.

## Terms
- **Historical revisionism**: The reinterpretation of historical facts and narratives, often to challenge established consensus.
- **Second-wave feminism**: The feminist movement from the early 1960s to the late 1980s, focusing on issues like workplace equality, reproductive rights, and sexuality.
- **Social history**: A branch of history focusing on the experiences and lives of ordinary people, rather than political elites.
- **Gender studies**: An interdisciplinary field examining how gender identity and relations shape society and culture.
- **Orientalism**: A Western scholarly and artistic tradition that often depicts Eastern societies as exotic, backward, or inferior.
- **Protofeminism**: Feminist ideas and activism that predate the formal feminist movements of the 19th and 20th centuries.
- **Comfort women**: Women and girls who were forced into sexual slavery by the Imperial Japanese Army before and during World War II.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Impact of Capitalism**: Did capitalism negatively impact women's status by confining them to domestic roles, or did it create conditions for their eventual emancipation?
- **Role of Islam**: To what extent has Islam shaped women's oppression in the Middle East, versus socioeconomic and political factors? Scholarship is often influenced by geopolitical tensions between the West and the Middle East.
- **Applicability of Western Feminism**: Can Western feminist frameworks be directly applied to women's history in non-Western regions, or do they risk being a form of Orientalism? Scholars advocate for transnational and collaborative approaches.
- **Institutional Resistance**: Why has the institutionalization of women's and gender history faced resistance in some regions (e.g., France, Hungary), and how does this affect scholarship?

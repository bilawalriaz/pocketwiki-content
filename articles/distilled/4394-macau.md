# Macau

## Overview
Macau is a Special Administrative Region of China, a former Portuguese colony that returned to Chinese sovereignty in 1999. It is the most densely populated region in the world and a global gambling hub, with an economy heavily reliant on casino gaming and tourism. Its unique history has created a blend of Chinese and Portuguese cultures, recognized as a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

## Timeline
- **1557** — Portugal leases Macau as a trading post from the Ming dynasty, paying annual rent.
- **1887** — Portugal gains perpetual colonial rights with the Sino-Portuguese Treaty of Peking.
- **1966** — The 12-3 incident riot leads to Portugal losing full control, cooperating with the Chinese Communist Party.
- **1999** — Macau is handed back to China, becoming a Special Administrative Region.
- **2002** — Casino industry is liberalized, ending a 40-year monopoly and attracting foreign investment.
- **2005** — The Historic Centre of Macau is designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
- **2018** — The Hong Kong–Zhuhai–Macau Bridge opens, providing a direct land link.
- **2019** — Phase 1 of the Macau Light Rapid Transit (LRT) begins operations.

## Body

### History and Governance
The region was first leased to Portugal in 1557 as a trading post. Portugal administered it under Chinese sovereignty until 1887, when it gained perpetual colonial rights. The territory remained under Portuguese rule until the 1999 handover. Today, Macau is a Special Administrative Region of China operating under the "one country, two systems" principle, which guarantees its separate governing and economic systems for 50 years after the handover. Its government has an executive-led structure with a Chief Executive, a Legislative Assembly, and a judiciary based on Portuguese civil law. The central government in Beijing handles defense and foreign affairs, but Macau maintains separate economic and cultural relations.

### Geography and Demographics
Macau consists of the Macau Peninsula, the islands of Taipa and Coloane, and the Cotai reclamation zone. It is the most densely populated region in the world, with a population of about 667,400 in 2018. The population is overwhelmingly Chinese (88.7%), with significant Filipino, Vietnamese, and Portuguese minorities. Cantonese is the predominant language, followed by Mandarin and English. Portuguese is an official language but spoken by only 2.3% of the population. The territory has a humid subtropical climate and has tripled its land area through reclamation in the last century.

### Economy
Macau has a capitalist service economy dominated by casino gaming and tourism. Its gambling industry is the largest in the world, generating revenue about seven times that of Las Vegas. Taxes from gambling fund a robust welfare system. The economy was liberalized in 2002, allowing foreign investors and triggering rapid growth. However, this heavy reliance on a single sector creates economic vulnerability. The government has attempted to diversify the economy, but gaming still accounted for 49.1% of GDP in recent years. The vast majority of casino patrons are tourists from mainland China.

### Culture and Heritage
Macau's culture is a blend of Chinese and Portuguese influences, reflected in its architecture, festivals, and cuisine. Its historic centre, featuring around twenty-five historic locations, is a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Cuisine is a fusion of Cantonese and Portuguese styles, with unique Macanese dishes. Major annual events include the Macau Grand Prix and various traditional Chinese and Catholic festivals. The government has faced criticism for urban development projects that threaten heritage sites, such as the Guia Lighthouse.

## Terms
- **Special Administrative Region (SAR):** A provincial-level administrative division of China that enjoys a high degree of autonomy under the "one country, two systems" principle.
- **"One country, two systems":** A constitutional principle describing the governance of Hong Kong and Macau, allowing them to maintain their own economic and legal systems separate from mainland China.
- **Basic Law:** The mini-constitution of the Macau SAR, guaranteeing its systems and way of life for 50 years after the 1999 handover.
- **Cotai:** A major area of reclaimed land between the islands of Taipa and Coloane, home to many large casinos and resorts.
- **Cantonese:** A variety of Chinese originating in Guangdong province and the predominant language spoken in Macau.
- **Macanese Patois (Patuá):** A local creole language blending Portuguese with Cantonese and other languages, now spoken by only a few elderly residents.
- **Haijin:** A series of maritime trade bans imposed by the Ming and Qing dynasties, which affected Macau's trade routes.
- **Canton System:** A 18th-century Qing dynasty policy restricting foreign trade to the port of Canton (Guangzhou), with Macau as a required first stop.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Economic Diversification:** There is ongoing concern about Macau's over-reliance on the gambling industry and the government's efforts to diversify the economy.
- **Heritage Conservation vs. Development:** Tensions exist between urban development projects and the preservation of Macau's UNESCO World Heritage sites, with criticism that the government prioritizes development over conservation.
- **Political Autonomy:** While generally congenial, the relationship with Beijing involves debates over jurisdictional independence, national security legislation, and the central government's adherence to the "one country, two systems" principle.

# Zambia

## Overview
Zambia is a landlocked, resource-rich country in Southern Africa, whose modern identity was shaped by Bantu migrations, colonial exploitation of its copper reserves, and a post-independence political journey from one-party rule to a stable multiparty democracy. Its economy remains heavily dependent on copper mining, making it vulnerable to global price fluctuations, while its diverse population practices a blend of Christianity and traditional beliefs.

## Timeline
- **c. 300,000–125,000 BC** — Fossil remains of Broken Hill Man (Kabwe Man) indicate early human habitation.
- **c. AD 300** — Bantu peoples begin migrating into the region, displacing earlier Khoisan inhabitants.
- **14th–17th C.** — The Luba and Lunda kingdoms, major Central African states, exert influence over the area.
- **1888** — The British South Africa Company obtains mineral rights from the Lozi people.
- **1911** — The British protectorates of Barotziland–North-Western Rhodesia and North-Eastern Rhodesia are merged to form Northern Rhodesia.
- **1953** — The Federation of Rhodesia and Nyasaland is created, grouping Northern Rhodesia with Southern Rhodesia and Nyasaland.
- **24 Oct 1964** — Northern Rhodesia becomes the independent Republic of Zambia, with Kenneth Kaunda as president.
- **1972–1991** — Zambia is a one-party state under Kaunda's United National Independence Party (UNIP).
- **1991** — Multiparty elections are held; Kaunda is defeated by Frederick Chiluba, marking a democratic transition.
- **2012** — Zambia wins the Africa Cup of Nations football tournament for the first time.
- **2021** — Hakainde Hichilema wins the presidential election, marking another peaceful transfer of power.

## Body

### Prehistory and Early Migrations
The area shows evidence of early human habitation, including the Broken Hill Man fossil. Around AD 300, migrating Bantu peoples began to settle the region, eventually forming various kingdoms and societies. The Luba and Lunda states, originating in the Congo Basin, had a profound influence, with many Zambian ethnic groups tracing their ancestry to them. Other significant pre-colonial states included the Maravi Confederacy and the Mutapa Empire. The 19th-century Mfecane, a period of widespread chaos and displacement caused by the rise of the Zulu kingdom, led to further migrations into the area, including by the Ngoni and Lozi peoples.

### Colonial Era and Independence
European exploration began in the late 18th century, followed by British colonization driven by mineral interests. The British South Africa Company administered the territory, which was named Northern Rhodesia in 1911. It was grouped into the Federation of Rhodesia and Nyasaland in 1953. After the federation's dissolution, Zambia gained independence in 1964 under President Kenneth Kaunda. The new nation faced challenges including a lack of trained administrators and an economy dependent on foreign expertise and copper exports. Kaunda's government pursued a socialist policy and supported anti-colonial movements in neighboring countries, which sometimes led to regional tensions.

### Political History and Governance
Kaunda's UNIP ruled as the sole legal party from 1972 to 1991. Economic decline, driven by falling copper prices and rising debt, led to civil unrest and pressure for reform. In 1991, Zambia transitioned to a multiparty democracy, with Kaunda losing the election to Frederick Chiluba. Since then, Zambia has experienced several peaceful transfers of power between competing parties. The government is a presidential representative democratic republic. Recent elections, such as in 2015, 2016, and 2021, have been competitive, though sometimes contested, with the 2021 election seeing a high voter turnout and a decisive victory for Hakainde Hichilema.

### Economy and Resources
Zambia's economy is dominated by mining, particularly copper, which accounts for the majority of export earnings. The industry was nationalized in 1973 but later privatized in the late 1990s. The country is Africa's second-largest copper producer. Agriculture provides more jobs than mining but is largely subsistence-based. Tourism, centered on Victoria Falls and national parks, is a growing sector. Despite mineral wealth, Zambia faces high poverty rates, with 47.9% of the population affected by multidimensional poverty as of 2018. The government is pursuing economic diversification to reduce reliance on copper.

### Demographics and Society
Zambia is highly urbanized, with the population concentrated around Lusaka and the Copperbelt Province. It is a multi-ethnic nation with over 70 recognized groups, the largest being the Bemba, Nyanja, and Tonga. English is the official language, while Bemba and Nyanja are widely spoken locally. The country is officially a Christian nation, with about 98% of the population identifying as Christian, though many incorporate traditional beliefs. Zambia hosts a significant number of refugees, primarily from the Democratic Republic of the Congo and Angola.

## Terms
- **Bantu expansion**: A millennia-long migration of Bantu-speaking peoples from West-Central Africa across much of the continent, bringing iron-working technology.
- **Copperbelt**: A region in north-central Zambia and DRC rich in copper deposits, forming the core of Zambia's mining economy.
- **Luba-Lunda states**: A group of interconnected Central African kingdoms (Luba and Lunda) that influenced political and cultural development in the region before the colonial era.
- **Mfecane**: A period of widespread chaos and displacement in Southern Africa during the early 19th century, triggered by the rise of the Zulu kingdom.
- **Northern Rhodesia**: The name of the territory during British colonial rule, from 1911 until independence as Zambia in 1964.
- **One-party state**: A political system where a single political party is legally permitted to hold power, as in Zambia from 1972 to 1991.
- **Victoria Falls (Mosi-o-Tunya)**: A massive waterfall on the Zambezi River at the border with Zimbabwe, a major tourist attraction and UNESCO World Heritage Site.

## Debates and open questions
- The precise number of distinct languages versus dialects in Zambia is debated, with estimates ranging from 20 to 73.
- The economic impact and social integration of significant Chinese investment and immigration in Zambia is a contemporary issue, with reports of both economic benefits and labor controversies.
- The government's handling of civil liberties, including press freedom and the criminalization of homosexuality, draws international criticism and domestic debate.

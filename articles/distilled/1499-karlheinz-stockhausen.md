# Karlheinz Stockhausen

## Overview
Karlheinz Stockhausen (1928–2007) was a German composer whose radical innovations in electronic music, spatialization, aleatory (chance) techniques, and formal structure made him a central, polarizing figure in 20th-century music. As a leading member of the Darmstadt School, he moved from "pointist" serialism to "group composition," treating time as a unified continuum where rhythm, pitch, and form are spectral manifestations of a single duration. His theoretical writings, especially "... wie die Zeit vergeht ..." (1957), provided a compositional framework for the postwar avant-garde. He directed the WDR Electronic Music Studio (1963–2000), pioneered live electronics and spatial projection (notably at Expo '70), and composed the seven-opera cycle *Licht* (1977–2003) based on a "superformula" derived from mystical and scientific concepts. His influence extends beyond art music into jazz, rock (The Beatles, Pink Floyd, Can, Kraftwerk), and electronic dance music.

## Body

### Early Life and Trauma
Stockhausen was born in the manor house Burg Mödrath. His mother Gertrud suffered a breakdown after three pregnancies and was institutionalized in 1932; she was murdered in the gas chamber at Hadamar on 27 May 1941 under the Nazi "Action T4" program (officially falsified as leukemia). His father Simon, a schoolteacher, was conscripted and went missing in action in Hungary in 1945. Stockhausen was conscripted as a stretcher bearer in late 1944. These traumas—maternal murder, paternal loss, wartime service—recur symbolically in *Licht* (e.g., *Donnerstag* act 1 scene 2 "Mondeva").

### Education and Darmstadt School
After studying music pedagogy and piano at the Hochschule für Musik Köln (1947–51), Stockhausen attended the 1951 Darmstadt Courses, where he met Karel Goeyvaerts and resolved to study with Olivier Messiaen in Paris (1952–53). He studied analysis and aesthetics with Messiaen but left Darius Milhaud's composition class quickly. In 1953 he joined the new NWDR Electronic Music Studio in Cologne under Herbert Eimert, becoming its director in 1963. From 1954–56 he studied phonetics, acoustics, and information theory with Werner Meyer-Eppler at the University of Bonn, which grounded his "statistical" compositional criteria. With Eimert he co-edited the journal *Die Reihe* (1955–62).

### Electronic Music and Spatialization
Stockhausen's first electronic works were *Studie I* and *II* (1953, 1954). *Gesang der Jünglinge* (1955–56), realized in 5-channel tape, fused electronically generated sine tones with a recorded boy's voice, spatializing sound around the listener. He theorized spatial music in lectures ("Music in Space," 1958), envisioning a spherical hall with a central audience platform surrounded by loudspeakers. This was realized at Expo '70 in Osaka: architect Fritz Bornemann built a spherical auditorium with seven rings of loudspeakers; Stockhausen performed 5½-hour daily programs for 183 days to ~1 million listeners. Later electronic works include *Telemusik* (1966, incorporating world music), *Hymnen* (1966–67, national anthems), and *Cosmic Pulses* (2006–07, 24 layers over 8 speakers).

### Serialism, Group Composition, and Variable Form
Rejecting Schoenberg's twelve-tone row, Stockhausen developed *punktuelle Musik* ("punctual/pointist" music)—isolated notes defined by pitch, duration, dynamics, timbre (*Kreuzspiel*, *Klavierstücke I–IV*, *Kontra-Punkte*). Simultaneously he formulated *group composition*: statistical fields of density, duration, and register (*Kontra-Punkte*, *Punkte*, *Gruppen*). His 1957 article "... wie die Zeit vergeht ..." derived a tempo scale (12 tempos = chromatic scale), subdivisions analogous to the overtone series, "partial fields" (time fields), and the concept of *variable form*: because electronic music is fixed, instrumental music should allow performer choice (hall acoustics, individual capability). *Zyklus* (1959) uses graphic notation readable from any page, upside down, or right-to-left (*polyvalent form*). *Klavierstück XI* (1956) is open form; *Momente* (1962–64/69) is closed but permutable.

### Process and Intuitive Music (1960s)
In the 1960s Stockhausen moved toward *process composition*: *Plus-Minus* (1963) provides note material and transformation rules yielding unlimited realizations. *Prozession* (1967), *Kurzwellen* and *Spiral* (1968) use shortwave radios and live transformation. *Aus den sieben Tagen* (1968) and *Für kommende Zeiten* (1968–70) are verbally described "intuitive music"—performers play from internal listening, not notation. He pioneered live electronics: *Mixtur* (1964/67/2003, orchestra + ring modulation), *Mikrophonie I* (1964, tam-tam + microphones/filters), *Mikrophonie II* (1965, choir + ring modulators), *Solo* (1966, melody instrument + feedback).

### Formula Composition and *Licht* (1970s–2003)
*Mantra* (1970) introduced *formula composition*: a single melodic formula (here, a 13-note pair) is projected, multiplied, and expanded across all parameters. This technique structures *Inori* (1973–74), *Harlekin/Der kleine Harlekin* (1975), *Jubiläum* (1977), and the entire *Licht* cycle. *Licht: Die sieben Tage der Woche* (seven operas, 1977–2003) assigns each weekday archetypal traits (Monday=birth, Tuesday=conflict, etc.) and centers on three characters: Michael, Lucifer, Eve. Each dominates a "solo" opera (*Donnerstag*, *Samstag*, *Montag*); pairings dominate three others; all three combine in *Mittwoch*. The *Licht superformula* (three-layered: Michael, Eve, Lucifer) generates all melodic, harmonic, rhythmic, and formal material. Influences include Japanese Noh theatre, Judeo-Christian/Vedic ritual, and Sri Aurobindo's "supramental" Truth-Consciousness. *Helikopter-Streichquartett* (1993, *Mittwoch* scene 3) places a string quartet in four helicopters; sound and video are transmitted to the hall.

### Late Cycle: *Klang* (2004–07)
After *Licht*, Stockhausen began *Klang* ("Sound"), 21 pieces based on the 24 hours of the day (21 completed). Hour 1: *Himmelfahrt* (organ/synth, soprano, tenor); Hour 5: *Harmonien* (solo flute/bass clarinet/trumpet); Hour 13: *Cosmic Pulses* (24-layer electronic); Hours 14–21: solo voice/instrument with 3-layer electronic accompaniment from *Cosmic Pulses*. The cycle premiered complete in 2010 at MusikTriennale Köln (176 concerts).

### Theoretical Writings
Stockhausen's *Texte zur Musik* (10 volumes) contain analyses of Webern, Mozart, Boulez, etc., but his own compositional theory is most influential. Key articles: "... wie die Zeit vergeht ..." (1957), "Elektronische und Instrumentale Musik" (1958), "Musik im Raum" (1958), "Momentform" (1960), "Die Einheit der musikalischen Zeit" (1961). He unified time: "the different experienced components such as colour, harmony and melody, meter and rhythm, dynamics, and form correspond to the different segmental ranges of this unified time"—the total musical result is the "timbre" (spectrum) of a fundamental duration. Critics (e.g., Stockhausen himself later) noted polemics around these theories; he paused theoretical publication 1961–70.

### Reception and Influence
**Art music:** Stravinsky studied *Gruppen* and *Zeitmaße* (influencing *Threni*, *Movements*, *Variations*). Boulez called him "greatest living composer... my peer." Birtwistle, Ferneyhough, Rihm, Barrett, Nørgård, Andriessen, Éloy cite decisive influence. **Jazz:** Miles Davis, Mingus, Hancock, Lateef, Braxton. **Pop/Rock:** The Beatles (cover of *Sgt. Pepper*), Zappa (*Freak Out!*), The Who (Townshend), Pink Floyd (Wright, Waters), Jefferson Airplane, Grateful Dead. **German experimental:** Can (Schmidt, Czukay studied with him), Kraftwerk (studied with him). **Electronic dance:** Aphex Twin, Plastikman, Scanner, Pemberton (BBC 1995 exchange).

### Controversies
- **Fresco premiere (1969):** Musicians protested "glissandos no faster than one octave per minute"; placards ("Stockhausen-Zoo") placed on stands; lights cut; performance abandoned after 260 minutes.
- **Sirius claims:** In *Die Zeit* (2007) he stated "I was educated at Sirius and want to return there." Conductor Michael Gielen called it "hubris and nonsense."
- **9/11 remarks (16 Sep 2001):** Called the attacks "the biggest work of art there has ever been... Lucifer's greatest work of art." Hamburg festival cancelled; daughter Majella dropped surname. He later clarified he meant "work of destruction personified in Lucifer."

## Terms
- ****punktuelle Musik**** — "Punctual/pointist" music: isolated notes each defined by individual pitch, duration, dynamics, timbre; rejects twelve-tone rows.
- ****group composition**** — Statistical organization of sound "groups" by density, register, duration; treats time as a field of probabilities rather than fixed points.
- ****variable form**** — Instrumental/vocal music where performer choices (acoustics, capability) determine aspects of the work, contrasting fixed electronic tape.
- ****polyvalent form**** — Score allows multiple valid realizations: open form (incomplete, e.g., *Klavierstück XI*) or closed form (permutable modules, e.g., *Momente*).
- ****process composition**** — Work provides material + transformation rules; each performance generates a unique realization (*Plus-Minus*, *Prozession*, *Spiral*).
- ****intuitive music**** — Verbally instructed music; performers play from internal listening without notation (*Aus den sieben Tagen*).
- ****live electronics**** — Real-time electronic transformation of acoustic instruments during performance (*Mikrophonie I/II*, *Mixtur*, *Solo*).
- ****formula composition**** — A single melodic formula (or superformula) is projected, multiplied, and expanded to generate all parameters of a work (*Mantra*, *Licht*).
- ****superformula**** — In *Licht*, a three-layered formula (Michael, Eve, Lucifer) that generates the entire cycle's melodic, harmonic, rhythmic, and formal structure.
- ****spatialization**** — Compositional projection of sound in physical space (multi-channel tape, distributed orchestras, spherical auditoriums).

## Debates and open questions
- **Theoretical coherence vs. polemics:** Stockhausen's 1950s temporal theories (unified time as timbre) were attacked as pseudo-scientific; he stopped publishing theory for a decade. Scholars debate whether the theory illuminates the music or imposes a retrospective system.
- **Intuitive music's ontology:** *Aus den sieben Tagen* raises questions: is it composition (fixed verbal instructions) or improvisation (no notation)? Where does authorship lie?
- **Formula composition's rigidity:** Critics argue *Licht*'s superformula constrains surface variety; defenders hear it as a "genetic code" enabling maximal diversity from minimal material.
- **9/11 remarks:** Whether Stockhausen's "Lucifer's greatest work of art" was a theological metaphor (Lucifer = cosmic rebellion) or an aestheticization of terror remains contested; the fallout damaged his late public reception.
- **Sirius cosmology:** His claims of education on/from Sirius—metaphor, mystical conviction, or neurological condition?—complicate biographical interpretation of *Licht* and *Sirius* (1975–77).

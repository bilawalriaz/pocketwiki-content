# Kenneth Kaunda

## Overview
Kenneth Kaunda (1924–2021) was Zambia's first president, leading the country from independence in 1964 until 1991. A key figure in the anti-colonial struggle, he later established a one-party socialist state and oversaw a period of economic decline. His legacy is complex, encompassing both his role as a liberation leader and his authoritarian rule.

## Timeline
- **28 April 1924** — Born at Lubwa Mission, Chinsali, Northern Rhodesia.
- **1949** — Entered politics as a founding member of the Northern Rhodesian African National Congress (ANC).
- **October 1958** — Broke from the ANC to form the Zambian African National Congress (ZANC).
- **January 1960** — Released from prison and elected president of the United National Independence Party (UNIP).
- **24 October 1964** — Became the first president of independent Zambia.
- **1968** — Initiated the Mulungushi Reforms to nationalise foreign-owned companies.
- **27 June 1973** — Signed the Choma Declaration, leading to a one-party state constitution.
- **1973** — Economic crisis began due to the oil crisis and falling copper prices.
- **2 November 1991** — Lost multi-party elections to Frederick Chiluba and peacefully handed over power.
- **17 June 2021** — Died in Lusaka at the age of 97.

## Body

### Early Life and Independence Struggle
Born to missionary parents, Kaunda was a teacher before entering politics. He was influenced by Mahatma Gandhi's writings. He co-founded the Northern Rhodesian ANC but later broke away to form ZANC in 1958, leading to his imprisonment. After his release, he became president of UNIP, the successor party. He led civil disobedience campaigns and negotiated for independence, which was achieved on 24 October 1964.

### Presidency and Domestic Policy
Kaunda's government invested heavily in education, establishing the University of Zambia in 1966 and providing free school materials. Economically, he pursued nationalisation through the Mulungushi Reforms, taking control of key industries like copper mining. However, a combination of socialist agricultural policies, the 1973 oil crisis, and a slump in copper prices led to severe economic crisis and debt. Politically, he consolidated power, banning all parties except UNIP after 1973 and establishing a one-party state under the ideology of "Zambian Humanism."

### Foreign Policy
Kaunda was a prominent anti-apartheid activist and supported liberation movements in neighbouring countries like Zimbabwe, Angola, and Namibia, allowing them to use Zambia as a base. This stance strained Zambia's economy but earned him international stature. He was a leader in the Non-Aligned Movement and maintained complex relationships with both Western powers (like the US) and Eastern bloc nations (like the USSR and Yugoslavia). He also mediated in regional conflicts, such as the Victoria Falls Conference on Rhodesia.

### Fall from Power and Later Life
Mounting economic problems and public unrest in 1990 forced Kaunda to reverse his one-party rule. He allowed a referendum and then multi-party elections. In the 1991 election, he was decisively defeated by Frederick Chiluba of the Movement for Multi-Party Democracy (MMD) and peacefully relinquished power. After his presidency, he clashed with the new government, which attempted to strip him of citizenship. He later focused on charitable work, notably the fight against HIV/AIDS.

## Terms
*   **UNIP (United National Independence Party):** The political party led by Kaunda that fought for and governed Zambia after independence.
*   **One-party state:** A political system where a single political party is legally permitted to hold power, which Kaunda established in 1973.
*   **Zambian Humanism:** The official state ideology combining socialist economic planning with traditional African communal values.
*   **Mulungushi Reforms:** A 1968 policy to nationalise foreign-owned companies, particularly in the mining and financial sectors.
*   **Non-Aligned Movement (NAM):** A forum of states not formally aligned with any major power bloc during the Cold War, which Kaunda actively supported.
*   **TAZARA Railway:** A key infrastructure project completed in 1975, linking Zambia to Tanzania's port to bypass white-minority-ruled territories.
*   **Movement for Multi-Party Democracy (MMD):** The opposition party that defeated Kaunda's UNIP in the 1991 elections.

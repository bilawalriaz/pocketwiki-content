# Sydney

## Overview
Sydney is the capital city of New South Wales and Australia's most populous city, with an estimated population of 5,638,830 in June 2025. It is a global Alpha+ city and a major financial hub, known for its harbour, iconic landmarks like the Opera House and Harbour Bridge, and its multicultural population, with over 40% of residents born overseas.

## Body

### Early History and Colonisation
Aboriginal Australians, the traditional custodians of the land, inhabited the Sydney region for at least 30,000 years. The area was home to clans of the Darug, Dharawal, and Eora peoples. British colonisation began in 1788 when the First Fleet, led by Arthur Phillip, founded Sydney as a penal colony. The settlement faced early hardships, including poor soil and a smallpox epidemic in 1789 that decimated the local Indigenous population. Governor Lachlan Macquarie (1810–1821) was instrumental in developing the colony's infrastructure, commissioning roads, public buildings, and a planned street layout.

### Growth and Federation
Sydney was declared a city in 1842. The discovery of gold in 1851 spurred economic growth and immigration, though Melbourne temporarily became Australia's largest city. The late 19th century saw significant urban development, including the construction of sandstone public buildings and the expansion of railways. A financial crisis in the 1890s led to depression. Following Australian federation in 1901, Sydney became the state capital. The 20th century was marked by major public works like the Harbour Bridge (1932), post-WWII mass migration that diversified the population, and the controversial construction of the Sydney Opera House, which opened in 1973.

### Modern Sydney
Since the 1970s, Sydney has transformed from a manufacturing centre into a global city with a service-based economy. High immigration, particularly from Asia, has made it one of the world's most multicultural cities. It hosted the 2000 Summer Olympics and continues to be a major centre for finance, tourism, and education. The city faces challenges such as high housing costs, traffic congestion, and environmental issues, including air quality affected by bushfires.

### Geography and Climate
Sydney is a coastal basin, spanning from the Pacific Ocean to the Blue Mountains. Its geography includes the flat Cumberland Plain and the dissected Hornsby Plateau. The city's iconic harbour, Port Jackson, is a ria (a drowned river valley) formed by rising sea levels. The climate is humid subtropical (Köppen Cfa) with warm summers and mild winters. Weather patterns are influenced by El Niño–Southern Oscillation, affecting rainfall and bushfire risk.

### Economy
Sydney has an advanced market economy. Its nominal GDP was AU$490 billion in 2021. Key sectors include financial and insurance services (18.1% of gross product), professional services, and tourism. The city is a major Asia-Pacific financial hub, hosting the headquarters of major banks and the Australian Securities Exchange. Manufacturing has declined but remains significant, while international education is a major export, with Sydney ranking as the top city globally for international students.

### Demographics and Culture
At the 2021 census, 40.5% of Sydney's population was born overseas, with the largest groups from China, India, England, Vietnam, and the Philippines. The city is culturally diverse, with 42% of households speaking a language other than English at home. Christianity is the largest religious affiliation (46%). Sydney's cultural life includes major institutions like the Art Gallery of New South Wales and the Museum of Contemporary Art, and it hosts festivals such as Vivid Sydney and the Sydney Festival.

### Government and Infrastructure
Sydney is governed by multiple layers: the state government of New South Wales, and 34 local government areas. The City of Sydney council manages the CBD and inner suburbs. Infrastructure includes a large public transport network of trains, buses, ferries, and light rail, anchored by Central Station. Sydney Kingsford Smith Airport is the country's busiest, and a second airport, Western Sydney Airport, is under construction. The city's water supply is managed by a system of dams and catchments.

## Terms
- **Ria**: A coastal inlet formed by the partial submergence of a river valley, such as Sydney Harbour.
- **Köppen–Geiger classification**: A system for classifying climates based on temperature and precipitation patterns.
- **Alpha+ city**: A classification by the Globalization and World Cities Research Network indicating a city's high level of integration and influence in the global economy.
- **Green bans**: Industrial action where unions refuse to work on projects deemed environmentally or heritage-significant.
- **Gentrification**: The process where wealthier people move into a deteriorating neighborhood, renovating housing and often displacing poorer residents.
- **Opal card**: A contactless smartcard used for ticketing on Sydney's public transport network.
- **TAFE**: Technical and Further Education; the public vocational education and training system in Australia.
- **Lockout laws**: Regulations introduced in 2014 to restrict entry to bars and clubs in inner Sydney late at night, aimed at reducing alcohol-fuelled violence.

## Debates and open questions
- The exact date and extent of the first human habitation in the Sydney region is debated, with evidence suggesting occupation from 30,000 to potentially 45,000-50,000 years ago.
- There is scholarly debate about the specific language groups and territorial boundaries of the Aboriginal clans living in the Sydney area prior to British colonisation.
- The effectiveness and social impact of the lockout laws (2014-2020) in curbing violence remain a topic of discussion.

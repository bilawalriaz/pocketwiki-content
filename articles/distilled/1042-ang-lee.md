# Ang Lee

## Overview
Ang Lee (born October 23, 1954) is a Taiwanese filmmaker celebrated for emotionally charged films exploring repressed emotions and cultural conflicts. He is one of the most decorated directors in history, winning three Academy Awards (including two for Best Director), five BAFTAs, and three Golden Globes. He remains the only filmmaker to win the Berlin Film Festival’s Golden Bear twice and one of only four to win the Venice Film Festival’s Golden Lion twice. Lee’s career bridges Eastern and Western cinema, moving from Mandarin-language family dramas examining tradition versus modernity to Hollywood literary adaptations, martial arts epics, and technological experiments in 3D and high frame rate.

## Timeline
- **October 23, 1954** — Born in Chaochou, Pingtung, Taiwan, to a *waishengren* (mainlander) family in a military dependents' village.
- **1975** — Graduates from National Arts School (now National Taiwan University of Arts) after failing university entrance exams twice.

- **1991–1994** — Directs the "Father Knows Best" trilogy: *Pushing Hands* (1991), *The Wedding Banquet* (1993), *Eat Drink Man Woman* (1994); *The Wedding Banquet* wins Golden Bear.
- **1995** — Breaks into Hollywood with *Sense and Sensibility*; wins second Golden Bear and Academy Award for Best Adapted Screenplay (Emma Thompson).
- **1997** — Releases *The Ice Storm*; wins Cannes Best Screenplay Award.
- **2000** — Releases *Crouching Tiger, Hidden Dragon*; becomes highest-grossing foreign-language film in U.S. history; wins Oscar for Best Foreign Language Film.
- **2005** — Releases *Brokeback Mountain*; wins Golden Lion, BAFTA Best Film/Director, and Lee’s first Oscar for Best Director (first non-white winner).
- **2007** — Releases *Lust, Caution*; wins second Golden Lion; disqualified from Oscar Foreign Language category due to insufficient Taiwanese crew.
- **2012** — Releases *Life of Pi*; wins second Oscar for Best Director.
- **2016** — Releases *Billy Lynn's Long Halftime Walk*; experiments with 120fps high frame rate; box office failure.
- **2019** — Releases *Gemini Man*; uses digital de-aging technology; critical and commercial disappointment.
- **2020** — Receives BAFTA Fellowship.
- **2021** — Receives Presidential Culture Award from Taiwan President Tsai Ing-wen.
- **2024** — Receives Japan’s Praemium Imperiale.
- **2026 (May)** — Begins production on *Gold Mountain* (formerly *Old Gold Mountain*).

## Body

### Early Life and Education
Lee was born into a *waishengren* family—mainland Chinese who fled to Taiwan after the Chinese Civil War ended in 1949—in a military dependents' village in Pingtung. His father was a high school principal who emphasized education, but Lee failed the Joint College/University Entrance Examination twice. He attended the National Arts School (now National Taiwan University of Arts), graduating in 1975. A formative moment came from seeing Ingmar Bergman’s *The Virgin Spring* (1960). After mandatory naval service, Lee moved to the U.S. in 1979, earning a B.F.A. in theater at the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign (1980). Struggling with English acting, he switched to directing and met his future wife, Jane Lin. He then earned an M.F.A. from NYU Tisch School of the Arts (1984), where he was a classmate of Spike Lee and worked on Spike’s thesis film. His NYU thesis, *Fine Line*, won the Wasserman Award for Outstanding Direction and aired on PBS.

### The "Father Knows Best" Trilogy (1991–1994)
After years of unemployment post-NYU, Lee won a Taiwanese government screenwriting competition in 1990. Producer Hsu Li-kong backed his debut, *Pushing Hands* (1991), a success in Taiwan. Their next collaboration, *The Wedding Banquet* (1993), about a gay Taiwanese man marrying a woman to appease his parents, won the Golden Bear at Berlin and earned an Oscar nomination for Best Foreign Language Film. *Eat Drink Man Woman* (1994), set in Taipei, depicted a master chef and his three daughters navigating tradition and modernity. It earned a second consecutive Oscar Foreign Language nomination and a BAFTA nomination. The trilogy, starring Sihung Lung as the patriarch, examines the Confucian family under pressure from modernity and diaspora.

### Hollywood Breakthrough and Literary Adaptations (1995–1999)
Lee’s first English-language film, *Sense and Sensibility* (1995), adapted Jane Austen’s novel for Columbia TriStar. It won the Golden Bear (making Lee a two-time winner), the Golden Globe for Best Drama, and an Oscar for Emma Thompson’s screenplay. Thompson noted Lee’s initial cultural shock at actors questioning his direction, which resolved into a productive collaboration. *The Ice Storm* (1997), set in 1970s suburban America, won the Cannes Best Screenplay Award and gained a lasting cult following via home video. *Ride with the Devil* (1999), a revisionist Civil War Western, received mixed reviews and flopped commercially, briefly interrupting Lee’s momentum.

### Global Blockbusters and Acclaim (2000–2012)
*Crouching Tiger, Hidden Dragon* (2000), a *wuxia* (martial arts) epic assembled with a pan-Asian crew, became a global phenomenon—the highest-grossing foreign-language film in the U.S. and U.K. It won the Oscar for Best Foreign Language Film and three technical awards. After the superhero film *Hulk* (2003) drew mixed reviews, Lee’s father encouraged him to continue. *Brokeback Mountain* (2005), adapted from Annie Proulx’s story, became a cultural landmark. It won the Golden Lion, 71 awards globally, and Lee’s first Best Director Oscar (the first for a non-white director), though it controversially lost Best Picture to *Crash*. *Lust, Caution* (2007), from an Eileen Chang novella set in WWII Shanghai, won a second Golden Lion but received an NC-17 rating in the U.S., limiting distribution. The Academy disqualified it from the Foreign Language category due to insufficient Taiwanese participation. *Life of Pi* (2012), adapting Yann Martel’s "unfilmable" novel with pioneering 3D, won Lee his second Best Director Oscar.

### Technological Experimentation and Recent Work (2013–Present)
Lee pushed technical boundaries with *Billy Lynn's Long Halftime Walk* (2016), shot at 120 frames per second (fps) in 4K 3D, and *Gemini Man* (2019), featuring a digitally de-aged Will Smith clone. Both films polarized critics and failed commercially, with criticism focused on the "soap opera effect" of high frame rates and script weaknesses. Lee served as Venice jury president (2009) and Cannes jury member (2013). He developed a long-gestating Muhammad Ali/Joe Frazier film (*Thrilla in Manila*) and a Bruce Lee biopic starring his son Mason Lee. In May 2026, he began production on *Gold Mountain*.

### Personal Life and Collaborators
Lee lives in Larchmont, New York, and Taipei with his wife Jane Lin, a microbiologist. She supported him during six years as a stay-at-home father before his breakthrough. They have two sons, including Mason. Lee describes himself as a permanent U.S. resident (not a naturalized citizen) and a believer in Taoist-Buddhist philosophy. Career-long collaborators include producer/screenwriter James Schamus, editor Tim Squyres, and composers Mychael Danna and Danny Elfman.

## Terms
- **Waishengren**: "Mainlanders"; Chinese who migrated to Taiwan after the Chinese Civil War (1949), distinct from *benshengren* (native Taiwanese).
- **Wuxia**: A genre of Chinese fiction and film concerning the adventures of martial artists in ancient China, often featuring chivalry and fantasy elements.
- **Golden Bear**: The highest prize awarded at the Berlin International Film Festival.
- **Golden Lion**: The highest prize awarded at the Venice International Film Festival.
- **NC-17**: An MPAA film rating meaning "No Children 17 and Under Admitted," often commercially limiting due to theater chain refusals.
- **High Frame Rate (HFR)**: A filming/projection rate higher than the standard 24 fps (e.g., 48, 60, or 120 fps), intended to reduce motion blur but often criticized for a "video" look.
- **Digital De-aging**: Visual effects technology used to make an actor appear younger, employed extensively in *Gemini Man* for Will Smith’s clone.
- **Order of Brilliant Star with Grand Cordon**: The second-highest civilian honor awarded by the Republic of China (Taiwan) government.
- **Praemium Imperiale**: A global arts prize awarded by the Japan Art Association, often considered the "Nobel Prize of the Arts."
- **BAFTA Fellowship**: The highest honor bestowed by the British Academy of Film and Television Arts, recognizing outstanding contribution to cinema.

## Debates and open questions
- **Oscar Best Picture Upset (2006)**: *Brokeback Mountain* was the heavy favorite but lost to *Crash*; the result remains a subject of debate regarding Academy voting biases and the treatment of LGBTQ+ themes.
- **High Frame Rate Viability**: Lee’s advocacy for 120fps (*Billy Lynn*, *Gemini Man*) has not achieved industry adoption; critics argue the hyper-realism undermines cinematic suspension of disbelief, while Lee maintains it is the future of immersion.
- **Cultural Authenticity vs. Global Appeal**: *Lust, Caution*’s Oscar disqualification highlighted tensions over what constitutes a "national" film in a globalized production environment.
- **Unfinished Projects**: The status of the *Thrilla in Manila* Ali/Frazier film and the Bruce Lee biopic remains uncertain after years of development limbo.

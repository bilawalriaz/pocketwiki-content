# Canary Islands

## Overview
The Canary Islands are a volcanic archipelago in the Atlantic Ocean, forming Spain's southernmost autonomous community and the most populous overseas territory of the European Union. Their strategic location between Africa, Europe, and the Americas has shaped their history as a crossroads for conquest, trade, and tourism, making them a vital economic and cultural hub.

## Timeline
- **c. 1st–3rd centuries AD** — Indigenous peoples, ancestors of the Guanches, settle the islands.
- **1402–1496** — Castilian conquest of the islands, beginning with Jean de Béthencourt and ending with the submission of Tenerife.
- **1479** — Treaty of Alcáçovas confirms Castilian control of the Canaries.
- **1833** — Province of Canary Islands created with Santa Cruz de Tenerife as its capital.
- **1927** — Province split into two: Santa Cruz de Tenerife and Las Palmas.
- **1982** — Statute of Autonomy establishes the Autonomous Community of the Canary Islands with a shared capital.
- **2021** — Cumbre Vieja volcano erupts on La Palma.
- **2024** — Record 46,843 illegal migrants arrive in the Canary Islands.

## Body

### Geography and Climate
The archipelago consists of seven main islands and several smaller ones, all of volcanic origin formed by the Canary hotspot. The islands vary from lush, subtropical laurisilva forests in the west (influenced by the moist Canary Current) to semi-desert landscapes in the east near Africa. Mount Teide on Tenerife is Spain's highest peak and the world's third-tallest volcano from its oceanic base. The climate is generally tropical and arid, with significant microclimates across the islands.

### Pre-Hispanic and Colonial History
The islands were inhabited for at least 2,000 years before European contact by indigenous peoples collectively known as the Guanches, who shared a common origin with North African Amazighs. The Castilian conquest, initiated in 1402, was a prolonged process of "aristocratic" and later "royal" conquest that ended in 1496. The native population declined rapidly due to war, epidemics, and enslavement. The islands were then integrated into the Spanish Empire, becoming a key stopover for galleons sailing to the Americas and developing a plantation economy based on sugarcane and later cochineal, worked largely by enslaved Africans.

### Politics and Governance
Since 1982, the Canary Islands have been an autonomous community of Spain with a unique dual-capital system: Santa Cruz de Tenerife and Las Palmas de Gran Canaria. The regional government consists of a Parliament and a President. The islands are divided into two provinces, each with its own capital. While pro-independence sentiment exists, most residents identify as both Spanish and Canarian.

### Economy and Society
Tourism is the economic cornerstone, accounting for 32% of GDP and attracting over 14 million visitors annually in recent years. Construction and tropical agriculture (bananas, tobacco) are also significant. The population is approximately 2.27 million, heavily concentrated on Tenerife and Gran Canaria. The islands are a major entry point for migration to the EU, with a record number of arrivals in 2024. The economy uses a local sales tax (IGIC) instead of EU VAT.

## Terms
- **Guanches**: The indigenous people of the Canary Islands before the Castilian conquest, of North African Amazigh origin.
- **Macaronesia**: A collection of five volcanic archipelagos in the North Atlantic, including the Canaries, Azores, Madeira, Cape Verde, and Savage Isles.
- **Canary hotspot**: The volcanic plume beneath the Earth's crust that formed the Canary Islands.
- **Laurisilva**: A type of subtropical cloud forest found in the wetter western islands.
- **Cabildo Insular**: The island council governing each of the major islands.
- **IGIC**: The local General Indirect Canarian Tax, which replaces VAT within the islands.
- **Zona Especial Canaria (ZEC)**: A special tax zone offering concessions to investors to stimulate economic development.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The exact timeline and nature of the first human settlement of the islands remain debated, with evidence suggesting contact as early as the 1st century BC.
- The extent to which the Castilian conquest constitutes a "genocide" of the Guanche population is a subject of scholarly discussion.
- The international boundary of the Canary Islands' territorial waters is disputed by Morocco, which contests Spain's right to claim an exclusive economic zone based on the islands' territory.

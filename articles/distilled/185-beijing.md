# Beijing

## Overview
Beijing is the capital of the People's Republic of China and the world's most populous national capital city, with over 21.8 million residents. As the last of the Four Great Ancient Capitals, it has served as China's political center for most of the past eight centuries. The city is a global hub for politics, economy, culture, education, and technology, hosting the headquarters of most of China's largest state-owned enterprises and the highest number of Fortune Global 500 companies worldwide. It is a leading center for scientific research, ranked first globally by the Nature Index since 2016, and home to elite universities including Tsinghua and Peking University. Beijing uniquely blends imperial architecture—such as the Forbidden City and Temple of Heaven—with modern development, and is the only city to have hosted both the Summer (2008) and Winter (2022) Olympic Games.

## Body

### Etymology and Names
The name **Beijing** (北京) means "Northern Capital," adopted in 1403 during the Ming dynasty to contrast with **Nanjing** ("Southern Capital"). The older English romanization **Peking** derives from a 1655 atlas by Jesuit missionary Martino Martini; it persists in the IATA code PEK for Beijing Capital International Airport and in the name Peking University. The single-character abbreviation is 京 (Jīng), used on license plates; the Latin abbreviation is BJ.

### Historical Evolution
**Early History:** Human habitation dates to Peking Man (*Homo erectus*) at Zhoukoudian (230,000–250,000 years ago). The first walled city, **Ji**, capital of the state of Ji, stood near present-day Guang'anmen. The state of Yan conquered Ji in the 11th century BC, making it their capital and later one of the seven Warring States.

**Imperial Era (Qin–Tang):** After Qin unification (221 BC), Ji became a commandery seat. Under the Han, it was capital of You Province. During the Tang dynasty, it served as the Youzhou military frontier command. An Lushan briefly made it the capital of his rebel Yan dynasty (Yanjing).

**Liao, Jin, and Yuan (938–1368):** The Khitan Liao took the city in 938, calling it Nanjing (Southern Capital). The Jurchen Jin captured it in 1122, made it their Central Capital (Zhongdu) in 1153, but it was razed by Mongols in 1215. Kublai Khan built **Dadu** (Khanbaliq) northeast of the ruins (1264–1293), centering it on the Drum Tower.

**Ming Dynasty (1368–1644):** Zhu Yuanzhang captured Dadu, renaming it Beiping. His son Zhu Di (Yongle Emperor) seized the throne, renamed the city Beijing in 1403, and built the Forbidden City (1406–1420). By the 15th century, Beijing took its essential shape, enclosed by the Ming city wall (demolished 1965 for the subway). It was likely the world's largest city for much of the 15th–18th centuries. Li Zicheng's peasant army captured it in 1644, ending the Ming.

**Qing Dynasty (1644–1911):** Qing made Beijing the sole capital, retaining the Ming layout. The Old Summer Palace was burned by Anglo-French forces in 1860. The 1900 Boxer Rebellion led to foreign occupation and the Boxer Protocol (1901), imposing a massive indemnity (US$335 million) and weakening the dynasty, which collapsed in 1911.

**Republic of China (1912–1949):** Yuan Shikai kept the capital at Beijing; after his death (1916), warlords controlled the north. The 1928 Northern Expedition moved the capital to Nanjing; Beijing was renamed **Beiping**. The 1937 Marco Polo Bridge Incident sparked the Second Sino-Japanese War; Japan occupied Beijing, establishing a puppet government.

**People's Republic of China (1949–present):** The PLA entered peacefully in January 1949; Mao proclaimed the PRC on October 1. The city expanded beyond the old walls; the Cultural Revolution (1966–76) destroyed over 70% of designated heritage sites. Post-1978 reforms spurred massive urban expansion (ring roads, CBD, Zhongguancun). Beijing hosted the 2008 Summer Olympics and 2022 Winter Olympics.

### Geography and Climate
Beijing sits at the northern edge of the North China Plain, shielded by mountains (Jundu, Xishan) to the north, northwest, and west. Mount Dongling (2,303 m) is the highest point. Major rivers (Chaobai, Yongding, Juma) flow southeast into the Hai River system. The city is the northern terminus of the Grand Canal and the South–North Water Transfer Project. Urban development spreads in concentric ring roads from Tian'anmen Square.

**Climate:** Humid continental (Köppen Dwa), bordering on cold semi-arid (BSk). Hot, humid summers (East Asian monsoon); cold, dry winters (Siberian anticyclone). Spring brings sandstorms from the Gobi. Annual precipitation averages ~528 mm, mostly June–August. Temperature extremes: −27.4 °C (1966) to 41.9 °C (1999).

### Environmental Issues
Rapid urbanization (urban extent quadrupled 2000–2009) and fossil fuel combustion caused severe air pollution. The 2013 "pollution shock" (affecting 600 million) spurred a "war on pollution." Measures included: reducing coal share from 24% (2012) to 10% (2017), converting coal plants to gas, capping coal at 20 million tons, restricting vehicles (license plate lottery, odd/even days), and relocating heavy industry (e.g., Capital Steel to Hebei). Regional sources (Hebei, Tianjin, Shanxi) contribute 35–60% of ozone. PM2.5 dropped 35% by 2017; 2022 average was 30 µg/m³ (best since 2013); 2024 saw only two heavy pollution days (down 96.6% from 58 in 2013). Dust storms from northern deserts remain a seasonal hazard.

### Government and Administration
Beijing is a provincial-level direct-administered municipality governed by the CCP Municipal Committee (Secretary is a Politburo member since 1987). It comprises **16 urban districts** (merged from 18 in 2010 and 2015). The municipal government oversees the economy, public security, and the Municipal People's Congress. As the national capital, it houses the National People's Congress and central government organs. The judiciary includes the Supreme People's Court, Beijing High People's Court, three intermediate courts (No. 1 in Shijingshan, No. 2 in Fengtai, No. 3 in Laiguangying), 14 basic courts, and specialized railway/Internet courts.

### Economy
**Overview (2025):** GDP CN¥5.2 trillion (~US$748B nominal, US$1.52T PPP), 3.71% of national total; per capita GDP CN¥238,571 (US$34,252 nominal), ranked 1st nationally. Classified Alpha+ by GaWC. 54 Fortune Global 500 HQs (2022), most of any city. "Billionaire capital of the world."

**Structure:** Post-industrial; tertiary sector 83.8% (services: professional, IT, real estate, research), secondary 15.8% (manufacturing, construction), primary 0.26%. Industry shrank to 12.1% of output (2022) after relocating 140 polluting firms. Agriculture shifted from grain to vegetables/fruit.

**Key Economic Zones (6 identified 2006, 43.3% of GDP 2012):**
1.  **Zhongguancun** (Haidian): "China's Silicon Valley," tech startups, 6,150 registrations in H1 2014.
2.  **Beijing Financial Street** (Xicheng): State banks, regulators (central bank, CSRC, SAFE).
3.  **CBD** (Chaoyang, East 3rd Ring): Skyscrapers, foreign firms, professional services (China Zun, 528m, tallest).
4.  **Yizhuang (BETDA)** (Daxing, South 5th Ring): Pharma, IT, materials engineering.
5.  **Airport Economic Zone** (Shunyi): Logistics, auto assembly.
6.  **Olympic Center Zone** (North): Sports, tourism, conventions.
*Shijingshan* (former Shougang steel) transformed into industrial culture/arts venue (2022 Winter Olympics site).

### Demographics
2024 population: 21.83 million (19.26M urban, 88.2%; 2.57M rural). OECD metro estimate (2010): 24.9M. Second in urban population (after Shanghai), third in municipal (after Shanghai, Chongqing). 2013: ~13M local *hukou* holders; 8M migrants without full benefits. Growth driven by migration; natural increase low (0.44% in 2013). 73.6% working age. Education: 49.1% college+ (2021), up from 20.4% (2002). Ethnicity: 96% Han; minorities: Manchu, Hui, Korean, Mongol, Tujia. 2017 population controls ("big city disease") caused slight decline; redistribution to Jing-Jin-Ji and Xiong'an New Area (300k–500k planned).

### Education and Research
World leader in scientific output (Nature Index #1 since 2016; 3rd globally behind China/US in 2025). Leads in chemistry, physical sciences, Earth/environmental sciences, and UN SDG-related output. Top in Asia-Pacific for biological/health sciences. **90+ public universities** (largest urban system in Asia). **Tsinghua** (#12) and **Peking** (#13) top Asia/Oceania (THE 2025); both C9 League members. >1/5 of 147 "Double First-Class" universities. Hosts Chinese Academy of Sciences (Nature Index #1 research institute since 2014), Chinese Academy of Engineering, Chinese Academy of Social Sciences, and numerous specialized academies. 15-year-olds (with Shanghai, Zhejiang, Jiangsu) topped OECD PISA 2018 in math, reading, science.

### Culture
**Language:** Beijing dialect (Mandarin basis for *Putonghua*, standard Chinese).
**Arts:** Beijing/Peking Opera (song, dialogue, acrobatics, archaic stage dialect). **Cloisonné** (Jingtailan) metalworking and carved lacquerware are signature crafts. UNESCO City of Design (2012).
**Cuisine:** Imperial court roots. **Peking duck** iconic. *Fuling jiabing* (fungus pancake). Instant-boiled mutton (copper hot pot, Yuan origin). Teahouses common.
**Heritage Sites:** 8 UNESCO sites: Forbidden City, Temple of Heaven, Summer Palace, Ming Tombs, Zhoukoudian, Beijing Central Axis, Great Wall sections, Grand Canal sections. *Siheyuan* (courtyard houses) and *hutongs* (alleys) disappearing due to high-rise replacement; residents compensated but community loss lamented.
**Religion:** Diverse: Chinese folk religion, Taoism (White Cloud Temple HQ, Dongyue Temple), Buddhism (11% population; Guangji Temple HQ Buddhist Assoc.; 6 National Key Temples), Islam (~70 mosques; Niujie oldest 996; Changying largest), Catholicism (Nantang 1605, CPCA oversight), Protestantism (19th c. missionaries; reorganized 1958 under Three-Self), Eastern Orthodox (Russian embassy church 2007).

### Transportation
**Rail:** Major hub. 10 conventional lines (Jinghu, Jingguang, Jingha, etc.). 6 high-speed lines: Beijing–Tianjin (2008), Beijing–Shanghai (2011), Beijing–Guangzhou (2012), Beijing–Xiong'an (2019), Beijing–Zhangjiakou (2019), Beijing–Shenyang (2021). Major stations: Beijing, Beijing West, Beijing South (HSR), Beijing North, Qinghe, Chaoyang, Fengtai, Tongzhou (2025). International links to Mongolia, Russia, Vietnam, North Korea.
**Roads:** 6 ring roads (2nd traces old wall; 5th/6th expressway standard; G95 Capital Loop 2018). 15 National Highways. 1,167 km expressways. Severe congestion; license plate lottery (EV preference); driving restrictions (non-local plates, odd/even).
**Air:** **Beijing Capital (PEK)**: 32 km NE, 2nd busiest globally (pre-COVID), Terminal 3 huge. **Beijing Daxing (PKX)**: 46 km S, opened 2019, largest single-terminal. Nanyuan closed 2019. 240-hr visa-free transit (53 countries, 2026).
**Public Transit:** **Subway**: 25 lines, 459 stations, 783 km (world's longest), 3.66B rides (2016). Distance-based fares since 2014. Target: 30 lines, 1,050 km by 2022. **Bus/Trolley**: ~1,000 lines, 4 BRT. **Taxi**: Metered, ¥13 flag fall. **Cycling**: Flat terrain, dedicated lanes; dockless bikeshare (Mobike, Ofo) revived usage since 2016.

### Defence and Aerospace
Central Military Commission (Ministry of National Defense, west Beijing). Rocket Force HQ (Qinghe, Haidian). Central Theater Command HQ (Gaojing) overseeing Beijing Garrison and 81st–83rd Armies (Hebei). PLA National Defence University, Academy of Military Science, 301/307 Hospitals. China National Space Administration, CASTC, CASIC, Beijing Aerospace Command and Control Center (Haidian) all based in city.

### Ecology
Diverse ecosystems: montane forests, plains, wetlands. Forest coverage 44.8% (2022), up from 1.3% (1949). Urban green coverage 49.3%. 2020–24 survey: 7,121 species, 151 ecosystem types. 608 terrestrial vertebrates, 2,088 plants, 515 bird species (2nd among G20 capitals). 20 nature reserves (1,339.7 km²). Protected wildlife: leopard, wolf, red fox, Père David's deer (reintroduced 1985 at Beijing Milu Park), Beijing barbastelle bat (endemic, discovered 2001). Migratory birds (200–300 species/year); cuckoos tracked to Africa/India. City flowers: Chinese rose, chrysanthemum. City trees: Chinese arborvitae, pagoda tree (scholar tree).

### International Relations
Hosts Asian Infrastructure Investment Bank, Silk Road Fund, Shanghai Cooperation Organisation HQ. 173 embassies (2020). Twin/sister city relationships maintained. Representative offices: Haiti, Faroe Islands, EU Delegation.

## Terms
- ****Beijing**** — "Northern Capital" (北京); name adopted 1403 to distinguish from Nanjing ("Southern Capital").
- ****Peking**** — Former English romanization (via 1655 Martini atlas); persists in airport code PEK and Peking University.
- ****Forbidden City**** — Imperial palace complex (1406–1420), home to Ming/Qing emperors; now Palace Museum; UNESCO site.
- ****Siheyuan**** — Traditional courtyard residence; buildings enclosing a central open space.
- ****Hutong**** — Narrow alleyway formed by rows of siheyuans; typically run east-west for Feng Shui.
- ****Hukou**** — Household registration system; determines eligibility for local social benefits (education, healthcare).
- ****Zhongguancun**** — High-tech hub in Haidian ("China's Silicon Valley"); core of Beijing-Tianjin-Shijiazhuang Hi-Tech Belt.
- ****Nature Index**** — Database tracking high-quality scientific output; Beijing ranked #1 city globally since 2016.
- ****Jing-Jin-Ji**** — Megalopolis cluster integrating Beijing, Tianjin, and Hebei for coordinated development.
- ****Boxer Protocol (1901)**** — Treaty ending Boxer Rebellion; imposed US$335M indemnity, foreign troop stationing, destruction of forts. |

## Debates and open questions
*   **Heritage vs. Modernization:** Rapid redevelopment has erased >70% of designated historic sites (Cultural Revolution, post-1980s expansion). *Hutong* demolition continues despite compensation policies; debate persists on preserving "sense of community" vs. housing needs.
*   **Air Quality Attribution:** 35–60% of ozone originates outside Beijing (Hebei, Tianjin, Shanxi). Effective control requires regional coordination, not just municipal action.
*   **Population Cap Policy:** 2017 controls ("big city disease") aim to cap population at 23M by 2020. Forced removal of low-income migrants raises equity concerns; effectiveness of redistribution to Xiong'an (new area, 100km south) is unproven.
*   **Water Scarcity:** Reliance on South–North Water Transfer Project (Yangtze diversion) and groundwater overdraft creates long-term sustainability risks for the North China Plain.

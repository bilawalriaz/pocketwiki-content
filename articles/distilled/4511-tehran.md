# Tehran

## Overview
Tehran is the capital and largest city of Iran, serving as the country's political, economic, and cultural center. With a population of around 9 million in the city proper and 16.8 million in its metropolitan area, it is the most populous city in West Asia and the Middle East. Its history spans over 6,000 years, evolving from a suburb of the ancient city of Rhages to becoming the 51st capital of Iran in 1786, and it remains at the heart of the nation's modern development and challenges.

## Timeline
- **c. 3000 BC** — Archaeological remains suggest settlement in the Tehran-Ray area dates back over 6,000 years.
- **641** — The last Sasanian emperor, Yazdgerd III, issues an appeal from Rhages before fleeing the Arab invasion.
- **13th century** — The Mongols invade and destroy Rhages; many inhabitants escape to Tehran.
- **1786** — Agha Mohammad Khan of the Qajar dynasty chooses Tehran as the capital of Iran.
- **1907** — Parliament passes the Baladie (municipal law), establishing a framework for city governance.
- **1920s-1930s** — Under Reza Shah, Tehran undergoes major modernization, including street widening and demolition of old walls.
- **1943** — The Tehran Conference is held, attended by Roosevelt, Stalin, and Churchill.
- **1971** — The Azadi Tower is built to commemorate the 2,500th anniversary of the Persian Empire.
- **1979** — The Iranian Revolution leads to profound political and urban transformations.
- **2007** — The Milad Tower is completed.
- **2014** — The Tabiat Bridge is completed.
- **2025-2026** — Tehran suffers major damage from Israeli airstrikes during the Twelve-Day War and the 2026 Iran war.

## Body

### History and Urban Development
Tehran's territory was historically part of the prominent Median city of Rhages. Tehran itself was a village until 1786, when Qajar king Agha Mohammad Khan selected it as capital due to its strategic location and lack of entrenched political factions. The city grew slowly until the late 19th century. Major modernization began under Reza Shah in the 1920s-30s, which involved demolishing historic structures and walls to create a modern grid of wide avenues. This rapid, often chaotic growth continued through the 20th century, fueled by mass migration. The 1979 Revolution and subsequent Iran-Iraq War (1980-1988) brought political upheaval and physical damage from airstrikes. In the 21st century, Tehran has faced further destruction from the 2025-2026 conflicts, with thousands of buildings damaged or destroyed.

### Demographics and Society
Tehran is a diverse "melting pot." The majority of residents identify as Persian and speak the Tehrani variety of Persian, though the region's historical native language was a different, now-extinct dialect. Iranian Azeris (10-15%) and Mazanderanis (5%) are the largest minority groups. The city experienced major demographic shifts after 1979, with waves of emigration following the revolution and the Iran-Iraq War, countered by internal migration from war-affected western regions. The dominant religion is Twelver Shia Islam, but the city hosts various other religious communities.

### Geography and Climate
Located at the foot of the Alborz Mountains, Tehran has a complex climate with significant topographic variation. Northern districts at higher elevations (up to 1,800m) are cooler and receive more precipitation, while southern plains (around 1,100m) are hotter and drier. The city has a cold semi-arid climate (BSk), with long, dry summers where temperatures can exceed 40°C, and cool winters with occasional heavy snowfall. Tehran suffers from severe air pollution, primarily from vehicles, and is located near major fault lines, posing earthquake risks. Water scarcity is a critical issue, exacerbated by drought.

### Economy and Infrastructure
Tehran is Iran's economic hub, hosting about 30% of the public-sector workforce and 45% of large industrial firms. Key industries include automobile manufacturing, electronics, and textiles. The city is highly car-dependent, with a vast network of highways, though it also has a metro system, bus rapid transit (BRT), and two airports (Mehrabad for domestic, Imam Khomeini for international). It is a major shopping destination, home to the Iran Mall, the world's largest mall by area.

### Culture and Landmarks
Tehran's architecture ranges from Qajar and Pahlavi-era palaces (like Golestan Palace, a World Heritage Site) to modern landmarks like the Azadi Tower and Milad Tower. The city is a center for arts, with numerous museums, theaters, and concert halls. It hosts major film and theater festivals. Popular sports include football and skiing, with several ski resorts in the nearby Alborz Mountains. The city's cultural life blends traditional Iranian festivals with modern influences.

## Terms
*   **Rhages (Ray):** An ancient and prominent Iranian city whose territory was part of present-day Tehran; it was destroyed by the Mongols in the 13th century.
*   **Baladie Law (1907):** A municipal law that provided a detailed framework for city governance, councils, and voting rights, later suspended under Reza Shah.
*   **White Revolution:** A series of reforms launched in 1962 by Mohammad Reza Shah, including land reforms, which accelerated Tehran's chaotic growth.
*   **Azadi Tower:** A landmark built in 1971, originally named Shahyad Tower, commemorating the 2,500th anniversary of the Persian Empire.
*   **Milad Tower:** A 435-meter telecommunications tower completed in 2007, one of the tallest in the world.
*   **Tabiat Bridge:** A 270-meter pedestrian overpass completed in 2014, designed by Leila Araghian.
*   **Köppen climate classification:** A system for classifying climates based on temperature and precipitation; Tehran is mostly BSk (cold semi-arid).
*   **Heat-island effect:** A phenomenon where urban areas are significantly warmer than surrounding rural areas due to human activities and infrastructure.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Capital Relocation:** Due to severe environmental issues (air pollution, water scarcity, earthquake risk), there have been long-standing plans and discussions to move Iran's capital from Tehran, though none have been approved.
*   **Urban Planning vs. Growth:** Tehran's development has often been characterized by chaotic, unbalanced growth that has outpaced planning efforts, leading to issues like high-density suburbs and inefficient infrastructure.
*   **Cultural Preservation:** The modernization of Tehran, especially under Reza Shah, involved the demolition of many historic buildings and neighborhoods, a process that continues to raise concerns about gentrification and the loss of cultural heritage.

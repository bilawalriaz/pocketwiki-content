# Serengeti National Park

## Overview
Serengeti National Park is a vast protected area in northern Tanzania, famous for hosting the world's largest annual animal migration. It is a critical ecosystem for wildlife conservation and a UNESCO World Heritage Site, but faces ongoing threats from human activity and development.

## Timeline
- **1930** — Major Richard Hingston proposes the Serengeti Game Reserve be designated a national park.
- **1940** — The area becomes a national park.
- **1948** — Strict protection granted; Serengeti National Park Board of Trustees formed.
- **1951** — Park boundaries finalized.
- **1959** — Ngorongoro Conservation Area split off from the park's eastern part.
- **1959** — Bernhard Grzimek's book and documentary *Serengeti Shall Not Die* published.
- **1966** — Serengeti Research Institute established at Seronera.
- **1981** — Park area recorded as 12,950 km².
- **1994** — Canine distemper outbreak kills approximately one-third of the lion population.
- **2005** — Park forms a Lion Conservation Unit with Maasai Mara National Reserve.
- **2012** — African wild dogs return to the area after disappearing in 1991.
- **2024** — Proposal to build a highway through the park is revived.

## Body

### History and Administration
The park was established in 1940 and granted strict protection in 1948. Its boundaries were finalized in 1951, which restricted the movements of the resident Maasai people. In 1959, a large eastern section was removed to create the Ngorongoro Conservation Area to accommodate traditional Maasai land use. The park is administered by the Tanzania National Parks Authority and is listed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site and an IUCN Category II protected area.

### Wildlife and the Great Migration
The Serengeti ecosystem supports over 3,000 lions, making it a key Lion Conservation Unit. Other large predators include leopards and cheetahs. Herbivore populations have fluctuated; elephants and buffalo recovered from poaching lows, while giraffe numbers declined significantly between 1977 and 2010. The park's most famous feature is the Great Migration, the world's second-longest overland migration. Each year, around 1.5 million wildebeest and 250,000 zebras, along with other herbivores, migrate north from the southern plains to Kenya's Maasai Mara and back, following seasonal rains. The journey involves perilous river crossings where thousands drown or are taken by crocodiles.

### Geography and Geology
The park covers 14,763 km² of savanna, grasslands, and woodlands. Its landscape is divided into three regions: the almost treeless southern Serengeti plains, the riverine forests of the Western Corridor, and the open woodlands of the Northern Serengeti. The geology is complex, featuring ancient greenstones, granite-gneiss, and Neoproterozoic rocks. The eastern volcanic grasslands grow on ash deposits from the Kerimasi and Ol Doinyo Lengai volcanoes. The Mara River is the only permanent river in the ecosystem.

### Threats
Major threats include deforestation in the Mau Forest, which alters the Mara River's hydrology, and invasive plant species. Human population growth on the park's western side is estimated at 4% yearly, increasing farming and ranching. Poaching remains severe, with an estimated 200,000 animals killed annually. A highly controversial proposal to build a 452-km highway through the park, first considered in 2005 and revived in 2024, threatens to disrupt the migration and cause ecosystem collapse.

## Terms
- **Savanna:** A mixed woodland-grassland ecosystem characterized by trees being sufficiently widely spaced so that the canopy does not close.
- **Great Migration:** The annual, large-scale movement of wildebeest, zebras, and other herbivores across the Serengeti-Mara ecosystem.
- **Koppies:** Granite rock formations that serve as observation posts for predators.
- **Edaphic:** Relating to soil, especially in regard to its influence on living organisms.
- **Natrocarbonatite:** A rare type of lava produced by Ol Doinyo Lengai volcano.
- **Category II protected area:** An IUCN designation for a protected area managed mainly for the protection of ecosystems and recreational activities.
- **Lion Conservation Unit:** A designated area important for the long-term viability of a lion population.

## Debates and Open Questions
The primary debate concerns the proposed 452-km highway through the park. Advocates argue it would improve connectivity and alleviate poverty, while conservationists warn it would disrupt the wildebeest migration and cause irreversible ecological damage. Alternative southern routes that bypass the park have been proposed as a less harmful solution.

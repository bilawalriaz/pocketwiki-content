# Rwandan genocide

## Overview
The Rwandan genocide was the systematic mass murder of Tutsi and moderate Hutu in Rwanda from 7 April to 19 July 1994. It resulted from long-standing ethnic tensions, colonial manipulation, and political extremism, causing an estimated 500,000 to 662,000 deaths. The genocide reshaped Rwanda and triggered regional conflicts, including the First and Second Congo Wars.

## Timeline
- **1959–1962** — Hutu Revolution leads to Hutu rule and mass Tutsi exile.
- **Oct 1990** — RPF invades from Uganda, starting the Rwandan Civil War.
- **Aug 1993** — Arusha Accords signed, creating a power-sharing government and UNAMIR.
- **Oct 1993** — Assassination of Burundi's Hutu president sparks ethnic violence, hardening Hutu extremist views in Rwanda.
- **Jan 1994** — UNAMIR commander Roméo Dallaire sends "Genocide Fax" warning of extermination plans; UN denies request to act.
- **6 Apr 1994** — President Habyarimana's plane is shot down, triggering the genocide.
- **7 Apr 1994** — Genocide begins; moderate Hutu PM Agathe Uwilingiyimana and 10 Belgian peacekeepers are killed.
- **Apr–Jul 1994** — Systematic killing of Tutsi and moderate Hutu across Rwanda by government forces, militias, and civilians.
- **4 Jul 1994** — RPF captures Kigali, effectively ending the genocide.
- **Jul 1994** — Genocide ends; RPF takes full control. Millions of Hutu, including génocidaires, flee to Zaire (now DRC).
- **1996–1997** — First Congo War begins, triggered by Rwandan incursions into Zaire to dismantle Hutu refugee camps.
- **2001–2012** — Rwanda establishes Gacaca community courts to try genocide suspects.

## Body

### Background and Colonial Legacy
The Hutu, Tutsi, and Twa share language and culture, with distinctions historically based on socio-economic class. Belgian colonial rule (from 1917) rigidified these into ethnic identities via mandatory identity cards in the 1930s, favoring the Tutsi minority. This created deep resentment, fueling the Hutu Revolution (1959–1962) that overthrew the Tutsi monarchy, led to Hutu dominance, and caused over 300,000 Tutsi to flee into exile by 1964.

### Civil War and Hutu Power Extremism
The RPF, formed by Tutsi exiles in Uganda, invaded Rwanda in 1990, starting a civil war. The Arusha Accords (1993) aimed for peace but were undermined by Hutu extremists. This faction, known as "Hutu Power," used propaganda (notably RTLM radio) to dehumanize Tutsi as "cockroaches" (*inyenzi*). They armed and trained civilian militias, the Interahamwe and Impuzamugambi, and compiled kill lists.

### The Genocide
Following President Habyarimana's assassination on 6 April 1994, Hutu extremists seized power. Using roadblocks and identity cards, they systematically identified and killed Tutsi. The Presidential Guard, army, and militias led the slaughter, often inciting ordinary Hutu civilians to participate. Killings occurred in homes, churches, and schools. Sexual violence was widespread, with an estimated 250,000 to 500,000 rapes. The Twa also suffered heavy losses, with an estimated 10,000 killed.

### RPF Victory and Aftermath
The RPF resumed its military campaign, steadily advancing to stop the killings. It captured Kigali on 4 July and took full control by 18 July, ending the genocide. The RPF's victory led to the flight of approximately two million Hutu refugees into Zaire, where génocidaires regrouped. This instability directly triggered the First Congo War (1996–1997) as Rwanda intervened to dismantle the camps.

### International Response and Failure
The international community largely failed to intervene. UNAMIR was undermanned and restricted by its mandate. The United States avoided engagement, haunted by the 1993 Somalia debacle. France, an ally of the Hutu government, launched Opération Turquoise in late June 1994, a humanitarian mission that saved some lives but was criticized for providing cover for fleeing génocidaires.

### Justice and Commemoration
Post-genocide Rwanda faced prosecuting over 100,000 suspects. The UN established the International Criminal Tribunal for Rwanda (ICTR) for high-level leaders. Domestically, Rwanda used conventional courts and later revived the traditional Gacaca court system. The government has since abolished the death penalty and promotes national unity, criminalizing "genocide ideology." The genocide is commemorated annually during *Kwibuka*.

## Terms
- **Tutsi, Hutu, Twa**: The three main social groups in Rwanda, sharing language and culture. Colonial rule hardened these into rigid ethnic identities.
- **Rwandan Patriotic Front (RPF)**: A predominantly Tutsi rebel group formed by exiles in Uganda; it invaded Rwanda in 1990 and ended the genocide in 1994.
- **Hutu Power**: An extremist political ideology promoting Hutu supremacy and the elimination of Tutsi influence.
- **Interahamwe & Impuzamugambi**: Hutu extremist youth militias trained and armed to carry out the genocide.
- **RTLM (Radio Télévision Libre des Mille Collines)**: A radio station that broadcast anti-Tutsi propaganda, inciting violence and earning the nickname "Radio Machete."
- **Arusha Accords**: A 1993 peace agreement meant to end the civil war and establish a power-sharing government; its collapse preceded the genocide.
- **UNAMIR**: The United Nations Assistance Mission for Rwanda, a peacekeeping force deployed to oversee the Arusha Accords.
- **Gacaca Courts**: A community-based justice system revived in Rwanda to try the overwhelming number of genocide suspects.
- **ICTR**: The International Criminal Tribunal for Rwanda, a UN court established in Arusha, Tanzania, to prosecute high-level genocide perpetrators.
- **Génocidaires**: A term used to describe the perpetrators of the genocide.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Planning and Conspiracy**: Historians debate the extent to which the genocide was centrally planned before Habyarimana's assassination. Prosecutors at the ICTR argued for a pre-existing conspiracy but were largely unable to prove it in court.
- **Responsibility for Habyarimana's Assassination**: Responsibility for shooting down the president's plane remains disputed. Investigations have alternately blamed the RPF and Hutu extremists, with no definitive consensus.
- **Death Toll**: The official Rwandan government figure is over 1 million, but most scholarly estimates range between 500,000 and 662,000 Tutsi killed.
- **RPF Atrocities**: The RPF committed killings of Hutu civilians during and after the genocide. The scale and command responsibility for these acts are disputed.
- **International Inaction**: The failure of the UN, US, and other powers to intervene is attributed to factors including the "Somalia effect," lack of political will, and the downplaying of intelligence warnings.

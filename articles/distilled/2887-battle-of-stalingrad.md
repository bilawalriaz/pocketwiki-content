# Battle of Stalingrad

## Overview
The Battle of Stalingrad (August 1942 – February 1943) was a pivotal World War II engagement on the Eastern Front where Nazi Germany and its allies fought the Soviet Union for control of the city of Stalingrad (now Volgograd). It is considered the largest and deadliest urban battle in history and the turning point of the war in Europe, ending Germany's strategic advance eastward and passing the initiative to the Soviet Union.

## Timeline
- **28 June 1942** — Germany launches Case Blue, its summer offensive in southern Russia.
- **17 July 1942** — German forces first clash with the Red Army's Stalingrad Front.
- **23 August 1942** — German 6th Army reaches Stalingrad; massive Luftwaffe bombing reduces the city to rubble.
- **13 September 1942** — The battle for the city itself begins with a major German assault.
- **19 November 1942** — The Soviet Union launches Operation Uranus, a counter-offensive targeting Axis flanks.
- **23 November 1942** — Soviet pincers meet at Kalach, encircling the German 6th Army in Stalingrad.
- **12 December 1942** — Germany begins Operation Winter Storm, a relief attempt for the 6th Army.
- **16 December 1942** — The Soviets launch Operation Little Saturn, threatening Army Group A and ending relief hopes.
- **31 January 1943** — German Field Marshal Friedrich Paulus surrenders the southern pocket.
- **2 February 1943** — The last German forces in the northern pocket surrender, ending the battle.

## Body

### Background and Strategic Importance
By spring 1942, Germany aimed to secure the Caucasus oil fields to fuel its war machine. The city of Stalingrad, a major industrial center and transport hub on the Volga River, was a key objective. Controlling it would disrupt Soviet supply lines and protect the flank of the advance into the Caucasus. The city also held immense symbolic value, bearing the name of Soviet leader Joseph Stalin. Hitler expanded the campaign's objectives to include the city's capture and annihilation, driven by overconfidence and a desire for propaganda victory.

### The Urban Battle
The German 6th Army and 4th Panzer Army attacked the city in late August 1942, supported by intensive aerial bombardment that destroyed most of its infrastructure. The battle devolved into brutal, close-quarters urban combat, which the Germans called "Rattenkrieg" (Rat War). Soviet commander General Vasily Chuikov employed "hugging" tactics, keeping front lines so close that German air and artillery support risked friendly fire. Fighting raged for individual buildings, streets, and factories, with positions changing hands repeatedly. Key sites included Mamayev Kurgan hill, the grain elevator, and the Pavlov's House strongpoint. The Soviets, despite catastrophic losses, maintained a tenuous supply line across the Volga River.

### Soviet Counter-Offensive and Encirclement
In November, the Soviets launched Operation Uranus, a double envelopment targeting the weak Romanian armies guarding the German flanks. The Axis flanks were overrun, and by 23 November, the German 6th Army was encircled in Stalingrad. Hitler forbade a breakout, ordering the army to hold its position while a relief attempt (Operation Winter Storm) and an airlift supplied it. The airlift failed to deliver adequate supplies, and the relief attempt was halted by Soviet offensives. Starvation, disease, and cold decimated the trapped forces.

### Surrender and Aftermath
On 31 January 1943, Field Marshal Paulus surrendered the southern pocket. The last German forces surrendered on 2 February. Of the approximately 91,000 prisoners taken, most would die in Soviet captivity. The defeat destroyed Germany's 6th Army and marked a catastrophic loss of men and material from which the Wehrmacht never recovered. It shifted the strategic initiative permanently to the Soviet Union and is widely regarded as the war's decisive turning point.

## Terms
*   **Case Blue (Fall Blau):** The code name for the German summer offensive of 1942 in southern Russia.
*   **Operation Uranus:** The Soviet counter-offensive launched on 19 November 1942 that encircled the German 6th Army.
*   **6th Army:** The primary German field army that attacked and was later trapped in Stalingrad.
*   **Rattenkrieg:** German term for the brutal, close-quarters urban warfare ("Rat War") fought in the ruins.
*   **"Hugging" tactic:** Soviet defensive tactic of keeping front lines extremely close to the enemy to negate German air and artillery superiority.
*   **Hiwis (Hilfswillige):** "Volunteer auxiliaries," often Soviet prisoners of war or civilians, who served in supporting or frontline roles with the German army.
*   **Blocking detachments:** Soviet NKVD or regular troops positioned behind front-line units to prevent desertion, sometimes executing those who retreated without orders.
*   **Operation Winter Storm:** The failed German attempt in December 1942 to relieve the encircled 6th Army.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Civilian Casualties:** Estimates of civilian deaths in Stalingrad vary widely, from around 40,000 to over 235,000, with some demographic studies suggesting even higher total losses for the region.
*   **Soviet Losses:** The exact number of Soviet military casualties is debated, with official archival figures around 1.13 million, but some historians argue this is an undercount, with true losses potentially higher.
*   **Role of Coercion vs. Patriotism:** Historians debate the relative importance of Soviet coercive measures (like Order No. 227 and blocking detachments) versus genuine patriotism and hatred of the invader in motivating the Red Army's resistance.
*   **Hitler's Decision to Forbid Breakout:** The strategic wisdom of Hitler's order for the 6th Army to hold its position at all costs, rather than attempting a breakout, remains a subject of military analysis.

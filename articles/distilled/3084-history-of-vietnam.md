# History of Vietnam

## Overview
Vietnam's history is shaped by its strategic location in Southeast Asia, making it a crossroads of trade and conflict. Its development involved the interplay of indigenous cultures, Chinese domination, regional kingdoms like Champa, and later European colonialism, culminating in a modern, unified state forged through prolonged wars and economic reform.

## Timeline
- **c. 2879 BC** — Legendary founding of the Hồng Bàng dynasty and state of Xích Quỷ.
- **257 BC** — Establishment of the Âu Lạc kingdom after the Âu Việt migration.
- **111 BC** — Han dynasty conquest of Nanyue begins a millennium of Chinese dominance.
- **939 AD** — Ngô Quyền defeats the Southern Han, establishing Vietnamese independence.
- **1407–1427** — Ming dynasty occupation of Vietnam.
- **1471** — Lê Thánh Tông conquers Champa, ending it as a major kingdom.
- **1802** — Nguyễn Ánh unifies Vietnam, founding the Nguyễn dynasty.
- **1887** — Vietnam is integrated into French Indochina.
- **1945** — Ho Chi Minh declares the Democratic Republic of Vietnam.
- **1954** — French defeat at Điện Biên Phủ leads to the partition of Vietnam.
- **1975** — Fall of Saigon ends the Vietnam War; South Vietnam is defeated.
- **1986** — Launch of Đổi Mới economic reforms.

## Body

### Prehistory and Early Cultures
Vietnam's earliest inhabitants included Hoabinhian hunter-gatherers. During the Neolithic period, migrations of Austroasiatic and Austronesian farmers introduced wet-rice agriculture. The Bronze Age Đông Sơn culture (c. 1000 BC) emerged in the Red River Delta, associated with the legendary kingdoms of Văn Lang and Âu Lạc. This period saw advanced bronze casting, including iconic drums, and the development of chiefdom societies.

### Chinese Domination and Independence
From 111 BC, much of Vietnam was under Chinese rule for a millennium, a period marked by frequent uprisings like that of the Trưng Sisters (40 AD). Buddhism and Hinduism arrived by the 2nd century CE. Independence was regained in 939 AD under the Ngô dynasty. Subsequent dynasties (Đinh, Lê, Lý, Trần) consolidated power, repelled Mongol invasions, and expanded southward against the Cham kingdom of Champa. The Lê dynasty (1428–1527) promoted Confucianism and further expansion.

### Division and Unification
The 16th to 18th centuries saw political fragmentation, most notably the Trịnh–Nguyễn War, which partitioned the country. The Tây Sơn rebellion (1771) briefly unified the country before the Nguyễn dynasty was established in 1802. French colonization began in 1858, leading to the creation of French Indochina in 1887. After Japanese occupation in WWII, Ho Chi Minh declared independence in 1945. The subsequent First Indochina War ended with French defeat in 1954, partitioning Vietnam at the 17th parallel.

### The Vietnam War and Reunification
The Cold War fueled the conflict between communist North Vietnam (supported by China and the USSR) and anti-communist South Vietnam (supported by the US). The Vietnam War escalated with major US involvement, ending with the fall of Saigon in 1975. Vietnam was officially reunified as the Socialist Republic of Vietnam in 1976. The country later fought a border war with China in 1979 and occupied Cambodia from 1978 to 1989.

### Đổi Mới and Modern Vietnam
Facing economic crisis, the Communist Party launched Đổi Mới (renovation) reforms in 1986, transitioning to a market-oriented economy. Vietnam normalized relations with China in 1991 and the US in 1995. It joined the WTO in 2007 and has since become a middle-income economy and regional power, though it maintains a one-party communist state and faces territorial disputes, particularly in the South China Sea.

## Terms
- **Đông Sơn culture**: A Bronze Age culture (c. 1000 BC) in the Red River Delta known for rice cultivation and sophisticated bronze drums.
- **Chữ Nôm**: A derivative script using Chinese characters to write native Vietnamese words, emerging by the 13th century.
- **Chữ Quốc ngữ**: The Romanized Vietnamese alphabet developed by missionaries in the 17th century.
- **Đổi Mới**: Economic renovation policies launched in 1986, shifting Vietnam from a centrally planned to a market-oriented economy.
- **Viet Minh**: The League for the Independence of Vietnam, a nationalist coalition led by Ho Chi Minh that fought for independence from France and later South Vietnam.
- **French Indochina**: The French colonial federation (1887–1954) comprising Vietnam (divided into Cochinchina, Annam, Tonkin), Cambodia, and Laos.
- **Geneva Accords (1954)**: The agreement that temporarily divided Vietnam at the 17th parallel after the First Indochina War.
- **Re-education camps**: Prisons established by the North Vietnamese government after 1975 to detain former officials and soldiers of South Vietnam.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The nature of Zhao Tuo's Nanyue kingdom (179–111 BC) is debated: some Vietnamese historians see it as a period of Chinese domination, while others view it as an era of independent Vietnamese rule due to the Zhao family's assimilation.
- The death toll of the Vietnam War is contested, with estimates ranging from 966,000 to 3.8 million. The long-term health impact of Agent Orange is also disputed, with the US government questioning Vietnamese casualty figures.
- The causes and scale of executions during North Vietnam's 1953–1956 land reform remain a subject of scholarly debate, with estimates of deaths varying significantly.

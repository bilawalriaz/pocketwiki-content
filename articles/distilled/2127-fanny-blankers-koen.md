# Fanny Blankers-Koen

## Overview
Fanny Blankers-Koen was a Dutch track and field athlete who became an international icon by winning four gold medals at the 1948 London Olympics. Competing as a 30-year-old mother, she shattered prevailing beliefs about age and motherhood as barriers to athletic success, earning the nickname "the Flying Housewife." Her victories and world records across a diverse range of events, from sprints to jumps, made her one of the most celebrated athletes of the 20th century.

## Timeline
- **26 April 1918** — Born Francina Elsje Koen in Lage Vuursche, Netherlands.
- **1935** — Begins competitive athletics; sets first national record.
- **1936** — Competes in Berlin Olympics in high jump and 4×100 m relay.
- **1940** — Marries coach Jan Blankers; World War II cancels Olympics.
- **1942-1944** — Sets six world records during WWII, including in high jump and long jump.
- **1946** — Wins 80 m hurdles and 4×100 m relay at European Championships.
- **2-9 August 1948** — Wins four gold medals (100 m, 200 m, 80 m hurdles, 4×100 m relay) at London Olympics.
- **1950** — Wins three individual titles at European Championships.
- **1952** — Competes in Helsinki Olympics; last major competition.
- **1955** — Wins 58th and final Dutch national title (shot put); retires.
- **1999** — Voted IAAF "Female Athlete of the Century."
- **25 January 2004** — Dies in Hoofddorp, Netherlands.

## Body

### Early Life and Career Beginnings
Born in 1918, Blankers-Koen was a natural athlete who excelled in multiple sports. A swimming coach advised her to focus on running for better Olympic prospects. She debuted in 1935 and was selected for the 1936 Berlin Olympics at age 18, competing in the high jump and relay. She set her first world record in 1938 (100 yards) and won European bronze medals, but the outbreak of World War II halted international competition.

### World War II and World Records
During the German occupation of the Netherlands, Blankers-Koen married her coach, Jan Blankers, in 1940. Despite giving birth to her first child in 1942—a time when motherhood was seen as the end of an athletic career—she resumed training quickly. Between 1942 and 1944, she set six world records in events including the 80 m hurdles, high jump (improving it by 5 cm to 1.71 m), long jump (6.25 m), and relays, often in acts of defiance against the occupying forces.

### The 1948 London Olympics
At the 1948 Olympics, the 30-year-old mother of two was questioned for her age. She silenced critics by winning four gold medals. She won the 100 m on a muddy track and the inaugural Olympic 200 m for women by a record margin. Her 80 m hurdles victory was a photo-finish over Maureen Gardner. She anchored the Dutch 4×100 m relay team to victory after arriving just in time, having gone shopping for a raincoat. Her four golds made her the most successful athlete of the Games.

### Later Career and Legacy
After London, strict amateurism rules limited her commercial opportunities. She won three individual titles at the 1950 European Championships but was hampered by illness at the 1952 Helsinki Olympics, her final Games. She retired in 1955 and later served as captain of the Dutch women's team. In 1999, she was named the IAAF's "Female Athlete of the Century." Her career is credited with helping to eliminate the belief that age and motherhood were barriers to success in women's sport.

## Terms
*   **World Record:** The best performance ever officially recorded in a specific athletic event.
*   **Olympic Gold Medal:** The award given to the winner of an event at the Olympic Games.
*   **Amateurism Rules:** Historical regulations that prohibited athletes from accepting payment for competition, limiting their ability to earn money from endorsements.
*   **Sex Verification Policy:** A controversial practice, implemented by the IAAF in 1950, to determine an athlete's biological sex, which led to the lifetime ban of Dutch sprinter Foekje Dillema.
*   **Pentathlon:** An athletic competition comprising five different track and field events.
*   **Hunger Winter (Hongerwinter):** The severe winter of 1944-45 in the German-occupied Netherlands, marked by widespread famine.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   The circumstances surrounding the 1950 sex verification test and lifetime ban of her rival Foekje Dillema remain controversial. While a posthumous forensic test found a Y-chromosome in Dillema's DNA, most of Blankers-Koen's contemporaries suspected it was an attempt by Blankers-Koen and her husband to eliminate an opponent, though this has never been confirmed.
*   A 2003 biography, *Een koningin met mannenbenen*, challenged her public image as a modest "flying housewife," portraying her instead as a fiercely competitive and emotionally reserved woman driven by a desire to win.

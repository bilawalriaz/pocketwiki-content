# Nairobi

## Overview
Nairobi is the capital and largest city of Kenya, serving as the nation's political, financial, and economic hub. Founded in 1898 as a railway depot, it rapidly grew to become the capital in 1907 and is now a major global city and the 8th most populous in Africa. Its unique distinction is hosting a national park within its boundaries, and it is a key center for international organizations, commerce, and tourism in East Africa.

## Timeline
- **1898** — Founded by colonial authorities as a rail depot on the Uganda–Kenya Railway.
- **1907** — Replaced Mombasa as the capital of the East Africa Protectorate.
- **1919** — Declared a municipality.
- **1950s** — Mau Mau Uprising, fueled in part by discontent over land expansion around the city.
- **1963** — Became the capital of the newly independent Republic of Kenya.
- **1973** — Kenyatta International Conference Centre (KICC) opened.
- **1978** — Jomo Kenyatta International Airport terminal completed and opened.
- **1998** — United States Embassy bombed by Al-Qaeda.
- **2012** — Thika Superhighway opened.
- **2017** — Standard Gauge Railway connecting Nairobi to Mombasa inaugurated.
- **2022** — Nairobi Expressway completed to reduce traffic congestion.

## Body

### Founding and Colonial Growth
The site of Nairobi was originally a swamp inhabited by the Maasai, Akamba, and Kikuyu peoples. The name derives from the Maasai phrase for "place of cool waters." In 1898, colonial engineer Sir George Whitehouse selected the site for the Uganda Railway due to its elevation, climate, and water supply, despite criticism that it was poorly drained. The railway arrived in 1899, and the town quickly grew, replacing Machakos as the regional administrative headquarters. By 1907, it had replaced Mombasa as the protectorate's capital. The early town faced challenges like malaria and plague, but its population grew from 5,000 in 1902 to 16,000 by 1910, driven by administration and tourism.

### Post-Independence Development
After independence in 1963, Nairobi remained the capital and became a center for coffee, tea, and sisal industries. Rapid growth strained infrastructure, leading to common power and water shortages. Major projects included the Kenyatta International Conference Centre (1973) and the expansion of Jomo Kenyatta International Airport (1978). The city's expansion has been marked by both development and conflict, including the 1998 US Embassy bombing. In the 21st century, significant infrastructure projects like the Thika Superhighway (2012), Standard Gauge Railway (2017), and Nairobi Expressway (2022) have been completed to modernize the city and alleviate congestion.

### Economy and Infrastructure
Nairobi is the major financial and economic hub of East Africa. It hosts the Nairobi Securities Exchange (NSE), one of Africa's largest stock exchanges, and the regional headquarters for numerous international companies and organizations, including the United Nations Office at Nairobi (UNON). The city's economy is diverse, with strengths in finance, technology (including a growing FinTech sector), tourism, and manufacturing. Infrastructure includes Jomo Kenyatta International Airport (the largest in Kenya), Wilson Airport for general aviation, and a transport network reliant on matatus (privately owned minibuses), buses, and railways. The city faces ongoing challenges with traffic congestion and water supply, with 40% of piped water lost to distribution inefficiencies.

### Demographics and Geography
Nairobi has a youthful, rapidly growing population estimated at 4.8 million in 2024, with a high urbanization rate. The population is predominantly Christian (88.85%), with a significant Muslim minority (7.53%) and the largest Hindu population in Kenya. The city spans 696 km² and is situated near the eastern edge of the Rift Valley, with the Ngong Hills to the west. It is characterized by stark economic inequality, with affluent suburbs like Karen and Muthaiga contrasting sharply with large informal settlements. Kibera is one of Africa's largest slums, with population estimates ranging from 185,777 (government) to 500,000–1,000,000 (NGOs).

### Parks, Culture, and Society
Nairobi is known as the "Green City under the Sun" for its many parks and green spaces, including Uhuru Park, Central Park, and the Nairobi Arboretum. The city is a cultural melting pot and a hub for Kenyan music, literature, and film. It is the setting for works like *Out of Africa* and has a vibrant food scene ranging from local nyama choma to international cuisines. Sports are central to city life, with football being the most popular sport and major facilities like the Moi International Sports Centre hosting events like the All Africa Games. Educationally, it is home to the University of Nairobi and numerous other institutions of higher learning.

## Terms
- **Matatu**: A privately owned minibus forming the most common form of public transport in Nairobi.
- **Nairobi Securities Exchange (NSE)**: One of Africa's largest stock exchanges, a key financial institution in the city.
- **Kibera**: A large informal settlement (slum) in Nairobi, with disputed population figures.
- **Mau Mau Uprising**: A 1950s rebellion against British colonial rule, partly fueled by land grievances around Nairobi.
- **Standard Gauge Railway (SGR)**: A modern railway line connecting Nairobi to Mombasa, opened in 2017.
- **Nairobi Expressway**: A 27 km toll road completed in 2022 to reduce traffic congestion in the city.
- **Uhuru Park**: A prominent public park in central Nairobi, saved from development by activist Wangari Maathai.
- **Jomo Kenyatta International Airport (JKIA)**: Kenya's largest airport and a major regional aviation hub.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Population of Kibera**: There is a significant discrepancy between the Kenyan government's official population count (185,777) and non-governmental estimates (500,000 to 1,000,000) for the Kibera slum.
- **Urban Planning and Public Space**: The colonial-era 1948 Master Plan, designed for 250,000 people, still guides planning. Rapid growth has led to the loss of public spaces, such as City Park losing approximately 20 hectares to private development.
- **Water Supply Security**: With 40% of piped water lost to distribution and only 40% of house connections receiving water continuously, the city's water supply system faces significant efficiency and equity challenges.

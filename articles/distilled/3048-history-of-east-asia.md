# History of East Asia

## Overview
East Asian history encompasses the interconnected yet distinct histories of China, Japan, Korea, Mongolia, and Taiwan. The region is characterized by shared cultural influences, notably Confucianism, Buddhism, and Chinese writing systems, alongside periods of significant political and military interaction. Understanding this history is crucial for comprehending the modern geopolitical and economic landscape, as East Asia is a pivotal world region with major global influence.

## Timeline
- **c. 2100–c. 1600 BC** — Xia dynasty, first in Chinese historical records.
- **c. 1046–256 BC** — Zhou dynasty, longest-lasting Chinese dynasty.
- **221 BC** — Qin dynasty unifies China, creating first imperial state.
- **206 BC–220 AD** — Han dynasty, establishes Silk Road, fights Xiongnu.
- **372 AD** — Buddhism introduced to Korea from China.
- **581–618 AD** — Sui dynasty reunifies China after centuries of division.
- **618–907 AD** — Tang dynasty, golden age of Chinese civilization and influence.
- **1206** — Genghis Khan unites Mongol tribes into the Mongol Empire.
- **1392–1897** — Joseon dynasty rules Korea, adopting Neo-Confucianism.
- **1603–1868** — Tokugawa shogunate rules Japan, enforcing isolationism.
- **1868** — Meiji Restoration begins Japan's rapid modernization.
- **1912** — End of China's imperial history with the abdication of Puyi.
- **1945** — End of World War II; Korea divided at 38th parallel.
- **1949** — People's Republic of China established; Republic of China retreats to Taiwan.
- **1950–1953** — Korean War ends in stalemate, solidifying division.

## Body

### Foundations and Early Civilizations
Recorded civilization in East Asia began with China's Shang dynasty (c. 1766–1046 BC). Early development was shaped by interaction between agrarian Chinese states and nomadic steppe peoples like the Xiongnu and later the Mongols. Korea's first organized state, Gojoseon, is traditionally dated to 2333 BC. Japan's early history includes the Jōmon and Yayoi periods, with cultural exchange via the Korean peninsula. Confucianism and Taoism emerged in China during the Spring and Autumn period. Buddhism was introduced from India during the Han dynasty and spread throughout the region, blending with local beliefs.

### Medieval Interactions and the Mongol Era
The Tang (618–907) and Song (960–1279) dynasties were highly influential. The Tang perfected the civil service examination system, which shaped governance in China, Korea, and Japan. The Mongol Empire, under Genghis Khan and his successors, conquered most of East Asia, establishing the Yuan dynasty (1271–1368) in China and making Korea a vassal state. The Mongols attempted but failed to invade Japan in 1274 and 1281, thwarted by typhoons. This period saw the spread of technologies like gunpowder and printing from China.

### Early Modern Consolidation and Isolation
Following the Mongol collapse, new dynasties emerged. China's Ming (1368–1644) and Qing (1644–1912) dynasties, and Korea's Joseon dynasty (1392–1897), adopted Neo-Confucianism as state ideology. Japan entered a long period of peace and isolation under the Tokugawa shogunate (1603–1868). Limited European contact began in the 16th century, with the Portuguese in Macau and attempts to Christianize Japan. By the 17th century, East Asian nations largely pursued isolationism in response to European presence.

### Western Imperialism and Modernization
The 19th century brought European imperial pressure. China's Qing dynasty suffered military defeats in the Opium Wars (1839–42, 1856–60), leading to unequal treaties and territorial concessions. Internal crises included the Taiping Rebellion (1851–64) and the Boxer Rebellion (1898–1900). In contrast, Japan's Meiji Restoration (1868) initiated rapid westernization and industrialization, allowing it to defeat China (1895) and annex Korea (1910). China's imperial system ended in 1912.

### War, Revolution, and the Cold War
The first half of the 20th century was dominated by Japanese expansionism, culminating in the Second Sino-Japanese War (1937–45) and Japan's entry into World War II after attacking Pearl Harbor (1941). Japan's defeat led to U.S. occupation. The Chinese Civil War concluded with the Communist victory in 1949. The Korean War (1950–53) solidified the division of the peninsula into two separate states, a central Cold War conflict.

### Contemporary East Asia
Post-war Japan experienced an "economic miracle." Since 1980, the economies of South Korea and China have grown exponentially. The end of the Cold War and globalization have integrated these nations into the world economy. In 2010, East Asia's population was approximately 24% of the world's total.

## Terms
*   **Sinicization:** The process by which non-Chinese societies adopt Chinese culture, language, and institutions.
*   **Neo-Confucianism:** A revival of Confucian ethics combined with metaphysical elements, adopted as state ideology in Song China, Joseon Korea, and Tokugawa Japan.
*   **Shogunate:** A Japanese feudal military government, with the shōgun as the de facto ruler.
*   **Tributary system:** A Chinese-led diplomatic framework where neighboring states acknowledged Chinese superiority through ritual tribute in exchange for trade and recognition.
*   **Meiji Restoration:** The 1868 political revolution that restored imperial rule in Japan and launched rapid modernization.
*   **Opium Wars:** Two 19th-century conflicts between China and Western powers (primarily Britain) that resulted in Chinese defeats and unequal treaties.
*   **Yangban:** The scholar-gentry class that dominated Joseon Korean society and government.
*   **Silk Road:** Ancient network of trade routes connecting East Asia to the Mediterranean, facilitating the exchange of goods, ideas, and religions.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Historical interpretation of Goguryeo:** While some Chinese narratives describe Goguryeo as a regional minority state within Northeast China, this is widely disputed in Korean and international scholarship, which recognizes it as an independent kingdom central to Korean history.
*   **Scope of East Asia:** Scholars debate the region's exact boundaries. Some argue East and Southeast Asia form a single ethno-cultural area, while others define it by shared adherence to Confucianism, Buddhist influences, and chopstick use.
*   **Decline of religion:** While cultural institutions of religions like Confucianism remain, actual belief has declined across the region due to factors like the abolition of the examination system in 1905, modernization, and the rise of secular ideologies.

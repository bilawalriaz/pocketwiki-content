# Iraq

## Overview
Iraq, officially the Republic of Iraq, is a country in West Asia whose modern borders encompass the ancient region of Mesopotamia, often called the "cradle of civilisation." Its history spans from the earliest urban societies to the modern era, marked by successive empires, the Islamic Golden Age, and recent decades of conflict and reconstruction. Today, it is a federal parliamentary republic and a major oil producer navigating post-war stability and development.

## Timeline
- **c. 6000–5000 BC** — Emergence of Sumer, the world's earliest civilisation, in Mesopotamia.
- **539 BCE** — Achaemenid conquest of Babylon; Mesopotamia becomes a core Persian province.
- **762 CE** — Founding of Baghdad as the capital of the Abbasid Caliphate.
- **1258 CE** — Mongol sack of Baghdad ends Abbasid political rule.
- **1534** — Ottoman conquest begins centuries of Ottoman rule.
- **1920** — British Mandate of Mesopotamia established after World War I.
- **1932** — Iraq gains formal independence from Britain.
- **1958** — Military coup overthrows the monarchy, establishing a republic.
- **1968** — Ba'ath Party seizes power; Saddam Hussein later becomes dominant leader.
- **1980–1988** — Iran–Iraq War ends in stalemate with massive casualties.
- **1990** — Iraq invades Kuwait, leading to the Gulf War and international sanctions.
- **2003** — U.S.-led coalition invades, overthrowing Saddam Hussein's government.
- **2014–2017** — War against the Islamic State (ISIS) group.

## Body

### Ancient and Medieval Foundations
Iraq largely coincides with Mesopotamia, where the Tigris and Euphrates rivers fostered the rise of early cities and empires like Sumer, Akkad, Babylonia, and Assyria. This region saw the invention of writing, the wheel, and early law codes. After the Arab conquest in the 7th century, Iraq became the heartland of the Abbasid Caliphate. With Baghdad founded in 762, it became a global intellectual hub during the Islamic Golden Age, home to institutions like the House of Wisdom. This era ended with the Mongol sack of Baghdad in 1258. Subsequent rule by various dynasties preceded centuries of Ottoman administration from the 16th century.

### Modern State Formation and Conflict
Following World War I and the Ottoman defeat, Britain established the Mandate of Mesopotamia. In 1921, Faisal I was made king, laying foundations for the modern state. Independence came in 1932. A 1958 military coup ended the monarchy, leading to a period of instability. The Ba'ath Party took power in 1968, with Saddam Hussein becoming the dominant figure. His rule included the costly Iran–Iraq War (1980–1988) and the 1990 invasion of Kuwait, which triggered the Gulf War and severe sanctions. The 2003 U.S.-led invasion toppled Saddam's government, leading to an insurgency, sectarian civil war, and the rise of ISIS. A U.S.-backed coalition and Iraqi forces defeated ISIS by 2017.

### Contemporary Iraq
Since 2022, Iraq has experienced a period of relative political and economic stabilization. The government has pursued infrastructure projects, like the Grand Faw Port, and economic diversification. However, challenges persist, including endemic corruption, a sectarian political system, and severe environmental pressures. Climate change is causing widespread drought, with water flows in the Tigris and Euphrates down 30-40% in 2023, threatening agriculture and electricity generation. The economy remains overwhelmingly dependent on oil, which provided about 92% of foreign exchange earnings as of 2021.

## Terms
- **Mesopotamia**: The historical region between the Tigris and Euphrates rivers, corresponding largely to modern Iraq, known as the "cradle of civilisation."
- **Abbasid Caliphate**: The third caliphate to succeed the Islamic prophet Muhammad, ruling from Baghdad during the Islamic Golden Age (8th–13th centuries).
- **Ba'ath Party**: A secular Arab nationalist party that ruled Iraq as a one-party state from 1968 to 2003.
- **Sectarianism**: Political or social conflict based on religious sect, a major factor in post-2003 Iraq.
- **Popular Mobilisation Forces (PMF)**: An umbrella group of mostly Shia militias, officially part of the Iraqi security forces, formed to fight ISIS.
- **Kurdistan Regional Government (KRG)**: The autonomous government of the Kurdistan Region in northern Iraq.
- **Shatt al-Arab**: The waterway formed by the confluence of the Tigris and Euphrates rivers, flowing into the Persian Gulf.
- **OPEC**: The Organization of the Petroleum Exporting Countries, of which Iraq is a founding member.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The precise historical extent of the region called "Iraq" in medieval times is debated among historians, with definitions varying.
- The causes and management of the post-2003 insurgency and civil war remain subjects of analysis, particularly regarding the disbanding of the Iraqi army and de-Ba'athification.
- The future balance of power between the central government in Baghdad and the Kurdistan Regional Government, especially regarding oil revenues and territorial disputes like Kirkuk, is unresolved.
- The long-term impact of climate change and water scarcity on Iraq's stability and agricultural viability is a critical ongoing concern.

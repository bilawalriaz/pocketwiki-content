# The Buddha

## Overview  
Siddhartha Gautama (c. 6th–5th century BCE), later known as the Buddha ("Awakened One"), was a wandering ascetic and teacher whose insights into suffering and liberation founded Buddhism. Born into the Shakya clan in Lumbini (modern Nepal), he renounced princely life after encountering human suffering, attained enlightenment under the Bodhi tree at Bodh Gaya, and spent decades teaching the path to liberation before dying in Kushinagar. His core doctrines—the Four Noble Truths, the Noble Eightfold Path, dependent origination, and anatta (non-self)—form the foundation of Buddhist thought. The historical Buddha emerged amid the rise of śramaṇa movements and urbanization in the Ganges basin, offering a middle path between ritualistic Brahmanism and extreme asceticism.

## Body  

### Historical Context and Sources  
The Buddha lived during the Mahajanapada period, likely under the reign of Bimbisara and Ajatashatru of Magadha, making him a contemporary of Mahavira. No written records from his lifetime survive; the earliest mentions appear in Ashoka’s edicts (c. 268–232 BCE). The Pali Canon and Āgamas preserve early discourses, while later biographies like the *Buddhacarita* (1st century CE) and *Lalitavistara Sūtra* (3rd century CE) add mythological elements. Archaeological evidence includes a 6th-century BCE structure at Lumbini and the Relic Stupa of Vaishali.

### Core Teachings  
The Buddha taught the **Middle Way**, avoiding extremes of sensual indulgence and self-mortification. Central doctrines include the **Four Noble Truths** (existence of suffering, its cause in craving, cessation, and the Eightfold Path), **dependent origination** (all phenomena arise interdependently), and **anatta** (no permanent self). The **five aggregates** (form, sensation, perception, mental formations, consciousness) explain how sensory experience binds beings to samsara. Liberation (nirvana) is achieved through ethical conduct, mental discipline, and wisdom.

### Biographical Narrative  
Born to Suddhodana and Maya of the Shakya clan, Siddhartha was shielded from suffering until the "four sights"—an old man, a sick person, a corpse, and an ascetic—prompted his renunciation. After studying meditation under Alara Kalama and Uddaka Ramaputta, he practiced extreme asceticism before adopting the Middle Way. Enlightenment came under the Bodhi tree, where he defeated Mara and realized the nature of reality. His first sermon at Sarnath initiated the saṅgha, and he spent 45 years teaching across the Ganges basin.

### Development of Traditions  
Buddhism evolved into Theravāda, Mahāyāna, and Vajrayāna traditions. Mahāyāna reinterpreted the Buddha as a cosmic figure, emphasizing the **trikāya** doctrine (three bodies: nirmāṇakāya, saṃbhogakāya, dharmakāya). The Buddha was incorporated into Hinduism as Vishnu’s ninth avatar and into other religions like Christianity (via St. Josaphat) and Islam (as Budd or Shakyamuni).

## Terms  
- **Buddha**: "Awakened One"; title for one who has attained enlightenment.  
- **Nirvana**: Liberation from suffering and rebirth; extinguishing the fires of desire, hatred, and delusion.  
- **Saṅgha**: The monastic community of Buddhist monks and nuns.  
- **Dharma**: Teachings of the Buddha; cosmic law.  
- **Karma**: Intentional action determining future rebirths; mental intention is primary.  
- **Anatta**: Doctrine of non-self; no permanent, unchanging soul.  
- **Śramaṇa**: Ascetic or renunciant; member of heterodox traditions challenging Vedic orthodoxy.  
- **Tathāgata**: Term the Buddha used for himself, meaning "one who has thus gone" or "thus come."  
- **Bodhi**: Enlightenment or awakening.  
- **Parinirvana**: Final release from conditioned existence upon death.

## Debates and Open Questions  
- **Dating**: Scholars debate the Buddha’s lifespan, with long chronology (624–544 BCE) and short chronology (448–368 BCE) proposed. Most favor c. 563–483 BCE.  
- **Historicity**: While his existence is widely accepted, details of his life are contested. Some scholars emphasize a rational, human teacher; others see mythologization.  
- **Core Teachings**: Disagreement exists over whether doctrines like the Four Noble Truths and Eightfold Path originated with the Buddha or were later additions.  
- **Omniscience**: Early texts do not attribute omniscience to the Buddha; later traditions deified him.  
- **Women’s Ordination**: The historicity of the eight gurudharmas (conditions for nun ordination) is questioned due to inconsistencies.  
- **Last Meal**: Theravāda tradition holds it was pork; Mahāyāna, a plant or mushroom. Scholars debate the cause of his illness (food poisoning vs. mesenteric infarction).

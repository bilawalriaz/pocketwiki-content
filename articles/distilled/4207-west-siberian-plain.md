# West Siberian Plain

## Overview
The West Siberian Plain is one of the world's largest unbroken lowlands, covering about a third of Siberia between the Ural Mountains and the Yenisei River. It is a vast, flat, and poorly drained region characterized by extensive swamps, harsh subarctic winters, and significant petroleum and natural gas reserves that were crucial to Russia's economy in the late 20th century.

## Timeline
- **c. 65 million years ago** — Formation begins with horizontal deposits from prolonged crustal subsidence.
- **Quaternary Period** — Glacial deposits extend south, forming low hills and ridges like the Ob Plateau.
- **1970s-1980s** — Most of Russia's oil and gas production is extracted from the plain's reserves.

## Body

### Geography and Topography
The plain extends 2,500 km north-south from the Arctic Ocean to the Altai Mountains and 1,900 km east-west from the Yenisei River to the Urals. Over 50% of its area is less than 100 meters above sea level, making it extraordinarily flat. Major rivers include the Ob, Irtysh, Nadym, Pur, and Taz, with the Yenisei forming its eastern boundary. The region is one of the world's largest peatland areas, featuring massive raised bogs like the Vasyugan Swamp (approx. 51,600 km²). The southern part contains important agricultural zones like the Ishim Plain and Baraba Lowland.

### Climate and Ecology
Winters are long and harsh, with a climate that is mostly subarctic or continental. The plain has eight distinct vegetation regions, from tundra in the north to steppe in the south. Animal species diversity increases from at least 107 in the tundra to over 278 in the forest-steppe. The southern grasslands, an extension of the Kazakh Steppe, were largely cleared by the early 21st century.

### Geology and Resources
The plain consists primarily of Cenozoic alluvial deposits and has undergone prolonged subsidence. Its flatness is such that a 50-meter sea level rise would inundate the land between the Arctic Ocean and the Ob-Irtysh confluence. The region's geology includes deposits resulting from ancient ice dams that redirected river flows. It holds large petroleum and natural gas reserves, which were the primary source of Russia's oil and gas production during the 1970s and 1980s.

## Terms
- **Alluvial deposits**: Sediments (like clay, silt, sand) deposited by flowing water.
- **Histosols**: Soils composed mainly of organic materials, such as peat.
- **Histels**: A type of soil order in areas with permafrost, also rich in organic matter.
- **Peatlands**: Wetlands characterized by the accumulation of peat (partially decayed vegetation).
- **Raised bogs**: Peatlands that are dome-shaped and fed primarily by precipitation, not groundwater.
- **Permafrost**: Ground that remains frozen for two or more consecutive years.
- **Subsidence**: The gradual caving in or sinking of an area of land.
- **Cenozoic**: The current geological era, spanning the last 66 million years.

## Debates and Open Questions
The source notes that the plain's deposits may result from ice dams that reversed the flow of the Ob and Yenisei rivers, redirecting them into the Caspian Sea, and "perhaps the Aral Sea as well," indicating some uncertainty about the full extent of this ancient hydrological event.

# Sviatoslav I

## Overview
Sviatoslav I (c. 943–972) was the Prince of Kiev whose aggressive military campaigns dramatically reshaped Eastern Europe. He is primarily known for destroying the Khazar Khaganate and severely weakening the First Bulgarian Empire, actions that opened new trade routes for Kievan Rus' but also set the stage for Byzantine intervention and his own death. His reign marked a period of rapid, though ultimately unsustainable, expansion for the Rus' state.

## Timeline
- **c. 943** — Sviatoslav is born.
- **945** — His father, Igor, is killed; his mother Olga becomes regent.
- **c. 962** — Sviatoslav reaches maturity and begins personal rule.
- **c. 965** — Destroys the Khazar fortress of Sarkel.
- **c. 965–969** — Conquers the Khazar capital of Atil and campaigns against Volga Bulgaria.
- **967/968** — Invades Bulgaria at Byzantine request, defeating Boris II.
- **969** — Moves his capital to Pereyaslavets on the Danube.
- **970** — Leads a coalition against Byzantium, defeated at the Battle of Arcadiopolis.
- **971** — Besieged at Dorostolon; signs a treaty with Emperor John I Tzimiskes to withdraw.
- **972** — Ambushed and killed by Pechenegs at the Dnieper rapids.

## Body

### Early Life and Character
Sviatoslav spent his youth in Novgorod under the regency of his mother, Olga, following his father Igor's death in 945. He was tutored by a Varangian named Asmud. Described as a staunch pagan who feared his warriors would mock him for converting, Sviatoslav was a warrior-prince who lived a spartan life on campaign, sharing his men's hardships. Contemporary accounts note his average height, sturdy build, bald head, and distinctive single gold earring.

### Eastern Campaigns and the Fall of Khazaria
Shortly after taking power, Sviatoslav launched campaigns to control the Volga trade route, then dominated by the Khazar Khaganate. He rallied Slavic tribes, attacked Volga Bulgaria, and destroyed key Khazar cities, including Sarkel and the capital Atil. This conquest eliminated a major regional power, allowing Kievan Rus' to dominate north-south trade routes and encouraging Slavic settlement in former Khazar lands. The exact chronology of these campaigns is debated.

### Balkan Campaigns and Conflict with Byzantium
In 967/968, Sviatoslav was paid by the Byzantine Empire to attack Bulgaria. He quickly conquered northern Bulgaria but refused to cede the territory, instead moving his capital to the Danube. This led to war with Byzantium. After initial successes, Sviatoslav was defeated by Byzantine forces and besieged in Dorostolon. He was forced to sign a treaty in 971, agreeing to abandon the Balkans and return to Rus'.

### Death and Succession
In 972, while returning to Kiev, Sviatoslav was ambushed and killed by Pechenegs, likely at Byzantine instigation. His failure to establish a stable succession led to a fratricidal war among his three sons: Yaropolk, Oleg, and Vladimir. Yaropolk and Oleg were killed, and Vladimir emerged as the sole ruler of Kievan Rus'.

## Terms
*   **Druzhina:** The prince's personal retinue or war band, whose loyalty was essential for military campaigns.
*   **Khazar Khaganate:** A powerful Turkic state in the Pontic-Caspian steppe that controlled key trade routes until its destruction by Sviatoslav.
*   **Pechenegs:** A nomadic Turkic people of the steppe, who were sometimes allies and sometimes enemies of the Rus'.
*   **Varangians:** The Old Norse term for the Vikings who were the early rulers and warriors of Kievan Rus'.
*   **Pereyaslavets:** A city on the Danube that Sviatoslav attempted to make his new capital due to its strategic trade location.
*   **Dorostolon:** A Bulgarian fortress (modern Silistra) where Sviatoslav was besieged by Byzantine forces in 971.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   The exact chronology of Sviatoslav's Khazar campaigns, particularly whether Sarkel fell before or after Atil, is uncertain and disputed by historians.
*   The origins of Sviatoslav's wives and the precise status of his sons (e.g., whether Oleg or Yaropolk was the eldest) are unclear in the sources.
*   The degree of Byzantine influence in inciting Sviatoslav's campaigns against the Khazars and later Bulgaria is a matter of scholarly speculation.

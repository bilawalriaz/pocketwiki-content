# Manchester

## Overview
Manchester is a major city in North West England, historically pivotal as the world's first industrialised city during the Industrial Revolution. Its economy, culture, and global profile have evolved from a textile manufacturing hub to a modern centre for finance, media, and sport, with a population of over 589,000 in 2024.

## Body

### Early History and Industrial Revolution
The settlement began with the Roman fort Mamucium (c. AD 79). After Roman withdrawal, it became a manorial township. Its transformation began with the textile trade, boosted by Flemish weavers in the 14th century and the navigable River Irwell by 1736. The Bridgewater Canal (1761) drastically reduced coal costs, and the first cotton mill was built in 1780. By the early 19th century, Manchester was the global centre of cotton processing, earning the nicknames "Cottonopolis" and "Warehouse City." This rapid, unplanned industrialisation made it the world's first industrialised city.

### Political and Social Change
Industrialisation brought social unrest and political innovation. The Peterloo Massacre (1819) saw soldiers charge a peaceful reform protest, killing at least 18. The city became a hub for radical politics, including the Anti-Corn Law League (1838), the Chartist Movement, and the Suffragettes. Friedrich Engels wrote *The Condition of the Working Class in England* (1844) based on his time in Manchester, where he met Karl Marx at Chetham's Library. The first Trades Union Congress was held there in 1868.

### Decline and Regeneration
The textile industry declined after World War I due to global competition. The Manchester Ship Canal (1894) enabled a port, but it closed in 1982 as ships grew too large. Heavy industry suffered from the 1960s onward, with 150,000 manufacturing jobs lost between 1961 and 1983. The 1996 IRA bombing, while devastating, catalysed extensive regeneration. The 2002 Commonwealth Games accelerated this, leading to modern architecture, redeveloped mills, and new cultural venues.

### Modern Economy and Governance
Today, Manchester's economy is based on business, financial, and professional services, with a GVA of £34.8 billion for the Greater Manchester South area in 2023. It is ranked as a Beta– global city. The city is governed by Manchester City Council, but strategic decisions are made by the Greater Manchester Combined Authority, led by a directly elected mayor. The city is notably unequal, containing both some of the UK's most deprived and affluent neighbourhoods.

### Culture and Education
Manchester is a UNESCO City of Literature with a radical literary history. It is globally renowned for its music scene, from the 1960s Hollies and Bee Gees to the 1980s/90s "Madchester" scene (Smiths, Stone Roses, Oasis). The city has two Premier League football clubs and major sporting venues built for the 2002 Games. It is a major media centre, home to The Guardian (founded 1821) and Granada Television. The University of Manchester, formed in 2004, is a leading research institution where the first stored-program computer (1948) and graphene (2004) were developed.

## Terms
- **Mancunian:** A native or resident of Manchester.
- **Cottonopolis:** A 19th-century nickname for Manchester, reflecting its dominance in the cotton trade.
- **Peterloo Massacre:** The 1819 violent dispersal of a pro-democracy rally in Manchester.
- **Manchester Ship Canal:** A 36-mile waterway opened in 1894 that allowed ocean-going ships to reach the city.
- **Deindustrialisation:** The decline of industrial activity in a region, as seen in Manchester post-WWII.
- **Greater Manchester Combined Authority (GMCA):** A strategic authority with an elected mayor, overseeing policy for transport, housing, and policing across Greater Manchester.
- **Bee Network:** Greater Manchester's integrated transport system for buses, trams, and cycling.
- **Madchester:** A late-1980s/early-1990s music and cultural scene blending alternative rock with acid house.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Second City Status:** Manchester is in an ongoing dispute with Birmingham over the title of the UK's unofficial "second city."
- **Name Origin:** The etymology of the Brittonic name is debated, with theories referencing a "breast-shaped hill" (*mamm-*) or a "mother" river goddess (*mamma*).
- **Urban Boundaries:** The name "Manchester" is informally applied to various areas (e.g., post town, congestion charge zone) that do not match the official metropolitan borough boundaries.

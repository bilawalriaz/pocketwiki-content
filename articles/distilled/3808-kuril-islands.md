# Kuril Islands

## Overview
The Kuril Islands are a volcanic archipelago stretching 1,300 km between Japan's Hokkaido and Russia's Kamchatka Peninsula, separating the Sea of Okhotsk from the Pacific Ocean. Administered by Russia since 1945, the islands are the subject of a long-standing territorial dispute with Japan over the four southernmost islands, known in Japan as the "Northern Territories." The chain is geologically active, ecologically rich, and strategically significant.

## Body

### Geography and Climate
The Kuril Islands are part of the Pacific Ring of Fire, formed by the subduction of the Pacific Plate under the Okhotsk Plate. The chain contains about 100 volcanoes, with approximately 40 active. The climate is severe, with long, cold, stormy winters and short, foggy summers. Vegetation ranges from tundra in the north to dense forests on the larger southern islands.

### Nature
The surrounding waters are among the most productive in the North Pacific, supporting abundant marine life including kelp beds, fish like walleye pollock, and marine mammals. Historically, fur seals and sea otters were heavily exploited. The islands host millions of seabirds. Terrestrial species are dominated by Asian and Kamchatkan taxa, with low endemism. The WWF divides the islands into two ecoregions: the South Sakhalin-Kurile mixed forests and the Kamchatka-Kurile meadows and sparse forests.

### Early History and Japanese Administration
The indigenous Ainu people inhabited the islands from early times. Japanese nominal control began during the Edo period (1603–1868). At the end of the 19th century, Japan began forced assimilation of the Ainu, granting them automatic Japanese citizenship and suppressing their cultural practices.

### World War II and Soviet Occupation
The islands were a strategic military site; the Japanese assembled their Pearl Harbor strike-force at Iturup. The U.S. bombed Japanese bases there in 1943-44. In August 1945, following the Yalta Agreement, the Soviet Union invaded and occupied the islands, expelling all Japanese civilians by 1946.

### Russian Administration and Dispute
The islands are administered by Russia as part of Sakhalin Oblast. Japan claims the four southernmost islands (Kunashir, Iturup, Shikotan, and the Habomai islets) as its "Northern Territories." The dispute remains unresolved. Russia has invested in infrastructure and established a special economic zone to develop the region.

### Demographics and Economy
As of 2013, about 19,400 people lived on the islands, mostly on the four disputed southern islands and Paramushir. The population includes ethnic Russians and other groups. Fishing is the primary occupation, and the islands have mineral deposits. Russia has upgraded infrastructure, including a geothermal power plant.

## Terms
- **Ring of Fire**: A zone of tectonic instability and frequent volcanoes and earthquakes encircling the Pacific Ocean.
- **Stratovolcano**: A conical volcano built up by many layers of hardened lava and tectonic materials.
- **Subduction**: A geological process where one tectonic plate moves under another and sinks into the mantle.
- **Ainu**: The indigenous people of the Kuril Islands and northern Japan.
- **Northern Territories**: The Japanese name for the four southernmost Kuril Islands claimed by Japan.
- **Miyabe Line**: A biogeographical dividing line between the North and South Kurils, corresponding to the Vries Strait.
- **Pinniped**: A widely distributed and diverse clade of carnivorous, fin-footed, semiaquatic marine mammals (seals, sea lions).
- **Endemism**: The state of a species being native to and found only in a specific geographic area.

## Debates and Open Questions
The primary ongoing debate is the Kuril Islands dispute between Russia and Japan. Japan claims the four southern islands, while Russia maintains sovereignty over the entire chain. The dispute has prevented the two countries from signing a formal peace treaty to end World War II hostilities.

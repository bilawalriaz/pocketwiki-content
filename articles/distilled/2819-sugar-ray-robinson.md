# Sugar Ray Robinson

## Overview
Sugar Ray Robinson (1921–1989) is widely regarded as the greatest pound-for-pound boxer in history. His career, spanning from 1940 to 1965, set new standards for skill, versatility, and showmanship, and he was the first boxer to win a divisional world championship five times.

## Timeline
- **May 3, 1921** — Born Walker Smith Jr. in Ailey, Georgia.
- **1939–1940** — Wins New York Golden Gloves titles at featherweight and lightweight.
- **Oct 4, 1940** — Makes professional debut, winning by second-round stoppage.
- **Dec 20, 1946** — Wins vacant World Welterweight title from Tommy Bell.
- **June 25, 1947** — Knockout of Jimmy Doyle results in Doyle's death later that night.
- **Feb 14, 1951** — Defeats Jake LaMotta to win undisputed World Middleweight title.
- **Sept 12, 1951** — Loses middleweight title to Randolph Turpin in London.
- **Sept 1951** — Regains middleweight title by knocking out Turpin in a rematch.
- **June 25, 1952** — Retires after a loss to light heavyweight champion Joey Maxim.
- **1955** — Returns to boxing, regaining the middleweight title from Bobo Olson.
- **1957** — Regains middleweight title for a fourth time with a KO of Gene Fullmer.
- **1958** — Defeats Carmen Basilio to become the first five-time divisional champion.
- **Nov 10, 1965** — Fights his final bout, losing a decision to Joey Archer.
- **April 12, 1989** — Dies in Los Angeles at age 67.

## Body

### Early Life and Amateur Career
Born Walker Smith Jr., he moved with his mother to Harlem at age 12. He adopted the name "Ray Robinson" from an expired AAU membership card to circumvent age requirements for amateur boxing. The nickname "Sugar" was added after a spectator remarked he was "sweet as sugar." His amateur record is commonly cited as 85–0 with 69 knockouts, though this is disputed. He won the New York Golden Gloves featherweight title in 1939 and lightweight title in 1940.

### Professional Rise and Welterweight Reign
Robinson turned professional in 1940 and quickly became a dominant force. He compiled a 91-fight unbeaten streak from 1943 to 1951, the sixth-longest in professional boxing history. After being denied a title shot due to his refusal to cooperate with the Mafia-controlled boxing establishment, he finally won the vacant World Welterweight title from Tommy Bell in 1946. He successfully defended the title until 1951. A notable and tragic event was his 1947 knockout of Jimmy Doyle, which resulted in Doyle's death. Robinson later gave his purse from his next four fights to Doyle's mother.

### Middleweight Championships and Comeback
Robinson moved up in weight and won the undisputed World Middleweight title in 1951 by defeating Jake LaMotta in their sixth and final meeting, the "St. Valentine's Day Massacre." After a brief retirement in 1952, he returned in 1955 and began a historic run at middleweight. He became the first boxer to win a divisional world championship five times, a feat he accomplished by defeating Carmen Basilio in 1958. His rivalry with Basilio produced two "Fight of the Year" bouts (1957 and 1958).

### Decline and Legacy
Robinson's skills declined in the 1960s. He lost his final title bid to Paul Pender in 1960 and fought his last bout in 1965. He retired with a professional record of 174–19–6 (2 no contests) and 109 knockouts. Outside the ring, Robinson was a flamboyant celebrity who pioneered the modern sports "entourage." He struggled financially after his career and died in 1989. He is consistently ranked as the greatest boxer of all time by major publications and boxing organizations.

## Terms
*   **Pound-for-pound:** A ranking concept created for Robinson to compare boxers of different weight classes on an equal basis.
*   **Boxer-puncher:** A fighting style combining the technical skills of a boxer with the power of a puncher.
*   **Undisputed champion:** A boxer who holds the world title from all major sanctioning bodies in a weight class.
*   **Lineal champion:** The fighter who has defeated the reigning champion in the ring, tracing a direct lineage of titleholders.
*   **Technical knockout (TKO):** A fight stopped by the referee or ringside doctor before a knockout occurs.
*   **Entourage:** A group of attendants and companions who accompany a celebrity, a concept Robinson popularized.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Amateur Record:** Robinson's amateur record is commonly listed as 85–0, but it has been reported he lost to Billy Graham and Patsy Pesca as a teenager.
*   **Fight of the Year Slight:** Robinson considered being passed over for the 1955 "Fight of the Year" award (given to Carmen Basilio) the biggest disappointment of his career.
*   **Cause of Death:** While the source states he died of heart disease, it also notes he was diagnosed with Alzheimer's disease and diabetes mellitus in his last years.

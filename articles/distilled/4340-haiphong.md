# Haiphong

## Overview
Haiphong is a major port city in northeastern Vietnam, the country's third-largest by population. It holds historical significance as the first city in Vietnam and mainland Southeast Asia to be electrified and has been a key economic and industrial center since the French colonial era.

## Body

### Geography and Climate
Haiphong is a coastal city at the mouth of the Cấm River, bordering the Gulf of Tonkin. Its total area is 3,194.72 km², including offshore islands like Bạch Long Vĩ and Cát Bà. The climate is humid subtropical (Koppen: Cwa) with hot, humid summers and warm, dry winters. About 90% of its annual rainfall (~1,700 mm) falls between April and October.

### History
The area's recorded history begins with the 1st-century resistance led by Lê Chân. In the 19th century, it was part of An Dương District. Following World War II, French forces reoccupied the city, leading to a 1946 naval bombardment that started the First Indochina War. During the Vietnam War, the city was bombed and its harbor mined by U.S. forces in 1972, though its physical structure was largely preserved due to a self-imposed U.S. prohibition zone. Post-war, it recovered as an industrial center. In 2025, the city's administrative area expanded significantly with the merger of Hải Dương Province.

### Economy
Haiphong is a major economic hub. Under French rule, it was a level-1 city planned as an economic capital for Indochina. Its budget revenues grew from 34,000 billion VND in 2009 to 56,288 billion in 2015. The city trades with over 40 countries. Key industries include food processing, textiles, cement, steel pipes, and shipbuilding. The fishing sector, particularly aquaculture, is also significant, though agriculture and fishery still employ nearly a third of the workforce.

### Demographics and Transport
Haiphong is Vietnam's third-most populous city, with a 2015 metropolitan population of 2,103,500. Life expectancy is 74.5 years overall. The city is a transport junction, connected to Hanoi by National Highway 5 and to southern provinces by Route 10. The Port of Haiphong on the Cấm River is a major commercial facility with three main docks.

## Terms
- **Humid subtropical climate (Cwa):** A climate classification featuring hot, humid summers and cool, dry winters.
- **Provincial Competitiveness Index (PCI):** A tool for evaluating the business environment in Vietnam's provinces.
- **Tidal flat ecosystem:** Coastal wetlands formed by tidal action, often reclaimed for human use.
- **Aquaculture:** The farming of aquatic organisms like fish and shellfish.
- **Belt and Road Initiative:** A global infrastructure development strategy led by China.

# Edward Drinker Cope

## Overview
Edward Drinker Cope (1840–1897) was a prolific American paleontologist and zoologist whose discoveries and publications helped define the field of American paleontology. His career was marked by a legendary fossil-finding rivalry with Othniel Charles Marsh, known as the Bone Wars, and by his influential, though later contested, Neo-Lamarckian views on evolution.

## Body

### Early Life and Career
Born to a Quaker shipping family, Cope was a child prodigy who published his first paper at 19. Despite his father's initial attempts to make him a farmer, Cope pursued science with little formal training, relying on fieldwork and self-education. He studied briefly at the University of Pennsylvania under Joseph Leidy and traveled Europe in 1863–64, where he first met O.C. Marsh. Upon returning, he secured a teaching post at Haverford College but soon resigned to focus on fossil prospecting in New Jersey and the West.

### The Bone Wars
Cope's relationship with Marsh deteriorated into the "Bone Wars," a bitter competition to discover and name new dinosaur species. The feud began with incidents like Cope's error in mounting *Elasmosaurus* and Marsh's secret deal with a fossil supplier. From 1877, both men raced to excavate sites like Como Bluff, Wyoming, employing spies, bribing collectors, and sabotaging each other's work. The rivalry consumed their finances and time, though it yielded a wealth of fossils. Marsh, backed by Yale and the U.S. Geological Survey, had institutional advantages, while Cope relied on personal funds and the *American Naturalist*.

### Scientific Contributions and Views
Cope was a prodigious author, publishing over 1,400 papers and naming more than 1,000 vertebrate species, including dinosaurs like *Camarasaurus* and *Coelophysis*. He was a founder of Neo-Lamarckism, arguing that traits acquired during an organism's lifetime could be inherited—a view dominant in paleontology at the time but later disproven. His evolutionary theory emphasized "use and disuse" and a life force directing organisms toward higher consciousness. Cope also held racist views, considering Europeans a more "evolved" race and opposing women's suffrage.

### Later Years and Legacy
Financially ruined by failed mining investments in the 1880s, Cope sold his collections and took a teaching position at the University of Pennsylvania. He continued publishing prolifically until his death from a gastrointestinal illness in 1897. His will donated his body to science; his skull and brain were preserved for study, a final challenge to Marsh. Cope's legacy is complex: he advanced paleontology through sheer output and discovery, but his rapid publications contained errors, and his scientific views and personal beliefs are now widely criticized.

## Terms
- **Neo-Lamarckism**: A 19th-century evolutionary theory, championed by Cope, stating that organisms can pass on characteristics acquired during their lifetime to offspring.
- **Bone Wars**: The intense, often unscrupulous, fossil-finding rivalry between Edward Drinker Cope and Othniel Charles Marsh in the late 19th century.
- **Use and Disuse**: A key Neo-Lamarckian principle where anatomical parts strengthened through use are passed to offspring, while unused parts atrophy.
- **Marl pits**: Excavation sites, like those in New Jersey, rich in Cretaceous-era fossils, crucial to early American paleontology.
- **U.S. Geological Survey (USGS)**: A federal agency formed in 1879 that consolidated government scientific surveys, later becoming central to the Bone Wars conflict.
- **American Naturalist**: A scientific journal co-owned by Cope, which he used to publish his work rapidly during the Bone Wars.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Cause of Death**: Officially listed as uremic poisoning, but contemporaries suspected syphilis. A 1995 skeletal examination found no evidence of bony syphilis, leaving the true cause uncertain.
- **Accuracy of Work**: Cope's rapid publication rate led to numerous errors and retracted names, sparking debates about the trade-off between speed and scientific rigor.
- **Racial Views**: Cope's writings on racial hierarchy, which he framed as scientific, are now condemned as racist and have led to the renaming of the journal *Copeia*.
- **Religious Conflict**: Scholars debate the extent of Cope's internal conflict between his Quaker upbringing and his scientific career, with some suggesting he downplayed doubts to maintain family support.

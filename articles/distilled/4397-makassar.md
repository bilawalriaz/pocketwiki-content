# Makassar

## Overview
Makassar is the capital of South Sulawesi province and the largest city in Eastern Indonesia. Historically a major international trading port, its strategic location made it a prize for rival sultanates, European colonial powers, and a focal point in the formation of modern Indonesia.

## Timeline
- **14th century** — Mentioned in the Javanese text *Nagarakretagama* as under Majapahit influence.
- **1512–1546** — King Tumaparisi Kallonna of Gowa allies with Tallo, establishing a dual kingdom centered at Sombaopu.
- **1603** — Sultan Alaud-din of Gowa accepts Islam, marking a major political and religious shift.
- **1607** — First official Friday Prayer held in the city, now celebrated as its anniversary.
- **1667–1669** — The Dutch East India Company (VOC) conquers Makassar with Bugis allies, destroying Sombaopu and rebuilding Fort Rotterdam.
- **1942** — Japanese forces land near Makassar during World War II.
- **1946** — Makassar becomes the capital of the State of East Indonesia, a Dutch-backed entity.
- **1950** — The Makassar Uprising leads to the integration of the State of East Indonesia into the Republic of Indonesia.
- **1971** — City renamed Ujung Pandang.
- **1999** — Official name reverts to Makassar.

## Body

### Pre-Colonial and Sultanate Era
Makassar's history is tied to the spice trade. The 9th King of Gowa, Tumaparisi Kallonna (1512–1546), first allied with the trade-focused polity of Tallo, creating a powerful dual kingdom centered at Sombaopu. This port attracted Malay traders expelled from Malacca and, from the 1540s, Portuguese sailors. The kingdom's policy of free trade made it a vital alternative port as Dutch power grew. Under leaders like Chancellor Karaeng Matoaya (c. 1573–1636) and his son Karaeng Pattingalloang (1600–54), Makassar resisted Dutch pressure and maintained open commerce with English, Danish, Chinese, and other traders. The conversion of the Gowa royalty to Islam in 1603 was a pivotal event.

### Dutch Colonial Period
After Pattingalloang's death in 1654, internal conflicts and a Bugis rebellion allowed the VOC to conquer the city. The 1667–69 wars resulted in the destruction of the old capital Sombaopu and the VOC rebuilding the northern fort of Ujung Pandang as Fort Rotterdam. The Dutch moved the city center northward and managed the region from this base. Makassar became a key collecting point for Eastern Indonesian produce like copra, rattan, pearls, and trepang (sea cucumber). The Dutch did not fully control the southern interior until the early 20th century.

### Post-Independence
After Indonesian independence in 1945, Makassar briefly served as the capital of the Dutch-backed State of East Indonesia (1946–1950). The 1950 Makassar Uprising, fought between pro-Federalist and Republican forces, resulted in the state's integration into the Republic of Indonesia. The city was renamed Ujung Pandang in 1971 to de-emphasize ethnic connotations but reverted to Makassar in 1999 due to local unpopularity of the new name.

### Economy and Connection with Australia
Makassar is a primary port for Eastern Indonesia, famous for the traditional *pinisi* sailing ships. Its economy is now dominated by the service sector (70%). Historically, it was known for exporting "Makassar oil" and "Makassar ebony." A significant historical industry was the trepang trade. From around 1700, Makassan sailors voyaged to northern Australia (Arnhem Land and the Kimberley) to fish for trepang, establishing contact with Indigenous Australians long before European settlement. This trade continued until the late 19th century.

## Terms
*   **Gowa-Tallo:** A dual kingdom that dominated the Makassar region from the 16th century until the Dutch conquest.
*   **VOC (Vereenigde Oostindische Compagnie):** The Dutch East India Company, a colonial trading company that conquered Makassar in the 1660s.
*   **Fort Rotterdam:** The Dutch fortress rebuilt on the site of the captured Ujung Pandang fort, which became the center of colonial Makassar.
*   **Trepang:** Sea cucumber, a key export from Makassar that drove trade with China and led to voyages to northern Australia.
*   **Pinisi:** A traditional Indonesian two-masted sailing ship, historically used for long-distance trade from Makassar.
*   **Makassarese:** The native ethnic group and language of the city.
*   **Lontara:** The traditional script used to write the Makassarese and Bugis languages.
*   **Pete-pete:** A modified share taxi used for public transportation in Makassar.

# First Indochina War

## Overview
The First Indochina War (1946–1954) was a conflict between France and its colonial allies against the Viet Minh and their communist backers for control of Vietnam. It marked the end of French colonial rule in Indochina and set the stage for the Vietnam War, becoming a key early conflict of the Cold War.

## Timeline
- **2 September 1945** — Ho Chi Minh declares the Democratic Republic of Vietnam (DRV).
- **23 September 1945** — French forces overthrow the DRV government in Saigon.
- **19 December 1946** — All-out war erupts between France and the Viet Minh.
- **January 1950** — China and the Soviet Union recognize the DRV; the U.S. recognizes the State of Vietnam.
- **September 1951** — U.S. begins direct economic aid to the State of Vietnam.
- **October 1952** — Viet Minh overruns much of the Black River valley.
- **November 1953** — French paratroopers seize Điện Biên Phủ.
- **13 March – 7 May 1954** — Battle of Điện Biên Phu ends in decisive French defeat.
- **21 July 1954** — Geneva Accords signed, partitioning Vietnam at the 17th parallel.
- **11 August 1954** — Final ceasefire takes effect.

## Body
### Origins and Outbreak
Following Japan's surrender in 1945, the Viet Minh, led by Ho Chi Minh, declared Vietnamese independence. French forces reasserted control in the south, leading to guerrilla warfare. After failed negotiations, full-scale war began in December 1946. France sought to maintain control through a political solution, creating the State of Vietnam under former Emperor Bảo Đại in 1949 as an associated state within the French Union.

### Internationalization and Stalemate
The war became internationalized in 1950 when the communist powers recognized the DRV and the Western powers recognized the State of Vietnam. The United States began funding the French war effort, viewing it through the lens of Cold War containment. The French initially succeeded with defensive tactics but were hampered by limited manpower and reliance on colonial troops. The Viet Minh, reorganized into conventional divisions and supplied by China and the Soviet Union, gained strength.

### Decisive Battles and French Defeat
After a period of stalemate, the French attempted to lure the Viet Minh into a conventional battle at Điện Biên Phủ. The Viet Minh, under General Võ Nguyên Giáp, besieged the French garrison for 57 days, using artillery and overwhelming numbers. The French defeat in May 1954 was decisive, leading directly to the Geneva Conference.

### Aftermath and Partition
The Geneva Accords temporarily divided Vietnam at the 17th parallel, with the Viet Minh controlling the North. Planned nationwide elections for reunification in 1956 never occurred. The United States and the State of Vietnam did not sign the accords. The conflict evolved into the Vietnam War as an insurgency, supported by the North, developed in the South.

## Terms
- **Viet Minh**: League for the Independence of Vietnam, a communist-led nationalist front founded by Ho Chi Minh in 1941.
- **French Union**: A political entity created in 1946 that replaced the French colonial empire, comprising France and its associated states.
- **State of Vietnam**: A non-communist Vietnamese state created by France in 1949 under Emperor Bảo Đại to counter the Viet Minh.
- **De Lattre Line**: A defensive line of fortifications around the Red River Delta built by the French in 1950–1951.
- **Hedgehog tactics**: A French strategy of establishing well-defended outposts to force the Viet Minh into conventional battles.
- **Geneva Accords**: The 1954 agreements that ended the war, partitioned Vietnam, and called for future reunification elections.
- **Domino theory**: The U.S. policy belief that the fall of one nation to communism would lead to the fall of neighboring nations.

## Debates and Open questions
- The war's human cost is debated, with soldier death estimates ranging from 400,000 to 842,707 and civilian deaths from 125,000 to 400,000.
- The extent of U.S. involvement is a point of historical interest, particularly the discussion of direct military intervention at Điện Biên Phủ, which was considered but rejected.
- The failure to hold the promised 1956 reunification elections remains a central issue in understanding the origins of the subsequent Vietnam War.

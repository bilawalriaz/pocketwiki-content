# Kharkiv

## Overview
Kharkiv is Ukraine's second-largest city and the historical capital of the Sloboda Ukraine region. Founded in 1654 as a Cossack fortress, it became a major industrial, scientific, and cultural center within the Russian Empire and the Soviet Union, serving as the capital of the Ukrainian SSR from 1919 to 1934. Its strategic location and industrial base have made it a focal point of conflict, particularly during the 2022 Russian invasion, which has caused extensive destruction.

## Timeline

- **1805** — Kharkiv University (now V.N. Karazin Kharkiv National University) is established.
- **Dec 1919 – Jan 1934** — Serves as the capital of the Ukrainian Soviet Socialist Republic.
- **1925–1928** — The constructivist Derzhprom (Palace of Industry) is built in Freedom Square.
- **Oct 1941** — Captured by Nazi Germany during World War II.
- **Aug 1943** — Liberated by the Red Army; 70% of the city is destroyed.
- **1 Dec 1991** — 86% of Kharkiv Oblast voters approve Ukraine's independence in a referendum.
- **Apr 2014** — Pro-Russian separatists briefly seize the regional administration building.
- **Mar 2022** — Designated a "Hero City of Ukraine" for its resistance during the Russian invasion.
- **May 2024** — Ukrainian forces halt a renewed Russian advance toward the city.

## Body

### Foundation and Imperial Growth
The settlement was established in 1654 by Cossack settlers and came under the authority of the Tsardom of Russia. It developed as a fortress and market town within the Sloboda Ukraine Governorate. In the 19th century, Kharkiv became a center of Ukrainian cultural and national awakening, hosting the first Ukrainian newspaper (1812) and secret societies (*hromadas*) that laid the groundwork for a national movement. The city's first university was founded in 1805.

### Soviet Capital and Industrialization
After the Russian Revolution, Bolsheviks established Kharkiv as the capital of the Ukrainian SSR in 1919. The 1920s–30s saw massive industrialization under Stalin's Five-Year Plans, with flagship projects like the Kharkiv Tractor Factory (HTZ) and the Malyshev Factory. This period also featured significant Constructivist architecture, such as the Derzhprom building. The city's ethnic composition shifted as Ukrainian peasants migrated from the countryside, though Russian remained the dominant spoken language. The capital was moved to Kyiv in 1934.

### World War II and Post-War Reconstruction
Kharkiv was the site of four major battles during WWII, changing hands between Soviet and German forces. The Nazi occupation (1941–1943) resulted in the massacre of an estimated 15,000 Jews at Drobytsky Yar and the deaths of tens of thousands of other residents. By liberation in 1943, the population had plummeted and the city was largely destroyed. Post-war reconstruction followed Stalinist Classicism styles, and the city's tank design industries, evacuated during the war, returned to become central to the Soviet military-industrial complex.

### Post-Soviet Independence and 2014 Unrest
Following Ukrainian independence in 1991, Kharkiv's economy struggled with the collapse of Soviet supply chains. The city maintained strong cultural and economic ties to Russia, supporting pro-Russian political parties like the Party of Regions. After the 2014 Euromaidan revolution, pro-Russian separatists attempted to seize government buildings, but their movement failed to gain widespread local support. The conflict in neighboring Donbas turned Kharkiv into a "border fortress," disrupting its trade and economy.

### 2022 Russian Invasion
Kharkiv was a primary target of the 2022 invasion. Russian forces advanced to the city's outskirts but were repelled by Ukrainian defenders. The city has since endured relentless shelling and missile strikes. By April 2024, reports indicated almost a quarter of the city was damaged or destroyed, including major historical buildings and industrial sites like the Kharkiv Tractor Plant. The conflict has caused significant civilian casualties and displacement.

## Terms
- **Sloboda Ukraine (Slobozhanshchyna):** A historical region in northeastern Ukraine where Cossack communities enjoyed special freedoms (*slobody*) from serfdom.
- **Hromada:** A secret community or society; in the 19th century, these were networks of Ukrainian intellectuals that fostered national consciousness.
- **Constructivism:** An architectural style (1920s–30s) emphasizing geometric forms and industrial materials, prominent in Soviet Kharkiv.
- **Holodomor:** The man-made famine in Soviet Ukraine (1932–33) caused by forced collectivization and grain seizures, which drove starving peasants into cities like Kharkiv.
- **Ostarbeiter:** Forced laborers from occupied Eastern European territories deported to work in Nazi Germany during WWII.
- **Derzhprom:** The "Palace of Industry," a landmark Constructivist building completed in 1928 in Kharkiv's Freedom Square.
- **Hero City of Ukraine:** An honorary title awarded to cities for outstanding heroism during wartime, given to Kharkiv in 2022.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The origin of the city's name is debated; a folk etymology links it to a legendary Cossack founder named Kharko, but the river name predates the fortress.
- The extent of local support for the 2014 pro-Russian separatist movement remains contested, with evidence suggesting it lacked broad popular backing.
- The long-term demographic and cultural impact of the 2022 invasion, including shifts in language use and the preservation of the city's architectural heritage, is an ongoing concern.

# Isabella I of Castile

## Overview
Isabella I of Castile (1451–1504) was a queen whose reign, alongside her husband Ferdinand II of Arragon, unified Spain, completed the Reconquista, initiated the Spanish Inquisition, and sponsored Christopher Columbus's voyage to the New World. Her rule marked the end of Muslim rule in Iberia and the beginning of the Spanish Empire, fundamentally shaping European and global history.

## Timeline
- **22 April 1451** — Born in Madrigal de las Altas Torres to King John II of Castile.
- **1468** — Named heir-presumptive of Castile by her half-brother Henry IV after the death of her brother Alfonso.
- **19 October 1469** — Secretly marries Ferdinand of Aragon in Valladolid.
- **11 December 1474** — Henry IV dies; Isabella is proclaimed Queen of Castile.
- **1 March 1476** — Battle of Toro against Portugal; outcome is indecisive but secures Isabella's throne politically.
- **4 September 1479** — Treaty of Alcáçovas ends war with Portugal, confirming Isabella as Queen of Castile.
- **1 February 1482** — Beginning of the Granada War against the Nasrid Emirate.
- **2 January 1492** — Isabella and Ferdinand enter Granada, ending the Reconquista.
- **31 March 1492** — Alhambra Decree issued, ordering the expulsion of Jews from Spain.
- **3 August 1492** — Christopher Columbus's first voyage departs, sponsored by Isabella.
- **1494** — Treaty of Tordesillas divides newly discovered lands with Portugal.
- **26 November 1504** — Isabella dies at Medina del Campo.

## Body

### Early Life and Path to the Throne
Isabella was born second in line to the Castilian throne. After her father's death in 1454, she and her brother Alfonso were neglected by their half-brother, King Henry IV. A civil war erupted in 1465 between Henry and nobles supporting Alfonso. After Alfonso's death in 1468, Isabella became the rebels' champion but negotiated a compromise, being named Henry's heir-presumptive in 1468 on the condition she not marry without his consent. She defied this by secretly marrying Ferdinand of Aragon in 1469, a union that formed the basis of a unified Spain. Henry repudiated the marriage and named his daughter Joanna as his heir, leading to a succession war upon his death in 1474.

### Securing the Crown and Reforms
Isabella's reign began with a war against Portugal, which backed the rival claimant Joanna la Beltraneja. The indecisive Battle of Toro (1476) was a political victory for Isabella, and the Treaty of Alcáçovas (1479) secured her throne. She then implemented major reforms. To combat rampant crime, she established *La Santa Hermandad* (the Holy Brotherhood) in 1476, a royal police force funded by a tax on households. To restore finances depleted by Henry IV, she reclaimed alienated royal estates and reformed the coinage system, shutting down many mints and establishing a royal monopoly. She also reformed the Royal Council, reducing the influence of hereditary nobles in favor of professional, legally trained administrators (*letrados*).

### The Year 1492 and Imperial Expansion
The year 1492 was pivotal. After a ten-year war, Isabella and Ferdinand conquered the Emirate of Granada, completing the *Reconquista*. The Alhambra Decree that same year ordered the mass expulsion of Jews who would not convert to Christianity. Isabella also sponsored Christopher Columbus's voyage west, which led to the discovery of the New World and the Treaty of Tordesillas (1494), dividing non-European lands with Portugal. She established policies for the treatment of indigenous peoples, generally opposing their enslavement but allowing exceptions. Her support for the first grammar of the Castilian language (1492) aided the spread of Spanish in the new territories.

### Later Years and Legacy
Isabella and Ferdinand pursued religious unification, institutionalizing the Spanish Inquisition and, after a Muslim uprising, ordering the conversion or expulsion of Muslims in 1502. They arranged strategic marriages for their children to link Spain with other European powers, though personal tragedies followed. Isabella died in 1504, having transformed Castile into the core of a global empire. Her reign established Spain as a major European power and initiated the Spanish Golden Age.

## Terms
- **Reconquista**: The centuries-long period of Christian reconquest of the Iberian Peninsula from Muslim rule, completed in 1492.
- **Catholic Monarchs**: The joint title given to Isabella I and Ferdinand II, reflecting their unified rule and religious policies.
- **La Santa Hermandad**: A royal police force established by Isabella in 1476 to regulate crime and enforce law and order.
- **Alhambra Decree**: The 1492 edict ordering the expulsion of Jews from Spain who refused to convert to Christianity.
- **Inquisition**: A judicial institution established to maintain Catholic orthodoxy, targeting conversos (converted Jews and Muslims) and heretics.
- **Treaty of Tordesillas**: A 1494 agreement between Spain and Portugal dividing newly discovered lands outside Europe.
- **Letrados**: Legally trained professional administrators who became central to Isabella's reformed government.
- **Emirate of Granada**: The last Muslim-ruled state in the Iberian Peninsula, conquered by the Catholic Monarchs in 1492.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The true number of Jews expelled in 1492 is debated; traditional estimates of 200,000 are considered exaggerated by modern historians like Henry Kamen, who suggests a maximum of 40,000.
- Isabella's personal role in the Spanish Inquisition and the expulsion of Jews is complex; sources note she initially opposed harsh measures on economic grounds but was ultimately convinced by figures like Tomás de Torquemada.
- Her sainthood cause, opened in 1958 and granted the title "Servant of God" in 1974, was suspended in 1991, partly due to her role in the expulsion of the Jews.

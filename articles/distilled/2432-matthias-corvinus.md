# Matthias Corvinus

## Overview
Matthias Corvinus (1443–1490) was King of Hungary and Croatia from 1458 to 1490, elected by the nobility at age 14. He is renowned for establishing one of Europe's first professional standing armies, promoting Renaissance culture, and waging extensive wars to expand his realm into Bohemia and Austria. His reign marked a peak of Hungarian power and cultural achievement, though his conquests were lost shortly after his death.

## Body

### Early Life and Election
Matthias was the son of John Hunyadi, a leading Hungarian regent. After his father's death in 1456, Matthias and his brother Ladislaus were imprisoned by King Ladislaus the Posthumous. Ladislaus was executed, sparking a rebellion. Following the king's sudden death in 1457, Matthias was held captive in Bohemia. The Hungarian Diet, seeking to avoid civil war, unanimously elected the 14-year-old Matthias king on 24 January 1458, with his uncle Michael Szilágyi as regent. Matthias took effective control within weeks.

### Consolidation and Early Wars
Matthias's early reign focused on consolidating power and securing the crown. He fought Czech mercenaries in Upper Hungary and defeated a baronial rebellion in 1459. A peace treaty with Emperor Frederick III in 1463 secured the return of the Holy Crown. After brief anti-Ottoman campaigns in Bosnia, he realized Christian powers would provide little aid and tacitly abandoned that policy. He introduced new taxes and centralized revenue collection, which provoked a revolt in Transylvania in 1467 that he brutally suppressed.

### War for the Bohemian Crown
In 1468, Matthias declared war on George of Poděbrady, the Hussite King of Bohemia, allied with Catholic lords and the Pope. He conquered Moravia, Silesia, and Lusatia, and was elected King of Bohemia by Catholic Estates in 1469. After George's death in 1471, a succession struggle with Vladislaus Jagiellon (elected by Hussite lords) continued. A conspiracy by Hungarian nobles, including Archbishop John Vitéz, offered the throne to Casimir Jagiellon, but Matthias crushed the rebellion. He defeated a combined Polish-Bohemian army at Breslau in 1474. A 1478 peace treaty divided the Bohemian Crown: Vladislaus ruled Bohemia proper, while Matthias held Moravia, Silesia, and Lusatia.

### War with the Ottomans and Austria
Matthian supported Stephen the Great of Moldavia against Ottoman invasions in the 1470s, leading to victories like the Battle of Vaslui (1475). He captured the Ottoman fortress of Šabac in 1476. After a truce with the Ottomans, he turned against Emperor Frederick III, occupying Lower Austria between 1482 and 1487. He captured Vienna in 1485 and assumed the title Duke of Austria in 1487. He died in 1490 while negotiating to be named Frederick's heir.

### Administration and Culture
Matthias established the "Black Army," a professional standing army funded by extraordinary taxes. He reformed justice by creating a supreme court and reducing baronial power, promoting officials based on ability. A great patron of Renaissance humanism, he attracted scholars to Buda and amassed the Bibliotheca Corviniana, one of Europe's largest libraries. His court fostered Italian art, architecture, and music, making Hungary a center of Renaissance culture outside Italy.

## Terms
- **Black Army**: The professional standing army established by Matthias Corvinus, one of the earliest in medieval Europe.
- **Bibliotheca Corviniana**: The royal library of Matthias Corvinus, one of the largest collections of books in 15th-century Europe.
- **Decretum maius**: A systematic law code passed in 1485 that reformed the administration of justice and defined the Palatine's role as regent.
- **Extraordinary tax**: A special levy, often one florin per peasant household (*porta*), imposed by the king with Diet consent for specific needs like war.
- **Hussites**: Followers of the religious reformer Jan Hus, considered heretics by the Catholic Church; George of Poděbrady was their king.
- **Lands of the Bohemian Crown**: The territories of Bohemia, Moravia, Silesia, and Lusatia, which were divided between Matthias and Vladislaus Jagiellon in 1478.
- **Palatine**: The highest-ranking official in Hungary after the king, who served as chief judge and, in the king's absence, as regent.
- **Renaissance humanism**: A cultural movement emphasizing classical learning and human potential, which Matthias promoted at his court.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The cause of Matthias's death is debated; while a stroke is suggested, poisoning has not been excluded.
- The extent of Josquin des Prez's presence and work at Matthias's court is uncertain, based on a single late remark.
- The long-term impact of Matthias's high taxation is debated: it funded his achievements but burdened the peasantry and contributed to instability after his death.

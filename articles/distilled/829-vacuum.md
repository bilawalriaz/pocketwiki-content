# Vacuum

## Overview
A vacuum is a region of space devoid of matter, derived from the Latin *vacuus* ("empty"). In practice, physicists distinguish an ideal **perfect vacuum** (free space) from a **partial vacuum**, an imperfect region with pressure significantly below atmospheric. The quality of a vacuum is defined by how closely it approaches zero pressure; lower gas pressure indicates higher quality. Historically debated by Greek philosophers and medieval scholars (often under the concept *horror vacui*, nature's abhorrence of a vacuum), empirical study began in the 17th century with Evangelista Torricelli’s mercury barometer (1643) and Otto von Guericke’s vacuum pump (1654). Vacuum technology became industrially vital in the 20th century for light bulbs and vacuum tubes, and is now essential for semiconductor fabrication, space simulation, and fundamental physics. In quantum field theory, the vacuum is the ground state of the Hilbert space, possessing zero-point energy and **vacuum fluctuations** (transient virtual particles), making "empty" space physically active.

## Body

### Historical Understanding
Ancient Greek atomists posited void as a fundamental element, but Aristotle argued a natural void was impossible because denser surrounding matter would instantly fill any rarity; he also claimed motion in a void would continue *ad infinitum* without a stopping point. Plato and later René Descartes treated void as a geometric extension rather than "nothingness." Medieval Islamic scholars like Al-Farabi (10th c.) rejected a perfect vacuum as incoherent, though Al-Bīrūnī noted no evidence ruled it out. The suction pump appeared in the 13th century (Al-Jazari). European scholars debated *horror vacui*; the 1277 Paris condemnations (Bishop Étienne Tempier) asserted God *could* create a vacuum, separating theological possibility from natural philosophy. By the 17th century, a supernatural void beyond the cosmos was widely accepted.

### Empirical Breakthroughs (17th–19th Century)
Evangelista Torricelli created the first laboratory vacuum in 1643 by inverting a mercury-filled tube into a bowl, leaving a **Torricellian vacuum** above the column. Blaise Pascal confirmed atmospheric pressure as the cause. In 1654, Otto von Guericke invented the first vacuum pump, famously demonstrating that teams of horses could not pull apart evacuated Magdeburg hemispheres due to external atmospheric pressure. Robert Boyle and Robert Hooke improved pump designs. Research lapsed until August Toepler (1850) and Heinrich Geissler (1855) developed pumps reaching ~10 Pa (0.1 Torr), revealing observable electrical properties (e.g., cathode rays) and renewing scientific interest. The concept of **luminiferous aether**—a rigid medium filling space for light propagation—persisted until early 20th-century experiments failed to detect "aether drag," leading to its abandonment.

### Classical Field Theories
In classical physics, the strictest definition of vacuum is a region where the **stress–energy tensor** is zero (no energy, momentum, particles, or fields).
*   **Gravity (General Relativity):** A vanishing stress–energy tensor implies a vanishing **Ricci tensor** via Einstein’s equations. However, spacetime curvature need not be flat; the **Weyl tensor** allows tidal forces and gravitational waves in vacuum. A black hole (zero charge) is a region "filled" with vacuum yet exhibiting strong curvature.
*   **Electromagnetism:** The **vacuum of free space** (classical vacuum) is a reference medium with defined constants: speed of light *c* = 299,792,458 m/s; permittivity *ε₀*; permeability *μ₀*; impedance *Z₀* ≈ 376.73 Ω. The superposition principle holds exactly. This is distinguished from **QED/QCD vacuum** where quantum fluctuations alter permittivity/permeability.

### Quantum Mechanics
In quantum field theory, the vacuum is the **ground state of the Hilbert space** (lowest energy state).
*   **QED Vacuum:** Contains no matter particles or photons, yet exhibits **vacuum fluctuations** (virtual particle-antiparticle pairs popping in/out of existence) and non-zero **vacuum energy**. Electric/magnetic fields have zero mean but non-zero variance. Verified effects: **spontaneous emission**, **Lamb shift** (energy level shift in hydrogen), and modification of Coulomb’s law near charges.
*   **QCD Vacuum:** Multiple vacuum states can coexist; transitions between them may drive cosmological inflation. String theory predicts a vast "landscape" of vacua.
*   **Heisenberg Uncertainty Principle (1927):** Implies fundamental limits on measuring energy and time, challenging the classical notion of static emptiness.

### Outer Space
Outer space is the closest physical approximation to a perfect vacuum but is not perfect. Intergalactic space averages a few hydrogen atoms per cubic meter. Atmospheres have no sharp boundary; density decreases with altitude. The **Kármán line** (100 km / 62 mi) marks where atmospheric pressure (~32 mPa) becomes negligible compared to solar radiation pressure and solar wind dynamic pressure. Satellites in **low Earth orbit** (below ~few hundred km) experience enough atmospheric drag to require periodic engine firings. The observable universe is filled with **cosmic microwave background radiation** (~3 K) and likely neutrinos.

### Measurement
Vacuum quality is primarily measured by **absolute pressure** (Pascal, Torr; 1 Torr = 133.322 Pa). Key parameter: **Mean Free Path (MFP)**—average distance between molecular collisions. At atmospheric pressure, MFP ≈ 70 nm; at 100 mPa (~10⁻³ Torr), MFP ≈ 100 mm. When MFP exceeds chamber dimensions, **high vacuum** (particle gas dynamics) begins.
*   **ISO 3529-1 Ranges:** Low vacuum (atmosphere to 100 Pa), Medium (100 Pa to 10⁻¹ Pa), High (10⁻¹ Pa to 10⁻⁶ Pa), Ultra-high (below 10⁻⁶ Pa).
*   **Instruments:** *Hydrostatic gauges* (mercury manometer, McLeod gauge—direct measurement to 10⁻⁶ Torr); *Mechanical/elastic gauges* (Bourdon tube, capacitance manometer—10³ to 10⁻⁴ Torr); *Thermal conductivity gauges* (Pirani gauge—10 to 10⁻³ Torr, gas-composition sensitive); *Ionization gauges* (hot cathode: 10⁻³ to 10⁻¹⁰ Torr; cold cathode: 10⁻² to 10⁻⁹ Torr, require mass spectrometer for gas ID).
*   **Relative vs. Absolute:** Gauges typically measure differential pressure relative to local ambient (1 atm on Earth). On Venus, higher relative vacuum is possible; on the Moon, nearly impossible.

### Pumping and Ambient Air Pressure
A vacuum cannot be "pulled"; it is created by expanding a volume to lower pressure, allowing higher external pressure to push fluid in. **Positive displacement pumps** (e.g., manual water pump) cyclically expand a sealed cavity, seal it from the chamber, exhaust to atmosphere, and compress. **Momentum transfer pumps** (turbomolecular, diffusion) achieve higher vacuums by imparting momentum to gas molecules. **Entrapment pumps** (cryopumps, ion pumps) capture gases in solid/adsorbed states, often with no moving parts. No pump is universal; all struggle with low molecular weight gases (H₂, He, Ne). Ultimate pressure depends on **vacuum technique**: seals, materials (outgassing), chamber geometry, pump stages, and procedures. Ultra-high vacuum (UHV) requires baking chambers to drive off absorbed water (main outgassing source), using metal seals, and considering metal permeability/grain direction. Lowest lab pressures: ~1×10⁻¹³ Torr (13 pPa); cryogenic systems indirectly measured ~5×10⁻¹⁷ Torr (6.7 fPa, ≈100 particles/cm³).

### Outgassing
**Outgassing** is evaporation/sublimation of materials into vacuum. All solids/liquids have vapor pressure; outgassing limits achievable vacuum and acts like a leak. Products may condense on cold surfaces, obscuring optics or reacting chemically—critical for space missions. Primary contaminant: water absorbed in chamber walls. Mitigation: baking (heating under vacuum to boil off volatiles), desiccating, removing organics, cooling (liquid nitrogen) to cryopump residuals.

### Uses
*   **Chemical Inertness:** Incandescent bulbs (filament protection), electron-beam welding, cold welding, vacuum packing/frying.
*   **Surface Science/Semiconductors:** Ultra-high vacuum preserves atomically clean surfaces (minutes to days). High/Ultra-high vacuum enables **chemical vapor deposition (CVD)**, **physical vapor deposition (PVD)**, and **dry etching** for semiconductors/optical coatings.
*   **Thermal/Phase Change:** Reduced convection insulates thermos bottles. Lowered boiling points enable freeze drying, distillation, metallurgy, process purging.
*   **Electrical Properties:** Electron microscopes, vacuum tubes (cathode-ray tubes), vacuum interrupters (switchgear).
*   **Mechanical:** Flywheel energy storage, ultracentrifuges (eliminate air friction). Vacuum arc melting for high-purity steel.

### Vacuum-Driven Machines
Suction (atmospheric pressure pushing into vacuum) drives diverse machines:
*   **Newcomen steam engine:** Used vacuum (condensing steam) to drive piston.
*   **Atmospheric railway (19th c., Brunel):** Vacuum traction.
*   **Train brakes:** Vacuum brakes (UK heritage lines) replaced by air brakes.
*   **Automobiles:** Manifold vacuum powers brake servo (power brakes); obsolete: vacuum wipers, Autovac fuel pumps.
*   **Aircraft:** Attitude/Heading Indicators vacuum-driven for redundancy against electrical failure.
*   **Steam turbines:** Condenser vacuum (5–15 kPa absolute) maintained by steam jet ejectors or liquid ring pumps for efficiency.
*   **Vacuum induction melting:** Electromagnetic induction in vacuum.

### Effects on Humans and Animals
Exposure causes loss of consciousness in seconds, death by hypoxia in minutes. Blood does **not boil** at 37 °C because vascular pressure keeps boiling point above body temperature. However, **ebullism** (gas bubble formation in fluids) bloats body to ~2x size; tissues prevent rupture. **Crew Altitude Protection Suit (CAPS)** prevents ebullism down to 2 kPa (15 Torr). Rapid boiling cools skin (frost in mouth), not a major hazard.
*   **Recovery:** Animal data (chimpanzees, NASA) shows full recovery normal for <90 sec exposure; longer full-body exposure fatal. Human accident data consistent.
*   **Decompression Sickness:** Spacesuits use ~20 kPa (150 Torr) pure O₂ to prevent ebullism; decompression sickness/gas embolisms possible if decompression rates unmanaged.
*   **Rapid Decompression (Barotrauma):** Far more dangerous than vacuum itself. Alveoli rupture if venting too slow; eardrums/sinuses rupture; tissues bruise/bleed; shock accelerates hypoxia. A 13 kPa (100 Torr) drop is harmless if gradual, fatal if sudden.
*   **Extremophiles:** Tardigrades survive vacuum for days/weeks. Plants survive ~1.5 kPa for ~30 min. Cold/O₂-rich environments sustain life at low pressure if O₂ density matches sea level.

## Terms
- ****Perfect Vacuum**** — Ideal state with zero particles, zero pressure, and zero fields; unachievable in practice.
- ****Partial Vacuum**** — Real-world region with pressure significantly below atmospheric; quality increases as pressure decreases.
- ****Torricellian Vacuum**** — Vacuum formed above a mercury column in an inverted tube sealed at one end (first lab vacuum, 1643).
- ****Horror Vacui**** — Aristotelian/medieval principle that "nature abhors a vacuum," implying voids cannot exist naturally.
- ****Mean Free Path (MFP)**** — Average distance a gas molecule travels before colliding with another; increases as pressure drops.
- ****Outgassing**** — Release of trapped gases (vapor/sublimation) from materials into a vacuum; limits ultimate pressure and contaminates surfaces.
- ****QED Vacuum**** — Quantum electrodynamics ground state: no real particles/photons, but contains vacuum fluctuations (virtual pairs) and zero-point energy.
- ****Vacuum Fluctuations**** — Temporary appearance of virtual particle-antiparticle pairs in quantum vacuum due to Heisenberg uncertainty (ΔE·Δt ≥ ħ/2).
- ****Lamb Shift**** — Small difference in energy levels of hydrogen atoms caused by interaction with vacuum fluctuations; key experimental proof of QED.
- ****Ebullism**** — Formation of gas bubbles in bodily fluids due to reduced ambient pressure; causes swelling but not rupture due to tissue elasticity.
- ****Kármán Line**** — Boundary at 100 km altitude where atmospheric pressure becomes negligible vs. solar radiation/wind pressure; common definition of space.
- ****Positive Displacement Pump**** — Vacuum pump that cyclically expands a sealed cavity to ingest gas, then exhausts it to atmosphere (e.g., rotary vane, diaphragm).
- ****Momentum Transfer Pump**** — Pump (e.g., turbomolecular, diffusion) that imparts momentum to gas molecules to direct them toward exhaust; achieves higher vacuum than displacement pumps.
- ****Entrapment Pump**** — Pump (e.g., cryopump, ion pump) that captures gas molecules in solid or adsorbed state; often no moving parts.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Nature of the Quantum Vacuum:** Whether the vacuum is a true "void" or a physical medium with energy (zero-point energy, fluctuations) remains a conceptual divide between classical intuition and quantum field theory.
*   **Cosmological Constant Problem:** Theoretical predictions of vacuum energy density from QFT exceed observed dark energy density by ~120 orders of magnitude; the largest discrepancy in physics.
*   **String Theory Landscape:** The vast number of possible vacuum states (vacua) in string theory raises questions about predictivity and the anthropic principle.
*   **QCD Vacuum Structure:** The exact nature of the QCD vacuum (condensates, instantons, topological structure) and its role in confinement and chiral symmetry breaking are active research areas.
*   **Vacuum Decay / Metastability:** Whether our current electroweak vacuum is stable, metastable, or unstable depends on precise Higgs/top quark masses; a metastable vacuum could tunnel to a lower-energy state, destroying the universe.
*   **Measurement Limits:** Defining and measuring pressure in extreme environments (e.g., near black holes, early universe) where thermodynamic equilibrium breaks down.

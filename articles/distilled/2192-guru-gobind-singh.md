# Guru Gobind Singh

## Overview
Guru Gobind Singh (1666–1708) was the tenth and final human Guru of Sikhism. A warrior, poet, and philosopher, he is most renowned for founding the Khalsa—a disciplined Sikh warrior community—and for finalizing the Guru Granth Sahib as the faith's eternal scripture. His leadership during a period of intense Mughal persecution and the martyrdom of his entire family cemented his legacy as a central figure in Sikh history.

## Timeline
- **22 Dec 1666** — Born Gobind Das in Patna, Bihar.
- **11 Nov 1675** — Father, Guru Tegh Bahadur, executed by Mughal Emperor Aurangzeb.
- **29 Mar 1676** — Formally becomes the 10th Sikh Guru.
- **1685** — Founds the town of Paonta Sahib.
- **1699** — Creates the Khalsa at Anandpur on Vaisakhi; introduces the Five Ks.
- **Dec 1704** — Elder sons Ajit Singh and Jujhar Singh killed at Battle of Chamkaur.
- **Dec 1704** — Younger sons Zorawar Singh and Fateh Singh executed by Mughals.
- **1705** — Last battle led by Guru, the Battle of Muktsar.
- **1707** — Supports Bahadur Shah I in the Mughal succession war.
- **7 Oct 1708** — Dies from an assassination wound at Nanded.

## Body

### Early Life and Succession
Guru Gobind Singh was the only son of the ninth Guru, Tegh Bahadur. After his father was executed in 1675 for refusing to convert to Islam, the nine-year-old Gobind Das was formally installed as the tenth Guru in 1676. He received an education in martial arts, languages, and scripture. He married three times and had four sons.

### Creation of the Khalsa
In 1699, Guru Gobind Singh gathered Sikhs at Anandpur and, through a dramatic ceremony, founded the Khalsa (literally, "Pure Ones"). He initiated the first five members (the Panj Pyare) with a new baptism ritual (khande ka pahul) and gave them the surname "Singh" (lion). He then had them initiate him, changing his own name to Gobind Singh. The Khalsa was a warrior community bound by a strict code of conduct and the Five Ks (articles of faith: uncut hair, wooden comb, steel bracelet, sword, and short breeches). This institutionalized equality across caste and gender and abolished the corrupt system of local community leaders (Masands). The Khalsa became the core of Sikh resistance against Mughal persecution.

### Scripture and Legacy
Guru Gobind Singh is credited with finalizing the Guru Granth Sahib, the primary Sikh scripture, and declaring it the eternal Guru after him. He also established the concept of Guru Panth (the community of Sikhs as a collective spiritual authority), though this is less emphasized today. He is the author of the Dasam Granth, a second scripture of disputed authority among Sikhs, which contains hymns, tales, and theological works. Parts of it, like the Jaap Sahib, are integral to Khalsa daily prayers and initiation.

### Wars and Martyrdom
Guru Gobind Singh fought 21 battles against the Mughal Empire and local hill chiefs, defending the Sikh faith and the principle of Dharamyudh (righteous war as a last resort). The most devastating period was the siege of Anandpur (1704). After accepting a Mughal promise of safe passage, his family was attacked. His mother and two younger sons were captured and executed. His two elder sons died fighting at Chamkaur. In 1708, he was assassinated by agents of the Mughal governor Wazir Khan. He wrote the Zafarnama, a defiant letter in Persian to Aurangzeb, condemning the emperor's injustice.

## Terms
- **Khalsa**: The community of initiated Sikhs, founded by Guru Gobind Singh, characterized by a distinct identity and code of conduct.
- **Five Ks (Panj Kakaar)**: The five articles of faith worn by Khalsa Sikhs: Kesh (uncut hair), Kangha (comb), Kara (bracelet), Kirpan (sword), Kacchera (breeches).
- **Guru Granth Sahib**: The central holy scripture of Sikhism, declared by Guru Gobind Singh as the eternal Guru.
- **Dasam Granth**: A scripture attributed to Guru Gobind Singh, containing his compositions; its authority is debated.
- **Dharamyudh**: A war fought as a last resort in defense of righteousness and justice.
- **Zafarnama**: A letter written by Guru Gobind Singh to Emperor Aurangzeb, indicting him for injustice.
- **Masand**: A local Sikh community leader; the system was abolished by Guru Gobind Singh due to corruption.
- **Guru Panth**: The concept of the Sikh community as a collective spiritual authority, established alongside the Guru Granth.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Birth Year**: While 1666 is commonly accepted, some scholars like Louis E. Fenech argue 1661 is more likely based on later life events.
- **Dasam Granth's Authority**: The text is considered sacred scripture by some Sikhs and of disputed or secondary authority by others.
- **Dohra Mahalla Dasvan**: A couplet in the Guru Granth Sahib is sometimes attributed to Guru Gobind Singh, but the SGPC does not include it in the standard published version, despite its presence in older manuscripts.
- **Factional Views**: Some groups, like the Namdhari sect, believe Guru Gobind Singh did not die in 1708 but lived on under an alias. The Radha Soami movement has also made claims linking him to their lineage, which are not widely accepted.

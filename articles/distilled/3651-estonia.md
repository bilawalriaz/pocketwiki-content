# Estonia

## Overview
Estonia is a Northern European country in the Baltic region, known for its advanced digital society, high level of development, and a history marked by centuries of foreign rule followed by a modern struggle for independence. It is a parliamentary republic and a member of the EU and NATO, with a population of nearly 1.4 million people. Its significance lies in its successful transition from Soviet occupation to a leading digital democracy and its role as a frontline state for Western institutions near Russia.

## Timeline
- **c. 9,000 BC** — Present-day Estonia inhabited since at least this time.
- **1208–1227** — Northern Crusades; Estonian pagan civilization conquered and Christianized.
- **1343–1345** — St. George's Night Uprising against the Teutonic Order is suppressed.
- **1558–1583** — Livonian War leads to the partition of Estonia between Sweden, Poland-Lithuania, and Denmark.
- **1710–1721** — Great Northern War results in the conquest of Estonia by the Russian Empire.
- **1850s–1860s** — The Estonian national awakening begins, fostering a distinct national identity.
- **24 February 1918** — Estonian Declaration of Independence issued.
- **2 February 1920** — Tartu Peace Treaty signed; Soviet Russia recognizes Estonia's independence.
- **16 June 1940** — Soviet Union occupies Estonia, leading to annexation in August 1940.
- **20 August 1991** — Estonia declares the restoration of independence following the "Singing Revolution."
- **29 March 2004** — Estonia joins NATO and the European Union.
- **1 January 2011** — Estonia adopts the euro, joining the Eurozone.

## Body

### Prehistory and Early History
The oldest known settlement in Estonia is the Pulli site, dating to the early 9th millennium BC. Prehistory saw a progression from Mesolithic hunter-gatherers (Kunda culture) to Neolithic farming (Narva and Corded Ware cultures). By the Iron Age, hillfort settlements emerged, and north Estonia became a trade hub. During the Viking Age, Estonian coastal areas, especially Saaremaa, were active in raiding and trade. The territory was divided into independent counties, with eight major ones by the 13th century.

### Foreign Rule and National Awakening
Estonia was one of the last pagan regions in Europe to be Christianized during the Northern Crusades (1208–1227). Following conquest, the territory was ruled successively by the Teutonic Order, Denmark, Poland, Sweden, and the Russian Empire. The Swedish era (16th–18th centuries) brought reforms and the establishment of the University of Tartu (1632), while Russian rule (from 1710) initially strengthened serfdom. A distinct Estonian national identity gained momentum during the 19th-century "Age of Awakening," fueled by cultural movements and the growth of an Estonian-speaking middle class.

### Independence, Occupation, and Restoration
Estonia declared independence in 1918 and fought a war of independence, securing sovereignty with the 1920 Tartu Peace Treaty. The interwar period was democratic until an authoritarian regime was established in 1934. In 1939, the Nazi-Soviet Pact assigned Estonia to the Soviet sphere of influence, leading to Soviet occupation in 1940. After a brief German occupation (1941–1944), the Soviet Union reoccupied Estonia, deporting tens of thousands and encouraging Russian settlement. Western nations never recognized the annexation, preserving Estonia's legal state continuity. The peaceful "Singing Revolution" (1988–1991) culminated in the restoration of independence in 1991.

### Government and Society
Estonia is a unitary parliamentary republic with a unicameral parliament (Riigikogu). It is a pioneer in e-government, offering most public services online and using internet voting. The population is highly educated but faces demographic challenges, including a significant Russian-speaking minority (about 24% of the population) concentrated in certain regions. The country is one of the least religious in the world, with a majority of the population claiming no religious affiliation.

### Economy and Technology
Estonia has a high-income, advanced economy that was among the fastest-growing in the EU after 2004. Key sectors include telecommunications, electronics, and services. Historically dependent on oil shale for energy, it is now expanding renewable sources like wind power. The country is renowned for its IT sector and startup culture, with a high number of startups per capita. It ranks highly for economic freedom, low corruption, and ease of doing business.

## Terms
*   **Finnic language family:** The language group to which the Estonian language belongs, related to Finnish.
*   **Northern Crusades:** A series of 13th-century military campaigns by German and Danish forces to Christianize and conquer pagan Baltic peoples.
*   **Teutonic Order:** A German medieval military order that ruled parts of Estonia after the Northern Crusades.
*   **Russification:** A policy of the Russian Empire (and later the USSR) to promote Russian language and culture, often suppressing local identities.
*   **Singing Revolution:** The peaceful movement (1988–1991) of mass singing demonstrations that led to the restoration of Estonian independence from the Soviet Union.
*   **E-government:** The use of digital technology to deliver public services, in which Estonia is a global leader.
*   **Oil shale:** A sedimentary rock containing kerogen, used as a primary domestic energy source in Estonia for electricity and heat.
*   **Runic songs (regilaulud):** The oldest form of Estonian folk songs, following a distinct poetic metre shared with other Baltic Finns.
*   **Kalevipoeg:** The Estonian national epic, published in 1862, which played a key role in the national awakening.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   The exact identity of the people referred to as "Aesti" by the Roman historian Tacitus around AD 98 is debated; they may have been Balts or all inhabitants of the eastern Baltic region.
*   The nature and extent of religious beliefs in pre-Christian Estonia are not well known, with deities like Tharapita known only from later chronicles.
*   The citizenship policy for the large Russian-speaking minority, a legacy of Soviet-era immigration, has been described as "discriminatory" in some international reports, though the government defends it as necessary for state continuity.
*   There is ongoing debate within Estonia about balancing the economic benefits of the forestry industry with environmental goals for biodiversity and carbon sequestration.

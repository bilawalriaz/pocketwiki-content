# Baku

## Overview
Baku is the capital and largest city of Azerbaijan, situated on the Caspian Sea's western coast. It is the largest city in the Caucasus region and the world's lowest-lying national capital, at 28 metres (92 ft) below sea level. As Azerbaijan's sole metropolis, it is the country's scientific, cultural, industrial, and economic heart, hosting about 25% of the nation's population.

## Timeline
- **1st century AD** — Roman campaigns reach the area; earliest written evidence (inscriptions) found nearby.
- **8th century AD** — Baku is the realm of the Shirvanshahs.
- **1191** — After an earthquake in Shamakhi, the Shirvanshah court moves to Baku.
- **1501** — Safavid king Ismail I captures Baku.
- **1813** — The Treaty of Gulistan cedes Baku to the Russian Empire.
- **1846** — The first oil well is drilled in the Bibi-Heybat suburb.
- **1918** — Baku becomes the capital of the newly independent Azerbaijan Democratic Republic.
- **1920** — The 11th Red Army invades, making Baku the capital of the Azerbaijan SSR.
- **2000** — The Old City (Icherisheher) is designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
- **2010s** — Baku hosts major international events, including Eurovision 2012 and the 2015 European Games.

## Body

### Etymology and Early History
The name "Baku" likely derives from the Old Persian word *Baga* ("God"). A popular 19th-century folk etymology linked it to *Bâd-kube* ("wind-pounded city"), reflecting its harsh winds. Archaeological evidence shows human settlement since the Stone Age. The city was a realm of the Shirvanshahs from the 8th century, frequently attacked by Khazars and Rus'. After a 1191 earthquake in Shamakhi, the Shirvanshah court relocated to Baku, spurring construction of major fortifications like the Maiden Tower.

### Safavid, Russian, and Oil Boom Eras
Baku became part of the Safavid dynasty in 1501 and remained under successive Iranian dynasties until ceded to Russia in 1813. The discovery and commercial exploitation of oil transformed the city. The first oil well was drilled in 1846, and by the early 20th century, Baku produced half the world's oil. This boom caused rapid population growth and attracted international investors like the Nobels and Rothschilds.

### 20th Century to Present
After a brief period as the capital of the Azerbaijan Democratic Republic (1918–1920), Baku was incorporated into the Soviet Union. It was a vital oil center during World War II, a primary target for Nazi Germany. Following the 1991 Soviet dissolution, Baku underwent major reconstruction and modernization. It has since become a venue for global events and a dynamic, growing city focused on sustainability.

## Terms
- **Primate city**: A city that is disproportionately larger and more influential than others in its country.
- **Raionlar**: Administrative districts; Baku is divided into 12.
- **Shirvanshahs**: A dynasty that ruled the area of present-day Azerbaijan from the 8th to the 16th centuries.
- **Safavid dynasty**: An Iranian dynasty that ruled from 1501 to 1736, establishing Shia Islam as the state religion.
- **Treaty of Gulistan (1813)**: The agreement that ended the first Russo-Persian War, ceding Baku and other territories to the Russian Empire.
- **Icherisheher**: The Old City or Walled City of Baku, a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
- **Mugham**: A traditional genre of Azerbaijani music, recognized by UNESCO as Intangible Cultural Heritage.
- **Khazri**: A cold, strong northern wind characteristic of Baku.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The exact etymology of "Baku" is unclear, with multiple competing theories.
- The historical identification of the 1st-century city of "Albana" or "Albanopolis," where St. Bartholomew is said to have preached, is debated, with some scholars associating it with Baku and others with locations in modern-day Armenia or Derbent.

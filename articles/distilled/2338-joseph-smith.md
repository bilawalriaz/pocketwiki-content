# Joseph Smith

## Overview
Joseph Smith Jr. (1805–1844) was an American religious leader who founded the Latter Day Saint movement, which now claims millions of adherents. He published the Book of Mormon in 1830 and organized the Church of Christ, claiming to restore the early Christian Church. His life was marked by charismatic leadership, rapid church growth, frequent conflict with non-Mormon neighbors, and controversial teachings including polygamy. His assassination in 1844 led to a succession crisis that split the movement into several denominations.

## Body

### Early Life and Visions (1805–1827)
Smith was born into a family facing financial hardship and engaged in folk magic practices like treasure seeking. In 1820, he reported a vision of God the Father and Jesus Christ. In 1823, he said an angel revealed the location of buried golden plates. After years of failed treasure retrievals, he claimed to have obtained the plates in 1827. He married Emma Hale in 1827.

### Founding the Church and Early Conflicts (1827–1838)
Smith dictated the Book of Mormon, published in 1830, using a seer stone. He organized the Church of Christ that same year. Following a revelation, he moved the church to Kirtland, Ohio, in 1831. He established a settlement in Independence, Missouri, designated as "Zion," but Mormons were violently expelled from Jackson County in 1833. The Kirtland Safety Society bank failed in 1837, causing financial ruin and defections. Smith fled to Missouri, where escalating conflict culminated in the 1838 Mormon War. Governor Lilburn Boggs issued an extermination order, and Smith was imprisoned on treason charges before escaping in 1839.

### Nauvoo and Final Years (1839–1844)
Smith founded Nauvoo, Illinois, which grew rapidly under a liberal city charter granting significant autonomy. He introduced new doctrines, including baptism for the dead and the temple endowment ceremony, which incorporated elements similar to Freemasonry. He secretly practiced and taught plural marriage (polygamy), causing internal dissent. In 1844, he launched a presidential campaign. After dissidents published the *Nauvoo Expositor* exposing his practices, Smith ordered the printing press destroyed. He was arrested for inciting a riot, then charged with treason. On June 27, 1844, a mob stormed his jail cell in Carthage, killing him and his brother Hyrum.

### Teachings and Legacy
Smith's teachings evolved from establishing a church to complex cosmology. He taught that God was an exalted man and that humans could achieve godhood (exaltation). He introduced a three-tiered heaven (degrees of glory) and the "New and Everlasting Covenant," which included celestial marriage and polygamy as essential for exaltation. He produced new scripture, including the Book of Mormon and the Book of Abraham. His death caused a succession crisis. Brigham Young led most followers to Utah, forming the LDS Church. Others followed different leaders, eventually forming the Community of Christ. Smith is revered as a prophet by Latter Day Saints but viewed as a charlatan by many non-Mormons.

## Terms
- **Latter Day Saint movement**: The religious tradition founded by Joseph Smith, encompassing several denominations.
- **Book of Mormon**: A sacred text published by Smith in 1830, described as a translation of ancient golden plates.
- **Polygamy (Plural Marriage)**: The practice of having more than one wife, taught secretly by Smith and later publicly practiced by the LDS Church until 1890.
- **Endowment**: A temple ceremony introduced by Smith containing symbolic rituals, later central to LDS temple worship.
- **Celestial Marriage**: A marriage ceremony performed in a temple with authority to bind families eternally, a key part of Smith's "New and Everlasting Covenant."
- **Exaltation**: The highest degree of salvation in LDS theology, allowing individuals to become gods.
- **Zion**: In Smith's teachings, a community of the righteous; initially designated as Independence, Missouri.
- **Danites**: A secret Mormon militia formed in 1838 to enforce church discipline and protect against enemies.
- **Nauvoo Expositor**: A newspaper published by dissenters in 1844 that exposed Smith's polygamy and theocratic ambitions; its destruction precipitated Smith's death.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Nature of Early Visions**: Scholars debate whether Smith's early accounts of angelic visitations were initially understood as dreams or physical events, and how his narratives evolved over time.
- **Book of Mormon Origins**: Theories range from it being a divinely translated ancient record to a 19th-century composition by Smith, drawing on sources like the Bible or *View of the Hebrews*.
- **Polygamy's Introduction**: The exact timing and manner in which Smith introduced polygamy are debated, with evidence suggesting private teachings began as early as 1831.
- **Succession Crisis**: Smith never clearly designated a successor, leading to multiple claimants and the permanent split of the movement.
- **Book of Abraham Authenticity**: Egyptologists have determined the papyri Smith used are from the Egyptian Book of Breathing, not writings of Abraham, sparking debate about the text's nature and translation.

# Ninja

## Overview
Ninja (or shinobi) were covert agents and mercenaries in pre-modern Japan, primarily active during the Sengoku period (1467–1615). Contrary to popular belief, their chief roles were espionage and sabotage, not assassination. They emerged from peasant-warrior communities in Iga and Kōka provinces, selling their services to feudal lords. Their influence waned after the establishment of the Tokugawa shogunate in the 17th century, though their legacy persists in popular culture.

## Timeline
- **c. 8th century** — The word *shinobi* first appears in the *Man'yōshū* poetry anthology.
- **6th century** — Prince Shōtoku reportedly employs the spy Otomono Sahito, a possible early ninja.
- **1162** — Daisuke Togakure, after losing his samurai status, meets Chinese warrior-monk Kain Doshi and helps formulate *ninjutsu* (the "ninja way" of guerrilla warfare).
- **15th century** — Spies begin to be specially trained; the term *shinobi* clearly identifies them as a secretive group.
- **1485–1581** — Iga and Kōka ninja are actively hired as mercenaries by feudal lords (*daimyō*).
- **1574** — The Kōka confederacy is forced into vassalage by Oda Nobunaga.
- **1581** — Oda Nobunaga invades and destroys the organized Iga ninja clans.
- **1560** — Tokugawa Ieyasu employs 80 Kōga ninja to raid an Imagawa clan outpost.
- **1600** — A ninja plants a flag on the gate during the Siege of Hataya.
- **1637–1638** — Kōga ninja are used for reconnaissance during the Shimabara Rebellion, the last major recorded use of ninja in warfare.
- **1676** — The *Bansenshūkai*, a comprehensive ninja manual, is compiled.
- **2017** — Mie University establishes the world's first research center for ninja studies.

## Body

### Origins and Social Status
Ninja were not a distinct social class but were often *jizamurai* (peasant-warriors) from the mountainous, remote provinces of Iga and Kōka. Their secretive nature stemmed from their role as mercenaries who traded service for money "without honor and glory," unlike the samurai code of *bushidō*. The first formal ninja schools (*ninjutsu ryū*) are traced to the 12th century, with the Togakureryu founded by descendants of Daisuke Togakure.

### The Iga and Kōka Confederacies
During the Sengoku period of civil war, the Iga and Kōka regions formed independent leagues (*ikki*). Their ninja were renowned for siege warfare (*shirotori*), including night attacks and ambushes. They were organized into guilds with a rank system: *jōnin* (leaders), *chūnin* (assistants), and *genin* (field agents). The confederacies were destroyed by Oda Nobunaga in the 1570s–1580s, but many survivors, including Hattori Hanzō, later served the Tokugawa shogunate.

### Roles and Tactics
The primary roles of ninja were espionage (*kanchō*), scouting (*teisatsu*), surprise attack (*kishu*), and agitation (*konran*). They used disguises, such as farmers or merchants, and specialized tools like *makibishi* (caltrops) and silent footwear. Sabotage, especially arson, was a key tactic. Manuals like the *Bansenshūkai* detailed 48 fighting techniques, from making noiseless sandals to using *kuji-kiri* (hand seals) to distract guard dogs.

### Decline and Legacy
After the Shimabara Rebellion (1637–1638), ninja faded from open warfare. Under the Tokugawa shogunate, they were employed as bodyguards, spies, and fire patrol, or transitioned into professions like medicine and security. Their image was later romanticized in Edo-period theatre, creating stereotypes like black costumes (modeled on stagehand attire) and magical abilities. Modern archaeological finds, such as weapons from the 1590 Siege of Odawara, confirm their historical role as saboteurs.

## Terms
- **Ninja/Shinobi**: A covert agent or mercenary in pre-modern Japan specializing in espionage and sabotage.
- **Ninjutsu**: The "ninja way"; the set of espionage and guerrilla warfare tactics used by ninja.
- **Jizamurai**: Peasant-warriors who formed the social base for many ninja in Iga and Kōka.
- **Ikki**: A league or revolt; the self-governing confederacies of Iga and Kōka.
- **Daimyō**: A feudal lord who employed ninja as mercenaries.
- **Genin**: The lowest rank of ninja; field agents who carried out missions.
- **Makibishi**: Iron or bamboo caltrops used to slow pursuers.
- **Kunoichi**: A term for a female ninja, originally slang for "woman."
- **Bansenshūkai**: A 1676 compendium of ninja knowledge and tactics.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Historical Evidence**: Direct historical accounts of ninja are scarce, and many stories are later legends. The existence of the first "dubbed ninja," Otomono Sahito, is based on scarce evidence.
- **Iga Ninja Theory**: The traditional story of Hattori Hanzō leading 300 ninja to rescue Tokugawa Ieyasu after Nobunaga's assassination is disputed. Some researchers, like those at Mie University, suggest it was Kōka militias, not ninja, who provided aid, and that the ninja army story may be Edo-period propaganda to elevate the status of Iga and Kōka clans.
- **Assassin Myth**: Despite popular belief, there is little evidence that ninja were primarily assassins. Their documented roles were overwhelmingly espionage and sabotage.

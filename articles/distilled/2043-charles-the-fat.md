# Charles the Fat

## Overview
Charles the Fat (839–888) was the last Carolingian emperor to rule a united Frankish kingdom. His reign saw the temporary reunification of Charlemagne's empire, but his perceived ineptitude and reliance on paying off Vikings led to his deposition and the empire's final fragmentation.

## Timeline
- **839** — Born, youngest son of Louis the German.
- **876** — Inherited Alamannia (Alemannia) after his father's death.
- **879** — Received Italy from his incapacitated brother Carloman.
- **12 Feb 881** — Crowned Emperor by Pope John VIII.
- **882** — Inherited East Francia (Saxony, Bavaria) from brother Louis the Younger, reuniting the kingdom.
- **884** — Inherited West Francia after the death of his cousin Carloman II, reuniting the entire Carolingian Empire.
- **885–886** — Vikings besiege Paris; Charles pays them off, damaging his prestige.
- **887** — Deposed in a coup led by his nephew Arnulf of Carinthia.
- **13 Jan 888** — Died in retirement at Donaueschingen.

## Body

### Rise to Power
Charles was the youngest son of Louis the German. After a youth marked by a recorded incident of demonic possession, he was granted lordship over Alamannia in 876. He acquired the Kingdom of Italy in 879 when his brother Carloman was incapacitated by a stroke. Crowned emperor in 881, he reunited East Francia in 882 and West Francia in 884, briefly ruling all of Charlemagne's former empire.

### Reign and Challenges
Charles is traditionally portrayed as lethargic and inept, frequently ill and possibly epileptic. His reign was defined by Viking threats and internal strife. He negotiated with Viking leaders, sometimes through bribery, which was criticized by later historians. His most infamous decision was during the 885–886 Siege of Paris: after encircling the Viking army, he allowed them to ravage Burgundy and paid them 700 pounds of silver to leave, severely diminishing his prestige. Internally, he faced the Wilhelminer War in Pannonia and struggled to secure a legitimate heir, first attempting to promote his illegitimate son Bernard, then adopting Louis of Provence.

### Deposition and Legacy
Charles's authority collapsed in 887. After personal scandals involving his wife and chief minister, his nephew Arnulf of Carinthia led a rebellion. By November 887, Charles was deposed in East Francia, Lotharingia, and Italy. He died in quiet retirement six weeks later. His death triggered the final splintering of the Carolingian Empire into five successor kingdoms, a fragmentation not reversed until the Napoleonic era.

## Terms
- **Carolingian Empire**: The Frankish dynasty and empire founded by Charlemagne, which reached its greatest extent under him.
- **Alamannia/Alemannia**: A region in southwestern Germany and German-speaking Switzerland, a key power base for Charles.
- **Diet**: A formal assembly of nobles and clergy in the Holy Roman Empire, convened to discuss matters of state.
- **Margrave**: A noble title for a military commander responsible for defending a border territory (a march).
- **Viking**: Norse seafarers and raiders who attacked and settled in parts of Europe during this period.
- **Imperial Synod**: A council of church officials convened by the emperor to discuss ecclesiastical and sometimes secular matters.
- **Ordeal of Fire**: A medieval judicial practice where an accused person's guilt or innocence was determined by their ability to handle hot metal without injury.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Competence vs. Illness**: Modern historians debate whether Charles's failures were due to inherent incompetence or were an excusable result of near-constant illness and infirmity.
- **Contemporary vs. Later Reputation**: Contemporary sources like the *Annales Fuldenses* praise his piety, while later historiography, influenced by the nickname "the Fat," is far more critical.
- **Cause of Empire's Collapse**: Scholars question whether the empire's rapid disintegration after his death was a direct result of his deposition, his death, or deeper structural weaknesses in late Carolingian rule.

# Islamabad

## Overview
Islamabad is the planned capital city of Pakistan, constructed in the 1960s to replace Karachi. It serves as the country's administrative and political center, located in the Islamabad Capital Territory. The city is known for its modern infrastructure, high literacy rate, and status as one of Pakistan's safest and most cosmopolitan urban centers.

## Timeline
- **1947** — Karachi becomes the provisional capital of newly independent Pakistan.
- **1958** — A commission is formed to select a new capital site near Rawalpindi.
- **1959** — The commission recommends the area northeast of Rawalpindi.
- **1960s** — Islamabad is constructed as a planned city based on a master plan.
- **1966** — Essential development is completed; the capital begins moving from Rawalpindi.
- **1967** — Islamabad is officially established as the capital.
- **1986** — The Faisal Mosque is completed.
- **2005** — The city suffers damage from the Kashmir earthquake (magnitude 7.6).

- **2015** — The first phase of the Rawalpindi-Islamabad Metrobus opens.
- **2018** — The new Islamabad International Airport is inaugurated.

## Body

### History and Construction
The site of Islamabad on the Pothohar Plateau has a deep history of human settlement, with Stone Age artifacts dating back 100,000 to 500,000 years. Evidence of Neolithic communities (c. 3000 BCE) and the Indus Valley civilisation (23rd-18th centuries BCE) has been found. After Pakistan's independence in 1947, Karachi served as the capital. In 1958, a commission was tasked with finding a new, more central and defensible location. The site near Rawalpindi was chosen, and the Greek architect Constantinos Apostolou Doxiadis developed the master plan. Islamabad was built in the 1960s and officially became the capital in 1967, replacing Karachi.

### Geography and Climate
Islamabad is located at about 540 metres (1,770 ft) above sea level at the foot of the Margalla Hills. It covers 906 square kilometres (350 sq mi) and forms a "Twin Cities" conurbation with Rawalpindi. The city has a humid subtropical climate with five distinct seasons. Temperatures range from 13 °C (55 °F) in January to 38 °C (100 °F) in June. The highest recorded temperature was 46.6 °C (115.9 °F) in 2005. The wettest month is July, with heavy monsoon rains; a record 620 mm (24 in) fell in 10 hours on 23 July 2001. Water supply is managed by reservoirs like Rawal and Simli.

### Administration and Urban Structure
The city is federally administered as the Islamabad Capital Territory (ICT). The local government is the Islamabad Metropolitan Corporation (IMC), supported by the Capital Development Authority (CDA). The master plan divided the city into eight zones: Administrative, Commercial, Educational, Industrial, Diplomatic Enclave, Residential, Rural, and Green. The city is further divided into five major zones (I-V), with residential sectors identified by letters and numbers (e.g., F-5, G-8). Zone I contains developed residential sectors, while Zone III consists mainly of the Margalla Hills National Park.

### Demographics and Economy
Islamabad is highly diverse, with Punjabi, Pashto, and Urdu as the major languages. It has Pakistan's highest literacy rate at 88% (2010 data). The population is predominantly Muslim (95.55%). The city has a high cost of living and is home to a large middle- and upper-middle-class population. It contributes 1% to Pakistan's GDP with only 0.8% of the population. Key economic sectors include information technology, with several software technology parks, and government services. The Islamabad Stock Exchange was merged into the Pakistan Stock Exchange.

### Culture and Landmarks
Islamabad blends modern architecture with Islamic and regional traditions. Major landmarks include the Faisal Mosque (1986), the largest mosque in South Asia, and the Pakistan Monument (2007). The city is home to numerous museums, such as the Lok Virsa Museum, which preserves folk heritage. Historic sites include the 15th-century Pharwala Fort and the 500-year-old Saidpur village. The annual Urs of Bari Imam is a major religious gathering.

### Transport and Education
Islamabad International Airport is Pakistan's largest. Public transport includes the Rawalpindi-Islamabad Metrobus system. The city is connected by motorways to Lahore (M-2) and Peshawar (M-1). Education is a key sector, with over 20 universities, including the National University of Sciences and Technology (NUST) and Quaid-e-Azam University. The Allama Iqbal Open University is one of the world's largest by enrollment.

## Terms
- **Islamabad Capital Territory (ICT):** The federal district administered directly by the Pakistani government, which contains the capital city.
- **Margalla Hills:** A hill range forming the northern boundary of Islamabad, home to the Margalla Hills National Park.
- **Conurbation:** A large metropolitan area formed by the merging of several cities, in this case, Islamabad and Rawalpindi.
- **Humid subtropical climate (Köppen: Cwa):** A climate characterized by hot, humid summers and mild to cool winters.
- **Capital Development Authority (CDA):** The agency responsible for the planning, development, and administration of Islamabad.
- **Faisal Mosque:** A major mosque in Islamabad, noted for its contemporary design lacking a traditional dome, funded by King Faisal of Saudi Arabia.
- **Software Technology Parks:** Designated areas in Islamabad that house information technology companies.

# Sun Yat-sen

## Overview
Sun Yat-sen (1866–1925) was a Chinese physician, revolutionary, and political philosopher who founded the Republic of China (ROC) and the Kuomintang (KMT). He is credited with leading the 1911 Revolution that overthrew the Qing dynasty, ending over 2,000 years of imperial rule. His political philosophy, the Three Principles of the People, sought to modernize China through nationalism, democracy, and people's livelihood, and he remains a revered figure in both the ROC (as "Father of the Nation") and the People's Republic of China (as "Forerunner of the Revolution").

## Timeline
- **12 Nov 1866** — Born in Cuiheng, Xiangshan County, Guangdong.
- **1892** — Graduates from Hong Kong College of Medicine for Chinese.
- **1894** — Founds the Revive China Society after his reform petition to the Qing is rejected.
- **26 Oct 1895** — First Guangzhou Uprising fails; Sun goes into exile.
- **11 Oct 1896** — Detained at the Chinese Legation in London; released after 12 days.
- **20 Aug 1905** — Forms the unified Tongmenghui (United League) in Tokyo.
- **10 Oct 1911** — Wuchang Uprising triggers the Xinhai Revolution.
- **1 Jan 1912** — Elected provisional president of the Republic of China in Nanjing.
- **12 Feb 1912** — Qing Emperor abdicates; Sun relinquishes presidency to Yuan Shikai.
- **25 Oct 1915** — Marries Soong Ching-ling in Japan.
- **1923** — Signs Sun-Joffe Manifesto with the Soviet Union; begins First United Front with the CCP.
- **12 Mar 1925** — Dies of cancer in Beijing.

## Body

### Early Life and Education
Born to a poor peasant family, Sun was educated in Hawaii, where he attended ʻIolani School and Oahu College, absorbing American political ideals. He later studied medicine in Hong Kong, graduating in 1892. His early exposure to Western education and Christianity profoundly shaped his revolutionary vision.

### Revolutionary Path
After his 1894 petition for Qing reform was rejected, Sun founded the Revive China Society and later the Tongmenghui, uniting revolutionary groups. He led a series of failed uprisings (e.g., Guangzhou 1895, Huizhou 1900) while in exile, raising funds primarily from overseas Chinese communities. The successful Wuchang Uprising in 1911, though not directly led by him, was the culmination of his revolutionary efforts.

### Founding the Republic and Political Struggles
Sun became the first provisional president of the Republic of China in 1912 but ceded power to Yuan Shikai to secure the Qing abdication. After Yuan's authoritarian turn and the assassination of KMT leader Song Jiaoren, Sun led a failed "Second Revolution" (1913) and fled to Japan. He later established rival governments in Guangzhou to challenge the Beiyang warlords.

### Ideology and Final Years
Sun's Three Principles of the People (nationalism, democracy, people's livelihood) formed the KMT's ideology. In his final years, he allied with the Soviet Union and the Chinese Communist Party (CCP) in the First United Front, establishing the Whampoa Military Academy to build a revolutionary army. He died in 1925 while advocating for a national assembly and the abolition of unequal treaties.

## Terms
- **Three Principles of the People**: Sun's political philosophy advocating for nationalism (minzu), democracy (minquan), and people's livelihood (minsheng).
- **Kuomintang (KMT)**: The Chinese Nationalist Party, founded by Sun Yat-sen in 1912.
- **Tongmenghui**: The United League, a secret society and underground resistance movement founded by Sun in 1905 to overthrow the Qing.
- **Xinhai Revolution**: The 1911 Revolution that ended the Qing dynasty and established the Republic of China.
- **Beiyang Government**: The government of the Republic of China from 1912 to 1928, dominated by military cliques.
- **First United Front**: The 1923–1927 alliance between the KMT and the CCP, supported by the Soviet Union.
- **Whampoa Military Academy**: A military academy founded in 1924 with Soviet help to train revolutionary officers for the KMT.
- **Unequal Treaties**: A series of treaties imposed on China by Western powers and Japan in the 19th and early 20th centuries, which Sun sought to abolish.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Cause of Death**: While long believed to be liver cancer, a 2016 analysis of the original autopsy report suggests Sun died of gallbladder cancer that metastasized to the liver.
- **Legacy in Mainland China vs. Taiwan**: Sun is honored as "Father of the Nation" in Taiwan but as "Forerunner of the Revolution" in the PRC, which reinterprets his Three Principles to align with communist ideology (the "New Three Principles of the People").
- **Relationship with Japan**: Sun had deep ties with Japanese supporters and admired the Meiji Restoration, but he later condemned Japan's Twenty-One Demands as a betrayal of Pan-Asian ideals.
- **"Father of the Nation" Debate**: In 2004, a proposal by Taiwan's Ministry of Education that Sun was not the "father of Taiwan" sparked significant controversy and protest.

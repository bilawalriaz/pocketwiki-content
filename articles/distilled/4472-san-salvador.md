# San Salvador

## Overview
San Salvador is the capital and largest city of El Salvador, serving as the nation's political, cultural, educational, and financial center. Located in a seismically active volcanic highland, its history is marked by repeated destruction from earthquakes and eruptions, followed by rebuilding. The city is the core of a major metropolitan area and has hosted significant international events.

## Timeline
- **1525** — Founded by Gonzalo de Alvarado; first mayor Diego de Holguín.
- **1528, 1545** — City relocated twice due to space and seismic activity.
- **1785** — Becomes seat of the Intendancy of San Salvador.
- **1917** — Damaged by an earthquake during an eruption of the San Salvador volcano.
- **1931** — Military coup installs Maximiliano Hernández Martínez as president.
- **1986** — Major earthquake destroys government buildings, kills hundreds.
- **1992** — Chapultepec Peace Accords signed, ending the civil war.
- **2001** — Two major earthquakes cause considerable damage.
- **2009** — Mayor Norman Quijano begins "Rescate del Centro Histórico" redevelopment.
- **2015** — Mayor Nayib Bukele presents redesigned city shield and flag.

## Body

### Etymology and Founding
The city was founded in 1525 by conquistador Gonzalo de Alvarado and named San Salvador, likely referencing the feast of the Transfiguration of Jesus (Divino Salvador). The name inspired the country's name, El Salvador. The Pipil people had previously established their capital, Cuzcatlan, near the site.

### History and Development
The city changed location twice in the 16th century, settling in the Valle de Las Hamacas ("Valley of the Hammocks") due to its seismic activity. It became the political epicenter during the independence struggle. The early 20th century saw construction of key buildings like the National Palace (1905) and National Theater (1917). The 1960s-1980s were a "golden age" of modernization, halted by the Salvadoran Civil War (1980s). The 1986 earthquake caused massive destruction. Peace came with the 1992 Chapultepec Accords. Recent decades have focused on downtown restoration and managing urban challenges.

### Geography and Climate
San Salvador is located in the Boquerón Volcano Valley at an average elevation of 659 meters (2,162 ft). It has a tropical wet and dry climate with warm temperatures year-round (daily mean 27°C/80.6°F). The terrain is hilly, surrounded by volcanoes like Boquerón and Cerro El Picacho. The Acelhuate River forms a boundary, and nearby Lake Ilopango is the country's largest natural body of water.

### Demographics and Society
The municipality had 525,990 inhabitants (2024), while the metropolitan area has over 2.4 million. Spanish is the predominant language. The population is predominantly mestizo (72.3%) and white (25.8%). Roman Catholicism is the main religion, with significant Protestant minorities. The city has a small Jewish community, some descended from refugees saved during WWII.

### Economy
San Salvador is the economic heart of El Salvador, with the metropolitan area attracting 70% of national investment. The economy is service-based, focusing on finance, retail, and business headquarters. The U.S. dollar has been legal tender since 2001. Major industries include food processing, beverage manufacturing (e.g., Industrías La Constancia), and construction. The city lacks a consolidated financial district but has business clusters in areas like Colonia Escalón.

### Architecture and Landmarks
Historic colonial structures were largely destroyed by earthquakes. Notable surviving or rebuilt landmarks include the Metropolitan Cathedral (1950s), the National Palace (1905-1911), and the National Theater (1917). Modern architecture includes high-rises like Centro Financiero Gigante and the World Trade Center San Salvador. The city features various styles, from Neo-Gothic to Futurist.

### Transportation
San Salvador is a regional transport hub. The primary airport is Monseñor Óscar Arnulfo Romero International Airport (40 km away). The road network includes the Pan-American Highway (CA-1). Public transport consists of buses and taxis, with the SITRAMSS bus system launched in 2013. A limited railway service exists.

### Disasters and Security
The city is highly vulnerable to earthquakes, volcanic eruptions, and tropical storms. Major disasters include the 1986 earthquake, the 2001 earthquakes, and Tropical Storm Amanda (2020). Security concerns rose after the civil war, with gang violence (e.g., 18th Street, Mara Salvatrucha) becoming a significant issue, though crime rates have been reduced in recent years.

## Terms
- **Mestizo**: A person of mixed European and Indigenous ancestry.
- **Intendancy**: An administrative district in the Spanish colonial system.
- **Caldera**: A large cauldron-like hollow that forms shortly after the emptying of a magma chamber in a volcanic eruption.
- **Köppen climate classification**: A system for classifying the world's climates based on temperature and precipitation patterns.
- **Remittances**: Money sent by migrants back to their home country.
- **Mara**: A Salvadoran street gang.
- **SITRAMSS**: San Salvador Metropolitan Area Integrated System of Transportation, a bus rapid transit system.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The exact inspiration for the city's name (feast of the Transfiguration vs. other "Divine Savior" references) is presented as likely but not definitive.
- The identity of the gunmen who fired on mourners at Archbishop Romero's funeral in 1980, causing a deadly stampede, was never confirmed.
- The long-term effectiveness of security measures and urban redevelopment projects like the "Rescate del Centro Histórico" in addressing crime and urban decay remains an ongoing challenge.

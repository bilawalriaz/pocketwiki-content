# Paraná River

## Overview
The Paraná River is a major river system in south-central South America, flowing through Brazil, Paraguay, and Argentina for approximately 4,880 km (3,030 mi). It is the second-longest river on the continent after the Amazon. Its vast drainage basin supports major cities, extensive agriculture, and critical hydroelectric power, making it a vital economic and ecological artery for the region.

- **1984** — The Itaipu Dam begins operating, flooding the historic Guaíra Falls.
- **2021** — A severe drought causes the river to hit a 77-year low water level.

## Body

### Course and Geography
The Paraná River is formed by the confluence of the Paranaiba and Rio Grande rivers in southern Brazil. It flows generally southwest, then south, forming borders between Paraguay and Brazil, and later Paraguay and Argentina. Its largest tributary is the Paraguay River. Near its end, the river splits into several arms, forming the Paraná Delta before merging with the Uruguay River to create the Río de la Plata estuary, which empties into the Atlantic Ocean.

### Major Infrastructure and Impact
Two massive hydroelectric dams have profoundly altered the river. The Itaipu Dam, which began operating in 1984, is the world's third-largest hydroelectric plant and created a reservoir that submerged the Guaíra Falls. Downstream, the Yacyretá Dam, a joint project between Argentina and Paraguay, has caused significant flooding in low-lying urban areas like Encarnación. These dams have made Paraguay a major exporter of hydroelectric power but have also blocked the river's use as a continuous shipping corridor.

### Economic and Ecological Significance
The Paraná's watershed encompasses much of south-central South America, including major cities like São Paulo and Buenos Aires. The river is a crucial waterway for navigation and trade, part of the Paraná–Paraguay and Tietê–Paraná Waterways. It supports commercial fishing for species like the surubí and sábalo, and its delta is a world-renowned bird-watching destination. Water table measurements from the river, used for oceangoing ships, date back to 1904.

### Navigation Rights Dispute
In the 1960s, a dispute over navigation rights arose between Argentina and Paraguay. Argentina sought to build a bridge and develop hydroelectric facilities, while Paraguay, having already built the Acaray Dam, was not in favor. A bilateral agreement endorsing free navigation and mutual treatment of vessels was finally signed in January 1973.

## Terms
- **Drainage Basin:** The area of land where all water drains into a common outlet, such as a river.
- **Confluence:** The point where two rivers meet and merge.
- **Hydroelectric Dam:** A structure built across a river to generate electricity from the flow of water.
- **Tributary:** A river or stream that flows into a larger river.
- **Waterway:** A navigable body of water used for transportation.
- **Delta:** A landform created by sediment deposited as a river flows into a slower-moving or stagnant body of water.

# Talaat Pasha

## Overview
Mehmed Talât Pasha was a leading Ottoman politician and de facto ruler of the Ottoman Empire from 1913 to 1918. As the civilian leader of the Committee of Union and Progress (CUP) triumvirate, he is widely considered the primary architect of the Armenian Genocide and other ethnic cleansing campaigns during World War I. His rule marked the shift from Ottoman multiculturalism to authoritarian Turkish nationalism, and his legacy remains deeply contested.

- **1913** — Became part of the ruling CUP triumvirate ("Three Pashas").
- **24 Apr 1915** — Ordered arrest of Armenian intellectuals in Constantinople, initiating the Armenian Genocide.
- **4 Feb 1917** — Became Grand Vizier (Prime Minister).
- **Mar 1918** — Personally negotiated Treaty of Brest-Litovsk with Bolsheviks.
- **8 Oct 1918** — Resigned as Grand Vizier amid Ottoman defeat in WWI.
- **2–3 Nov 1918** — Fled the Ottoman Empire for Germany.
- **5 Jul 1919** — Sentenced to death in absentia by Ottoman court-martial.
- **15 Mar 1921** — Assassinated in Berlin by Soghomon Tehlirian (Operation Nemesis).
- **25 Feb 1943** — Remains transferred to Turkey for a state funeral.

## Body

### Early Life and Activism
Born into a middle-class family, Talât's early life was marked by the 1877–78 Russo-Turkish War, which fostered his nationalism. After working as a postal clerk, he joined the anti-autocratic Committee of Union and Progress (CUP). He rose through the CUP ranks in Salonika, merging his local group with the Paris-based CUP in 1907 and becoming a key internal organizer.

### Rise to Power (1908–1913)
Talât was elected to parliament after the 1908 Young Turk Revolution. The political crises of the era, including the 31 March Incident and the Balkan Wars, disillusioned him with pluralism and pushed him toward hard-line Turkish nationalism. He helped orchestrate the 1913 coup that established CUP one-party rule, forming a ruling triumvirate with Enver and Cemal Pashas.

### Rule and the Armenian Genocide (1913–1918)
As the dominant civilian leader, Talât pursued aggressive Turkification policies. He was instrumental in bringing the Ottoman Empire into World War I. On 24 April 1915, he ordered the arrest of Armenian intellectuals, a key step in the Armenian Genocide. He promulgated the 1915 Tehcir Law (Temporary Law of Deportation), which authorized mass deportations leading to the deaths of approximately 1 million Armenians. He also ordered deportations of Assyrians and Greeks. As Grand Vizier from 1917, he introduced social reforms but faced military defeat and resigned in October 1918.

### Exile, Trial, and Death
Talât fled to Berlin, where he was granted asylum. The Ottoman government tried him in absentia, convicting and sentencing him to death for organizing massacres and subverting the constitution. In exile, he supported the Turkish nationalist movement led by Mustafa Kemal. He was assassinated in 1921 by an Armenian avenger as part of Operation Nemesis. His remains were later moved to Turkey and given a state funeral in 1943.

## Terms
*   **Committee of Union and Progress (CUP):** A revolutionary Young Turk organization that became the ruling party, establishing a one-party dictatorship.
*   **Three Pashas:** The ruling triumvirate of the Ottoman Empire during WWI: Talât (civilian), Enver (military), and Cemal.
*   **Grand Vizier:** The prime minister and highest-ranking official in the Ottoman Empire.
*   **Tehcir Law:** The 1915 Temporary Law of Deportation that authorized the mass deportation of Armenians.
*   **Operation Nemesis:** A covert operation by the Armenian Revolutionary Federation to assassinate Ottoman perpetrators of the Armenian Genocide.
*   **Turkification:** Policies aimed at promoting Turkish ethnic and cultural dominance within the multi-ethnic empire.
*   **Muhacir:** Muslim refugees, often from the Balkans, resettled in Anatolia, sometimes in properties vacated by deported Christians.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Nature of Rule:** Historians debate whether the CUP regime was a triumvirate (1913–1914) or if Talât became the sole dictator afterward, especially after 1917.
*   **Legacy in Turkey:** Talât is viewed as a "great statesman" and "founding father" in Turkey, where he is primarily criticized for leading the empire into WWI, not for the genocide. His 1922 conviction was rescinded by the Kemalist government.
*   **Genocide Responsibility:** While historians widely identify him as the main architect of the Armenian Genocide, his posthumous memoirs propagated a narrative blaming Armenians as rebels and secessionists.

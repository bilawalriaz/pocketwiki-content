# Lê Lợi

## Overview
Lê Lợi (1385–1433) was a Vietnamese rebel leader who founded the Later Lê dynasty. He led a decade-long guerrilla war (1418–1427) that successfully expelled the Ming dynasty's colonial forces from Đại Việt (Vietnam), restoring its independence after a period of Chinese occupation. He is celebrated as one of Vietnam's greatest national heroes.

## Timeline
- **1385** — Lê Lợi is born in Lam Sơn, Thanh Hóa province.
- **1406** — Ming dynasty invades Đại Việt, defeating Hồ Quý Ly and establishing colonial rule.
- **1418** — Lê Lợi launches the Lam Sơn revolt against Ming rule.
- **1423** — After defeats and a failed peace deal, Lê Lợi's revolt is temporarily suppressed.
- **1424** — Following a change in Ming policy, Lê Lợi rebuilds his army and captures Nghệ An.
- **1425** — Lê Lợi's forces liberate the region from Thanh Hóa southward.
- **1426** — Lê Lợi's armies push north into the Red River Delta; a major Ming force is defeated at Tốt Động.
- **1427** — Ming reinforcements are defeated at Chi Lang; the siege of Xương Giang ends the war.
- **1428** — Lê Lợi is enthroned as Emperor of the restored Đại Việt, taking the reign name Thuận Thiên.
- **1431** — The Ming dynasty formally recognizes Lê Lợi as the king of Đại Việt.
- **1433** — Lê Lợi dies, appointing his son and a regent to succeed him.

## Body

### Background and Early Life
The 14th century saw Đại Việt weakened by ecological crisis, internal strife, and repeated Cham attacks. In 1399, the reformer Hồ Quý Ly usurped the throne, providing a pretext for a massive Ming invasion in 1406. The Ming conquered the country, renamed it Jiaozhi, and imposed Chinese administration and culture, which met widespread resistance. Lê Lợi was born into a noble family in Lam Sơn. He initially participated in earlier revolts led by Trần princes, was imprisoned by the Ming (1413–1415), and later worked as a translator before being denounced as a rebel. In early 1418, he raised the banner of resistance at Lam Sơn, declaring himself Bình Định vương (Prince of Pacification).

### The Lam Sơn Revolt (1418–1427)
Lê Lợi's revolt began with guerrilla tactics, using small, mobile bands to ambush larger Ming forces. He was supported by prominent families like the Trịnh and Nguyễn. The early years were difficult; in 1421, a Laotian army allied with the Ming besieged him, leading to a temporary defeat and a failed peace treaty in 1423. The tide turned in 1424 when a new Ming emperor pursued a less aggressive policy. Lê Lợi rebuilt his army, captured Nghệ An province, and by 1425 had liberated the southern regions. In 1426, he marched north into the Red River Delta. A decisive Vietnamese victory at Tốt Động in December 1426 killed or captured about 30,000 Ming soldiers. By 1427, Ming reinforcements were defeated at Chi Lang, and the final Ming stronghold at Xương Giang fell in October, ending the war. The Ming withdrew, repatriating over 86,000 prisoners.

### Reign and Legacy
In 1428, Lê Lợi became emperor, restoring Đại Việt and renaming the capital Đông Kinh (later Tonkin). He reorganized the government, rebuilt infrastructure, revived classical examinations, and distributed land to soldiers. He maintained a tributary relationship with the Ming, paying for formal recognition. His reign saw campaigns in the western hills and population expansion through settlement. He died in 1433, having appointed a regent for his son. His death was followed by internal court purges that eliminated most of his trusted comrades. Lê Lợi is immortalized in legends, most famously the story of a magical sword ("The Will of Heaven") lent by a Dragon King and later returned to Hoàn Kiếm Lake in Hanoi.

## Terms
- **Đại Việt**: The historical name for Vietnam, used during various dynastic periods.
- **Ming dynasty**: The Chinese imperial dynasty (1368–1644) that invaded and occupied Vietnam in the early 15th century.
- **Guerrilla tactics**: A form of irregular warfare using small, mobile groups to ambush and harass a larger, conventional army.
- **Bình Định vương**: The royal title ("King of Pacification") adopted by Lê Lợi at the start of his revolt.
- **Thuận Thiên**: Lê Lợi's reign name, meaning "To obey Heaven," signifying his claim to a mandate to rule.
- **Tributary state**: A state that acknowledges the supremacy of a larger power (here, the Ming) in exchange for recognition and peace, often involving symbolic payments.
- **Hoàn Kiếm Lake**: The "Lake of the Returned Sword" in Hanoi, named for the legend of Lê Lợi returning his magical sword to a golden turtle.

## Debates and open questions
- The ethnic origin of Lê Lợi's family is debated, with hypotheses suggesting Mường ancestry, though recent genealogical studies confirm his Kinh (Vietnamese) ethnicity.
- The extent of popular support for the Ming colonial administration, particularly in the capital Thăng Long, versus the widespread resistance in the countryside.
- The precise catalyst for Lê Lợi's 1418 uprising, with sources noting both the broader colonial oppression and a specific incident involving the abduction of his daughter by a Ming eunuch.

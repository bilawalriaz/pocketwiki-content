# Colonization

## Overview
Colonization is the process of sending people to live in and govern another country, often involving settlement and control. It has shaped global history from ancient times to the present, involving the movement of peoples, establishment of settlements, and the imposition of political or cultural control, with significant impacts on indigenous populations and global power structures.

## Timeline
- **c. 3000 BC–500 AD** — Classical period: Greek and Phoenician city-states establish colonies in the Mediterranean; Roman Empire expands through conquest and settlement.
- **5th–11th centuries** — Post-Roman migrations and Viking colonization of Iceland, Greenland, and briefly North America (Vinland).
- **1500–1900** — European colonial era: Major powers (Spain, Portugal, Britain, etc.) colonize the Americas, Africa, Asia, and Oceania.
- **1824** — Mexico's General Colonization Law enacted.
- **1830s–1840s** — U.S. doctrine of "manifest destiny" promotes westward expansion and colonization.
- **1884–1885** — Berlin Conference formalizes European colonization of Africa.
- **1888** — Britain establishes official colony in South East New Guinea.
- **1920s** — Soviet Union implements *korenizatsiia* policy to promote ethnic cultures among non-Russians.
- **1934** — Soviet government establishes the Jewish Autonomous Oblast in the Far East.
- **1944–1991** — Soviet reoccupation of Baltic states leads to mass settlement of ethnic Russians and suppression of local cultures.
- **1948** — State of Israel established following Zionist colonization of Palestine.
- **Late 20th–21st century** — Indonesian transmigration program moves people from Java to less populous islands; settler colonization documented in Russian-occupied territories (Ukraine, Georgia) and Western Sahara.

## Body

### Pre-modern Colonization
In the classical period, maritime powers like Greece and Phoenicia established colonies across the Mediterranean. The Roman Empire expanded through conquest, founding cities such as Cologne and London that became major urban centers. After Rome's decline, large-scale migrations—triggered by groups like the Huns—led to the formation of modern European nations. Vikings from Scandinavia colonized Iceland, Greenland, and briefly reached North America around L'Anse aux Meadows.

### Colonial Era (c. 1500–1900)
Western European nations dominated global colonization, exploiting economic opportunities and pursuing long-term planning. Colonization often involved cultural assimilation through linguistic imperialism, where colonial languages displaced indigenous ones. Settler colonialism, as seen in Australia and New Zealand, was justified by concepts like *terra nullius* ("empty land"), leading to laws and doctrines such as Mexico's 1824 General Colonization Law and the United States' manifest destiny.

### 20th–21st Century Developments
The Soviet Union pursued internal colonization through policies like *korenizatsiia* (promoting ethnic cultures) and later Russification, which reduced the number of recognized nationalities from 172 in 1927 to 98 in 1939. In the Baltic states, mass settlement of ethnic Russians after 1944 led to demographic shifts—for example, Latvia's ethnic Russian population grew from 8.8% in 1935 to 34% in 1989—accompanied by repression and economic exploitation. The Jewish Autonomous Oblast, established in 1934, saw its Jewish population peak at 30,000 in 1948 before declining to 1,628 by 2010.

In the late 19th century, the Zionist movement began colonizing Palestine through immigration and settlement, leading to the establishment of Israel in 1948. Whether this constitutes settler colonialism is debated. Other modern examples include Indonesia's transmigration program, settler colonization in Russian-occupied territories, and the use of guest workers in advanced nations, which can create exploitative labor conditions. The concept of "endo-colonization" describes the inward use of security technologies to control domestic populations, as seen in various 20th-century dictatorships.

## Terms
- **Colonization**: The act or process of sending people to live in and govern another country.
- **Settler colonialism**: A form of colonization where settlers establish a new society while maintaining ties to a metropolitan country, often through assimilation or oppression of indigenous peoples.
- **Metropole**: The metropolitan country or ruling state that maintains control over a colony.
- **Terra nullius**: Latin for "nobody's land," a legal concept used to justify colonization by declaring land unoccupied.
- **Linguistic imperialism**: The use of a colonial language to the exclusion of indigenous languages in administration and public life.
- **Korenizatsiia**: A Soviet policy in the 1920s promoting ethnic cultures among non-Russians to build trust.
- **Russification**: The promotion of Russian language and culture, often at the expense of local identities.
- **Transmigration**: Government programs to relocate people from densely populated to less populous areas, as in Indonesia.
- **Endo-colonization**: The use of security technologies and narratives to control a domestic population, turning inward against perceived threats.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Classification of Zionism**: Scholars debate whether Zionism should be classified as settler colonialism, with some arguing it lacks an imperial metropole, while others see it as a colonial movement.
- **Soviet colonialism in the Baltic states**: Researchers like Epp Annus note that Soviet rule did not fully fit traditional colonial models, as the Baltic states were already industrialized nations with strong identities, though it developed colonial characteristics over time.
- **Nature of capital transfer**: Marxian economist Samir Amin argues that Soviet capital transfer aimed to develop poorer regions, contrasting with traditional colonial exploitation, while others highlight economic exploitation in the Baltic states.
- **Contemporary settler colonization**: Examples in Russian-occupied territories and Western Sahara raise questions about ongoing colonial practices in the 21st century.

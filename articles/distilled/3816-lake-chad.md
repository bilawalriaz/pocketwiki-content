# Lake Chad

## Overview
Lake Chad is a vital, shallow freshwater lake in West-Central Africa, shared by Nigeria, Niger, Chad, and Cameroon. It is a critical wetland ecosystem and a major source of fish and water for over 30 million people. Historically massive, its area has dramatically shrunk due to climate change and human water diversion, causing severe ecological and socio-economic crises, though its decline has recently stabilized.

## Timeline
- **c. 39,000 BC–300 BC** — "Mega-Chad" heydays; lake covered ~400,000 km².
- **c. 1800 BC** — Emergence of the Gajiganna pottery culture near the lake.
- **c. 1200–1000 BC** — Earliest domesticated Pearl millet found in the region.
- **c. 900 AD** — Kanem Empire established northeast of the lake.
- **1574** — Ottoman Empire invades Fezzan, reaching the Lake Chad area.
- **1884–1885** — Berlin Conference divides Africa; colonial borders defined.
- **1960** — Open water area is ~25,000 km².
- **1964** — Lake Chad Basin Commission established.
- **1970s–1980s** — Major Sahel droughts cause severe lake shrinkage.
- **1980** — Open water area decreases to ~2,000 km².
- **1994** — Lake area hits a low of ~1,756 km².
- **2013–2016** — Average surface water area stabilizes at ~1,876 km².

## Body

### Prehistory and History
The Chad Basin formed from a geological depression. During the Quaternary period, a much larger "Mega-Chad" lake existed, covering ~400,000 km² and flowing to the Atlantic via the Benue River. This vast water body supported early fishing settlements, agriculture, and migrations. By 500 BC, permanent villages and the Sao civilization emerged. The region later became linked to trans-Saharan trade and the Kanem-Bornu Empires, which rose and fell between the 9th and 19th centuries. European colonization in the late 19th and early 20th centuries established the modern borders of the four surrounding countries.

### Geography and Hydrology
Lake Chad is a shallow, endorheic lake (a closed drainage basin) divided into a deeper south and shallower north by a sill. Its primary water source is the Chari River system, which provides 95% of inflow. The lake's area is highly sensitive to climate, with seasonal and long-term fluctuations. It shrank catastrophically from ~25,000 km² in 1960 to a low of ~1,756 km² in 1994 due to drought and human water diversion. Since the mid-1990s, the area has stabilized but remains a fraction of its former size. Beneath the lake lies a massive, resilient Quaternary aquifer that recharges from rainfall and seepage, providing a crucial groundwater buffer.

### Ecology and Human Impact
The lake is an Important Bird Area and a rich fishery with 84 species, supporting local livelihoods. However, its ecosystem is under severe stress. The dramatic shrinkage has reduced biodiversity, altered vegetation, and displaced communities. Human activities, including large-scale irrigation projects that divert feeder rivers and overgrazing, have accelerated the decline. This has led to conflicts over scarce water and land, economic hardship, and regional instability, exacerbated by insurgencies like Boko Haram. The Lake Chad Basin Commission manages the shared resource, and a major inter-basin water transfer project from the Congo River has been proposed to replenish the lake.

## Terms
- **Endorheic**: A closed drainage basin that retains water and allows no outflow to other external bodies of water, like rivers or oceans.
- **Catchment area**: The area from which rainfall flows into a particular river, lake, or reservoir.
- **Mega-Chad**: The name for the prehistoric, vastly larger version of Lake Chad that existed during wetter periods.
- **Quaternary aquifer**: A large, underground layer of water-bearing rock (here, beneath Lake Chad) that is a major transboundary groundwater resource.
- **Phreatic aquifer**: An unconfined aquifer where the water table is at atmospheric pressure.
- **Rift basin**: A geological depression caused by the stretching and thinning of Earth's crust.
- **Ramsar Wetland**: A wetland site designated to be of international importance under the Ramsar Convention.
- **Natron**: A naturally occurring salt (sodium carbonate decahydrate) historically mined from the lake's shores for trade.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The primary causes of the lake's shrinkage are debated, with relative contributions of **climate change** (e.g., Sahel droughts, Atlantic multidecadal oscillation) versus **human water diversion** (irrigation projects) being a key question.
- The feasibility and environmental impact of the proposed **inter-basin water transfer** from the Congo River are major subjects of debate.
- The long-term resilience of the **Quaternary aquifer** and its capacity to buffer against future climate and usage pressures is an ongoing area of study.

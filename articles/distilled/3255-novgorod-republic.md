# Novgorod Republic

## Overview
The Novgorod Republic was a medieval city-state in northwestern Russia that existed from the 12th to the 15th centuries. It was a major political and commercial power, renowned for its unique republican government, its role as the easternmost trading post of the Hanseatic League, and its vibrant cultural and artistic life. Its eventual annexation by the Grand Duchy of Moscow in 1478 was a pivotal step in the unification of Russia.

## Timeline
- **862** — Varangian brothers Rurik, Sineus, and Truvor are "invited" to rule, with Rurik in Novgorod.
- **1136** — Novgorodians depose their prince, establishing the veche's power to elect and dismiss princes.
- **1240** — Prince Alexander Yaroslavich defeats the Swedes at the Battle of the Neva.
- **1242** — Alexander Yaroslavich defeats German crusaders at the Battle on the Ice.
- **1348** — The Treaty of Bolotovo formally grants independence to Pskov, a former part of Novgorod Land.
- **1456** — The Treaty of Yazhelbitsy marks the beginning of Novgorod's loss of independence to Moscow.
- **1471** — Moscow defeats Novgorod at the Battle of Shelon River, severely limiting its freedom.
- **1478** — Ivan III of Moscow annexes Novgorod, abolishing the veche and its republican government.

## Body

### Origins and Early History
The area was populated by East Slavic tribes before being ruled by Varangians from the 9th century. Novgorod is traditionally viewed as the birthplace of the Russian monarchy. From the 11th century, Novgorodians asserted greater control over their rulers, rejecting dependence on Kiev. This culminated in 1136 when they dismissed their prince, leading to a system where the popular assembly, or *veche*, elected and dismissed princes from other principalities, reducing their power to that of hired officials.

### Government and Society
Novgorod's government was a complex system with the *veche* (popular assembly) as the supreme theoretical authority. Key elected officials included the *posadnik* (chief executive), the *tysyatsky* (military and commercial official), and the archbishop, who held significant secular and religious power. Princes were invited from other dynasties but their power was severely restricted by contracts (*ryads*). Society was hierarchical, dominated by the *boyar* aristocracy, who controlled most land and political offices, followed by merchants, clergy, and the *chyornye lyudi* ("black people," or commoners). The *boyar* dominance led some historians to characterize the system as an oligarchy or a "boyar republic."

### Economy and Trade
Novgorod's wealth was built on its control of the fur trade from its vast northeastern territories. It was a key member of the Hanseatic League, with German merchants trading from the Peterhof *kontor* (trading post). The economy also included agriculture, fishing, salt production, and iron mining. Novgorod imported silver, cloth, and wine from Western Europe. Its economic power began to wane in the 15th century due to resource exhaustion and growing Muscovite competition.

### Culture and Literacy
Novgorod was a major center of Russian art and culture. It was famous for its prolific icon painting, which blended Byzantine, Russian, and European styles. The city's architecture featured stone churches in distinctive styles. Archaeological discoveries of over a thousand birch bark manuscripts indicate widespread literacy among the urban population, including women and children, for everyday and business purposes.

### Fall to Moscow
In the 15th century, as Moscow grew in power, Novgorod's autonomy eroded. A pro-Lithuanian faction among the boyars sought an alliance with Poland-Lithuania, leading to war with Moscow. After a decisive defeat at the Battle of Shelon River in 1471, Novgorod's independence was fatally weakened. In 1478, Ivan III of Moscow formally annexed the republic, abolished its government, and removed the veche bell to Moscow, ending its republican era.

## Terms
- **Veche**: The popular assembly of Novgorod, which theoretically held supreme power and elected key officials.
- **Posadnik**: The chief executive of Novgorod, elected by the veche to oversee city affairs and co-chair courts.
- **Tysyatsky**: An official originally heading the town militia, later handling judicial and commercial functions, elected by the veche.
- **Boyars**: The highest aristocratic class in Novgorod, who dominated political and economic life.
- **Ryad**: A contract signed between Novgorod and an invited prince, outlining the prince's limited rights and responsibilities.
- **Kontor**: A foreign trading post, specifically the Hanseatic League's Peterhof in Novgorod.
- **Chyornye lyudi**: Literally "black people," a term for the free commoners of Novgorod who could participate in the veche.
- **Votchina**: A hereditary estate, primarily owned by boyars and monasteries.

## Debates and open questions
- The precise nature of Novgorod's political system is debated: scholars have variously described it as a democratic republic, an oligarchy, or a "boyar republic," depending on the degree of popular participation versus elite control.
- The historical role of Marfa Boretskaya in the pro-Lithuanian alliance is contested; some historians view her as a central figure, while others argue her influence was exaggerated and she was scapegoated.
- The extent of "class struggle" as a driver of the numerous internal uprisings in Novgorod is unclear, with many conflicts likely stemming from factional disputes among the boyar elite.

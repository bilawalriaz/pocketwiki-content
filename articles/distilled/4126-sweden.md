# Sweden

## Overview
Sweden is a Nordic country on the Scandinavian Peninsula, known for its long history from prehistoric times to its modern role as a highly developed welfare state. It transitioned from a Viking-era tribal society to a major European empire before losing most of its territories, eventually establishing a stable democracy and a comprehensive social welfare system. Sweden's history is marked by periods of expansion and contraction, neutrality in major wars, and a recent shift from military non-alignment to joining NATO.

## Timeline
- **c. 12,000 BC** — First inhabitants arrive during the Allerød oscillation.
- **8th–11th centuries** — Swedish Viking Age, with extensive eastern trade and raids.
- **Late 10th century** — Unified Swedish state established under Erik the Victorious.
- **1397** — Sweden joins the Kalmar Union with Denmark and Norway.
- **1523** — Gustav Vasa elected king, ending the Kalmar Union and founding modern Sweden.
- **1611–1632** — Reign of Gustavus Adolphus, marking the rise of the Swedish Empire.
- **1709** — Decisive defeat by Russia at the Battle of Poltava, beginning the decline of the empire.
- **1809** — Loss of Finland to Russia; Sweden's eastern half.
- **1814** — Sweden forces Norway into a personal union, its last war.
- **1905** — Peaceful dissolution of the union with Norway.
- **1995** — Sweden joins the European Union.
- **2024** — Sweden becomes a member of NATO.

## Body

### Early History and Kingdom Formation
Sweden has been inhabited since around 12,000 BC. The inhabitants emerged as the Geats and Swedes, part of the Norsemen. A unified state began in the late 10th century. Christianity was introduced in 829 but did not fully replace paganism until the 12th century. The Kalmar Union of 1397 united Sweden, Norway, and Denmark, but Sweden left in 1523 under Gustav Vasa after the Stockholm Bloodbath. Vasa's reign established a hereditary monarchy and led the Protestant Reformation in Sweden.

### The Swedish Empire and Its Decline
Sweden became a great power during the 17th century, controlling much of the Baltic Sea. Its empire was founded on military strength and economic reforms from the 16th century. Key events include the Thirty Years' War and the Great Northern War. The defeat at Poltava in 1709 was a turning point, leading to the loss of most overseas territories by the early 18th century. The eastern half, Finland, was lost to Russia in 1809.

### Modern Development and Neutrality
After 1814, Sweden avoided direct involvement in wars. It industrialized later than other Western European nations, with a largely agricultural economy until the late 19th century. Mass emigration to the U.S. occurred in the late 1800s. A strong grassroots movement in the late 19th century laid the foundation for modern democracy. Sweden remained neutral in both World Wars, though its neutrality is debated. After WWII, it developed a comprehensive welfare state while maintaining official neutrality during the Cold War.

### Recent History and International Alignment
In the 1990s, Sweden faced a fiscal crisis and implemented reforms, including reducing the welfare state. It joined the EU in 1995 but rejected the Euro in 2003. Historically non-aligned militarily, Sweden joined NATO in 2024 in response to the 2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine. The country has become more culturally diverse due to immigration, which has brought social and political challenges.

## Terms
- **Kalmar Union**: A personal union of Denmark, Norway, and Sweden from 1397 to 1523.
- **Riksdag**: The unicameral parliament of Sweden, with 349 members.
- **Instrument of Government**: Sweden's principal constitutional law, establishing the framework of government.
- **Nordic welfare model**: A system providing universal health care, education, and social security, funded by high taxes.
- **Non-alignment**: Sweden's historical policy of not joining military alliances in peacetime.
- **Götar (Geats)**: One of the two main tribes that formed the early Swedish people, inhabiting Götaland.
- **Svear (Swedes)**: The other main tribe, inhabiting Svealand, whose name gave the country its name.
- **Rus**: The name for Swedish Vikings who traveled east and are believed to have founded Kievan Rus'.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The exact age of the Swedish kingdom is debated, depending on whether it is defined by the rule of the Svear over Svealand or the later unification with the Geats.
- The extent of Sweden's neutrality during World War II is disputed, with arguments that it could have done more to oppose Nazi Germany.
- The causes and consequences of Sweden's significant increase in income inequality since the mid-1980s are subjects of ongoing analysis.

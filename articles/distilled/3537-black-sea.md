# Black Sea

## Overview
The Black Sea is a marginal sea between Europe and Asia, bordered by six countries. It is a critical waterway for trade, ecology, and geopolitics, notable for its unique hydrology, including a vast anoxic (oxygen-depleted) deep layer that preserves ancient shipwrecks. Its history is marked by shifting connections to the global ocean, ancient civilizations, and modern conflicts.

## Timeline
- **c. 5600 BC** — Hypothesized catastrophic flooding from the Mediterranean via the Bosporus (Black Sea deluge hypothesis).
- **9th century BC** — Greek colonies established along the southern coast.
- **1479** — The Black Sea becomes an Ottoman-controlled lake after the Republic of Genoa loses Crimea.
- **1783** — Imperial Russia establishes the Black Sea Fleet and occupies Crimea.
- **1853–1856** — Crimean War; Treaty of Paris demilitarizes the Black Sea.
- **1914–1918** — Significant naval theatre in World War I.
- **1941–1945** — Naval and land battles in World War II, including the Siege of Sevastopol.
- **1936** — Montreux Convention regulates passage through the Turkish Straits.
- **1990s** — Ecological recovery begins after pollution reduction efforts.
- **2010** — Discovery of the Black Sea undersea river, a saline current along the seabed.
- **2022** — Russian cruiser *Moskva* sunk by Ukraine; Snake Island campaign.
- **2023** — Russian Su-27 damages an American MQ-9 Reaper drone over the sea.

## Body

### Geography and Hydrology
The Black Sea covers 436,400 km² and reaches a maximum depth of 2,212 m. It is supplied by major rivers like the Danube, Dnieper, and Dniester, giving it a drainage basin spanning 24 countries. The sea is connected to the Mediterranean via the Turkish Straits (Bosporus and Dardanelles) and to the Sea of Azov via the Kerch Strait. Its water balance is positive, with a net outflow through the Bosporus.

A defining feature is its meromictic basin, where deep waters do not mix with oxygenated surface layers. This creates a permanent anoxic layer below about 100–200 m, preserving organic matter and shipwrecks. Surface circulation is cyclonic, dominated by the Rim Current. A halocline (salinity gradient) and pycnocline (density gradient) isolate the deep water. In 2010, scientists discovered a unique undersea river of dense, saline Mediterranean water flowing along the Bosporus seabed.

### Geology and Formation
The basin formed from two relict back-arc basins during the Mesozoic era. Its connection to the World Ocean via the Turkish Straits has been intermittent. During the last ice age, sea levels were over 100 m lower, and the Black Sea may have been a landlocked freshwater lake. The "Black Sea deluge hypothesis" proposes a catastrophic Mediterranean inflow c. 5600 BC, though the timing and magnitude are debated. Tectonic activity, including the collision of the Eurasian and African plates, continues to influence the basin.

### Ecology and Environment
The Black Sea ecosystem is adapted to brackish, nutrient-rich conditions. Its biodiversity is about one-third that of the Mediterranean. Phytoplankton blooms are seasonal, with diatoms, dinoflagellates, and coccolithophores (e.g., *Emiliania huxleyi*) being major groups. Marine mammals include endemic subspecies of common and bottlenose dolphins and the harbour porpoise, all endangered. The Mediterranean monk seal is likely extinct in the basin.

Since the 1960s, industrial expansion and dam construction increased nutrient pollution, causing more frequent algal blooms and anoxia. Overfishing and the invasive ctenophore *Mnemiopsis leidyi* further damaged the ecosystem. Partial recovery occurred in the 1990s due to pollution reduction and EU-related sewage treatment improvements, though ecological decline was noted in the early 21st century.

### History and Human Activity
The sea was a crossroads for ancient peoples, including Greeks, Persians, and Romans. Greek colonies, established from the 9th century BC, developed a distinct Pontic culture. The region was later dominated by the Ottoman Empire, which controlled it for centuries until Imperial Russia emerged as a major power in the late 18th century. The Crimean War (1853–1856) and both World Wars saw major naval conflicts.

In the 21st century, the sea remains a contested zone, particularly following the 2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine, which saw the sinking of the flagship *Moskva* and battles over Snake Island. The Montreux Convention (1936) governs military passage, giving Turkey control over the straits. Economically, the sea is vital for fishing (e.g., Turkish anchovy catch), tourism, and hydrocarbon exploration. Major gas discoveries, like Turkey's 320 billion m³ find in 2020, highlight its resource potential.

## Terms
- **Anoxic**: Lacking dissolved oxygen, a condition of the Black Sea's deep waters that preserves organic material.
- **Meromictic**: A lake or sea with layers of water that do not intermix, as seen in the Black Sea's stratified structure.
- **Halocline**: A vertical salinity gradient in a body of water; in the Black Sea, it separates fresher surface water from saltier deep water.
- **Pycnocline**: A layer where water density increases rapidly with depth, inhibiting vertical mixing.
- **Rim Current**: The dominant cyclonic (counter-clockwise) surface current flowing around the perimeter of the Black Sea.
- **Endorheic**: A closed drainage basin that retains water and allows no outflow to other external bodies of water, like the ancient Black Sea may have been.
- **Montreux Convention**: The 1936 treaty giving Turkey control over the Bosporus and Dardanelles straits and regulating naval passage.
- **Sapropel**: Organic-rich sediment layers formed during algal blooms, found in Black Sea sediments.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Deluge Hypothesis**: Debate continues over the suddenness, exact date (c. 5600 BC), and magnitude of the hypothesized catastrophic flooding from the Mediterranean.
- **Ecological Recovery**: Sources conflict on whether the Black Sea ecosystem fully recovered in the early 21st century after 1990s improvements.
- **Military Control**: As of January 2025, the Black Sea is described as a "contested maritime area" where neither Ukraine nor Russia has full control, with coastal areas being more heavily influenced by land-based weapons.

# Joe Louis

## Overview
Joe Louis (1914–1981) was an American boxer who held the world heavyweight championship for a record 11 years and 8 months, defending his title 25 consecutive times. Beyond his athletic dominance, he became a pivotal cultural figure, breaking racial barriers as a nationwide hero and symbolizing American strength against fascism during World War II.

## Timeline

- **1926** — Family moves to Detroit, Michigan, as part of the Great Migration.
- **1934** — Wins the light heavyweight United States Amateur Champion National AAU tournament.
- **June 22, 1937** — Defeats James J. Braddock to become world heavyweight champion.
- **June 19, 1936** — Loses his first professional fight to Max Schmeling.
- **June 22, 1938** — Defeats Max Schmeling in a politically charged rematch, lasting 2 minutes and 4 seconds.
- **January 9, 1942** — Volunteers for the U.S. Army, serving in the Special Services Division.
- **June 19, 1946** — Knocks out Billy Conn in their long-awaited rematch.
- **March 1, 1949** — Announces his temporary retirement as champion.
- **September 27, 1950** — Loses the heavyweight title to Ezzard Charles in a comeback attempt.
- **October 26, 1951** — Is knocked out by Rocky Marciano, ending his professional career.
- **April 12, 1981** — Dies of cardiac arrest in Las Vegas.

## Body

### Early Life and Amateur Career
Born the seventh of eight children to parents who were children of former slaves, Louis faced early hardships, including his father's institutionalization and a family move to Detroit in 1926 to escape racial violence. He began boxing at a local recreation center, initially hiding it from his mother. Louis compiled an amateur record of 50–4, with 43 knockouts, winning the 1934 National AAU light heavyweight title.

### Professional Rise and Title Contention
Managed by John Roxborough and Julian Black, Louis was carefully presented to the public to avoid the backlash that had followed the first Black heavyweight champion, Jack Johnson. His handlers drafted "Seven Commandments" for his conduct, emphasizing modesty. Louis rose quickly, defeating former champions like Primo Carnera and Max Baer. His first loss came to Max Schmeling in 1936, after which he defeated James J. Braddock to win the heavyweight title on June 22, 1937.

### Championship Reign and the Schmeling Rivalry
Louis's reign was defined by his historic rematch with Max Schmeling on June 22, 1938. The fight, broadcast globally, was framed as a symbolic battle against Nazism. Louis won by knockout in just 2 minutes and 4 seconds. He then embarked on a period of frequent defenses against a series of contenders nicknamed the "Bum of the Month Club," though many were top-ranked. His 1941 bout against Billy Conn is considered one of the greatest heavyweight fights, with Louis winning by a last-second knockout.

### World War II and Later Career
Louis enlisted in the Army in 1942, using his celebrity for recruitment and morale, though he served in a segregated unit. His charitable fights for the war effort later resulted in a massive tax debt. After the war, he returned to boxing, defeating Conn again but struggling against aging. He retired in 1949 but was forced back into the ring by the IRS, eventually losing the title to Ezzard Charles and suffering a final knockout loss to Rocky Marciano in 1951.

### Financial Troubles and Legacy
Despite earning over $4.6 million, Louis received only about $800,000 due to management deals and taxes. His post-career life was marked by debt, leading him to work as a wrestling referee and greeter. He was a pioneer in golf, helping to break the PGA's color barrier. Louis is buried at Arlington National Cemetery and remains a symbol of athletic excellence and racial progress.

## Terms
- **Great Migration:** The movement of six million African Americans from the rural South to cities in the North, Midwest, and West between 1916 and 1970.
- **Lineal Champion:** The fighter who has defeated the previous champion in the ring, considered the "true" champion in a direct lineage.
- **TKO (Technical Knockout):** A fight stopped by the referee before a knockout occurs, usually because a fighter is deemed unable to safely continue.
- **Split Decision:** A boxing decision where two judges score the fight for one boxer and the third judge scores it for the opponent.
- **Unanimous Decision:** A boxing decision where all three judges score the fight for the same boxer.
- **"Bum of the Month Club":** A derisive nickname for the series of less-famous opponents Louis defeated during his frequent title defenses from 1939 to 1941.
- **Legion of Merit:** A U.S. military decoration awarded for exceptionally meritorious conduct in the performance of outstanding services.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The exact cause of Louis's 1969 collapse was initially reported as a "physical breakdown" but was later revealed by Louis to be cocaine use.
- The racial dynamics of his fights, particularly the Schmeling bouts, are analyzed as complex intersections of sport, nationalism, and propaganda.
- The fairness of his financial management and the subsequent IRS debt remains a point of discussion regarding the exploitation of athletes.

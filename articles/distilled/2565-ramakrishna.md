# Ramakrishna

## Overview
Ramakrishna (1836–1886) was an Indian Hindu mystic and saint whose life and teachings emphasized the harmony of all religions. He is a central figure in the Bengali Renaissance and the founder of the modern Hindu monastic order, the Ramakrishna Math and Mission, through his chief disciple, Swami Vivekananda. His direct, experience-based spirituality and inclusive philosophy have made him one of the most influential religious figures of modern India.

## Timeline
- **18 February 1836** — Born Ramakrishna Chattopadhyay in Kamarpukur, Bengal.
- **c. 1846** — First reported "superconsciousness experience" (samadhi) at age 10 or 11.
- **1855** — Becomes a priest at the newly consecrated Dakshineswar Kali Temple.
- **1859** — Marries Sarada Devi (aged 5); marriage is never consummated.
- **1861** — Initiated into Tantra by the Bhairavi Brahmani.
- **1864** — Practices Vaishnava bhakti under guru Jatadhari.
- **1865** — Initiated into sannyasa (monasticism) by the Vedanta monk Tota Puri; attains Nirvikalpa Samadhi.
- **1866** — Initiated into Islam by Govinda Roy; practices Sufism.
- **1873–74** — Practices Christianity; reports visions of Jesus Christ.
- **1875** — Meets Brahmo Samaj leader Keshab Chandra Sen, who publicizes his teachings.
- **1881–85** — Meets Narendranath Dutta (later Swami Vivekananda) and other key disciples.
- **16 August 1886** — Dies of throat cancer at Cossipore, Kolkata.

## Body

### Early Life and Priesthood
Born into a poor Brahmin family in Kamarpukur, Ramakrishna had little formal education but was deeply immersed in Puranic stories and devotional practices. After his father's death, he moved to Calcutta to assist his brother Ramkumar, who became the chief priest at the Dakshineswar Kali Temple, founded by the philanthropist Rani Rasmani. After Ramkumar's death in 1856, Ramakrishna succeeded him as priest. His intense devotion led to a profound, life-altering vision of the goddess Kali, which he described as an experience of infinite, conscious light.

### Spiritual Practices Across Traditions
Following his vision of Kali, Ramakrishna embarked on a decade of intense spiritual practice (*sadhana*) under various teachers, culminating in realizations within each path. He practiced Tantra under the Bhairavi Brahmani, completing 64 major tantric sadhanas. He then practiced Vaishnava bhakti, experiencing visions of Rama and Krishna. In 1865, the Vedanta monk Tota Puri initiated him into sannyasa, guiding him to the non-dual state of Nirvikalpa Samadhi, which he sustained for six months. He later practiced Sufi Islam and Christianity, reporting visions in each tradition. He concluded that all paths led to the same ultimate reality, which he identified with the Divine Mother Kali.

### Teachings and Later Years
Ramakrishna's teachings, recorded in the *Kathamrita* by his disciple "M," were delivered in rustic Bengali through parables and stories. He emphasized direct God-realization as life's goal and the essential unity of religions, stating, "So many religions, so many paths to reach one and the same goal." He advised householders to follow the path of devotion (*bhakti*) and monks the path of knowledge (*jnana*). His fame attracted intellectuals and elites, including Keshab Chandra Sen, who helped publicize his message. His most influential disciple, Swami Vivekananda, later founded the Ramakrishna Order to carry forward his mission of spiritual and social uplift.

### Death and Legacy
Ramakrishna died of throat cancer in 1886. Before his death, he transferred spiritual authority to Vivekananda. After his passing, his monastic disciples formed the first monastery (math) of the Ramakrishna Order. The organization, formally established by Vivekananda in 1897, became a global movement dedicated to spiritual training and humanitarian service. Ramakrishna is revered by followers as an avatar (divine incarnation) and remains a symbol of religious harmony and mystical experience.

## Terms
- **Samadhi**: A state of meditative consciousness where the individual self merges with the divine or absolute reality.
- **Bhakti**: A Hindu path of devotion focused on loving worship of a personal deity.
- **Tantra**: A set of esoteric rituals and practices aimed at liberation by transcending conventional distinctions between sacred and profane.
- **Advaita Vedanta**: A school of Hindu philosophy teaching non-dualism—the ultimate reality (Brahman) is one without a second.
- **Sannyasa**: The Hindu stage of life involving renunciation of worldly ties for spiritual pursuit.
- **Nirvikalpa Samadhi**: The highest form of samadhi, a state of complete absorption in the formless absolute, beyond duality.
- **Bhavamukha**: A state of consciousness between absorption in the absolute and engagement with the relative world.
- **Avatar**: A deliberate descent of a deity to earth in incarnate form.
- **Kali**: A major Hindu goddess associated with time, change, and destruction; worshipped as the Divine Mother.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Philosophical Interpretation**: Scholars debate whether Ramakrishna's teachings are best understood as a form of Advaita Vedanta (as emphasized by Vivekananda), a synthesis of Tantra and Vedanta, or a unique "Integral Vedanta."
- **Psychoanalytic Readings**: Some psychoanalysts, like Jeffrey Kripal, have interpreted Ramakrishna's mystical experiences and practices through the lens of repressed sexuality, a view strongly contested by traditional scholars and the Ramakrishna Order.
- **Biographical Transformation**: Historians note that Ramakrishna's image was significantly shaped and "mythologized" by his followers, particularly Vivekananda, who presented him as a calm Vedantin, downplaying aspects of his ecstatic and tantric practices.
- **Source Reliability**: The primary source, the *Kathamrita*, is based on diary notes and has been criticized for potential editorial manipulation and for reflecting the perspectives of urban, educated devotees rather than Ramakrishna's own unfiltered voice.

# John Hunyadi

## Overview
John Hunyadi (c. 1406–1456) was a leading Hungarian military and political figure who served as regent of the Kingdom of Hungary from 1446 to 1453. He is renowned for his decades-long struggle against the Ottoman Empire, earning the nickname "Turk-buster" and the papal title *Athleta Christi* (Christ's Champion). His victories, particularly the defense of Belgrade in 1456, halted Ottoman expansion into Central Europe for over 60 years and cemented his legacy as a national hero across the region.

- **1448** — Suffers a catastrophic defeat at the Second Battle of Kosovo.
- **1453** — Resigns as regent; receives the first hereditary title in Hungary (Perpetual Count of Beszterce).
- **1456** — Leads the defense of Belgrade, defeating Sultan Mehmed II's army.
- **11 August 1456** — Dies of plague in the crusader camp near Belgrade.

## Body

### Early Life and Rise to Command
Hunyadi was born into a noble family of likely Wallachian ancestry, first documented in 1409 when his father received Hunyad Castle. His exact birth year and place are uncertain, with theories placing it in Transylvania or Wallachia. He spent his youth serving in the courts of powerful magnates, including a period in the army of Milan, where he learned contemporary military tactics. He married Elisabeth Szilágyi around 1429 and entered the service of King Sigismund, accompanying him to Bohemia where he likely studied Hussite wagon tactics.

### Military Innovations and Ottoman Wars
Hunyadi mastered warfare on Hungary's southern frontier. Appointed to key defensive posts in 1439 and 1441, he adopted innovative tactics, including using wagons as mobile fortresses and employing professional mercenaries alongside local peasantry. His victories in 1441 and 1442, notably defeating a large Ottoman army led by the Governor of Rumelia, made him famous throughout Christendom. He shifted Hungary's strategy from defense to offensive campaigns, aiming to expel the Ottomans from Europe. Despite defeats at Varna (1444) and Kosovo (1448), his "Long Campaign" (1443–44) and the triumphant defense of Belgrade in 1456 established his reputation as a great general.

### Regent and Statesman
During the minority of King Ladislaus V, Hunyadi was elected sole regent in 1446. He managed complex internal politics, facing opposition from magnates like Ulrich II of Celje and the Palatine, while also dealing with mercenary captain John Jiskra in northern Hungary. He intervened in Wallachian and Moldavian affairs to secure the frontier. His regency was marked by efforts to organize anti-Ottoman crusades, though his authority weakened after the defeat at Kosovo and a costly ransom to Despot Đurađ Branković. He resigned the regency in 1453 but remained the kingdom's supreme military commander.

### Final Victory and Death
In 1456, as Sultan Mehmed II besieged Belgrade, Hunyadi assembled a relief force. With the help of crusaders rallied by friar John of Capistrano, he destroyed the Ottoman fleet on the Danube and broke the siege on 22 July. This victory halted the Ottoman advance. Hunyadi died of plague in the camp three weeks later. His fame was a decisive factor in the election of his son, Matthias Corvinus, as king in 1458.

## Terms
- **Regent/Governor**: A ruler appointed to govern a kingdom in the name of a minor or absent monarch.
- **Voivode**: A historical title for a military commander or governor of a province, especially in Transylvania and Wallachia.
- **Ban**: A title for a provincial governor or military commander in the Kingdom of Hungary and Croatia.
- **Janissaries**: Elite infantry units forming the Ottoman Sultan's household troops and bodyguards.
- **Sanjak Bey**: A provincial governor in the Ottoman Empire.
- **Hussites**: Followers of a pre-Protestant Christian movement in Bohemia, known for innovative wagon-fort tactics.
- **Cadastral acre**: A historical unit of land measurement used for taxation and land registry.
- **Perpetual Count**: A hereditary noble title, first granted in Hungary to Hunyadi in 1453.
- **Bugarštica**: A Serbian epic poem or folk song.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Parentage and Origin**: The Wallachian ancestry of Hunyadi's father is accepted by many scholars, but some argue he was a native of the Hunyad region. A popular but unverified legend claims Hunyadi was the illegitimate son of King Sigismund.
- **Mother's Identity**: His mother's identity is uncertain; she may have been from a local noble family or of Greek origin, which could imply Orthodox faith.
- **Birth Year and Place**: His birth year is estimated between 1405–1407, and his birthplace is unknown, with theories pointing to Transylvania or Wallachia.
- **Role in Wallachia**: Historians debate whether Hunyadi installed Vladislav II or Dan as voivode of Wallachia after 1447.
- **Legacy and National Identity**: Hunyadi is claimed as a hero by multiple nations (Hungarian, Romanian, Serbian, etc.), with historiography sometimes reflecting later national ideologies.

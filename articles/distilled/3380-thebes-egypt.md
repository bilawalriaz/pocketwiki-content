# Thebes, Egypt

## Overview
Thebes (ancient Egyptian: Waset) was an ancient Egyptian city located on the Nile in Upper Egypt, whose ruins now lie within modern Luxor. It served as the capital of Egypt for long periods during the Middle and New Kingdoms and was the country's most important religious center, dedicated to the god Amun. Its monumental temple complexes and royal necropolises represent the pinnacle of ancient Egyptian civilization.

## Timeline
- **c. 3200 BC** — Thebes is first inhabited as a small trading post.
- **c. 2160 BC** — Theban rulers of the 11th Dynasty begin controlling Upper Egypt from Thebes.
- **c. 2050 BC** — Mentuhotep II reunites Egypt, establishing the Middle Kingdom with Thebes as a key city.
- **c. 1657–1549 BC** — During the Second Intermediate Period, Thebes becomes the capital after the Hyksos take Memphis.
- **c. 1550 BC** — Ahmose I expels the Hyksos, founding the New Kingdom with Thebes as its capital.
- **c. 1479–1458 BC** — Queen Hatshepsut renews trade networks, boosting Thebes's economy.
- **c. 1388–1350 BC** — Amenhotep III expands construction in Thebes to unprecedented levels.
- **c. 1351–1334 BC** — Akhenaten moves the capital to Amarna, temporarily abandoning Thebes.
- **c. 1279–1213 BC** — Ramesses II carries out extensive building projects in Thebes.
- **c. 1114 BC** — Investigations into tomb plundering reveal corruption in Thebes.
- **721 BC** — Kushite King Shabaka makes Thebes the capital of his unified kingdom.
- **663 BC** — The Assyrians plunder and destroy Thebes.

## Body

### Geography and Economy
Thebes was situated on the Nile in Upper Egypt, about 800 km south of the Delta. A significant change in the Nile's floodplain around 4,000 years ago increased arable land, supporting the city's growth. It was strategically located near trade routes, including the Wadi Hammamat to the Red Sea. The city's wealth, noted by Homer, came from its control of trade and agricultural resources.

### Demographics and Culture
Thebes grew from a small post to a major metropolis. By 1500 BC, it may have been the world's largest city with about 75,000 inhabitants. The city celebrated over sixty annual festivals, including the Beautiful Feast of Opet and the Beautiful Festival of the Valley.

### Rise to Capital (Old Kingdom to Second Intermediate Period)
Inhabited from c. 3200 BC, Thebes was the capital of the fourth Upper Egyptian nome. It rose to prominence during the First Intermediate Period when the 11th Dynasty ruled from there. Mentuhotep II reunified Egypt c. 2050 BC, starting the Middle Kingdom. Although the capital later moved north, Thebes remained a major religious center. During the Second Intermediate Period, Theban rulers resisted the Hyksos, eventually expelling them under Ahmose I.

### Height of Power (New Kingdom)
Ahmose I established Thebes as the capital of the New Kingdom. The city flourished as the center of the cult of Amun, whose temple at Karnak was massively expanded. Pharaohs like Thutmose I, Hatshepsut, and Amenhotep III built extensively. Amenhotep III's reign marked a peak in construction and wealth. A brief interruption occurred when Akhenaten moved the capital to Amarna, but it was returned after his death. Ramesses II later continued major building projects.

### Decline and Later History
Thebes declined politically after the New Kingdom. The High Priests of Amun gained local control during the Third Intermediate Period. The Kushites later made it their capital. In 663 BC, the Assyrians sacked the city. It remained an important religious center but never regained political power. Revolts against Ptolemaic rule were suppressed, and by the Roman period, it had dwindled to a village.

### Major Sites
The city's ruins span both banks of the Nile. The eastern bank housed the city and the great temples of Karnak and Luxor. The western bank contained the necropolis, including the Valley of the Kings, the Valley of the Queens, and mortuary temples like the Ramesseum and Hatshepsut's temple at Deir el-Bahri. The Karnak complex, dedicated to Amun, was built over more than 2,000 years.

## Terms
- **Nome**: An administrative division of ancient Egypt.
- **Theban Triad**: The group of three chief deities of Thebes: Amun, Mut, and Khonsu.
- **Amun**: The chief god of Thebes, later considered king of the gods.
- **Hyksos**: A people of Asiatic origin who ruled Lower Egypt during the Second Intermediate Period.
- **New Kingdom**: The period of ancient Egyptian history (c. 1550–1070 BC) when Egypt was an imperial power.
- **Mortuary Temple**: A temple dedicated to the cult of a deceased pharaoh, serving as his memorial.
- **Pylon**: A monumental gateway in the form of a pair of tapering towers.
- **God's Wife of Amun**: A powerful priestly title held by royal women in the Third Intermediate Period.

## Debates and open questions
- The exact etymology of the Greek name "Thebes" is doubtful, as the proposed Demotic Egyptian origin postdates Homer's use of the name.
- The population estimates for Thebes at its height (e.g., 75,000 in 1500 BC) are scholarly reconstructions.
- The circumstances surrounding the death of Mentuhotep IV, which ended the 11th Dynasty, are mysterious.

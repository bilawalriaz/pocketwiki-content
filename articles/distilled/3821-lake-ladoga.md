# Lake Ladoga

## Overview
Lake Ladoga is a large freshwater lake in northwestern Russia, near Saint Petersburg. It is the largest lake entirely within Europe and the 14th largest in the world by area. Its formation and history are tied to glacial activity, ancient trade routes, and significant geopolitical conflicts.

## Timeline
- **c. 130,000–115,000 years ago** — During the Last Interglacial, the lake basin was part of a marine channel between the Baltic and White Seas.
- **c. 17,000 years BP** — During the Last Glacial Maximum, the basin likely channeled ice from the Fennoscandian Ice Sheet.
- **12,500–11,500 BP** — Deglaciation occurred; the lake was initially part of the Baltic Ice Lake.
- **c. 9,500 BP** — Lake Onega began draining into Ladoga via the River Svir.
- **c. 8,800 BP** — Lake Ladoga became isolated from the Ancylus Lake (a freshwater stage of the Baltic).
- **c. 5,000 BP** — The River Vuoksi formed, raising the lake's level.
- **4,000–2,000 BP** — The River Neva formed, creating the lake's current outlet to the Gulf of Finland.
- **8th century** — The Norse emporium at Staraya Ladoga was established, defending the Volkhov mouth.
- **Early 14th century** — Fortresses of Korela and Oreshek were built on the lake's banks.
- **1617** — The Treaty of Stolbovo ceded the northern and western coasts to Sweden.
- **1721** — The Treaty of Nystad returned the coasts to Russia.
- **1940** — The Moscow Peace Treaty made the lake an internal Soviet basin after the Winter War.
- **1941–1944** — The lake provided the only supply route ("Road of Life") to besieged Leningrad.

## Body

### Etymology and Geography
The name "Ladoga" likely originated from a river name, possibly from the Finnish *Alodejoki* ('river of the lowlands'), and was borrowed into Old East Slavic via Old Norse. The lake has an average surface area of 17,891 km², is 219 km long, and has an average depth of 47 m. It drains into the Gulf of Finland via the Neva River and is part of the Volga–Baltic Waterway. Its basin includes about 50,000 lakes and 3,500 rivers.

### Geological History
The lake basin is a Proterozoic-age graben structure. It was shaped by Pleistocene glaciations, which deepened the depression. After the last ice age, the lake's connection to the Baltic Sea changed through various stages (Baltic Ice Lake, Yoldia Sea, Ancylus Lake). The modern outlet, the Neva River, formed between 4,000 and 2,000 years BP when lake waters broke through a threshold.

### Wildlife and Ecology
The lake is rich in fish, with 48 forms recorded, including roach, perch, and vendace. Commercial fishing peaked at 4,900 tonnes annually between 1945–1954 but declined due to overfishing; restrictions like a 1956 trawling ban helped recovery. The lake has its own subspecies of ringed seal, the Ladoga seal, and has become considerably eutrophicated since the 1960s.

### Human History
The lake was a vital medieval trade route for Varangians. Control was contested between the Novgorod Republic and Sweden, leading to fortress construction. In the 18th century, the Ladoga Canal was built to bypass its stormy waters. From 1812–1940, it was shared between Finland and Russia. During World War II, it was the sole lifeline for besieged Leningrad via the "Road of Life." After the war, it became an internal Soviet basin.

## Terms
- **Graben:** A depressed block of the Earth's crust bordered by parallel faults.
- **Eutrophication:** Excessive richness of nutrients in a lake, causing dense plant growth and oxygen depletion.
- **Varangians:** Norsemen, particularly Swedish Vikings, who traveled and traded along the river routes of Eastern Europe.
- **Road of Life:** The ice road across Lake Ladoga that was the only supply route to Leningrad during the Siege.
- **Endemic:** A species or subspecies native and restricted to a certain place, like the Ladoga seal.
- **Radiocarbon years BP:** A date expressed in "years before present," where "present" is defined as 1950 AD.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The exact timing of Lake Ladoga's isolation from the Baltic Sea's Yoldia Sea stage is uncertain.
- It is hypothesized but not proven that waters from the Litorina Sea (a brackish Baltic stage) occasionally invaded Ladoga between 7,000 and 5,000 BP.
- The precise date of the Neva River's formation is debated, with sediment evidence suggesting around 3,100 radiocarbon years BP.

# Jack Nicklaus

## Overview
Jack Nicklaus, nicknamed "the Golden Bear," is an American retired professional golfer and golf course designer widely considered one of the greatest golfers of all time. He holds the record for 18 major championship victories and 117 professional tournament wins, defining an era of golf through his skill, longevity, and sportsmanship.

## Body

### Early Life and Amateur Career
Nicklaus was born in 1940 in Columbus, Ohio, and took up golf at age 10. He was coached by Jack Grout and overcame a mild case of polio at 13. He became a dominant amateur, winning five straight Ohio State Junior titles, the U.S. Amateur in 1959 and 1961, and the NCAA Championship in 1961. His second-place finish at the 1960 U.S. Open, two shots behind Arnold Palmer, signaled his arrival. He married Barbara Bash in 1960 and turned professional in late 1961 to support his family.

### Professional Breakthrough and "The Big Three" (1962–1967)
Nicklaus won his first major at the 1962 U.S. Open, defeating Palmer in an 18-hole playoff. This victory, along with the rising fame of Nicklaus, Palmer, and Gary Player, led to the trio being dubbed "The Big Three." Nicklaus won the Masters and PGA Championship in 1963. He became the first player to win back-to-back Masters (1965, 1966) and completed the career grand slam in 1966 by winning The Open Championship at age 26. He set a 72-hole scoring record at the 1967 U.S. Open (275).

### Resurgence and Triple Career Grand Slam (1971–1978)
After a brief downturn, Nicklaus won the 1971 PGA Championship to become the first player to win all four majors twice. He won the Masters and U.S. Open in 1972, tying Bobby Jones's record of 13 majors. He surpassed Jones's mark in 1973. Nicklaus won his fifth Masters in 1975 and achieved the triple career grand slam in 1978 by winning The Open Championship, becoming the only player to win each major three times.

### Final Major and Senior Career (1980–1996)
Nicklaus won the U.S. Open and PGA Championship in 1980. His final major victory came at the 1986 Masters, where he became the oldest winner at age 46. He joined the Senior PGA Tour in 1990, winning 10 tournaments, including eight senior majors. His last official win was the 1996 Tradition.

### Course Design and Legacy
Nicklaus founded Nicklaus Design, one of the world's largest golf course design firms. He founded and hosts the Memorial Tournament. He received the Presidential Medal of Freedom (2005) and the Congressional Gold Medal (2015). He was instrumental in golf's return to the Olympics.

## Terms
- **Major Championships:** The four most prestigious annual men's golf tournaments: The Masters, U.S. Open, The Open Championship, and PGA Championship.
- **Career Grand Slam:** Winning all four major championships at least once in a career.
- **PGA Tour:** The main professional golf tour in North America.
- **Senior PGA Tour (PGA Tour Champions):** A tour for golfers aged 50 and over.
- **Ryder Cup:** A biennial men's golf competition between teams from the United States and Europe.
- **Fade:** A controlled golf shot that curves gently from left to right (for a right-handed golfer).
- **Greens in Regulation (GIR):** Hitting the green in one shot less than par (e.g., on a par 4, reaching the green in two shots).

# Fidel Castro

## Overview
Fidel Castro (1926–2016) was a Cuban revolutionary and politician who led Cuba from 1959 to 2011. He overthrew the Batista dictatorship, established the first communist state in the Western Hemisphere, and became a defining figure of the Cold War. His rule was marked by significant social achievements in healthcare and education, but also by political repression and economic hardship, making him one of the most polarizing leaders of the 20th century.

## Timeline
- **26 July 1953** — Failed attack on the Moncada Barracks; Castro imprisoned.
- **1955** — Released from prison; forms the 26th of July Movement in Mexico.
- **2 Dec 1956** — Returns to Cuba on the yacht *Granma* to launch a guerrilla war.
- **1 Jan 1959** — Batista flees Cuba; Castro's forces take power.
- **16 Feb 1959** — Castro is sworn in as Prime Minister.
- **17 Apr 1961** — Defeat of the US-backed Bay of Pigs Invasion.
- **Oct 1962** — Cuban Missile Crisis brings the world to the brink of nuclear war.
- **1976** — New constitution institutionalizes the Communist Party's role.
- **1979–1983** — Castro serves as President of the Non-Aligned Movement.
- **1991** — Collapse of the Soviet Union triggers Cuba's "Special Period" economic crisis.
- **31 Jul 2006** — Castro delegates duties to his brother Raúl due to illness.
- **25 Nov 2016** — Fidel Castro dies in Havana at age 90.

## Body

### Early Life and Revolutionary Path
Born in Birán to a wealthy Spanish farmer, Castro studied law at the University of Havana, where he became involved in anti-imperialist activism and leftist politics. After participating in failed rebellions in the Dominican Republic and Colombia, he planned the overthrow of Cuban president Fulgencio Batista. The 1953 attack on the Moncada Barracks failed, but it made Castro a political symbol. Imprisoned, he formed the 26th of July Movement. After his release in 1955, he trained revolutionaries in Mexico with his brother Raúl and Ernesto "Che" Guevara. Returning to Cuba in 1956, he led a guerrilla war from the Sierra Maestra mountains, which ultimately forced Batista to flee on 1 January 1959.

### Consolidation of Power and the Cold War
Castro assumed the role of Prime Minister and quickly consolidated power, overseeing trials and executions of Batista supporters. His government nationalized industry and implemented agrarian reform, alienating the United States. After the failed Bay of Pigs Invasion in 1961, Castro openly declared Cuba a socialist state. He aligned closely with the Soviet Union, allowing the placement of nuclear missiles on the island, which precipitated the 1962 Cuban Missile Crisis. Domestically, he established a one-party state under the Communist Party, with state control of the press and suppression of dissent.

### Internationalism and the "Special Period"
Castro positioned Cuba as a leader of Third World revolution, supporting Marxist movements in Latin America and Africa. Cuban troops fought in conflicts in Angola, Ethiopia, and elsewhere. His leadership of the Non-Aligned Movement (1979–1983) increased Cuba's global profile. The collapse of the Soviet Union in 1991 ended vital subsidies, plunging Cuba into a severe economic "Special Period." Castro responded by legalizing some private enterprise, promoting tourism, and embracing environmentalism. He forged new alliances with leftist Latin American governments, most notably Hugo Chávez's Venezuela, forming the Bolivarian Alliance.

### Final Years and Legacy
In 2006, failing health led Castro to transfer power to his brother Raúl, who was formally elected president in 2008. Fidel remained an influential figure until his death in 2016. His legacy is deeply contested. Supporters credit him with securing Cuban independence, achieving universal healthcare and literacy, and championing anti-imperialism. Critics condemn his authoritarian rule, human rights abuses, and the economic impoverishment of Cuba. He was the longest-serving non-royal head of state in the 20th and 21st centuries.

## Terms
*   **26th of July Movement (MR-26-7):** The revolutionary organization founded by Fidel Castro in 1955, named for the date of the Moncada Barracks attack, which led the Cuban Revolution.
*   **Bay of Pigs Invasion:** A failed 1961 landing on the coast of Cuba by US-trained Cuban exiles, intended to overthrow Castro's government.
*   **Cuban Missile Crisis:** A 1962 confrontation between the US and Soviet Union over Soviet ballistic missile deployment in Cuba, considered the closest the Cold War came to escalating into nuclear war.
*   **Special Period:** The severe economic crisis in Cuba following the dissolution of the Soviet Union in 1991, characterized by food shortages and energy rationing.
*   **Non-Aligned Movement (NAM):** An international organization of states not formally aligned with any major power bloc; Castro served as its president from 1979 to 1983.
*   **Committees for the Defense of the Revolution (CDR):** A nationwide network of neighborhood organizations established in 1960 to promote revolutionary ideals and report counter-revolutionary activity.
*   **Bolivarian Alliance for the Americas (ALBA):** A regional integration initiative founded in 2004 by Cuba and Venezuela to promote social, political, and economic cooperation among Latin American and Caribbean states.
*   **Marxism–Leninism:** The political ideology combining Karl Marx's theories of class struggle and socialism with Vladimir Lenin's theories on revolutionary vanguardism and the one-party state, which became Cuba's official state ideology.

## Debates and Open questions
*   **Nature of his rule:** Scholars debate whether Castro was a dictator who wielded absolute power or the leader of a participatory system where the Communist Party and mass organizations held real influence.
*   **Economic impact:** Assessments vary on whether his policies brought net social progress or economic ruin. Supporters point to world-class healthcare and education, while critics emphasize chronic shortages and low living standards.
*   **Role in Africa:** The scale and motivation of Cuba's military interventions in Africa, particularly Angola, are debated—whether they were primarily ideological internationalism or served Soviet strategic interests.
*   **Personal wealth:** Despite official denials, reports (e.g., from *Forbes*) have alleged Castro accumulated significant personal wealth, a claim his family and the Cuban government have vehemently rejected.

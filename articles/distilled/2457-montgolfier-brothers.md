# Montgolfier brothers

## Overview
The Montgolfier brothers, Joseph-Michel and Jacques-Étienne, were French paper manufacturers and aviation pioneers who invented the hot air balloon. Their work led to the first confirmed piloted human ascent in 1783, launching the age of ballooning and marking a foundational moment in the history of flight.

## Timeline

- **14 Dec 1782** — First test flight of a larger balloon; it floats 3 km but is destroyed.
- **4 Jun 1783** — First public demonstration at Annonay; balloon flies 2 km for 10 minutes.
- **19 Sep 1783** — First flight with living creatures (a sheep, duck, and rooster) at Versailles.
- **15 Oct 1783** — Étienne Montgolfier makes the first tethered human ascent.
- **21 Nov 1783** — First free flight by humans (Pilâtre de Rozier and the Marquis d'Arlandes).
- **1 Dec 1783** — Jacques Charles ascends in a competing hydrogen balloon.
- **1796** — Joseph-Michel invents the self-acting hydraulic ram.
- **1799** — Jacques-Étienne Montgolfier dies.
- **1810** — Joseph-Michel Montgolfier dies.

## Body

### Early Years and Family Business
Joseph-Michel and Jacques-Étienne were born into a family of paper manufacturers in Annonay, France. Joseph was the 12th child, described as a dreamer, while Étienne, the 15th, was more businesslike. After their eldest brother's death in 1772, Étienne returned from architectural training in Paris to run the family business, successfully incorporating modern Dutch papermaking innovations.

### Invention of the Hot Air Balloon
The brothers' aeronautical work began with Joseph's observations of laundry drying over a fire and his contemplation of an aerial assault on Gibraltar. In November 1782, he built a small taffeta-covered box that lifted off when paper was burned beneath it. He recruited Étienne, and together they built a larger model. Their first significant public demonstration was on 4 June 1783 at Annonay, where a large sackcloth-and-paper balloon flew for 10 minutes. To secure their claim, Étienne went to Paris for further demonstrations.

### Public Demonstrations and First Flights
In Paris, Étienne collaborated with wallpaper manufacturer Jean-Baptiste Réveillon to build larger, more elaborate balloons. On 19 September 1783, they flew the first living creatures—a sheep, duck, and rooster—from Versailles before King Louis XVI, proving flight was survivable. This led to permission for human flight. On 15 October 1783, Étienne made the first tethered human ascent. The landmark event came on 21 November 1783, when Pilâtre de Rozier and the Marquis d'Arlandes completed the first free flight by humans, traveling 9 km over Paris in 25 minutes. The brothers' father was ennobled by the king in December 1783.

### Competing Claims and Later Developments
The Montgolfiers' achievement is sometimes contested by claims that the Brazilian priest Bartolomeu de Gusmão invented a hot air balloon around 1709, though this is not widely recognized by aviation historians. Shortly after the Montgolfiers' success, Jacques Charles flew a hydrogen balloon in December 1783. The first female aeronaut, Élisabeth Thible, flew in a hot air balloon named *La Gustave* in June 1784.

### Other Inventions and Legacy
Beyond ballooning, the brothers invented a process for making transparent paper. In 1796, Joseph-Michel invented the self-acting hydraulic ram, a water pump for his paper mill. After Étienne's death in 1799 and Joseph-Michel's in 1810, the family paper company continued, eventually becoming the modern Canson company. The brothers were inducted into the International Air & Space Hall of Fame in 1983.

## Terms
*   **Montgolfière:** The style of hot air balloon invented by the Montgolfier brothers, using heated air for lift.
*   **Globe aérostatique:** French term for the balloon itself, meaning "aerostatic globe."
*   **Levity:** The property Joseph Montgolfier believed made smoke buoyant, which he called "Montgolfier Gas."
*   **Tethered flight:** An ascent where the balloon is secured to the ground by a rope, limiting its altitude and movement.
*   **Hydraulic ram:** A water pump invented by Joseph-Michel that uses the energy of flowing water to lift a portion of that water to a higher elevation.
*   **Fédération Aéronautique Internationale (FAI):** The world air sports federation, which does not generally recognize the pre-Montgolfier balloon claims.

## Debates and Open Questions
The primary historical debate concerns the claim that the hot air balloon was invented decades earlier by Bartolomeu de Gusmão. This claim is not generally accepted by mainstream aviation historians outside the Portuguese-speaking community.

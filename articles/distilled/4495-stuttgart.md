# Stuttgart

## Overview
Stuttgart is the capital and largest city of the German state of Baden-Württemberg, located in a fertile valley on the Neckar river. It is a major economic, cultural, and industrial hub, renowned as the "cradle of the automobile" and home to global corporations like Mercedes-Benz and Porsche. Its unique topography, high-tech economy, and significant immigrant population make it a distinctive and prosperous European metropolis.

## Timeline
- **c. 90 AD** — Roman castrum (Cannstatt Castrum) built to protect vineyards and roads.
- **950 AD** — Duke Liudolf of Swabia founds a stud farm ("Stuotgarten"), the city's origin.
- **1320** — Stuttgart is granted a charter and civic rights.
- **1488** — Stuttgart becomes the de facto residence of the Count of Württemberg.
- **1805** — Stuttgart is named capital of the newly formed Kingdom of Württemberg.
- **1886** — Karl Benz patents the first automobile; Daimler and Maybach industrialize it in Stuttgart.
- **1945** — French forces capture Stuttgart after WWII; city becomes capital of Württemberg-Baden.
- **1952** — Baden-Württemberg is formed, with Stuttgart as its capital.
- **1974 & 2006** — Stuttgart hosts FIFA World Cup matches.
- **2010** — Major protests occur against the Stuttgart 21 rail project.

## Body

### History and Foundation
Stuttgart's history begins with a Roman castrum at Bad Cannstatt (c. 90 AD). The city itself was founded in 950 AD by Duke Liudolf of Swabia as a stud farm for warhorses, giving it its name (Old High German *Stuotgarten*). It grew steadily, overshadowed by nearby Cannstatt, and was granted civic rights in 1320. Its fortunes became tied to the House of Württemberg, which made it the capital of their county, duchy, and kingdom from the 15th century until 1918. Despite devastation in the Thirty Years' War and heavy Allied bombing in WWII, the city recovered to become the capital of the new state of Baden-Württemberg in 1952.

### Economy and Industry
Stuttgart is a powerhouse of high-tech industry, particularly automotive. It is considered the birthplace of the automobile, with Karl Benz, Gottlieb Daimler, and Wilhelm Maybach conducting pioneering work there. Today, it hosts the headquarters of Mercedes-Benz Group, Porsche, and automotive parts giants Bosch and Mahle. The region has Germany's highest density of research institutions and files the most patents. The Stuttgart Stock Exchange is Germany's second-largest, and the city is a major financial center.

### Geography and Climate
The city is uniquely spread across hills, valleys, and parks in a fertile basin known as the "Stuttgarter Kessel." It is described as being "between forest and vines." Its oceanic climate features warm summers and chilly winters. Due to its basin location, Stuttgart is prone to air stagnation; it has become a case study in urban climatology for using "ventilation corridors"—preserved open spaces that allow cooler air to flow into the city core.

### Demographics and Society
Stuttgart has a population of about 630,000, making it Germany's sixth-largest city. It is a city of high immigration; 47% of its population has a foreign background, with significant communities from Turkey, Italy, Greece, and Croatia. Historically Protestant since the Reformation, the religious landscape is now diverse, with 49.8% of inhabitants belonging to other groups or no religion. The city has a very low unemployment rate compared to national averages.

### Culture and Landmarks
Stuttgart is a major cultural center, home to the State Theatre (opera, ballet, theatre) and the State Gallery. Key landmarks include the Old and New Castles, the Stiftskirche, and the Mercedes-Benz and Porsche Museums. The city is famous for its viticulture, with vineyards within the urban area, and hosts the large annual Cannstatter Volksfest. Its public transport includes the unique Zahnradbahn (rack railway) and the Stadtbahn light rail system.

## Terms
*   **Stuttgarter Kessel:** The "Stuttgart Cauldron," the fertile basin in which the city is located.
*   **Stuotgarten:** The Old High German name meaning "stud farm," the city's etymological root.
*   **Gleichschaltung:** The Nazi policy of forced coordination, which stripped Stuttgart of its political importance as a state capital.
*   **Wirtschaftswunder:** The "economic miracle," the rapid reconstruction and growth of West Germany's economy after WWII.
*   **Gastarbeiter:** "Guest workers," economic migrants recruited from countries like Italy, Greece, and Turkey during the post-war boom.
*   **Stadtbahn:** Stuttgart's light rail system, which runs underground in the city center.
*   **Zahnradbahn:** A unique urban rack railway in Stuttgart, powered by electricity.
*   **Stuttgart 21:** A major, controversial project to convert the city's main train station from a terminus to an underground through-station.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Stuttgart 21:** The massive rail project has been a source of intense public debate and protest, notably in 2010, over its cost, disruption, and the destruction of historic parkland.
*   **Urban Climate:** The city's basin geography creates challenges with heat and air pollution, making the effectiveness and preservation of its ventilation corridors an ongoing planning issue.
*   **Post-War Reconstruction:** The controversial modernist rebuilding of the city center under Mayor Arnulf Klett, which demolished many historic buildings, remains a point of historical critique.

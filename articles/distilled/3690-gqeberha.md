# Gqeberha

## Overview
Gqeberha, formerly Port Elizabeth, is a major seaport and the most populous city in South Africa's Eastern Cape province. Founded in 1820 as a British colonial settlement, it grew into the region's economic and cultural hub. The city's history reflects broader South African narratives, from prehistoric human settlement and colonial expansion to apartheid-era resistance and modern economic development.

## Body

### Prehistory and Early Inhabitants
The area shows continuous human occupation for tens of thousands of years. Archaeological sites like Howieson's Poort (dating to 65,000–62,000 years ago) provide crucial evidence for early modern human behaviour. Hunters and gatherers ancestral to the San settled the area at least 10,000 years ago, later being assimilated by agriculturalist populations ancestral to the Xhosa people around 2,000 years ago.

### Colonial Foundation and Growth
The first European visitors were Portuguese explorers in the late 15th century. The area became part of the Cape Colony, with Fort Frederick built in 1799. The modern city was founded in 1820 when 4,000 British settlers arrived to strengthen the border with Xhosa territories. Named Port Elizabeth after the wife of Acting Governor Sir Rufane Donkin, the settlement grew rapidly. The completion of a railway to Kimberley in 1873 stimulated trade and population growth, earning it the nickname "the Liverpool of South Africa." It was granted municipal status in 1861.

### Apartheid Era and Resistance
Under apartheid, the city was a site of forced removals and racial segregation, with non-white populations relocated from areas like South End from 1965. It was also a centre of resistance. The 1952 Defiance Campaign saw the first mass civil disobedience against apartheid laws, with over 2,000 arrested in the city. In the 1980s, effective consumer boycotts organized by the Port Elizabeth Black Civic Organisation (PEBCO) crippled white-owned businesses and pressured the government. The city was also where anti-apartheid leader Steve Biko was tortured in 1977 before his death.

### Modern Development
Since the end of apartheid, the city has focused on economic development. The Coega Industrial Development Zone (IDZ) has attracted significant investment, particularly in the automotive sector, with plants for manufacturers like Volkswagen, Ford, and Isuzu. The formation of the Nelson Mandela Bay Metropolitan Municipality in 2001 consolidated the city with neighbouring towns. Major events like the 2010 FIFA World Cup and 2013 Africa Cup of Nations boosted its profile. The city's name was officially changed to Gqeberha in 2021, after the Xhosa name for the Baakens River.

### Geography and Climate
Located on Algoa Bay, the city has a mild oceanic climate (Cfb) with warm summers and cool winters. It sits at the confluence of five of South Africa's seven biomes, giving it high biodiversity. The area has faced severe water challenges, including a drought declared a disaster in 2019, leading to emergency interventions and water restrictions.

## Terms
- **Apartheid**: The system of institutionalized racial segregation and discrimination enforced in South Africa from 1948 to the early 1990s.
- **Defiance Campaign**: A 1952 mass civil disobedience movement against unjust apartheid laws, organized by the ANC and SAIC.
- **Consumer Boycott**: A form of protest where participants refuse to purchase goods or services from targeted businesses, used effectively in the 1980s to pressure white-owned institutions.
- **Coega Industrial Development Zone (IDZ)**: A major industrial and economic development area near the city, now a special economic zone (SEZ).
- **Nelson Mandela Bay Metropolitan Municipality**: The administrative area governing Gqeberha and surrounding towns, formed in 2001.
- **Howieson's Poort**: An archaeological site near the city dating to 65,000–62,000 years ago, significant for early evidence of modern human behaviour.
- **Baakens River**: The river flowing through the city, after which the name Gqeberha is derived.
- **Blue-flag beaches**: Beaches certified for meeting high environmental and quality standards.

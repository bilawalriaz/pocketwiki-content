# History of the Peloponnesian War

## Overview
*The History of the Peloponnesian War* is an unfinished historical account of the war (431–404 BC) between the Peloponnesian League (led by Sparta) and the Delian League (led by Athens). Written by the Athenian historian and general Thucydides, it is considered a foundational work of Western history and political realism, renowned for its analytical method and focus on human causation.

## Timeline
- **c. 460 BC** — Thucydides is born in Athens.
- **431–404 BC** — The Peloponnesian War is fought.
- **424 BC** — Thucydides is exiled from Athens after a military failure.
- **411 BC** — The narrative of the *History* ends abruptly mid-sentence.
- **c. 400 BC** — Thucydides likely dies, leaving the work unfinished.
- **1628** — Thomas Hobbes publishes the first English translation.

## Body

### Historical Method and Style
Thucydides pioneered a rigorous historical method. He employed strict chronology, recording events by year (divided into summer campaigns and winter periods), a contrast to his predecessor Herodotus. His work extensively features speeches, including famous addresses like Pericles' Funeral Oration. While some speeches he heard personally, others were reconstructed based on eyewitness accounts or his judgment of "what was called for in each situation," a practice that raises questions about their exact fidelity.

Despite being an Athenian participant, Thucydides strives for neutrality, stating his work was "done to last for ever." However, scholars debate this, with some arguing for a pro-Athenian bias or an epic literary agenda. A key innovation is his secular approach: the gods play no active role in causing events. History is driven by human choices, actions, and the interplay of justice and power, though religion remains a critical motivator for the characters, especially the Spartans.

### Subject Matter and Themes
The *History* focuses on the causes and events of the Peloponnesian War, tracing the primary cause to the growth of Athenian power and the alarm it inspired in Sparta. It details the war's degenerative social effects, the lawlessness of civil strife (stasis), and early examples of realpolitik, as in the Melian Dialogue. Thucydides emphasizes the importance of naval supremacy for empire, highlighting technological developments like the trireme. He also provides early scientific observations, such as correlating an earthquake with a tsunami in the Malian Gulf.

### Composition and Interpretation
The work is notoriously dense and ends in 411 BC, six years before the war's conclusion. This has led to the "strata of composition" debate: "analysts" see contradictions as evidence of evolving views, while "unitarians" seek to reconcile all passages. Thucydides is reticent about his sources, creating a seamless narrative but leaving scholars to infer his methods, which likely involved interviews and inference from actions. His work has been immensely influential, studied by figures from Thomas Hobbes to Woodrow Wilson, though its reputation has fluctuated over time.

## Terms
*   **Peloponnesian War:** The protracted conflict (431–404 BC) between the Peloponnesian League (Sparta-led) and the Delian League (Athens-led).
*   **Delian League:** An alliance of Greek city-states led by Athens, which evolved into the Athenian Empire.
*   **Peloponnesian League:** An alliance of Greek city-states led by Sparta.
*   **Trireme:** A warship with three banks of oars, dominant in the Mediterranean for centuries, crucial for naval power.
*   **Realpolitik:** Politics based on practical and material factors rather than theoretical or ethical objectives.
*   **Stasis:** Civil strife or factional conflict within a city-state, a major theme in Thucydides' account of war's social decay.
*   **Archaeology:** Thucydides' term for his preliminary review of early Greek history in Book 1.
*   **Pentecontaetia:** The fifty-year period (c. 479–431 BC) between the Persian Wars and the Peloponnesian War, covered in Book 1.
*   **Oligarchy:** A form of government in which power rests with a small number of people, contrasted with democracy.

## Debates and Open Questions
Scholars debate whether Thucydides achieved true objectivity or had a pro-Athenian, oligarchic bias. The "strata of composition" debate questions whether the text represents a single, unified work or reflects evolving views over time. The reliability and purpose of his reconstructed speeches remain a central issue of interpretation. Finally, the abrupt ending of the work has led to speculation about his intended final form and conclusions.

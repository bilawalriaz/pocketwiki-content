# Ukraine

## Overview
Ukraine is a large Eastern European country, the second-largest in Europe by area, with a complex history shaped by its position between major powers. It gained independence from the Soviet Union in 1991 and has since pursued a path toward Euro-Atlantic integration, a process dramatically altered by the 2022 Russian invasion. Its fertile agricultural lands, strategic location, and ongoing conflict make it a significant player in global geopolitics and food security.

- **2022** — Russia launches a full-scale invasion of Ukraine.

## Body

### Early History and Kievan Rus'
The territory of modern Ukraine has been inhabited since at least 32,000 BC. In the Middle Ages, it became a core area for early Slavic expansion. The state of Kievan Rus', emerging in the 9th century, became the largest and most powerful realm in Europe during its 10th-11th century "Golden Age" under rulers like Vladimir the Great and Yaroslav the Wise. It fragmented thereafter and was devastated by the Mongol invasion in the 13th century.

### Foreign Domination and the Cossack Hetmanate
For centuries, Ukrainian lands were contested and ruled by external powers, including the Grand Duchy of Lithuania, the Kingdom of Poland, and the Russian and Austrian Empires. In the 17th century, the Cossack Hetmanate emerged as a semi-autonomous state in central Ukraine. After the Khmelnytsky Uprising (1648), it allied with the Russian Tsar but was eventually partitioned and absorbed by the Russian Empire by the late 18th century.

### Nationalism, Revolution, and Soviet Rule
Ukrainian nationalism grew in the 19th century. Following the Russian Revolution, the Ukrainian People's Republic was briefly established but was defeated by Bolshevik forces, leading to the creation of the Ukrainian Soviet Socialist Republic (Ukrainian SSR) in 1922. Under Stalin, Ukraine suffered the Holodomor famine (1932–33) and the Great Purge, which decimated its intelligentsia. During World War II, Ukraine was a major battleground, suffering immense civilian and military losses under Nazi occupation.

### Independence and Modern Conflict
Ukraine declared independence in 1991, confirmed by a referendum. The transition to a market economy was difficult, marked by corruption and oligarchic influence. Political divisions between pro-Western and pro-Russian orientations led to the Orange Revolution (2004) and the Euromaidan Revolution (2014). Russia responded by annexing Crimea and fomenting war in the Donbas region. The 2022 Russian invasion marked a full-scale escalation of this conflict, causing massive destruction and a humanitarian crisis.

### Government and International Relations
Ukraine is a unitary semi-presidential republic. Its constitution, adopted in 1996, was amended in 2019 to enshrine strategic goals of joining the EU and NATO. Following the 2022 invasion, Ukraine was granted EU candidate status. Its military, one of the largest in the world, has been defending the country with significant international support. Ukraine is a member of the UN, Council of Europe, and WTO.

### Economy and Demographics
Ukraine has a transition economy with the lowest nominal GDP per capita in Europe as of 2024. Agriculture is a key sector; it is a major global exporter of grain, sunflower oil, and other products, though the war has severely disrupted production and threatened global food security. The population was over 41 million before 2022 but has declined significantly due to the war, with millions displaced internally and abroad. Ethnic Ukrainians form about 78% of the population, with Russians as the largest minority.

## Terms
*   **Kievan Rus':** A medieval East Slavic state, considered a cultural ancestor of modern Ukraine, Russia, and Belarus.
*   **Cossack Hetmanate:** A semi-autonomous Cossack state in central Ukraine from the 17th to 18th centuries.
*   **Holodomor:** A man-made famine in Soviet Ukraine (1932–33) that killed millions, recognized by some countries as a genocide.
*   **Euromaidan:** A series of protests in 2013–2014 against President Yanukovych's decision to reject an EU association agreement, leading to his ouster.
*   **Russo-Ukrainian War:** The ongoing conflict that began in 2014 with Russia's annexation of Crimea and war in Donbas, escalating to a full-scale invasion in 2022.
*   **Oligarchs:** A small group of extremely wealthy individuals who gained control of key industries during Ukraine's post-Soviet privatization, wielding significant political influence.
*   **Budapest Memorandum:** A 1994 agreement in which Ukraine agreed to give up its nuclear weapons in exchange for security assurances from Russia, the US, and the UK.

## Debates and open questions
*   The interpretation of the name "Ukraine" (as "borderland" vs. "region/country") and the political implications of using the definite article "the Ukraine."
*   The historical origins of Kievan Rus' and the relative roles of Varangian (Scandinavian) and East Slavic peoples in its formation.
*   The classification of the Holodomor as a genocide versus a broader Stalinist policy of repression.
*   The degree of agency and collaboration of Ukrainian nationalist groups (like the OUN and UPA) with Nazi Germany during World War II.
*   The future trajectory of Ukraine's integration with the EU and NATO, especially in the context of the ongoing war with Russia.

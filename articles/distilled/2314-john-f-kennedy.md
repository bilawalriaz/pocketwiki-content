# John F. Kennedy

## Overview
John F. Kennedy was the 35th president of the United States, serving from 1961 until his assassination in 1963. He was the youngest person elected president and the first Catholic to hold the office. His presidency, set against the Cold War, was marked by high-stakes foreign policy crises, the launch of major domestic initiatives, and a legacy shaped by his tragic death.

## Timeline
- **May 29, 1917** — Born in Brookline, Massachusetts.
- **1940** — Graduated from Harvard University.
- **1943** — Commanded PT-109 in the Pacific; survived its sinking and rescued crewmates.
- **1947–1953** — Served as a U.S. Representative from Massachusetts.
- **1953–1960** — Served as a U.S. Senator from Massachusetts.
- **1957** — Published *Profiles in Courage*, winning a Pulitzer Prize.
- **November 8, 1960** — Elected president, defeating Richard Nixon.
- **April 17, 1961** — Bay of Pigs Invasion in Cuba fails.
- **October 16–28, 1962** — Cuban Missile Crisis brings the world to the brink of nuclear war.
- **June 26, 1963** — Delivers famous "Ich bin ein Berliner" speech in West Berlin.
- **July 2, 1963** — Signs the Limited Nuclear Test Ban Treaty.
- **November 22, 1963** — Assassinated in Dallas, Texas.

## Body

### Early Life and Military Service
Born into a prominent Irish-American family, Kennedy graduated from Harvard in 1940. He joined the U.S. Naval Reserve in 1941. During World War II, he commanded PT boats in the Pacific. His heroism after his boat, PT-109, was sunk in 1943—towing an injured crewman to safety—made him a public figure and earned him the Navy and Marine Corps Medal. Chronic back problems from the incident plagued him for life.

### Congressional Career
Kennedy served in the U.S. House of Representatives from 1947 to 1953 and the U.S. Senate from 1953 to 1960. In the Senate, he focused on regional economic issues and co-authored the labor reform bill resulting from the McClellan Committee investigations into union racketeering. His 1956 book, *Profiles in Courage*, won a Pulitzer Prize.

### 1960 Election and "New Frontier"
Kennedy won the 1960 Democratic nomination and the general election in a very close race against Vice President Richard Nixon. The first televised presidential debates were a pivotal moment, with Kennedy's telegenic performance giving him an advantage. He called his domestic agenda the "New Frontier," focusing on economic growth, tax cuts, and social welfare, though many of these proposals faced congressional opposition during his term.

### Foreign Policy and the Cold War
Kennedy's presidency was dominated by Cold War tensions. He authorized the failed Bay of Pigs Invasion in 1961 and later faced the Cuban Missile Crisis in 1962, where a U.S. naval "quarantine" of Cuba led to the removal of Soviet nuclear missiles. He increased U.S. military advisers in South Vietnam and supported a coup against South Vietnamese leader Ngô Đình Diệm in 1963. He established the Peace Corps and the Alliance for Progress for Latin America. His administration negotiated the Limited Nuclear Test Ban Treaty in 1963.

### Domestic Policy and Civil Rights
Kennedy's domestic record included the Area Redevelopment Act, the Housing Act of 1961, and the Equal Pay Act of 1963. He proposed a major tax cut to stimulate the economy. Initially cautious on civil rights to avoid alienating Southern Democrats, Kennedy was pushed by the civil rights movement to take stronger action. In June 1963, he delivered a major televised address calling for comprehensive civil rights legislation, which was passed after his death as the Civil Rights Act of 1964.

### Assassination and Legacy
Kennedy was assassinated in Dallas on November 22, 1963. Lee Harvey Oswald was arrested for the killing but was himself murdered two days later. The Warren Commission concluded Oswald acted alone, a finding that remains disputed. Kennedy's death elevated his legacy, and many of his legislative proposals were enacted under President Lyndon B. Johnson. He is often remembered through the mythic "Camelot" image of his presidency.

## Terms
- **Flexible Response**: Kennedy's defense strategy that relied on multiple options for responding to the Soviet Union, moving away from the Eisenhower-era policy of massive retaliation.
- **Peace Corps**: A volunteer program run by the United States government whose official mission is "to provide social and economic development abroad through technical assistance, while promoting mutual understanding between Americans and target populations."
- **Alliance for Progress**: A program initiated by Kennedy in 1961 to establish economic cooperation between the U.S. and Latin America.
- **Bay of Pigs Invasion**: A failed landing operation on the southwestern coast of Cuba in April 1961 by Cuban exiles who opposed Fidel Castro's Cuban Revolution.
- **Cuban Missile Crisis**: A 13-day confrontation between the United States and the Soviet Union initiated by the discovery of American ballistic missile deployment sites in Cuba.
- **New Frontier**: The term used by Kennedy to describe his domestic agenda, which aimed to address economic stagnation, education, health care, and civil rights.
- **Limited Nuclear Test Ban Treaty**: A 1963 treaty that prohibited nuclear weapons tests in the atmosphere, in outer space, and underwater.
- **Warren Commission**: The President's Commission on the Assassination of President Kennedy, established by Lyndon B. Johnson to investigate the assassination.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Assassination Conspiracy**: The Warren Commission's conclusion that Lee Harvey Oswald acted alone is widely disputed. A 1979 House committee concluded Kennedy was "probably assassinated as a result of a conspiracy," though subsequent acoustic analysis challenged this finding.
- **Vietnam Policy**: Historians debate whether Kennedy would have escalated U.S. involvement in Vietnam after 1964 or withdrawn, based on conflicting statements from his advisers and a 1963 withdrawal order (NSAM 263).
- **"Camelot" Legacy**: The mythic, idealized image of the Kennedy presidency is often contrasted with the more complex reality of his legislative struggles and personal life, raising questions about how historical memory is shaped.
- **Health and Medication**: Kennedy's severe chronic health problems and use of various medications, including steroids and amphetamines, were concealed during his lifetime and raise questions about their potential impact on his decision-making.

# Antananarivo

## Overview
Antananarivo is the capital and largest city of Madagascar, situated at 1,280 m (4,199 ft) in the Central Highlands. It has been the island's primary political, economic, and cultural center since the 18th century, serving as the capital of the Merina Kingdom, French colonial administration, and independent Madagascar. The city's history reflects the broader political and social transformations of the nation, from pre-colonial kingdoms to modern urban challenges.

## Timeline
- **c. 1610** — Founded by Merina King Andrianjaka, who expelled the Vazimba and built a rova (fortified royal compound).
- **1675–1710** — Renamed Antananarivo ("City of the Thousand") by King Andriamasinavalona.
- **1710** — Kingdom of Imerina splits; Antananarivo becomes capital of the southern quadrant.
- **1794** — King Andrianampoinimerina reunites Imerina and restores Antananarivo as the capital.
- **1820s** — British missionaries introduce brick-making and public schools.
- **1869** — First brick house built, establishing the "trano gasy" (Malagasy house) style.
- **1894** — French military invades; city becomes colonial capital (Tananarive).
- **1960** — Madagascar gains independence; Antananarivo remains the capital.
- **1995** — Fire destroys most of the Rova's royal palaces.
- **2009** — Political crisis leads to unconstitutional change of national leadership.
- **2023** — Crush incident at Mahamasina Stadium kills at least 12 people.

## Body

### Founding and Kingdom of Imerina
Antananarivo was established c. 1610 when Merina King Andrianjaka seized the hilltop village of Analamanga ("Blue Forest") from the Vazimba. He built a rova, which expanded into the royal palaces of the Kingdom of Imerina. The city was renamed Antananarivo in the late 17th century. After a period of civil war following the 1710 split of Imerina, King Andrianampoinimerina reunited the kingdom in 1794, making Antananarivo its capital. His son, Radama I, expanded Merina control over much of the island, and Antananarivo became the de facto capital of Madagascar.

### Colonial and Post-Independence Development
The French colonized Madagascar in 1897, retaining Antananarivo as the capital and transcribing its name as Tananarive. They introduced modern infrastructure, including tunnels, paved streets, sewer systems, and electricity. The city expanded significantly, with planned districts like the Avenue de l'Indépendance and the railway station at Soarano. After independence in 1960, population growth accelerated, leading to uncontrolled urban sprawl, infrastructure strain, and the emergence of impoverished, flood-prone neighborhoods in the lower city.

### Urban Structure and Challenges
The city is built on three intersecting ridges, with the historic Rova at the summit. It is divided into the prestigious haute ville (upper town), the densely populated ville moyenne (middle town), and the flood-prone ville basse (lower town). The Commune Urbaine d'Antananarivo (CUA) manages the city but faces severe challenges from limited funds, mismanagement, and explosive population growth, leading to problems with traffic, waste, pollution, and inadequate public services. Crime has increased since 2009, and the city lacks a comprehensive strategy for reduction.

### Economy and Demographics
Antananarivo is the economic hub of Madagascar, with industries including soap, textiles, and food processing. However, unemployment and poverty are growing, and many residents work in the informal sector. The city's population was 1,274,225 in the 2018 census, with a metropolitan area approaching three million. It is home to all eighteen Malagasy ethnic groups, with the Merina forming the majority. Rural migration drives rapid growth, straining housing, water, and sanitation systems.

## Terms
- **Rova**: A fortified royal compound or palace complex.
- **Trano gasy**: A traditional two-story brick house with a veranda, style introduced in the 19th century.
- **Fady**: A Malagasy taboo or prohibition.
- **Fanampoana**: A system of statute labor used for public works.
- **Hova**: The commoner class in the traditional Merina social hierarchy.
- **Andriana**: The noble class in the traditional Merina social hierarchy.
- **Andevo**: The slave class in the traditional Merina social hierarchy.
- **Famadihana**: A Malagasy ceremony of reburial and celebration of ancestors.
- **Commune Urbaine d'Antananarivo (CUA)**: The municipal governing body of the city.

## Debates and Open questions
- The exact founding date of Antananarivo is debated, with accounts varying between c. 1610 and 1625.
- The effectiveness of the CUA's governance and urban planning strategies is a persistent issue, hampered by debt, corruption, and high staff turnover.
- The impact of the 2009 political crisis on the city's administrative autonomy and development trajectory remains significant.

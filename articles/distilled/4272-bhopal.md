# Bhopal

## Overview
Bhopal is the capital of Madhya Pradesh, India, known as the "City of Lakes." It is a major economic and educational hub with a complex history, from a princely state ruled by female nawabs to the site of the 1984 Union Carbide gas disaster, the world's worst industrial accident.

## Timeline
- **c. 11th century** — Folklore attributes founding to Paramara king Bhoja.
- **1707** — Modern city established by Dost Mohammad Khan, founding the Bhopal State.
- **1737** — Marathas defeat Mughal-Nawab forces in the Battle of Bhopal.
- **1818** — Bhopal State signs a treaty with the British East India Company.
- **1819–1926** — The state is ruled by four successive female Begums.
- **1947** — India gains independence; Bhopal is one of the last princely states to accede.
- **1949** — Bhopal State merges with the Union of India on 1 June.
- **1984** — Union Carbide pesticide plant leaks toxic gases, causing a massive disaster.
- **2003** — Bhimbetka Caves are declared a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
- **2017–2019** — Bhopal is rated India's cleanest state capital for three consecutive years.
- **2025** — First stage of the Bhoj Metro rail system commences operations.

## Body

### History and Governance
Bhopal's modern history began in 1707 with Dost Mohammad Khan, a Pashtun soldier who established the Bhopal State. After Maratha control in the 18th century, it became a British princely state in 1818. A unique period followed from 1819 to 1926, when the state was ruled by four female Begums, who introduced railways, waterworks, and a postal system. After Indian independence in 1947, Bhopal was the second-largest Muslim-ruled princely state. Following public agitation, it acceded to India on 1 June 1949. Today, it is the administrative headquarters for its district and division, with governance handled by the Bhopal Municipal Corporation (BMC) and state legislative bodies.

### The 1984 Disaster
In December 1984, a Union Carbide pesticide plant leaked approximately 32 tons of toxic gases, including methyl isocyanate (MIC). The official death toll was initially around 4,000, though other estimates suggest fatalities were significantly higher (15,000). The disaster caused widespread immediate and long-term health issues, including neurological disabilities and birth disorders. The site remains contaminated with toxic waste, and the environmental cleanup and victim rehabilitation continue to be a major socio-political and logistical challenge. Legal efforts for additional compensation are ongoing.

### Geography and Climate
Bhopal is located on the Malwa plateau with an average elevation of 500 meters. The city is defined by its numerous lakes, including the 11th-century Upper Lake (Bada Talab) and Lower Lake. It has a humid subtropical climate with hot summers (peaking in May), a monsoon season from June to September, and cool winters. The city has been recognized for its cleanliness and air quality.

### Economy and Infrastructure
Bhopal has a strong industrial base with manufacturing in pharmaceuticals, automobiles, and textiles. Major industrial units include Bharat Heavy Electricals Limited (BHEL) and the industrial suburb of Mandideep. The city is also a growing IT hub. Transport is served by Raja Bhoj International Airport, major railway junctions like Bhopal Junction and Habibganj (Rani Kamalapati) station, and a network of national highways. A Bus Rapid Transit System (BRTS) operated from 2013 until its discontinuation in 2023.

### Education and Culture
Bhopal is a major educational center, hosting numerous institutes of national importance such as IISER, MANIT, AIIMS, and NLIU. The city's culture reflects its history, with major Hindu festivals like Diwali and Muslim festivals like Eid being widely celebrated. Architectural landmarks include the Taj-ul-Masajid mosque and the Bharat Bhavan cultural complex. Nearby UNESCO World Heritage Sites include the Bhimbetka rock shelters and the Sanchi Stupas.

## Terms
- **Princely State:** A semi-sovereign territory in the British Indian Empire ruled by a local ruler under British suzerainty.
- **Begum:** A female Muslim ruler or noblewoman, specifically referring to the four women who ruled Bhopal State.
- **Instrument of Accession:** A legal document executed by princely state rulers to accede to the Dominion of India.
- **Methyl Isocyanate (MIC):** A highly toxic chemical compound that was the primary gas leaked in the 1984 Bhopal disaster.
- **BRTS (Bus Rapid Transit System):** A high-capacity bus-based public transport system with dedicated lanes, operational in Bhopal from 2013 to 2023.
- **Malwa Plateau:** A volcanic plateau in west-central India, on which Bhopal is located.
- **Ramsar Site:** A wetland site designated to be of international importance under the Ramsar Convention, like the Bhoj Wetland.
- **Suzerainty:** A relationship in which one state has control over the international actions of a client state, while allowing it internal autonomy.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The exact death toll of the 1984 Bhopal disaster remains disputed, with official figures challenged by higher independent estimates.
- The long-term environmental contamination of the soil and groundwater near the former Union Carbide plant site is an ongoing challenge, with debates over responsibility for cleanup between Dow Chemical (which acquired Union Carbide) and government authorities.
- The effectiveness and future of urban transport projects like the now-discontinued BRTS and the newly launched metro system are subjects of ongoing evaluation.

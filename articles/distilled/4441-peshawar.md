# Peshawar

## Overview
Peshawar is the capital and largest city of Pakistan's Khyber Pakhtunkhwa province, with a population of over 1.9 million. Located in the Valley of Peshawar near the Khyber Pass, it is one of the oldest continuously inhabited cities in South Asia, serving as a vital political, cultural, and economic hub for millennia due to its strategic position on trade routes between Central Asia and the Indian subcontinent.

## Body

### Ancient and Medieval History
Peshawar's recorded history begins in the 6th century BC. It was established as Purushapura on the Gandhara Plains. The city rose to prominence as the primary capital of the Kushan Empire in the 1st century CE, with an estimated population of 120,000. Its golden age ended with Sasanian and White Hun invasions, which damaged its Buddhist monuments. Islam was introduced in the 7th century. The Ghaznavids established Muslim rule in 1001 CE. Subsequently, the city was ruled by the Delhi Sultanate, Mongols, and various Pashtun tribes before the Mughal conquest in 1526.

### Mughal, Durrani, and Sikh Rule
Under the Mughals, Peshawar was an important trading center on the Grand Trunk Road. The city's name changed from Begram to Peshawar during Akbar's reign. After Mughal decline, it was captured by the Afsharids in 1738 and then became part of the Durrani Empire in 1747, serving as its winter capital. The Sikh Empire captured it in 1823, leading to economic decline and the desecration of landmarks like the Mohabbat Khan Mosque.

### British Raj and Colonial Era
The British captured Peshawar in 1849. They established it as a frontier headquarters, built a cantonment, and linked it by railway. In 1901, Peshawar became the capital of the new North-West Frontier Province. The colonial period saw the rise of both Hindkowan and Pashtun intellectual centers and a non-violent resistance movement led by Ghaffar Khan. A major incident occurred in 1930 when British troops fired on protesters in Qissa Khwani Bazaar.

### Modern Era and Demographics
After joining Pakistan in 1947, Peshawar's demographics shifted significantly. The departure of many Hindu and Sikh residents weakened the historical Hindko-speaking community. From the 1960s, it was a base for CIA operations and a stop on the Hippie trail. The Soviet–Afghan War (1980s) brought a massive influx of Afghan refugees, straining infrastructure and transforming Peshawar into a predominantly Pashto-speaking city. Since the 2000s, it has been severely affected by Tehrik-i-Taliban violence, including the 2014 school massacre. Security improved after Operation Zarb-e-Azb in 2014.

### Economy and Infrastructure
Peshawar's economy is historically linked to the Khyber Pass trade route. It is relatively poor compared to national averages. The city faces constraints from political instability, poor infrastructure, and energy shortages. The China Pakistan Economic Corridor (CPEC) aims to improve this. Transportation includes the Grand Trunk Road, motorways (M-1), Bacha Khan International Airport, and the modern BRT Peshawar rapid bus service launched in 2020.

## Terms
- **Gandhara**: An ancient region and civilization centered in the Peshawar valley, known for its Greco-Buddhist art.
- **Kushan Empire**: A syncretic empire that ruled from the 1st to 3rd centuries CE, with Peshawar as its primary capital.
- **Hindko**: An Indo-Aryan language historically dominant in Peshawar's urban trade and business community.
- **Pashto**: An Iranian language now the major language of Peshawar, spoken by the Pashtun majority.
- **Peshoris**: The original Hindko-speaking urban community of Peshawar, now a minority.
- **Khyber Pass**: A historic mountain pass connecting Afghanistan and Pakistan, crucial for trade and invasions.
- **Grand Trunk Road**: One of Asia's oldest and longest major roads, connecting Central Asia to the Indian subcontinent.
- **BRT Peshawar**: A modern bus rapid transit system launched in 2020 to address the city's public transportation needs.

## Debates and open questions
- The exact etymology of "Peshawar" is debated, with theories deriving it from Sanskrit "Purushapura" ("City of Men") or Persian "Pesh Awardan" ("frontier city").
- The historical height of the Kanishka stupa is uncertain, with ancient claims of 560 ft (170 m) versus modern estimates of 400 ft (120 m).
- The precise demographic impact and current percentage of Afghan refugees in Peshawar is difficult to determine, with estimates varying.

# Dag Hammarskjöld

## Overview
Dag Hammarskjöld was a Swedish economist and diplomat who served as the second Secretary-General of the United Nations from 1953 until his death in 1961. He is the youngest person to have held the post and is widely regarded for strengthening the UN's administrative and peacekeeping capacity. His tenure was marked by significant diplomatic interventions, but his legacy is highly contested due to the UN's controversial role in the Congo Crisis, which ended with his death in a plane crash under mysterious circumstances.

## Timeline
- **29 July 1905** — Born in Jönköping, Sweden.
- **10 April 1953** — Sworn in as UN Secretary-General.
- **1956** — Oversaw creation of first UN peacekeeping force (UNEF) during Suez Crisis.
- **1957** — Unanimously re-elected for a second term.
- **1960** — UN peacekeeping force (ONUC) deployed to Congo.
- **September 1960** — Soviet Union demands Hammarskjöld's resignation, proposes "troika" leadership.
- **18 September 1961** — Dies in a plane crash near Ndola, Northern Rhodesia (now Zambia).
- **1961** — Posthumously awarded the Nobel Peace Prize.

## Body

### Early Life and Pre-UN Career
Hammarskjöld was the son of a former Swedish Prime Minister. He had a distinguished career in Swedish public service, holding key economic and financial positions, including state secretary in the Ministry of Finance and chairman of the Riksbank's General Council. He was a delegate to the UN General Assembly before his election as Secretary-General.

### Election as Secretary-General
Following Trygve Lie's resignation in 1952, prolonged negotiations between superpowers deadlocked. Hammarskjöld emerged as a compromise candidate in March 1953, seen by the Soviet Union as a non-political "technocrat." He was unaware of his nomination until informed by a journalist on April 1, 1953, initially believing it an April Fool's joke. He was elected by the General Assembly on April 7.

### Tenure and Peacekeeping
Hammarskjöld worked to improve UN administration and morale. He pioneered the use of UN peacekeeping forces, establishing the United Nations Emergency Force (UNEF) in 1956 to resolve the Suez Crisis. He also mediated in other crises, including securing the release of US pilots from China in 1955.

### The Congo Crisis and Controversy
The UN deployed a large peacekeeping force (ONUC) to the newly independent Congo in 1960. Hammarskjöld's handling of the crisis was deeply controversial. He refused to place UN forces in support of the elected government of Patrice Lumumba, a stance criticized by the Soviet Union and many non-aligned nations as insufficiently anti-colonial. Declassified documents suggest Hammarskjöld privately viewed Lumumba as an obstacle and communicated with Western officials in ways that were not politically neutral. The UN's actions during the crisis are widely seen as having disastrous long-term consequences for the Congo.

### Death and Investigation
Hammarskjöld died in a plane crash while en route to negotiate a ceasefire in the Congo. The cause remains disputed. While initial inquiries cited pilot error, subsequent investigations have found evidence suggesting the plane was shot down. Multiple theories implicate various parties, including Belgian mercenaries, mining interests, and intelligence agencies. A 2015 UN panel found new evidence, including eyewitness accounts and radio transcripts, that gave "moderate value" to the possibility of foul play. The investigation is ongoing.

## Terms
*   **Secretary-General:** The chief administrative officer of the United Nations.
*   **Peacekeeping Force:** A UN military deployment authorized to help countries navigate the difficult path from conflict to peace.
*   **UNEF (United Nations Emergency Force):** The first UN peacekeeping force, established in 1956 during the Suez Crisis.
*   **ONUC (United Nations Operation in the Congo):** The UN peacekeeping mission in the Congo from 1960 to 1964.
*   **Troika:** A proposed system of three equal heads of state for the UN, advocated by the Soviet Union to replace the Secretary-General.
*   **Katanga Secession:** The 1960 secession of the mineral-rich Katanga province from the Congo, backed by Belgian interests.
*   **Non-aligned Movement:** A group of states not formally aligned with or against any major power bloc.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **The Congo Crisis:** Scholars debate whether Hammarskjöld's actions were a principled defense of UN neutrality or a politically biased intervention that undermined a sovereign government. His legacy in the Third World remains extremely controversial.
*   **Cause of Death:** The circumstances of the 1961 plane crash are unresolved. Evidence points to possible sabotage or shooting down, but no definitive conclusion has been reached. Investigations continue to examine new evidence and theories.
*   **Neutrality:** Declassified documents have fueled debate about the extent of Hammarskjöld's political neutrality, particularly regarding his private communications with Western powers during the Congo Crisis.

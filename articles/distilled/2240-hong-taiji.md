# Hong Taiji

## Overview
Hong Taiji (1592–1643) was the second khan of the Later Jin dynasty and the founding emperor of the Qing dynasty. He consolidated the Manchu state founded by his father, Nurhaci, expanded its territory, and laid the essential groundwork for the eventual conquest of the Ming dynasty, though he died before that conquest was completed. His reign was pivotal in transforming a regional Jurchen power into a multi-ethnic empire poised to rule China.

## Timeline
- **1592** — Born as the eighth son of Nurhaci.
- **1626** — Succeeds Nurhaci as khan of the Later Jin dynasty.
- **1627** — Invades the Joseon dynasty of Korea.
- **1632** — Arranges mass marriages of Han Chinese officers to Manchu women to promote harmony.
- **1635** — Changes the name of his people from Jurchen to Manchu.
- **1636** — Proclaims the founding of the Qing dynasty, changing the dynastic name from "Great Jin" to "Great Qing."
- **1636** — Invades Joseon Korea again, forcing it to become a Qing tributary state.
- **1636** — Establishes the Mongol Yamen for governing Inner Mongolia.
- **1640** — Completes the conquest of the Amur region.
- **1642** — Ming general Hong Chengchou surrenders to the Qing.
- **1643** — Dies without naming an heir, leading to a succession crisis.

## Body

### Consolidation of Power
Hong Taiji succeeded his father Nurhaci in 1626 as the fourth-ranked among the Four Senior Beiles. Through political skill and military merit, he gained the support of the other beiles, most importantly Daišan, to become khan. He gradually consolidated power by controlling the elite Upper Three Banners (the two Yellow Banners and the Plain Blue Banner) and sidelining his rivals. He adopted Ming governmental institutions like the Six Ministries, staffing them with a mix of Manchu, Mongol, and Han officials to build an effective bureaucracy.

### Ethnic Policies and Expansion
To build a multi-ethnic state, Hong Taiji actively recruited Han Chinese officials and integrated them into the government and military. He promoted assimilation through policies like mass marriages. Militarily, he expanded the Later Jin/Qing state by conquering Inner Mongolia, subjugating Korea as a tributary, and completing the conquest of Manchuria and the Amur region. He upgraded his army with artillery and conducted several raids into Ming territory, though a direct invasion was initially rebuffed.

### Renaming the Dynasty
In 1635, Hong Taiji changed the name of his people from Jurchen to Manchu. In 1636, after capturing a Yuan dynasty imperial jade seal and receiving the submission of Mongol nobles, he proclaimed the Qing dynasty, changing the dynastic name from "Great Jin" to "Great Qing." The reasons are debated, but the change likely aimed to distance the state from the earlier Jurchen Jin dynasty (which evoked Han Chinese hostility) and to adopt a name with auspicious connotations, possibly related to the Chinese five elements theory where water (Qing) overcomes fire (Ming).

### Death and Succession
Hong Taiji died in 1643 without having designated an heir. The Deliberative Council of Princes and Ministers faced a choice between his capable half-brother Dorgon and his eldest son Hooge. As a compromise, they selected his five-year-old son Fulin (the Shunzhi Emperor), with Dorgon and Jirgalang serving as prince regents. This arrangement allowed the Qing to proceed with its conquest of China, seizing Beijing in 1644.

## Terms
- **Beile**: A Manchu noble title, similar to a prince. Hong Taiji was one of the Four Senior Beiles.
- **Eight Banners**: A Manchu social, military, and administrative system. The Upper Three Banners were directly controlled by the emperor.
- **Kurultai**: A traditional Mongol and Manchu assembly of nobles to elect a leader or make major decisions.
- **Lifanyuan**: The Qing government agency for managing relations with Inner Asian peoples, originally established as the Mongol Yamen in 1636.
- **Manchu**: The name Hong Taiji gave to his people in 1635, replacing "Jurchen."
- **Nikan**: A Manchu term for Han Chinese people.
- **Qing**: The name of the dynasty founded by Hong Taiji in 1636, meaning "clear" or "pure."
- **Tributary state**: A state that acknowledges the superiority of another (the Qing) through periodic tribute missions, in exchange for recognition and trade.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **The name "Hong Taiji":** Scholars debate whether it was his personal name or a title. Evidence suggests it was likely his real name, not a title meaning "crown prince."
- **The name "Abahai":** This Western appellation is now considered a mistake, unattested in Manchu and Chinese sources. It may stem from a confusion with his father's consort, Lady Abahai.
- **Reasons for the dynastic name "Qing":** The exact motivation is unclear. Theories include avoiding the negative connotations of the earlier Jin dynasty, following five-element theory (water overcomes Ming's fire), or using a name with a water radical to symbolically quell fraternal strife.
- **The founding myth:** Hong Taiji promoted a legend of his clan's divine origin from a virgin birth near the Changbai Mountains. An older, recorded version places the event near the Amur River, suggesting he altered the story to align with a more southern, central location.

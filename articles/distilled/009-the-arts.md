# The arts

## Overview

The arts, or creative arts, are a vast range of human practices involving creative expression, storytelling, and cultural participation. They are divided into three main branches: visual arts (architecture, painting, sculpture, photography), literature (fiction, drama, poetry, prose), and performing arts (dance, music, theatre). The arts have developed into increasingly stylized and intricate forms through sustained study and training, serving as a medium for transmitting values, ideas, and experiences across time and space. From prehistoric cave paintings to modern films, the arts register and preserve the ever-shifting relationships of humans with each other and the world.

## Timeline

- **Upper Palaeolithic** — Prehistoric cave paintings created
- **Ancient Greece** — Art and craft referred to as *techne*; veneration of animal form introduced
- **Ancient Rome** — Gods depicted as idealized humans with distinguishing features
- **Middle Ages** — Church dominates Byzantine and Gothic art; liberal arts taught in universities
- **17th century** — Classical ballet emerges, combining orchestral music with dance
- **19th century** — Richard Wagner develops *Gesamtkunstwerk* ("synthesis of the arts")
- **1960s** — Term "conceptual art" coined for idea-based art
- **1990s** — Conceptual art becomes synonym for contemporary art via Young British Artists and Turner Prize
- **2006** — Hideo Kojima argues video games are a service, not art
- **2011** — National Endowment for the Arts includes video games in definition of "work of art"
- **2012** — Smithsonian American Art Museum presents *The Art of the Video Game*

## Body

### Definition and Classification

The arts are practices or objects done by people with skill, creativity, and imagination. Classificatory disputes about art concern whether something qualifies as a work of art; twentieth-century disputes have included Cubist and Impressionist paintings, Marcel Duchamp's *Fountain*, conceptual art, and video games.

In Ancient Greece, art and craft were referred to by the word *techne*. Ancient Greek art introduced veneration of the animal form and skills showing musculature, poise, beauty, and anatomically correct proportions. Ancient Roman art depicted gods as idealized humans with distinguishing features, such as Zeus' thunderbolt. In Byzantine and Gothic art of the Middle Ages, the dominant church insisted on Christian themes due to the overlap of church and state. Asian art concentrated on surface patterning and local colour defined by outline. Islamic art avoids representing living beings in religious contexts, expressing religious ideas through calligraphy and geometrical designs.

In the Middle Ages, liberal arts were taught in European universities as part of the trivium (grammar, rhetoric, logic) and quadrivium (arithmetic, geometry, music, astronomy). The arts have been classified into seven forms: painting, architecture, sculpture, literature, music, theatre, and filmmaking. Some arts derive from others: drama is literature with acting, dance is music expressed through motion, songs are music with literature and voice. Television is sometimes called the "eighth" and comics the "ninth art" in Francophone scholarship.

### Visual Arts

Visual art forms include architecture, ceramic art, crafts, design, drawing, filmmaking, painting, photography, printmaking, and sculpture. The applied arts—industrial design, graphic design, fashion design, interior design, and decorative arts—are also included.

Architecture is the art and science of designing buildings and structures, from urban planning to furniture; design must address feasibility and cost for the builder, and function and aesthetics for the user. Ceramic art is art made from ceramic materials, including pottery, tiles, figurines, and sculptures; it excludes glass and mosaics made from glass tesserae. Conceptual art prioritizes concepts or ideas over traditional aesthetic and material concerns. Drawing involves making marks on a surface using tools like graphite pencils, pens, charcoals, and pastels, with techniques including line drawing, hatching, cross-hatching, and stippling. Painting is a form of self-expression using drawing, gesture, composition, narration, or abstraction; some modern painters incorporate sand, cement, or straw for texture. Photography as art stands in contrast to photojournalism and commercial photography. Sculpture operates in three dimensions, originally using carving (removal) and modelling (addition) in stone, metal, ceramic, and wood, but modernism led to almost complete freedom of materials and processes.

### Literary and Performing Arts

Literature is generally identified as a collection of writings, mainly prose (fiction and non-fiction), drama, and poetry. Artistic linguistic expression can also be oral, including epic, legend, myth, ballad, and folktales.

Performing arts comprise dance, music, theatre, opera, and mime, distinguished by human performance as the principal product. Each discipline is temporal—performed over time—and products are either repeatable (by script or score) or improvised. Dance refers to human movement for expression or in social, spiritual, or performance settings; choreography is the art of making dances. Music is an art form whose medium is a combination of sounds; core elements include pitch (melody and harmony), duration (rhythm and tempo), intensity (dynamics), and timbre. Theatre acts out stories before an audience using speech, gesture, music, dance, sound, and spectacle, taking forms such as opera, ballet, mime, kabuki, and Indian classical dance.

### Multidisciplinary Works

Some artistic works incorporate multiple fields. Opera combines sets, costumes, acting, a libretto, singers, and an orchestra. Richard Wagner emphasized the fusion of disciplines in his cycle *Der Ring des Nibelungen*, calling it *Gesamtkunstwerk* rather than opera. Performance art combines instruments, objects, and art within a structure, which may be scripted, unscripted, or improvised, sometimes with audience participation. John Cage is regarded by many as a performance artist; his 1940 composition *Living Room Music* is a quartet for non-melodic objects found in a typical living room.

Video games include uncontroversial artistic elements (visuals, sound) plus an emergent experience from interactivity. Debates surround whether video games are art and whether developers are artists. Hideo Kojima argued in 2006 that video games are a type of service. Cultural economists show that playing video games is conducive to involvement in more traditional art forms.

### Criticism, Education, and Politics

Art criticism is the discussion or evaluation of art, usually in the context of aesthetics or the theory of beauty. A goal is the pursuit of a rational basis for art appreciation, though it is questionable whether criticism can transcend prevailing sociopolitical circumstances. Criticism divides into historical criticism (a form of art history) and contemporary criticism of living artists. Critics of the past have been ridiculed for dismissing now-venerated artists, such as the early Impressionists. Some movement names—Impressionism, Cubism—were originally disparaging labels later adopted as badges of honour.

Arts in education is a field of research and practice informed by investigations into learning through arts experiences, including performing arts education, literature, storytelling, and visual arts education.

A strong relationship exists between the arts and politics. Artists use their work to express political views and promote social change, from hate speech to artivism. Governments use art, or propaganda, to promote their own agendas. Alexander Pushkin attracted the irritation of Russian officialdom for composing "arrogant, independent, and wicked verse." Banksy, an England-based graffiti artist, has conflicted with authorities. The Catholic Church declared in 1963 that the arts are "not exempt" from "the absolute primacy of the objective moral order."

## Terms

- **Techne**: Ancient Greek word for art and craft
- **Trivium**: Medieval introductory curriculum of grammar, rhetoric, and logic
- **Quadrivium**: Medieval curriculum of arithmetic, geometry, music, and astronomy
- **Classificatory disputes**: Debates over whether something qualifies as a work of art
- **Applied arts**: Design and decoration applied to everyday functional objects
- **Choreography**: The art of making dances
- **Gesamtkunstwerk**: Wagner's term for "synthesis of the arts" or "music drama"
- **Artivism**: Political art promoting social change
- **Art criticism**: Discussion or evaluation of art in the context of aesthetics

## Debates and open questions

Scholars debate whether criticism can transcend prevailing sociopolitical circumstances. The definition of music's core elements is disputed, though scholars agree music generally consists of a few. The dividing lines between music genres are subtle, open to individual interpretation, and controversial. Whether video games should be classified as an art form and whether developers should be classified as artists remains debated. Classificatory disputes about art continue, with 20th-century examples including Cubist paintings, Duchamp's *Fountain*, conceptual art, and video games.

# Azerbaijan

## Overview
Azerbaijan is a transcontinental country in the South Caucasus, straddling Western Asia and Eastern Europe. Its modern identity is shaped by a history of Persian, Russian, and Soviet rule, the emergence of a distinct national identity in the late 19th century, and a post-independence struggle over the Nagorno-Karabakh region. It is a major oil and gas producer and the world's first secular democratic Muslim-majority state.

## Timeline
- **1813 & 1828** — Treaties of Gulistan and Turkmenchay force Qajar Iran to cede its Caucasian territories to the Russian Empire.
- **1918** — The Azerbaijan Democratic Republic (ADR) declares independence, becoming the first secular democratic Muslim-majority state.
- **1920** — The Bolshevik 11th Soviet Red Army invades, establishing the Azerbaijan Soviet Socialist Republic.
- **1991** — The modern Republic of Azerbaijan proclaims independence from the Soviet Union.
- **1994** — The First Nagorno-Karabakh War ends, with Armenian forces controlling Nagorno-Karabakh and seven surrounding districts.
- **2003** — Ilham Aliyev succeeds his father, Heydar Aliyev, as President, continuing the Aliyev family's rule.
- **2020** — The Second Nagorno-Karabakh War ends with Azerbaijan regaining control of the seven surrounding districts and parts of Nagorno-Karabakh.
- **2023** — An Azerbaijani offensive dissolves the self-proclaimed Republic of Artsakh and results in the expulsion of Nagorno-Karabakh Armenians.

## Body

### Etymology and Early History
The name "Azerbaijan" derives from Atropates, a Persian satrap under the Achaemenid Empire, with roots in Zoroastrianism meaning "Protected by the (Holy) Fire." The name was first adopted for the independent state in 1918; previously, it referred only to the adjacent region in northwestern Iran. The territory shows evidence of human settlement from the late Stone Age. It was later ruled by Caucasian Albania, which adopted Christianity in the 4th century, and was subsequently conquered by Arab, Turkic, and Mongol powers. The region was gradually Turkicized from the 11th century onward.

### From Persian Rule to Russian Conquest
For centuries, the area was part of various Persian empires. Following the Safavid dynasty, it was ruled by the Afsharid and Qajar dynasties. From the late 18th century, Imperial Russia aggressively sought control of the Caucasus. The Russo-Persian Wars of 1804–1813 and 1826–1828 resulted in treaties that forced Qajar Iran to cede its Caucasian territories, including modern-day Azerbaijan, to the Russian Empire. Despite Russian conquest, Iranian cultural influence remained strong among intellectuals throughout the 19th century.

### Independence, Soviet Era, and Modern Conflicts
After the Russian Empire collapsed, the short-lived Azerbaijan Democratic Republic was established in 1918. It was the first modern parliamentary republic in the Muslim world and granted women equal political rights. The republic lasted only 23 months before being conquered by the Bolsheviks in 1920. During World War II, Azerbaijan was a crucial energy supplier for the Soviet Union. Following the Soviet Union's dissolution, Azerbaijan declared independence in 1991. The early years were marked by the First Nagorno-Karabakh War, which ended in 1994 with a ceasefire and significant territorial loss. In 1993, Heydar Aliyev came to power; his son, Ilham Aliyev, has ruled since 2003. The Second Nagorno-Karabakh War in 2020 and a 2023 offensive resulted in Azerbaijan regaining full control over the Nagorno-Karabakh region.

### Government and Politics
Azerbaijan is a unitary semi-presidential republic. In practice, it functions as an authoritarian regime under the continuous rule of the Aliyev family and the New Azerbaijan Party since 1993. Elections are marred by fraud, and the government is criticized for severe restrictions on press freedom, human rights, and political dissent. The country is a member of numerous international organizations, including the UN and the Council of Europe, and maintains a "special relationship" with Turkey.

### Economy and Energy
Azerbaijan's economy is heavily dependent on oil and natural gas, which account for a large portion of its exports and government revenue. The country shows signs of "Dutch disease," where a booming energy sector causes inflation and makes non-energy exports more expensive. Major infrastructure projects, like the Baku–Tbilisi–Ceyhan pipeline, are key to its role as an energy corridor to Europe. The government has used oil revenues to fund development and modernization, though wealth inequality and corruption remain significant issues.

### Demographics and Culture
The population is 94.8% Azerbaijani, with minorities including Lezgins, Talysh, and Russians. Azerbaijani, a Turkic language, is the official language. The population is approximately 97% Muslim, but the country is constitutionally secular. Azerbaijani culture blends Turkic, Persian, and Caucasian influences. Key cultural forms include mugham (a classical music tradition), ashik (a bardic performance art), and intricate carpet weaving, all recognized by UNESCO. The capital, Baku, features a mix of medieval and modern architecture.

## Terms
*   **Caucasian Albania:** An ancient kingdom and vassal state in the South Caucasus, which later became a province within various Persian empires.
*   **Khanate:** A type of political entity or principality ruled by a khan, common in the region before Russian conquest.
*   **Mugham:** A traditional Azerbaijani musical suite combining poetry and instrumental interludes, noted for its improvisational nature.
*   **Ashiq:** A traditional troubadour or bard who performs a blend of poetry, storytelling, dance, and music.
*   **Dutch disease:** An economic phenomenon where rapid growth in a natural resource sector (like oil) leads to currency appreciation, harming other export sectors.
*   **Nagorno-Karabakh:** A region in the South Caucasus, internationally recognized as part of Azerbaijan, which was the site of two major wars between Azerbaijan and Armenian forces.
*   **New Azerbaijan Party (YAP):** The ruling political party in Azerbaijan since 1993, founded by Heydar Aliyev.
*   **SOCAR:** The State Oil Company of Azerbaijan Republic, the national oil and gas company.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Historical Identity:** The use of the name "Azerbaijan" for the post-Soviet republic is contested by Iran, which argues the name historically refers only to its own northwestern region.
*   **Governance and Human Rights:** The Aliyev government is consistently criticized by international observers for authoritarianism, election fraud, corruption, and severe repression of civil liberties and political opposition.
*   **Nagorno-Karabakh Conflict:** The resolution of the conflict, including the status of the region and the fate of displaced populations, remains a central and sensitive issue in regional politics.

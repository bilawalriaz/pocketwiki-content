# Cannes Film Festival

## Overview
The Cannes Film Festival is the world's most prestigious annual film event, held in Cannes, France. Founded in 1946, it serves as a major platform for premiering new films of all genres and is a key marketplace for the global film industry. It is considered one of the "Big Three" European festivals (with Venice and Berlin) and one of the "Big Five" international festivals (adding Toronto and Sundance).

## Timeline
- **1938** — Idea for an international festival conceived by French officials to rival Venice.
- **1939** — Cannes selected as location; first edition cancelled due to the outbreak of WWII.
- **1946** — Festival officially relaunched and held for the first time.
- **1951** — Festival moved to spring to avoid competition with Venice; formally accredited by FIAPF.
- **1955** — Palme d'Or created as the top prize, replacing the Grand Prix.
- **1962** — International Critics' Week founded as the first parallel section.
- **1968** — Festival halted by filmmaker protests in solidarity with student and labour strikes.
- **1969** — Directors' Fortnight created as a new, non-competitive parallel section.
- **1972** — Festival committee takes over film selection from national representatives.
- **1978** — Un Certain Regard section and Caméra d'Or award introduced.
- **1983** — New, larger Palais des Festivals et des Congrès venue opens.
- **1998** — Cinéfondation section created to support student filmmakers.
- **2020** — Festival cancelled for the first time since 1968 due to the COVID-19 pandemic.
- **2022** — Iris Knobloch becomes the first woman elected president of the festival.

## Body

### Origins and Early History
The festival was conceived in 1938 by French officials, including Jean Zay and Philippe Erlanger, as a direct response to the fascist bias perceived at the Venice Film Festival. After Mussolini and Hitler overruled the jury in 1938, French, British, and American members withdrew, spurring the creation of a new event. Cannes was chosen for its appeal and municipal support. The inaugural 1939 edition was cancelled after the German invasion of Poland. The festival was successfully relaunched in 1946, though it was skipped in 1948 and 1950 due to budget issues. A dedicated venue, the Palais des Festivals, was built in 1949.

### Institutional Growth and Artistic Identity
Throughout the 1950s and 1960s, the festival developed its artistic credibility alongside its glamorous reputation. Key awards like the Palme d'Or (1955) and sections like the Critics' Prize were established. The 1968 festival was famously halted by filmmaker protests, leading to the creation of the influential Directors' Fortnight in 1969. In 1972, a major change occurred when the festival's own committee, led by Maurice Bessy, took over film selection from national representatives, reducing diplomatic pressure. The 1980s saw expansion under President Pierre Viot, including the opening of a new, larger venue in 1983 and the inclusion of films from more countries.

### Modern Era and Contemporary Issues
Since the 1990s, the festival has expanded its support for new talent through sections like Cinéfondation (1998) and adapted to industry changes, such as the rise of digital technology. It has also faced modern controversies, including the 2015 "Heelgate" dress code incident and the 2017 conflict with Netflix over theatrical release rules. The festival was cancelled in 2020 due to the pandemic and held with restrictions in 2021. Recent years have seen increased focus on gender parity, with a record 45% of films directed by women in 2025, and political statements, such as condemnations of the war in Gaza.

## Terms
- **Palme d'Or**: The highest prize awarded at the festival for the best film in the Main Competition.
- **Un Certain Regard**: A competitive section focusing on innovative and audacious works, often from emerging filmmakers.
- **Directors' Fortnight**: An influential, non-competitive parallel section founded in 1969 to showcase independent and avant-garde cinema.
- **Marché du Film**: The festival's associated film market, the world's busiest, where industry professionals buy and sell distribution rights.
- **FIAPF**: The International Federation of Film Producers Associations, which accredited the festival in 1951.
- **Caméra d'Or**: An award for the best first feature film presented in any of the festival's main sections.
- **Cinéfondation**: A section dedicated to short and medium-length films from film schools worldwide.
- **Parallel Sections**: Independent sections like Critics' Week and Directors' Fortnight that run alongside the official festival.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Political Neutrality**: The festival has historically aimed to be a "no man's land" free from politics, but has increasingly been a platform for political statements and has faced criticism for selections like Kirill Serebrennikov's film in 2022.
- **Commercial vs. Artistic Integrity**: The conflict with Netflix over theatrical release rules highlights the ongoing tension between traditional cinematic exhibition and new streaming models.
- **Inclusivity and Representation**: While progress has been made, as seen in the 2025 gender parity record, past controversies like "Heelgate" and the #MeToo movement have prompted ongoing scrutiny of the festival's culture and policies.

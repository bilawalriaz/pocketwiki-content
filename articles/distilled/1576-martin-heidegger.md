# Martin Heidegger

## Overview
Martin Heidegger (1889–1976) was a German philosopher whose analysis of human existence (*Dasein*) in *Being and Time* (1927) redirected 20th-century philosophy toward fundamental ontology, hermeneutics, and existentialism. He argued that Western metaphysics had forgotten the "question of being"—what makes entities intelligible as entities—by treating being as a supreme being rather than as the condition for intelligibility. His later work critiqued modern technology as a nihilistic "enframing" of the world and turned to poetry and language as sites for a possible "turn" (*Kehre*) toward a more originary relation to being. Heidegger’s legacy is profoundly shadowed by his 1933–1934 rectorate at the University of Freiburg under the Nazi regime, his party membership until 1945, and the posthumous revelation of antisemitic passages in his private *Black Notebooks*, sparking enduring debate over whether his philosophy is inherently entangled with his politics.

## Body

### Fundamental Ontology and the Question of Being
Heidegger distinguishes his "fundamental ontology" from traditional ontology. While the latter asks "Why is there anything?" (treating being as an entity), Heidegger asks "What does it mean for something to be?" He argues that the Western tradition, from Plato to Descartes, committed a "forgetfulness of being" (*Seinsvergessenheit*) by seeking a grounding entity (God, substance, subjectivity) rather than interrogating the *meaning* of being itself—the condition that allows entities to show up as intelligible. This inquiry is "fundamental" because it concerns the pre-theoretical understanding of being that all scientific and metaphysical investigation presupposes.

### *Being and Time*: Dasein and Being-in-the-World
To access the meaning of being, Heidegger analyzes *Dasein* ("being-there"), his term for the human way of being characterized by an implicit, "pre-ontological" understanding of being. *Dasein* is not a subject confronting an object-world but a unitary phenomenon: **being-in-the-world** (*In-der-Welt-sein*). The "in" signifies not spatial containment but *involvement* or *dwelling*—"having to do with" things. The "world" is not a collection of objects but a holistic context of significance structured by our projects and equipment.

Heidegger analyzes everyday dealings with **ready-to-hand** (*Zuhanden*) equipment (e.g., a hammer used transparently in hammering). Equipment becomes **present-at-hand** (*Vorhanden*)—a mere object with properties—only upon breakdown (e.g., the hammer breaks). This demonstrates that the subject-object split is a derivative, deficient mode of our primary practical engagement. *Dasein*’s existential structure (**existentialia**) comprises three equiprimordial features:
1.  **Understanding** (*Verstehen*): Practical "know-how" projecting future possibilities.
2.  **Attunement** (*Befindlichkeit*): "State-of-mind" or "disposition" (e.g., moods like anxiety) revealing our thrownness into a world that already matters.
3.  **Discourse** (*Rede*): The articulation of the world into communicable patterns of meaning, making language possible.

These three are unified as **Care** (*Sorge*): "ahead-of-itself-being-already-in-the-world as being-amidst entities." Temporality is the ontological meaning of Care: understanding projects toward the future, attunement retrieves the past, and discourse discloses the present.

### *Das Man*, Authenticity, and Guilt
*Dasein* is essentially social (*Mitsein*), but this sociality manifests as **das Man** ("the They"): an anonymous, leveling publicness where "one" thinks, enjoys, and judges as "they" do. This "dictatorship of the They" threatens to obscure the question of being by absorbing *Dasein* into inauthentic everydayness. **Authenticity** (*Eigentlichkeit*, "ownedness") is not discovering a pre-existing self but resolutely taking responsibility for one’s finite existence. It is achieved through:
1.  **Anxiety** (*Angst*): Discloses the world’s insignificance and *Dasein*’s radical individualization.
2.  **Being-towards-death**: Confronting death as *Dasein*’s "ownmost, non-relational, certain, and unsurpassable possibility" frees *Dasein* from the They’s trivialization of mortality.
3.  **The Call of Conscience**: Reveals *Dasein*’s **guilt** (*Schuld*)—not moral fault, but the ontological debt of having to choose among inherited possibilities one did not create.

In later work, the voluntaristic notion of authenticity is supplanted by **Ereignis** ("event" or "appropriation"): the mutual belonging of *Dasein* and being in a cosmic play of concealment and unconcealment.

### The Turn (*Kehre*) and Later Philosophy
From the 1930s onward, commentators detect a "Turn" (*die Kehre*)—a shift from analyzing *Dasein*’s temporality to thinking the **history of being** (*Seinsgeschichte*). Heidegger rejected a sharp "about-face," insisting on continuity, but acknowledged a reversal from "being and time" to "time and being." Key shifts include:
*   **History of Being**: Western history is a sequence of epochs (Greek *phusis*, Roman *essentia*, medieval *creatura*, modern *objectivity*, technological *enframing*) each dominated by a specific understanding of being. Heidegger seeks to retrieve the pre-Socratic (Heraclitus, Parmenides) experience of being as *event* (*Ereignis*)—an unfolding "happening" (*Wesung*)—before it was "metaphysically" frozen into static presence by Plato and Aristotle.
*   **Language**: In *Being and Time*, language expresses a pre-linguistic understanding. Post-Turn, language is **world-disclosive**: "Language is the house of being." Humans do not "have" language; "it is language that speaks," and humans correspond to it. Poetry (especially Hölderlin) names the holy and preserves the openness of being against technological leveling.
*   **Technology**: In "The Question Concerning Technology" (1954), modern technology is not merely instrumental but a **Gestell** ("enframing"): a challenging-forth that orders nature as standing-reserve (*Bestand*)—energy stockpiled for calculation. This destiny risks reducing humans to mere orderers. The only potential saving power lies in a meditative thinking that relearns **dwelling** (*Wohnen*)—letting things be—exemplified in "Building Dwelling Thinking" (1951).

### Nazism, Denazification, and the *Black Notebooks*
Heidegger’s political engagement remains the central controversy of his reception.
*   **Rectorate (1933–34)**: Elected rector days after Hitler’s chancellorship, Heidegger joined the NSDAP (May 1933), delivered a pro-regime inaugural address, signed the professors’ vow of allegiance to Hitler, and pursued *Gleichschaltung* (coordination) of the university. He resigned in April 1934, citing administrative frustration rather than principled dissent, but remained a party member until 1945.
*   **Treatment of Husserl**: Heidegger did not intervene when his Jewish mentor Edmund Husserl was stripped of privileges in 1933; he later removed the dedication to Husserl from *Being and Time* (1941) under publisher pressure (restored post-war).
*   **Post-war**: Classified as *Mitläufer* (1949), teaching ban lifted 1950. In a 1949 lecture, he equated industrial agriculture with extermination camps ("essentially the same"), drawing charges of trivializing the Holocaust. The 1966 *Der Spiegel* interview (published 1976) defended his rectorate as an attempt to shield the university, claiming a 1934 break with Nazism contradicted by Karl Löwith’s 1936 eyewitness account (swastika pin, avowed faith in Hitler).
*   **Black Notebooks (2014)**: Private philosophical journals (1931–1970s) contain explicit **antisemitic** tropes (e.g., "world Judaism," racial calculus, blaming Jews for "calculative rationality"). Scholars (e.g., Donatella Di Cesare) argue these reveal a deep connection between his philosophy and *völkisch* antisemitism, not merely personal prejudice.

### Influences and Reception
**Early**: Husserl (phenomenology, but Heidegger replaced *intentionality* with *being-in-the-world*); Aristotle (via Brentano and medieval ontology); Dilthey (hermeneutics of factical life); Kierkegaard (anxiety, death, subjectivity).
**Later**: Nietzsche (read as culmination of Western metaphysics via *Will to Power*); Hölderlin (poetry as herald of a new beginning).
**Reception**: Defined continental philosophy (Sartre, Gadamer, Arendt, Derrida, Levinas, Rorty). Derrida’s *deconstruction* translates *Destruktion/Abbau*. Dreyfus applied *being-in-the-world* to AI (frame problem), influencing enactivism and situated robotics. Engagement with East Asian thought (Kyoto School, Taoism/Zen) is noted, though Reinhard May alleges unacknowledged appropriation of Daoist/Zen concepts. Analytic philosophy (Carnap, Russell, Ayer) largely dismissed Heidegger as obscurantist, unverifiable, or nonsensical. Marxist/Frankfurt School critics (Adorno, Habermas) associated his style with irrationalism and ideological complicity. Feminist and post-structuralist critics (Irigaray, Sloterdijk) target neglect of sexual difference and space.

## Terms
- ****Dasein**** — "Being-there": Heidegger’s term for the human way of being, defined by its capacity to understand the meaning of being. Not a "subject" or "consciousness," but the entity for whom its own being is an issue.
- ****Being-in-the-world** (*In-der-Welt-sein*)** — The unitary, primordial structure of *Dasein*; not a spatial relation but practical engagement/involvement ("dwelling") within a holistic context of significance.
- ****Ready-to-hand** (*Zuhanden*)** — The mode of being of equipment when used transparently in skilled activity (e.g., hammering); understood only *in* use, not as an object with properties.
- ****Present-at-hand** (*Vorhanden*)** — The mode of being of entities as mere objects with observable properties, arising from theoretical contemplation or equipment breakdown.
- ****Das Man** ("The They")** — The anonymous, leveling publicness of everyday *Dasein*; the "they-self" that dictates interpretation and behavior, threatening authentic self-appropriation.
- ****Authenticity** (*Eigentlichkeit*)** — "Ownedness": Resolute acceptance of one’s finite existence, guilt, and death, wresting oneself from the dictatorship of *das Man*.
- ****Care** (*Sorge*)** — The unified structural whole of *Dasein*: "ahead-of-itself-being-already-in-the-world as being-amidst entities"; temporality is its ontological meaning.
- ****Ereignis**** — "Event" / "Appropriation": In later thought, the mutual belonging of *Dasein* and being; the happening of being’s unconcealment, replacing the voluntaristic subjectivity of authenticity.
- ****Gestell** ("Enframing")** — The essence of modern technology: a challenging-forth that orders all of nature (and humans) as *standing-reserve* (*Bestand*) for calculative manipulation.
- ****Seinsvergessenheit**** — "Forgetfulness of being": The Western metaphysical tradition’s failure to distinguish being from beings, treating being as a supreme entity rather than the condition of intelligibility.

## Debates and open questions
1.  **Philosophy–Politics Entanglement**: Does Heidegger’s ontology (e.g., *Volk*, *destiny*, *rootedness*, critique of "calculative reason") structurally align with Nazism, or is the connection contingent/personal? The *Black Notebooks* intensified this debate, suggesting antisemitism was philosophically motivated.
2.  **The Reality of the Turn (*Kehre*)**: Is there a substantive break between early (anthropological/existential) and later (historical/poetic) Heidegger, or merely a shift in focus/vocabulary? (Sheehan/Wrathall vs. standard periodization).
3.  **Husserl and the Dedication**: Was Heidegger’s removal of the Husserl dedication (1941) and failure to attend his funeral (1938) active complicity or passive cowardice? Arendt’s shift from "potential murderer" to "error" illustrates the interpretive divide.
4.  **Ethics and the "They"**: Does *Being and Time* lack an ethics (Levinas: "the priority of ontology" violates the face of the Other), or does authenticity imply a formal ethical structure (Varga/Guignon)?
5.  **Language and Agency**: If "language speaks, not man," does this dissolve human agency into linguistic determinism, or does it describe the finite condition of *corresponding* to disclosure?
6.  **East Asian Appropriation**: Did Heidegger unacknowledgedly incorporate Daoist/Zen concepts (May), or do parallels reflect convergent thinking?

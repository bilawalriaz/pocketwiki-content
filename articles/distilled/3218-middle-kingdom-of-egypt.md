# Middle Kingdom of Egypt

## Overview
The Middle Kingdom (c. 2040–1782 BC) was a period of political reunification, cultural flourishing, and economic prosperity in ancient Egypt following the fragmented First Intermediate Period. It is considered one of Egypt's classical ages, marked by strong central government, extensive building projects, and the creation of literature later regarded as "classic." The period ended with a gradual decline into the Second Intermediate Period, a time of division and foreign rule.

## Timeline
- **c. 2055 BC** — Mentuhotep II ascends the Theban throne, beginning the reunification of Egypt.
- **c. 2040 BC** — Mentuhotep II completes the conquest of Herakleopolis, founding the Middle Kingdom.
- **c. 1991 BC** — Amenemhet I founds the Twelfth Dynasty, establishing a new capital at Itjtawy.
- **c. 1962 BC** — Senusret I becomes coregent, beginning a series of stable successions.
- **c. 1878 BC** — Senusret III launches major campaigns in Nubia, establishing a formal southern boundary.
- **c. 1842 BC** — Amenemhet III begins a long reign, the height of Middle Kingdom economic prosperity.
- **c. 1802 BC** — Sobekneferu, the first attested female pharaoh, rules briefly, ending the Twelfth Dynasty.
- **c. 1782 BC** — The Thirteenth Dynasty begins, marking the start of the decline into the Second Intermediate Period.

## Body

### Reunification and the Eleventh Dynasty
After the First Intermediate Period, two rival dynasties fought for control: the Theban Eleventh Dynasty in the south and the Herakleopolitan Tenth Dynasty in the north. Mentuhotep II reunified Egypt by conquering the north, consolidating power by his 39th regnal year. He restored Egyptian control in Nubia and the Sinai and revived the cult of the ruler. His successors, Mentuhotep III and IV, continued consolidation. The dynasty ended when Amenemhet I, likely a usurper not of royal blood, founded the Twelfth Dynasty.

### The Twelfth Dynasty: Stability and Expansion
The Twelfth Dynasty (c. 1991–1782 BC) is noted for its strong pharaohs and centralized administration. Early kings like Amenemhet I and Senusret I focused on securing the borders, building fortifications, and directly appointing provincial governors (nomarchs) to curb their hereditary power. This created a more feudal state structure. Military campaigns expanded Egyptian territory deep into Nubia to the Second Cataract and maintained influence in the Levant. The dynasty practiced co-regency to ensure smooth succession.

### Height and Decline
Senusret III was a warrior-king who brutally campaigned in Nubia, building massive forts and establishing a formal border at Semna. He is credited with administrative reforms that further centralized power. His son, Amenemhet III, presided over the kingdom's economic peak, exploiting mining resources and continuing major irrigation projects in the Faiyum. After his long reign, the dynasty weakened. Succession problems and possible climate-related crop failures contributed to its end with the short reign of Sobekneferu.

### Administration and Society
The Middle Kingdom government was highly centralized. The vizier served as chief minister, often split into northern and southern roles. The state controlled land registration and provincial borders. The power of hereditary nomarchs was gradually suppressed, especially under Senusret III, though some families may have been absorbed into the bureaucracy. The period saw the growth of a middle class and an expanded scribal bureaucracy.

### Art, Literature, and Technology
Art returned to Old Kingdom models but achieved new heights of quality and elegance. Innovations included the block statue and refined royal portraiture. Literature flourished, with works like *The Story of Sinuhe* and *The Satire of the Trades* considered classics. Technologically, the use of arsenical bronze was common, with evidence of intentional alloying processes.

## Terms
- **Middle Kingdom**: The period of ancient Egyptian history from c. 2040 to 1782 BC, characterized by reunification and cultural achievement.
- **First Intermediate Period**: The era of political fragmentation and weak central rule that preceded the Middle Kingdom.
- **Nomarch**: The hereditary governor of a nome (province) in ancient Egypt.
- **Coregency**: The practice of a pharaoh appointing a successor to rule jointly before the pharaoh's death.
- **Vizier**: The chief minister or highest official in the ancient Egyptian government.
- **Wadi Hammamat**: A dry riverbed in the Eastern Desert, used as a route for expeditions to the Red Sea and for quarrying stone.
- **Faiyum**: A fertile oasis region west of the Nile, extensively developed for agriculture during the Middle Kingdom.
- **Block statue**: A type of Egyptian sculpture depicting a person squatting with knees drawn up, creating a block-like form.
- **Rishi-coffin**: A type of coffin shaped like a wrapped body, first appearing in the late Middle Kingdom.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The exact end date of the Middle Kingdom is debated, varying from c. 1782 BC (end of the Twelfth Dynasty) to c. 1650 BC (if the entire Thirteenth Dynasty is included).
- The cause of the transition from the Twelfth to the Thirteenth Dynasty is unclear; it may have involved usurpation, civil war, or gradual decline.
- Scholars debate whether the powerful nomarch families were violently suppressed by Senusret III or gradually absorbed into the central administration.
- The extent of Egyptian control in Canaan during this period is uncertain, though administrative seals and military references suggest increased activity.

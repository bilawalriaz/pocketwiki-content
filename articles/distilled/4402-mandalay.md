# Mandalay

## Overview
Mandalay is Myanmar's second-largest city and the historical, cultural, and economic heart of Upper Myanmar. Founded as the last royal capital of the Konbaung dynasty in 1857, it remains a vital commercial hub and a symbol of Burmese identity, though its demographics and economy have been profoundly reshaped by a massive influx of Chinese immigrants since the late 20th century.

## Body

### Founding and Royal Capital
Mandalay was founded by King Mindon in 1857 to fulfill a Buddhist prophecy, replacing Amarapura as the capital of the Konbaung dynasty. The city was meticulously planned with a grid-patterned citadel, a central royal palace, and massive walls and moats. It served as Burma's final royal capital for 26 years until its annexation by the British Empire in 1885. The British exiled the last king, Thibaw Min, and looted the palace, though the structures were spared.

### Colonial and Post-Independence Era
Under British rule, Mandalay remained the chief city of Upper Burma and a center of Burmese culture and Buddhist learning, even as political and commercial power shifted to Yangon. The city was heavily bombed during the Japanese conquest in World War II, destroying much of the palace and three-fifths of its houses. After independence in 1948, Mandalay continued as the main cultural, educational, and economic hub of the north. Its infrastructure deteriorated under Ne Win's isolationist rule (1962–1988), and major fires in the 1980s and 2000s caused further destruction.

### Demographic and Economic Transformation
Since the 1980s, a massive influx of illegal Han Chinese immigrants, primarily from Yunnan province, has transformed Mandalay. An estimated 250,000–300,000 arrived in the 1990s alone. Today, ethnic Chinese are believed to comprise 40%–50% of the city's population. This migration has reshaped the city's ethnic makeup and economy. Chinese entrepreneurs now dominate retail, real estate, hotels, and gem trading, with an estimated 50% of commercial activity in downtown Mandalay generated by Chinese-owned businesses. This economic dominance has led to tensions with the local Burmese population.

### Geography and Climate
Mandalay is located in the central Dry Zone on the east bank of the Irrawaddy River, at an elevation of 80 meters. It lies along the Sagaing Fault, a seismically active tectonic plate boundary. The city has a tropical wet and dry climate, with very hot pre-monsoon months (April-May) and distinct wet and dry seasons.

## Terms
- **Konbaung dynasty:** The last dynasty of Burmese kings, ruling until British annexation in 1885.
- **Dry Zone:** The rain-shadow region of central Myanmar where Mandalay is located.
- **Sagaing Fault:** A major tectonic plate boundary running through central Myanmar, responsible for significant earthquakes.
- **Kuthodaw Pagoda:** A pagoda complex housing 729 stone slabs inscribed with the entire Pāli Canon, known as the "world's largest book."
- **Pāli Canon:** The standard collection of scriptures in the Theravada Buddhist tradition.
- **Bamar:** The dominant ethnic group in Myanmar, also known as Burman.
- **Yunnan:** A province in southwestern China, the origin of most recent Chinese immigrants to Mandalay.
- **Mandalay City Development Committee (MCDC):** The municipal organization governing Mandalay City.

## Debates and Open Questions
The exact Pali root word for the name "Mandalay" is unclear, with speculation pointing to *maṇḍala* (circular plains) or *Mandara* (a mythical mountain). The long-term social and political impact of the large-scale Chinese immigration on Mandalay's identity and stability remains a significant ongoing issue.

# Austin, Texas

## Overview
Austin, Texas's capital and a major U.S. metropolitan center, is the "Live Music Capital of the World" and a hub for technology and education. Its 2020 population was 961,855. Rapid growth and an economic shift from government and education to high-tech define its modern identity, while the motto "Keep Austin Weird" reflects tensions between development and preserving bohemian roots.

## Timeline
- **c. 9200 BC** — Earliest known human habitation, linked to the Clovis culture.
- **1730** — Three Catholic missions are temporarily reestablished on the south side of the Colorado River.
- **1839** — The Texas Congress selects the site for the new capital; it is briefly named "Waterloo" before being renamed Austin.
- **1841** — President Sam Houston attempts to move the capital, leading to the "Archive War."
- **1845** — Congress reconvenes in Austin, settling it as the permanent seat of government.
- **1871** — The Houston and Texas Central Railway opens, making Austin a major trading center.
- **1888** — The Texas State Capitol building is completed.
- **1930s** — The Lower Colorado River Authority is established, creating the Highland Lakes system.
- **1960s-70s** — Technology companies like Tracor, IBM, and Texas Instruments establish operations, beginning Austin's tech boom.
- **1970s** — Austin emerges as a national music scene with artists like Willie Nelson and venues like the Armadillo World Headquarters.
- **2010** — Austin FC is announced as the city's first major professional sports team, beginning play in 2021.
- **2021** — Winter Storm Uri causes widespread power outages and a water crisis.

## Body

### History and Founding
The Austin area has been inhabited since at least 9200 BC. Spanish expeditions passed through in the 18th century, and brief missions were established. In 1839, the Republic of Texas selected the site for its new capital, naming it after Stephen F. Austin. The city's early years were marked by political conflict over its location, including the "Archive War" of 1841, but it was confirmed as the permanent capital in 1845. The post-Civil War era brought growth via railroads, and the late 19th century saw the completion of the State Capitol and the founding of the University of Texas. The 20th century brought civic development, the creation of the Highland Lakes for flood control and power, and the emergence of a significant high-tech industry beginning in the 1960s.

### Economy and Technology
Austin's economy has evolved from a focus on government and education to become a major center for technology and business. Tech roots trace to the 1960s with companies like Tracor and IBM. The 1990s saw rapid growth, earning the nickname "Silicon Hills." Today, Austin hosts headquarters or major offices for numerous Fortune 500 companies, including Tesla, Apple, Google, and Dell. The University of Texas at Austin provides a steady pipeline of engineering and computer science graduates. The city is also a hub for biotechnology and is the headquarters of Whole Foods Market.

### Culture and Identity
Austin's culture is defined by its live music scene, with more venues per capita than any other U.S. city, anchored by the South by Southwest (SXSW) festival and the television show *Austin City Limits*. The unofficial slogan "Keep Austin Weird" promotes local businesses and eccentricity amid rapid growth. The city is also known for its outdoor recreation, centered on Lady Bird Lake and the Barton Creek Greenbelt, and for iconic events like the Austin City Limits Music Festival. The "City of the Violet Crown" is a historic nickname referencing the sunset glow over its hills.

### Geography and Climate
Austin is located in Central Texas on the Colorado River, at the edge of the Texas Hill Country. Its geography features a mix of flat eastern plains and western limestone hills, making it prone to flash floods. The city has several artificial lakes, including Lady Bird Lake and Lake Travis. The climate is humid subtropical with very hot summers, mild winters, and variable humidity. It experiences occasional severe weather, including ice storms and rare tornadoes, and is susceptible to drought, as seen in 2011.

### Government and Politics
Austin operates under a council-manager system with an 11-member city council. It is the county seat of Travis County. Politically, Austin is a Democratic stronghold in a Republican state, though recent redistricting has split the city among multiple congressional districts. A defining political movement is environmentalism, which has influenced debates over development, "sense of place," and issues like plastic bag bans.

## Terms
- **Silicon Hills**: A nickname for Austin adopted in the 1990s, referencing its rapid influx of technology companies.
- **Keep Austin Weird**: An unofficial slogan promoting support for local, independent businesses and the city's eccentric culture.
- **Live Music Capital of the World**: Austin's official slogan, referencing its high concentration of musicians and live music venues.
- **South by Southwest (SXSW)**: An annual conglomeration of parallel film, interactive media, and music festivals held in Austin.
- **Capitol View Corridors**: Zoning restrictions that preserve views of the Texas State Capitol from various locations around Austin.
- **Moonlight Towers**: Historic 165-foot towers built in the late 19th century to illuminate the city, now recognized as landmarks.
- **Highland Lakes**: A system of dams and reservoirs along the Colorado River created by the Lower Colorado River Authority (LCRA) for flood control, water supply, and power.
- **Balcones Escarpment**: A geological fault zone at the foot of which Austin is situated, influencing its topography and flood risk.
- **Freedomtown**: A term for original post-Civil War settlements founded by former African-American slaves, such as Clarksville in Austin.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Growth vs. Identity**: Ongoing rapid development and gentrification challenge Austin's bohemian roots and fuel nostalgia for "Old Austin," creating tension between preserving local culture and accommodating economic growth.
- **Political Redistricting**: The 2003 Texas redistricting split Austin among multiple congressional districts, a move opponents characterized as partisan gerrymandering. The Supreme Court upheld the plan in 2006.
- **Homelessness Policy**: The city has oscillated on public camping bans, lifting a 25-year ban in 2019 following a federal court ruling, then reinstating it via a 2021 ballot proposition after public debate.
- **Infrastructure and Resilience**: Events like the 2011 drought, 2018 water crisis, and 2021 winter storm have raised questions about the city's preparedness for climate-related disasters and infrastructure failures.

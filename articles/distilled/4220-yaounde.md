# Yaoundé

## Overview
Yaoundé is the capital city of Cameroon, serving as the nation's political and administrative center. With a population exceeding 2.8 million, it is the country's second-largest city after Douala. Its economy is primarily driven by government services and diplomacy, and it is a key hub for transportation, education, and culture in Central Africa.

## Body

### History and Founding
The earliest inhabitants of the region were likely the Baka people. The site that became Yaoundé was founded as a German trading post for rubber and ivory between 1887 and 1889. A military garrison built in 1895 facilitated colonization. After Germany's defeat in World War I, France administered the region and chose Yaoundé as its capital in 1922. Although Douala remained more prominent, Yaoundé grew rapidly after 1957 and continued as the capital upon Cameroon's independence in 1960.

### Economy and Infrastructure
Yaoundé's economy is centered on the civil service and diplomatic corps, giving it a higher standard of living than the rest of the country. Major industries include tobacco, dairy, beer, and timber, and it is a distribution center for agricultural products like coffee and cocoa. The city has faced chronic flooding, with over 130 major floods between 1980 and 2014. A sanitation master plan, initiated in 2010, has significantly reduced flood frequency and water-borne diseases. Transport links include two trans-African highways, a major international airport, and rail lines to Douala and N'Gaoundéré.

### Architecture and Culture
The city center contains government offices and the central market. The Bastos district houses foreign embassies, while the Presidential Palace is in the Etoudi district. Notable landmarks include the Reunification Monument and the Palais des Congrès. Cultural institutions include the Cameroon Art Museum, the National Museum, and the Mvog-Betsi Zoo-Botanic Park. The local language is Ewondo Populaire, a Beti-based pidgin.

### Education and Healthcare
Yaoundé hosts several universities, including the University of Yaoundé II and the Catholic University of Central Africa. The education system is bilingual, with both French and English systems. The largest hospital is the Central Hospital of Yaoundé, with 650 beds. Other major medical facilities include the Yaoundé General Hospital and the University Hospital Center.

- **Trans-African highway:** A planned network of roads crossing the African continent.
- **Baccalaureate:** The diploma granting access to university in the French educational system.

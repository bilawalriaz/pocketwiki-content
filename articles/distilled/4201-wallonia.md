# Wallonia

## Overview
Wallonia is the southern, primarily French-speaking region of Belgium, covering 55% of the country's territory but only 31% of its population. Historically the industrial heart of Belgium, its economic decline after World War II relative to the Flemish north has been a central factor in the country's modern political conflicts and federalization.

## Timeline
- **57 BC** — Julius Caesar conquers Gaul; the region becomes part of Gallia Belgica.
- **843** — Treaty of Verdun assigns the territory to Middle Francia, later Lotharingia.
- **1830** — Walloons play an active part in the Belgian Revolution, leading to independence.
- **19th c.** — Wallonia becomes the first fully industrialized area in continental Europe.
- **1960-61** — The Winter General Strike for Walloon autonomy triggers Belgium's state reforms.
- **1980** — The Walloon Region gains its own parliament and government.
- **1993** — Belgium becomes a federal state, formally establishing the Walloon Region.

## Body

### History and Identity
The region's ancestors, the Gallo-Romans, were called "Walha" (strangers) by Germanic neighbors, giving rise to the name Wallonia. A distinct language border between Romance and Germanic speakers crystallized between 700 and 1000 AD. After periods under Burgundian, Habsburg, and French rule, the region became part of the Kingdom of the Netherlands before the 1830 Belgian Revolution. The term "Wallonia" was first used with a political meaning of cultural affirmation in 1886.

### Economy and Industry
Wallonia's economy was built on coal and iron. In the 12th-13th centuries, Liège iron masters developed the "Walloon Method" for refining ore. The region's *sillon industriel* (industrial valley) along the Sambre and Meuse rivers made it the second industrial power in the world after the UK by the 19th century. This prosperity reversed after World War II as heavy industry declined, leading to high unemployment and a lower GDP per capita than Flanders. The economy is now diversified, with strengths in specialized manufacturing and logistics.

### Politics and Governance
Wallonia is one of Belgium's three regions, with its own parliament and government. It is politically left-leaning, in contrast to the more right-wing Flanders. The region's economic struggles fueled demands for autonomy, leading to major state reforms and the federalization of Belgium. The Walloon Region is distinct from, but closely linked to, the French Community of Belgium, which handles culture and education for French-speakers in Wallonia and Brussels.

### Geography and Demographics
Wallonia is landlocked and divided into three main geographical zones: the fertile Central Belgian Plateau to the north, the industrial Sambre and Meuse valley, and the forested Ardennes highlands to the south and east. The capital is Namur, and the largest city is Charleroi. A small German-speaking community (less than 1% of Belgium's population) resides in nine eastern municipalities, forming its own cultural community.

## Terms
- **Walloon Region**: The official federal region of Belgium covering the southern, French-speaking part of the country.
- **French Community of Belgium**: A political entity responsible for culture and education, encompassing Wallonia and the bilingual Brussels-Capital Region.
- **Sillon industriel**: The historical industrial backbone of Belgium, an east-west valley along the Sambre and Meuse rivers.
- **Langues d'oïl**: A group of Romance languages that evolved from Vulgar Latin in northern France and Wallonia, including Walloon, Picard, and Lorrain.
- **Mosan art**: A regional style of Romanesque art from the Meuse river valley, known for metalwork, enamel, and manuscript illumination.
- **Cordon sanitaire**: A media practice in Wallonia of excluding far-right politicians from interviews and live appearances.

## Debates and Open questions
- The economic and political inequalities between Wallonia and Flanders remain a major source of conflict, fueling Flemish separatism and ongoing debates about the future of the Belgian federal state.
- The relationship between the Walloon Region and the French Community of Belgium is complex; while constitutionally separate, they are governed by the same political individuals, and the French Community has controversially begun calling itself the "Wallonia-Brussels Federation."

# Bengaluru

## Overview
Bengaluru (formerly Bangalore) is the capital and largest city of Karnataka, India. It is a major economic and cultural hub, known globally as India's "Silicon Valley of India" for its dominant information technology sector. The city's history spans from ancient settlements to its modern status as one of the world's fastest-growing technology hubs.

## Timeline
- **c. 4000 BCE** — Archaeological evidence indicates early human settlement in the region.
- **890 CE** — First known inscription mentioning the name "Bengaluru" found at Begur.
- **1537 CE** — Kempe Gowda I establishes a mud fort, considered the foundation of the modern city.
- **1638 CE** — Adil Shahi army defeats Kempe Gowda III; city becomes a jagir of Shahaji.
- **1791 CE** — British forces under Charles Cornwallis capture the Bengaluru fort during the Third Anglo-Mysore War.
- **1809 CE** — British establish a military cantonment outside the old Pete.
- **1949 CE** — The "City" (Pete) and "Cantonment" merge under a single administration.
- **1956 CE** — Bengaluru becomes the capital of the newly formed Mysore State.
- **1973 CE** — Mysore State is renamed Karnataka, with Bengaluru as its capital.
- **2008 CE** — Kempegowda International Airport opens, replacing the old HAL Airport.
- **2011 CE** — Namma Metro, South India's first operational metro, opens.
- **2014 CE** — Official name changed from Bangalore to Bengaluru.

## Body

### Early History and Foundation
The region shows evidence of human settlement from around 4000 BCE. It was ruled by successive South Indian kingdoms, including the Western Ganga dynasty (from 350 CE), the Chola Empire (early 11th century), the Hoysalas, and the Vijayanagara Empire. The modern city was founded in 1537 CE by Kempe Gowda I, a feudal lord under the Vijayanagara Empire, who built a mud fort and established the initial town divisions known as *petes*.

### Colonial and Princely Rule
After the fall of the Vijayanagara Empire, the city passed through the control of the Adil Shahi dynasty, the Mughals, and then the Kingdom of Mysore under the Wodeyar dynasty and later Hyder Ali and Tipu Sultan. The British East India Company captured the fort in 1791. Following Tipu Sultan's defeat in 1799, the city became part of the Princely State of Mysore. The British established a separate cantonment in 1809, creating two distinct urban entities: the native Pete and the British Cantonment.

### Modern Growth and IT Hub
After Indian independence in 1947, Bengaluru became the capital of Mysore State (later Karnataka). The two urban entities merged in 1949. The city experienced rapid growth in the post-independence decades, driven by public sector industries and educational institutions. From the late 1980s, the information technology (IT) industry transformed the city, establishing it as the "Silicon Valley of India." This led to massive migration, infrastructure challenges, and significant economic expansion, with the metropolitan area's GDP estimated at $590.4 billion in 2024.

### Geography and Climate
Bengaluru is located on the Deccan Plateau at an average elevation of 900 m (2,953 ft). It has a tropical savanna climate with distinct wet and dry seasons, enjoying a generally moderate climate due to its altitude. The city has faced environmental challenges, including the depletion of its historic lakes (from 265 in the 1960s to 98 by the late 2010s) and periodic water shortages, such as the crisis in early 2024.

### Demographics and Culture
As per the 2011 census, the city had a population of 8.4 million, making it India's third-most populous city. It is highly diverse, with significant populations of Kannadigas, Tamils, Telugus, and migrants from across India. Kannada is the most widely spoken mother tongue (42.1%), followed by Tamil, Telugu, and Urdu. The city's culture is a blend of traditional South Indian arts and a modern, cosmopolitan lifestyle, known for its vibrant music scene, theatre, and cuisine.

### Administration and Infrastructure
The city is administered by the Bruhat Bengaluru Mahanagara Palike (BBMP), which in 2025 was reorganized into five city corporations under the Greater Bengaluru Authority (GBA). Key infrastructure includes the Namma Metro rapid transit system, the Kempegowda International Airport, and an extensive road network. The city faces significant infrastructure strains from rapid growth, including traffic congestion and waste management issues.

## Terms
- **Pete**: The traditional market town or commercial area established by Kempe Gowda I in 1537, forming the core of the old city.
- **Cantonment**: A British military and civil station established in 1809, developed separately from the native Pete.
- **Jagir**: A feudal estate or land grant given to a ruler or noble in exchange for military service or revenue.
- **Silicon Valley of India**: A moniker for Bengaluru due to its status as the country's largest and most prominent information technology (IT) hub.
- **Namma Metro**: Bengaluru's rapid transit rail system, which opened in 2011 and is the first operational metro in South India.
- **Bruhat Bengaluru Mahanagara Palike (BBMP)**: The municipal corporation responsible for civic administration and infrastructure of Bengaluru.
- **Bangalore Metropolitan Region Development Authority (BMRDA)**: The agency responsible for planning the development of the entire Bengaluru metropolitan region.
- **Kaveri (Cauvery) Water Supply Scheme**: The primary source of piped water for the city, drawing from the Kaveri River basin.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Etymology of "Bengaluru"**: The origin of the name is debated. Theories include a derivation from "benda-kāl-ūru" (town of boiled beans), from "benga" (the Kannada term for the Indian Kino Tree), or from local temples or stones.
- **Urban Governance and Infrastructure**: The city grapples with challenges from its rapid, often unplanned growth, including severe traffic congestion, inadequate public transport, water scarcity, and waste management problems. The recent administrative restructuring into multiple corporations is a response to these governance challenges.
- **Cultural and Linguistic Identity**: As a cosmopolitan city with a large migrant population, there are ongoing debates and occasional controversies regarding the use of Kannada versus English and other languages in public signage, education, and administration.

# Tegucigalpa

## Overview
Tegucigalpa is the capital and largest city of Honduras, forming a single political entity with its sister city, Comayagüela, known as the Central District. It serves as the nation's political, administrative, and economic center, but its rapid, often unplanned growth has led to significant challenges in infrastructure, public services, and security.

## Timeline
- **29 September 1578** — Spanish settlers claim the site, founding Real de Minas de San Miguel de Tegucigalpa.
- **10 June 1762** — The mining town is elevated to Real Villa de San Miguel de Tegucigalpa y Heredia.
- **1821** — Tegucigalpa legally becomes a city.
- **1824** — The first Congress declares Tegucigalpa and Comayagua will alternate as the national capital.
- **30 October 1880** — President Marco Aurelio Soto declares Tegucigalpa the permanent capital.
- **30 January 1937** — The 1936 constitution establishes Tegucigalpa and Comayagüela as the Central District.
- **30 October 1998** — Hurricane Mitch devastates the capital, causing massive flooding and landslides.
- **15 October 2021** — Comayagua International Airport opens, intended to replace Toncontín for international flights.

## Body

### History and Etymology
Founded on a native Lenca and Tolupan settlement, Tegucigalpa was established as a Spanish mining town in 1578. Its name likely derives from the Nahuatl language, with the most accepted theory being *taguz-galpa* ("hills of silver"), though this is debated. The city's status fluctuated until it was declared the permanent capital in 1880, a move often attributed to President Soto's business ties with a nearby American mining company. The 1936 constitution formally merged Tegucigalpa and Comayagüela into the Central District, a union reaffirmed by the 1982 constitution.

### Geography and Climate
The city is situated in a valley in the southern-central highlands, surrounded by mountains and bisected by the Choluteca River, which separates Tegucigalpa from Comayagüela. It has a tropical savanna climate with distinct dry (November–April) and rainy (May–October) seasons. The valley's topography traps pollution and makes the city prone to flooding, a vulnerability catastrophically demonstrated by Hurricane Mitch in 1998.

### Urban Structure and Demographics
The Central District is divided into hundreds of *barrios* (older neighborhoods) and *colonias* (newer suburbs). Tegucigalpa's side is split into historic, contemporary, and peripheral zones, while Comayagüela is generally less developed. The 2013 census recorded a population of 1,157,509. The city is predominantly mestizo (80%) and faces high poverty rates (47.6% in 2006). It has the highest Human Development Index in Honduras but also suffers from extreme violence, with one of the world's highest murder rates.

### Economy and Infrastructure
The Central District's economy accounts for 19.3% of Honduras's GDP, driven by commerce, services, and manufacturing. It hosts national and international banks, including the headquarters of the Central American Bank for Economic Integration. Infrastructure has not kept pace with growth, leading to severe traffic congestion from over 400,000 daily vehicles. The public transportation system is oversupplied and disorganized, with a planned bus rapid transit (BRT) system intended to provide an upgrade.

### Government and Services
As the capital, the city houses all three branches of the national government. The Central District is governed by a mayor and a Municipal Corporation. Law enforcement is handled by the National Police, but crime and gang activity remain pervasive challenges. The city is the national education hub, hosting the National Autonomous University of Honduras (UNAH) and numerous private schools and universities.

## Terms
- **Central District (Distrito Central):** The single municipality formed by the merger of Tegucigalpa and Comayagüela, which together constitute the capital of Honduras.
- **Barrio:** An older, typically inner-city neighborhood.
- **Colonia:** A newer, often middle-class residential suburb.
- **Mestizo:** A person of mixed European and Indigenous American ancestry; the predominant ethnic group in the city.
- **Maquiladora:** A manufacturing plant in a free-trade zone that imports materials for assembly and export.
- **Human Development Index (HDI):** A composite statistic of life expectancy, education, and per capita income used to rank regions by development.
- **Bus Rapid Transit (BRT):** A high-capacity bus-based public transport system designed to improve efficiency and capacity over conventional bus services.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The etymology of "Tegucigalpa" is uncertain, with competing theories from Nahuatl and other languages.
- The primary reason for the 1880 capital relocation from Comayagua is debated, with theories ranging from a personal vendetta by President Soto to his business interests in a nearby mining company.
- The long-term effectiveness of infrastructure projects, such as the proposed BRT system, in alleviating the city's chronic congestion and disorganized growth remains to be seen.

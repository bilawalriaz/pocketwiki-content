# Rhine

## Overview
The Rhine is one of Europe's major rivers, stretching approximately 1,230 km from the Swiss Alps to the North Sea. It has served as a critical trade route, cultural boundary, and political border for millennia, shaping the history and development of the regions it flows through. Its basin drains 185,000 km² across four countries, and it remains a vital commercial waterway, transporting millions of tonnes of cargo annually.

## Timeline
- **c. 180 million years ago** — Opening of the Tethys Ocean sets the stage for the Alpine orogeny.
- **Miocene period** — The first Rhine river system develops in the Upper Rhine Graben, not yet draining the Alps.
- **Early Pleistocene** — The Rhine captures most of its current Alpine watershed from the Rhône via stream capture.
- **c. 450,000 years ago** — During the Anglian glaciation, the Rhine's course is diverted through the English Channel.
- **12 BC** — Roman general Drusus establishes military camps along the Rhine, beginning the Germanic Wars.
- **AD 50** — Cologne is acknowledged as a Roman city (Colonia Claudia Ara Agrippinensium).
- **1421** — The St. Elizabeth's flood alters the course of the Meuse and Rhine delta.
- **1817** — Treaty for the Rectification of the Rhine flow is signed among German and French states.
- **1939** — The "Rhine-kilometer" measurement scale is introduced.
- **2002** — The Rhine Gorge is designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
- **2026** — Severe drought exposes historic artifacts and hunger stones along the riverbed.

## Body

### Name and Etymology
The name "Rhine" derives from the Gaulish *Rēnos*, itself from a Proto-Indo-European root meaning "to move, flow." Latin *Rhenus* and Greek *Rhēnos* were classical adaptations. Modern variants like German *Rhein* and French *Rhin* reflect Greek orthography, while the vowel shift to "-i-" comes from Proto-Germanic adoption as *Rīnaz*. The name remains masculine in most modern languages.

### Geography and Course
The Rhine's length is conventionally measured at 1,036.20 km from Konstanz to the Hook of Holland, though its total natural length is about 1,232 km. Its course is divided into distinct sections:
*   **Headwaters:** The river is traditionally considered to begin at Lake Toma (Lai da Tuma) in the Swiss Alps, flowing as the Anterior Rhine (Vorderrhein). It is joined by the Posterior Rhine (Hinterrhein) near Reichenau to form the Alpine Rhine.
*   **Lake Constance:** The Alpine Rhine flows into Lake Constance, a tri-national lake. The Rhine's cold, sediment-laden water creates a visible plume before exiting via the short Seerhein into the Untersee.
*   **High Rhine:** From Stein am Rhein, the river flows west, forming the Swiss-German border. It passes the Rhine Falls and is joined by the Aare, which more than doubles its discharge.
*   **Upper Rhine:** At Basel, the river turns north. This section, flowing through the Upper Rhine Plain, was heavily engineered in the 19th century to straighten its course and reduce flooding. It forms the Franco-German border before flowing entirely within Germany.
*   **Middle Rhine:** Between Bingen and Bonn, the river cuts through the Rhine Gorge, a UNESCO site known for its castles and vineyards. It receives major tributaries like the Moselle.
*   **Lower Rhine:** From Bonn, the river enters the North German Plain, flowing through the industrial Rhine-Ruhr region. Its largest inland port is at Duisburg.
*   **Delta:** In the Netherlands, the Rhine splits into three main distributaries: the Waal (carrying two-thirds of the flow), the Nederrijn/Lek, and the IJssel. The Rhine-Meuse-Scheldt delta is Europe's largest.

### Geologic History
The Rhine's watershed developed through the Alpine orogeny, which began in the Oligocene. A north-south rift system, including the Upper Rhine Graben, formed. Through millions of years of stream capture, the Rhine extended its watershed southward to the Alps, capturing rivers from the Rhône and Danube. During Ice Ages, lower sea levels caused the Rhine to flow through a dry English Channel to the Atlantic. The current delta began forming about 8,000 years ago as sea levels rose.

### Military and Cultural History
The Rhine formed the northern boundary of the Roman Empire, separating Gaul from Germania. Roman legions were stationed along its banks. After the Roman period, it became a border within the Frankish Empire and later the Holy Roman Empire. Its castles attest to its importance as a trade route. In the 19th century, it became a symbol of German nationalism. The Treaty of Versailles made the Rhineland a demilitarized zone after World War I. In World War II, its bridges were key military objectives, such as at Arnhem and Remagen.

## Terms
*   **Alpine Orogeny:** The mountain-building process that formed the Alps, which began in the Oligocene and continues today, shaping the Rhine's watershed.
*   **Stream Capture:** A geological process where a river erodes and diverts the flow of a neighboring stream, which is how the Rhine acquired its Alpine tributaries.
*   **Rhine-kilometer:** A measurement scale introduced in 1939, running from 0 km at Konstanz to 1,036.20 km at the Hook of Holland.
*   **Upper Rhine Graben:** A major rift valley in southwest Germany and eastern France through which the Upper Rhine flows.
*   **Avulsion:** The rapid abandonment of a river channel and the formation of a new one, a key process in shaping the Rhine delta.
*   **Delta Works:** A series of Dutch construction projects, begun in the 20th century, to protect the Rhine-Meuse delta from flooding.

## Debates and Open Questions
The International Commission for the Hydrology of the Rhine Basin (CHR) and EUWID contend that the river could experience a massive decrease in volume, or even dry up completely in case of drought, within the next 30 to 80 years as a result of the climate crisis. The exact length of the Rhine was a point of confusion until 2010, when a 1932 typographical error (1,320 km) was corrected to the accepted 1,232 km.

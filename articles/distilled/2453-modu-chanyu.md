# Modu Chanyu

## Overview
Modu Chanyu (c. 234–174 BCE) was the founder of the Xiongnu Empire, a powerful nomadic state on the Mongolian Plateau. He rose to power by killing his father and then unified the Mongolian-Manchurian tribes, creating a formidable empire that became a major rival to the early Han dynasty of China. His reign established the Xiongnu as a dominant force in Central Eurasia for centuries.

## Timeline
- **c. 234 BCE** — Modu is born.
- **215 BCE** — Qin forces under Meng Tian invade and seize Xiongnu pasture lands.
- **209 BCE** — Modu kills his father, Touman, and proclaims himself chanyu.
- **208 BCE** — Modu defeats the Donghu, splitting them into the Xianbei and Wuhuan.
- **203 BCE** — Modu subdues the Yuezhi.
- **200 BCE** — Battle of Baideng: Modu surrounds Han Emperor Gaozu, leading to a peace treaty (heqin).
- **192 BCE** — Modu sends a provocative marriage proposal to Empress Dowager Lü Zhi.
- **178 BCE** — Xiongnu forces overrun the Yuezhi and Wusun in Gansu and the Tarim Basin.
- **174 BCE** — Modu dies and is succeeded by his son, Laoshang Chanyu.

## Body

### Origins and Rise to Power
Modu was the son of the Xiongnu leader Touman. His father, favoring another son, sent Modu as a hostage to the Yuezhi and then attacked them, hoping Modu would be killed. Modu escaped, returned as a hero, and was given command of 10,000 horsemen. To secure absolute loyalty, he trained his warriors to shoot a whistling arrow (*mingjian*). He tested their obedience by ordering them to shoot his favorite horse and then his favorite wife, executing those who hesitated. Once assured of their loyalty, he had his warriors shoot and kill his father during a hunt, seizing the title of chanyu. He then purged potential rivals, including his half-brother and step-mother.

### Rise of the Xiongnu Empire
Modu aggressively expanded his territory. He first attacked the Donghu to the east, defeating them by 208 BCE and causing their remnants to split into the Xianbei and Wuhuan tribes. He then subdued northern peoples like the Dingling and defeated the Yuezhi in 203 BCE, bringing all Xiongnu lords under his control. These conquests gave him control of vital trade routes, providing significant income.

### War with the Han Dynasty
In 200 BCE, a Han vassal king surrendered to the Xiongnu. In response, Han Emperor Gaozu led a large army north but became isolated at Pingcheng with only 40,000 men. Modu surrounded him with 40,000 cavalry but eventually withdrew, allowing Gaozu to escape. This near-capture convinced the Han to adopt a policy of *heqin* (peace through marriage alliances), sending "princesses" and tribute (silk, grain) to the Xiongnu in exchange for peace. Modu continued to exert pressure, forcing the Yuezhi and Wusun into vassalage and raiding Han territory.

### Analysis of the Xiongnu's Rise
The Xiongnu's rise was facilitated by a sequence of events. The Qin invasion of the Ordos Plateau in 215 BCE prompted a militarization of the Xiongnu, which Modu then centralized into a powerful political structure. The rapid collapse of the Qin dynasty and the initial weakness of the Han, which supplied a steady flow of tribute, allowed Modu and his successors to expand and maintain control over their vast empire.

## Terms
- **Chanyu**: The supreme ruler of the Xiongnu Empire.
- **Heqin**: A Chinese diplomatic policy of forming marriage alliances with nomadic groups, often involving sending a princess and tribute in exchange for peace.
- **Mingjian**: A whistling signaling arrow invented by Modu to coordinate his warriors' attacks.
- **Xiongnu**: A nomadic people who formed a powerful empire on the Mongolian Plateau, rivaling ancient China.
- **Ordos Plateau**: A region within the bend of the Yellow River, a key area of conflict between the Xiongnu and Chinese states.
- **Donghu**: An eastern neighbor of the Xiongnu, defeated and split by Modu into the Xianbei and Wuhuan.
- **Yuezhi**: A people originally located in the Gansu corridor, subdued by Modu and later driven west by the Xiongnu.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The exact reason for Modu's withdrawal at the Battle of Baideng is unclear; Sima Qian suggests his consort persuaded him, but a prolonged siege may also have been impractical.
- The name "Modu" is reconstructed in Old Chinese and may represent a foreign word like *baɣatur* ("hero"), but its precise etymology and connection to later names (e.g., Oghuz Khagan, Attila's ancestor) are subjects of scholarly speculation.
- Some historians argue Modu established the first fully developed decimal military organization, based on Chinese sources, but this is a point of analysis rather than a definitive fact from the records.

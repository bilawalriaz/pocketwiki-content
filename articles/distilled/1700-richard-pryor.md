# Richard Pryor

## Overview
Richard Pryor (1940–2005) was an American stand-up comedian and actor widely considered one of the most influential performers in comedy history. He transformed the field by abandoning conventional "middlebrow" jokes for raw, autobiographical storytelling that confronted race, sexuality, drug addiction, and personal trauma with profane intimacy. His five Grammy-winning comedy albums and concert films—particularly *That Nigger's Crazy* (1974) and *Live on the Sunset Strip* (1982)—established a new standard for vulnerability in performance. Despite a life marked by severe substance abuse, a near-fatal 1980 freebasing accident, and multiple sclerosis, Pryor maintained a prolific acting career, notably in films with Gene Wilder, and received the inaugural Kennedy Center Mark Twain Prize for American Humor in 1998.

## Body

### Early Life and Formative Trauma
Pryor was born in Peoria, Illinois, into an environment of profound instability. His grandmother, Marie Carter, ran a brothel where he was raised alongside three siblings; his mother, Gertrude (of Puerto Rican descent), was a prostitute, and his father, LeRoy "Buck Carter" Pryor, was a former boxer, hustler, and pimp. After his mother lost custody when he was 10, his strict grandmother raised him, though she physically punished his eccentricities. At age seven, he was sexually abused; at 14, he was expelled from school. A pivotal intervention came from Juliette Whittaker, director of the Carver Center theater program, who mentored him with the philosophy: "You shoot for the moon, you’re bound to hit a star." Pryor enlisted in the Army in 1958 but served virtually his entire two-year stint in military prison in West Germany after he and other Black soldiers attacked a white soldier who was laughing at racially charged scenes in the film *Imitation of Life*.

### The Evolution of a Comic Voice (1963–1973)
Arriving in New York in 1963, Pryor initially modeled his act on Bill Cosby’s clean, middlebrow style, appearing on *The Ed Sullivan Show* and *The Tonight Show*. A breakthrough in vulnerability occurred early when Nina Simone, witnessing his paralyzing stage fright at the Village Gate, rocked him "like a baby" to calm him. By 1967, despite Las Vegas success, Pryor suffered an onstage epiphany at the Aladdin Hotel: realizing he was performing a lie, he exclaimed, "What the fuck am I doing here!?" and walked off. He subsequently integrated profanity and the word "nigger" into his act, aligning with the counterculture after moving to Berkeley in 1969, where he associated with Huey P. Newton and Ishmael Reed. During this period, he wrote for *Sanford and Son* and *The Flip Wilson Show*, winning an Emmy for a 1973 Lily Tomlin special.

### Breakthrough Albums and *Blazing Saddles* Controversy (1974–1979)
Pryor’s commercial peak began with *That Nigger's Crazy* (1974), recorded for Stax/Warner Bros. after a legal battle with his former label, Laff Records. It won the first of five consecutive Grammys for Best Comedy Album, followed by *...Is It Something I Said?* (1975) and *Bicentennial Nigger* (1976). He co-wrote Mel Brooks’s *Blazing Saddles* (1974) but was denied the lead role of Bart. Brooks claimed Warner executives vetoed Pryor as "uninsurable" due to drug issues, though no executive corroborated this; director Michael Schultz alleged Brooks "chased him out" to claim sole writing credit. Pryor became the first Black host of *Saturday Night Live* in 1975, famously performing the "word association" sketch with Chevy Chase. His own variety show, *The Richard Pryor Show* (1977), was cancelled after four episodes because Pryor refused to censor controversial material, including a sketch portraying a Black President and a nude visual distortion bit. A 1979 trip to Kenya prompted him to swear off using the word "nigger" in his stand-up.

### The Freebasing Accident and 1980s Career
On June 9, 1980, while freebasing cocaine during the filming of *Stir Crazy*, Pryor doused himself in 151-proof rum and set himself on fire, suffering second- and third-degree burns over half his body. (Police cited a freebasing explosion; Pryor later joked it was caused by dunking a cookie in low-fat milk.) He spent six weeks in the Grossman Burn Center. The incident forced his replacement in *History of the World, Part I* and *The Muppet Show*. He returned within a year, directing and starring in *Richard Pryor: Live on the Sunset Strip* (1982) and the semi-autobiographical film *Jo Jo Dancer, Your Life Is Calling* (1986). A $40 million Columbia Pictures deal (1983) yielded softer vehicles like *Superman III*, *Brewster's Millions*, and *See No Evil, Hear No Evil*. He briefly hosted the children's show *Pryor's Place* (1984), which tackled sober inner-city issues but was quickly cancelled. On set, Pryor developed a reputation for erratic behavior; Gene Wilder alleged Pryor used accusations of racism—such as a watermelon-throwing incident—to leverage money and concessions from producers.

### Decline, Illness, and Final Years
Diagnosed with multiple sclerosis in 1986, Pryor relied on a mobility scooter by the mid-1990s, joking "MS stands for 'More Shit'." He suffered heart attacks in 1977, 1990 (in Australia), and fatally in 2005, undergoing triple bypass surgery in 1991. His final film role was a cameo in David Lynch’s *Lost Highway* (1997). In 2002, Pryor and his wife/manager Jennifer Lee won legal rights to 40 hours of master tapes from Laff Records, enabling the authorized compilation *Evolution/Revolution: The Early Years* (2005). His last TV appearance was a 1999 episode of *The Norm Show*.

### Personal Life: Marriages, Sexuality, and Addiction
Pryor married seven times to five women: Patricia Price (1960–61), Shelley Bonus (1967–69), Deborah McGuire (1977–78), Jennifer Lee (1981–82; remarried 2001 until death), and Flynn Belaine (1986–87, 1990–91). He fathered seven children with six women. Regarding sexuality, Pryor discussed homosexual experiences in his 1971 debut *Live & Smokin'* and at a 1977 Hollywood Bowl gay rights rally, stating, "I have sucked a dick" and describing a 1952 encounter at age 11. Posthumously, biographer Scott Saul (2014), Quincy Jones, and Jennifer Lee (2018) stated Pryor acknowledged bisexuality and had a relationship with Marlon Brando; daughter Rain Pryor disputed this. Pryor’s substance abuse centered on freebasing cocaine in the late 1970s; Jennifer Lee recalled the drug "became his lover and everything. I did not exist." He was a vegetarian and active PETA supporter, campaigning against KFC and Burger King.

### Legacy and Honors
Peers regard Pryor as the pivotal figure in modern comedy. Jerry Seinfeld called him "the Picasso of our profession"; Dave Chappelle likened him to "the dude walking upright" on an evolution chart. Bill Cosby noted Pryor "drew the line between comedy and tragedy as thin as one could possibly paint it." Honors include the inaugural Mark Twain Prize (1998), #1 on Comedy Central’s and *Rolling Stone*’s greatest stand-up lists, a Grammy Lifetime Achievement Award (2006), a bronze statue in Peoria (2015), and induction into the National Comedy Center (2022). Numerous documentaries—*The Funny Life of Richard Pryor* (2002), *Omit the Logic* (2013), *I Am Richard Pryor* (2019)—chronicle his influence on generations including Chris Rock, Eddie Murphy, and Wanda Sykes.

## Terms
- **Freebasing**: A method of smoking cocaine hydrochloride converted to a base form (freebase) using ether or ammonia, producing an intense, rapid high; highly addictive and dangerous due to flammable solvents.
- **Mark Twain Prize for American Humor**: The Kennedy Center’s highest comedy honor, awarded annually since 1998 to individuals who impact American society in the spirit of Twain; Pryor was the first recipient.
- **Laff Records**: A small, independent comedy label (run by Louis Drozen) that signed Pryor early; later released unauthorized "bootleg" albums capitalizing on his Warner Bros. fame.
- **Multiple Sclerosis (MS)**: A chronic autoimmune disease damaging the central nervous system; caused Pryor’s mobility loss, requiring a scooter from the mid-1990s onward.
- **Grossman Burn Center**: Specialized unit at Sherman Oaks Hospital (Los Angeles) where Pryor spent six weeks recovering from 1980 burns covering >50% of his body.
- **Word Association Sketch**: Famous *SNL* (1975) segment where Pryor and Chevy Chase escalate a job interview into a slur-shouting match, satirizing racial power dynamics.
- **Blazing Saddles (1974)**: Mel Brooks western satire; Pryor co-wrote but was denied the starring role (given to Cleavon Little), a dispute involving allegations of studio racism vs. director credit-hoarding.
- **Pryor's Place (1984)**: Short-lived CBS children’s show featuring puppets (Sid & Marty Krofft) in an inner-city setting; addressed serious issues like poverty but cancelled quickly.
- **Jo Jo Dancer, Your Life Is Calling (1986)**: Pryor’s written/directed semi-autobiographical drama fictionalizing his 1980 freebasing accident and childhood trauma.
- **Evolution/Revolution: The Early Years (2005)**: Authorized 2-CD compilation of Pryor’s 1966–1974 Laff Records material, released after he regained master rights in 2002.

## Debates and open questions
- **Blazing Saddles casting**: Brooks claims Warner Bros. executives blocked Pryor as "uninsurable" due to drug arrests; no studio executive from that era has corroborated this. Director Michael Schultz and others allege Brooks maneuvered to keep the lead role for himself (or a non-Black actor) to secure sole directorial credit.
- **1980 accident cause**: Pryor and some sources describe pouring rum and self-immolating; Los Angeles police and medical reports cite a freebasing cocaine explosion. Pryor’s daughter Rain attributes it to drug-induced psychosis.
- **Sexuality**: Posthumous claims by Quincy Jones, Jennifer Lee, and biographer Scott Saul assert Pryor was bisexual and had a relationship with Marlon Brando. Pryor’s own autobiography and stand-up reference homosexual encounters (including at age 11), but daughter Rain Pryor disputes the bisexuality label, suggesting denial or differing interpretation.
- **On-set behavior vs. racism**: Gene Wilder and crew members describe Pryor as manipulative, using racism allegations (e.g., the watermelon incident on *Stir Crazy*) to extract money/concessions. The extent to which this reflects genuine encounters with racism versus drug-fueled leverage tactics remains contested by witnesses.

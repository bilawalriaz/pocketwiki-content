# Dubai

## Overview
Dubai is the most populous city in the United Arab Emirates (UAE) and a major global hub for trade, tourism, and finance. Its transformation from a small pearling and fishing settlement into a modern metropolis with a skyline of world-record-breaking skyscrapers is a story of strategic economic diversification, leveraging oil revenues to build non-oil industries like real estate, aviation, and tourism.

## Timeline
- **Early 18th century** — Established as a pearling and fishing village.
- **1833** — Al Bu Falasah tribe, led by Maktoum bin Butti, secedes from Abu Dhabi and establishes the Maktoum dynasty in Dubai.
- **1892** — Dubai enters an exclusivity agreement with the United Kingdom for security.
- **1901** — Maktoum bin Hasher Al Maktoum establishes Dubai as a free port with no taxation.
- **1961** — Dubai Creek is extended to accommodate larger vessels.
- **1966** — Oil is discovered in territorial waters off Dubai.
- **1971** — Dubai joins five other Trucial States to form the United Arab Emirates.
- **1979** — Jebel Ali port, a deep-water port, is established.
- **1985** — Jebel Ali Free Zone (JAFZA) is created around the port.
- **2010** — The Burj Khalifa, the world's tallest building, opens.
- **2021-2022** — Expo 2020 Dubai is held (postponed due to COVID-19).

## Body

### History and Foundation
The area shows ancient settlement, with archaeological finds from periods like the Umm Al Nar and Iron Age. The modern emirate was founded in 1833 by the Maktoum dynasty. Dubai's early economy was based on pearling and trade. A pivotal moment came in 1901 when it declared itself a free port, attracting merchants and establishing itself as a regional trade hub. The pearling industry collapsed in the 1930s, causing economic depression.

### Oil Discovery and Development
Oil was discovered in 1966, though in smaller quantities than in neighboring Abu Dhabi. Oil revenue, flowing from 1969, funded massive infrastructure projects like Port Rashid (opened 1972) and the expansion of Dubai Creek. Crucially, Sheikh Rashid bin Saeed Al Maktoum used oil wealth to diversify the economy before reserves were depleted; oil accounted for less than 1% of GDP by 2018.

### Formation of the UAE and Modern Growth
Following the British withdrawal from the region, Dubai was a key founder of the UAE in 1971. The 1980s and 1990s saw further diversification with the creation of Jebel Ali Free Zone (1985) and the expansion of Dubai International Airport. The late 1990s and 2000s triggered a construction boom, producing artificial islands (Palm Jumeirah, The World) and the world's tallest skyscrapers, including the Burj Khalifa (2010). This period solidified Dubai's global brand as a luxury tourism and business destination.

### Economy and Real Estate
Dubai's economy is dominated by trade, tourism, aviation, financial services, and real estate. The Dubai International Financial Centre (DIFC) is a major global financial hub. The real estate market is a significant economic driver but has experienced volatility, including a major downturn in 2008-2009. Investigations like "Dubai Uncovered" have highlighted concerns about the market being used for money laundering by sanctioned individuals and criminals, exploiting regulatory gaps. Despite this, property transactions reached an all-time high in 2024.

### Demographics and Society
Dubai has a population of 4 million (2025), of which 92% are expatriates. The largest expatriate groups are from South Asia. Arabic is the official language, but English is widely used. Islam is the state religion, but the constitution provides for freedom of worship for other faiths. The city has a reputation for luxury and opulence, driven by its tourism and entrepreneurial culture.

### Architecture and Landmarks
Rapid construction has created one of the world's densest skylines. Key landmarks include the Burj Khalifa (828 m, world's tallest building), the Burj Al Arab hotel, and extensive artificial islands like Palm Jumeirah. Land reclamation has added over 300 km of artificial coastline. The city is recognized as a UNESCO "Design City."

### Transportation
Dubai is a major global transport hub. Dubai International Airport (DXB) is the world's busiest for international passenger traffic. The city has an extensive road network, a driverless metro system (opened 2009), a monorail, and a tramway. Jebel Ali port is the largest human-made harbour in the world. The Roads and Transport Authority (RTA) manages public transport.

## Terms
- **Free Port/Zone:** An area where goods can be landed, handled, manufactured, or reconfigured and re-exported without the intervention of customs authorities, often with tax exemptions.
- **Entrepôt:** A port, city, or other center to which goods are brought for import and export, and for collection and distribution.
- **Trucial States:** A group of sheikhdoms in the Persian Gulf that were under British protection from 1820 until the formation of the UAE in 1971.
- **Maktoum dynasty:** The ruling family of Dubai since 1833.
- **Land reclamation:** The process of creating new land from the sea, riverbeds, or wetlands.
- **Sukuk:** Islamic financial certificates, similar to bonds, that comply with Sharia law.
- **DIFC (Dubai International Financial Centre):** A federal financial free zone in Dubai with its own independent judicial system and financial regulator.
- **JAFZA (Jebel Ali Free Zone):** A free zone established in 1985 around Jebel Ali Port, allowing unrestricted import of labor and export of capital for foreign companies.

## Debates and open questions
- The origin of the name "Dubai" is debated, with theories linking it to a souq, a word for money, "two brothers," or "baby locust."
- The long-term sustainability of Dubai's economic model, heavily reliant on real estate, tourism, and finance, remains a subject of analysis, especially given the volatility of its property market.
- The city's regulatory framework for real estate and finance has faced scrutiny over its adequacy in preventing illicit financial flows, as highlighted by investigative reports.

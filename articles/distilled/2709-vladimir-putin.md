# Vladimir Putin

## Overview
Vladimir Putin is a Russian politician and former intelligence officer who has dominated Russia's political landscape since 1999. Serving as President or Prime Minister for most of the past two decades, he transformed Russia from a post-Soviet state into an authoritarian power with a centralized, personality-driven government. His rule is marked by economic growth fueled by energy exports, the reassertion of Russian influence abroad, and the systematic suppression of domestic political opposition and independent media.

## Timeline
- **7 October 1952** — Born in Leningrad (now Saint Petersburg).
- **1975** — Joins the KGB; serves as a foreign intelligence officer, including in Dresden, East Germany (1985–1990).
- **20 August 1991** — Resigns from the KGB during the Soviet coup attempt.
- **1990–1996** — Works in the Saint Petersburg administration under Mayor Anatoly Sobchak.
- **1996** — Moves to Moscow to join President Boris Yeltsin's administration.
- **August 1999** — Appointed Prime Minister of Russia.
- **31 December 1999** — Becomes Acting President after Yeltsin's resignation.
- **March 2000** — Elected President of Russia for the first term.
- **2008–2012** — Serves as Prime Minister under President Dmitry Medvedev.
- **March 2012** — Returns to the presidency for a third term.
- **2014** — Russia annexes Crimea from Ukraine.
- **February 2022** — Launches a full-scale invasion of Ukraine.
- **March 2024** — Reelected to another presidential term in an election marked by allegations of fraud.

## Body

### Early Life and Intelligence Career
Born in Leningrad, Putin's early life was shaped by World War II; his maternal grandmother was killed by German occupiers, and his paternal brother died during the Siege of Leningrad. He studied law at Leningrad State University, graduating in 1975, the same year he joined the KGB. After training, he worked in counterintelligence and later foreign intelligence. From 1985 to 1990, he served in Dresden, East Germany, as a liaison to the Stasi. He resigned from the KGB in August 1991 during the coup attempt against Mikhail Gorbachev.

### Rise to Power
Putin's political career began in Saint Petersburg in 1990 as an advisor to Mayor Anatoly Sobchak, his former university professor. He moved to Moscow in 1996 after Sobchak's electoral defeat. His ascent was rapid: he became deputy chief of the Presidential Staff in 1997, director of the Federal Security Service (FSB) in 1998, and Secretary of the Security Council in 1999. Appointed Prime Minister in August 1999, he was endorsed by Yeltsin as his successor. Following Yeltsin's resignation on New Year's Eve 1999, Putin became Acting President and won the subsequent election in March 2000.

### Domestic Policy and Governance
Putin's domestic agenda focused on creating a "vertical of power," centralizing control over Russia's federal subjects and curbing the influence of regional governors and business oligarchs. His administration has been characterized as an "authoritarian dictatorship" with a strong personality cult. The political system has been transformed to eliminate meaningful opposition, with laws targeting "foreign agents," restricting protests, and censoring media. The economy, initially boosted by high oil prices and reforms, has faced stagnation and financial crises due to international sanctions following the annexation of Crimea and the invasion of Ukraine. By 2023, nearly 40% of public expenditures were planned for defense and security.

### Foreign Policy and Conflicts
Putin's foreign policy has sought to reassert Russia as a global power and challenge the U.S.-led international order. Key actions include the 2008 war with Georgia, the 2014 annexation of Crimea and support for separatists in eastern Ukraine, and the 2015 military intervention in Syria to support President Bashar al-Assad. The full-scale invasion of Ukraine in February 2022 led to widespread international condemnation, expanded sanctions, and Russia's growing isolation from Western economies. Relations with the West have deteriorated sharply, while ties with China have deepened into a "special relationship." Russia has also pursued closer ties with various nations in Asia, the Middle East, Africa, and Latin America.

### Public Image and Assessments
Putin has cultivated a macho, athletic public image, participating in extreme sports and interacting with wildlife. His approval ratings have remained high domestically, often exceeding 80%, though analysts note this is influenced by state-controlled media and the absence of political competition. Internationally, assessments are sharply divided. Supporters credit him with restoring stability and national pride after the chaos of the 1990s. Critics, including many Western leaders and Russian dissidents, label him a dictator responsible for corruption, human rights violations, and aggressive wars. Following the 2022 invasion of Ukraine, he has been widely condemned as a war criminal.

## Terms
- **Vertical of Power**: A system of centralized governance where regional authorities are subordinate to the federal center, reducing their autonomy.
- **Oligarchs**: Wealthy business individuals who gained political influence, particularly during the privatization of state assets in the 1990s.
- **FSB (Federal Security Service)**: Russia's primary internal security and counterintelligence agency, successor to the KGB's domestic functions.
- **Sovereign Democracy**: A political concept promoted by Putin's administration, emphasizing that Russia's political system should be determined by its own traditions and popular support, not external influence.
- **Foreign Agents Law**: Legislation requiring organizations and individuals receiving foreign funding and engaging in broadly defined "political activity" to register as "foreign agents," subjecting them to restrictions and stigma.
- **Annexation of Crimea**: Russia's 2014 incorporation of the Crimean Peninsula from Ukraine, following a disputed referendum, which was condemned internationally as illegal.
- **Special Military Operation**: The official term used by the Russian government for its 2022 invasion of Ukraine; the use of terms like "war" or "invasion" is criminalized in Russia.
- **Cult of Personality**: The deliberate creation of an idealized, heroic public image for a leader through propaganda and media, used to legitimize and maintain power.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Nature of Putin's Rule**: Scholars debate whether Putin's system is best described as an authoritarian regime, a dictatorship, or a unique form of "managed democracy" or "sovereign democracy."
- **Economic Legacy**: Assessments vary on whether Putin's economic policies were successful reforms that stabilized Russia or whether they created a stagnant, resource-dependent economy plagued by corruption.
- **Motivations for the Ukraine Invasion**: Analysts disagree on the primary drivers, citing factors ranging from security concerns about NATO expansion to imperial ambitions and domestic political calculations.
- **Public Support**: The true level of domestic support for Putin and his policies, especially the war in Ukraine, is contested, with some arguing poll numbers are unreliable due to fear and state propaganda.
- **Wealth and Corruption**: While official disclosures show modest wealth, numerous investigations allege Putin secretly controls a vast fortune through proxies, a claim the Kremlin denies.

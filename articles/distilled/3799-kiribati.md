# Kiribati

## Overview
Kiribati is a remote archipelagic nation in the central Pacific Ocean, comprising 32 atolls and one raised coral island. It is one of the world's most vulnerable countries to climate change and sea-level rise, with its low-lying islands facing potential submersion. The nation's economy is heavily dependent on foreign aid, fishing licenses, and remittances, making it one of the least developed countries globally.

## Timeline
- **c. 3000 BC–1300 AD** — Austronesian peoples settle the islands.
- **c. 1820** — Russian and French explorers name the archipelago the Gilbert Islands.
- **1892** — The Gilbert Islands (and later Banaba) become a British protectorate.
- **1916** — The Gilbert and Ellice Islands Colony is formed.
- **1943** — The Battle of Tarawa occurs during World War II.
- **1976** — The Ellice Islands separate and become the independent nation of Tuvalu.
- **1979** — The Gilbert Islands gain independence as the Republic of Kiribati.
- **1995** — Kiribati moves the International Date Line eastward to unify the country's time zone.
- **1999** — Kiribati becomes a full member of the United Nations.
- **2008** — President Anote Tong declares the country has reached "the point of no return" regarding climate change.
- **2015** — Kiribati experiences flooding from Cyclone Pam.
- **2023** — Kiribati confirms its intention to rejoin the Pacific Islands Forum.

## Body

### Etymology and History
The name "Kiribati" is the Gilbertese language rendition of "Gilberts," chosen at independence in 1979 to reflect the inclusion of the Phoenix and Line Islands beyond the core Gilbert chain. The islands were first settled by Austronesian peoples, with significant Polynesian influence arriving from Samoa around AD 1300. By the 15th century, distinct governance systems emerged between northern chiefly rule and southern council rule. European contact began with chance visits in the 17th and 18th centuries, followed by missionary activity and the coercive labour trade known as "blackbirding." The British established a protectorate in 1892, later forming the Gilbert and Ellice Islands Colony in 1916. The territory gained independence in 1979 as Kiribati, incorporating the Gilbert, Phoenix, and Line Islands.

### Politics and Foreign Relations
Kiribati is a parliamentary democratic republic. The president (te Beretitenti) is elected by the legislature from its members and then by popular vote. The unicameral legislature is the Maneaba ni Maungatabu (House of Assembly). The country maintains close ties with Australia, New Zealand, and Japan, which provide significant aid. In 2019, Kiribati switched diplomatic recognition from Taiwan to the People's Republic of China. As a frontline state against climate change, Kiribati is an active member of the Alliance of Small Island States (AOSIS) and has pursued international advocacy and contingency planning, including purchasing land in Fiji for potential future migration.

### Geography and Climate
Kiribati's land area is just 811 km², dispersed over 3.4 million km² of ocean. Its islands are low-lying atolls, rising only one or two meters above sea level, with the exception of the raised coral island of Banaba. The country is acutely vulnerable to sea-level rise, with two uninhabited islets disappearing underwater in 1999. While some studies show urbanized islands have gained land area through sediment movement, their height has not increased, leaving them extremely vulnerable to inundation. The climate is tropical, with a wet season from November to April. Kiribati itself rarely experiences cyclones but is affected by distant storms.

### Economy and Demographics
Kiribati has the lowest GDP of any sovereign state in Oceania. The economy relies on fishing license fees, development aid, remittances from seafarers, and subsistence agriculture and fishing. Phosphate mining on Banaba, once a key revenue source, is now exhausted. The population was 119,940 in the 2020 census, with over half living on the crowded capital atoll of Tarawa. The I-Kiribati people are ethnically Micronesian, speaking the Gilbertese language. Christianity is the predominant religion. Health challenges include high rates of smoking, waterborne diseases, and low life expectancy.

## Terms
- **Atoll:** A ring-shaped coral reef including a coral rim that encircles a lagoon partially or completely.
- **Copra:** Dried coconut meat, used to extract coconut oil.
- **Exclusive Economic Zone (EEZ):** A sea zone prescribed by the United Nations Convention on the Law of the Sea over which a state has special rights regarding the exploration and use of marine resources.
- **I-Kiribati:** The demonym for the people of Kiribati.
- **Gilbertese:** The Oceanic language spoken by the I-Kiribati people.
- **Revenue Equalization Reserve Fund (RERF):** A sovereign wealth fund established to manage earnings from phosphate mining.
- **Blackbirding:** The coercive recruitment of people through trickery, kidnapping, or force, often for labour on plantations.
- **Maneaba ni Maungatabu:** The House of Assembly, Kiribati's unicameral legislature.

## Debates and open questions
- The long-term habitability of Kiribati's islands is debated. While some research shows islands can dynamically respond to sea-level rise by gaining land area, scientists agree this does not increase their elevation, leaving them vulnerable to submergence.
- The legal status of "climate refugees" remains unsettled. New Zealand courts rejected a claim by a Kiribati citizen, but the Supreme Court left open the possibility that extreme environmental degradation could someday create a pathway to refugee status.
- The effectiveness of international climate finance and adaptation programs like the Kiribati Adaptation Program (KAP) in securing the nation's future is an ongoing concern.

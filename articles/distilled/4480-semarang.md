# Semarang

## Overview
Semarang is the capital and largest city of Central Java province, Indonesia. It is a major port and regional economic hub with a history stretching back to the 9th century, evolving from a fishing village into a key Dutch colonial trading center and a modern metropolis. Its strategic location on Java's north coast and its multi-ethnic character have shaped its development and significance.

## Timeline
- **9th century** — Known as Bergota; earliest recorded history.
- **1547** — Sultan Hadiwijaya declares Ki Ageng Pandan Arang the first *bupati* (mayor), administratively founding Semarang.
- **1678** — Sultan of Mataram hands control of Semarang to the Dutch East India Company (VOC).
- **1705** — Semarang officially becomes a VOC city after a deal with Susuhunan Pakubuwono I.
- **1811** — British forces capture Semarang during the Napoleonic Wars, transferring Java to British rule.
- **1817** — Dutch reorganize Java; Semarang becomes the seat of the Semarang Residency.
- **1847** — Great Mail Road project connects coastal cities, boosting Semarang's trade.
- **1870** — Railway connection to Surakarta (Solo) established.
- **1906** — Officially designated a city (*stadsgemeente*).
- **1942** — Japanese occupation begins during World War II.
- **1945** — After Indonesian independence, Semarang becomes the capital of Central Java.
- **1945** — Site of the "Five Days Battle" between Indonesian forces and Japanese soldiers.

## Body

### Colonial Development and Urban Planning
Semarang's modern urban form began under Dutch rule. The early colonial city (1678–1870) was a segregated settlement with a Dutch fortress, a Chinese quarter, and a Muslim area called Kauman. Development was slow due to the costly Java War. A shift occurred with infrastructure projects like the 1847 Great Mail Road and late-19th-century railways, which reoriented the city from the river to roads and spurred economic growth.

In the late 19th and early 20th centuries, rapid growth led to overcrowding and poor health in Javanese *kampongs* (villages). Reformers like Dr. Willem T. de Vogel and Hendrik Tillema advocated for expansion into healthier hill areas. Their initial plan for the poor was rejected, but a revised plan by Thomas Karsten (approved c. 1917) created the upscale Candi Baru district. Karsten's planning emphasized aesthetic, practical, and social needs over pure racial segregation. By the 1920s, Semarang had doubled in size, becoming a multi-ethnic metropolis and the capital of Central Java.

### Post-Independence and Modern Era
After Japanese occupation (1942–1945), Semarang became the capital of Central Java on 18 August 1945. The city saw significant conflict, including the 1945 "Five Days Battle" and an 87-day battle that expelled British and Dutch forces. Today, it is a major economic center with growing trade, industry, and services. The city government has implemented a "smart city" concept since 2013. It has been recognized for cleanliness, winning the Adipura Award six times since 2012, and was named the cleanest tourist destination in Southeast Asia for 2020–2022.

### Geography and Climate
Semarang has a unique topography: a narrow, flood-prone lowland ("Semarang Ngisor") along the coast and hilly terrain ("Semarang Dhuwur") to the south, reaching up to 340 m (1,120 ft). The lowland is the economic core but faces annual flooding and tidal surges. The climate is tropical monsoon (Köppen: Am), with a wet season (December–February) and an average annual rainfall of about 2,800 mm (110 in). Flood control projects, like the Jatibarang Dam, are ongoing.

### Demographics and Economy
The population is predominantly Javanese, with significant Chinese, Arab, and other minorities. The Chinese community, about 3.5% of the population, has a historic presence reflected in the Chinatown (Kampong Pecinan Semawis) and the creole language Javindo. As a provincial capital, Semarang's economy is large and dynamic, with major financial institutions and industrial parks in the western part. The Port of Tanjung Mas remains a key trade hub.

### Transportation and Landmarks
Semarang is a transportation hub on National Route 1, with toll roads, a bus rapid transit system (Trans Semarang), two major train stations, and Ahmad Yani International Airport. Key landmarks include the colonial-era Old Town (Kota Lama), Lawang Sewu (former railway HQ), Blenduk Church (1753), and the Sam Poo Kong Chinese temple. The Tugu Muda monument commemorates the 1945 battle for independence.

## Terms
- **Bupati**: A mayor or regent, the head of a city or regency in Indonesia.
- **Kampong**: A traditional village or urban neighborhood, often with a distinct ethnic character.
- **VOC (Vereenigde Oostindische Compagnie)**: The Dutch East India Company, a major colonial trading power.
- **Stadsgemeente**: A Dutch colonial term for a municipality or city.
- **Kedungsepur**: The name for the Greater Semarang metropolitan area.
- **Javindo**: A creole language blending Javanese and Dutch, historically spoken in Semarang.
- **Jamu**: Traditional Indonesian herbal medicines.
- **Warak Ngendog**: A mythical dragon-like puppet, the icon of Semarang's Dugderan festival.
- **Adipura**: An Indonesian award for cities achieving excellence in cleanliness and greenery.

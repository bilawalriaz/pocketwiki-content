# Gujarat

## Overview
Gujarat is a state on India's western coast, known for its long coastline, ancient Indus Valley Civilisation sites, and role as a major historical and modern trade hub. It is one of India's most industrialised states, with a strong economy driven by manufacturing, ports, and agriculture, and is the sole home of the Asiatic lion.

## Timeline
- **c. 3000–1500 BCE** — Gujarat is a major centre of the Indus Valley Civilisation, with key sites at Lothal, Dholavira, and Gola Dhoro.
- **322–294 BCE** — Chandragupta Maurya conquers Gujarat; his governor builds a dam on Sudarshan lake.
- **c. 100 CE** — Saka ruler Rudradaman I of the Western Satraps rules Gujarat.
- **470 CE** — Senapati Bhatarka establishes the Kingdom of Valabhi after the Gupta Empire's decline.
- **730 CE** — Arab Umayyad Caliphate campaigns in India are defeated by a coalition including the Gurjara-Pratihara dynasty.
- **960–1243 CE** — The Chaulukya dynasty rules the Kingdom of Gujarat; their capital Anhilwara (Patan) is a major city.
- **1297 CE** — Alauddin Khalji of the Delhi Sultanate defeats the last Hindu ruler, making Gujarat part of the Sultanate.
- **1411–1442 CE** — Sultan Ahmed Shah establishes Ahmedabad as the capital of the independent Gujarat Sultanate.
- **1572 CE** — Mughal Emperor Akbar conquers and annexes Gujarat.
- **1614 CE** — The British East India Company establishes its first factory in Surat.
- **1 May 1960** — Gujarat is formed as a separate state from Bombay State on a linguistic basis.
- **2001 CE** — A magnitude 7.7 earthquake in Kutch kills around 20,000 people.

## Body

### Ancient and Medieval History
Gujarat was a core area of the Indus Valley Civilisation, containing about 50 settlement ruins. Its coastal cities like Bharuch and Khambhat were ancient ports, with trade ties to Egypt and the Persian Gulf by 1000–750 BCE. The region saw successive rule by the Mauryan, Satavahana, Gupta, and Gurjara-Pratihara empires. The independent Gujarat Sultanate (1411–1572) was a wealthy maritime power, with its capital at Ahmedabad and major ports like Khambhat and Surat. It was a centre of Indian Ocean trade, with Gujarati merchants operating vast commercial networks from East Africa to Southeast Asia.

### Islamic and Mughal Rule
After the Delhi Sultanate's conquest in 1297, Gujarat eventually became an independent sultanate. The Mughal Empire annexed it in 1572, making Surat its principal western port. The state's wealth was legendary; its customs revenue in the 1570s was nearly three times the total revenue of the Portuguese Empire in Asia. The Mughal period saw the arrival of Zoroastrian (Parsi) refugees and the flourishing of Sufi and other religious traditions.

### Colonial and Post-Independence Era
European powers, notably the Portuguese and later the British, established bases along the coast. The British East India Company's first Indian factory was in Surat (1614). After Maratha incursions, the British gained control in the early 19th century. Post-independence, Gujarat was formed from Bombay State in 1960. The state has experienced significant industrial growth, major earthquakes (2001), and communal riots (2002).

### Economy and Industry
Gujarat is India's most industrialised state, with the highest exports (30.7% of national total in 2024–25). Its economy is the fourth-largest in India. Key sectors include chemicals, petrochemicals, pharmaceuticals (33% of India's drug manufacturing), textiles, and gems (92% of the world's diamonds were cut in Surat in 2003). It has the longest coastline and handles about 40% of India's ocean cargo, with Mundra Port being the country's largest. Agriculture is significant, with Gujarat being the largest producer of milk (via the Amul cooperative) and a major producer of cotton and groundnuts.

### Demographics and Culture
Gujarati is the official language, spoken by 86% of the population. The state is predominantly Hindu (88.57%), with significant Muslim (9.67%) and Jain (0.96%) minorities. Culture is marked by festivals like Navratri (celebrated with garba dance) and Makar Sankranti (kite-flying). Gujarati cuisine is primarily vegetarian. The state is the birthplace of Mahatma Gandhi and Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel.

## Terms
- **GSDP (Gross State Domestic Product):** The total value of goods and services produced within a state, a measure of its economic size.
- **Western Satraps (Kshatraps):** Indo-Scythian rulers who governed parts of western and central India from the 1st to 4th centuries CE.
- **Subah:** A top-level province of the Mughal Empire.
- **Princely State:** A territory in British India under the nominal sovereignty of an Indian ruler, but with British paramountcy.
- **Cooperative:** An autonomous association of people united voluntarily to meet their common economic and social needs, as exemplified by the Amul dairy model.
- **Three-language formula:** An educational policy in India where students learn three languages: a regional language, Hindi, and English.
- **Jyotigram Yojana:** A scheme in Gujarat to provide 24-hour electricity to villages.
- **Rann:** A Gujarati term for a seasonally marshy saline clay desert, as in the Rann of Kutch.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The **Sardar Sarovar Project** on the Narmada River is a major dam that has increased irrigation but is a contested issue due to the displacement of communities.
- The impact of **agricultural cooperatives** is debated; while they increased productivity, their effect on equity is questioned as membership has tended to favour landowners.
- **Groundwater depletion** is a significant issue, with abstraction rates exceeding recharge rates in many districts, posing a long-term sustainability challenge.

# Hamburg

## Overview
Hamburg is Germany's second-largest city and a major European port, media, and commercial hub. As one of Germany's three city-states, it has a long history as a sovereign entity, from a medieval Hanseatic League member to a modern state within the German federation. Its identity is deeply tied to its port, its history of resilience after disasters, and its vibrant cultural and economic life.

## Timeline
- **AD 808** — Emperor Charlemagne orders construction of the Hammaburg castle, giving the city its name.
- **834** — Hamburg established as the seat of a bishopric.
- **1189** — Emperor Frederick Barbarossa grants Hamburg the status of a Free Imperial City.
- **1241** — Alliance with Lübeck is regarded as the founding moment of the Hanseatic League.
- **1842** — The Great Fire destroys about a quarter of the inner city.
- **1892** — A major cholera epidemic kills about 8,600 people.
- **1943** — Allied firebombing (Operation Gomorrah) devastates the city, killing at least 42,600 civilians.
- **1945** — City surrendered to British forces on 3 May.
- **1962** — The North Sea flood inundates one-fifth of Hamburg, killing over 300.
- **2015** — Speicherstadt and Kontorhausviertel declared UNESCO World Heritage Sites.
- **2017** — Elbphilharmonie concert hall opens.

## Body

### History and Governance
Hamburg's name derives from the *Hammaburg* castle built in AD 808. It became a bishopric in 834 and a Free Imperial City in 1189, which granted tax-free access to the North Sea. Its location between the North and Baltic Seas fueled its rise as a major port and its central role in the Hanseatic League, a medieval trading alliance. The city maintained sovereignty as a city-state until German unification in 1871, and it remains one of Germany's 16 federal states today. Its history is marked by repeated destruction and recovery, from Viking raids and the Black Death to the Great Fire of 1842, the firebombing of 1943, and the 1962 flood.

### Economy and Port
Hamburg's economy is driven by its port, media, and industrial sectors. The Port of Hamburg is Germany's largest and Europe's third-largest, handling 8.9 million TEU of cargo in 2016. The city is a major center for media, hosting publishers like *Der Spiegel* and *Die Zeit*, and the broadcaster NDR. Key industries include aerospace (Airbus), shipbuilding (Blohm + Voss), and logistics. The HafenCity project, Europe's largest urban development, is transforming former port areas into a mixed-use district. Tourism is significant, with over 6.7 million visitors in 2017.

### Culture and Society
Hamburg is a major cultural hub with over 40 theatres, 60 museums, and 100 music venues. It is the birthplace of composer Johannes Brahms and was an early home to the Beatles. The city is known for its "Hamburger Schule" alternative music scene and hosts major events like the Reeperbahn Festival. Its cuisine includes dishes like *Aalsuppe* and *Labskaus*, and the *Franzbrötchen* pastry. The city is diverse, with the largest Portuguese and Afghan communities in Germany. The Reeperbahn in St. Pauli is a world-famous red-light district and entertainment area.

### Geography and Infrastructure
Hamburg sits at the confluence of the Alster and Elbe rivers, with a sheltered natural harbour. It is crossed by approximately 2,500 bridges, more than any other city in Europe. The city has an oceanic climate. Its public transport system, organized by the HVV (the world's first transit authority of its kind), includes S-Bahn, U-Bahn, bus, and ferry lines. Hamburg Airport, established in 1912, is the oldest in Germany still in operation.

## Terms
- **Free Imperial City:** A city within the Holy Roman Empire that was subject only to the emperor, not to a local lord.
- **Hanseatic League:** A medieval commercial and defensive confederation of merchant guilds and market towns in Northern Europe.
- **City-state:** A sovereign state that consists of a single city, like Hamburg, Berlin, or Bremen in Germany.
- **TEU (Twenty-foot Equivalent Unit):** A standard unit of cargo capacity, based on the volume of a 20-foot-long intermodal container.
- **HafenCity:** A large-scale urban redevelopment project on former port land in Hamburg.
- **Elbphilharmonie:** A major concert hall opened in 2017, built atop an old warehouse in the HafenCity.
- **Reeperbahn:** A famous street and entertainment district in the St. Pauli quarter, known for nightlife and red-light activities.
- **Hamburger Schule:** A term for a movement of German alternative rock bands that emerged in Hamburg in the 1980s.

## Debates and Open Questions
The source notes scholarly uncertainty regarding the etymology of the "Hamma" part of Hamburg's name. It also mentions that the precise number of civilian deaths in the 1943 firebombing is not known, though at least 42,600 is the established figure. The origin of the American "hamburger" is linked to Hamburg's *Frikadeller*, but the exact path of culinary influence is a matter of historical discussion.

# Vercingetorix

## Overview
Vercingetorix (c. 82–46 BC) was a Gallic chieftain of the Arverni tribe who united most of Gaul in a major revolt against Roman conquest during the Gallic Wars. His leadership represented the most significant unified Gallic resistance to Rome, culminating in a famous defeat at the Siege of Alesia. He became a potent symbol of French national identity and resistance to foreign domination from the 19th century onward.

## Timeline
- **c. 82 BC** — Born in Gergovia to the Arvernian noble Celtillus.
- **c. 70–60 BC** — Father Celtillus is murdered for attempting to restore kingship among the Arverni.
- **Early 52 BC** — Exiled from Gergovia for inciting revolt; returns with supporters, expels opponents, and is proclaimed king.
- **Early 52 BC** — Elected supreme commander (*imperator*) of a major Gallic coalition against Rome.
- **Spring 52 BC** — Adopts a scorched-earth strategy against Roman forces.
- **52 BC** — Wins the Battle of Gergovia, one of only three recorded Roman defeats in the Gallic Wars.
- **Summer 52 BC** — After the Aedui defect to his side, is formally recognized as sole commander of the pan-Gallic coalition at Bibracte.
- **September 52 BC** — Surrenders to Julius Caesar at the Battle of Alesia after a failed relief attempt.
- **46 BC** — Executed in Rome after being paraded in Caesar's triumph.

## Biography and Campaign
**Sources and Early Life:** Knowledge of Vercingetorix comes primarily from Julius Caesar's biased account, supplemented by later writers like Plutarch and Cassius Dio. He was born into the elite of the Arverni, a powerful Gallic tribe in modern Auvergne. His father, Celtillus, was killed for trying to restore monarchy, which may have marginalized Vercingetorix. In early 52 BC, amid a general Gallic uprising, he was exiled for plotting revolt but quickly returned to seize power in Gergovia.

**Military Leadership and Strategy:** Vercingetorix unified disparate tribes through harsh discipline and centralized command. He initially used mobile forces and a scorched-earth strategy to exhaust Roman supplies. After early setbacks, he won a major victory at Gergovia, which nearly forced Caesar to withdraw from Gaul. This defeat triggered the defection of the Aedui tribe, leading to the formation of a unified Gallic coalition with Vercingetorix as its sole commander.

**Defeat at Alesia:** In a decisive engagement, Vercingetorix withdrew to the fortified stronghold of Alesia. Caesar besieged it with extensive fortifications. A large Gallic relief army failed to break the siege, and with starvation imminent, Vercingetorix surrendered himself to Caesar to spare his people. He was imprisoned in Rome for six years and executed after Caesar's triumph in 46 BC, likely as a political necessity to appease Roman public opinion.

## Terms
- **Alesia:** The fortified Mandubian stronghold where Vercingetorix made his final stand and surrendered to Caesar in 52 BC.
- **Arverni:** A powerful Gallic tribe in central Gaul (modern Auvergne) to which Vercingetorix belonged.
- **Imperator:** The title of supreme military commander conferred upon Vercingetorix by the Gallic coalition.
- **Oppidum:** A large, fortified Iron Age settlement, often the chief town of a tribe (e.g., Gergovia, Alesia).
- **Scorched-earth:** A military strategy of destroying crops, settlements, and supplies to deny them to the enemy, employed by Vercingetorix against the Romans.
- **Vergobret:** The supreme magistrate of certain Gallic tribes, possibly the office held by Vercingetorix's father.
- **Gallic Wars:** The series of military campaigns (58–50 BC) led by Julius Caesar to conquer Gaul.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Birth Date:** His birth is traditionally placed c. 82 BC, but scholars suggest a range from the late 80s to 72 BC, as Caesar's description of him as a "young man" in 52 BC is imprecise.
- **Prior Relationship with Caesar:** Cassius Dio's account suggests Vercingetorix surrendered hoping for clemency based on past friendship, leading to scholarly speculation that he may have collaborated with Caesar between 57 and 54 BC.
- **Surrender Scene:** Ancient sources differ on the details of his surrender. Caesar's narrative likely shaped the event to enhance his own prestige, while later accounts add dramatic elements like Vercingetorix riding around Caesar's tribunal.
- **Reason for Execution:** While Caesar acknowledged Gallic bravery, Vercingetorix's execution was likely a political necessity to counter Roman public hostility towards Gauls, rather than standard Roman practice for captured leaders.

# Christ the Redeemer (statue)

## Overview
Christ the Redeemer is a colossal Art Deco statue of Jesus Christ in Rio de Janeiro, Brazil. It is a globally recognized symbol of Christianity and a cultural icon of Brazil, having been voted one of the New 7 Wonders of the World. Its construction in the early 20th century was a major engineering feat, and it remains the largest Art Deco sculpture in the world.

## Timeline
- **Mid-1850s** — First proposal for a Christian monument on Mount Corcovado is made and rejected.
- **1920** — The Catholic Circle of Rio launches a new campaign and fundraising for the statue.
- **1922** — Construction begins; French sculptor Paul Landowski creates the work.
- **1931** — The statue is completed and inaugurated on October 12.
- **1990** — A major restoration agreement is formed by multiple organizations.
- **2003** — Access infrastructure (escalators, elevators) is installed.
- **2008** — Lightning causes damage to the statue's fingers, head, and eyebrows.
- **2010** — A massive restoration project cleans and repairs the statue.
- **2014** — Lightning again damages the statue, dislodging a finger.

## Body

### Conception and Design
The idea for a monument on Mount Corcovado was first proposed in the 1850s but was rejected. After Brazil became a republic in 1889, a new proposal was made in 1920 by the Catholic Circle of Rio, motivated by a perceived "Godlessness" in society. The final design, chosen over alternatives like a Christ holding a globe or cross, was an open-armed figure symbolizing peace. The project was led by Brazilian engineer Heitor da Silva Costa and sculpted by Paul Landowski, with the face sculpted by Romanian artist Gheorghe Leonida.

### Construction and Materials
Construction lasted from 1922 to 1931. The statue is made of reinforced concrete, a choice over steel for its suitability to the cross-shaped design, and is clad in soapstone for its durability. It stands 30 meters (98 ft) high on an 8-meter (26 ft) pedestal, with arms stretching 28 meters (92 ft) wide. The total cost was equivalent to US$250,000 at the time. The statue was inaugurated on October 12, 1931; a planned remote lighting by Guglielmo Marconi from Rome failed due to bad weather.

### Restoration and Maintenance
The statue requires periodic maintenance due to wind, erosion, and lightning strikes. A major restoration in 2010 involved cleaning, repairing the internal iron structure, replacing mortar and soapstone, and installing new lightning rods. This work used over 60,000 pieces of stone from the original quarry. The original pale soapstone is no longer available in sufficient quantity, leading to a gradual darkening of the replaced stones. In 2006, a chapel was consecrated beneath the statue.

## Terms
- **Art Deco:** An artistic style of the 1920s and 1930s characterized by bold geometric forms and rich colors, exemplified by the statue's design.
- **Reinforced concrete:** Concrete embedded with steel rods or mesh to increase its strength, used as the statue's primary structural material.
- **Soapstone:** A soft, talc-rich metamorphic rock chosen for the statue's outer layer due to its durability and workability.
- **Pedestal:** The base or platform on which a statue stands, adding 8 meters to the statue's height.
- **Corcovado:** The 700-meter (2,300 ft) mountain in the Tijuca National Park where the statue is located.
- **Tijuca National Park:** The urban forest surrounding Mount Corcovado in Rio de Janeiro.

## Debates and Open Questions
The source notes that the statue's design was not static; the initial accepted plan featured Christ holding a globe and cross, which was later changed to the current open-armed pose. The 1998 Pirelli commercial featuring Ronaldo replacing the statue was controversial with the Catholic Church, highlighting ongoing tensions about the use of religious symbols in popular culture.

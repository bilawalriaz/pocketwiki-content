# Chandragupta II

## Overview
Chandragupta II (r. c. 375–c. 415), also known as Vikramaditya, was the emperor of the Gupta Empire during its zenith. He expanded the empire through military conquests and strategic marriages, creating a vast, prosperous realm. His reign is celebrated for its cultural flourishing, administrative stability, and religious tolerance, making him one of India's most celebrated monarchs.

## Timeline
- **c. 375** — Chandragupta II succeeds his father Samudragupta as Gupta emperor.
- **c. 380–409** — Conducts military campaigns, including the conquest of the Western Kshatrapas (Shakas) in west-central India.
- **c. 390** — His daughter Prabhavatigupta becomes regent of the Vakataka kingdom after her husband's death.
- **c. 401–402** — Feudatory Maharaja Sanakanika builds a Vaishnava temple at Udayagiri (Gupta year 82).
- **c. 412–413** — Last known dated inscription of Chandragupta (Sanchi, Gupta year 93).
- **c. 415** — Chandragupta II dies and is succeeded by his son Kumaragupta I.

## Body

### Military Conquests and Empire
Chandragupta II continued his father's expansionist policy. His most significant victory was the subjugation of the Western Kshatrapas (Shakas), extending Gupta control to the Arabian Sea coast in Gujarat. This is evidenced by the abrupt end of independent Kshatrapa coinage in the late 4th century and the subsequent appearance of Gupta-era silver coins modeled on Kshatrapa designs. The Delhi iron pillar inscription, attributed to a king "Chandra" (generally identified as Chandragupta II), credits him with defeating an alliance in Bengal (Vanga) and crossing the Indus to defeat the Vahlikas (in Balkh, Afghanistan). Inscriptions in Gilgit-Baltistan also mention "Chandra" and "Vikramaditya," suggesting Gupta influence in the northern regions. At its peak, the empire stretched from the Oxus River in the west to Bengal in the east, and from the Himalayan foothills to the Narmada River.

### Matrimonial Alliances and Administration
Chandragupta II used marriage to consolidate power and extend influence. He married Kuveranaga, a Naga princess, and his daughter Prabhavatigupta married the Vakataka king Rudrasena II. After Rudrasena's death, Prabhavatigupta's 20-year regency effectively integrated the Vakataka kingdom into the Gupta sphere. A later alliance with the Kadamba dynasty of Karnataka is also indicated. His administration relied on feudatories (e.g., Maharaja Sanakanika) and ministers (e.g., foreign minister Virasena). The Chinese pilgrim Faxian, who visited during his reign, described a peaceful, prosperous kingdom with a light-touch administration, no heavy taxation, and a justice system based on fines rather than corporal punishment (except for repeat rebels).

### Culture, Religion, and Legacy
Chandragupta II was a devout Vaishnavite (devotee of Vishnu) but tolerant of other faiths, as shown by inscriptions recording Shiva and Buddhist donations by his officials. His court is legendary for the "Navaratnas" (nine gems), a group of scholars, though this tradition is considered historically unreliable. However, the renowned poet Kalidasa may have been his court poet. His reign saw the construction of the Udayagiri cave shrines. He was the first Gupta king to issue silver coins. The legendary emperor Vikramaditya, an ideal king of Indian folklore, is believed by many historians to be based on Chandragupta II, particularly his victory over the Shakas.

## Terms
- **Gupta Empire:** An ancient Indian empire (c. 319–543 CE) considered a "Golden Age" of Indian culture, science, and administration.
- **Western Kshatrapas (Shakas):** Indo-Scythian rulers who controlled parts of western and central India before being conquered by the Guptas.
- **Vakataka dynasty:** A Deccan-based dynasty that became closely allied with the Guptas through marriage.
- **Feudatory:** A ruler or territory that owes allegiance and service to a more powerful sovereign in exchange for protection.
- **Navaratnas ("Nine Gems"):** A legendary group of nine scholars said to have adorned the court of Vikramaditya.
- **Parama-bhagvata:** A title meaning "supreme devotee of Vishnu," used by Chandragupta II.
- **Digvijaya:** A "conquest of all quarters," a military campaign to establish universal sovereignty.
- **Vikramaditya:** A legendary ideal emperor; a title adopted by Chandragupta II.
- **Gupta era:** A calendar era starting c. 319–320 CE, used for dating inscriptions during the Gupta period.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Identification with King Chandra:** While the Delhi iron pillar inscription is generally attributed to Chandragupta II, this identification is not absolutely certain.
- **Historicity of Ramagupta:** The Sanskrit play *Devichandraguptam* narrates that Chandragupta had an elder brother, Ramagupta, whom he overthrew. Historians debate whether this is based on fact or is fiction.
- **Date of Reign:** The exact start of Chandragupta's reign is debated. Earlier scholars placed it c. 376–381 CE based on the Mathura pillar inscription, but Indologist Harry Falk's 2004 theory on the dating system suggests the inscription cannot be used to determine the reign's start.
- **Vikramaditya Legends:** The extent to which the legendary Vikramaditya is based on Chandragupta II (versus other kings) is a subject of scholarly discussion.
- **Navaratnas:** The tradition of the "nine gems" at Chandragupta's court is considered by many historians to be a later literary invention with no historical basis.

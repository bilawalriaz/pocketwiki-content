# Johor Bahru

## Overview
Johor Bahru (JB) is the capital city of the Malaysian state of Johor, located at the southern tip of the Malay Peninsula bordering Singapore. It is Malaysia's largest state capital by population and a major financial and business centre, second only to Kuala Lumpur in economic competitiveness. The city is a critical hub for cross-border activity with Singapore, hosting the world's busiest land border crossing, and is a core component of the Iskandar Malaysia special economic zone.

## Timeline
- **1819** — The Johor-Riau Sultanate splits; Temenggong Daeng Ibrahim gains control of the mainland Johor territory.
- **1855** — Sultan Ali cedes sovereignty of Johor (except Kesang) to Temenggong Ibrahim via a treaty.
- **1858** — Temenggong Ibrahim renames the area Iskandar Puteri.
- **1862** — After Temenggong Ibrahim's death, Sultan Abu Bakar renames the town Johor Bahru.
- **1886-1895** — Sultan Abu Bakar modernizes the town, building infrastructure and railways.
- **1914** — British advisory status is elevated, leading to direct colonial influence.
- **1923** — The Johor–Singapore Causeway is completed.
- **1940** — The Sultan Ibrahim Building is completed.
- **1942** — Japanese forces occupy Johor Bahru.
- **1946** — The United Malay National Organisation (UMNO) is formed in the city.
- **1994** — Johor Bahru is officially granted city status.
- **2006** — Iskandar Malaysia, the nation's largest special economic zone, is formed.

## Body

### Etymology and Early History
The area was originally a Malay fishing village called Tanjung Puteri. In 1858, Temenggong Daeng Ibrahim renamed it Iskandar Puteri after acquiring the territory. Following his death in 1862, his son Sultan Abu Bakar renamed it Johor Bahru, meaning "New Johor" in Malay. The city was also known as "Shantou" or "Little Swatow" by its Chinese community, and is generally called "Xinshan" (New Mountain) in Chinese.

### Development and Colonial Era
Under Sultan Abu Bakar (reigned 1886–1895), Johor Bahru modernized from a fishing village with the construction of administrative buildings, schools, and railways. The British relationship evolved from recognition of Abu Bakar as a Sultan to the establishment of a formal advisory system in 1914. Key infrastructure like the Johor–Singapore Causeway (1923) and the Sultan Ibrahim Building (1940) were completed during this period.

### World War II and Post-War Nationalism
Japanese forces occupied the city on 31 January 1942, using the Bukit Serene Palace as a base to plan the invasion of Singapore. After the war, Johor Bahru became a cradle of Malay nationalism. On 11 May 1946, Onn Jaafar formed the United Malay National Organisation (UMNO) at the Istana Besar in response to British policies, marking a pivotal moment in the path to independence.

### Modern City and Economy
Johor Bahru was granted city status on 1 January 1994. It is the core of the Iskandar Malaysia special economic zone and has a close economic relationship with Singapore, with many residents working there. The city is a major commercial centre, hosting international conferences and featuring a high concentration of shopping malls. It is ranked as Malaysia's second-best-performing city for economic competitiveness and has the fastest urbanization growth in the country.

### Governance and Demographics
The city is administered by the Johor Bahru City Council. Its population (858,118 in 2020) is a mix of Malays, Chinese (predominantly Hokkien and Teochew), and Indians (predominantly Tamil). Malay is the official language, but Mandarin Chinese and Tamil are widely spoken, and about 60% of the population is fluent in English.

### Transportation
Johor Bahru is a major transport hub. It is served by Senai International Airport and the Port of Tanjung Pelepas (world's 15th-busiest port). The city has the world's busiest land border crossing via the Johor–Singapore Causeway. Public transport includes buses, KTM intercity rail, and the upcoming RTS Link light rail to Singapore. Plans for an elevated Autonomous Rail Rapid Transit (ART) network were approved in 2026 to address traffic congestion.

## Terms
*   **Iskandar Malaysia:** Malaysia's largest special economic zone by investment value, with Johor Bahru as its core city.
*   **Johor–Singapore Causeway:** A 1.056 km-long causeway connecting Johor Bahru to Woodlands, Singapore, carrying road and rail traffic.
*   **RTS Link:** An under-construction light rail line that will provide a new cross-border service between Johor Bahru and Singapore.
*   **Autonomous Rail Rapid Transit (ART):** A planned elevated public transport network for Johor Bahru, approved as an alternative to a light rail transit (LRT) system.
*   **UMNO (United Malay National Organisation):** A major political party in Malaysia, founded in Johor Bahru in 1946.
*   **Bumiputra:** A Malaysian term for indigenous peoples, including Malays.
*   **Kangchu system:** A historical system of land administration and labour contracts introduced by Chinese communities for pepper and gambier cultivation.
*   **Sultan Ibrahim Building:** A historical secretariat building completed in 1940, serving as the centre of Johor's administration.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Public Transport Solution:** There is an ongoing debate about the best solution for the city's traffic congestion. While an elevated ART network was approved in 2026, there remain calls for a higher-capacity rail system to serve the busiest corridors.
*   **Urban Planning Challenges:** The city is characterized by significant urban sprawl and high car dependency, presenting ongoing challenges for sustainable development and public transport implementation.

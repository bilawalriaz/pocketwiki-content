# Charles XII of Sweden

## Overview
Charles XII (1682–1718) was the King of Sweden whose reign was dominated by the Great Northern War (1700–1721). A brilliant but stubborn military leader, he spent nearly his entire rule at war, defending the Swedish Empire against a coalition of Denmark-Norway, Saxony-Poland-Lithuania, and Russia. His campaigns initially brought Sweden to the height of its power, but a catastrophic defeat in Russia led to the empire's collapse and a fundamental shift in Swedish governance from absolute monarchy to parliamentary rule.

## Timeline
- **17 June 1682** — Born in Stockholm, son of King Charles XI.
- **April 1697** — Ascends the Swedish throne at age 14 after his father's death.
- **1700** — Great Northern War begins; Charles defeats Denmark-Norway in months.
- **November 1700** — Wins the Battle of Narva against a much larger Russian army.
- **1706** — Swedish victory at Fraustadt forces Saxony out of the war.
- **July 1709** — Decisive defeat at the Battle of Poltava against Russia.
- **1709–1714** — Takes refuge in the Ottoman Empire after the Poltava disaster.
- **1714** — Returns to Sweden after a 15-day horseback ride across Europe.
- **1716** — First invasion of Norway fails to capture key fortresses.
- **30 November 1718** — Killed by a projectile while besieging Fredriksten fortress in Norway.

## Body

### Early Life and Accession
Charles was the only surviving son of King Charles XI, receiving a rigorous education and physical training. He inherited the throne in 1697 at age 15, becoming the first Swedish ruler to directly inherit absolute monarchical authority. His early reign was shaped by his father's legacy of strengthening royal power and the "great reduction" of noble lands, which created enemies like Johann Reinhold Patkul, who helped forge the anti-Swedish alliance.

### The Great Northern War: Early Victories
In 1700, a triple alliance attacked Sweden's territories, aiming to exploit its young king. Charles responded with swift, aggressive campaigns. He forced Denmark-Norway to sue for peace within months. At the Battle of Narva (1700), his outnumbered army routed the Russians, compelling Tsar Peter the Great to sue for peace—an offer Charles rejected. By 1706, after defeating Saxony-Poland-Lithuania and installing his ally Stanisław Leszczyński as king, Charles had subdued all foes except Russia.

### The Russian Campaign and Defeat
Charles marched on Moscow, winning battles like Holowczyn despite being outnumbered. However, the campaign ended in disaster at the Battle of Poltava (1709). Wounded and unable to command, Charles saw his army destroyed by a modernized Russian force. The defeat marked the end of the Swedish Empire and the rise of Russia. Charles fled to the Ottoman Empire, where he lived for five years, attempting to incite war between the Ottomans and Russia. His persistent presence earned him the nickname *demirbaş* ("iron-head").

### Exile, Return, and Death
After a stark warning from the Swedish government that they would make peace without him, Charles returned to Sweden in 1714. Finding his empire under attack, he launched two invasions of Norway to pressure Denmark. Both campaigns failed. During the second siege of Fredriksten in 1718, Charles was killed by a projectile to the head. His death remains debated, with theories ranging from enemy fire to assassination by Swedish conspirators opposed to his war and a proposed wealth tax.

### Legacy and Succession
Charles never married or had children. He was succeeded by his sister, Ulrika Eleonora, who was soon forced to cede power to the Riksdag (parliament), ending Sweden's absolute monarchy. The subsequent "Age of Liberty" lasted until 1772. Charles was later idealized as a heroic warrior king in 19th-century Swedish nationalism, though his legacy is also associated with the ruin of the Swedish Empire.

## Terms
- **Great Northern War (1700–1721):** A conflict in which a coalition led by Denmark-Norway, Saxony-Poland-Lithuania, and Russia challenged the Swedish Empire's dominance in the Baltic region.
- **Absolute monarchy:** A system of government where the monarch holds supreme, unchecked authority, which Charles XII inherited and which ended in Sweden after his death.
- **Riksdag of the Estates:** The Swedish parliament, representing the nobility, clergy, burghers, and peasants, which gained significant power during the Age of Liberty.
- **Battle of Narva (1700):** A major early victory where Charles XII's outnumbered Swedes defeated a much larger Russian army.
- **Battle of Poltava (1709):** The decisive defeat of Charles XII's army by Peter the Great's forces, which ended Swedish dominance in the Baltic.
- **Age of Liberty (1718/1719–1772):** The period in Swedish history when parliamentary government replaced royal autocracy.
- **Demirbaş:** The nickname given to Charles XII in the Ottoman Empire, meaning "iron-head," reflecting his stubborn persistence.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Cause of Death:** The definitive circumstances are unclear. While likely killed by enemy fire from the Norwegian fortress, theories of assassination by Swedish conspirators (possibly to end the war or block a tax) persist.
- **Personal Life:** Historians debate Charles's sexuality and reasons for never marrying. Some suggest he was homosexual, while others argue he was "married to the military" and avoided relationships due to strong religious faith.
- **Historical Legacy:** His reputation has shifted over time, from a romantic national hero in the 19th century to a more critical view in the 20th century as a ruler who brought ruin to Sweden.

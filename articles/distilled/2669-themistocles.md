# Themistocles

## Overview
Themistocles (c. 524–c. 459 BC) was an Athenian statesman and general whose career defined the rise of Athens as a naval power. A populist who championed the common people, he was instrumental in building the fleet that defeated the Persian Empire at the Battle of Salamis (480 BC), a victory considered crucial for the survival of Greek civilization. His later life ended in exile and service to the Persian king, but his legacy as the architect of Athenian maritime dominance endured.

## Timeline
- **c. 524 BC** — Born in the Attic deme of Phrearrhii.
- **493 BC** — Elected Archon; begins development of the port of Piraeus.
- **490 BC** — Fights at the Battle of Marathon.
- **483 BC** — Persuades Athens to build a fleet of 200 triremes using Laurion silver.
- **482 BC** — His rival Aristides is ostracised, cementing Themistocles's political dominance.
- **480 BC** — Commands the Greek fleet at the battles of Artemisium and Salamis.
- **479 BC** — After the Persian defeat, oversees the re-fortification of Athens.
- **472/471 BC** — Ostracised from Athens; goes into exile.
- **c. 465 BC** — Enters the service of Persian King Artaxerxes I; becomes governor of Magnesia.
- **459 BC** — Dies at Magnesia, probably of natural causes.

## Body

### Rise in Athenian Democracy
Themistocles was a new type of politician who rose to power in the early Athenian democracy. Unlike the traditional nobility, he built a power base among the lower classes by actively courting their support in markets and docks. As Archon in 493 BC, he began his lifelong advocacy for Athenian naval power by developing the natural harbors of Piraeus. His greatest political victory came in 483 BC when, after a rich silver seam was found at Laurion, he persuaded the Athenians to use the wealth to build a fleet of 200 triremes (warships). He cleverly justified this by citing a local war with Aegina, avoiding the more distant threat of Persia. This policy was opposed by the aristocratic faction led by Aristides, but Themistocles's populist strategy prevailed, leading to Aristides's ostracism in 482 BC.

### The Persian Wars and the Battle of Salamis
When the second Persian invasion began in 480 BC, Themistocles became the de facto leader of the Greek naval alliance. He convinced the Athenians to abandon their city and commit their entire fleet to the defense of Greece, a pivotal decision. At the naval battle of Artemisium, he helped hold the line against the Persians. After the Greek defeat at Thermopylae, he masterminded the decisive engagement at Salamis. Using subterfuge, he lured the larger Persian fleet into the narrow straits, where their numbers became a disadvantage. The resulting Greek victory was the turning point of the war, ensuring the survival of Greece. As Plutarch states, Themistocles was "the man most instrumental in achieving the salvation of Hellas."

### Exile and Service to Persia
After the war, Themistocles's arrogance and the jealousy of his fellow citizens led to his ostracism in 472 or 471 BC. The Spartans then implicated him in a treasonous plot, forcing him to flee Greece. He eventually sought refuge with the Persian King Artaxerxes I, to whom he offered his services. He was made governor of Magnesia in Asia Minor, where he lived in wealth and honor until his death in 459 BC. Ancient sources debate whether he died naturally or by suicide to avoid fighting against Athens.

## Terms
*   **Trireme:** A Greek warship with three banks of oars, the dominant naval vessel of the era.
*   **Ostracism:** A procedure in Athenian democracy where citizens could vote to exile a politician for ten years without loss of property.
*   **Archon:** One of the chief magistrates in ancient Athens.
*   **Strategos:** A general or military commander; in Athens, a board of ten strategoi held significant power.
*   **Polis:** The Greek city-state; Athens was a polis.
*   **Thetes:** The lowest social class in Athens, who served as rowers in the fleet.
*   **Hoplite:** A heavily armed foot soldier, typically from the middle class.
*   **Delian League:** An alliance of Greek city-states led by Athens, formed after the Persian Wars, which evolved into the Athenian Empire.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Role at Marathon:** The sources are uncertain whether Themistocles was one of the ten generals (strategoi) at the Battle of Marathon in 490 BC.
*   **Motives at Salamis:** Scholars debate whether Themistocles's message to Xerxes before the battle was purely a stratagem to trap the Persians, or also a calculated attempt to secure Athenian mercy in case of defeat.
*   **Cause of Death:** While Thucydides reports natural causes, other traditions claim Themistocles committed suicide by poison or bull's blood to avoid being ordered by the Persian king to attack Greek lands.
*   **Coin Portraiture:** There is doubt whether coins issued by Themistocles as governor of Magnesia depicted his own portrait or the image of Zeus.

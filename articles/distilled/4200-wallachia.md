# Wallachia

## Overview
Wallachia is a historical region of modern Romania, situated north of the Lower Danube and south of the Southern Carpathians. It was one of the two principalities (along with Moldavia) that formed the foundation of the modern Romanian state. Its history is defined by the struggle for autonomy, first from the Kingdom of Hungary and later from the Ottoman Empire, culminating in its 1859 union with Moldavia.

## Timeline
- **c. 1330** — Basarab I defeats Hungary at the Battle of Posada, consolidating Wallachian statehood.
- **1417** — Wallachia accepts Ottoman suzerainty for the first time.
- **1593–1601** — Michael the Brave rules, briefly uniting Wallachia, Moldavia, and Transylvania.
- **1715** — Phanariote rule begins, with princes appointed from the Greek community in Constantinople.
- **1821** — Tudor Vladimirescu's uprising and the Greek-led revolution lead to the end of Phanariote rule.
- **1829** — Treaty of Adrianople places Wallachia under Russian protection and grants it a constitution (Regulamentul Organic).
- **1848** — A liberal revolution is suppressed by Ottoman and Russian forces.
- **1859** — Alexander John Cuza is elected prince of both Wallachia and Moldavia, uniting the principalities.
- **1866** — Carol I becomes prince, securing the union's permanence.
- **1881** — The United Principalities officially become the Kingdom of Romania.

## Body

### Formation and Early History
Wallachia formed as a principality in the 13th century from the unification of smaller Vlach (Romanian) polities. Its independence was secured by Basarab I's victory over Hungary in 1330. The region was historically divided into Muntenia (Greater Wallachia) and Oltenia (Lesser Wallachia). The name "Wallachia" is an exonym derived from a Germanic term for Roman-speakers; Romanians called their land "Țara Românească" (the Romanian Land).

### Ottoman Suzerainty and Internal Struggles
From 1417, Wallachia was under the suzerainty of the Ottoman Empire, though it often preserved autonomy. Rulers like Vlad the Impaler (Vlad III Dracula) and Michael the Brave resisted Ottoman control. Internally, the period was marked by conflicts between rival boyar (noble) families and the establishment of serfdom. The capital moved from Târgoviște to Bucharest in the late 17th century, closer to the Ottoman border.

### Phanariote Rule and Foreign Influence
After 1715, Ottoman sultans appointed Phanariotes (Greek officials from Constantinople) as princes, increasing foreign control. Wallachia became a battleground in Russo-Turkish wars. The 1829 Treaty of Adrianople ended Phanariote rule, granted a constitution, and placed Wallachia under Russian protection, fostering economic growth and early nationalist movements.

### Path to Modern Romania
The 1848 revolution, though suppressed, advanced liberal ideals. Following the Crimean War, the Treaty of Paris (1856) placed the principalities under a European congress. A union movement led to the 1859 election of Alexander John Cuza as prince of both Wallachia and Moldavia, creating the United Principalities. This union was solidified under Carol I, leading to the 1881 establishment of the Kingdom of Romania.

### Society: Slavery
Slavery (*robie*) existed in Wallachia from its founding until the mid-19th century, primarily affecting the Roma population. Slaves were owned by the state, monasteries, or boyars. Abolition was achieved in stages between 1843 and 1856, driven by Enlightenment ideas and revolutionary demands.

## Terms
- **Suzerainty**: A relationship where a state (Wallachia) is under the political control of another (Ottoman Empire) but retains internal autonomy.
- **Boyars**: The land-owning aristocracy in Wallachia and Moldavia.
- **Phanariotes**: Greeks from the Phanar district of Constantinople who served as princes in Wallachia and Moldavia under Ottoman rule.
- **Regulamentul Organic**: A quasi-constitutional organic law imposed on Wallachia and Moldavia in 1831–32 under Russian supervision.
- **Vlach**: An exonym used by Slavs and others for Romanian-speaking populations.
- **Muntenia**: The eastern, larger part of Wallachia, often used synonymously with the whole region.
- **Oltenia**: The western part of Wallachia, historically a banat.
- **Robie**: The feudal form of slavery in Wallachia, where slaves were legally considered humans with limited rights.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The exact origins of Wallachian slavery and whether the Roma arrived as free people or slaves remain debated among historians.
- The extent of Bulgarian suzerainty over Wallachian lands in the late 14th century is a point of scholarly discussion.
- The legendary figure Radu Negru, traditionally credited with founding Wallachia in 1290, is considered mythical, though he is often associated with the historical Basarab I.

# Doha

## Overview
Doha is Qatar's capital and largest city, serving as its primary financial, cultural, and administrative hub. Located on the Persian Gulf coast, it houses over 80% of the nation's population, having transformed from a small pearling settlement into a modern global city known for major international events and architectural projects.

## Timeline
- **1681** — Earliest documented mention of Al Bidda, Doha's predecessor.
- **1820s** — Doha is founded as an offshoot of Al Bidda.
- **1821** — British forces bombard and raze Al Bidda for alleged piracy.
- **1868** — British agent Colonel Lewis Pelly imposes a treaty, implicitly recognizing Qatar as distinct from Bahrain.
- **1871** — Ottoman troops establish a presence in Doha.
- **1893** — Battle of Al Wajbah leads to the decline of Ottoman influence in Qatar.
- **1916** — Qatar becomes a British protectorate with Doha as its official capital.
- **1971** — Qatar gains independence; Doha is confirmed as the capital.
- **1996** — Al Jazeera Arabic news channel begins broadcasting from Doha.
- **2006** — Doha hosts the Asian Games, spurring major infrastructure development.
- **2010** — Qatar wins the bid to host the 2022 FIFA World Cup.
- **2014** — Hamad International Airport opens, replacing the old airport.
- **2019** — The new National Museum of Qatar opens.
- **2022** — Doha hosts the FIFA World Cup.

## Body

### History and Founding
Founded in the 1820s near Al Bidda, Doha was initially conflated with the older settlement. Conflict included a British bombardment in 1821 and the Qatari–Bahraini War in 1867. The Al Thani family, who arrived in 1847, eventually assumed control. A pivotal 1868 treaty recognized Qatar as distinct from Bahrain. Ottoman presence from 1871 waned after the 1893 Battle of Al Wajbah. Under a British protectorate from 1916, Doha remained a modest port until 20th-century oil discoveries spurred growth.

### 20th Century to Independence
The pearling industry was central until its early 20th-century collapse. Oil concessions in the 1920s-30s brought slow progress, with significant returns in the 1950s-60s funding modernization. The population grew from about 14,000 in the 1950s to over 83,000 by 1970. Qatar gained independence in 1971, with Doha as its capital. The 1970s saw demolition of old neighborhoods for suburbs, and the metropolitan population surged from 89,000 in the 1970s to over 434,000 by 1997.

### Modern Development and Global Events
Since independence, Doha has pursued rapid modernization and economic diversification. Major projects include Education City, The Pearl-Qatar artificial island, and Msheireb Downtown redevelopment. The city has hosted numerous global events, including the 2006 Asian Games, the 2013 WTO Ministerial Conference, and the 2022 FIFA World Cup. Development is guided by the Qatar National Vision 2030. The city's population more than doubled between 2000 and 2010.

### Geography and Climate
Located on Qatar's central-east coast at 10 meters elevation, Doha has expanded via extensive land reclamation. The geology is mainly dolomitic limestone. It has a hot desert climate with extremely hot summers (average highs over 38°C from May to September) and mild winters. Annual rainfall averages 75 mm. Qatar's highest recorded temperature, 50.4°C, occurred in Doha in 2010.

### Demographics and Economy
Doha's population is overwhelmingly expatriate, primarily from South and Southeast Asia. Arabic is the official language, with English widely used. The economy is built on hydrocarbon revenues but is diversifying into tourism, finance, and technology. Doha was the world's second-highest spending city for international travelers in 2022. Real estate prices have risen significantly due to population growth and events like the World Cup.

### Architecture and Urban Planning
Traditional architecture used limestone, mud, and mangrove beams, with climate-adaptive designs like thick walls and windcatchers. Much of old Doha was demolished in the late 20th century. Modern architecture features iconic skyscrapers like the Doha Tower and cultural institutions like the Museum of Islamic Art. Major planned communities include Lusail City. Transportation has been upgraded with new highways, Hamad International Airport (2014), and the Doha Metro (2019).

## Terms
*   **Al Bidda:** Historical settlement from which Doha originated, first documented in 1681.
*   **British Protectorate:** Status where Qatar was under British protection in foreign affairs, from 1916 until 1971.
*   **Corniche:** A seven-kilometer-long waterfront promenade in Doha.
*   **Education City:** A 14 km² complex housing branch campuses of international universities, launched by the Qatar Foundation.
*   **Hamad International Airport:** Qatar's principal international airport, opened in 2014.
*   **Msheireb Downtown Doha:** A large-scale urban regeneration project preserving the historic downtown area.
*   **Pearling:** Historical industry of harvesting pearls, central to Qatar's economy before oil.
*   **Qatar National Vision 2030:** Long-term development plan focusing on economic, social, and environmental goals.
*   **Souq Waqif:** Traditional market restored and completed in 2008 to preserve its heritage.
*   **The Pearl-Qatar:** Artificial island off Doha's coast, a major real estate development.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   The exact founding date and early relationship between Doha and Al Bidda are subjects of historical conflation.
*   The long-term sustainability of Doha's rapid, resource-funded growth and its economic diversification beyond hydrocarbons.
*   The impact of climate change, with projections suggesting parts of Qatar could become uninhabitable by the end of the 21st century.

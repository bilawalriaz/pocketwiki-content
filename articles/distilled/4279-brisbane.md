# Brisbane

## Overview
Brisbane is the capital and largest city of Queensland, Australia, and the nation's third-most populous city with approximately 2.8 million people. It is a major economic, cultural, and transport hub for the Asia-Pacific region, with a history shaped by Indigenous habitation, colonial settlement, and rapid 20th and 21st-century growth. The city will host the 2032 Summer Olympics and Paralympics.

## Body

### History and Settlement
The Brisbane region has been inhabited for over 22,000 years. The first British outpost was founded in 1824 as a penal settlement for secondary offenders from Sydney. The settlement, known for its harsh conditions, closed in 1842, opening the area to free colonisation. Brisbane became the capital of Queensland in 1859. The early 20th century was marked by labour unrest, and during World War II, the city served as a major Allied headquarters. Post-war decades saw large-scale immigration, suburban expansion, and significant events like the 1974 flood. The late 20th century brought political reform and cultural renewal, leading to major redevelopment projects like South Bank Parklands after World Expo 88.

### Geography and Climate
Brisbane is centred on a winding, tidal estuary of the Brisbane River, which flows into Moreton Bay. The metropolitan area sprawls across a low-lying floodplain between the Pacific Ocean and the Taylor and D'Aguilar mountain ranges. The city is hilly, with prominent rises like Mount Coot-tha. It has a humid subtropical climate with hot, wet summers and mild, dry winters. The city is vulnerable to flooding, with major events in 1893, 1974, 2011, and 2022, and is within the southern reaches of the tropical cyclone risk zone.

### Urban Structure and Demographics
Greater Brisbane is informally divided by the Brisbane River into northside and southside. The central business district (CBD) is walkable and located on a river peninsula. The city has a sprawling metropolitan area with a diverse population; over 36% of residents are foreign-born, with significant communities from New Zealand, England, India, China, and the Philippines. The most common religious affiliations are Christianity (44.3%) and "No religion" (41.4%).

### Economy and Infrastructure
Brisbane is a global city and major business hub with strengths in mining, finance, transport, and IT. The Port of Brisbane is Australia's third-busiest for value of goods. The city has an extensive transport network, including the Brisbane Airport (Australia's third-busiest), a large rail and bus network coordinated by Translink, and iconic CityCat ferries. Major road tunnels form the TransApex network. The Cross River Rail project is a significant infrastructure addition.

### Culture and Sport
Brisbane has a vibrant cultural scene, anchored by the Queensland Art Gallery and Gallery of Modern Art (GOMA). It is known for its live music history and hosts major annual events like the Royal Queensland Exhibition (Ekka). The city is home to numerous professional sports teams, including the Brisbane Broncos (rugby league) and Brisbane Lions (Australian rules football). It has hosted major international events and will host the 2032 Olympics.

## Terms
- **LGA (Local Government Area):** An administrative division of a state or territory, such as the City of Brisbane.
- **GCCSA (Greater Capital City Statistical Area):** A statistical geography defining the functional extent of a major city.
- **Queenslander:** A style of residential architecture common in Queensland, typically featuring timber construction, large verandahs, and elevation on stumps.
- **Brisbane tuff:** A form of welded ignimbrite rock characteristic of the region, used in historical construction.
- **TransApex:** A network of major road tunnels under metropolitan Brisbane.
- **CityCat:** A high-speed catamaran ferry service operating on the Brisbane River.
- **Humid subtropical climate (Köppen: Cfa):** A climate zone with hot, humid summers and mild, dry winters.

## Debates and Open Questions
- There is a difference of opinion between local traditional owners over the spelling, provenance, and pronunciation of the Indigenous name for Brisbane (Meanjin vs. Magandjin).
- The trade of blackbirded labour through Brisbane in the 19th century is debated; while administered as an indenture system, many historians regard it as a form of slavery or slavery-like coercion.

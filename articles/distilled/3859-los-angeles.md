# Los Angeles

## Overview
Los Angeles is the most populous city in California and the second-most populous in the United States, serving as the commercial, financial, and cultural center of Southern California. Its history spans from indigenous Tongva settlements to Spanish colonization, Mexican rule, and U.S. statehood, followed by explosive 20th-century growth fueled by oil, water infrastructure, and the entertainment industry. Today, it is a global metropolis known for its ethnic diversity, economic power, and cultural influence, though it faces challenges like air pollution, housing costs, and homelessness.

## Body

### History and Growth
The area was originally inhabited by the Tongva people. Spanish explorer Juan Rodríguez Cabrillo claimed it in 1542, and the Spanish founded the Mission San Gabriel Arcángel in 1771. The city itself was founded in 1781 as a small pueblo. After Mexican independence in 1821, it became the regional capital of Alta California. Following the Mexican–American War, the 1848 Treaty of Guadalupe Hidalgo transferred the area to the United States. The arrival of railroads in the 1870s-1880s and the discovery of oil in 1892 triggered rapid growth. The completion of the Los Angeles Aqueduct in 1913 was critical for sustaining this expansion. The early 20th century saw the rise of the film industry, with Hollywood merging into Los Angeles in 1910. During World War II, the city became a major center for aircraft and shipbuilding. Post-war suburbanization, fueled by the interstate highway system, led to significant sprawl.

### Geography and Climate
Los Angeles covers about 469 square miles (1,210 km2) of land, featuring a mix of flat basins and hilly terrain. The highest point in the city is Mount Lukens at 5,074 ft (1,547 m). The Los Angeles River is the primary drainage channel. The city has a semi-arid climate with dry, warm summers and mild, wetter winters. It experiences significant microclimates due to its topography. The area is seismically active, located on the Pacific Ring of Fire and near the San Andreas Fault system, making it prone to earthquakes.

### Demographics and Society
The 2020 U.S. census reported a population of 3,898,747. The city is highly diverse, with a 2023 estimate showing 47.2% Hispanic or Latino, 28.3% non-Hispanic White, 12.0% Asian, and 8.5% Black residents. Numerous ethnic enclaves, such as Chinatown, Koreatown, and Little Armenia, reflect this diversity. Over 200 languages are spoken. As of January 2024, the city had 45,252 homeless people, with Skid Row being a major epicenter.

### Economy and Infrastructure
Los Angeles has a diverse economy driven by international trade, entertainment, aerospace, technology, and tourism. The contiguous ports of Los Angeles and Long Beach form one of the world's busiest container port complexes. The city is a major manufacturing center. Transportation is heavily reliant on an extensive freeway network, which is among the most congested in the U.S. Public transit includes the Los Angeles Metro bus and rail system. Los Angeles International Airport (LAX) is the primary airport. The city hosted the Olympics in 1932 and 1984 and will host them again in 2028.

### Arts and Culture
Los Angeles is a global center for the entertainment industry, home to Hollywood and major film studios. It is also a major hub for the performing arts, with institutions like the Los Angeles Music Center. The city has a vast number of museums and art galleries, including the Los Angeles County Museum of Art and the Getty Center. Its architecture reflects Spanish, Mexican, and American influences. The food culture is a fusion of global cuisines, heavily influenced by immigrant communities.

## Terms
- **Pueblo**: A type of Spanish colonial settlement or town.
- **Tongva**: The indigenous people native to the Los Angeles Basin and surrounding areas.
- **Secularization**: The process of transferring church properties to state control, which occurred in Mexican-era California.
- **Zoning ordinance**: A law that regulates how land and buildings can be used in specific areas.
- **Smog**: A type of air pollution formed when sunlight reacts with nitrogen oxides and volatile organic compounds in the atmosphere.
- **Microclimate**: A local set of atmospheric conditions that differ from those in the surrounding area, often due to topography.
- **Gross Metropolitan Product (GMP)**: The total economic output of a metropolitan area.
- **Atmospheric inversion**: A weather phenomenon where a layer of warm air traps cooler air and pollutants near the ground.
- **TEU (Twenty-foot Equivalent Unit)**: A standard unit of cargo capacity for container ships and terminals.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The original name of the 1781 settlement is disputed, with variations recorded in historical sources.
- The correct English pronunciation of "Los Angeles" was debated for decades, with multiple variants in use before a standard was adopted.
- The city's zoning policies in the 20th century, which drastically reduced housing capacity, are a point of analysis regarding current housing shortages and affordability.
- The causes and solutions for the city's persistent homelessness crisis, particularly the roles of economic hardship, housing costs, and substance abuse, are ongoing issues.

# John Knox

## Overview
John Knox (c. 1514–1572) was a Scottish Reformed theologian and the principal leader of the Scottish Reformation. He was instrumental in overthrowing Catholic authority in Scotland and establishing the Presbyterian Church of Scotland (the Kirk), shaping its theology, governance, and national identity.

## Timeline
- **c. 1514** — Born in or near Haddington, East Lothian.
- **1536** — Ordained a Catholic priest in Edinburgh.
- **1546** — Associate of reformer George Wishart; Wishart executed.
- **1547** — Preaches first sermon at St Andrews Castle; castle falls to French; Knox enslaved in French galleys.
- **1549** — Released; goes to England, licensed in Church of England.
- **1553** — Mary I ascends English throne; Knox flees to Continent.
- **1554–55** — Minister in Geneva and Frankfurt; final break with Church of England.
- **1559** — Returns to Scotland; leads Protestant "Congregation" in revolution against regent Mary of Guise.
- **1560** — Scottish Parliament abolishes papal authority; Scots Confession approved; Knox drafts Book of Discipline.
- **1561** — Mary, Queen of Scots, returns to Scotland; series of confrontations with Knox begin.
- **1572** — Dies in Edinburgh; buried at St Giles'.

## Body

### Early Life and Conversion
Born to a merchant family, Knox was educated at the University of St Andrews and ordained a Catholic priest in 1536. He worked as a notary and tutor for Protestant lairds. His conversion to Protestantism was influenced by reformers Patrick Hamilton and George Wishart. After Wishart's execution in 1546, Knox joined the Protestant garrison at St Andrews Castle, where he was called to preach in 1547.

### Captivity and Exile
Following the French capture of St Andrews, Knox spent 19 months as a galley slave in France (1547–49). After release, he served in the Church of England under Edward VI, becoming a royal chaplain and influencing the Book of Common Prayer. He married his first wife, Margery Bowes, in England. With the Catholic accession of Mary I, Knox fled to the Continent in 1554. In Geneva, he learned Reformed theology and polity from John Calvin. He later led an English refugee congregation in Frankfurt but was forced out over liturgical disputes, ending his formal ties to the Church of England.

### Leadership of the Scottish Reformation
Knox returned to Scotland in 1559, galvanizing the Protestant "Congregation" of nobles against the regent, Mary of Guise. His preaching sparked iconoclastic riots and a civil conflict that, with English military support, led to the regent's deposition and death in 1560. The subsequent Parliament abolished papal jurisdiction, condemned Catholic doctrine, and authorized a new Reformed church. Knox and other ministers drafted the Scots Confession and the Book of Discipline, which outlined a Presbyterian system of church governance with elected pastors and superintendents, though Parliament did not fully fund it.

### Conflict with Mary, Queen of Scots
After Mary's return in 1561, Knox repeatedly clashed with her over her Catholic practices and her right to rule. He argued that subjects could resist rulers who violated God's law. Their confrontations, including debates over her proposed marriage to Don Carlos, were intense but did not lead to his conviction for treason. Knox's influence waned as the nobility grew more cautious, but he continued to preach against the queen, especially after the murder of her secretary David Rizzio and her subsequent marriage to the Earl of Bothwell. He supported her deposition in 1567 and called for her execution.

### Final Years and Legacy
Knox spent his last years preaching in Edinburgh and writing his *History of the Reformation in Scotland*. He died in 1572. His primary legacy was not just the rejection of Catholicism, but the establishment of a Presbyterian polity—a system of church governance by elected elders—which became the foundation of the Church of Scotland. His ideas on resisting unjust rule also influenced later Puritan and political thought.

## Terms
*   **Reformation:** The 16th-century religious movement to reform the Catholic Church, leading to the establishment of Protestant churches.
*   **Kirk:** The common name for the Church of Scotland, established by the Reformation.
*   **Presbyterian polity:** A system of church governance by elected elders (presbyters) and regional assemblies, as opposed to rule by bishops.
*   **Congregation:** The name for the coalition of Scottish Protestant nobles and their supporters who opposed Catholic authority.
*   **Book of Common Prayer:** The foundational prayer book of the Church of England, which Knox helped revise.
*   **Scots Confession:** The Reformed statement of faith drafted by Knox and others, approved by the Scottish Parliament in 1560.
*   **Book of Discipline:** The document outlining the proposed organization and funding of the Reformed Kirk, drafted in 1560.
*   **Regent:** A person appointed to administer a state because the monarch is a minor, absent, or incapacitated.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   The exact date and circumstances of Knox's conversion from Catholicism to Protestantism are unknown.
*   The precise reason for his release from the French galleys in 1549 is uncertain.
*   The extent of Knox's personal responsibility for the iconoclastic riots of 1559 versus broader social and political tensions is debated by historians.
*   The practical implementation and financial support for the Presbyterian system he envisioned remained contentious and incomplete during his lifetime.

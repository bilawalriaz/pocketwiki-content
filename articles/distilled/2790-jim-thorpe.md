# Jim Thorpe

## Overview
Jim Thorpe (1887–1953) was a Sac and Fox Nation athlete widely regarded as one of the most versatile in modern sports history. He won two Olympic gold medals in 1912, played professional football, baseball, and basketball, and was the first Native American to win an Olympic gold medal for the United States. His career and legacy are marked by extraordinary athletic achievement, a controversial loss and restoration of his Olympic titles, and the racial inequalities of his era.

## Timeline
- **May 22 or 28, 1887** — Born in Indian Territory (now Oklahoma).
- **1904** — Enrolled at Carlisle Indian Industrial School in Pennsylvania.
- **1911** — Gained national fame as a football star at Carlisle, leading the team to an 11–1 record.
- **July 1912** — Won gold medals in pentathlon and decathlon at the Stockholm Olympics.
- **September 1912** — Won the AAU All-Around Championship, setting a new record.
- **January 1913** — Newspaper reports revealed he had played semi-professional baseball before the Olympics.
- **1913** — IOC stripped him of his Olympic titles for violating amateurism rules.
- **1913–1919** — Played Major League Baseball for the New York Giants and others.
- **1915–1928** — Played professional football, primarily for the Canton Bulldogs.
- **1920–1921** — Served as the first president of the American Professional Football Association (later NFL).
- **March 28, 1953** — Died of heart failure in Lomita, California.
- **1983** — IOC restored his Olympic medals with replicas, declaring him co-champion.
- **July 14, 2022** — IOC officially restored Thorpe as the sole champion in both 1912 Olympic events.

## Body

### Early Life and Education
Thorpe's birth details are uncertain, with no birth certificate found. He was raised in the Sauk culture with the name Wa-Tho-Huk ("Bright Path"). After his twin brother and mother died, he ran away from schools before enrolling at the Carlisle Indian Industrial School in 1904. There, his athletic talent was recognized by coach Glenn "Pop" Warner. He excelled in football, track, and other sports, becoming a three-time All-American in football.

### Olympic Career and Controversy
At the 1912 Stockholm Olympics, Thorpe won gold medals in the newly introduced pentathlon and decathlon, setting an Olympic record in the decathlon. He also competed in the long jump and high jump. In 1913, it was revealed he had been paid for playing semi-professional baseball in 1909 and 1910, violating contemporary amateurism rules. The IOC stripped him of his titles, though the protest was filed after the 30-day limit stipulated in the 1912 rulebook. His medals were posthumously restored in 1983 as co-champion, and he was definitively reinstated as sole champion in 2022.

### Professional Sports Career
After being declared a professional, Thorpe signed with the New York Giants baseball team in 1913, playing in the major leagues until 1919. He also played professional football, joining the Canton Bulldogs in 1915 and helping them win three Ohio League championships. He was the nominal first president of the American Professional Football Association (APFA) from 1920 to 1921, which became the NFL in 1922. He played for six NFL teams until 1928 and also barnstormed with all-Native American football and basketball teams.

### Later Life and Legacy
Thorpe struggled financially after his sports career ended, working various jobs and appearing in over 70 films, often as an extra. He suffered from alcoholism and died in poverty in 1953. His remains were controversially moved to Pennsylvania, where the towns of Mauch Chunk and East Mauch Chunk merged and renamed themselves Jim Thorpe in his honor. He has been inducted into numerous halls of fame, including the inaugural classes of the Pro Football and College Football Halls of Fame. In 2024, he was posthumously awarded the Presidential Medal of Freedom.

## Terms
*   **Amateurism:** A principle in early 20th-century sports that barred athletes who had received payment for playing from competing in events like the Olympics.
*   **Pentathlon:** An Olympic track and field contest consisting of five events: long jump, javelin throw, 200-meter dash, discus throw, and 1500-meter run.
*   **Decathlon:** A two-day Olympic track and field contest comprising ten events, testing versatility in running, jumping, and throwing.
*   **American Professional Football Association (APFA):** The predecessor to the National Football League (NFL), founded in 1920.
*   **Barnstorming:** Traveling from place to place performing sports exhibitions, common in early professional sports.
*   **Reserve Clause:** A standard contract provision in early professional sports that bound a player to one team indefinitely, limiting free agency.
*   **Native American Graves Protection and Repatriation Act (NAGPRA):** A U.S. federal law that requires institutions receiving federal funds to return Native American cultural items and human remains to their respective peoples.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Reason for Medal Stripping:** While the official reason was violation of amateurism rules, it has often been suggested that Thorpe's Native American ethnicity was a factor in the IOC's decision, reflecting the racial inequality of the era.
*   **Burial Site Dispute:** Thorpe's remains are interred in Jim Thorpe, Pennsylvania, a town he never visited. His sons have sought to have his remains returned to Sac and Fox land in Oklahoma, citing the wishes of other family members and NAGPRA. Courts have ruled against this, stating the town is not a "museum" under the law.
*   **Co-Champion vs. Sole Champion:** From 1983 to 2022, the IOC listed Thorpe as a co-champion with the silver medalists, who themselves always considered Thorpe the rightful sole winner. This was officially corrected in 2022.

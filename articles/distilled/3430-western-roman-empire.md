# Western Roman Empire

## Overview
The Western Roman Empire refers to the western provinces of the Roman Empire when administered separately from the eastern provinces by a distinct imperial court, particularly from AD 395 to 476. Its collapse in 476, marked by the deposition of the last emperor Romulus Augustulus, was a transformative political event of late antiquity, leading to the transition from the classical world to the Middle Ages in Western Europe.

## Timeline
- **286** — Emperor Diocletian establishes the Tetrarchy, formally dividing imperial administration between East and West.
- **395** — Death of Theodosius I; the Empire is permanently divided between his sons, Honorius (West) and Arcadius (East).
- **410** — Visigoths under Alaric sack Rome, the first time the city had fallen to a foreign enemy in nearly 800 years.
- **439** — Vandals conquer the wealthy province of Africa, seizing Carthage and disrupting Roman tax and food supplies.
- **451** — A Roman-Visigothic coalition defeats Attila the Hun at the Battle of the Catalaunian Plains.
- **455** — Vandals sack Rome for fourteen days following the assassination of Emperor Valentinian III.
- **461-472** — General Ricimer dominates the Western court, appointing a series of puppet emperors.
- **476** — Odoacer, leader of Germanic foederati, deposes the last Western emperor, Romulus Augustulus.
- **480** — Eastern Emperor Zeno abolishes the separate Western imperial court after the murder of the exiled Western emperor Julius Nepos.
- **533-554** — Eastern Emperor Justinian I's generals reconquer North Africa, Italy, and parts of Spain for the Empire.
- **800** — Pope Leo III crowns Charlemagne as "Roman Emperor," creating a new imperial title in Western Europe.

## Body

### Origins and Division
The concept of dividing the Empire's administration arose from the practical difficulty of governing its vast territories. Emperor Diocletian institutionalized this after the Crisis of the Third Century by creating the Tetrarchy in 286, with two senior emperors (Augusti) and two junior heirs (Caesars). Although the Tetrarchy collapsed, the east-west division persisted. The permanent political split is conventionally dated to the death of Theodosius I in 395, which left the Western Empire to his young son Honorius and the Eastern Empire to Arcadius. Contemporary Romans viewed this as a single polity with two courts, not two separate empires.

### Decline and Collapse
The Western Empire faced relentless pressure from barbarian groups, many of whom were settled as *foederati* (allied troops) within its borders. Key crises included the Visigothic sack of Rome in 410 and the Vandal conquest of Africa in 439, which crippled the Western economy. Internal instability was marked by weak emperors and powerful Germanic generals like Ricimer, who ruled through puppet rulers. The final collapse came in 476 when the Germanic chieftain Odoacer deposed the last emperor, Romulus Augustulus. However, the Eastern Emperor Zeno did not recognize this event as the end of the Western court, maintaining that the exiled emperor Julius Nepos was the legitimate ruler until his murder in 480.

### Aftermath and Legacy
Following 476, Germanic kingdoms like the Ostrogoths in Italy and Visigoths in Hispania maintained Roman administrative systems and laws. The Eastern Empire under Justinian I temporarily reconquered parts of the West in the 6th century, but these gains were lost to Lombard invasions and the Islamic conquests. The ideal of a Roman Empire in the West was revived with the papal coronation of Charlemagne in 800, which established the precedent for the Holy Roman Empire. The Western Empire's enduring legacy includes the spread of Latin, the foundation of Romance languages, the adoption of Roman law, and the central role of the Catholic Church.

## Terms
*   **Tetrarchy:** A system of government introduced by Diocletian with two senior emperors (Augusti) and two junior heirs (Caesars) to manage the Empire's vast territory.
*   **Foederati:** Barbarian groups granted land within the Empire in exchange for military service, often becoming semi-independent and destabilizing.
*   **Magister militum:** A high-ranking military commander, often of barbarian origin, who became the de facto ruler behind puppet Western emperors in the 5th century.
*   **Praetorian Prefecture:** A major administrative division of the Empire, each governed by a Praetorian Prefect who served as the emperor's chief minister.
*   **Exarchate:** A type of Byzantine province established by Emperor Maurice in the late 6th century, combining civil and military authority to defend reconquered territories like Ravenna and Africa.
*   **Translatio imperii:** The concept of the transfer of imperial authority from Rome to other realms, used to justify the legitimacy of the Holy Roman Empire.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **The "Fall" Date:** Historians debate whether 476 (deposition of Romulus Augustulus) or 480 (abolition of the Western court by Zeno) is the true end, as the Eastern Empire continued to claim sovereignty and the last Western emperor, Julius Nepos, was alive until 480.
*   **Cause of Collapse:** Scholarship emphasizes multiple factors over a single cause, including barbarian invasions, economic decline, administrative decay, over-reliance on mercenary armies, and the loss of tax-rich provinces like Africa.
*   **Continuity vs. Catastrophe:** Some historians argue for a gradual transformation and cultural continuity into the Middle Ages, while others stress a sharp political and economic break, especially in Italy and Gaul.

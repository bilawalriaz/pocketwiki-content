# West Bengal

## Overview
West Bengal is a state in eastern India, situated along the Bay of Bengal. It is the fourth-most populous state in India and a major cultural and intellectual center, historically known for the Bengali Renaissance. The state's modern identity was shaped by the 1947 Partition of Bengal, which created a Hindu-majority West Bengal and a Muslim-majority East Bengal (later Bangladesh), leading to significant demographic and political changes.

## Timeline
- **c. 1000 BCE** — The Dravidian tribe "Bang" possibly settles the region.
- **3rd century BCE** — The Maurya Empire, under Ashoka, extends over Bengal.
- **8th century CE** — The Pala dynasty begins a 400-year rule of the region.
- **1352** — The Islamic Bengal Sultanate is founded, becoming a major global trading nation.
- **1576** — Bengal is absorbed into the Mughal Empire.
- **1757** — The British East India Company defeats the Nawab of Bengal at the Battle of Plassey.
- **1764** — The Battle of Buxar leads to the company gaining revenue collection rights.
- **1947** — The Partition of Bengal creates West Bengal (India) and East Bengal (Pakistan).
- **1977** — The Left Front, led by the Communist Party, wins the state election, beginning 34 years of rule.
- **2011** — The Trinamool Congress coalition defeats the Left Front, ending its long rule.

## Body

### History and Etymology
The region's early history features ancient kingdoms like Vanga and Magadha, part of major pan-Indian empires. Islam arrived via trade and later conquest, leading to the wealthy Bengal Sultanate. After Mughal rule, the region became semi-independent under the Nawabs before British colonization following the 1757 Battle of Plassey. Calcutta (now Kolkata) served as the capital of British India until 1911. The area was a hub for the Indian independence movement. The name "Bengal" may derive from the ancient Vanga kingdom. In 1947, the province was partitioned along religious lines, creating West Bengal. Proposals to rename the state to "Bengal" or "Bangla" have been turned down by the central government.

### Geography and Environment
West Bengal stretches from the Himalayas in the north to the Bay of Bengal in the south. Its geography includes the Darjeeling Himalayan hill region, the Ganges delta, the Rarh region, and the Sundarbans mangrove forest. The Ganges river is the main waterway, splitting into the Bhagirathi and Hooghly rivers. The state's climate ranges from tropical savanna to humid subtropical. Forest cover is about 18.93% of the area. The Sundarbans, a UNESCO World Heritage site, is a critical habitat for the endangered Bengal tiger.

### Demographics and Society
As of the 2011 census, West Bengal had a population of about 91 million, with a density of 1,029 people per km². The literacy rate is 77.08%, and the total fertility rate is 1.6, the lowest among Indian states. Bengalis are the majority ethnic group. Bengali is the primary language, with Nepali having official status in the Darjeeling hills. Hindus (70.54%) and Muslims (27.01%) are the two largest religious communities.

### Economy
West Bengal's economy is the sixth-largest in India. Agriculture is a leading sector, with rice as the principal crop. Industries are concentrated in the Kolkata region and the mineral-rich western highlands. The state has faced challenges including economic stagnation during the Left Front era, industrial shutdowns, and difficulty attracting foreign investment due to infrastructure and policy issues. The service sector is a major and growing component of the economy.

### Culture
West Bengal has a rich cultural heritage. Its literary tradition includes the medieval Charyapada and modern giants like Nobel laureate Rabindranath Tagore. The Bengali film industry, based in Kolkata's "Tollywood," is renowned for art-house cinema. Major festivals include Durga Puja, a massive cultural carnival, and Christmas, celebrated widely in Kolkata. Traditional cuisine emphasizes rice and fish, with distinctive sweets like Rôshogolla.

## Terms
- **Bengali Renaissance:** A cultural, social, and intellectual movement in the 19th and early 20th centuries that modernized Bengali society.
- **GSDP (Gross State Domestic Product):** The total value of goods and services produced within a state in a year.
- **Left Front:** A coalition of communist parties that governed West Bengal from 1977 to 2011.
- **Naxalite:** A radical Maoist communist movement that was active in parts of West Bengal in the 1960s and 70s.
- **Sundarbans:** A vast mangrove forest and UNESCO World Heritage site in the Ganges delta, shared with Bangladesh.
- **TFR (Total Fertility Rate):** The average number of children born to a woman over her lifetime; West Bengal's is 1.6.
- **Pandal:** A temporary structure erected for festivals, especially during Durga Puja.

## Debates and open questions
- The origin of the name "Bengal" is unknown, with theories linking it to the Dravidian tribe "Bang" or the ancient Vanga kingdom.
- Proposals to officially rename the state to "Bengal" or "Bangla" have been contentious, with the central government rejecting them to avoid confusion with Bangladesh.
- The state's economic recovery since the end of Left Front rule is ongoing, with persistent challenges in infrastructure, industrial growth, and attracting investment.

# Jackie Robinson

## Overview
Jackie Robinson was an American baseball player who became the first African American to play in Major League Baseball (MLB) in the modern era, breaking the sport's color line in 1947. His integration of baseball was a pivotal moment in the broader civil rights movement, challenging racial segregation and demonstrating the possibility of equality through talent and character.

## Timeline
- **January 31, 1919** — Born in Cairo, Georgia.
- **April 15, 1947** — Makes MLB debut for the Brooklyn Dodgers, breaking the color barrier.
- **1947** — Wins the inaugural MLB Rookie of the Year Award.
- **1949** — Wins the National League Most Valuable Player (MVP) Award.
- **1955** — Helps the Brooklyn Dodgers win the World Series.
- **1962** — Inducted into the Baseball Hall of Fame in his first year of eligibility.
- **October 24, 1972** — Dies in Stamford, Connecticut, at age 53.
- **April 15, 1997** — MLB retires his uniform number, 42, across all teams.
- **April 15, 2004** — MLB establishes the first annual "Jackie Robinson Day."

## Body

### Early Life and College Career
Born into a family of sharecroppers, Robinson moved to Pasadena, California, after his father left. He was a four-sport star at John Muir High School and later at Pasadena Junior College and the University of California, Los Angeles (UCLA), where he became the school's first athlete to earn varsity letters in four sports: baseball, basketball, football, and track. His athletic prowess, particularly in football, was notable, but he left UCLA just short of graduation.

### Military Service and Negro Leagues
Robinson was drafted into the U.S. Army during World War II. He was court-martialed in 1944 for refusing to move to the back of a segregated Army bus, but was acquitted and honorably discharged. After the war, he played one season for the Kansas City Monarchs of the Negro American League. In 1945, he was interviewed by Branch Rickey, general manager of the Brooklyn Dodgers, who was seeking a player to break MLB's color line. Rickey required Robinson to promise he would not fight back against the inevitable racial abuse. Robinson agreed, and Rickey signed him to a contract.

### Breaking the Color Barrier (1947)
Robinson debuted for the Brooklyn Dodgers on April 15, 1947, ending approximately 60 years of segregation in professional baseball. He faced intense hostility from fans and opposing players, including threats of strikes and racist epithets. Despite this, he performed brilliantly, winning the first Rookie of the Year Award and leading the Dodgers to the National League pennant. His success and dignified response to racism were a powerful challenge to segregation.

### MLB Career and Legacy
Over a 10-year career (1947–1956), Robinson was a six-time All-Star, the 1949 NL MVP, and a key contributor to the Dodgers' 1955 World Series championship. He was a dynamic player who combined hitting, speed, and aggressive baserunning, helping to define a new era of baseball. After retiring, he became the first Black television analyst in MLB and the first Black vice president of a major American corporation, Chock full o'Nuts. He also helped found the Freedom National Bank and was active in civil rights and politics. Robinson was posthumously awarded the Presidential Medal of Freedom and the Congressional Gold Medal. In 1997, MLB retired his number 42 league-wide, and since 2004, all players have worn it on Jackie Robinson Day.

## Terms
*   **Color Line:** The informal practice of excluding African American players from Major League Baseball, which was in effect from the 1880s until 1947.
*   **Negro Leagues:** Professional baseball leagues for African American players that operated during the era of MLB's color line.
*   **Branch Rickey:** The Brooklyn Dodgers general manager who signed Jackie Robinson with the explicit goal of integrating Major League Baseball.
*   **Rookie of the Year Award:** An annual MLB award; Robinson won the first-ever version in 1947.
*   **Most Valuable Player (MVP):** An annual award given to the best player in each league; Robinson won the NL MVP in 1949.
*   **Congressional Gold Medal:** The highest civilian award bestowed by the U.S. Congress, awarded posthumously to Robinson in 2005.
*   **Presidential Medal of Freedom:** The highest civilian honor in the United States, awarded posthumously to Robinson in 1984.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   The exact nature and threat of a planned player strike by the St. Louis Cardinals in 1947 to protest Robinson's debut is debated. While a newspaper report detailed the plot, the Cardinals players denied it, and some historians question the account's veracity.
*   The circumstances of Robinson's departure from baseball are complex. The Dodgers traded him to the New York Giants after the 1956 season, but the trade was never completed because Robinson had already privately agreed to retire and become an executive with Chock full o'Nuts.

# Palembang

## Overview
Palembang is the capital of South Sumatra province in Indonesia, a major city on the Musi River. It was the historic capital of the powerful Srivijaya maritime empire, which dominated regional trade from the 7th to 13th centuries. Today, it is a significant economic and cultural center, known for its riverine geography, diverse population, and role as a host for major international sporting events.

## Timeline
- **682 AD** — The Kedukan Bukit Inscription, the oldest in Palembang, is dated, marking the early history of Srivijaya.
- **7th–13th C.** — Palembang is the capital of the Srivijaya kingdom, a major Buddhist maritime trade power.
- **1407** — Ming Admiral Zheng He defeats pirate Chen Zuyi at Palembang, restoring order to the Strait of Malacca.
- **1659** — The Sultanate of Palembang is proclaimed.
- **1825** — The Dutch abolish the Palembang Sultanate and establish direct colonial rule.
- **1906** — Palembang is chartered as a city.
- **1942** — Japanese forces capture Palembang after a battle, seizing its vital oil refineries.
- **1945** — Indonesian nationalists raise the flag, declaring Republican control.
- **1947** — Dutch forces occupy Palembang after the "Five Days and Nights Battle."
- **1965** — The Ampera Bridge, a key city landmark, is opened.
- **2018** — Palembang co-hosts the 2018 Asian Games with Jakarta; Indonesia's first light rail system begins operation.

## Body

### History and Significance
Palembang's importance stems from its role as the capital of Srivijaya, a Buddhist kingdom that controlled maritime trade through the Strait of Malacca for centuries. The city was a thriving international trade hub and Buddhist scholarly center. After Srivijaya's decline, Palembang faced periods of piracy and Javanese influence before establishing the Palembang Sultanate in 1659, which made Islam the dominant religion. The Dutch abolished the sultanate in 1825, incorporating the city into the Dutch East Indies. Palembang was a major objective in World War II due to its oil refineries, which were captured by Japan in 1942. The city played a role in the Indonesian National Revolution and later experienced rapid economic growth under Dutch and Indonesian rule, marked by infrastructure projects like the Ampera Bridge.

### Geography and Climate
The city is defined by the Musi River, which divides it into Seberang Ilir (north) and Seberang Ulu (south). It lies in a vast, low-lying wetland area with an average elevation of 8 meters. The climate is tropical rainforest (Köppen Af), hot and humid year-round with significant rainfall. The low topography makes the city prone to flooding during the wet season, while dried peatlands in the dry season can lead to wildfires and haze.

### Demography and Culture
Palembang is ethnically diverse, with the indigenous Palembang people alongside significant Javanese, Minangkabau, Arab, Indian, and Chinese communities. The local language, Palembang, is a Malayic variety with Javanese influence. Islam is the predominant religion (93.21% in 2023). The culture is a blend of Malay, Javanese, and foreign influences, evident in its cuisine (famous for *pempek*), traditional *songket* textiles, woodcarving, and dances like the Tanggai Dance.

### Economy and Transportation
As a regional business center, Palembang's economy relies on manufacturing, construction, and trade. Key industries include oil refining, cement production, and rubber processing. The city is a major port for coal and rubber exports. Transportation includes the Trans-Sumatra Toll Road, an extensive bus network, and the Palembang Light Rail Transit (LRT), which was Indonesia's first. The Musi River remains important for local transport, and the city is served by Sultan Mahmud Badaruddin II International Airport.

## Terms
- **Srivijaya**: A powerful Buddhist maritime empire based in Palembang that controlled trade in the Strait of Malacca from the 7th to 13th centuries.
- **Musi River**: One of the longest rivers in Sumatra, it flows through Palembang, dividing the city and historically serving as its economic heart.
- **Ampera Bridge**: A major landmark bridge crossing the Musi River, connecting the city's two main areas; opened in 1965.
- **Seberang Ilir / Seberang Ulu**: The two main divisions of Palembang, north and south of the Musi River, respectively.
- **Songket**: A traditional hand-woven fabric from Palembang, patterned with gold or silver threads, used in ceremonial attire.
- **Pempek**: A famous Palembang fish cake dish, a staple of the local cuisine.
- **Kecamatan**: An administrative district; Palembang is divided into 18 of these.
- **Kelurahan**: An urban village; the smallest administrative unit within a *kecamatan*.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The exact founding date and early political structure of Srivijaya, the kingdom for which Palembang was the capital, remains a subject of scholarly debate, with inscriptions like the Kedukan Bukit providing key but debated evidence.
- The etymology of the name "Palembang" has multiple proposed origins, including from Malay words meaning "a place to pan gold" or "a place which was constantly inundated by water."

# John Snow

## Overview
John Snow (1813–1858) was an English physician and a pioneer in both anaesthesia and public health. He is a foundational figure in modern epidemiology for his work tracing a cholera outbreak to a contaminated water pump, which challenged prevailing disease theories and led to major improvements in urban water and sanitation systems worldwide.

## Timeline
- **15 March 1813** — Born in York, England.
- **1827** — Began medical apprenticeship in Newcastle-upon-Tyne.
- **1832** — First encountered a cholera epidemic while an apprentice.
- **1836** — Enrolled at the Hunterian school of medicine in London.
- **1844** — Graduated MD from the University of London.
- **1847** — Published *On the Inhalation of the Vapor of Ether*; began studying chloroform.
- **1849** — Published his cholera theory in *On the Mode of Communication of Cholera*.
- **7 April 1853** — Administered chloroform to Queen Victoria during childbirth.
- **1854** — Investigated the Soho cholera outbreak, identifying the Broad Street pump.
- **1855** — Published a detailed treatise on cholera incorporating his Soho investigation.
- **16 June 1858** — Died in London from a stroke.

## Body

### Early Life and Medical Training
Snow was born into a poor neighbourhood in York, where he experienced unsanitary conditions and contaminated water. He demonstrated an early aptitude for mathematics and began a medical apprenticeship at age 14. During this time, he first treated cholera victims in 1832, gaining early experience with the disease. He later moved to London to complete his medical studies, earning his MD in 1844.

### Contributions to Anaesthesia
Snow became a leading authority on anaesthesia, a new field in the 1840s. He studied the effects of ether and chloroform, calculating safe dosages and designing administration apparatus. He was the first to systematically use chloroform for obstetric anaesthesia, administering it to 77 patients. His work gained widespread acceptance after he successfully administered chloroform to Queen Victoria during the births of her children in 1853 and 1857.

### Investigation of Cholera
Snow was a skeptic of the dominant miasma theory, which blamed cholera on "bad air." Instead, he hypothesized that cholera was transmitted through contaminated water. His theory was published in 1849 and expanded in 1855. His most famous work was his investigation of the 1854 Soho cholera outbreak. By interviewing residents and mapping deaths, he traced the source to the Broad Street public water pump. His evidence persuaded the local council to remove the pump's handle, an action credited with ending the outbreak, though Snow noted the epidemic was already declining. He further supported his theory with statistics showing a 14-fold higher cholera rate in homes supplied by sewage-polluted Thames water versus cleaner sources.

### Political Controversy and Legacy
Despite his evidence, Snow's theory was initially rejected by officials and many of his peers, as it implied an unpleasant fecal-oral route of disease transmission. It was not until 1866, during another outbreak, that his chief opponent William Farr accepted the role of contaminated water. Snow's work is now regarded as the founding event of epidemiology. His legacy includes numerous honours, such as a commemorative pump in London and York, and the annual Pumphandle Lecture by the John Snow Society.

## Terms
- **Epidemiology**: The study and analysis of the distribution, patterns, and determinants of health and disease conditions in defined populations.
- **Miasma theory**: The obsolete theory that diseases like cholera are caused by "bad air" or noxious vapours from rotting organic matter.
- **Germ theory**: The theory that many diseases are caused by the presence and reproduction of specific microorganisms.
- **Anaesthesia**: The use of agents (like ether or chloroform) to induce insensitivity to pain during medical procedures.
- **Cholera**: An acute diarrhoeal infection caused by ingestion of food or water contaminated with the bacterium *Vibrio cholerae*.
- **Fecal-oral route**: A route of transmission of a disease whereby pathogens in fecal particles pass from one host to the oral cavity of another host.
- **Obstetric anaesthesia**: The use of anaesthesia during childbirth to relieve pain.
- **Teetotalism**: The practice of complete abstinence from alcoholic drinks.
- **Vegetarianism**: A diet that excludes meat, and sometimes other animal products.

## Debates and Open questions
- The precise impact of removing the Broad Street pump handle is debated; Snow himself observed the epidemic was already in rapid decline due to population flight before the handle was removed.
- The extent to which Snow's work was immediately accepted versus gradually vindicated over time is a point of historical discussion, particularly regarding the resistance from contemporaries like William Farr.
- The role of Thomas Shapter, who conducted similar point-based mapping studies of cholera in Exeter seven years before Snow, is noted as a parallel but less influential effort.

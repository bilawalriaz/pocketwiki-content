# Richard I of England

## Overview
Richard I of England (1157–1199), known as Richard the Lionheart, was a medieval king famed for his military prowess and leadership during the Third Crusade. His reign was defined by continental warfare, crusading, and a lengthy captivity, making him an iconic but complex figure whose legacy as a warrior-king often overshadowed his duties as a ruler of England.

## Timeline
- **8 September 1157** — Born in Oxford, England, to Henry II and Eleanor of Aquitaine.
- **1172** — Formally recognized as Duke of Aquitaine and Count of Poitou.
- **1173–1174** — Leads a rebellion against his father, Henry II, which fails.
- **4 July 1189** — Defeats Henry II's army; succeeds him as King of England.
- **3 September 1189** — Crowned King of England at Westminster Abbey.
- **September 1190** — Arrives in Sicily with Philip II of France for the Third Crusade.
- **May–June 1191** — Conquers Cyprus en route to the Holy Land.
- **12 May 1191** — Marries Berengaria of Navarre in Cyprus.
- **7 September 1191** — Wins the Battle of Arsuf against Saladin's forces.
- **2 September 1192** — Signs a truce with Saladin, ending the crusade without retaking Jerusalem.
- **December 1192** — Captured near Vienna by Leopold of Austria.
- **4 February 1194** — Released after a ransom of 150,000 marks is paid.
- **11 March 1194** — Recrowned in England to nullify the shame of his captivity.
- **6 April 1199** — Dies from a crossbow wound at Châlus-Chabrol, France.

## Body

### Early Life and Accession
Richard was the third of five sons of Henry II and Eleanor of Aquitaine, making him an unlikely heir to the English throne. He spent his childhood in England but became Duke of Aquitaine in 1172. In 1173, he joined his brothers in a failed rebellion against their father. After reconciliation, he spent years pacifying his duchy, earning his "Lionheart" nickname for fierce leadership. He became heir after his brother Henry's death in 1183. In 1189, allied with Philip II of France, he defeated Henry II, who died shortly after. Richard was crowned king in September 1189.

### The Third Crusade
Richard spent most of his reign preparing for and fighting in the Third Crusade, which he had vowed to join in 1187. To fund it, he sold offices and lands, raising vast sums. He departed in 1190, first stopping in Sicily, where he intervened in a succession dispute and captured Messina. In 1191, he conquered Cyprus, which he later sold, before marrying Berengaria of Navarre. In the Holy Land, he supported Guy of Lusignan's claim to the Kingdom of Jerusalem, captured Acre, and won the Battle of Arsuf. However, he failed to take Jerusalem, twice retreating due to strategic concerns and internal dissent. He signed a three-year truce with Saladin in September 1192, allowing Christian pilgrims access to Jerusalem.

### Captivity and Later Reign
Returning from the crusade, Richard was shipwrecked and captured in December 1192 by Leopold of Austria, who resented him. He was later handed to Holy Roman Emperor Henry VI, who demanded a ransom of 150,000 marks. Released in February 1194 after the ransom was paid, Richard returned to England, forgave his brother John for rebelling, and was recrowned. He then spent his final years waging war against Philip II of France to reclaim lost Angevin territories. He built the innovative Château Gaillard in Normandy and won several victories, including at Fréteval in 1194. He died in 1199 from a crossbow wound while suppressing a revolt.

### Character and Legacy
Contemporaries saw Richard as the epitome of chivalry—a valiant, generous, and pious warrior-king. However, he was also criticized for greed, pride, and cruelty, such as the execution of Muslim prisoners at Acre. He spent as little as six months in England, viewing it primarily as a source of revenue. Modern historians debate this "Little England" view, noting his wider continental responsibilities. He was a patron of troubadours and a poet himself. His heraldic shield of three lions became a lasting symbol of England. Legends, like his minstrel Blondel finding him in captivity, and his association with Robin Hood, cemented his romantic image.

## Terms
- **Angevin Empire**: The collection of territories ruled by the Plantagenet dynasty, including England, Normandy, and Aquitaine.
- **Third Crusade (1189–1192)**: A military campaign by European Christian leaders to recapture Jerusalem from Saladin after its fall in 1187.
- **Saladin**: Sultan of Egypt and Syria, founder of the Ayyubid dynasty, and Richard's primary Muslim adversary during the Crusade.
- **Château Gaillard**: An innovative castle built by Richard in Normandy (1196–1198) to defend against Philip II of France.
- **Scutage**: A feudal tax paid by a vassal in lieu of military service.
- **Arnaldia**: A disease similar to scurvy that afflicted Richard during the Crusade.
- **Troubadours**: Poet-musicians from southern France who composed and performed lyric poetry, often about chivalry and courtly love.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Richard's Sexuality**: Historians are divided on whether Richard was homosexual. Evidence includes his childless marriage, public confessions, and contemporary rumors, but he also had an illegitimate son and reports of relations with women.
- **The "Little England" View**: Was Richard a negligent king who exploited England, or a competent ruler managing a vast continental empire? Modern scholars increasingly argue the latter, calling the earlier view anachronistic.
- **Role in Conrad's Murder**: Was Richard involved in the assassination of Conrad of Montferrat in 1192? Contemporaries suspected him, but it was never proven.
- **His Deathbed Mercy**: Did Richard truly forgive the crossbowman who killed him, or was the story a later romantic embellishment?

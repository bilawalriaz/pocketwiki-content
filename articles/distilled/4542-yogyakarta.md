# Yogyakarta

## Overview
Yogyakarta is a city in south-central Java, Indonesia, and the capital of the Special Region of Yogyakarta. It is the only Indonesian city still ruled by a monarchy, the Sultanate of Yogyakarta, making it a vital center for classical Javanese culture and a major educational hub. Its strategic importance was cemented when it served as the capital of the Indonesian Republic during the National Revolution (1946–1948).

## Timeline
- **732 CE** — The Canggal inscription identifies the Mataram area as the capital of the Medang Kingdom.
- **c. 929 CE** — The Mataram Kingdom's capital moves from Central to East Java, possibly due to a Mount Merapi eruption.
- **1587–1613** — Kotagede (now a Yogyakarta district) is the capital of the Mataram Sultanate.
- **13 February 1755** — The Treaty of Giyanti ends a civil war, establishing the Yogyakarta Sultanate under Sultan Hamengkubuwono I.
- **20 June 1812** — British forces under Stamford Raffles capture and sack the Yogyakarta Kraton (palace).
- **17 August 1945** — Sultan Hamengkubuwono IX pledges support for the newly proclaimed Indonesian Republic.
- **1946–1948** — Yogyakarta becomes the capital of the Indonesian Republic during the National Revolution.
- **1 March 1949** — The General Offensive results in a strategic Indonesian victory and the recapture of Yogyakarta from the Dutch.
- **19 April 1930** — The Football Association of Indonesia (PSSI) is founded in Yogyakarta.

## Body

### History and Political Status
The area was the heartland of the ancient Mataram Kingdom (8th–10th century), known for constructing temples like Borobudur and Prambanan. The modern Yogyakarta Sultanate was created in 1755 from a civil war within the later Mataram Sultanate, formalized by the Treaty of Giyanti with the Dutch East India Company. The city's support was crucial for Indonesian independence; Sultan Hamengkubuwono IX's immediate backing of the 1945 proclamation led to Yogyakarta being granted special autonomous status within the Republic. It served as the national capital from 1946 to 1948 after Jakarta fell to the Dutch.

### Geography and Climate
The city covers 32.82 km² and is centered on the Kraton (Sultan's palace). Its urban core features colonial-era buildings and the commercial street Jalan Malioboro. The city lies on the southern slopes of Mount Merapi, an active stratovolcano that is Indonesia's most active. Yogyakarta has a tropical monsoon climate (Köppen: Am), with a wet season from October to May and a dry season from June to September. The annual average temperature is about 26–27°C.

### Demographics and Culture
As of the 2020 census, the city population was 373,589. It is a multicultural center with Islam as the majority religion (83.81%), followed by Christianity (15.8%). Yogyakarta is renowned for classical Javanese arts, including batik, wayang puppetry, and silversmithing. It is also the birthplace of Muhammadiyah, one of Indonesia's largest Islamic organizations, founded in 1912.

### Economy and Infrastructure
The economy is dominated by the tertiary sector (services), contributing about 78% of the Gross Regional Domestic Product. Key sectors include education, tourism, and trade. The city is a major transportation hub, served by Yogyakarta International Airport and two main railway stations (Yogyakarta and Lempuyangan) on Java's main east-west line. Public transport includes the Trans Jogja bus rapid transit system.

### Education
Yogyakarta is Indonesia's premier educational center, home to Gadjah Mada University, the country's largest and one of its most prestigious universities. The city hosts numerous other public and private universities, creating a large student population.

## Terms
- **Kraton**: The royal palace and seat of the Sultan of Yogyakarta.
- **Sultanate**: A state ruled by a sultan; the Yogyakarta Sultanate is a hereditary monarchy within Indonesia.
- **Mataram Sultanate**: A historical Javanese kingdom that preceded the Yogyakarta and Surakarta sultanates.
- **Treaty of Giyanti (1755)**: The agreement that ended a Mataram civil war and established the Yogyakarta Sultanate.
- **Special Region (Daerah Istimewa)**: An administrative division in Indonesia with a degree of autonomy, granted to Yogyakarta due to its role in independence.
- **Mount Merapi**: An active stratovolcano on the border of Central Java and Yogyakarta, influencing the region's history and geography.
- **Trans Jogja**: The bus rapid transit system serving Yogyakarta.
- **Gadjah Mada University**: Indonesia's largest and most prominent public university, located in Yogyakarta.

## Debates and Open questions
The exact cause of the Mataram Kingdom's capital move from Central to East Java around 929 CE is uncertain, with theories pointing to either a severe eruption of Mount Merapi or a power struggle with the Srivijaya kingdom.

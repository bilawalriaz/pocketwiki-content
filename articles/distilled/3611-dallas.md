# Dallas

## Overview
Dallas is a major city in North Texas and the ninth-most populous city in the United States. It serves as the economic and cultural core of the Dallas–Fort Worth metroplex, the fourth-largest metropolitan area in the U.S. The city's growth was driven by its strategic location as a transportation hub for railroads and highways, later becoming a center for finance, technology, and diverse industries.

## Timeline
- **1841** — John Neely Bryan establishes a permanent settlement named Dallas.
- **1846** — Dallas County is established after Texas is annexed by the United States.
- **1856** — Dallas is formally incorporated as a city.
- **1873** — Construction of multiple rail lines through Dallas spurs major growth.
- **1909** — The 15-story Praetorian Building, an early skyscraper, is completed.
- **1930** — Oil is discovered near Kilgore, making Dallas the financial center for the Texas oil industry.
- **1963** — President John F. Kennedy is assassinated in Dealey Plaza.
- **1974** — Dallas/Fort Worth International Airport opens.
- **1996** — Dallas Area Rapid Transit (DART) begins operating Texas's first light rail system.
- **2011** — The Dallas Mavericks win the NBA Championship.
- **2016** — A shooting at a protest in Downtown Dallas kills five police officers.

## Body

### History and Development
The area was originally inhabited by tribes including the Caddo and Comanche. Spanish and later Mexican claims preceded the establishment of the Republic of Texas in 1836. John Neely Bryan founded Dallas in 1841, and it was incorporated in 1856. The arrival of railroads in the 1870s transformed Dallas into a major trading center for cotton and cattle. The discovery of oil in East Texas in 1930 cemented its role as a financial hub. During World War II, the city was a significant manufacturing center for military vehicles and aircraft. The 1980s saw a real estate boom followed by a bust, but the city's economy diversified and recovered.

### Geography and Climate
Dallas is located in North Texas on the Trinity River, with a mostly flat topography. The city covers approximately 386 square miles (1,000 km²). It has a humid subtropical climate with hot summers, mild winters, and is prone to severe weather, including tornadoes, due to its location in Tornado Alley. The average annual rainfall is about 39.1 inches (993 mm).

### Demographics
As of the 2020 census, Dallas had a population of 1,304,379. The city is ethnically diverse, with Hispanic or Latino Americans forming the largest ethnic group (41.2% in 2019 estimates). The non-Hispanic White population has declined to less than one-third of the city's population. The Dallas area is a major destination for immigrants, including significant Hispanic, African American, Asian, and Russian-speaking communities.

### Economy
Dallas's economy is highly diversified, with major sectors including defense, financial services, information technology, telecommunications, and transportation. The Dallas–Fort Worth metroplex hosts 23 Fortune 500 companies, with 11 located within Dallas city limits. Key companies headquartered in the area include AT&T, Texas Instruments, and Southwest Airlines. The city is also a major center for retail and tourism.

### Arts and Culture
The Dallas Arts District is the largest contiguous urban arts district in the United States, housing venues like the Dallas Museum of Art and the Meyerson Symphony Center. The city has a vibrant music and arts scene, with historic neighborhoods like Deep Ellum and Bishop Arts District. Major cultural events include the annual State Fair of Texas. Dallas is also known for its barbecue and Tex-Mex cuisine.

### Government and Politics
Dallas operates under a council-manager government. While the city is a Democratic stronghold in presidential elections, it elected a Republican mayor in 2023 after the incumbent switched parties. The city's budget for the 2017–2018 fiscal year was $3.3 billion.

### Education
The Dallas Independent School District is the 12th-largest in the U.S. The city and its metro area are home to numerous colleges and universities, including Southern Methodist University, the University of Texas at Dallas, and the UT Southwestern Medical Center, which has employed six Nobel laureates.

### Infrastructure and Transportation
Dallas is a major transportation hub. Dallas Fort Worth International Airport is the second-largest airport in the U.S. by area and one of the busiest. Dallas Love Field is a major hub for Southwest Airlines. The city is served by a network of interstate highways and the DART light rail system, the largest light rail operator in the U.S.

## Terms
- **Metroplex:** A large metropolitan area, specifically referring to the Dallas–Fort Worth region.
- **Council-manager government:** A system where an elected council sets policy and a hired city manager administers it.
- **Humid subtropical climate:** A climate characterized by hot, humid summers and mild winters.
- **Fortune 500:** An annual list of the 500 largest U.S. corporations by revenue.
- **Light rail:** A form of urban rail transit using lighter rail vehicles and lower capacity than heavy rail.
- **Tornado Alley:** A colloquial term for an area of the U.S. with a high frequency of tornadoes.
- **Silicon Prairie:** A nickname for the technology industry cluster in the Dallas–Fort Worth area.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The origin of the city's name is disputed. The official marker cites Vice President George M. Dallas, but other theories point to his brother or a village in Scotland.
- The city's political alignment is in flux, highlighted by the 2023 party switch of its mayor, despite consistent Democratic voting in major elections.
- The economic impact of the 2014 oil price drop was mitigated by Dallas's diversified economy, but the long-term effects of such volatility on the region are a subject of ongoing analysis.

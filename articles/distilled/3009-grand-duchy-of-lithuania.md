# Grand Duchy of Lithuania

## Overview
The Grand Duchy of Lithuania was a major European state that existed from the 13th to the late 18th century. It began as a pagan Baltic tribal union, expanded to become the largest state in Europe by the 15th century, and was a multi-ethnic, multi-confessional realm. Its history is defined by its struggle against the Teutonic Knights, its union with Poland, and its eventual dissolution in the Partitions of Poland.

## Timeline
- **1253** — Mindaugas crowned as the Catholic King of Lithuania.
- **1386** — Union of Krewo: Jogaila converts to Christianity and establishes a dynastic union with Poland.
- **1410** — Battle of Grunwald: Decisive Polish-Lithuanian victory over the Teutonic Order.
- **1569** — Union of Lublin creates the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth.
- **1791** — Constitution of 3 May attempts to transform the Commonwealth into a unitary state.
- **1795** — Third Partition of Poland–Lithuania: The Grand Duchy's territory is fully annexed by Russia, Prussia, and Austria.

## Body

### Establishment and Early Conflicts
The state consolidated from pagan Baltic tribes in Aukštaitija in the late 13th century. Mindaugas, the first ruler, was crowned king in 1253 after a temporary conversion to Christianity, but later reverted to paganism and was assassinated in 1263. The state survived constant warfare with the Teutonic and Livonian Orders, who conducted a religious crusade against it.

### Expansion and the Gediminid Dynasty
Under the Gediminid dynasty, starting with Vytenis and peaking under Gediminas, Lithuania expanded rapidly eastward. It annexed or vassalized large parts of the former Kievan Rus', including Kiev by 1321. This expansion was possible partly because Mongol control over the Rus' lands was weak. The state became a multi-ethnic empire, with Ruthenian (ancestors of Belarusians and Ukrainians) becoming a primary language of administration.

### Christianization and Union with Poland
In 1386, Grand Duke Jogaila signed the Union of Krewo, converting to Christianity and marrying the Polish queen, creating a dynastic union. This ended the pagan era and began centuries of close ties with Poland. The state reached its greatest territorial extent under Jogaila's cousin, Vytautas the Great, who ruled as Grand Duke from 1401 to 1430. His reign culminated in the victory at the Battle of Grunwald in 1410, which ended the major military threat from the Teutonic Order.

### The Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth
Wars with the Teutonic Order and the rising Grand Duchy of Moscow forced Lithuania into a closer alliance with Poland. The Union of Lublin (1569) formally created the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth, a federation where Lithuania retained separate ministries, laws, and treasury. The Commonwealth was a major European power but suffered from internal political weaknesses. The Constitution of 3 May 1791, a reformist document, briefly attempted to create a unitary state, but the Commonwealth was soon partitioned by its neighbors. The Grand Duchy's territory was fully partitioned in 1795.

### Demographics and Administration
The Grand Duchy was ethnically diverse. While ethnic Lithuanians were the majority in the core lands (Lithuania proper), they were a minority in the vast eastern territories. By the 16th century, the Ruthenian language was widely used in administration, but Polish gradually replaced it, becoming the official chancellery language in 1697. The state's laws were codified in the Statutes of Lithuania, first issued in 1529.

## Terms
- **Gediminid dynasty:** The ruling dynasty of the Grand Duchy of Lithuania for over a century, founded by Gediminas.
- **Teutonic Knights:** A German crusading military order that waged a long war against pagan Lithuania from bases in Prussia.
- **Union of Krewo (1386):** The agreement that established a dynastic union between Lithuania and Poland, leading to Lithuania's Christianization.
- **Union of Lublin (1569):** The treaty that created the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth, a formal federation of the two states.
- **Ruthenian:** The East Slavic language (ancestor of Belarusian and Ukrainian) used as a primary language of administration in the Grand Duchy.
- **Statute of Lithuania:** The codified law of the Grand Duchy, first issued in 1529, with the final version in 1588.
- **Polonization:** The process by which the Lithuanian nobility and administration increasingly adopted Polish language and culture, especially after the Union of Lublin.

## Debates and open questions
The article notes a long-running historical debate among Lithuanian, Belarusian, and Ukrainian historians about the essential character of the state: whether it was fundamentally a Lithuanian empire or a Ruthenian-Lithuanian state where Ruthenian culture played a central role. The state's multi-ethnic nature and the use of Ruthenian in administration are key points in this discussion.

# Las Vegas

## Overview
Las Vegas is Nevada's most populous city and a global resort destination famed for gambling, entertainment, and nightlife. Its economy is driven by tourism, gaming, and conventions, making it a major regional financial and cultural center. Its rapid growth and unique identity, often called "Sin City," make it a key case study in urban development and resource management.

## Timeline
- **1829** — Mexican trader Antonio Armijo's party notes the area's springs; the name "Las Vegas" (the meadows) is applied.
- **1905** — Las Vegas is founded when 110 acres are auctioned adjacent to Union Pacific Railroad tracks.
- **1911** — Las Vegas is officially incorporated as a city.
- **1931** — Nevada legalizes casino gambling and shortens divorce residency to six weeks; Hoover Dam construction begins.
- **1941** — Las Vegas Army Airfield is established (later Nellis Air Force Base).
- **1955** — The Moulin Rouge Hotel opens as the first racially integrated casino-hotel.
- **1989** — The Mirage opens, starting a trend of major resort development on the Las Vegas Strip.
- **1995** — The Fremont Street Experience opens in downtown Las Vegas.
- **2012** — "The Year of Downtown" sees the debut of major revitalization projects like The Smith Center.
- **2017** — The Vegas Golden Knights (NHL) begin play as an expansion team.
- **2020** — The Las Vegas Raiders (NFL) relocate from Oakland; the Circa Resort & Casino opens on Fremont Street.
- **2023** — The Las Vegas Grand Prix (Formula One) is held on a temporary street circuit.

## Body

### History and Development
Settled in 1905 and incorporated in 1911, Las Vegas saw pivotal growth in 1931 when Nevada legalized casino gambling and Hoover Dam construction began, attracting workers and preventing economic depression. Post-World War II, it became synonymous with lavish hotels. The 1989 opening of The Mirage catalyzed massive Strip development, located in unincorporated Paradise and Winchester. Downtown revitalization, including the 1995 Fremont Street Experience and 2012 "Year of Downtown" projects, aimed to draw tourists back to the city's core.

### Geography and Climate
Las Vegas sits in a Mojave Desert basin, surrounded by mountains. It has a subtropical hot desert climate with long, extremely hot summers and short, mild winters, making it one of North America's sunniest and driest cities. July averages a high of 104.5 °F (40.3 °C). The highest recorded temperature is 120 °F (48.9 °C) on July 7, 2024. Water conservation is critical; daily per-resident consumption fell from 314 US gallons in 2003 to about 205 in 2015.

### Demographics and Culture
The 2020 census recorded a population of 644,883, with a racial composition of 49.2% white, 11.9% black, and 34.1% Hispanic or Latino. The city has a significant Hawaiian population, earning it the nickname "ninth island of Hawaii." Cultural institutions include the Neon Museum and The Mob Museum, while the Downtown Arts District hosts events like the "First Friday" festival. Its tolerance for adult entertainment contributes to the "Sin City" moniker.

### Economy and Tourism
Tourism, gaming, and conventions are the primary economic drivers. As of 2023, Las Vegas attracts over 40.8 million visitors annually. The Las Vegas Strip is the industry's center, while downtown casinos along Fremont Street include historic venues like the Golden Gate Hotel (opened 1906). Diversification efforts have attracted companies like Zappos.com (headquartered downtown since 2013) and Allegiant Air. Growth strains water resources, with the Southern Nevada Water Authority undertaking major projects to secure supply from Lake Mead.

### Sports and Transportation
Las Vegas hosts major professional teams: the Vegas Golden Knights (NHL), Las Vegas Raiders (NFL), and Las Vegas Aces (WNBA). It also hosts prominent combat sports and the Las Vegas Grand Prix. Transportation is car-dependent, with 77.1% of commuters driving alone in 2016. Public transit includes the RTC bus system and the Las Vegas Monorail. Harry Reid International Airport is the primary air gateway. Brightline West, a high-speed rail project, began construction in 2024 to connect Las Vegas to Los Angeles.

## Terms
*   **Xeriscaping:** Landscaping designed to reduce or eliminate irrigation, promoted due to water scarcity.
*   **Subtropical hot desert climate (BWh):** A classification for regions with very hot summers, mild winters, and low precipitation, typical of the Mojave Desert.
*   **Council–manager government:** A system where an elected council sets policy and a professional manager administers operations.
*   **Joint Operating Agreement:** A legal arrangement allowing two newspapers to share business operations while maintaining separate editorial control, as between the *Review-Journal* and *Las Vegas Sun*.
*   **R1 research university:** A Carnegie Classification for universities with very high research activity, like UNLV.
*   **Land-grant university:** A university designated to receive benefits from the Morrill Acts, focused on practical education, like UNLV.
*   **Class I railroad:** The largest freight railroad classification in North America by revenue; Union Pacific is the sole provider in Las Vegas.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Water Sustainability:** Long-term growth viability is debated due to arid location and dependence on shrinking Lake Mead. The Southern Nevada Water Authority's proposed $3.2 billion pipeline across Nevada is controversial.
*   **Economic Diversification:** Efforts to attract tech, health, and commercial industries continue, but the economy remains heavily reliant on tourism and gaming, making it vulnerable to downturns.
*   **Urban Revitalization:** The success and impact of downtown projects like the Fremont Street Experience and the Tony Hsieh-funded Downtown Project are ongoing subjects of analysis regarding their effect on tourism and local communities.

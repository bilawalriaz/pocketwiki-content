# Irtysh

## Overview
The Irtysh is a major river flowing through China, Kazakhstan, and Russia. It is the chief tributary of the Ob River and is the longest tributary river in the world. The river is vital for transportation, hydroelectric power, and irrigation across the three nations it traverses.

## Timeline
- **657** — Tang dynasty general Su Dingfang defeats the Western Turkic Khaganate at the Battle of Irtysh River.
- **1208** — Battle of Irtysh River between the Mongol Empire and an alliance of Merkit and Naimans.
- **1580s** — Russians conquer the Tatar Khanate of Sibir, which controlled the lower and middle Irtysh.
- **1587** — Russians found Tobolsk at the confluence of the Tobol and Irtysh.
- **1716-1752** — Russians found Omsk, Semipalatinsk, Ust-Kamenogorsk, and Petropavlovsk along the river during confrontation with the Dzungar Khanate.
- **1750s** — Chinese Qing Empire conquers Dzungaria, becoming Russia's southern neighbor on the upper Irtysh.
- **1860** — The Convention of Peking establishes the border between the Russian and Qing empires in the Irtysh basin.
- **1864** — The Protocol of Chuguchak draws the actual border line, leaving Lake Zaysan on the Russian side.
- **1881** — The Treaty of Saint Petersburg slightly readjusts the border in Russia's favor.
- **1962-1974** — The Irtysh–Karaganda Canal is built to supply water to the Kazakh steppes.
- **1987** — A canal is constructed in China to divert Irtysh water to Lake Ulungur.
- **2004** — The last downstream bridge on the Irtysh opens at Khanty-Mansiysk.

## Body

### Etymology
The river's name appears in 7th-8th century Turkic epitaphs. Early interpretations include the Turkic word *ertishmak* ("quick jump") from philologist Mahmud al-Kashgari (1029–1101), and a legend where a slave said "irtysh" ("stop") to Turks. Other theories derive the name from a Tatar Khan, from Turkic words meaning "digging the earth," or from a pre-Ket root meaning "stormy river."

### Geography and Hydrology
The Irtysh originates as the Kara-Irtysh (Black Irtysh) in the Mongolian Altai mountains in Xinjiang, China. It flows northwest through Lake Zaysan in Kazakhstan and merges with the Ob near Khanty-Mansiysk in Russia after 4,248 km (2,640 mi). Its main tributaries include the Tobol, Demyanka, and Ishim. The Ob-Irtysh system forms a major drainage basin covering most of Western Siberia and the Altai Mountains.

### Economic Use
The river is navigable for tankers, passenger ships, and cargo vessels between April and October. Omsk is the largest river port in Western Siberia. Kazakhstan operates three major hydroelectric plants on the river, including at Ust-Kamenogorsk, which has the world's deepest lock (42 m drop). China has also constructed several dams. Significant water diversion projects include the Irtysh–Karaganda Canal in Kazakhstan and the Irtysh–Karamay–Urümqi Canal in China. Increased Chinese water use has raised environmental concerns, with reports indicating only about two-thirds of the river's natural flow reaches Kazakhstan.

### History and Borderlands
The river banks were occupied by Mongol and Turkic peoples for centuries. After the Russian conquest of the Khanate of Sibir in the 1580s, Russia expanded eastward. In the 17th century, the Dzungar Khanate controlled the upper Irtysh, leading to Russian fortification. The border between the Russian and Qing empires was formally established by the Convention of Peking (1860) and subsequent protocols, leaving Lake Zaysan in Russian territory. The Qing military presence collapsed during the Dungan Revolt (1862–77), and the border was further adjusted by the Treaty of Saint Petersburg (1881).

## Terms
- **Tributary**: A river that flows into a larger river.
- **Drainage basin**: An area of land where all water drains into a common outlet, like a river or lake.
- **Endorheic**: A closed drainage basin that retains water and allows no outflow to other external bodies of water.
- **Khanate**: A Mongol or Turkic state or territory ruled by a khan.
- **Taiga**: A biome characterized by coniferous forests.
- **Steppe**: A large area of flat grassland without trees.
- **Amban**: A high-ranking official in the Qing dynasty government.
- **Dungan Revolt**: A mainly ethnic and religious war fought in 19th-century western China.

## Debates and Open questions
The etymology of the river's name is debated, with multiple competing theories from different linguistic and historical sources. The environmental impact of increased water diversion in China on the river's flow to Kazakhstan remains a point of concern and potential conflict.

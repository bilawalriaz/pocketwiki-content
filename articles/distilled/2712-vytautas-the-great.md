# Vytautas the Great

## Overview
Vytautas the Great (c. 1350–1430) was the Grand Duke of Lithuania who transformed the Grand Duchy into a major European power. His reign was defined by a relentless struggle for power, strategic alliances and wars with the Teutonic Order, and the expansion of Lithuanian influence eastward. He is a revered national hero in Lithuania, symbolizing its medieval golden age and statehood.

## Timeline
- **1377** — Death of Grand Duke Algirdas; succession crisis begins between his son Jogaila and brother Kęstutis.
- **1381** — Kęstutis seizes Vilnius and imprisons Jogaila after discovering the secret Treaty of Dovydiškės.
- **1382** — Kęstutis dies in captivity; Vytautas escapes and allies with the Teutonic Order.
- **1385** — Union of Krewo: Jogaila marries Jadwiga of Poland, becoming King Władysław II Jagiełło.
- **1392** — Astrava Treaty: Vytautas becomes regent of Lithuania, breaking with the Teutonic Order.
- **1398** — Battle of the Vorskla River: Crushing defeat for Vytautas's crusade against the Golden Horde.
- **1401** — Union of Vilnius and Radom: Vytautas is formally recognized as Grand Duke for life.
- **1410** — Battle of Grunwald: Decisive Polish-Lithuanian victory over the Teutonic Order.
- **1413** — Union of Horodło: Establishes a formal political union between Polish and Lithuanian nobility.
- **1422** — Treaty of Lake Melno: Samogitia is returned to Lithuania in perpetuity.
- **1430** — Vytautas dies in Trakai, ending negotiations for his coronation as King of Lithuania.

## Body

### Struggle for Power
Vytautas's early life was a series of shifting alliances in a dynastic conflict. After his father Kęstutis died in 1382, Vytautas repeatedly allied with the Teutonic Order against his cousin and rival, Grand Duke Jogaila, seeking the throne for himself. This struggle ended in 1392 with the Astrava Treaty, where Jogaila made Vytautas regent of Lithuania. Vytautas recovered his family's lands and gained de facto power, though he ruled in Jogaila's name. His formal recognition as Grand Duke for life came in 1401.

### Rule and Eastern Policy
As Grand Duke, Vytautas pursued an aggressive eastern policy to control Ruthenian lands. He allied with the exiled Khan Tokhtamysh, briefly extending Lithuanian territory from the Baltic to the Black Sea in 1398. This expansion ended with a catastrophic defeat at the Battle of the Vorskla River in 1399. The loss forced a re-evaluation of Lithuania's relationship with Poland and led to the Union of Vilnius and Radom in 1401, which granted Vytautas autonomy but subordinated Lithuania to Poland after his death.

### Wars with the Teutonic Order
The central conflict of Vytautas's reign was with the Teutonic Knights over the region of Samogitia, which separated their Prussian and Livonian territories. After several treaties and rebellions, the conflict culminated in the Battle of Grunwald in 1410, where Vytautas commanded the Lithuanian forces in a decisive victory. The subsequent Treaty of Thorn (1411) granted him Samogitia for life, but disputes continued until the Treaty of Lake Melno (1422) permanently returned Samogitia to Lithuania, establishing a stable border for centuries.

### Relationship with Poland and Reforms
Vytautas's relationship with Poland evolved from conflict to formal union. The Union of Horodło (1413) created a political partnership, granting Lithuanian Christian nobles equal rights with the Polish *szlachta* (nobility) and establishing joint parliamentary sessions. Domestically, Vytautas centralized power by replacing local princes with loyal governors, forming a new nobility. He also settled Karaim and Tatar communities for military and administrative roles. In 1429, a plan to crown him King of Lithuania sparked a major crisis with Poland, which was unresolved at his death.

## Terms
- **Grand Duchy of Lithuania**: A vast medieval state that was, at its height, the largest in Europe.
- **Teutonic Order**: A German Catholic military order that waged a long crusade against pagan Lithuania.
- **Samogitia**: A region of Lithuania crucial to the Teutonic Order as it connected their separate territories.
- **Union of Krewo (1385)**: The agreement that united Poland and Lithuania through the marriage of Jogaila and Jadwiga.
- **Union of Horodło (1413)**: A political union that formalized cooperation between the Polish and Lithuanian nobility.
- **Szlachta**: The privileged noble class of Poland and Lithuania.
- **Gediminids**: The ruling dynasty of Lithuania to which Vytautas belonged.
- **Battle of Grunwald (1410)**: A pivotal battle where Polish-Lithuanian forces defeated the Teutonic Order.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The exact cause of Kęstutis's death in 1382 is debated; it is unclear whether he died of natural causes or was murdered.
- The precise motivations and terms of Vytautas's frequent alliances and breakaways with the Teutonic Order are complex, reflecting a pragmatic struggle for power rather than consistent ideology.
- The failure to crown Vytautas as King of Lithuania in 1430, due to Polish opposition, highlights the enduring tension within the Polish-Lithuanian union over sovereignty and status.

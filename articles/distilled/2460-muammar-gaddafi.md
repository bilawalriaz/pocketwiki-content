# Muammar Gaddafi

## Overview
Muammar Gaddafi ruled Libya for over four decades, transforming it from a monarchy into a unique state he called a Jamahiriya ("state of the masses"). His rule was marked by radical domestic reforms funded by oil wealth, an erratic and often confrontational foreign policy, and the establishment of a pervasive cult of personality. His regime was overthrown and he was killed during the 2011 Libyan Civil War, part of the broader Arab Spring.

## Timeline
- **1 September 1969** — Gaddafi's Free Officers movement overthrows King Idris in a bloodless coup.
- **1973** — Gaddafi initiates the "Popular Revolution" and outlines his Third International Theory.
- **2 March 1977** — The Libyan Arab Republic is dissolved and replaced by the Great Socialist People's Libyan Arab Jamahiriya.
- **15 April 1986** — The United States bombs Libya in Operation El Dorado Canyon.
- **21 December 1988** — Pan Am Flight 103 is destroyed over Lockerbie, Scotland; Libya is later held responsible.
- **1992** — The UN imposes economic sanctions on Libya for refusing to hand over Lockerbie bombing suspects.
- **1999** — Libya begins secret talks with the UK and shifts focus from pan-Arabism to pan-Africanism.
- **December 2003** — Libya renounces its weapons of mass destruction programs, improving relations with the West.
- **17 February 2011** — Major protests against Gaddafi's government begin, escalating into civil war.
- **20 October 2011** — Gaddafi is captured and killed by rebel forces in Sirte.

## Body

### Rise to Power and Early Rule (1969–1977)
Gaddafi, an Arab nationalist inspired by Egypt's Gamal Abdel Nasser, founded the Free Officers movement while in the military. On 1 September 1969, they deposed the Western-backed monarchy of King Idris in a bloodless coup. Gaddafi became chairman of the Revolutionary Command Council (RCC), ruling by decree. He expelled Italian settlers and Western military bases, nationalized the oil industry, and used revenues for social programs in housing, healthcare, and education. In 1973, he launched a "Popular Revolution" to create a system of direct democracy through Basic People's Congresses, though he retained ultimate control.

### The Jamahiriya and Ideology (1977–1986)
In 1977, Gaddafi formally restructured Libya into the Jamahiriya, a system based on his "Third International Theory" outlined in *The Green Book*. This theory rejected both Western capitalism and Soviet communism. While officially a direct democracy, power was concentrated in Gaddafi and the Revolutionary Committees, which enforced ideological conformity. His foreign policy was marked by support for various militant groups worldwide, conflicts with neighbors like Egypt and Chad, and increasing isolation due to alleged involvement in terrorist attacks, including the 1988 Lockerbie bombing. This led to UN sanctions and a 1986 U.S. bombing.

### Isolation, Reforms, and Re-engagement (1987–2010)
The late 1980s saw economic liberalization and a partial curbing of the Revolutionary Committees' power. After the 1991 collapse of the Soviet Union and facing continued sanctions, Gaddafi gradually shifted his foreign policy. He abandoned pan-Arabism for pan-Africanism, becoming a champion of African unity and chairperson of the African Union (2009–2010). From 1999, he pursued rapprochement with the West, renouncing weapons of mass destruction in 2003 and paying compensation for the Lockerbie bombing. Domestically, he allowed some privatization while maintaining state control over key sectors like oil.

### Civil War and Death (2011)
Inspired by the Arab Spring, protests against corruption and unemployment erupted in February 2011. Gaddafi's violent crackdown led to a civil war, with the National Transitional Council (NTC) forming in Benghazi. NATO intervened militarily to protect civilians. After months of fighting, rebel forces captured Tripoli in August. Gaddafi retreated to his hometown of Sirte, where he was captured and killed by NTC militants on 20 October 2011.

## Terms
- **Jamahiriya**: "State of the masses," the name Gaddafi gave to Libya's system of direct democracy from 1977.
- **Third International Theory**: Gaddafi's political ideology, outlined in *The Green Book*, rejecting both Western capitalism and Soviet communism.
- **The Green Book**: A three-volume work by Gaddafi (1975–1979) detailing his political and economic theories.
- **Revolutionary Command Council (RCC)**: The military junta that ruled Libya after the 1969 coup.
- **Basic People's Congresses (BPCs)**: Local assemblies intended to be the foundation of the Jamahiriya's direct democracy.
- **Revolutionary Committees**: Zealous groups established to enforce ideological purity and suppress dissent within the Jamahiriya.
- **Pan-Arabism**: The ideology advocating political unity among Arab peoples, which Gaddafi promoted early in his rule.
- **Pan-Africanism**: The ideology promoting solidarity and unity among African peoples, which Gaddafi adopted later in his rule.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The extent to which Libya under Gaddafi achieved a true "socialist revolution" versus maintaining a state capitalist or authoritarian system is debated by scholars.
- The precise role and knowledge of Gaddafi's government in terrorist attacks like the Lockerbie bombing remains contested, despite official Libyan payments and convictions.
- The impact of Western military intervention in 2011 is debated; some argue it was decisive for the rebels' victory, while others view it as an imperialist overreach.

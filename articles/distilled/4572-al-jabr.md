# Al-Jabr

## Overview
*Al-Jabr* (full title: *The Compendious Book on Calculation by Completion and Balancing*) is a foundational Arabic mathematical treatise written by Al-Khwarizmi in Baghdad around 820 CE. It is the first text to teach algebra as an independent discipline, establishing its core methods and giving the field its name. Its systematic approach to solving equations and its later translation into Latin made it the principal mathematical textbook in European universities until the 16th century.

## Timeline
- **c. 820 CE** — Al-Khwarizmi writes *Al-Jabr* in Baghdad.
- **1145 CE** — Robert of Chester produces the first Latin translation.
- **12th–16th c.** — The Latin translation becomes the standard algebra textbook in European universities.

## Body

### Core Concept and Legacy
*Al-Jabr* established algebra as a distinct mathematical discipline, separate from geometry and arithmetic. It introduced the fundamental operations of "reduction" (*al-jabr*) and "balancing" (*al-muqābala*). Reduction involves transposing a subtracted term to the other side of an equation (e.g., transforming *x² = 40x − 4x²* into *5x² = 40x*). Balancing involves subtracting the same positive quantity from both sides (e.g., transforming *x² + 5 = 40x + 4x²* into *5 = 40x + 3x²*). These operations allowed for the systematic simplification of equations and the elimination of negative quantities.

### Method for Quadratic Equations
The book provides a complete method for solving positive roots of polynomial equations up to the second degree. It classifies quadratic equations into six basic types, described verbally in terms of "squares" (*x²*), "roots" (*x*), and "numbers" (constants). This classification excluded equations with negative coefficients, as Islamic mathematicians did not work with negative numbers. Al-Khwarizmi provided both algebraic and geometric methods for solving these types.

### Other Mathematical and Practical Content
Beyond algebra, the second chapter covers methods for calculating area and volume, including three approximations for pi (π): 3 1/7, √10, and 62832/20000 (3.1416). The book also explicates the Jewish calendar's 19-year cycle and dedicates about half its content to solving complex problems in Islamic inheritance law, which required first-order algebraic equations.

## Terms
- **Al-Jabr**: The operation of "restoring" or "forcing," meaning the transposition of a subtracted term to the other side of an equation to eliminate negative quantities.
- **Al-Muqābala**: The operation of "balancing," meaning the subtraction of the same positive quantity from both sides of an equation to simplify it.
- **Quadratic Equation**: A polynomial equation of the second degree, which Al-Khwarizmi classified into six types based on positive coefficients.
- **Rhetorical Algebra**: A style of algebra where equations and operations are described entirely in words, without symbolic notation.
- **Reduction**: The process of simplifying an equation by applying the *al-jabr* and *al-muqābala* operations.

## Debates and Open Questions
The source text does not detail specific scholarly debates about the work itself, but it notes that several other authors later published texts under the same title (*Al-Jabr*), indicating its foundational influence and the ongoing development of the discipline it created.

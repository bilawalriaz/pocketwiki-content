# Louis de Broglie

## Overview
Louis de Broglie (1892–1987) was a French theoretical physicist and aristocrat whose 1924 doctoral thesis proposed that all matter, not just light, exhibits wave-like properties. This revolutionary idea, known as the de Broglie hypothesis, is a cornerstone of quantum mechanics and established the concept of wave-particle duality. His work earned him the 1929 Nobel Prize in Physics and profoundly influenced the development of modern physics.

- **19 March 1987** — Dies in Louveciennes, France, at age 94.

## Body

### Early Life and Education
Louis de Broglie was born into a prominent French aristocratic family. Initially trained in history, he switched to science, earning a physics degree in 1913. His studies were interrupted by World War I, during which he served in the army's engineering corps, working on radio communications with his brother Maurice and Léon Brillouin. This technical work delayed his entry into fundamental research.

### The de Broglie Hypothesis and Wave Mechanics
De Broglie's groundbreaking work began with studying X-rays and the photoelectric effect in his brother Maurice's lab. Influenced by Einstein's light quanta and the need to unify wave and particle concepts, he proposed in 1923 that any moving particle has an associated "phase wave." His 1924 thesis formalized this, stating a particle's wavelength (λ) equals Planck's constant (h) divided by its momentum (p): λ = h/p. This idea, initially met with skepticism, was supported by Einstein and experimentally verified in 1927. It provided the foundation for Erwin Schrödinger's wave mechanics.

### Later Work and Pilot-Wave Theory
De Broglie sought a causal, non-probabilistic interpretation of quantum mechanics. At the 1927 Solvay Conference, he presented a "pilot-wave" concept where a real wave guides a particle, but he later abandoned it. The idea was revived and developed by David Bohm in the 1950s, forming the de Broglie–Bohm theory. De Broglie revisited the concept in 1956, incorporating ideas from Bohm and Jean-Pierre Vigier. His later work explored a "hidden thermodynamics" of particles, attempting to unify mechanics and thermodynamics.

### Institutional Roles and Legacy
Beyond research, de Broglie held prestigious positions, including Perpetual Secretary of the French Academy of Sciences. He was instrumental in the proposal for a multinational physics laboratory, which led to the creation of CERN. He received numerous honors, including the Legion of Honour and the Kalinga Prize, and was a prolific author on physics and its philosophy.

## Terms
- **De Broglie Hypothesis**: The theory that all matter has wave-like properties, with a wavelength inversely proportional to its momentum.
- **Wave-Particle Duality**: The concept that quantum entities exhibit both wave and particle characteristics.
- **Matter Wave (de Broglie Wave)**: A wave associated with a moving particle, described by the de Broglie wavelength.
- **Pilot-Wave Theory**: An interpretation of quantum mechanics where a wave guides a particle's motion, offering a deterministic alternative to standard theory.
- **Schrödinger Equation**: The fundamental equation of wave mechanics that describes how the quantum state of a physical system changes over time.
- **Phase Wave**: The wave de Broglie associated with a particle, propagating at a phase velocity of c²/v.
- **Group Velocity**: The velocity at which the overall shape of a wave's amplitude propagates; de Broglie identified this with the particle's velocity.
- **Compton Frequency**: The frequency (E/h) de Broglie associated with a particle's internal periodic process.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The physical reality of the de Broglie wave remains a point of interpretation. Standard quantum mechanics treats the wavefunction as a mathematical tool for calculating probabilities, while pilot-wave theories posit a real, guiding wave.
- De Broglie's conjecture of an internal "clock" for the electron, part of the pilot-wave mechanism, has not been conclusively verified, though some recent data is compatible with it.
- His later, more speculative ideas—such as the non-zero rest mass of the photon and the unification of dynamics and thermodynamics—remain outside the mainstream of physics.

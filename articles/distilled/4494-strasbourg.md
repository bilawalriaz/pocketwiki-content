# Strasbourg

## Overview
Strasbourg is a major city in northeastern France, serving as the prefecture of the Bas-Rhin department and the Grand Est region. It is a de facto capital of the European Union, hosting the European Parliament and the Council of Europe, making it one of the few non-national capitals with such a significant international role. Historically situated on the Franco-German border, it has been a cultural bridge between the two nations for centuries, reflected in its architecture, university, and frequent changes in sovereignty.

## Timeline
- **12 BC** — Roman camp of Argentoratum first mentioned.
- **590** — Name "Strateburgus" first recorded by Gregory of Tours.
- **1262** — Citizens rebel against bishop's rule (Battle of Hausbergen); Strasbourg becomes a free imperial city.
- **1681** — Conquered by Louis XIV, becoming a French city.
- **1871** — Becomes part of Imperial Germany after the Franco-Prussian War.
- **1918** — Reverts to France after World War I.
- **1940-1944** — Under German control during World War II.
- **1949** — Becomes seat of the Council of Europe.
- **1952** — Becomes seat of the European Parliament.
- **1988** — Historic city centre (Grande Île) classified as a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
- **2009** — Three universities merge to form the Université de Strasbourg.
- **2016** — Promoted from capital of Alsace to capital of the new Grand Est region.

## Body

### Identity and European Role
Strasbourg is one of the three de facto capitals of the European Union, alongside Brussels and Luxembourg. It is the official seat of the European Parliament and hosts the Council of Europe, including the European Court of Human Rights. The city is also home to numerous other international bodies, such as the Central Commission for Navigation on the Rhine and the International Institute of Human Rights. This role makes it a global centre for diplomacy, second in France only to Paris for international congresses.

### History and Sovereignty
The city's history is marked by its contested location. Founded as the Roman camp Argentoratum, it was governed by bishops from the 4th to 13th centuries before becoming a free imperial city in 1262. It was annexed by France in 1681, then became German in 1871 after the Franco-Prussian War. It returned to France in 1918, was annexed by Nazi Germany from 1940-1944, and has been French since its liberation in 1944. This history has created a unique Franco-German cultural blend.

### Architecture and Landmarks
The city is renowned for its Gothic Cathedral, featuring a famous astronomical clock, and its medieval timber-framed buildings, especially in the Petite France district. The historic Grande Île is a UNESCO World Heritage Site. The German Neustadt district, built after 1871, showcases grand Wilhelmian architecture. Notable structures include the Palais Rohan, the Ponts Couverts bridges, and the modern European Court of Human Rights building.

### Economy and Transport
Strasbourg is an important manufacturing and engineering hub. Its port on the Rhine is the second-largest river port in France. The city is a major transport node, connected to the French TGV network and served by its own airport. The Strasbourg tramway, opened in 1994, is central to a public transport system that prioritizes pedestrians and cyclists in the city centre.

### Education and Culture
The Université de Strasbourg, formed in 2009 from three older universities, is the second-largest in France and has produced 19 Nobel laureates. The city has a rich cultural life, hosting institutions like the Orchestre philharmonique de Strasbourg and the annual Christkindelsmärik Christmas market, which dates to 1570. The Bibliothèque nationale et universitaire is France's second-largest library.

## Terms
- **Prefecture**: The administrative capital of a French department.
- **Commune**: The lowest level of administrative division in France, equivalent to a municipality.
- **Free imperial city**: A city in the Holy Roman Empire subject only to the authority of the Emperor.
- **Eurodistrict**: A transnational administrative entity created by France and Germany, combining Greater Strasbourg and the Ortenau district.
- **Incunabula**: Books printed in Europe before the year 1501.
- **Filtered permeability**: An urban planning principle that favors pedestrian and bicycle traffic by making certain streets discontinuous for cars while connecting paths for active transport.
- **ZAD (Zone to Defend)**: A term for an area occupied by protesters to block a development project.

## Debates and Open Questions
The source notes that the origin of the city's pre-Germanic name, Argantorati, is debated: it may refer to a fortified enclosure near a gold mining site or to a hoard of gold. The destruction of the city's original library in 1870 is also a point of historical contention, with differing accounts of responsibility between French and German sources.

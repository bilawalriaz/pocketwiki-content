# Russia

## Overview  
Russia, officially the Russian Federation, is the world's largest country by land area, spanning Eastern Europe and northern Asia across eleven time zones. With over 140 million people, it is both the most populous European nation and a global great power, wielding the largest nuclear arsenal and significant military and economic influence. Its history traces from the medieval East Slavic state of Kievan Rus' through imperial expansion, revolutionary upheaval, Soviet dominance, and post-Soviet transformation under Vladimir Putin’s increasingly authoritarian rule.

---

## Timeline  

- **c. 862** — Varangian leader Rurik establishes rule in Novgorod, founding the Rurikid dynasty.
- **988** — Kievan Rus' adopts Orthodox Christianity from Byzantium under Vladimir the Great.
- **1237–1240** — Mongol invasion destroys Kievan Rus'; the Golden Horde begins ruling much of Russia.
- **1480** — Ivan III throws off Mongol suzerainty, asserting independence.
- **1547** — Ivan IV ("the Terrible") crowned first Tsar of All Russia, establishing the Tsardom.
- **1721** — Peter the Great proclaims the Russian Empire after victory in the Great Northern War.
- **1917** — February and October Revolutions lead to the end of the Romanov dynasty and rise of the Bolsheviks.
- **1922** — Formation of the Union of Soviet Socialist Republics (USSR).
- **1991** — Dissolution of the USSR; Russian SFSR becomes the Russian Federation.
- **2000** — Vladimir Putin becomes President, initiating a centralized, authoritarian system.
- **2014** — Russia annexes Crimea following Ukraine's Euromaidan revolution.
- **2022** — Full-scale Russian invasion of Ukraine triggers widespread sanctions and international isolation.

## Body  

### Early History and Kievan Rus'  
Human habitation in Russia dates back to the Lower Paleolithic, with evidence of Homo erectus and later Neanderthals. The East Slavs emerged between the 3rd and 8th centuries AD, forming the basis for Kievan Rus', established in the 9th century. The state flourished under Vladimir the Great (980–1015), who introduced Orthodox Christianity, and his son Yaroslav the Wise (1019–1054), who codified laws and strengthened central authority. However, weak succession mechanisms led to fragmentation into regional principalities. The Mongol invasion of 1237–1240 devastated Kievan Rus', shifting power northeast to Vladimir-Suzdal and Novgorod.

### Rise of Moscow and Imperial Expansion  
Moscow rose to prominence during the late medieval period, especially under Ivan III ("the Great"), who expelled the Mongols and claimed succession to Byzantium after its fall in 1453. Ivan IV became the first tsar in 1547, expanding territory eastward and centralizing power. The Romanov dynasty emerged after the Time of Troubles (1605–1613), ushering in stability and further expansion. Peter the Great transformed Russia into an empire in 1721, defeating Sweden and founding St. Petersburg as a window to Europe. Catherine the Great expanded borders to the Black Sea and Central Asia, solidifying Russia’s status as a major European power.

### Soviet Era  
The 1917 revolutions ended imperial rule, leading to the creation of the USSR in 1922 under Lenin. Joseph Stalin’s rule (1924–1953) brought brutal purges, forced collectivization, and rapid industrialization at enormous human cost. The USSR played a decisive role in WWII, suffering immense casualties but emerging as a superpower. The Cold War era saw intense rivalry with the U.S., marked by nuclear arms races and proxy conflicts. Despite repression, the Soviet period achieved landmark scientific milestones, including the launch of Sputnik 1 (1957) and Yuri Gagarin’s historic spaceflight (1961).

### Post-Soviet Transition and Modern Russia  
The USSR collapsed in 1991, leaving Russia economically devastated and politically unstable. Boris Yeltsin implemented shock therapy reforms, leading to hyperinflation and the rise of oligarchs. A constitutional crisis in 1993 solidified presidential authority. Vladimir Putin assumed leadership in 2000, restoring stability but consolidating power through controlled elections, media suppression, and crackdowns on dissent. His regime has pursued aggressive foreign policy, including wars in Georgia (2008) and Ukraine (2014, 2022), drawing condemnation and sanctions. Domestically, Putinism is characterized by nationalism, statism, and suppression of civil liberties.

## Terms  

- **Kievan Rus'**: Medieval East Slavic state centered on Kiev, foundational to Russian identity.  
- **Tsardom of Russia**: Centralized Russian state (1547–1721) under the rule of the tsars.  
- **Third Rome**: Concept that Moscow succeeded Constantinople as the center of Orthodox Christianity.  
- **Soviet**: Democratic council or committee; term used for worker and peasant councils during the revolution.  
- **Perestroika**: Policy of economic restructuring introduced by Mikhail Gorbachev in the 1980s.  
- **Glasnost**: Policy of political openness and transparency promoted by Gorbachev.  
- **Putinism**: Political system under Vladimir Putin marked by authoritarianism, nationalism, and state control.  
- **Great Game**: 19th-century rivalry between the Russian and British Empires in Central Asia.  
- **Shock Therapy**: Rapid market liberalization policies implemented in post-Soviet Russia.  
- **Tandemocracy**: Period (2008–2012) when Putin served as PM while Medvedev was president to circumvent term limits.

## Debates and Open Questions  

- **Origins of Rus'**: Scholars debate whether the East Slavs or Scandinavian Varangians played the dominant role in founding Kievan Rus'.  
- **Impact of Mongol Rule**: Some argue Mongol domination had minimal cultural impact, while others see it as shaping Russian political centralization.  
- **Stalin’s Legacy**: Views differ sharply on whether his industrialization justified the massive loss of life.  
- **Nature of Putinism**: Analysts disagree on whether Russia constitutes a full dictatorship, competitive authoritarian regime, or hybrid system.  
- **Future of Russian Democracy**: Whether democratic institutions can recover amid ongoing repression and war remains uncertain.  
- **Ethnic Identity vs. Civic Nationalism**: Tensions persist between ethnic Russian identity and the multiethnic character of the Russian state.

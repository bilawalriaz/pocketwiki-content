# Vinson Massif

## Overview
Vinson Massif is a large mountain massif in Antarctica's Sentinel Range, home to Mount Vinson, the highest peak on the continent at 4,892 meters (16,050 ft). It is a significant geographical and mountaineering landmark, representing one of the "Seven Summits" and a major objective for polar climbers.

## Body
### Geography and Geology
Vinson Massif is a 21 km long and 13 km wide massif within the Sentinel Range of the Ellsworth Mountains. It is composed of steeply inclined strata known as the Crashsite Group, which consists of about 3,000 meters of mostly marine sandstones and argillites. The highest point, Mount Vinson, is formed by the erosion-resistant beds of the Mount Wyatt Earp Formation, which contains Devonian fossils. The massif's current official height of 4,892 m was established by a 2004 GPS survey.

### Climate and Glaciers
The climate is controlled by the polar ice cap's high-pressure system, generally creating stable conditions but with possibilities of high winds and snowfall. Annual snowfall is low, but high winds can cause significant accumulation at base camp. Limited snowfall compacts over time to form glaciers that flow down the mountain's valleys, such as the Branscomb, Crosswell, and Roché Glaciers.

### History and First Ascent
The massif was first seen in 1958 and named in 1961. The first ascent of Mount Vinson was achieved in December 1966 by the American Antarctic Mountaineering Expedition (AAME), led by Nicholas Clinch. The expedition, sponsored by the American Alpine Club and the National Geographic Society, saw all ten of its members reach the summit over three days. In 2001, a NOVA-sponsored team completed the first ascent from the eastern side and took the first ground-based GPS reading from the summit.

### Later Ascents
As one of the Seven Summits, Mount Vinson attracts well-funded climbers, with guided expeditions costing around US$45,000. Notable later ascents include the fastest ascent by skyrunner Fernanda Maciel in 2012 and the first ascent via a new route by an Italian team in 2008 after a 270 km overland ski traverse.

## Terms
- **Massif:** A compact group of mountains.
- **Sentinel Range:** The highest mountain range in Antarctica, part of the Ellsworth Mountains.
- **Topographic isolation:** The distance to the nearest point of equal or higher elevation.
- **Crashsite Group:** The geological formation of steeply inclined strata that forms Vinson Massif.
- **Syncline:** A fold in rocks where the strata slope downward from either side towards the axis.
- **Seven Summits:** The highest mountains on each of the seven continents.
- **US-ACAN:** The U.S. Board on Geographic Names' Advisory Committee on Antarctic Names.

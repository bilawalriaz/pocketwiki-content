# Fez, Morocco

## Overview
Fez is a major city in northern Morocco and the capital of the Fez-Meknes region. Founded in the 8th century CE, it is a historic center of Moroccan political power, Islamic scholarship, and craftsmanship. Its ancient medina, a UNESCO World Heritage Site, is one of the world's largest car-free urban areas and home to the University of al-Qarawiyyin, the oldest continuously operating institution of higher education.

## Body

### Foundation and Early History
Fez was founded in 789 by Idris I, an Idrisid ruler, as Madinat Fas. His son, Idris II, established a second settlement, al-ʿĀliyá, in 809. The city's early Arab character was shaped by immigration, notably from Kairouan (Tunisia) and al-Andalus (Spain/Portugal) in the early 9th century. After the Idrisid dynasty's decline, the city was contested by the Umayyad Caliphate of Córdoba and the Fatimid Caliphate, before being ruled by the Zenata tribe allied with Córdoba from the late 10th century.

### Unification and Golden Age
In 1070, the Almoravid Sultan Yusuf ibn Tashfin unified the two rival settlements, destroying the dividing walls and building new ones to create Fes el-Bali (Old Fez). Under the Marinid dynasty (13th–15th centuries), Fez reached its zenith as the political capital. The Marinids constructed numerous madrasas and mosques, establishing the city's reputation for religious scholarship. In 1276, they founded the adjacent royal district of Fes Jdid, where the Royal Palace is still located.

### Later Dynasties and Colonial Era
After the Marinids, Fez competed with Marrakesh for influence. It became the capital again under the 'Alawi dynasty in the 17th century. The French colonial administration, established in 1912, moved the capital to Rabat and built a new European-style city (Ville Nouvelle) outside the old medina. This policy preserved the historic medinas but created a social and urban divide.

### Modern City and Demographics
Today, Fez consists of the historic medinas (Fes el-Bali and Fes Jdid) and the larger, modern Ville Nouvelle. The 2024 census recorded a population of 1.256 million. The predominant language is Moroccan Arabic (Darija), with a distinctive Fessi dialect historically associated with the city's elite. The city's economy relies on tourism, traditional crafts, and agriculture from the surrounding Saïss plain.

## Terms
*   **Medina:** The historic, walled core of a North African city, typically characterized by narrow streets and traditional architecture.
*   **Fes el-Bali:** The oldest part of Fez, formed by the unification of the original two settlements in 1070.
*   **Fes Jdid:** The "New Fez," a royal and administrative district founded by the Marinids in 1276.
*   **Ville Nouvelle:** The modern city district built outside the historic medinas during the French colonial era.
*   **Madrasa:** An Islamic educational institution, often featuring ornate architecture.
*   **Zawiya:** A Sufi shrine and religious complex, often containing a mosque, school, and tomb.
*   **Mellah:** The historic Jewish quarter of a Moroccan city.
*   **Kasbah:** A fortified citadel or military structure within a city.
*   **Funduq:** A traditional urban inn or caravanserai for merchants and travelers.
*   **Hammam:** A traditional public bathhouse.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   The exact etymology of the name "Fez" is debated, with theories linking it to a pickaxe, an ancient city name, or a local region.
*   The precise date and circumstances of the formation of the Mellah (Jewish quarter) in Fes Jdid are not firmly established, though many scholars date it to the 15th century.
*   The impact of French colonial "policy of association" is debated; scholar Janet Abu-Lughod argued it created a form of urban "apartheid" between stagnant indigenous areas and expanding European cities.

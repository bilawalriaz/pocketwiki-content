# Cook Islands

## Overview
The Cook Islands is a self-governing island country in free association with New Zealand, consisting of 15 islands in the South Pacific. It is a major tourism destination with a unique political status, where its citizens hold New Zealand citizenship but New Zealanders are not automatically Cook Islands nationals. The nation has developed an independent foreign policy and economy while maintaining a complex constitutional relationship with New Zealand.

## Timeline
- **c. AD 1000** — First settled by Polynesian migrants from Tahiti.
- **1595** — First European sighting by Spanish navigator Álvaro de Mendaña de Neira.
- **1773 & 1777** — Visits by British explorer Captain James Cook.
- **1821** — Arrival of English missionaries; Christianity spreads rapidly.
- **1888** — Becomes a British protectorate following a petition from local leaders.
- **1900** — Islands are ceded to Britain and annexed by New Zealand in 1901.
- **1948** — Cook Islanders automatically gain New Zealand citizenship.
- **1965** — A constitution is promulgated, granting self-government in free association with New Zealand.
- **2017** — Marae Moana, the world's largest protected marine area by size, is established.
- **2023** — Legislation is passed legalising homosexuality.

## Body

### Political Status and Foreign Relations
The Cook Islands is a representative democracy in "free association" with New Zealand. Under its constitution, New Zealand cannot pass laws for the Cook Islands. While the head of state is the King of New Zealand, represented locally by the King's Representative, the country conducts its own independent foreign and defence policy. As of 2018, it had diplomatic relations with 52 countries. It is not a UN member state but has its "full treaty-making capacity" recognised and is a member of several UN specialised agencies like WHO and UNESCO. Defence is shared with New Zealand, which can provide naval and air support, but the Cook Islands Police Service is responsible for policing its vast 1.96 million km² Exclusive Economic Zone (EEZ), using a Guardian-class patrol boat provided by Australia.

### Economy
Tourism is the economic base, accounting for approximately 67.5% of GDP. The economy is also supported by offshore banking, particularly asset protection trusts established under the International Trusts Act of 1989, which are known for shielding assets from foreign creditors. Other sectors include pearl farming and marine exports. In 2019, the Sea Bed Minerals Act was passed to manage seabed minerals in the EEZ, and exploration licences have been granted. The economy is aided by foreign aid, primarily from New Zealand and China.

### Geography and Demographics
The 15 islands are divided into two groups: the southern volcanic islands and the northern coral atolls. The total land area is about 236.7 km². The 2021 census recorded a population of 14,987, with the majority (10,863) living on the main island of Rarotonga. A much larger diaspora exists in New Zealand (80,532 in the 2018 census) and Australia (28,000). The official languages are English and Cook Islands Māori, with Pukapukan also spoken. The population is predominantly Christian.

### Culture
Traditional arts include woodcarving, with distinct styles on islands like Mangaia, and the weaving of fine rito hats from coconut palm fibre. A major communal art form is *tivaevae*, handmade patchwork quilts introduced by missionaries. Contemporary art is vibrant, with internationally recognised artists. Rugby league is the most popular sport. The national flower is the tiare māori.

## Terms
- **Free association**: A relationship where a smaller state conducts its own affairs with the consent of a larger state, which retains responsibility for defence and external affairs.
- **Exclusive Economic Zone (EEZ)**: A maritime zone extending 200 nautical miles from a coast, over which a state has special rights to explore and use marine resources.
- **Vaka**: A traditional Polynesian voyaging canoe; also used to refer to traditional districts or councils.
- **Tivaevae**: A traditional Cook Islands art form of elaborate, handmade patchwork quilts.
- **Rito**: The uncurled, immature fibre of the coconut palm, used to weave high-quality hats.
- **House of Ariki**: A de facto upper house of the Cook Islands parliament, composed of high chiefs.
- **Marae Moana**: The Cook Islands' large-scale marine protected area, established in 2017.
- **Asset protection trust**: A legal trust designed to shield assets from creditors and legal claims in other jurisdictions.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Constitutional status and sovereignty**: There is ongoing discussion about the Cook Islands' relationship with New Zealand. New Zealand officials have stated that the Cook Islands is "not a fully independent and sovereign state" and that any change to its status or the introduction of Cook Islands passports would require a referendum.
- **Name change**: In 2019, plans to change the country's name to reflect its Polynesian nature were poorly received by the diaspora. A compromise was reached to adopt a new Cook Islands Māori name while keeping the English name, with discussions continuing as of 2020.
- **UN membership**: Prime Minister Mark Brown has stated the Cook Islands does not currently meet the requirements for UN membership, and any decision would require a referendum and re-evaluation of the relationship with New Zealand.
- **Commonwealth membership**: Efforts to join the Commonwealth of Nations as a full member were "ongoing" as of 2024, but the government was unable to secure an invitation to the 2024 Commonwealth Heads of Government Meeting.

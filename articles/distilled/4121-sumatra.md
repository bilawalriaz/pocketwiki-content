# Sumatra

## Overview
Sumatra is the sixth-largest island in the world and the largest island fully within Indonesian territory. It is a major geographical, ecological, and historical region of Indonesia, known for its rich biodiversity, complex ethnic and linguistic landscape, and a history marked by powerful sultanates, colonial conflict, and significant natural disasters.

## Timeline
- **c. 74,000 years ago** — Supervolcanic eruption at Lake Toba occurs, temporarily changing Earth's climate.
- **692 CE** — The Melayu Kingdom is absorbed by the Srivijaya empire.
- **1025** — Srivijaya's influence decreases after a defeat by the Chola Empire.
- **13th century** — Islam begins spreading in Sumatra; the Samudra kingdom's king converts.
- **16th century** — Aceh becomes a major pepper trade center and the Aceh Sultanate rises.
- **1797 & 1833** — Major earthquakes (8.9 and 9.2 magnitude) and tsunamis strike western Sumatra.
- **1873–1903** — The Dutch Empire fights the long and costly Aceh War.
- **1942** — Japan invades Sumatra during World War II.
- **2004** — A massive Indian Ocean earthquake and tsunami devastate Aceh, killing over 170,000 Indonesians.
- **2005** — The Aceh Insurgency ends after a peace agreement.

## Body

### Etymology and Historical Kingdoms
Sumatra was known in ancient Sanskrit texts as *Svarṇadvīpa* ("Island of Gold"). The name "Sumatra" first appeared in 1017. Arab traders called it *Ramni*. The form "Sumatra" gained popularity in the 14th century due to the rise of the Samudera Pasai and Aceh Sultanates. Historically, the island was dominated by the Srivijaya empire, which declined after the 11th century. Islam arrived via traders in the 6th-7th centuries and became established by the 13th century. The Aceh Sultanate, a major pepper trading power, reached its golden age under Iskandar Muda in the 16th-17th centuries, rivaling European colonial powers.

### Colonial Era and Modern History
European powers, notably the Dutch, gradually controlled Sumatran states, though Aceh resisted until the costly Aceh War (1873–1903). Japan occupied the island during World War II. After Indonesian independence, Aceh experienced a long insurgency (1976–2005) that ended with a peace agreement. The island has been severely impacted by natural disasters, including the 1883 Krakatoa eruption and the 2004 Indian Ocean tsunami.

### Geography and Climate
Sumatra is an elongated island spanning a northwest-southeast axis, crossed by the equator. Its western spine is the Bukit Barisan mountain range, containing active volcanoes. The eastern side consists of vast, swampy lowlands and plains. The island is highly seismically active due to the Great Sumatran fault and the Sunda megathrust. Its climate is tropical, hot, and humid. Major rivers include the Musi, Asahan, and Siak.

### Demographics and Culture
As of mid-2025, Sumatra's population is estimated at about 62.3 million. The largest indigenous ethnic groups are Malays, Minangkabaus, Bataks, Acehnese, and Lampungs. Over 52 languages are spoken, all part of the Malayo-Polynesian branch of the Austronesian family, except Chinese and Tamil. Indonesian is the official language, with regional Malay variants also common. The majority religion is Islam (87.12%).

### Ecology and Environment
Sumatra hosts exceptional biodiversity, including 17 endemic plant genera and unique species like the Rafflesia flower. It is home to critically endangered fauna such as the Sumatran tiger, rhinoceros, elephant, and orangutan. However, the island has lost almost 50% of its tropical rainforest since 1980 due to deforestation, which has caused severe seasonal haze affecting neighboring countries. This environmental destruction is often described as ecocide.

## Terms
- **Sunda Islands**: The archipelago in western Indonesia that includes Sumatra, Java, and Borneo.
- **Strait of Malacca**: The narrow waterway between Sumatra and the Malay Peninsula, a major shipping route.
- **Sunda Strait**: The strait separating Sumatra from Java.
- **Bukit Barisan**: The mountain range forming the western backbone of Sumatra.
- **Srivijaya**: A powerful maritime empire based in Sumatra that dominated from the 7th to 13th centuries.
- **Aceh Sultanate**: A major Islamic kingdom in northern Sumatra, prominent in the 16th-17th centuries.
- **Giling Basah**: A wet-hulling coffee processing technique used in Sumatra.
- **Ecocide**: The widespread destruction of an environment, a term used by academics to describe deforestation in Sumatra.

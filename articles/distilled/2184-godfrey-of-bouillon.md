# Godfrey of Bouillon

## Overview
Godfrey of Bouillon (c. 1060–1100) was a preeminent leader of the First Crusade and the first ruler of the Kingdom of Jerusalem. He is a pivotal figure in medieval history, symbolizing the crusading ideal, though his actual role was part of a collective leadership. His short reign established the Christian kingdom in the Holy Land, setting the stage for centuries of conflict.

## Timeline
- **c. 1060** — Born, second son of Eustace II, Count of Boulogne.
- **1076** — Inherits Lordship of Bouillon after his uncle's death.
- **1087** — Confirmed as Duke of Lower Lorraine by Emperor Henry IV.
- **1096** — Joins the First Crusade with his brothers.
- **1097** — Participates in the sieges of Nicaea and Antioch.
- **15 July 1099** — Plays a key role in the capture of Jerusalem.
- **22 July 1099** — Becomes ruler of Jerusalem as Advocate of the Holy Sepulchre.
- **August 1099** — Defeats the Fatimids at the Battle of Ascalon.
- **18 July 1100** — Dies in Jerusalem; succeeded by his brother Baldwin.

## Body

### Early Life and Inheritance
Godfrey was born c. 1060 to Eustace II of Boulogne and Ida of Lorraine. In 1076, he inherited the Lordship of Bouillon from his uncle, Godfrey the Hunchback, but his claim to the larger Duchy of Lower Lorraine was contested. Emperor Henry IV initially bypassed him, appointing his infant son Conrad. Godfrey spent years in conflict with rivals like Albert III of Namur and Matilda of Tuscany. He proved a loyal vassal to Henry IV, fighting for him in the Investiture Controversy, and was finally granted Lower Lorraine in 1087.

### The First Crusade
In 1096, Godfrey sold or mortgaged his estates to fund a Crusader army. He and his brothers, Eustace and Baldwin, joined the mass movement to the Holy Land. After reaching Constantinople, Godfrey and other leaders swore a modified oath to Byzantine Emperor Alexios I Komnenos to restore some lands. Godfrey fought at the sieges of Nicaea (1097) and Antioch (1097–1098). After Antioch, he joined the march on Jerusalem, which was captured in July 1099.

### Rule in Jerusalem
Following the capture of Jerusalem, a council was held. Raymond IV of Toulouse declined the crown, and Godfrey accepted leadership, taking the title *Advocatus Sancti Sepulchri* (Advocate of the Holy Sepulchre) rather than king. He secured the new kingdom by defeating a Fatimid army at Ascalon in August 1099. His brief reign involved defending against external threats and internal power struggles, notably with Dagobert of Pisa, the Latin Patriarch. He died in July 1100, likely from disease, and was succeeded by his brother Baldwin, who became the first titled king.

## Terms
*   **Advocatus Sancti Sepulchri:** Latin for "Advocate of the Holy Sepulchre," the title Godfrey used to rule Jerusalem, signifying a protector rather than a monarch.
*   **First Crusade:** The military expedition (1096–1099) called by Pope Urban II to recapture Jerusalem and the Holy Land from Muslim rule.
*   **Investiture Controversy:** A conflict between the papacy and Holy Roman Emperors over the appointment of church officials, in which Godfrey supported Emperor Henry IV.
*   **Fatimids:** A Shia Muslim caliphate based in Egypt that controlled Jerusalem before the Crusaders captured it in 1099.
*   **Seljuk Empire:** A Sunni Muslim empire that had taken Jerusalem from the Fatimids in 1073, prompting the call for the First Crusade.
*   **Assizes of Jerusalem:** The legal code of the Kingdom of Jerusalem, later attributed to Godfrey but actually developed gradually after his death.
*   **Nine Worthies:** A medieval literary and artistic concept of nine ideal warriors, to which Godfrey was later added.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Godfrey's Title:** Chroniclers disagree on whether Godfrey used the title *princeps* (prince), *dux* (duke), or *rex* (king). The meaning and practical use of his chosen title, Advocate, are debated.
*   **Role in Jerusalem's Governance:** The exact nature of Godfrey's conflict with Patriarch Dagobert of Pisa is unclear. Some sources suggest Dagobert tried to make Jerusalem a papal fief, while others indicate he swore an oath to accept Godfrey's successor.
*   **Cause of Death:** Muslim sources claim Godfrey was killed by an arrow at Acre, but Christian chronicles state he died of illness (possibly typhoid) in Jerusalem. The arrow story is considered unlikely.

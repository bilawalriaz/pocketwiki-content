# Zenobia

## Overview
Septimia Zenobia (c. 240–c. 274) was the third-century queen of the Palmyrene Empire in Syria. After her husband King Odaenathus was assassinated in 267, she became regent for her young son Vaballathus and de facto ruler. She expanded her realm to control most of the Roman East, including Egypt, before being defeated and captured by the Roman Emperor Aurelian in 272. Her brief, powerful reign and dramatic fall have made her a legendary figure and a patriotic symbol in Syria.

## Timeline

- **272** — Emperor Aurelian defeats Palmyrene forces at the battles of Immae and Emesa.
- **272** — Aurelian besieges Palmyra; Zenobia is captured while fleeing to Persia.
- **274** — Zenobia is paraded in Aurelian's triumph in Rome; her subsequent fate is uncertain.

## Body

### Origin and Early Life
Zenobia's ancestry is debated. She bore the Palmyrene Aramaic name Bat-Zabbai ("daughter of Zabbai") and the Roman gentilicium Septimia. Inscriptions name her father as Antiochus, possibly a distant ancestor, while other scholars suggest her father was named Zabbai. Legends, including those in the unreliable *Historia Augusta*, claimed she descended from Cleopatra and the Ptolemies—a connection likely made for political legitimacy regarding Egypt. She married Odaenathus, the ruler of Palmyra, around 255.

### Regent and Expansion
After Odaenathus's assassination in 267, Zenobia secured a smooth transition of power for her son Vaballathus, who inherited his father's royal titles. She initially maintained Palmyra's subordinate relationship with Rome. However, exploiting the Roman Empire's weakness during the Crisis of the Third Century, she launched military campaigns. In 270, her general Zabdas conquered Arabia Petraea and Syria. Later that year, Palmyrene forces invaded and annexed Egypt, a major source of grain for Rome. By 271, her empire extended from central Anatolia to Upper Egypt.

### Governance and Culture
Zenobia ruled a multicultural, multiethnic empire. She projected an image as a Syrian monarch, a Hellenistic queen, and a Roman empress to gain broad support. Her court in Antioch became a center of learning, attracting philosophers like Longinus. She was tolerant of religious minorities, including Christians and Jews, though her own beliefs are debated—some sources claim she was Jewish or a Manichaean sympathizer, but these are likely later fabrications. She maintained Roman administrative forms but appointed her own governors.

### Rebellion and Downfall
In early 272, Zenobia and Vaballathus dropped the pretense of subordination, assuming the imperial titles of Augusta and Augustus and removing Emperor Aurelian's image from coinage. This open usurpation prompted Aurelian's eastern campaign. He swiftly reconquered Asia Minor and Egypt. In May 272, Aurelian defeated the Palmyrene army at the Battle of Immae near Antioch and again at Emesa. Zenobia retreated to Palmyra, which was besieged. While attempting to flee to Persia for aid, she was captured before crossing the Euphrates. Palmyra surrendered.

### Captivity and Legacy
Zenobia was taken to Emesa for trial and later to Rome. She was publicly humiliated in Aurelian's triumph in 274. Ancient sources conflict on her fate: some say she died before reaching Rome, others that she was executed, and the *Historia Augusta* claims she was given a villa in Tibur and married a Roman nobleman. Her legacy is immense; she is a national symbol in Syria and has inspired countless works of art, literature, and opera, often romanticized as a heroic, tragic figure.

## Terms
- **Regent**: A person appointed to administer a state because the monarch is a minor, absent, or incapacitated.
- **Palmyrene Empire**: A third-century breakaway state centered on the city of Palmyra in Syria, which briefly controlled much of the Roman East.
- **Sassanid Empire**: The last Persian imperial dynasty before the Muslim conquest, a major rival of Rome.
- **Gentilicium**: A hereditary Roman surname indicating family or clan membership.
- **Augusta/Augustus**: The titles for Roman empress and emperor, respectively.
- **Corrector totius orientis**: A title meaning "governor of the entire East," held by Odaenathus.
- **Antoninianus**: A Roman silver coin introduced in the 3rd century.
- **Paideia**: The Greek concept of education and training, forming the ideal citizen.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Zenobia's Parentage**: Her father's identity is uncertain, with candidates including Julius Aurelius Zenobius, Antiochus, or Zabbai.
- **Role in Odaenathus's Assassination**: Some modern scholars speculate she was involved, but ancient sources do not accuse her directly, and she continued his policies.
- **Extent of Early Authority**: Historians debate whether her power was initially limited to Palmyra and Emesa until 270 or if she controlled all her husband's territories from the start.
- **Agreement with Rome**: It is unclear if Aurelian tacitly accepted Palmyrene rule in the East for a time, as suggested by joint coinage and papyri dating, or if this was a Roman ruse.
- **Zenobia's Fate**: Ancient accounts disagree on whether she died before reaching Rome, was executed, or lived out her days in Italy.

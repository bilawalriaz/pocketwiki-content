# Mohenjo-daro

## Overview
Mohenjo-daro was one of the largest and most advanced cities of the ancient Indus Valley Civilisation (also known as the Harappan Civilisation), built around 2500 BCE in modern-day Pakistan. It represents a pinnacle of early urban planning, with sophisticated water management and grid-based architecture, and its study provides crucial insights into one of the world's earliest major urban societies. The city was abandoned around 1700 BCE and its ruins were rediscovered in the 1920s.

## Timeline
- **c. 3000 BCE** — Indus Valley Civilisation develops from prehistoric Indus culture.
- **c. 2500 BCE** — Mohenjo-daro is built and prospers as a major urban center.
- **c. 1700 BCE** — Mohenjo-daro is abandoned as the Indus Civilisation declines.
- **1919–1920** — R. D. Banerji identifies the site's antiquity.
- **1924–1926** — First large-scale excavations led by Kashinath Dikshit and John Marshall.
- **1930s** — Major excavations conducted under Marshall, Dikshitar, and Mackay.
- **1945** — Excavations by Mortimer Wheeler, Ahmad Dani, and F. A. Khan.
- **1964–1965** — Last major excavations by George Dales.
- **1980** — Site designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
- **2012** — Archaeologists warn the site could disappear by 2030 without improved conservation.

## Body

### Historical Context and Rediscovery
Mohenjo-daro was a major urban center of the Indus Valley Civilisation, which at its height spanned much of modern Pakistan and North India. The city's ruins lay undocumented for about 3,700 years until rediscovered in the 1920s. Excavations revealed a highly organized society, but the site has been threatened by weathering since major digging was banned in 1965. Recent surveys suggest the city is larger than the currently excavated area.

### Urban Planning and Architecture
The city featured a planned grid layout with fired brick buildings, divided into a raised "Citadel" and a "Lower City." It showcased advanced civil engineering, including a centralized drainage system with covered street drains and over 700 wells—one for roughly every three houses. Major structures included the Great Bath (a large waterproofed pool possibly for ritual purification), a "Great Granary" (whose function is debated), and large assembly halls. The city's fortifications were relatively light, suggesting it was an administrative center.

### Society, Economy, and Artifacts
Artifacts indicate a complex society with specialized crafts, including metalworking (using the lost-wax technique), shell-working, and standardized weights and measures. Notable finds include the "Dancing Girl" bronze statuette, the "Priest-King" soapstone figure, and seals like the Pashupati seal, which may depict a proto-Shiva figure. The undeciphered Indus script appears on many objects. The city's decline is linked to environmental factors, with theories pointing to repeated flooding, overuse of land, or a combination of stressors leading to abandonment.

### Conservation and Current Threats
Designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1980, Mohenjo-daro faces severe threats from groundwater salinity, erosion, and improper past restoration. Conservation efforts have been intermittent due to funding issues. The site was further endangered in 2014 by plans to hold a festival there, which experts warned could cause irreversible damage.

## Terms
- **Indus Valley Civilisation (Harappan Civilisation):** A Bronze Age civilisation (c. 3000–1300 BCE) in the northwestern regions of South Asia, known for its urban centers.
- **Citadel:** A raised, fortified area within an ancient city, often housing public or elite structures.
- **Hypocaust:** An ancient underfloor heating system, as found in some Mohenjo-daro buildings.
- **Bitumen:** A viscous, waterproofing substance used to line the Great Bath.
- **Lost-wax technique:** A method of metal casting using a wax model that is melted away.
- **Indus script:** The undeciphered writing system used by the Indus Valley Civilisation.
- **Angula:** The base unit of length (about 17 mm) used in the Indus measurement system.
- **UNESCO World Heritage Site:** A location designated as having special cultural or physical significance, warranting protection.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The function of the "Great Granary" is debated; some scholars argue it was a "Great Hall" due to a lack of grain evidence.
- The cause of the city's abandonment is not settled. Theories include catastrophic flooding, gradual environmental degradation from overuse, or a combination of factors.
- The nature of political or administrative control in Indus cities like Mohenjo-daro remains unclear, despite evidence of standardized layouts and weights.
- The meaning of the Indus script and the identity of figures on seals (e.g., the Pashupati seal) are unknown.

# New Imperialism

## Overview
New Imperialism refers to the period of intense colonial expansion by Western powers and Japan from the late 19th to early 20th centuries. It was driven by economic competition, nationalist rivalries, and a "civilizing mission" ideology, resulting in the near-total conquest of Africa and significant gains in Asia. This era reshaped global power dynamics and set the stage for later decolonization.

## Timeline
- **1884–1885** — Berlin Conference defines "effective occupation" for African claims.
- **1882** — Britain occupies Egypt, securing the Suez Canal.
- **1885** — Belgian King Leopold II establishes the Congo Free State.
- **1898** — Spanish-American War; U.S. acquires Philippines, Puerto Rico, Guam.
- **1898** — Fashoda Incident: Anglo-French crisis over Sudan.
- **1904–1905** — Russo-Japanese War; Japan emerges as an imperial power.
- **1908** — Belgian government annexes Congo Free State due to atrocities.
- **1910** — Japan annexes the Korean Empire.

## Body

### Rise and Motivations
The era followed the decline of the first European imperial wave and mercantilism. Britain initially dominated as the "workshop of the world," but its hegemony eroded after the Franco-Prussian War (1871) and during the Long Depression (1873–1896), which pressured states to abandon free trade and seek new markets. Competition was formalized at the Berlin Conference (1884–1885), which set rules for the "Scramble for Africa" without African representation. Motivations included economic desire for resources and markets, geopolitical rivalry, and a paternalistic "civilizing mission" (French: *mission civilisatrice*), often justified by Social Darwinism.

### Socioeconomic Implications
Imperialism served domestic political and economic needs. Elites used "social imperialism" (a term coined by historian Hans-Ulrich Wehler) to divert internal tensions and preserve the status quo by promoting jingoism. Mercantilist policies restricted colonies to trade with their metropole, though later shifted toward coerced free trade. The expansion created brutal client states abroad and fostered racism and xenophobia at home.

### Regional Expansion
**South Asia:** The British East India Company consolidated control over India, culminating in direct Crown rule after the 1857 Rebellion. Britain reoriented India's economy to supply raw materials (e.g., cotton, tea) and serve as a market for British textiles, while building infrastructure like railroads and telegraphs.

**Southeast Asia:** Britain expanded into Burma and Malaya. France conquered Indochina (Vietnam, Cambodia, Laos). The U.S. acquired the Philippines after the Spanish-American War. The Dutch East Indies (Indonesia) were governed through indirect rule, manipulating existing indigenous hierarchies.

**Africa:** By 1914, nearly all of Africa was colonized. Britain's holdings included Egypt, Sudan, and South Africa. The Congo Free State, personally owned by King Leopold II, became infamous for its brutal rubber extraction regime, causing millions of deaths before being annexed by Belgium in 1908.

**Oceania:** France, the U.S., Britain, Germany, and others partitioned the Pacific islands. Chile annexed Easter Island in 1888.

### Imperial Rivalries
Colonial competition intensified great power tensions. France's seizure of Tunisia (1881) and Britain's occupation of Egypt (1882) caused diplomatic friction. The Fashoda Incident (1898) nearly led to war between Britain and France. German challenges to French influence in Morocco (Tangier Crisis 1905, Agadir Crisis 1911) heightened pre-WWI tensions. These rivalries contributed to the formation of alliances like the Triple Entente.

## Terms
- **New Imperialism:** The late 19th/early 20th century wave of colonial expansion by industrialized powers.
- **Effective Occupation:** Criterion from the Berlin Conference requiring actual administrative control to claim territory.
- **Civilizing Mission:** Ideology justifying imperialism as a duty to bring Western culture and Christianity to "uncivilized" peoples.
- **Social Darwinism:** Pseudo-scientific theory applying "survival of the fittest" to human societies and races.
- **Social Imperialism:** Use of foreign expansion to divert domestic social and political tensions.
- **Mercantilism:** Economic system where colonies exist to enrich the mother country through controlled trade.
- **Great Game:** Strategic rivalry between the British and Russian Empires for influence in Central Asia.
- **Appeasement:** British 1930s policy of making concessions to aggressive powers to avoid conflict.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Motivations:** Was imperialism driven primarily by economic interests (as argued by Lenin and Hobson), geopolitical rivalry, or sincere (if misguided) humanitarianism?
- **Economic Impact:** Did colonialism provide net economic benefits to the colonizers, or did the costs of administration and military often exceed financial returns?
- **Theories:** How well do theories like "accumulation theory" or "World-Systems theory" explain the actions of less industrialized imperial powers like Italy or Japan?

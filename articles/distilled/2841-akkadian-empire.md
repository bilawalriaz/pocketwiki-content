# Akkadian Empire

## Overview
The Akkadian Empire (c. 2334–2154 BC) was the first empire in world history, unifying the independent Sumerian city-states of Mesopotamia under a single Semitic-speaking dynasty. Centered on the city of Akkad (not yet located), it created a vast, centralized state that stretched from the Mediterranean to the Persian Gulf, establishing a model for future Mesopotamian states and demonstrating the possibility of multi-ethnic, imperial rule.

## Timeline
- **c. 2334** — Sargon of Akkad defeats Sumerian king Lugal-zage-si, establishing the empire.
- **c. 2278–2270** — Reign of Rimush; widespread revolts are brutally suppressed.
- **c. 2269–2255** — Reign of Manishtushu; campaigns in the Persian Gulf region.
- **c. 2254–2218** — Reign of Naram-Sin; empire reaches peak, he declares himself a living god.
- **c. 2200** — The 4.2-kiloyear climate event causes severe drought across the region.
- **c. 2154** — Empire collapses; Gutian tribes invade and seize control of central Mesopotamia.
- **c. 2112** — Ur-Nammu of the Ur III dynasty expels the Gutians, ending the Akkadian period.

## Body

### Founding and Expansion
The empire was founded by Sargon of Akkad, who rose from being a cupbearer to the king of Kish to conquer all of Sumer and then expand aggressively. He replaced local rulers with loyal Akkadian governors and established a centralized administration. His conquests reached from the "Lower Sea" (Persian Gulf) to the "Upper Sea" (Mediterranean), and he organized naval expeditions to distant lands like Magan (Oman) and Dilmun (Bahrain). Sargon's successors, Rimush and Manishtushu, faced constant revolts and were both assassinated.

### Zenith under Naram-Sin
Naram-Sin, grandson of Sargon, brought the empire to its greatest extent. He adopted the unprecedented title "King of the Four Quarters" and was deified during his lifetime, a major break from Sumerian tradition where kings were merely divine representatives. He campaigned successfully in Syria, Anatolia, and against the Lullubi and Gutians, as recorded on his famous Victory Stele. His reign also saw the first known named author, his daughter Enheduanna, who served as high priestess of the moon god Nanna at Ur.

### Administration and Economy
The Akkadian government created a "classical standard" for Mesopotamian states, with the king as a supreme, often divine, authority. The economy was a planned system based on agricultural surpluses from both irrigated southern lands and rain-fed northern regions. This supported a large bureaucracy and military. Extensive trade networks imported metals, timber, and stone from as far as Anatolia, Lebanon, and the Indus Valley (Meluhha). The Akkadian language became the lingua franca of administration and diplomacy across the Middle East.

### Collapse
The empire's decline was caused by a combination of factors. Internal instability followed Naram-Sin's death, with weak successors and frequent revolts. A major, prolonged drought associated with the 4.2-kiloyear climate event (c. 2200 BC) caused crop failures, famine, and the collapse of the rain-fed agricultural subsidies from the north. This economic crisis, coupled with external pressure from groups like the Amorites and finally a full-scale invasion by the Gutians, led to the empire's disintegration and a subsequent "Dark Age" until the rise of the Ur III dynasty.

## Terms
- **Lingua franca**: A language used for communication between people who do not share a native language; Akkadian served this role in the empire.
- **Ensi**: A Sumerian title for a city-state ruler or governor; under the Akkadians, these became more like provincial administrators.
- **Lugal**: A Sumerian term for "king," which by the Akkadian period denoted a supreme ruler.
- **Dingir/Ilu**: The Sumerian/Akkadian word for "god"; Naram-Sin was the first king to be addressed with this title during his lifetime.
- **Year-names**: A dating system where each year was named after a significant event, used during the Akkadian period instead of regnal numbers.
- **4.2-kiloyear event**: A severe, centuries-long drought that occurred around 2200 BC, linked to the empire's collapse.
- **Sprachbund**: A region where languages have common features due to prolonged contact; used to describe the Sumerian-Akkadian linguistic relationship.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **The "First Empire"**: While often called the first empire, the term's definition is debated, and some scholars note earlier Sumerian claimants to large-scale rule.
- **Cause of Collapse**: The primary cause is debated. Some emphasize the catastrophic drought (the 4.2-kiloyear event), while others critique this theory, pointing to uncertain chronology and the empire's internal political weaknesses and revolts as sufficient explanations.
- **Location of Akkad**: The capital city, Akkad (Agade), has never been definitively located archaeologically, hindering a complete understanding of the empire's core.
- **Extent of Trade**: The precise nature and scale of Akkadian trade with distant regions like the Indus Valley (Meluhha) and Cyprus (Kaptara) are still being clarified through archaeological evidence.

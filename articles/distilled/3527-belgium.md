# Belgium

## Overview
Belgium is a federal parliamentary constitutional monarchy in Northwestern Europe, known for its complex political structure based on linguistic communities and regions. Its strategic location has made it a historical crossroads for European powers, earning it the nickname "the Battlefield of Europe." Today, it is a highly developed economy and a founding member of the European Union, with its capital, Brussels, serving as the de facto EU capital.

## Timeline
- **c. 57 BC** — Julius Caesar conquers the Belgae tribes, incorporating the region into the Roman Empire as Gallia Belgica.
- **843** — Treaty of Verdun divides the Carolingian Empire; most of modern Belgium falls into the Middle Kingdom (Lotharingia).
- **1302** — Flemish militias defeat French knights at the Battle of the Golden Spurs.
- **15th century** — The Duchy of Burgundy unites much of the Low Countries, creating the Burgundian Netherlands.
- **1568–1648** — The Eighty Years' War leads to the split of the Low Countries; the southern part remains under Spanish Habsburg rule.
- **1794–1815** — The region is annexed by Revolutionary France.
- **1815** — The Congress of Vienna creates the United Kingdom of the Netherlands, uniting the former Austrian Netherlands and the Dutch Republic.
- **1830** — The Belgian Revolution leads to independence from the Netherlands.
- **1908** — Belgium assumes control of the Congo Free State, renaming it the Belgian Congo.
- **1914–1918** — Germany invades Belgium, making it a major theatre of World War I.
- **1940–1944** — Germany occupies Belgium during World War II.
- **1951** — Belgium becomes a founding member of the European Coal and Steel Community, precursor to the EU.
- **1960** — The Belgian Congo gains independence.
- **1970–1993** — A series of state reforms transition Belgium from a unitary to a federal state.
- **2010–2011** — Belgium sets a world record for the longest period without a government (589 days).

## Body

### History and Formation
The region's history is marked by successive foreign dominations. After Roman rule, it was part of the Frankish kingdoms, the Carolingian Empire, and later the Holy Roman Empire. From the 15th century, it was controlled by the Burgundian and then Habsburg dynasties, first under Spanish and later Austrian rule. Following annexation by Revolutionary France, the Congress of Vienna (1815) placed it in the United Kingdom of the Netherlands. The Belgian Revolution of 1830 established an independent, Catholic, and officially French-speaking constitutional monarchy. The 19th century saw early industrialization, particularly in Wallonia, and the acquisition of colonies, most notably the Congo. The 20th century was defined by two German occupations and the gradual decolonization of the Congo (1960) and Ruanda-Urundi (1962).

### Federal Structure and Linguistic Divide
Belgium's political system is a complex federal compromise designed to manage tensions between its linguistic communities. The country is divided into three regions: the Dutch-speaking Flemish Region (Flanders) in the north, the French-speaking Walloon Region (Wallonia) in the south, and the bilingual Brussels-Capital Region. Parallel to these are three linguistic communities: the Flemish, French, and German-speaking communities. This structure, established through state reforms between 1970 and 1993, grants significant autonomy to each community and region over matters like culture, education, and economic policy. The linguistic divide, historically favoring French, has fueled political conflict and separatist sentiment, particularly in Flanders, leading to a fragmented party landscape and frequent government formation crises.

### Economy and International Role
Belgium has a highly globalized, service-oriented economy integrated with the rest of Europe. It was the first continental European country to industrialize, though post-WWII economic decline in Wallonia's steel industry contrasted with growth in Flanders. The country is a major trading nation with a key transport infrastructure, including the ports of Antwerp and Zeebrugge. As a founding member of the EU and NATO, Belgium plays a central role in European integration. Brussels hosts the headquarters of both organizations, making it a hub for international diplomacy.

## Terms
- **Federal State:** A system of government where sovereignty is constitutionally divided between a central governing authority and constituent political units (like states, provinces, or regions).
- **Linguistic Community:** A political entity in Belgium defined by language (Dutch, French, or German), with authority over cultural, educational, and personal matters for its speakers.
- **Region:** A political entity in Belgium defined by territory (Flanders, Wallonia, Brussels-Capital), with authority over economic, environmental, and territorial matters.
- **Belgae:** The collective name given by Julius Caesar to the Celtic and Germanic tribes inhabiting northern Gaul, including most of modern Belgium.
- **Gallia Belgica:** A large Roman province encompassing most of Northern Gaul, including the lands of the Belgae.
- **Burgundian Netherlands:** A composite state in the 15th-16th centuries that united much of the Benelux region under the Duchy of Burgundy, a precursor to modern Belgium.
- **Congress of Vienna (1815):** The conference that reorganized Europe after the Napoleonic Wars, creating the United Kingdom of the Netherlands which included modern Belgium.
- **Belgian Revolution (1830):** The uprising that led to the secession of the southern provinces from the United Kingdom of the Netherlands and the establishment of an independent Belgium.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The source highlights persistent tensions and separatist sentiment in Flanders, driven by economic disparities and linguistic-political conflicts, which continue to challenge national unity.
- The record-breaking 589-day government formation crisis after the 2010 election underscores the deep-seated political fragmentation and the difficulty of forming stable coalitions across the linguistic divide.
- The complex federal structure, while a compromise, creates ongoing jurisdictional conflicts between communities and regions, resolved by the Constitutional Court.

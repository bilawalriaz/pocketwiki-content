# Bamako

## Overview
Bamako is the capital and largest city of Mali, serving as the nation's administrative and economic center. Situated on the Niger River, it is a major West African urban hub with a population of over 4.2 million, experiencing rapid growth and significant challenges related to urbanization, infrastructure, and security.

## Timeline
- **c. 600s BCE** — Early trade routes established in the Niger River Valley.
- **c. 1650** — The kafu (state) of Bamako is founded by the Niare family.
- **1806** — Scottish explorer Mungo Park visits Bamako.
- **1883** — French forces establish a fort at Bamako.
- **1904** — A railroad connecting Bamako to Kayes is built.
- **1908** — Bamako is named the capital of French Sudan.
- **1960** — Mali gains independence; Bamako's population is ~160,000.
- **1991** — A large protest in Bamako is violently suppressed, leading to a coup.
- **2015** — A terrorist attack occurs at the Radisson Blu hotel in Bamako.
- **2021** — A military coup d'état seizes control of Mali.
- **2024** — A major jihadist attack targets a military school and the airport in Bamako.

## Body

### History and Political Significance
The area has evidence of Palaeolithic settlements, with early chiefdoms growing wealthy from trans-Saharan trade. The kafu of Bamako was founded around 1650. French colonial forces established a fort in 1883, and the city grew after becoming the capital of French Sudan in 1908. Following Mali's independence in 1960, Bamako was the center of political life, including a socialist period and the 23-year rule of Moussa Traoré. In 1991, a large protest in Bamako was violently suppressed, leading to a military coup and a transition to democracy. In recent years, the city has been targeted by jihadist terrorist attacks, notably in 2015 and 2024.

### Geography and Administration
Bamako is located on the Niger River floodplain, with a relatively flat landscape except for an escarpment to the north. The city is divided by the river, connected by three bridges. Administratively, it is a separate capital district divided into six communes (numbered, not named), each with its own elected council. The city's rapid, often uncontrolled population growth has strained infrastructure, traffic, and sanitation.

### Economy and Infrastructure
The District of Bamako concentrates 70% of Mali's industrial activity, with a dominant service sector. The downtown area is congested, while a modern business district (ACI-2000) is developing. Key infrastructure includes the Bamako-Sénou International Airport and the Dakar-Niger Railway. The city's drinking water supply is sometimes insufficient, leading to shortages. Much of the electricity is generated from the hydroelectric Sélingué Dam.

### Culture and Institutions
Bamako is home to major cultural institutions, including the National Museum of Mali, the National Library, and the University of Bamako. The city's architecture features unique styles, such as the BCEAO Tower, which is modeled on Sudano-Sahelian mosque designs. A music boom in the 1990s, led by artists like Salif Keita and Ali Farka Touré, brought international attention. The city hosts the biennial African Photography Encounters festival.

## Terms
- **Kafu**: A historical state or political entity in the region.
- **Cercle**: An administrative division in Mali, equivalent to a district.
- **Commune**: A municipal administrative division within a city or region.
- **Sudano-Sahelian architecture**: A traditional building style of the Sahel region, characterized by the use of earth and distinctive designs.
- **BCEAO**: The Central Bank of West African States, which provides financial services to several Francophone West African nations.
- **JNIM**: Jama'at Nasr al-Islam wal-Muslimin, an al-Qaeda affiliate responsible for attacks in the Sahel.
- **Bush taxi**: A common, informal mode of public transportation in West Africa.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The effectiveness and social impact of the 2015 privatization of waste collection in Bamako, which led to unemployment and protests over inadequate service.
- The long-term political and security stability of Bamako and Mali following the 2021 coup and ongoing jihadist insurgency.
- The challenges of managing Bamako's rapid, uncontrolled population growth and its effects on infrastructure, housing, and services.

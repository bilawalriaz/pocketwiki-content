# Sicily

## Overview
Sicily is the largest and most populous island in the Mediterranean Sea, located south of the Italian Peninsula. Its strategic position has made it a crossroads of civilizations for millennia, resulting in a rich, layered culture shaped by Greek, Roman, Arab, Norman, and Spanish influences. Today, it is an autonomous region of Italy known for its distinct history, cuisine, and landmarks like Mount Etna.

## Timeline
- **c. 14,000 BC** — Earliest archaeological record of human activity on the island.
- **c. 750 BC** — Greek colonies established, making Sicily a center of Magna Graecia.
- **241 BC** — Sicily becomes the first Roman province outside the Italian Peninsula.
- **827–965** — Arab conquest and rule of Sicily, establishing the Emirate of Sicily.
- **1071** — Norman conquest begins with the fall of Palermo.
- **1130** — Roger II establishes the Kingdom of Sicily.
- **1282** — The Sicilian Vespers revolt leads to Aragonese rule.
- **1860** — Giuseppe Garibaldi's Expedition of the Thousand leads to Sicily's annexation by the Kingdom of Sardinia.
- **1943** — Allied invasion of Sicily during World War II.
- **1946** — Sicily is granted special autonomous status within the Italian Republic.

## Body

### Ancient and Medieval History
Sicily's earliest inhabitants included the Sicani, Elymians, and Sicels. From about 750 BC, Greek colonization created powerful city-states like Syracuse, making the island a core part of Magna Graecia. After the Punic Wars, Sicily became a vital Roman province and granary. Following the fall of the Western Roman Empire, it was ruled by Vandals, Ostrogoths, and then the Byzantine Empire. The Arab conquest, beginning in 827, introduced new crops and irrigation systems, with Palermo becoming a major cultural center. The Norman conquest in the 11th century led to the Kingdom of Sicily under Roger II, a prosperous and multicultural state.

### From the Kingdom of Sicily to Italian Unification
After the Norman Hauteville dynasty, Sicily passed to the Hohenstaufen and then to the French Angevins. The War of the Sicilian Vespers (1282) expelled the French and brought the island under the Crown of Aragon. Over the following centuries, it was ruled by Spanish and Austrian dynasties before becoming the Kingdom of the Two Sicilies under the Bourbons. In 1860, Giuseppe Garibaldi's Expedition of the Thousand conquered the island, and a plebiscite led to its annexation by the Kingdom of Sardinia, forming part of the newly unified Italy.

### Modern Sicily
After unification, Sicily faced economic challenges and significant emigration. The Allied invasion in 1943 led to the fall of Fascist Italy. In 1946, Sicily became one of Italy's five autonomous regions. The post-war period saw economic development funded by the *Cassa per il Mezzogiorno* (Fund for the South), though the island continues to have a GDP per capita below the Italian average. The Sicilian Mafia (*Cosa Nostra*) has been a persistent issue, though major state crackdowns in the 1980s and 1990s weakened its power.

### Geography and Climate
Sicily is triangular, separated from Calabria by the Strait of Messina. Its terrain is mostly hilly, dominated by Mount Etna, Europe's tallest active volcano. The climate is typically Mediterranean, with hot, dry summers and mild, wet winters. The island's fertile volcanic soil supports agriculture, notably citrus, olives, grapes, and pistachios.

### Culture and Society
Sicilian culture is a blend of its many rulers. The Sicilian language, a distinct Romance language, is widely spoken alongside Italian. Cuisine reflects Greek, Arab, and Spanish influences, with famous dishes like *cannoli* and *arancini*. The island has a strong tradition of puppet theater (*Opera dei Pupi*), a UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage. Roman Catholicism is the predominant religion. Sicily has produced notable figures in literature, science, and the arts, including Nobel laureates Luigi Pirandello and Salvatore Quasimodo.

## Terms
- **Magna Graecia**: The name for the coastal areas of Southern Italy that were colonized by ancient Greeks.
- **Emirate of Sicily**: An Islamic state on the island from 831 to 1091, established after the Arab conquest.
- **Sicilian Vespers**: A 1282 revolt in Palermo against the rule of the French Angevin king, leading to Aragonese control.
- **Risorgimento**: The 19th-century movement for Italian unification.
- **Cassa per il Mezzogiorno**: A fund established by the Italian government in 1950 to promote economic development in Southern Italy, including Sicily.
- **Cosa Nostra**: The Sicilian Mafia, a criminal syndicate originating in Sicily.
- **Opera dei Pupi**: Traditional Sicilian marionette theater, recognized by UNESCO.
- **Trinacria**: The ancient Greek name for Sicily, referring to its triangular shape and three capes.

## Debates and open questions
- The ethnic origins of the pre-Greek inhabitants, particularly the Sicani, are debated, with theories suggesting Iberian or Illyrian roots.
- The extent and nature of cultural assimilation during the Arab and Norman periods remain subjects of scholarly study, especially regarding language and religion.
- The ongoing effectiveness of the Italian state's efforts to fully dismantle the Mafia's political and economic influence is a persistent question.

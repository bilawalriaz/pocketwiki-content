# Angel Falls

## Overview
Angel Falls is the world's tallest uninterrupted waterfall, located in Venezuela's Canaima National Park. Its immense height and remote location in the jungle-covered Auyán-tepui mountain make it a significant natural landmark and a major tourist attraction, though its exact status as the world's tallest is debated.

- **9 Oct 1937** — Angel attempts to land on Auyán-tepui; his plane is damaged, and he and his companions descend on foot.

- **13 Jan 1971** — The first successful climb to the top of the cliff face is completed by a team led by George Bogel.
- **Nov 1983** — Max Botto of Venezuela performs the first BASE jump from the falls.
- **2009** — President Hugo Chávez proposes changing the name to an indigenous Pemon term.

## Body

### Physical Characteristics
Angel Falls is the world's tallest uninterrupted waterfall with a total height of 979 metres (3,212 ft). This measurement includes the main plunge of 807 m (2,648 ft), followed by about 400 m (1,300 ft) of sloped cascade and rapids, and a final 30-metre (100 ft) plunge. The falls drop from the edge of the Auyán-tepui mountain in Canaima National Park, a UNESCO World Heritage Site. The water flows into the Churún River, a tributary of the Orinoco River. There is ongoing uncertainty about whether Angel Falls or South Africa's Tugela Falls is the world's tallest, as both measurements were taken from a considerable distance.

### History and Naming
The falls are named after Jimmie Angel, the American aviator who first flew over them in 1933. After his plane crashed atop the tepui in 1937, news of his adventure spread, and the name "Angel Falls" was adopted. In 2009, President Hugo Chávez proposed renaming the falls to the indigenous Pemon name "Kerepakupai-Merú," stating the landmark should bear an indigenous name, but later said he would not decree the change. The first European to reach the base was Latvian explorer Aleksandrs Laime in 1946. The official height was established by a 1949 expedition led by Ruth Robertson. The first successful climb of the cliff face was completed in 1971.

### Tourism and Media
Angel Falls is a top tourist attraction in Venezuela, though access is complex, requiring flights and river trips from June to December when water levels are sufficient. The falls have inspired fictional locations, most notably Paradise Falls in Pixar's film *Up* (2009), and have appeared in other films like *What Dreams May Come* (1998) and *Point Break* (2015). The first BASE jump was performed in 1983.

## Terms
- **Tepui:** A table-top mountain or mesa, characteristic of the Gran Sabana region.
- **Uninterrupted waterfall:** A waterfall where water falls without hitting any ledges or outcrops.
- **Plunge:** The vertical drop of a waterfall.
- **Cascade:** A waterfall that descends over a series of rock steps or a steep slope.
- **Talus rapids:** Rapids formed by the accumulation of broken rock debris at the base of a cliff.
- **Canaima National Park:** A large national park in Venezuela and a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
- **Gran Sabana:** A region of rolling grasslands, tepuis, and waterfalls in southeastern Venezuela.
- **Pemon:** The indigenous people of the Gran Sabana region.

## Debates and open questions
- **Tallest Waterfall:** There is ongoing debate and uncertainty about whether Angel Falls or South Africa's Tugela Falls holds the title of the world's tallest waterfall, as both measurements were taken from a considerable distance.
- **Indigenous Naming:** The use and authenticity of indigenous Pemon names for the falls and local streams are discussed, with some sources noting the area was considered dangerous and not visited by the Pemon.

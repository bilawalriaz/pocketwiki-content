# Gobi Desert

## Overview
The Gobi is a large, cold desert and grassland region spanning southern Mongolia and northern China. It is the world's sixth-largest desert, significant for its extreme climate, unique biodiversity, important fossil discoveries, and ongoing desertification challenges.

## Timeline
- **c. 13500–8000 cal BP** — Oasis I period (Mesolithic); early oasis use begins.
- **c. 8000–5000 cal BP** — Oasis II period (Neolithic); extensive oasis use. A warm, wet phase begins.
- **c. 7500 cal BP** — Lake levels in the Western Gobi reach their peak.
- **c. 5000–3000 cal BP** — Oasis III period (Bronze Age); herder burials and bronze artifacts found.
- **c. 5000–4500 cal BP** — A period of desertification occurs.
- **c. 3500–3000 cal BP** — Increasing aridity leads to a decline in human habitation.
- **1923** — First dinosaur eggs discovered in the Gobi.
- **1978** — China begins the Three-North Shelter Forest Program ("Green Great Wall") to combat desertification.
- **1996–2016** — Dust storm frequency increases in the Gobi region.

## Body

### Geography and Climate
The Gobi measures approximately 1,600 km from southwest to northeast and 800 km from north to south, with an area of about 1,295,000 km². It is a cold desert located on a plateau roughly 910–1,520 m above sea level, contributing to its low temperatures. Annual rainfall averages about 194 mm, with additional winter moisture from Siberian snow. Temperatures can range from −40 °C in winter to 45 °C in summer, with rapid changes of up to 35 °C in 24 hours. The climate is characterized by extreme dryness, especially in winter due to the Siberian anticyclone.

### Ecology and Economy
Despite harsh conditions, the Gobi sustains unique animal species, including black-tailed gazelles, wild Bactrian camels, and Mongolian wild ass. Common vegetation includes drought-adapted shrubs like gray sparrow's saltwort and low grasses. Livestock grazing, particularly by goats raised for cashmere, has degraded grasslands. The region is a major source of important fossil finds, including the first dinosaur eggs discovered in 1923 and prehistoric stone implements in the Nemegt Basin. Several large nature reserves, such as Gobi Gurvansaikhan National Park, have been established.

### Desertification and Conservation
The Gobi is expanding through desertification, most rapidly on its southern edge into China, where an estimated 3,600 km² of grassland is overtaken annually. This expansion is attributed primarily to human activities like deforestation, overgrazing, and water depletion, as well as climate change. China's Three-North Shelter Forest Program, begun in 1978, aims to reverse desertification by planting trees across 36.5 million hectares in northern China.

### Ecoregions
The Gobi is divided into five distinct dry ecoregions based on climate and topography: the Eastern Gobi desert steppe, Alashan Plateau semi-desert, Gobi Lakes Valley desert steppe, Dzungarian Basin semi-desert, and the Taklamakan Desert (sometimes considered separate). The Eastern Gobi features eroded surfaces and flat-topped mountains, while the Alashan Plateau is a level plain of saline clay and sand. The Dzungarian Basin is a hollow plain where rivers from the Tian Shan mountains form marshes.

### Prehistory and Exploration
Early habitation is categorized into three oasis periods: Oasis I (Mesolithic), Oasis II (Neolithic), and Oasis III (Bronze Age). A warm, wet phase began around 8000 cal BP, peaking at 7500 cal BP, followed by desertification between 5000–4500 cal BP and a decline in habitation from 3500–3000 cal BP due to aridity. The region was historically inhabited by nomadic Mongols, Uyghurs, and Kazakhs, and was explored by various European and American travelers.

## Terms
- **Cold desert**: A desert with low temperatures, often due to high latitude or elevation.
- **Desertification**: The process by which fertile land becomes desert, typically due to drought, deforestation, or inappropriate agriculture.
- **Evapotranspiration**: The combined process of water evaporation from the land and transpiration from plants.
- **Siberian anticyclone**: A high-pressure system over Siberia that brings cold, dry air in winter.
- **Cal BP**: "Calibrated years Before Present," a standard for radiocarbon dating.
- **Oasis**: A fertile spot in a desert where water is found.
- **Petroglyphs**: Images created by removing part of a rock surface by incising, picking, or carving.
- **Three-North Shelter Forest Program**: A Chinese government tree-planting project to combat desertification.

## Debates and Open questions
- The exact boundaries of the Gobi are debated; some geographers consider the western area (Tarim Basin, Lop Nor, Hami) as a separate Taklamakan Desert.
- The classification of certain mountain ranges (e.g., Tuge-tau) within the Dzungarian Basin is disputed between scholars like Grigory Grum-Grshimailo and Sven Hedin.
- The causes and relative impacts of natural climate change versus human activities on the Gobi's expansion are subjects of ongoing study.

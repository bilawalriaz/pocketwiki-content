# History of Ethiopia

## Overview
Ethiopia is one of Africa's oldest nations, with a civilization spanning millennia. Its history is marked by the rise and fall of powerful kingdoms like Aksum, the adoption of Christianity, resistance to colonialism, and complex internal ethnic and political dynamics. Understanding Ethiopia's past is crucial for comprehending modern African history, state formation, and the challenges of nation-building in a diverse society.

## Timeline
- **c. 980 BC** — Kingdom of Dʿmt rises to power, with its capital at Yeha.
- **1st century CE** — Kingdom of Aksum emerges as a major Red Sea power.
- **Early 4th century** — Christianity declared state religion under King Ezana of Aksum.
- **c. 960** — Queen Yodit (Gudit) overthrows the Aksumite dynasty.
- **c. 1270** — Solomonic dynasty established by Yekuno Amlak, claiming descent from Solomon.
- **1529–1543** — Ethiopian-Adal War; Ahmad ibn Ibrahim al-Ghazi's jihad is defeated with Portuguese aid.
- **1636** — Emperor Fasilides founds Gondar as the capital.
- **1855** — Tewodros II ends the "Era of the Princes" and reunifies the empire.
- **1896** — Ethiopia defeats Italy at the Battle of Adwa, preserving independence.
- **1936–1941** — Italy occupies Ethiopia; Emperor Haile Selassie goes into exile.
- **1974** — Emperor Haile Selassie is overthrown by the Derg military junta.
- **1991** — The Derg is overthrown; a transitional government is established.
- **2018** — Abiy Ahmed becomes Prime Minister; peace deal signed with Eritrea.
- **2020–2022** — Tigray War causes massive humanitarian crisis.

## Body

### Antiquity and the Kingdom of Aksum
Ethiopian civilization traces its roots to ancient kingdoms like Dʿmt (c. 980 BC) and the legendary Land of Punt. The first verifiable great power was the Kingdom of Aksum, which rose in the 1st century CE. Aksum became a major trading empire on the Red Sea, subjugating parts of South Arabia and Meroe. In the early 4th century, King Ezana converted to Christianity, making it the state religion. Aksum's power later declined due to the rise of Islam, which shifted trade routes away from the Christian kingdom.

### Medieval Dynasties and Isolation
After Aksum's decline, the Zagwe dynasty (c. 960–1270) ruled from a new capital at Lalibela, famous for its rock-hewn churches. They were succeeded by the Solomonic dynasty in 1270, which claimed descent from King Solomon and the Queen of Sheba. The early Solomonic period saw military expansion and the spread of Christianity, but also increasing isolation from other Christian nations. Internal religious conflicts and the expansion of the Oromo people in the 16th and 17th centuries weakened the empire.

### Early Modern Period and European Contact
The Gondarine period (1632–1769) saw a new capital at Gondar and cultural flourishing, but also religious strife and the expulsion of Jesuit missionaries. The "Era of the Princes" (Zemene Mesafint) followed, where regional warlords eclipsed the monarchy. This ended with Emperor Tewodros II (r. 1855–1868), who began modernizing and centralizing the state. His successor, Yohannes IV, died in battle in 1889. Menelik II then expanded the empire to its modern borders, defeating Italy at the Battle of Adwa in 1896 to maintain independence.

### Modern Era and Revolution
Menelik II's successor, Haile Selassie, modernized Ethiopia but was overthrown in 1974 by the Derg, a Marxist military junta led by Mengistu Haile Mariam. The Derg's rule was marked by the "Red Terror," forced resettlements, and a devastating famine. It was overthrown in 1991 by the Ethiopian People's Revolutionary Democratic Front (EPRDF). A federal democratic republic was established, but ethnic tensions persisted, leading to conflicts like the Eritrean–Ethiopian War (1998–2000) and the Tigray War (2020–2022).

## Terms
*   **Aksumite Kingdom:** A major trading empire centered in modern Tigray, dominant from the 1st to 7th centuries CE.
*   **Solomonic Dynasty:** The ruling line of Ethiopia from 1270 to 1974, claiming descent from King Solomon and the Queen of Sheba.
*   **Derg:** The Marxist military junta that ruled Ethiopia from 1974 to 1991.
*   **Zemene Mesafint:** The "Era of the Princes," a period of decentralized rule by regional warlords (c. 1769–1855).
*   **Adwa:** The site of Ethiopia's decisive victory over Italy in 1896, securing its sovereignty.
*   **EPRDF:** The coalition of ethnically-based parties that governed Ethiopia from 1991 to 2019.
*   **Tigray War:** A brutal civil war (2020–2022) between the federal government and the Tigray People's Liberation Front (TPLF).

## Debates and Open Questions
*   The exact location and nature of the ancient Land of Punt remain debated among scholars.
*   The extent of Sabaean (Yemeni) influence on the pre-Aksumite kingdom of Dʿmt is contested.
*   The causes of the Aksumite Kingdom's decline are multiple and debated, including drought, trade shifts, and overgrazing.
*   The historical accuracy of the Solomonic dynasty's claim of descent from King Solomon is a matter of legend versus verifiable history.
*   The human cost and precise casualty figures of the Tigray War are still being determined, with estimates varying widely.

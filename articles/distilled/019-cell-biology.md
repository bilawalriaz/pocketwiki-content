# Cell (biology)

## Overview

The cell is the basic structural and functional unit of all forms of life. The term comes from the Latin word *cellula* meaning "small room." A biological cell consists of a semipermeable cell membrane enclosing cytoplasm that contains genetic material. Most cells are only visible under a microscope, and most are capable of replication and protein synthesis. Cells emerged on Earth about four billion years ago, and all organisms are grouped into prokaryotes (single-celled bacteria and archaea) and eukaryotes (which can be single-celled or multicellular).

## Timeline

- **c. 4 billion years ago** — First cells emerge on Earth, likely heterotrophs
- **3–3.5 billion years ago** — First evidence of multicellularity in cyanobacteria-like organisms
- **c. 2.2 billion years ago** — Eukaryogenesis: symbiogenesis between an archaean and a bacterium creates the first eukaryotic common ancestor
- **c. 1.6 billion years ago** — Green plants created by a second symbiogenesis adding chloroplasts from cyanobacteria
- **1665** — Robert Hooke discovers cells in cork, coins the term "cell"
- **1632–1723** — Antonie van Leeuwenhoek builds microscopes, draws protozoa and bacteria
- **1839** — Theodor Schwann and Matthias Jakob Schleiden found cell theory
- **1855** — Rudolf Virchow states new cells come from pre-existing cells (*omnis cellula ex cellula*)
- **1931** — Ernst Ruska builds the first transmission electron microscope
- **1981** — Lynn Margulis publishes *Symbiosis in Cell Evolution* detailing symbiogenesis

## Body

### Prokaryotes

All prokaryotes are single-celled and include bacteria and archaea, two of the three domains of life. Prokaryotic cells were likely the first form of life on Earth. They are simpler and smaller than eukaryotic cells, lack a nucleus (having a nucleoid region instead), and typically lack membrane-bound organelles. Most prokaryotes range from 0.1 to 5.0 μm in diameter; the largest known bacterium, *Thiomargarita magnifica*, is visible to the naked eye with an average length of 1 cm.

**Bacteria** are enclosed in a cell envelope consisting of a plasma membrane covered by a cell wall (made of peptidoglycan), sometimes with a third gelatinous capsule layer. The cell wall prevents the cell from bursting (cytolysis) from osmotic pressure. Bacterial DNA typically consists of a single circular chromosome in the nucleoid; some bacteria have multiple circular or linear chromosomes. Extrachromosomal DNA plasmids encode additional genes, such as antibiotic resistance. Some bacteria have protein-based microcompartments (gas vesicles, carboxysomes) and membrane-bound organelles (magnetosomes, anammoxosomes). Cell-surface appendages include flagella (for movement) and pili (for attachment and communication).

**Archaea** have a cell envelope with a plasma membrane and cell wall, except *Thermoplasma* which has only the membrane. Their membranes are unique, consisting of ether-linked lipids. Archaea are noted for extremophile species thriving in extreme heat, cold, acidity, alkalinity, or high salt. A unique form of archaean metabolism is methanogenesis. There are no known archaean pathogens.

### Eukaryotes

A eukaryotic cell can be 2 to 100 times larger in diameter than a typical prokaryotic cell. Eukaryotes are distinguished by a membrane-bound nucleus (from Greek meaning "true nut"). The nucleus houses chromosomes and is where almost all DNA replication and transcription occur. Other organelles include the endoplasmic reticulum, ribosomes, Golgi apparatus, mitochondria, lysosomes, peroxisomes, endosomes, and vesicles. The endomembrane system encompasses the cell membrane and all organelle membranes except mitochondria.

The cell membrane is a selectively permeable lipid bilayer of amphiphilic phospholipids, best described by the fluid mosaic model. The cytoplasm contains the cytosol (gel-like, forming 30%–50% of cell volume) and the cytoskeleton (microtubules, intermediate filaments, microfilaments) which maintains shape and anchors organelles. Mitochondria generate energy via aerobic respiration (oxidative phosphorylation producing ATP); they descend from bacteria via endosymbiosis, have their own DNA, and multiply by binary fission. Lysosomes contain over 60 hydrolytic enzymes for digestion. Peroxisomes rid the cell of toxic peroxides. Vacuoles sequester waste; contractile vacuoles in *Amoeba* pump out excess water. Ribosomes synthesize proteins from mRNA. Vaults are large ribonuclear protein particles of speculative function. Proteasomes degrade regulatory and damaged proteins.

### Animal, plant, algal, fungal, and protist cells

**Animal cells** develop from a totipotent diploid zygote that differentiates into specialized tissues. The ectoderm gives rise to epithelial and nervous tissue; triploblastic animals (the clade Bilateria) have an additional mesodermal layer. There are an estimated 200 different cell types in the human body, with around 30 trillion cells (36 trillion in males, 28 trillion in females). Centrosomes are unique to animal cells, serving as the main microtubule organizing center. Specialized cells include muscle cells (with sarcolemma and sarcoplasm) and electrocytes in electric fish that create and store electrical energy.

**Plant cells** have cell walls of cellulose, hemicelluloses, and pectin; lignin forms secondary walls in sclerenchyma. Plasmodesmata are pores allowing cell-to-cell communication. Chloroplasts capture the sun's energy for photosynthesis; chromoplasts contain carotenoid pigments; leucoplasts store nutrients. Plant cells have large water-storage vacuoles and two types of peroxisomes (one for photorespiration, one—the glyoxysome—converting fatty acids to sugars in germinating seeds). The plant cytoskeleton has microtubules and microfilaments but no intermediate filaments.

**Algal cells** are photoautotrophs using plastids for photosynthesis. Alginate, a polysaccharide in brown algal cell walls, has food and pharmacological uses. **Fungal cells** have a spitzenkörper (an aggregation of membrane-bound vesicles) associated with hyphal tip growth, and cell walls of a chitin-glucan complex. **Protist cells** may have a cell membrane only, a cell wall, a pellicle (ciliates), a test (testate amoebae), or a frustule (diatoms). Ciliates have both a diploid micronucleus (generative) and an ampliploid macronucleus (vegetative).

### Cellular processes

**Replication**: Prokaryotes divide by binary fission; eukaryotes undergo mitosis followed by cytokinesis. Diploid cells may undergo meiosis to produce haploid gametes. DNA replication occurs during the S phase of the cell cycle; in meiosis, DNA replicates only once before two divisions.

**Signaling** involves three components: the ligand (first messenger), the receptor, and the signal itself. Errors in signaling can cause cancer, autoimmunity, and diabetes. **Protein targeting** directs proteins to appropriate destinations; errors are linked to multiple diseases. **DNA repair** systems include nucleotide excision repair, mismatch repair, non-homologous end joining, recombinational repair, and photoreactivation. **Metabolism** has two divisions: catabolism (breaking down molecules for energy) and anabolism (constructing complex molecules). **Protein synthesis** involves transcription (DNA to mRNA) and translation (mRNA to polypeptide via ribosomes and tRNA). **Motility** uses flagella, cilia, or pseudopodia; in 2020, scientists described how cells navigate mazes by generating gradients after breaking down diffused chemoattractants. **Cell death** occurs via necrosis (injury) or programmed death (apoptosis, autophagy); other pathways include anoikis, pyroptosis, mitoptosis, parthanatos, and necroptosis.

### Origins and multicellularity

The origin of cells relates to the origin of life. Small molecules may have arrived on meteorites, formed at deep-sea hydrothermal vents, or been synthesized by lightning. RNA may have been the earliest self-replicating molecule. The first cells were likely heterotrophs with simpler, more permeable membranes. Eukaryotic cells arose ~2.2 billion years ago via symbiogenesis between an archaean and a bacterium. The last eukaryotic common ancestor had a nucleus, aerobic mitochondria, at least one centriole and cilium, sex, peroxisomes, and a dormant cyst. Multicellularity's first evidence comes from cyanobacteria-like organisms (3–3.5 billion years ago) with functional cell differentiation. The extracellular matrix (ECM), similar to bacterial extracellular polymeric substances, enabled multicellularity; basement membranes coincided with its origin. Multicellularity has been replicated in laboratory evolution experiments using predation as selective pressure.

## Terms

- **Prokaryote**: Single-celled organism lacking a membrane-bound nucleus; includes bacteria and archaea
- **Eukaryote**: Organism with a membrane-bound nucleus; can be single-celled or multicellular
- **Nucleoid**: Region in prokaryotes where the circular chromosome contacts the cytoplasm
- **Organelle**: Compartment of the cell specialized for one or more functions
- **Endosymbiosis**: Process by which mitochondria and chloroplasts descended from bacteria living inside ancient prokaryotes
- **Symbiogenesis**: The merging of an archaean and a bacterium to create the first eukaryotic cell
- **Cytokinesis**: Division of the cell following nuclear division (mitosis)
- **Meiosis**: Division producing four haploid cells that serve as gametes
- **Apoptosis**: Programmed cell death, a natural process of replacing dead cells
- **Extracellular matrix (ECM)**: Material enabling multicellularity, similar in function to bacterial EPS

## Debates and open questions

There is little experimental data defining what the first self-replicating forms were; RNA is hypothesized as the earliest self-replicating molecule because it can both store genetic information and catalyze chemical reactions. The functions of vaults are purely speculative; they may play a role in nuclear-cytoplasmic transport or serve as scaffolds for signal transduction proteins. The origin of life's small molecules is debated among meteorite delivery, deep-sea hydrothermal vents, and lightning synthesis in a reducing atmosphere.

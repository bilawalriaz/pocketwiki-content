# Socotra

## Overview
Socotra is a Yemeni island in the Indian Ocean, renowned for its extreme ecological uniqueness and high rates of endemic species, with a third of its plant life found nowhere else. Geographically part of Africa but politically part of Yemen, its long isolation has created a distinct cultural and biological landscape, making it a critical site for studying evolution and biodiversity.

## Timeline
- **c. 3000 BC** — Oldowan lithic culture present; stone tools found near Hadibo in 2008.
- **1st–6th c. AD** — Hoq Cave inscriptions left by sailors from diverse cultures, indicating active trade networks.
- **880** — Aksumite (Ethiopian) expeditionary force conquers the island.
- **10th c.** — Arab geographer Ibn Hawqal describes Socotra as a nest of pirates.
- **1507** — Portuguese fleet captures the port of Suq from the Mahra Sultanate.
- **1511** — Portuguese abandon the island due to lack of harbor and famine.
- **1886** — British government concludes a protectorate treaty with the Mahra Sultan.
- **1967** — Socotra becomes part of South Yemen after the British withdrawal.
- **1990** — Socotra becomes part of the unified Republic of Yemen.
- **2008** — Socotra is recognized as a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
- **2015** — Cyclones Chapala and Megh cause severe damage; UAE begins increasing its presence.
- **2018** — UAE and Saudi troops land on the island; a deal returns administrative control to Yemen.
- **2020** — UAE-backed Southern Transitional Council (STC) seizes control of the island.

## Body

### Geography and Climate
Socotra is a continental fragment, geologically linked to the Somali plate, that detached from Gondwana during the Miocene epoch. It is one of the most isolated landforms of continental origin. The island features three main regions: narrow coastal plains with monsoon-formed dunes; limestone plateaus with karst topography used for traditional agro-pastoralism; and the central granite Hajhir Mountains, rising to 1,503 meters. The climate is transitional hot desert to semi-desert, with light year-round rainfall that increases in the mountains due to orographic lift. The southwest monsoon brings strong winds and high seas from June to September.

### Endemism and Ecology
Socotra's long isolation and varied climatic conditions have produced exceptional biodiversity, earning it the nickname "Galapagos of the Indian Ocean." About 37% of its 835 vascular plant species, 90% of its reptiles, and 95% of its land snails are endemic. Notable endemic flora include the Dragon's Blood Tree (*Dracaena cinnabari*), with its umbrella-like crown for condensing atmospheric moisture, and the cucumber tree (*Dendrosicyos socotranus*). Many endemic plants are slow-growing and long-lived, making them vulnerable to climate change. The most ecologically diverse areas are wet refugia like the Hajhir massif, which support unique speciation. Current threats include erosion, overgrazing, invasive species, and intensified winds from global warming.

### History and Politics
Socotra has a long history of foreign influence due to its strategic location. It was part of ancient Indian Ocean trade networks, conquered by Aksumites in 880, and later controlled by the Mahra Sultanate. The Portuguese (1507–1511) and British (1834–1835, 1886) attempted to control it but were deterred by poor anchorages. It became part of South Yemen in 1967 and unified Yemen in 1990. Since 2015, the UAE has increased its presence, leading to a 2018 military intervention and a 2020 coup by the UAE-backed STC. In 2023, Yemen's internationally recognized government integrated the STC and recognized its rule over the archipelago.

### Demographics and Culture
The inhabitants, called Socotrans, are primarily indigenous Soqotri people of Southern Arabian descent, with some of African origin. They speak Arabic and the Semitic language Soqotri, which has an ancient poetic tradition. The population is about 50,000, mostly on the main island, with Hadibu as the principal city. Christianity was present from the 6th century but declined after the 16th century; today, only ruins and cross engravings remain. The population is now predominantly Muslim.

## Terms
- **Endemic**: A species native and restricted to a certain place, not found naturally elsewhere.
- **Continental fragment**: A piece of continental crust that has broken off and drifted away from the main continent.
- **Orographic lift**: The lifting of air over mountains, causing it to cool and produce precipitation.
- **Karst topography**: A landscape formed from the dissolution of soluble rocks like limestone, characterized by sinkholes and caves.
- **Agro-pastoralism**: A mixed farming system combining crop cultivation and livestock herding.
- **Vicariant speciation**: The formation of new species due to the geographic separation of a population.
- **Cryptic diversity**: The existence of genetically distinct species that are morphologically similar and not easily distinguished.
- **Wet refugia**: Areas that retain moisture and support biodiversity during otherwise dry periods.

## Debates and open questions
- The exact origin of the name "Socotra" is debated, with theories linking it to a Greek derivation from a South Arabian tribe or to an Arabic term meaning "market of dragon's blood."
- The actual reptile diversity may be greater than currently recognized due to high levels of cryptic diversity revealed by genetic surveys.
- The future of Socotra's endemic species is uncertain, as they are highly vulnerable to climate change, invasive species, and habitat fragmentation from development.

# Susa

## Overview
Susa was one of the most important cities of the Ancient Near East, serving as the capital of Elam and the winter capital of the Achaemenid Empire. Located in modern-day Iran, its strategic position made it a cultural and political crossroads for millennia, from its founding c. 4200 BC through the Islamic period. Its long history, rich archaeological record, and mention in biblical and classical texts make it a key site for understanding the development of urban civilization in the region.

## Timeline
- **c. 4200 BC** — Foundation of the earliest settlement at Susa.
- **c. 4200–3800 BC** — Susa I period; characterized by distinctive painted pottery and a monumental platform.
- **c. 3800–3100 BC** — Susa II period; strong cultural influence from the Uruk civilization in Mesopotamia.
- **c. 3100–2700 BC** — Susa III ("Proto-Elamite") period; emergence of Proto-Elamite script.
- **c. 2700 BC** — First recorded battle between Sumer (Kish) and Susa.
- **c. 2330 BC** — Susa incorporated into the Akkadian Empire.
- **c. 2100 BC** — Governor Kutik-Inshushinak rebels, making Susa an independent Elamite capital.
- **c. 1175 BC** — Elamites plunder the stele of the Code of Hammurabi from Babylon and take it to Susa.
- **647 BC** — Neo-Assyrian king Ashurbanipal sacks and destroys Susa.
- **540–539 BC** — Susa captured by Cyrus the Great, becoming part of the Achaemenid Empire.
- **c. 522–486 BC** — Darius the Great constructs a major palace complex at Susa.
- **324 BC** — Alexander the Great celebrates a mass wedding of Persians and Macedonians at Susa.
- **224 AD** — Susa conquered and destroyed by the Sassanid Ardashir I, then rebuilt.
- **339 AD** — Sassanid Shapur II destroys Susa after a Christian rebellion, later rebuilding and renaming it.
- **642–643 AD** — Susa captured by Arab Muslim forces during the conquest of Persia.
- **1218 AD** — Susa razed by invading Mongols, never regaining its former importance.
- **2015** — Archaeological remains of Susa inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

## Body

### Early Settlement and the Susa I Period
Susa was founded c. 4200 BC, one of the oldest-known settlements in the region. Its early inhabitants built a monumental platform and a cemetery containing thousands of graves. The "Susa I" style pottery found there, with its distinctive painted designs, represents a regional variant of the Mesopotamian Ubaid tradition. Copper metallurgy was also practiced, with numerous axes and other objects found in burials.

### Uruk Influence and the Proto-Elamite Period
During the Susa II period (c. 3800–3100 BC), Susa came under the cultural influence of the Uruk civilization in Mesopotamia, adopting elements like cylinder seals and monumental architecture. The extent of this influence is debated; some scholars see Susa as a Uruk colony, while others argue for selective adoption and local independence. By the Susa III or "Proto-Elamite" period (c. 3100–2700 BC), Susa had become the center of Elamite civilization, using an undeciphered script.

### Elamite Capital
Susa served as the capital of the Elamite state for centuries. Its control shifted between Elam, Sumer, and Akkad. The Old Elamite period (c. 2700–1500 BC) saw rule by dynasties like Awan and Simashki. A major cultural shift occurred in the Middle Elamite period (c. 1500–1100 BC) with the "Elamisation" of Susa, promoting the Elamite language and pantheon. In c. 1175 BC, Elamites brought the Code of Hammurabi stele to Susa. The city was devastated by the Neo-Assyrian king Ashurbanipal in 647 BC.

### Achaemenid to Sasanian Periods
Cyrus the Great captured Susa c. 540 BC, making it an Achaemenid capital. Darius I built a grand palace there. After Alexander the Great's conquest, Susa became a regional capital under the Seleucids. It was a frequent refuge for Parthian kings when the Romans sacked their capital. The Sassanids destroyed and rebuilt the city multiple times, notably after a Christian rebellion in 339 AD, after which it was renamed Eran-Khwarrah-Shapur.

### Islamic Period and Decline
Susa was captured by Arab Muslim forces in 642–643 AD. The city recovered and remained a regional center with a mosque, Christian community, and Jewish synagogue. However, it was razed by the Mongols in 1218 and further declined in the 15th century as its population moved to Dezful. Today, the ancient ruins are adjacent to the modern town of Shush.

## Terms
- **Elam**: An ancient civilization centered in what is now southwestern Iran, with Susa as its capital.
- **Achaemenid Empire**: The first Persian Empire (c. 550–330 BC), for which Susa served as a winter capital.
- **Uruk period**: A prehistoric period in Mesopotamia (c. 4000–3100 BC) whose cultural influence spread to Susa.
- **Proto-Elamite**: The earliest known script of the Elamites, used at Susa c. 3100–2700 BC, which remains undeciphered.
- **Kudurrus**: Babylonian boundary stones, several of which were found at Susa.
- **Stele**: An upright stone slab, often inscribed or carved, such as the Code of Hammurabi stele found at Susa.
- **Satrapy**: A province of the ancient Persian empires, governed by a satrap.
- **Ziggurat**: A massive stepped pyramid structure, a type of temple tower common in ancient Mesopotamia and Elam.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The nature of Uruk influence at Susa is debated: was it colonial domination, selective cultural adoption, or evidence of a long-lasting, shared cultural sphere?
- The comparative chronology of Susa and Uruk periods remains disputed among scholars.
- The extent of Susa's independence versus its integration into larger empires (Uruk, Akkadian, Achaemenid) during various periods is a recurring question.
- The precise reasons for the city's repeated destruction and rebuilding cycles, and the relative impact of each, are subjects of ongoing archaeological and historical study.

# Mediterranean and Middle East theatre of World War II

## Overview
The Mediterranean and Middle East theatre was a major, interconnected theatre of World War II, encompassing land, sea, and air campaigns across Southern Europe, North Africa, the Middle East, and the Horn of Africa. It was the longest-duration theatre, beginning with Italy's declaration of war in June 1940 and ending with the final Axis surrender in Italy in May 1945. The conflict was driven by Italian imperial ambitions and the Allied goal of maintaining the status quo, ultimately destroying the Italian Empire and significantly diverting German resources.

## Timeline
- **10 June 1940** — Italy declares war on the UK and France, starting the theatre's combat.
- **28 October 1940** — Italy invades Greece, triggering the Greco-Italian War.
- **April 1941** — Germany invades and conquers Yugoslavia and Greece.
- **18 May 1941** — Italian forces in East Africa surrender, ending the East African Campaign.
- **12 November 1941** — British victory at the Battle of Taranto damages the Italian fleet.
- **21 January 1942** — Axis forces recapture Benghazi in North Africa.
- **23 October 1942** — Second Battle of El Alamein begins, a major Allied turning point.
- **8 November 1942** — Allied Operation Torch landings in French North Africa.
- **13 May 1943** — Axis forces in Tunisia surrender, ending the North African Campaign.
- **10 July 1943** — Allies invade Sicily (Operation Husky).
- **8 September 1943** — Italy signs an armistice with the Allies; Germany occupies much of the country.
- **2 May 1945** — All Axis forces in Italy surrender at Caserta.

## Body

### Italian Imperial Ambitions and Early Operations
Fascist Italy, under Benito Mussolini, sought hegemony in the Mediterranean-Danubian-Balkan region and an empire from Gibraltar to Hormuz, viewing itself as a "prisoner in the Mediterranean" controlled by Britain and France. After invading Ethiopia in 1935 and Albania in 1939, Italy entered the war in June 1940. Initial operations included invading France, bombing Malta, and attacking Allied shipping. The invasion of Greece in October 1940 failed, requiring German intervention. In North Africa, an Italian invasion of Egypt was halted and reversed by the British Operation Compass, which destroyed the Italian 10th Army by February 1941.

### Axis Success and Expansion (1941)
Germany intervened to support its struggling ally. In North Africa, the German Afrika Korps under Erwin Rommel arrived and, despite being subordinate to Italian command, quickly went on the offensive, pushing the British back to the Egyptian border. In the Balkans, the German invasion of Yugoslavia and Greece in April 1941 swiftly conquered both countries, leading to the British evacuation from Greece and the subsequent Battle of Crete. In East Africa, British forces from Kenya and Sudan defeated the Italians, restoring the Ethiopian Empire by May 1941.

### The Middle East and Strategic Supply Lines
The theatre expanded into the Middle East due to Axis interference. In Iraq, a pro-Axis coup in April 1941 led to the Anglo-Iraqi War, which the British won by May. This prompted the Allied invasion of Vichy French-controlled Syria and Lebanon (Operation Exporter) in June-July 1941 to secure the region. In August 1941, the Allies invaded neutral Iran to secure supply lines to the Soviet Union and its oil fields, deposing the pro-German Shah. Axis air raids also targeted British Mandatory Palestine.

### The Battle for the Mediterranean and Allied Turnaround
Control of the Mediterranean Sea was vital. The British fortress of Malta, under constant Axis siege, served as a base for attacks on Axis supply convoys to North Africa. After early setbacks, the Allies turned the tide. The Second Battle of El Alamein (October-November 1942) ended the Axis threat to Egypt. The subsequent Operation Torch landings in French North Africa (November 1942) opened a second front. Axis forces in Tunisia were squeezed between Allied armies and surrendered in May 1943, ending the war in Africa.

### The Italian Campaign and Civil War
The Allies invaded Sicily in July 1943, leading to the deposition of Mussolini and Italy's armistice in September. Germany quickly occupied most of the peninsula, rescued Mussolini, and established the puppet Italian Social Republic in the north. This sparked the Italian Civil War, with the Italian Co-Belligerent Army fighting alongside the Allies against German and Italian Fascist forces. The Allied advance up the mountainous peninsula was slow, halted for months at the Gustav Line. Rome was captured in June 1944, but the final Allied offensive did not break the Gothic Line until spring 1945. Mussolini was executed by partisans on 28 April 1945, and all Axis forces in Italy surrendered on 2 May.

## Terms
*   **Mediterranean Theatre:** The interconnected area of WWII operations spanning Southern Europe, North Africa, the Middle East, and the Horn of Africa.
*   **Afrika Korps:** The German expeditionary force in North Africa, commanded by Erwin Rommel from 1941.
*   **Operation Compass:** The first large-scale Allied military operation of the Western Desert Campaign, which destroyed the Italian 10th Army in Egypt and Libya (1940-41).
*   **Operation Torch:** The Allied amphibious invasion of French North Africa (Morocco and Algeria) in November 1942.
*   **El Alamein:** Site of two decisive battles (1942) in Egypt that halted and then reversed the Axis advance into the Middle East.
*   **Italian Social Republic:** The German puppet state in northern Italy (1943-45), also known as the Republic of Salò, headed by the rescued Benito Mussolini.
*   **Italian Co-Belligerent Army:** The army of the Kingdom of Italy that fought alongside the Allies after the September 1943 armistice.
*   **Gustav Line:** A formidable German defensive line across central Italy, whose capture was key to the Allied advance in 1944.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Strategic Impact of the Balkans:** Historians debate the extent to which the German invasion of Greece and Yugoslavia in 1941 delayed the invasion of the Soviet Union and contributed to its failure. Hitler blamed Mussolini, while some historians argue this deflected blame from his own strategic errors.
*   **The "Soft Underbelly" Strategy:** There is ongoing debate about the strategic wisdom of the Allied focus on the Mediterranean and Italy (the "soft underbelly" strategy) versus a more direct cross-Channel invasion in 1943 or 1944.
*   **Italian Civil War:** The classification of the conflict between Italian partisans/co-belligerents and Fascist forces as a "civil war" is a term that gained traction in historiography later, particularly after Claudio Pavone's 1991 work, and remains a subject of historical and political discussion in Italy.

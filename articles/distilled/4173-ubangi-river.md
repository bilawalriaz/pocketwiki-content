# Ubangi River

## Overview
The Ubangi River is a major waterway in Central Africa and the largest right-bank tributary of the Congo River. It serves as a critical transport route and forms international borders between the Central African Republic, the Democratic Republic of the Congo, and the Republic of the Congo. Its historical connection to the Lake Chad basin has led to modern proposals for large-scale water diversion projects.

## Timeline
- **Early Pleistocene** — The Ubangi's upper reaches are believed to have been captured by the Congo River from the Chari River system.
- **1960s** — The "Transaqua" plan is proposed to divert Ubangi waters to the Chari River to replenish Lake Chad.
- **1980s-1990s** — Inter-basin water transfer schemes are proposed by engineer J. Umolu and the firm Bonifica.
- **1994** — The Lake Chad Basin Commission (LCBC) proposes a similar diversion project.
- **March 2008** — Heads of state of LCBC member countries commit to the diversion project at a summit.
- **April 2008** — The LCBC advertises a request for proposals for a feasibility study.

## Body

### Geography and Hydrology
The Ubangi River begins at the confluence of the Mbomou and Uele Rivers. It flows west, forming the border between the Central African Republic (CAR) and the Democratic Republic of the Congo (DRC). After passing through Bangui, the capital of the CAR, it turns southwest and then south, forming the border between the DRC and the Republic of the Congo. It joins the Congo River at Liranga. The river is approximately 1,060 km (660 mi) long, or 2,270 km (1,410 mi) when measured with its longest tributary, the Uele. Its drainage basin covers about 651,915 km² (251,706 mi²). Discharge at Bangui varies from about 800 m³/s to 11,000 m³/s, with an average of ~4,000 m³/s. The mean annual discharge at its mouth is around ~6,000 m³/s.

### Geologic History and Transport
Geologists believe the Ubangi's upper course originally flowed into the Chari River and Lake Chad before being captured by the Congo River in the early Pleistocene. Today, together with the Congo, it forms a vital transport artery for river boats connecting Bangui and Brazzaville.

### Lake Chad Replenishment Project
A recurring proposal, first named "Transaqua" in the 1960s, seeks to divert water from the Ubangi to the Chari River to revitalize Lake Chad. The goal is to support fishing and agriculture for millions in the Sahel. The Lake Chad Basin Commission (LCBC) formally proposed such a project in 1994. In 2008, member state leaders committed to the plan, and the LCBC began seeking feasibility studies.

## Terms
- **Tributary**: A river or stream that flows into a larger river.
- **Drainage basin**: An area of land where all water drains into a common outlet, like a river.
- **Discharge**: The volume of water flowing through a river channel per unit of time, measured in cubic meters per second (m³/s).
- **Inter-basin water transfer**: The engineered movement of water from one river basin to another.
- **Pleistocene**: A geological epoch lasting from about 2.6 million to 11,700 years ago, known for repeated glaciations.
- **Captured (river capture)**: A geomorphological process where a river's headwaters are diverted into a different drainage system.

## Debates and Open questions
The primary open question is the feasibility and impact of the proposed inter-basin transfer to replenish Lake Chad. The source text notes the project has been proposed repeatedly since the 1960s, with formal commitments and studies initiated as recently as 2008, but does not report on its completion or outcome, implying it remains a debated and unresolved proposal.

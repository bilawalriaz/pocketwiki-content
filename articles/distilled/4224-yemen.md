# Yemen

## Overview
Yemen, officially the Republic of Yemen, is a country in West Asia on the southern Arabian Peninsula. It has been a crossroads of civilizations for over 7,000 years due to its strategic location. The modern state was formed in 1990 by the unification of North and South Yemen. Since 2014, it has been engulfed in a devastating civil war, creating the world's worst humanitarian crisis.

## Body

### Ancient and Islamic History
Yemen's early history features powerful kingdoms like Saba (biblical Sheba), Himyar, and others. The Himyarite Kingdom unified the region by AD 275. Christianity arrived in the 4th century, followed by Islam in the 7th century. Yemen became a center of Islamic learning, and its tribes played a key role in early Islamic conquests. From the 9th to 16th centuries, various dynasties ruled, including the Zaidi imamate (founded 897), the Sulayhid dynasty (notable for Queen Arwa), and the Rasulid dynasty, which made Taiz a major Islamic learning center.

### Colonial and Ottoman Era
The Ottoman Empire first conquered Yemen in 1538 to protect trade routes and holy cities. Their control was mainly in the coastal lowlands, while Zaidi imams resisted in the highlands. The British occupied Aden in 1839, creating a protectorate in the south. The Ottomans reasserted control in the 19th century but faced constant rebellion from Imam Yahya Hamidaddin, leading to a 1911 treaty recognizing his autonomy in the north.

### Unification and Modern Conflict
After World War I, Imam Yahya ruled the north as the Kingdom of Yemen. In 1962, a coup sparked the North Yemen Civil war, ending with the Yemen Arab Republic. South Yemen gained independence from Britain in 1967 as a communist state. The two states unified in 1990 under President Ali Abdullah Saleh. Political tensions and a brief civil war in 1994 followed. Saleh ruled until 2011, when he resigned after mass protests. His successor, Abdrabbuh Mansur Hadi, faced a Houthi insurgency that escalated into a full-scale civil war in 2014. A Saudi-led coalition intervened in 2015. The conflict has caused massive displacement, famine, and cholera outbreaks, with over 377,000 deaths estimated by the UN by 2021.

### Geography and Economy
Yemen covers 455,503 km² and has four main geographic regions: the coastal Tihamah plain, the western highlands (with the highest rainfall), the central highlands (where Sanaa is located), and the eastern desert. The economy is one of the poorest in the Middle East, historically reliant on oil exports and agriculture. Severe water scarcity is a critical challenge, with groundwater tables dropping dramatically. The civil war has caused GDP to fall by over 50% since 2014.

### Society and Demographics
Yemen's population is about 34.7 million, predominantly Arab Muslims. The main Muslim sects are Sunni (53%) and Zaidi Shia (45%). The population is very young, with 46% under 15. Yemen is a largely tribal society with significant internal displacement due to conflict. Arabic is the official language, and several Modern South Arabian languages are also spoken.

## Terms
*   **Zaidi Imamate:** A theocratic state based on the Zaidi branch of Shia Islam, historically ruling parts of northern Yemen.
*   **Tihamah:** The arid coastal plain along Yemen's Red Sea coastline.
*   **Jizyah:** A tax levied on non-Muslim subjects in an Islamic state.
*   **Sharia:** Islamic law, which serves as the main source of legislation in Yemen.
*   **Houthi movement:** A Zaidi Shia political and armed group that controls much of northern Yemen.
*   **Presidential Leadership Council (PLC):** The internationally recognized government of Yemen formed in 2022.
*   **Supreme Political Council (SPC):** The governing body for Houthi-controlled territory in Yemen.
*   **Qat (Khat):** A psychoactive plant widely chewed in Yemen, which consumes a large portion of the country's water.

## Debates and open questions
*   The exact etymology of "Yemen" is debated, with theories linking it to "south," "right," or "blessed."
*   The political future of Yemen remains uncertain, with competing governments (PLC and SPC) and the role of external powers like Saudi Arabia, the UAE, and Iran being central issues.
*   The long-term impact of the civil war on Yemen's social fabric, tribal structures, and state institutions is a subject of ongoing analysis.

# Etruscan civilization

## Overview
The Etruscans were a pre-Roman civilization in ancient Italy, flourishing from c. 900 BC to 1 BC. They formed a federation of city-states in a region called Etruria, with a distinct language and culture that significantly influenced early Rome. Their origins have been debated for centuries, but modern archaeological and genetic evidence strongly indicates they were an indigenous population of the Italian peninsula.

## Timeline
- **c. 900 BC** — Earliest evidence of identifiably Etruscan culture (Villanovan culture).
- **c. 700 BC** — Earliest known Etruscan writing appears; adoption of alphabet from Greek colonies.
- **c. 540 BC** — Battle of Alalia; Etruscans and Carthaginians ally against Greeks, leading to Etruscan decline in the western Mediterranean.
- **c. 500 BC** — Etruscan civilization reaches its maximum territorial extent.
- **508 BC** — Fall of the Etruscan kingship in Rome; Etruscan influence wanes.
- **474 BC** — Etruscan defeat by Syracuse at the Battle of Cumae.
- **396 BC** — Rome destroys the Etruscan city of Veii.
- **310 BC** — Etruscans defeated by Romans at Lake Vadimone.
- **90 BC** — Etruscans granted Roman citizenship.
- **27 BC** — All Etruscan territory incorporated into the Roman Empire.

## Body

### Origins and Identity
The Etruscans' origins were a subject of ancient debate, with Greek and Roman writers proposing migrations from Lydia or Greece. However, modern consensus, based on archaeological continuity from the Bronze Age Villanovan culture and recent archaeogenetic studies, identifies them as an autochthonous (indigenous) population. Genetic analysis shows they were genetically similar to their Latin neighbors, with no evidence of recent migration from Anatolia or the Eastern Mediterranean. They called themselves *Rasenna* (the people) and were known to Greeks as Tyrrhenians and to Romans as Tuscī or Etruscī.

### Society and Government
Etruscan society was organized into city-states, each ruled by aristocratic families. A loose confederation, the Etruscan League (Dodecapolis), likely comprised 12 major cities that met annually at the Fanum Voltumnae sanctuary. Government blended political and religious authority; the king (*lucomo*) was also a chief priest, and magistrates interpreted divine will through haruspicy (divining from entrails) and augury (observing birds). Women appear to have enjoyed greater public freedom than in contemporary Greece or early Rome.

### Economy and Expansion
The Etruscan economy was based on agriculture, mining (especially copper and iron), and Mediterranean trade. Their strategic coastal position and metal wealth brought them into contact—and conflict—with Greeks and Carthaginians. Expansion occurred north into the Po Valley and south into Campania. Their power declined after defeats by Greeks in the 5th century BC and was progressively dismantled by the expanding Roman Republic from the 4th century BC onward.

### Culture and Language
Etruscan culture was heavily influenced by Greek art and religion but maintained distinct characteristics. Their religion was polytheistic, with deities like Tinia (sky god) and Uni (goddess). They created elaborate tomb paintings and terracotta sculptures. The Etruscan language, written in an alphabet adapted from Greek, is a pre-Indo-European isolate related only to Raetic and Lemnian. About 13,000 inscriptions survive, but the language is only partly understood, limiting knowledge of their literature and society.

## Terms
- **Autochthonous**: Indigenous; originating in the place where found.
- **Villanovan culture**: The early Iron Age culture (c. 900-700 BC) considered the earliest phase of Etruscan civilization.
- **Dodecapolis**: The legendary league of twelve Etruscan city-states.
- **Haruspicy**: A form of divination involving the inspection of the entrails of sacrificed animals.
- **Orientalizing period**: A phase (c. 750-600 BC) of strong artistic and cultural influence from the Eastern Mediterranean.
- **Tyrsenian language family**: A proposed language isolate grouping Etruscan with the Raetic and Lemnian languages.
- **Rasenna**: The endonym (self-name) of the Etruscan people, meaning "the people."
- **Liber Linteus**: The longest surviving Etruscan text, a ritual book written on linen.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Origins**: While the indigenous theory is now dominant, the exact process of ethnogenesis from the Villanovan culture and the nature of the Tyrsenian linguistic connection remain areas of study.
- **Language and Literature**: The Etruscan language is only partially deciphered. The extent and nature of their lost literary tradition, referenced by Roman authors, is unknown.
- **Political Structure**: The precise organization and function of the Etruscan League, and the balance of power between its member cities, are not fully understood due to limited native sources.

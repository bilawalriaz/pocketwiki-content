# Beaufort Sea

## Overview
The Beaufort Sea is a marginal sea of the Arctic Ocean, north of Alaska, Yukon, and the Northwest Territories. It is a region of extreme climate, significant ecological importance, and major hydrocarbon resources, but its development is complicated by an ongoing maritime border dispute between the United States and Canada.

## Timeline
- **1825** — Treaty of Saint Petersburg establishes the 141st meridian as the land boundary between Russian Alaska and British territory.
- **1950s–1980s** — Major petroleum and natural gas reserves are discovered beneath the sea's shelf.
- **1972** — Offshore drilling for hydrocarbons begins in the Beaufort Sea.
- **1984** — The Amauligak field, the largest known oil deposit, is discovered.
- **2009** — The U.S. announces a moratorium on commercial fishing in the Beaufort Sea north of Alaska.
- **2011** — Canada and the Inuvialuit sign a memorandum of understanding for ocean management.
- **2014** — Canada announces no new commercial fisheries will be considered until sustainable stocks are proven.
- **2017** — The U.S. approves a plan for Eni to drill exploration wells on Spy Island.

## Body

### Geography and Climate
The Beaufort Sea is defined by the International Hydrographic Organization, with its limits running from Point Barrow, Alaska, to Prince Patrick Island and Banks Island. The coastline is low-lying, with a narrow continental shelf that widens near the Mackenzie River delta. The region has a severe climate, with permafrost extending year-round below the top layer of soil. The sea is frozen for most of the year; historically, a narrow strip of open water appeared near the coast in late summer, but due to climate change, the ice-free area has greatly enlarged in recent decades. The water column is stratified into four distinct layers with varying temperatures and salinity.

### Hydrocarbons and the Border Dispute
The Beaufort Sea holds significant oil and gas reserves, with exploration beginning in the 1950s. Major fields include Taglu, Parson Lake, and the large Amauligak field. Development is hindered by remote location and the unresolved maritime border dispute with the U.S. Canada claims the boundary follows the 141st meridian west from the land border, based on the 1825 treaty. The U.S. argues for a boundary based on the principle of equidistance from the coasts. This creates a disputed wedge of about 21,000 km², believed to contain substantial hydrocarbon resources. Both nations agree the boundary should be "equitable" but disagree on the principle to determine it. The dispute has not been formally resolved, though both countries cooperate on environmental measures.

### Ecology and Human Activity
The sea's ecosystem is relatively simple, starting with phytoplankton and ice algae. Key species include polar cod, which is a major food source, and the Eastern Beaufort beluga whale population, estimated at 39,000 and considered stable. Two polar bear subpopulations depend on the sea ice for hunting seals. Human population density is very low. The Inuvialuit and other Indigenous groups have practiced subsistence fishing and hunting for millennia, with evidence of Arctic char bones at 4,000-year-old settlements. Commercial activity is dominated by oil and gas, notably at the Prudhoe Bay Oil Field in Alaska. To protect the ecosystem, the U.S. enacted a fishing moratorium in 2009, and Canada has established Marine Protected Areas like the Tarium Niryutait at the Mackenzie delta.

## Terms
- **Marginal sea:** A partially enclosed sea adjacent to a larger ocean.
- **Beaufort Gyre:** The dominant clockwise ocean current in the Beaufort Sea, which historically helped form thick, multi-year sea ice.
- **Permafrost:** Ground that remains frozen year-round.
- **Natural prolongation principle:** The legal argument that a nation's maritime boundary should extend naturally from its land territory.
- **Equidistance principle:** The legal argument that a maritime boundary should be a median line equidistant from the coasts of neighboring nations.
- **Marine Protected Area (MPA):** A designated zone where human activities are restricted to protect marine ecosystems.
- **Multi-year ice:** Sea ice that has survived at least one summer melt season, making it thicker and stronger.
- **Rotten ice:** A term for structurally weak, thinner ice that has undergone changes due to climate warming.

## Debates and open questions
- The primary debate is the unresolved U.S.-Canada maritime boundary dispute, centered on whether the 1825 treaty's 141st meridian extends into the sea or if equidistance should apply.
- The potential impact of climate change on the sea's ice cover, ecosystem, and future resource accessibility is a major ongoing concern.
- The sustainability of beluga whale hunting in the eastern Beaufort Sea is considered, with the 2016 assessment finding it sustainable, but long-term effects of offshore oil exploration on the population are monitored.

# Zhejiang

## Overview
Zhejiang is a coastal province in East China, historically and economically significant as a center of trade, culture, and private enterprise. It is known for its hilly terrain, long coastline, and the Qiantang River, from which it derives its name. The province has been a major economic driver, particularly since China's reform and opening up, and is home to important cities like Hangzhou, Ningbo, and Wenzhou.

## Timeline
- **6000–5000 BC** — Kuahuqiao culture, an early Neolithic settlement, exists in the Hangzhou area.
- **5500 BC** — Hemudu culture begins in the region.
- **3400 BC** — Liangzhu culture begins.
- **Spring and Autumn period** — The Kingdom of Yue controls the area of modern Zhejiang.
- **473 BC** — King Goujian of Yue annexes the rival Kingdom of Wu.
- **333 BC** — The state of Chu destroys the Kingdom of Yue.
- **222 BC** — The Qin Empire annexes the area, organizing it into Kuaiji Commandery.
- **589** — The settlement of Qiantang is renamed Hangzhou.
- **907** — The Kingdom of Wuyue is established with Hangzhou as its capital.
- **1127** — Hangzhou becomes the capital of the Southern Song dynasty.
- **1843** — Ningbo is opened as a treaty port following the Treaty of Nanking.
- **1949** — The People's Republic of China captures most of Zhejiang after the Chinese Civil War.
- **1955** — The PRC captures the remaining offshore islands, bringing the entire province under its control.
- **1978** — China's reform and opening up begins, leading to Zhejiang's rapid economic growth.

## Body
### History
The area was inhabited by Neolithic cultures and later controlled by the Kingdom of Yue during the Spring and Autumn period. After its annexation by the Qin Empire in 222 BC, the region saw periods of prosperity and turmoil. It was a wealthy economic heartland during the Six Dynasties, Sui, and Tang periods, with the Grand Canal transporting its resources north. The Kingdom of Wuyue (907–978) brought stability and cultural flourishing. Under the Southern Song dynasty (1127–1279), Hangzhou became the capital and a global metropolis. In the modern era, the province was a battleground during the Taiping Rebellion and World War II, and later a base for the Kuomintang before the Communist takeover. Under Mao Zedong, the economy stagnated, but post-1978 reforms transformed it into a powerhouse of private enterprise and digital innovation.

### Geography and Climate
Zhejiang is predominantly hilly (about 70% of its area), with higher altitudes in the south and west. It has the longest coastline of any mainland Chinese province and over 3,000 islands. The Qiantang River flows through the province, and Hangzhou Bay separates Shanghai from Ningbo. The climate is humid subtropical with four distinct seasons, hot summers, and vulnerability to typhoons.

### Economy
Zhejiang is one of China's wealthiest provinces, ranking fourth in GDP. Its economy is driven by a strong private sector, manufacturing (electromechanical, textiles, chemicals), and a leading digital economy centered on companies like Alibaba. The "Zhejiang model" emphasizes entrepreneurship, small businesses, and infrastructure investment. Key agricultural products include rice, tea (notably Longjing), and seafood from the Zhoushan fishery. Major ports include Ningbo, Wenzhou, and Zhoushan.

### Culture and Society
The population is predominantly Han Chinese, with the largest subgroup speaking Wu Chinese dialects. The province is culturally diverse, with distinct local opera traditions like Yueju. Zhejiang cuisine is one of China's eight great culinary traditions, known for fresh ingredients and regional styles. Religiously, folk religions, Buddhism, and Taoism are predominant, with a significant Protestant minority, especially in Wenzhou. The province is a leader in research and education, with top-ranked cities for scientific output.

## Terms
- **Wu Chinese**: A major group of Sinitic languages spoken in Zhejiang and neighboring regions.
- **Grand Canal**: A historic canal system connecting Hangzhou to northern China, used for transporting grain and resources.
- **Kingdom of Yue**: An ancient state that controlled the Zhejiang area during the Spring and Autumn period.
- **Kingdom of Wuyue**: A 10th-century kingdom that brought stability and prosperity to the region after the Tang dynasty.
- **Zhejiang model**: An economic development model based on entrepreneurship, small businesses, and market responsiveness.
- **Longjing tea**: A prestigious green tea originating from Hangzhou.
- **Yueju**: A form of Chinese opera originating in Shengzhou, Zhejiang.
- **Nature Index**: A database tracking scientific research output by city and institution.

## Debates and Open questions
- The sustainability of the "Zhejiang model" is debated, with concerns about its efficiency, resource demands, and ability to transition to high-tech industries.
- The implementation of religious regulations, such as the "Three Remodelings and One Demolition" operations, has been controversial, particularly regarding the demolition of religious sites and removal of crosses.

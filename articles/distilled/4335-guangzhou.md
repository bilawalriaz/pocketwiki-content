# Guangzhou

## Overview
Guangzhou (Canton) is the capital of Guangdong province in southern China, a major port city on the Pearl River with over 2,200 years of history. It was a critical terminus of the Silk Road and, for centuries, the primary legal port for Sino-European trade under the Canton System. Today, it is a global Alpha city, a leading financial and manufacturing hub, and the center of the world's most populous metropolitan area, the Guangdong–Hong Kong–Macau Greater Bay Area.

## Timeline
- **214 BC** — Panyu, the precursor to Guangzhou, is founded on the Pearl River.
- **204 BC** — Zhao Tuo establishes the Nanyue kingdom with Panyu as its capital.
- **111 BC** — Han dynasty annexes Nanyue after a major war.
- **AD 226** — Panyu becomes the seat of Guang Prefecture, giving the city its modern name.
- **758 & 878** — Arab/Persian pirates sack the city; later, rebel Huang Chao massacres foreign communities, closing the port for 50 years.
- **1757–1842** — The Canton System restricts all foreign trade to Guangzhou's Thirteen Factories.
- **1841** — British forces capture Guangzhou during the First Opium War.
- **1923–1926** — Sun Yat-sen and the KMT use Guangzhou as a revolutionary base, allied with the USSR and CCP.
- **1938** — Japanese forces occupy the city.
- **1949** — The People's Liberation Army captures Guangzhou.
- **1957** — The first Canton Fair is held, establishing the city's modern trade identity.
- **2010** — Guangzhou hosts the Asian Games and Asian Para Games.

## Body

### Historical Significance and Trade
Founded as Panyu in 214 BC, the city was the capital of the Nanyue kingdom before its annexation by the Han dynasty. It became a major international port during the Tang dynasty, though relations were sometimes violent, as seen in the 878 massacre that closed the port for decades. Under the Qing dynasty's Canton System (1757–1842), Guangzhou was the sole legal port for Western trade, centered on the Thirteen Factories. This monopoly ended after the British capture during the First Opium War, leading to the rise of Shanghai and Hong Kong. The city remained a revolutionary base for Sun Yat-shek's Kuomintang (KMT) in the 1920s, where the KMT-CCP alliance was formed with Soviet help.

### Modern Economy and Status
Since China's opening up in the 1980s, Guangzhou has leveraged its proximity to Hong Kong to become a leading manufacturing and commercial hub. It is the host of the Canton Fair, China's oldest and largest trade fair. The city is a major financial center, ranking 21st globally in the 2020 Global Financial Centres Index, and is classified as an Alpha (global first-tier) city. Administratively, it is a sub-provincial city and one of China's nine National Central Cities. Its economy is part of the Pearl River Delta Economic Zone, and it anchors the Greater Bay Area, a metropolitan region of about 70 million people.

### Demographics and Culture
Guangzhou has a large and mobile population, with 18.68 million registered residents as of the 2020 census, a 47% increase from 2010. A significant portion are migrants from other provinces. The native language is Cantonese, though Mandarin use is increasing among younger generations. The city's culture is predominantly Cantonese, featuring distinct cuisine, opera, and embroidery. It is also home to Hakka communities and has a history of religious diversity, including the ancient Huaisheng Mosque and the Sacred Heart Cathedral.

### Transportation and Infrastructure
Guangzhou is a major transportation hub. Its Baiyun International Airport briefly became the world's busiest in 2020. The Guangzhou Metro, opened in 1997, is one of the world's busiest and longest systems. The city is a terminus for several high-speed rail lines, including the Wuhan–Guangzhou line. It also has an extensive Bus Rapid Transit (BRT) system and maintains ferry links to Hong Kong and Macau.

## Terms
*   **Canton System:** The Qing dynasty policy (1757–1842) that restricted all Western maritime trade to the port of Guangzhou, conducted through a guild of Chinese merchants known as the Cohong.
*   **Thirteen Factories:** The area in Guangzhou where foreign traders were confined to live and work under the Canton System.
*   **Nanyue:** An ancient kingdom founded in 204 BC with its capital at Panyu (modern Guangzhou), covering parts of southern China and northern Vietnam.
*   **Greater Bay Area:** A megalopolis consisting of nine cities in Guangdong province plus Hong Kong and Macau, with Guangzhou as a central hub.
*   **Cantonese:** A variety of Chinese (Yue Chinese) native to Guangzhou and Guangdong province, distinct from Mandarin.
*   **Hukou:** China's household registration system, which ties access to social services to one's official place of residence.
*   **Floating Population:** The large number of migrants living in a city without official local hukou registration.
*   **Alpha City:** A global first-tier city, as classified by the Globalization and World Cities Research Network, indicating high integration into the global economy.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Language Shift:** There is ongoing concern and debate about the declining use of Cantonese among younger generations in favor of Mandarin, attributed to educational policies and the prestige of Mandarin-speaking cities like Shenzhen. Studies show varying levels of fluency and daily use.
*   **Population Capacity:** Officials and researchers have noted that Guangzhou's actual population, including migrants, far exceeds the official registered figure, placing severe strain on infrastructure and public services, raising questions about sustainable urban planning and capacity.

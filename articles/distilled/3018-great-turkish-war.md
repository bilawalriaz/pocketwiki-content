# Great Turkish War

## Overview
The Great Turkish War (1683–1699) was a major conflict between the Ottoman Empire and a European Holy League. It marked a decisive turning point, resulting in the first substantial territorial losses for the Ottomans in Europe and establishing Russia as a participant in Western European alliances. The war's conclusion with the Treaty of Karlowitz reshaped the map of southeastern Europe and signaled the beginning of Ottoman military decline.

## Timeline
- **14 Jul 1683** — Ottoman siege of Vienna begins.
- **12 Sep 1683** — Battle of Vienna: Holy League relief force defeats Ottomans.
- **1684** — Holy League formed (HRE, Poland-Lithuania, Venice).
- **1686** — Russia joins the Holy League; Buda captured from Ottomans.
- **1687** — Second Battle of Mohács: decisive Habsburg victory.
- **1688** — France breaks truce, starting the Nine Years' War.
- **1690** — Ottomans recapture Belgrade.
- **1691** — Battle of Slankamen: Habsburg victory secures Hungary.
- **1695-96** — Russia captures Azov from Ottomans.
- **11 Sep 1697** — Battle of Zenta: catastrophic Ottoman defeat.
- **22 Jan 1699** — Treaty of Karlowitz signed, ending the war.

## Body

**Origins and the Battle of Vienna**
Following earlier conflicts with Poland-Lithuania, the Ottoman Empire, under Grand Vizier Kara Mustafa Pasha, besieged Vienna in 1683, seeking to control key trade routes. The city's garrison held until a relief army led by Polish King John III Sobieski arrived. On 12 September 1683, Sobieski commanded the largest cavalry charge in history, breaking the Ottoman lines and lifting the siege. This victory halted Ottoman expansion and prompted the formation of the Holy League.

**The Holy League's Campaigns and French Intervention**
The Holy League, initiated by Pope Innocent XI, included the Holy Roman Empire, Poland-Lithuania, Venice, and later Russia. Their campaigns recaptured Buda in 1686 and won major battles like Mohács (1687). However, France, allied with the Ottomans, intervened by attacking the Holy Roman Empire in 1688, triggering the Nine Years' War. This diverted crucial Imperial resources westward, allowing the Ottomans to recapture Belgrade in 1690 and prolonging the conflict.

**Theater-Specific Conflicts**
The war was fought on multiple fronts. In the Balkans, Habsburg forces and Serbian militia fought for control of Serbia and Kosovo. In the Peloponnese, Venice, allied with Greek irregulars, conquered the Morea peninsula between 1685 and 1687. On the northern front, Poland-Lithuania fought to reclaim Podolia, while Russia's initial Crimean campaigns failed but later succeeded in capturing the fortress of Azov (1696).

**Conclusion and Treaty of Karlowitz**
The war's decisive moment came at the Battle of Zenta (1697), where Habsburg forces under Prince Eugene of Savoy annihilated an Ottoman army. This crushing defeat forced the Ottomans to sue for peace. The 1699 Treaty of Karlowitz formalized major territorial losses: Hungary, Transylvania, and Slavonia went to the Habsburgs; Podolia returned to Poland; and Venice gained the Morea and parts of Dalmatia. The treaty marked a permanent shift in the European balance of power.

## Terms
*   **Holy League:** A coalition formed in 1684 by Pope Innocent XI, primarily comprising the Holy Roman Empire, Poland-Lithuania, and Venice, to counter Ottoman expansion.
*   **Grand Vizier:** The chief minister and commander-in-chief of the Ottoman Empire's military. Kara Mustafa Pasha held this role during the siege of Vienna.
*   **Winged Hussars:** The elite heavy cavalry of the Polish-Lithuanian Commonwealth, famed for their distinctive feathered armor and decisive charge at Vienna.
*   **Treaty of Karlowitz (1699):** The peace agreement that ended the Great Turkish War, resulting in significant Ottoman territorial losses in Europe.
*   **Morea:** The historical name for the Peloponnese peninsula in southern Greece, conquered by Venice during the war.
*   **Azov:** A strategic Ottoman fortress on the Sea of Azov, captured by Russia in 1696, giving it access to the Black Sea.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   The extent of participation by the Crimean Tatar cavalry, allied with the Ottomans, at the Battle of Vienna is debated.
*   The impact of the concurrent Nine Years' War (1688–1697) on the Great Turkish War's duration and outcome is a key scholarly consideration, as it diverted major Imperial resources.
*   The long-term significance of Russia's first formal alliance with Western European powers, initiated by its entry into the Holy League, is analyzed as a pivotal moment in European geopolitics.

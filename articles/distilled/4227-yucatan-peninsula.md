# Yucatán Peninsula

## Overview
The Yucatán Peninsula is a large, low-lying limestone landmass in southeast Mexico, Guatemala, and Belize, separating the Gulf of Mexico from the Caribbean Sea. It is geologically significant as the site of the Chicxulub asteroid impact that ended the Cretaceous Period and was the heartland of the ancient Maya civilization. Today, its economy is driven by tourism, and its population includes a substantial Maya and Mestizo community.

## Timeline
- **c. 66 million years ago** — Chicxulub asteroid impact occurs, creating a massive crater.
- **At least 9,900 years ago** — A woman lives in the Chan Hol cave near Tulum, as evidenced by a skeleton found in 2020.
- **9th century** — A widespread political collapse occurs in the central Maya region, leading to a northward population shift.
- **1517** — Francisco Hernández de Córdoba arrives on the peninsula, initiating Spanish contact.
- **1519–1521** — Hernán Cortés references the name "Yucatán" in his letters to Charles V.
- **1840s** — The Caste War of Yucatán begins, driving non-natives from the region.
- **1901** — The Mexican federal army conquers the independent Maya state of Chan Santa Cruz.
- **1970s** — The peninsula's economy begins reorienting towards tourism.

## Body

### Geology and Formation
The peninsula is the exposed portion of the Yucatán Platform, composed almost entirely of porous limestone, creating a flat karst landscape with widespread sinkholes called cenotes. It sits on the Maya Block of the North American plate. The deeply buried Chicxulub crater, centered off the north coast, is linked to the Cretaceous–Paleogene mass extinction. Evidence for the impact includes the "Ring of Cenotes," shocked quartz, and tektites.

### Prehistory and the Maya Civilization
Archaeological evidence, such as the 9,900-year-old skeleton from Chan Hol cave, indicates early human presence. The peninsula was the central location of the Maya Civilization, hosting major archaeological sites like Chichen Itza, Tulum, and Uxmal. A 9th-century collapse in the central Maya region led to a northward shift, with Chichen Itza rising in the Postclassic period. The Spanish conquest was prolonged, lasting nearly two centuries due to fierce Maya resistance.

### Climate and Ecology
The peninsula has a tropical climate, ranging from semi-arid in the northwest to humid in the south, with rainfall varying seasonally. It is vulnerable to hurricanes and experiences short, intense storms called nortes. Vegetation varies from dry forests in the north to evergreen rainforest in the south. The largest continuous tracts of tropical rainforest in Central America are found here, though they suffer from deforestation. The Mesoamerican Barrier Reef System stretches along its eastern coast.

### Governance and Economy
Politically, the peninsula comprises the Mexican states of Yucatán, Campeche, and Quintana Roo, Guatemala's Petén Department, and most of Belize. Historically, the economy was based on cattle ranching, logging, and henequen production. Since the 1970s, it has shifted to tourism, particularly in Quintana Roo, with Cancún and the Riviera Maya as major centers. Logging, chicle, and oil extraction are also important.

### Population
Population density and ethnic composition vary across the peninsula. The most populated area is Mérida in Yucatán state, while Quintana Roo is the least populated. The population consists largely of Maya and Mestizos (people of mixed Maya and European descent), and Mayan languages are widely spoken.

## Terms
- **Karst landscape:** A terrain formed from the dissolution of soluble rocks like limestone, characterized by sinkholes and caves.
- **Cenote:** A natural sinkhole resulting from the collapse of limestone bedrock, exposing groundwater; a key water source for the Maya.
- **Maya Block:** The southernmost tectonic block of the North American plate, upon which the Yucatán Peninsula sits.
- **Chicxulub crater:** The buried impact crater off the peninsula's coast, formed by an asteroid 66 million years ago.
- **Mestizo:** A person of mixed European and Indigenous American ancestry.
- **Henequen:** A fiber plant (sisal) historically cultivated in the region for rope production.
- **Chicle:** A natural gum tapped from sapodilla trees, historically used as the base for chewing gum.
- **Nortes:** Strong, short-lived storms that bring heavy rain and high winds to the peninsula.

## Debates and Open questions
- The proper etymology of the word "Yucatán" is widely debated, with theories ranging from a misinterpretation of a Mayan phrase ("I don't understand") to a connection with the yuca crop.
- The skull of the 9,900-year-old woman from Chan Hol cave shows trauma and deformities possibly caused by a bacterial relative of syphilis, leading to speculation about the circumstances of her death.
- The existence of at least two morphologically diverse early human groups in the region during the Pleistocene-Holocene transition is suggested by the distinctive features of early skeletons found in the peninsula's caves.

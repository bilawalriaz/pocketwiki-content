# Chelyabinsk

## Overview
Chelyabinsk is a major industrial city in Russia, serving as the administrative center of Chelyabinsk Oblast and the second-largest city in the Ural Federal District. Located east of the southern Ural Mountains along the Miass River, it is a key historical gateway to Siberia, known for its heavy industry, particularly metallurgy and military production, and for the 2013 superbolide meteor event.

## Timeline
- **2nd millennium BC** — Ancient Sintashta culture settlement of Arkaim exists in the region.
- **1736** — Fortress of Chelyaba is founded by Colonel Alexey Tevkelev.
- **1787** — Chelyabinsk is granted town status.
- **1892** — Samara-Zlatoust Railway connects Chelyabinsk to Moscow; construction of the Trans-Siberian Railway begins from the city.
- **1933** — Chelyabinsk Tractor Plant is built during Soviet industrialization.
- **1941-1945** — During WWII, the city becomes a major tank and ammunition producer, earning the nickname "Tankograd."
- **15 February 2013** — A superbolide meteor explodes over the city, injuring over 1,000 people.

## Body
### Ancient and Early History
The area contains the ruins of Arkaim, a fortified settlement of the Sintashta culture (c. 2nd millennium BC), which is associated with a proto-Indo-Iranian civilization. The modern city began with the founding of the Chelyaba fortress in 1736 to protect trade routes. It withstood a siege during Pugachev's Rebellion in 1774 but was captured in 1775. Chelyabinsk became a town in 1787.

### Growth as a Transportation Hub
Chelyabinsk was a small town until the late 19th century. The completion of the Samara-Zlatoust Railway in 1892 and the start of the Trans-Siberian Railway from the city transformed it into the main hub for travel to Siberia. Over 15 million people passed through in 15 years, fueling rapid population growth from 20,000 in 1897 to 70,000 by 1917. A "customs fracture" imposing duties between European and Asian Russia spurred local industry, leading to its nickname "the Chicago of the Urals."

### Soviet Industrialization and WWII
Under Soviet five-year plans in the 1930s, Chelyabinsk became a major industrial center with the construction of plants like the Chelyabinsk Tractor Plant (1933) and Metallurgical Plant. During WWII, factories were evacuated from western USSR to Chelyabinsk. The city produced 18,000 tanks and vast quantities of ammunition, earning the informal name "Tankograd" (Tank City).

### Modern Challenges and Economy
Post-Soviet economic difficulties in the 1990s and 2000s led to the closure of some industries. As of the 2020s, the city faces ongoing ecological and economic challenges. Nevertheless, it remains a major industrial center where industry accounts for nearly 40% of the gross municipal product, dominated by metallurgy and military production. Key enterprises include the Chelyabinsk Metallurgical Combine and the Chelyabinsk Tube Rolling Plant.

### The 2013 Meteor
On 15 February 2013, a superbolide meteor exploded at an altitude of 25–30 km over the city. The shock wave injured over 1,100 people, mostly from broken glass. The meteor was estimated to be 17 meters in diameter with a mass of 10,000–11,000 metric tons. Its explosion released energy equivalent to about 500 kilotons of TNT, 20–30 times the Hiroshima bomb. A shallow entry angle caused a high-altitude explosion, distributing energy and preventing greater destruction.

### Urban Development and Geography
The city developed from its fortress core. Major urban plans were created in 1936, 1947, and 1967 to guide its expansion around industrial districts. Chelyabinsk is bisected by the Miass River, considered the border between the Urals and Siberia, and is known as "the gateway to Siberia." It has a humid continental climate with warm summers and cold winters.

## Terms
- **Superbolide**: A very bright meteor that explodes in the atmosphere, brighter than the sun.
- **Sintashta culture**: An archaeological culture of the 2nd millennium BC in the southern Urals, associated with early Indo-Iranian speakers.
- **Trans-Siberian Railway**: A network of railways connecting Moscow with the Russian Far East.
- **Metallurgy**: The production of metals from ore, a core industry in Chelyabinsk.
- **Gross municipal product**: The total value of goods and services produced within a city's economy.
- **Humid continental climate**: A climate with warm summers and cold winters, with significant seasonal temperature differences.
- **Customs fracture**: A historical term for a customs barrier imposing duties on goods moving between regions.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The identification of the Sintashta culture as proto-Indo-Iranian is based on linguistic and archaeological similarities to the Rigveda and funerary rituals, but this remains a subject of scholarly interpretation.
- The exact size and mass of the 2013 Chelyabinsk meteor are estimates based on analysis of its trajectory and energy release.

# Cotonou

## Overview
Cotonou is the largest city and de facto seat of government of Benin, though the official capital is Porto-Novo. It is the country's economic hub, home to its main port and international airport, and a major center for trade in West Africa. The city's rapid growth and strategic coastal location have made it vital to the nation's economy, but it also faces challenges like coastal erosion.

## Timeline
- **c. 1830** — Founded as a fishing village by King Ghezo of Dahomey.
- **1851** — French Second Republic establishes a trading post via treaty with King Ghezo.
- **1878** — Territory ceded to the Second French Empire by treaty under King Glele.
- **1883** — French Navy occupies the city to prevent British conquest.
- **1908** — Harbour construction leads to rapid population growth.
- **1960** — Population is approximately 70,000.
- **1961** — Coastal erosion worsens after construction of the Nangbeto Dam and deep-water port.
- **2012** — Official city proper population is 679,012.

## Body
### History and Geography
The name "Cotonou" means "by the river of death" in the Fon language. It began as a small fishing village and grew as a center for the slave trade, and later for palm oil and cotton. French influence began with an 1851 treaty, leading to formal cession in 1878 and military occupation in 1883. The city is situated on a coastal strip between the Atlantic Ocean and Lake Nokoué, bisected by a French-dug canal. The Ouémé River flows into the ocean at Cotonou. A major environmental issue is severe coastal erosion, which has caused the coastline to retreat by 400 meters in 40 years in some areas.

### Demographics and Transport
Cotonou's population has grown rapidly, from 70,000 in 1960 to over 679,000 in the city proper by 2012, with over two million in the larger urban area. French is the official language, with Fon, Aja, and Yoruba also spoken. The city is a major transport hub with the Autonomous Port of Cotonou, one of West Africa's largest, and Cotonou International Airport. It is connected by railway to Parakou and by road to neighboring countries. A common local transport mode is the motorcycle-taxi, known as a *zémidjan*.

### Economy and Institutions
Cotonou's economy is based on manufacturing (palm oil, brewing, textiles, cement), vehicle assembly, and trade. It is a center for the automotive trade and exports petroleum products, bauxite, and iron. The city hosts the national broadcaster, ORTB, and numerous educational institutions, including the University of Science and Technology of Benin and several prestigious private universities. The 12th arrondissement features the second tallest statue in Africa, honoring the Dahomey Amazons.

## Terms
*   **Seat of government:** The city where a country's government departments and foreign embassies are located, even if it is not the official capital.
*   **Zémidjan:** A prevalent mode of motorcycle-taxi transport in Cotonou.
*   **Harmattan:** A dry, dusty wind that affects the city in December and January.
*   **Coastal erosion:** The wearing away of land along the coastline, a significant problem for Cotonou.
*   **Dahomey Amazons:** An all-female military regiment of the historical Kingdom of Dahomey, honored by a statue in Cotonou.

# Puebla (city)

## Overview
Puebla de Zaragoza, the capital of Puebla state, is Mexico's fourth-largest city and a major industrial and cultural hub. Founded as a Spanish colonial city in 1531, its historic center is a UNESCO World Heritage Site, renowned for its architecture, cuisine, and role in key historical events like the 1862 Battle of Puebla.

## Timeline
- **1531** — Official foundation as Puebla de los Ángeles in the Valley of Cuetlaxcoapan.
- **1575** — Construction of the Puebla Cathedral begins.
- **1646** — The Biblioteca Palafoxiana, the first public library in the Americas, is established.
- **1847** — U.S. forces under General Winfield Scott occupy the city during the Mexican-American War.
- **1862** — Mexican forces defeat the French army at the Battle of Puebla on May 5.
- **1863** — French forces capture Puebla after a siege.
- **1910** — Serdán brothers killed in a gun battle with federal troops, a precursor to the Mexican Revolution.
- **1950** — City officially renamed Heroica Puebla de Zaragoza.
- **1987** — Historic center declared a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

## Body

### Founding and Colonial Significance
Puebla was founded in 1531 as a Spanish city, not on an existing indigenous site, in the previously unpopulated Valley of Cuetlaxcoapan. Its strategic location between Mexico City and the port of Veracruz made it a vital commercial and administrative center. The city became the second most important in New Spain, known for its agricultural production, textile industry, and as a center of learning. Its classic Spanish layout, centered on the Zócalo, and its wealth of churches and monasteries established its architectural legacy.

### 19th and 20th Century Conflicts
Puebla was a site of major conflicts. It was occupied without a fight by U.S. forces in 1847 and later besieged. The city's most famous event is the Battle of Puebla on May 5, 1862, where Mexican troops under Ignacio Zaragoza defeated the French army, leading to the city's renaming. The French captured it in 1863 but left in 1866. The city was also a hotbed of early revolutionary activity against the Díaz government, culminating in the 1910 gun battle at the Serdán family home.

### Culture and Economy
Puebla is celebrated for its cuisine, including mole poblano and chiles en nogada, and its distinctive Talavera pottery, a tin-glazed ceramic tradition begun by Spanish artisans in the 16th century. The city's economy is overwhelmingly industrial (about 80%), anchored by the world's largest Volkswagen factory outside Germany and an Audi plant, which have attracted numerous suppliers. It is also a major educational center with prestigious universities like BUAP and UDLAP.

## Terms
- **Cuetlaxcoapan**: The Nahuatl name for the Valley of Puebla, meaning "where serpents change their skin."
- **Encomienda**: A Spanish colonial system granting settlers control over indigenous labor and tribute, which Puebla's founders sought to avoid.
- **Mole poblano**: A complex sauce from Puebla, often containing chocolate and spices, traditionally served over turkey.
- **Talavera pottery**: A style of tin-glazed ceramic from Puebla, combining Spanish techniques with indigenous designs, known for its blue and white patterns.
- **Zócalo**: The main public square or plaza in a Mexican city, the historic center of Puebla's social and political life.
- **Chiles en nogada**: A dish of stuffed poblano peppers in walnut sauce, said to have been invented in Puebla, representing the colors of the Mexican flag.
- **Cemita**: A type of sandwich from Puebla served on a sesame-seed bun, often filled with meat, cheese, and avocado.
- **China Poblana**: A legendary figure, Catarina de San Juan, whose Indian sari may have inspired a traditional Mexican dress style.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The origin of mole poblano is disputed, with legends attributing it to 16th-century nuns improvising for an archbishop's visit or to pre-Hispanic preparations served to Hernán Cortés.
- The historical use of chocolate in pre-Hispanic sauces like "chilmulli" is debated, with no clear evidence it was used before the colonial period.
- The existence and extent of a rumored tunnel system under the city, rediscovered in 2015, are still being explored.

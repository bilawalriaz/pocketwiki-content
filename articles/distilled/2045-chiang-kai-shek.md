# Chiang Kai-shek

## Overview
Chiang Kai-shek was a Chinese military and political leader who led the Kuomintang (KMT) party, unified China in the late 1920s, and led the nation through the Second Sino-Japanese War. After being defeated by the Chinese Communist Party (CCP) in the Chinese Civil War, he relocated the Republic of China (ROC) government to Taiwan, where he ruled as an authoritarian president until his death. His legacy is deeply contested, viewed by supporters as a unifier and patriot, and by critics as a corrupt and oppressive dictator.

## Timeline
- **31 October 1887** — Born in Xikou, Zhejiang.
- **1908** — Joins Sun Yat-sen's Tongmenghui revolutionary society in Japan.
- **1924** — Becomes Commandant of the Whampoa Military Academy.
- **1926–1928** — Leads the Northern Expedition, largely unifying China under the KMT.
- **12 April 1927** — Purges Communists in Shanghai, triggering the Chinese Civil War.
- **1937–1945** — Leads China against Japan in the Second Sino-Japanese War.
- **1945** — China emerges victorious from WWII; civil war with CCP resumes.
- **1949** — KMT defeated; Chiang and the ROC government retreat to Taiwan.
- **1950–1975** — Serves as President of the Republic of China in Taiwan.
- **5 April 1975** — Dies in Taipei, Taiwan.

## Body

### Rise to Power
Born to a merchant family, Chiang received a military education in China and Japan. He joined Sun Yat-sen's revolutionary Tongmenghui in 1908. After Sun's death in 1925, Chiang outmaneuvered rivals like Wang Jingwei to seize control of the KMT. As Commandant of the Whampoa Military Academy, he cultivated a loyal officer corps. From 1926 to 1928, he led the Northern Expedition, which defeated regional warlords and established a national government in Nanjing. In 1927, he violently purged Communists from the KMT, ending the First United Front and starting the Chinese Civil War.

### Rule on the Mainland (1928–1949)
During the "Nanjing Decade" (1928–1937), Chiang pursued modernization and state-building while prioritizing the suppression of the CCP over confronting Japanese aggression in Manchuria. His "first internal pacification, then external resistance" policy was unpopular. In 1936, the Xi'an Incident forced him into a nominal Second United Front with the CCP against Japan. He led China through the brutal Second Sino-Japanese War (1937–1945), employing scorched-earth tactics that caused massive civilian casualties, such as the 1938 Yellow River flood. After WWII, the civil war resumed. Despite initial advantages, the KMT was weakened by corruption, hyperinflation, and military defeats. In 1949, Chiang's government was defeated and retreated to Taiwan.

### Rule in Taiwan (1949–1975)
In Taiwan, Chiang imposed martial law and a "White Terror" to suppress dissent, including the aftermath of the 1947 February 28 Incident. He ruled as a de facto one-party state, re-elected multiple times by a "Parliament" elected on the mainland in 1947. His government pursued land reform and economic development, laying the groundwork for Taiwan's later economic success. He maintained the ROC's claim to represent all of China, holding China's UN seat until 1971. His rule was authoritarian, but he also implemented social and economic policies that transformed Taiwan.

### Philosophy and Legacy
Chiang's ideology blended Sun Yat-sen's Three Principles of the People with strong Confucian conservatism. He rejected Western liberalism and Marxism. His legacy is polarized. Supporters credit him with unifying China, leading the resistance against Japan, and fostering economic growth in Taiwan. Critics condemn his early appeasement of Japan, the massive civilian deaths from his wartime policies, pervasive corruption within his government (linked to the "Four Big Families"), and his authoritarian rule on both the mainland and Taiwan.

## Terms
*   **Kuomintang (KMT):** The Nationalist Party of China, founded by Sun Yat-sen, which Chiang led.
*   **Northern Expedition (1926–1928):** A military campaign led by Chiang to unify China by defeating regional warlords.
*   **Whampoa Military Academy:** A military academy founded in 1924 with Soviet help; Chiang was its commandant, and it trained many elite KMT officers.
*   **Second Sino-Japanese War (1937–1945):** The full-scale war between the Republic of China and the Empire of Japan, part of World War II.
*   **White Terror:** The period of political repression and mass killings of suspected opponents, carried out by the KMT on both the mainland and later in Taiwan.
*   **February 28 Incident (1947):** An anti-government uprising in Taiwan that was violently suppressed by KMT forces, leading to thousands of deaths.
*   **Three Principles of the People (San-min Zhuyi):** Sun Yat-sen's political philosophy forming the official ideology of the KMT, emphasizing nationalism, democracy, and people's livelihood.
*   **Long March (1934–1935):** A military retreat undertaken by the Red Army of the CCP to evade KMT encirclement campaigns, which solidified Mao Zedong's leadership.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Fascism:** Some historians argue Chiang's regime, with its New Life Movement and Blue Shirts Society, exhibited fascist characteristics. Others contend it lacked the totalitarian mass mobilization of European fascism and was better described as developmental nationalism or military authoritarianism.
*   **Reasons for Losing the Civil War:** Historians debate whether Chiang's defeat was primarily due to internal factors like corruption, military incompetence, and loss of popular support, or external factors such as the U.S. limiting aid and the Soviet Union supporting the CCP.
*   **Historical Perception:** Chiang's image is deeply divided. In Taiwan, he is a polarizing figure, with the DPP viewing him as a dictator and the KMT emphasizing his role in preserving Chinese culture. In mainland China, his portrayal has softened since the 2000s, acknowledging his leadership during the war against Japan.

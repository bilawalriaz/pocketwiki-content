# Guatemala City

## Overview
Guatemala City is the capital and largest city of Guatemala, serving as the nation's political, economic, and cultural center. Its history spans from the ancient Maya site of Kaminaljuyu to its founding by the Spanish in 1776, making it a key location for understanding Central American colonial and post-colonial development. The city is prone to natural disasters, including earthquakes and volcanic activity, which have repeatedly shaped its urban landscape.

## Timeline
- **c. 300 BCE** — Maya settlement of Kaminaljuyu rises to prominence.
- **c. 800–900 CE** — Kaminaljuyu collapses due to instability and drought.
- **1773** — The Santa Marta earthquake destroys the old capital, Antigua Guatemala.
- **1776** — The Spanish found the new capital, Nueva Guatemala de la Asunción.
- **September 1821** — The Act of Independence of Central America is signed in the city.
- **1847** — Guatemala declares itself an independent republic; the city becomes its national capital.
- **1917–1918** — Devastating earthquakes destroy much of the city, leading to modern reconstruction.
- **1944** — The Guatemalan Revolution overthrows the dictatorship of Jorge Ubico.
- **2007** — A massive sinkhole, classified as a piping pseudokarst, opens in the city.

## Body

### Ancient and Colonial Foundations
The site of Guatemala City was first settled by the Maya, who built the major ceremonial center of Kaminaljuyu. This city thrived from around 300 BCE due to obsidian trade but collapsed between AD 550 and 900. The Spanish colonial capital was originally Antigua Guatemala, but after the 1773 Santa Marta earthquake, the seat of government was moved to the current, safer valley location in 1776. The new city's central plaza was anchored by the Metropolitan Cathedral, built between 1782 and 1871.

### Independence and National Capital
Guatemala City was the site where the Act of Independence of Central America was signed in September 1821, ending Spanish rule. It became the capital of the subsequent United Provinces of Central America. After the federation dissolved, Guatemala declared itself an independent republic in 1847, with Guatemala City as its capital. The 19th century saw the construction of landmarks like the Carrera Theater and the Presidential Palace.

### 20th-Century Destruction and Modernization
The 1917–1918 earthquakes destroyed many historic structures, leading to a reconstruction that implemented a modern grid layout inspired by cities like Paris and Washington, D.C. Under the authoritarian rule of Jorge Ubico (1931–1944), public works projects were initiated, including the National Palace (1939–1943). The 1944 Guatemalan Revolution ended this dictatorship. Rapid population growth from rural migration since the 1970s has strained infrastructure, leading to issues like traffic congestion and the expansion of slums.

### Urban Structure and Climate
The city is divided into 22 numbered zones, with a grid-based address system designed by Raúl Aguilar Batres. Zone One is the historic center. The city has a tropical savannah climate with a rainy season from May to October. It is located in the Valle de la Ermita, surrounded by four visible stratovolcanoes, including the active Pacaya. The region lies on the Ring of Fire, making it seismically active; the last major earthquake was in 1976.

### Contemporary City and Challenges
Today, Guatemala City is the economic hub of Central America, housing the central bank and major corporations. It is a major transportation hub with La Aurora International Airport and the Transmetro bus rapid transit system. The city faces significant challenges from rapid, unplanned growth, including infrastructure deficits, crime, and vulnerability to natural disasters like mudslides and sinkholes caused by erosion of the underlying volcanic geology.

## Terms
- **Kaminaljuyu**: An ancient Maya ceremonial center and city that occupied the site of modern Guatemala City.
- **Captaincy General of Guatemala**: The administrative division of the Spanish Empire in Central America, with its capital moving from Antigua to Guatemala City.
- **Act of Independence of Central America**: The 1821 declaration that freed Central America from Spanish rule.
- **Piping pseudokarst**: A geological feature, like a sinkhole, formed by the erosion of subsurface material by flowing water, as seen in Guatemala City.
- **Transmetro**: The bus rapid transit (BRT) system that serves as a key part of Guatemala City's public transportation.
- **Zonas**: The numbered districts into which Guatemala City is subdivided for urban planning and addressing.
- **Valle de la Ermita**: The mountain valley in south-central Guatemala where the city is located.

## Debates and Open questions
- The exact causes of the collapse of the ancient Maya city of Kaminaljuyu are debated, with recent research pointing to agricultural instability, political factors, and severe droughts.
- The rapid growth of Guatemala City has led to ongoing debates about urban planning, inequality, and the adequacy of municipal infrastructure and maintenance, particularly regarding the sewerage system's role in sinkhole formation.

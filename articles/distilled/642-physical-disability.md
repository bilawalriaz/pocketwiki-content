# Physical disability

## Overview
A physical disability is a broad term for limitations in a person’s physical functioning or in executing activities such as walking or eating. Disabilities range from mild to severe, and their impact on daily life varies widely. The World Health Organization does not distinguish between physical and mental disabilities, and people with physical disabilities may face persecution and discrimination. Inclusive and accessible environments can mitigate many restrictions.

## Timeline
- **Prenatal period** — Disabilities acquired before birth due to maternal exposure, developmental accidents, or genetic disorders
- **Perinatal period** — Disabilities acquired weeks before to four weeks after birth, often from oxygen deprivation or birth complications
- **Throughout life** — Post-natal disabilities acquired after birth from accidents, illness, or injury; road traffic injuries are a leading cause

## Body
### Causes
Physical disabilities arise from pre-natal, perinatal, or post-natal factors. Prenatal disabilities result from maternal exposure to diseases or substances, embryonic or fetal developmental accidents, or genetic disorders. Perinatal disabilities occur between weeks before to up to four weeks after birth and may stem from prolonged oxygen deprivation, respiratory tract obstruction, brain damage during delivery, premature birth, genetic disorders, or accidents. Post-natal disabilities develop after birth and can occur at any age due to accidents, injuries, obesity, infections, or other illnesses. Research indicates road traffic injuries are the leading cause of post-natal disabilities.

### Types
Physical disability encompasses many impairments. Some severely restrict daily functioning and require caregiver assistance, while others are managed with assistive technology such as wheelchairs or hearing aids. Mobility impairments include limb loss or impairment, poor manual dexterity, paraplegia, and damage to one or multiple organs, with severity varying widely. Sensory impairments involve partial or complete loss of one or more senses; the most common are visual impairments and hearing loss. Deaf and hard-of-hearing individuals often benefit from sign language and may use hearing aids. Tactile, gustatory, and olfactory disabilities exist but are less common. Some individuals have multiple disabilities, including co-occurring cognitive and physical conditions.

### Assistive Technology
Management depends on the specific condition, and pain is a significant concern for many. Inaccessible environments create challenges that assistive devices can help counter, promoting inclusion. Mobility aids include wheelchairs, walkers, canes, and prosthetic limbs. Hearing aids support those with hearing loss. Visual aids encompass guide dogs, canes, and screen readers. Communication devices provide augmentative and alternative communication for people with expressive language impairments.

## Terms
- **Physical disability**: A limitation on a person’s physical functioning or ability to execute activities such as walking or eating.
- **Prenatal disability**: A disability acquired before birth, potentially due to maternal exposure, developmental accidents, or genetic disorders.
- **Perinatal disability**: A disability acquired between weeks before to up to four weeks after birth, often linked to oxygen deprivation or birth complications.
- **Post-natal disability**: A disability gained after birth, caused by accidents, illness, injury, or other factors throughout life.
- **Mobility impairment**: Impairments affecting movement, including limb loss, poor manual dexterity, paraplegia, or organ damage.
- **Sensory impairment**: Partial or complete loss of one or more senses, most commonly vision or hearing.
- **Assistive technology**: Devices or tools that help people with disabilities manage daily activities and increase inclusion.
- **Paraplegia**: A condition involving impairment of the lower extremities and trunk, typically due to spinal cord injury.
- **Sign language**: A visual language using hand shapes, movements, and facial expressions to communicate, used by many deaf individuals.
- **Augmentative and alternative communication (AAC)**: Tools and strategies to support or replace spoken language for people with communication difficulties.

## Debates and Open Questions
The World Health Organization does not differentiate between physical and mental disabilities, reflecting ongoing debate about how best to classify and categorize disability. The extent to which inclusive and accessible environments can mitigate restrictions is also a subject of discussion, as is the relative importance of assistive technology versus systemic societal change in improving quality of life for people with physical disabilities.

# San Juan, Puerto Rico

## Overview
San Juan is the capital and most populous municipality of Puerto Rico, a U.S. territory. Founded by Spanish colonists in 1521, it is the second-oldest European-established capital in the Americas and a major cultural, financial, and tourism hub for the Caribbean region.

## Timeline
- **1508** — Juan Ponce de León founds the original settlement, Caparra.
- **1509** — The settlement is moved to a site called Puerto Rico.
- **1521** — The settlement is formally named Ciudad de Puerto Rico de San Juan Bautista.
- **1595** — English forces led by Sir Francis Drake attack San Juan.
- **1625** — Dutch forces sack the city, but Fort El Morro withstands the assault.
- **1898** — U.S. Navy arrives at San Juan Bay; Spain cedes Puerto Rico to the U.S. after the Spanish-American War.
- **1951** — The municipalities of San Juan and Río Piedras are merged.
- **1983** — San Juan's historic sites are declared a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
- **2004** — The Tren Urbano metro system opens.
- **2017** — Hurricane Maria makes a direct impact, causing widespread damage.

## Body

### History and Founding
The area was originally inhabited by the Taíno people. Spanish colonization began with the founding of Caparra in 1508, which was relocated in 1509. The city was formally named in 1521. Its strategic importance as a port for Spanish treasure fleets led to the construction of extensive fortifications, including Fort San Felipe del Morro and Fort San Cristóbal. The city successfully repelled attacks by English forces in 1595 and 1598, and by the Dutch in 1625. San Juan was ceded to the United States in 1898 following the Spanish-American War. In 1951, the city expanded significantly by annexing the neighboring municipality of Río Piedras.

### Geography and Climate
San Juan is located on Puerto Rico's northeastern coast. The municipality covers 76.93 square miles (199.2 km²), with 37.83% being water. It has a tropical monsoon climate with an average temperature of 81.0 °F (27.2 °C). Annual rainfall averages 56.35 inches (1,431.3 mm). The city is vulnerable to hurricanes, most notably Hurricane Maria in 2017, which caused catastrophic damage to infrastructure.

### Cityscape and Architecture
San Juan's architecture reflects its colonial and modern history. Old San Juan features Spanish colonial buildings, cobblestone streets, and massive defensive walls. Notable structures include La Fortaleza, the oldest executive mansion in continuous use in the Americas, and the Cathedral of San Juan Bautista. Other districts like Santurce and Hato Rey showcase more varied styles, including Art Deco and Mid-Century Modern. The city is administratively divided into 18 barrios, with Santurce being the most populous.

### Demographics and Economy
The municipality's population was 342,259 in the 2020 census. The population is predominantly Hispanic, with Puerto Ricans making up 87.5% of the Hispanic population. San Juan's economy is driven by manufacturing (pharmaceuticals, rum, electronics), tourism, and finance. The Hato Rey district is known as the "Wall Street of the Caribbean." The Port of San Juan is the fourth busiest seaport in the Western Hemisphere and a major cruise ship hub.

### Government and Infrastructure
San Juan is the territorial capital, housing Puerto Rico's government branches. It is served by the Luis Muñoz Marín International Airport, the busiest in the Caribbean. The city has an extensive highway network but suffers from severe traffic congestion. Public transportation includes the Metropolitan Bus Authority and the Tren Urbano metro system. The Port of San Juan is a critical commercial and cruise facility.

## Terms
- **Barrio**: An administrative division or ward within a Puerto Rican municipality.
- **Cacique**: A chief or leader of a Taíno tribe.
- **Commonwealth**: The political status of Puerto Rico as an unincorporated territory of the United States.
- **Milla de Oro**: (Golden Mile) A section of Hato Rey known for its concentration of banks and financial institutions.
- **Yucayeque**: A Taíno village or settlement.

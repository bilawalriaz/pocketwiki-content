# Ouagadougou

## Overview
Ouagadougou is the capital and largest city of Burkina Faso, serving as the nation's administrative, economic, and cultural center. With a population of over 2.4 million in 2019, it is a major West African metropolis whose history spans from a pre-colonial Mossi kingdom to a modern city shaped by colonialism, independence, and recent security challenges.

## Timeline
- **c. 1050** — City founded possibly by Soninke Wangara diaspora from the Ghana Empire.
- **14th century** — Mossi people conquer the city, contributing to the decline of the Mali Empire.
- **15th century** — Wagadugu Kingdom founded, becoming the main center of the Mossi States.
- **Mid-17th century** — The 10th Moro Naba, Nyadfo, becomes the first to live in Ouagadougou.
- **Early 19th century** — The 24th Moro Naba builds the first mosque in the city.
- **5 September 1896** — French forces enter and burn the city.
- **1919** — French colonial administration makes Ouagadougou the capital of Upper Volta.
- **1954** — Railroad from Ivory Coast reaches the city, spurring population growth.
- **15 January 2016** — Major jihadist attack at the Cappuccino restaurant and Splendid Hotel kills 28.
- **March 2017** — International competition launched to design the new "Yennenga" city nearby.

## Body

### History and Governance
Ouagadougou was founded possibly as early as 1050 by the Soninke Wangara. The Mossi people conquered it in the 14th century, and the Wagadugu Kingdom was established in the 15th century. The Moro-Naba, the Mossi ruler, made it his permanent capital in the 18th century. French colonization began violently in 1896, but the city was rebuilt as the colonial capital in 1919. The city's first municipal elections were held in 1956. Today, it is divided into five arrondissements and 30 sectors.

### Economy and Transport
The economy is based on industry (food processing, textiles) and commerce, with the informal sector dominating. It is a key transport hub served by Thomas Sankara International Airport and rail links to Abidjan, Ivory Coast. Highways connect it to neighboring countries. The city hosts one of West Africa's largest markets, which was rebuilt after a 2003 fire.

### Climate and Geography
Ouagadougou has a hot semi-arid climate (BSh) with a rainy season from May to September. The hot season (March-April) can see temperatures reach 43°C. The climate is determined by the dry harmattan wind and the West African Monsoon.

### Culture and Education
The city is a cultural center hosting major festivals like FESPACO (Panafrican Film and Television Festival) and SIAO (International Art and Craft Fair). It has several museums, including the National Museum of Music. Education includes the University of Ouagadougou (founded 1974), though literacy rates are not high. The official language is French, with More, Dyula, and Fulfulde as principal local languages.

### Parks and Notable Sites
Key sites include the Bangr-Weoogo urban park (formerly a sacred forest), the L'Unité Pédagogique botanical garden, and the Naba Koom statue. The Moro-Naba Ceremony is performed every Friday at the Moro-Naba Palace.

## Terms
- **Moro-Naba**: The title of the ruler of the Mossi people, based in Ouagadougou.
- **Wangara**: A Soninke diaspora group from the Ghana Empire credited with founding the city.
- **Harmattan**: A dry, dusty wind from the Sahara that influences the region's climate.
- **FESPACO**: The Panafrican Film and Television Festival of Ouagadougou, Africa's largest festival of its kind.
- **SIAO**: The International Arts and Crafts Fair held in Ouagadougou.
- **Arrondissement**: A level of municipal administrative division in the city.
- **Informal sector**: An economic sector characterized by petty commodity production and unregulated, often unsalaried, work.

## Debates and Open Questions
The exact founding date of Ouagadougou is uncertain, with sources stating it was "possibly as early as 1050." The city's name and its precise etymology from the Soninke "Wagadugu" are noted, but the historical details of its early governance are presented as legend (e.g., its capture by Oubri). The ongoing jihadist insurgency, with major attacks in 2016, 2017, and 2018, represents a current security challenge for the city.

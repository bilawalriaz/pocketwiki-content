# Malcolm X

## Overview
Malcolm X (1925–1965) was a transformative African American civil rights leader and Muslim minister whose evolving philosophy—from black separatism to a more inclusive human rights framework—profoundly influenced the Black liberation movement. His life and assassination remain central to discussions of race, religion, and justice in America.

## Timeline
- **May 19, 1925** — Born Malcolm Little in Omaha, Nebraska.
- **1931** — Father dies; family life unravels.
- **1946** — Sentenced to prison for burglary.
- **1952** — Paroled; becomes a minister for the Nation of Islam.
- **1957** — Hinton Johnson incident brings him to public attention.
- **Dec. 1, 1963** — Makes controversial "chickens coming home to roost" remarks about JFK assassination.
- **March 8, 1964** — Publicly breaks from the Nation of Islam.
- **April 1964** — Completes Hajj to Mecca, converting to Sunni Islam.
- **Nov. 30, 1964** — Debates at Oxford Union.
- **Feb. 21, 1965** — Assassinated in New York City.
- **2021** — Convictions of two men for his murder are vacated.

## Body

### Early Life and Prison Conversion
Malcolm X's childhood was marked by racial violence and family trauma. His father, a Garveyite organizer, died in 1931 under circumstances the family believed were murder. His mother's subsequent mental breakdown led to Malcolm's placement in foster homes. After a criminal career in Boston and Harlem, he was imprisoned in 1946. In prison, he was introduced to the Nation of Islam (NOI) by his siblings and converted, adopting "X" to symbolize his lost African name.

### Rise in the Nation of Islam
After his 1952 parole, Malcolm X became the NOI's most effective spokesman, rapidly expanding its membership from around 1,200 to as high as 100,000. He advocated black empowerment and separation from whites, criticizing the mainstream civil rights movement's integrationist and nonviolent approach. His public profile grew after the 1957 Hinton Johnson incident, where he organized a crowd to demand police accountability. He married Betty Sanders in 1958, and they had six daughters.

### Disillusionment and Departure
Malcolm X's relationship with NOI leader Elijah Muhammad deteriorated due to several factors. He was angered by the NOI's lack of response to a 1962 police shooting of members in Los Angeles. He also discovered Muhammad's extramarital affairs, which violated NOI teachings. After being censured for his "chickens coming home to roost" remarks on JFK's assassination, he felt marginalized. He formally left the NOI in March 1964, founding the Muslim Mosque, Inc. and the Organization of Afro-American Unity (OAAU).

### Global Vision and Final Year
After his Hajj, Malcolm X embraced Sunni Islam and a more inclusive view of race, stating he had "tossed aside some of [his] previous conclusions." He traveled extensively in Africa and the Middle East, seeking support to internationalize the African American struggle as a human rights issue at the United Nations. He advocated for self-defense "by any means necessary" and began critiquing capitalism. His growing prominence and public criticism of the NOI led to escalating death threats.

### Assassination and Aftermath
On February 21, 1965, Malcolm X was assassinated while speaking in New York's Audubon Ballroom. Three NOI members were convicted, though one confessed and named others. In 2021, two convictions were vacated after a review found the FBI and NYPD withheld key evidence. Persistent speculation about broader conspiracies involving the NOI or law enforcement agencies continues.

## Terms
- **Nation of Islam (NOI):** A religious movement founded in 1930 that preached black self-reliance and separation from whites, with its own interpretation of Islam.
- **Hajj:** The Islamic pilgrimage to Mecca, obligatory for Muslims who are able to undertake it.
- **Pan-Africanism:** A political and cultural movement advocating solidarity and unity among all people of African descent.
- **Black nationalism:** A political ideology emphasizing self-determination and the cultural and economic independence of Black communities.
- **OAAU (Organization of Afro-American Unity):** A secular organization founded by Malcolm X in 1964 to advance human rights for African Americans.
- **COINTELPRO:** A series of covert and illegal FBI programs aimed at surveilling, infiltrating, and disrupting domestic political organizations, including civil rights groups.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Responsibility for his assassination:** While three men were convicted, questions persist about the full extent of involvement by the Nation of Islam, the FBI, or the NYPD, especially after the 2021 vacating of two convictions.
- **Evolution of his views:** Scholars debate the depth and consistency of his ideological shift from racial separatism to a more inclusive human rights perspective in his final year.
- **Allegations of bisexuality:** Based on biographer Manning Marable's work, some historians claim Malcolm X had early homosexual encounters, a claim his family vehemently rejects.

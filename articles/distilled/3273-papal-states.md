# Papal States

## Overview
The Papal States were a conglomeration of territories on the Italian Peninsula under the direct sovereign rule of the pope from 756 to 1870. They were a major Italian state whose existence exemplified the temporal power of the papacy, distinct from its spiritual authority, and their eventual dissolution was a key event in the unification of Italy.

## Timeline
- **756** — Pepin the Short donates lands to Pope Stephen II, legally establishing the Papal States.
- **774** — Charlemagne extends papal temporal sovereignty over additional regions.
- **800** — Pope Leo III crowns Charlemagne Emperor, cementing Carolingian-papal cooperation.
- **1305-1378** — The Avignon Papacy; popes reside in France, weakening direct control over Italian territories.
- **1357** — Cardinal Albornoz promulgates the *Constitutiones Aegidianae*, a uniform legal code for the states.
- **1598-1631** — Papal States reach greatest extent after annexing Ferrara and Urbino.
- **1798** — French forces declare a Roman Republic; Pope Pius VI flees.
- **1814-15** — Congress of Vienna restores the Papal States after the Napoleonic era.
- **1860** — Kingdom of Sardinia annexes most of the Papal States, except Lazio.
- **1870** — Italian forces capture Rome, annexing Lazio and ending the Papal States.
- **1929** — Lateran Treaty creates Vatican City, resolving the "Prisoner in the Vatican" period.

## Body

### Origins and Legal Foundation
The papacy's temporal rule began with the Donation of Pepin in 756, which granted the pope lands as a temporal sovereign. This followed centuries where the Church, after being legalized by Constantine in 321, accumulated property as a private landowner. As Byzantine authority in Italy weakened due to Lombard invasions and administrative failure, the popes, as major landowners, assumed de facto ruling authority in the Duchy of Rome. The Donation of Pepin formalized this by giving the pope sovereignty over territories formerly held by the Lombards and the Exarchate of Ravenna.

### Relationship with the Holy Roman Empire
From the 9th to 12th centuries, the precise relationship between the Papal States and the Holy Roman Empire was disputed. The states were sometimes treated as part of the imperial realm, while popes asserted their independence. This era saw periods of instability, such as the "Saeculum obscurum" (10th century), when local Roman nobles controlled the papacy. The Diploma Ottonianum (955) established the emperor as the guarantor of papal independence, but conflicts continued until the Papal States were effectively independent by 1300.

### Renaissance Expansion and Administrative Reform
During the Renaissance, popes like Alexander VI and Julius II expanded papal territory significantly, making the pope a major secular ruler. However, control was often contested. A major administrative reform came with Cardinal Albornoz's *Constitutiones Aegidianae* (1357), which created a uniform legal code for the states, replacing a patchwork of local laws. This code remained in effect until 1816.

### Napoleonic Era and Restoration
The French Revolution and Napoleonic invasions (1796-1809) dismantled the Papal States, creating republics and annexing territories to France. After Napoleon's defeat, the Congress of Vienna (1814-15) restored the states, but not the Avignon territory. The restored papacy pursued reactionary policies, leading to revolts in 1830 and 1848.

### Unification and Dissolution
Italian nationalism led to the Second Italian War of Independence (1859), after which Piedmont-Sardinia annexed most of the Papal States in 1860. In 1870, during the Franco-Prussian War, Italian troops captured Rome, ending the Papal States. The papacy refused to recognize the Italian state, leading to the "Prisoner in the Vatican" period until the 1929 Lateran Treaty established Vatican City.

## Terms
- **Temporal Power**: The pope's authority as a secular ruler over territories, as opposed to his spiritual authority over the Church.
- **Donation of Pepin**: The 756 grant by King Pepin the Short of lands to the pope, forming the legal basis of the Papal States.
- **Exarchate of Ravenna**: A Byzantine administrative division in Italy whose fall to the Lombards in 751 isolated the Duchy of Rome.
- **Constitutiones Aegidianae**: A uniform legal code for the Papal States promulgated by Cardinal Albornoz in 1357.
- **Saeculum obscurum**: A term for the 10th century when the papacy was dominated by the corrupt Theophylacti family.
- **Prisoner in the Vatican**: The period from 1870 to 1929 when popes refused to leave the Vatican, not recognizing Italian sovereignty over Rome.
- **Lateran Treaty**: The 1929 agreement between Italy and the Holy See that created Vatican City and resolved the Roman Question.

## Debates and Open Questions
The precise nature of the relationship between the popes and the Holy Roman Emperors was long disputed: whether the Papal States were a separate sovereign realm or part of the empire over which popes had administrative control. The extent of effective papal control over the states' territories was also debated, with much of the land often ruled by minor princes or local nobles, especially before the 16th century.

# Guinea

## Overview
Guinea is a West African coastal nation with a population of over 17.5 million. It is a resource-rich country, notably the world's second-largest producer of bauxite, but faces significant challenges including poverty, political instability, and a history of military coups. Its modern history is defined by a transition from French colonial rule to decades of authoritarian government, followed by a fragile democratic period that was interrupted by another military takeover in 2021.

## Timeline
- **2 October 1958** — Guinea declares independence from France.
- **1960** — President Ahmed Sékou Touré declares the Democratic Party of Guinea the only legal party.
- **22 November 1970** — Portuguese-backed exiles stage a raid on Conakry (Operation Green Sea).
- **3 April 1984** — Colonels Lansana Conté and Diarra Traoré seize power in a coup after Touré's death.
- **27 June 2010** — Alpha Condé wins Guinea's first democratic presidential election.
- **28 September 2009** — Soldiers kill 157 people at a protest against junta leader Moussa Dadis Camara.
- **25 March 2014** — WHO reports an Ebola virus outbreak in Guinea.
- **14 October 2019** — Mass protests erupt against constitutional changes allowing President Condé a third term.
- **5 September 2021** — Lieutenant Colonel Mamady Doumbouya overthrows President Alpha Condé in a coup.
- **21 September 2025** — A constitutional referendum is held, establishing a new constitution.
- **28 December 2025** — Mamady Doumbouya wins the presidential election, the first since the 2021 coup.

## Body

### History and Politics
The territory of modern Guinea was part of several historic West African empires, including the Ghana, Mali, and Songhai Empires. French colonial control was established by the late 19th century. Guinea voted overwhelmingly for independence in 1958, led by Ahmed Sékou Touré, who established a one-party state. After his death in 1984, Lansana Conté took power in a coup and ruled until 2008. A transitional junta led by Moussa Dadis Camara was marked by a 2009 massacre of protesters. Guinea held its first democratic election in 2010, won by Alpha Condé. In 2021, Condé was overthrown in a coup led by Mamady Doumbouya, who was subsequently elected president in December 2025. Guinea is a member of the UN, African Union, and ECOWAS, though its AU and ECOWAS memberships were suspended after the 2021 coup and later reinstated.

### Geography and Demographics
Guinea is divided into four natural regions: Maritime Guinea (coastal), Middle Guinea (the Fouta Djallon highlands), Upper Guinea (savannah), and Forest Guinea (tropical forests). The country is ethnically diverse, with the Fula (43.4%), Mandinka (25.4%), and Soussou (17.2%) as the largest groups. French is the official language of administration, though many indigenous languages are spoken. The population is predominantly Muslim (approximately 87-90%).

### Economy and Resources
Guinea's economy relies on agriculture and mineral production. It holds over 25% of the world's known bauxite reserves, and bauxite and alumina are its major exports. The country also has significant deposits of iron ore, gold, and diamonds. Despite its mineral wealth, poverty is widespread; a 2018 survey found 66.2% of the population affected by multidimensional poverty.

### Health and Society
Guinea faces severe public health challenges. It has one of the world's highest rates of female genital mutilation (FGM), performed on over 96% of women. The country experienced a major Ebola outbreak from 2014-2016, which began in the village of Meliandou and killed thousands. Other health issues include high maternal and child mortality rates, malaria, and HIV/AIDS. Literacy rates are low, estimated at 41% for adults in 2010.

## Terms
*   **Bauxite:** The primary ore of aluminium; Guinea holds over 25% of the world's known reserves.
*   **Coup d'état:** A sudden and illegal seizure of power from a government, often by the military.
*   **ECOWAS:** The Economic Community of West African States, a regional political and economic union.
*   **Fouta Djallon:** A highland region in central Guinea, historically a Fulani Islamic state.
*   **Multidimensional poverty:** Poverty defined by deprivations in health, education, and living standards, not just income.
*   **Operation Green Sea:** A 1970 raid on Conakry by Portuguese-backed Guinean exiles.
*   **Simandou:** A major iron ore deposit in Guinea, the subject of significant international mining investment.
*   **Susu, Pular, Maninka:** The three largest indigenous language groups in Guinea.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   The 2021 coup and subsequent 2025 election, in which the coup leader Mamady Doumbouya won with 86.72% of the vote, raise questions about the genuine transition to civilian rule and democratic legitimacy.
*   Guinea's vast mineral wealth, particularly bauxite and iron ore, has not translated into broad-based development, leading to debates about resource governance, corruption, and the role of foreign mining corporations.
*   The country's history of political violence, ethnic tensions (e.g., clashes between Malinke and Fula groups), and military intervention complicates efforts to establish stable, inclusive governance.

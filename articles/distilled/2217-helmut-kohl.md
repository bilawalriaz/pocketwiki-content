# Helmut Kohl

## Overview
Helmut Kohl (1930–2017) was a German politician who served as Chancellor of Germany from 1982 to 1998, the longest tenure in post-war German history. He is a pivotal figure for his role in overseeing German reunification and co-architecting the European Union, shaping the continent's post-Cold War order.

## Timeline
- **3 April 1930** — Born in Ludwigshafen, Germany.
- **1946** — Joins the Christian Democratic Union (CDU).
- **19 May 1969** — Becomes Minister-President of Rhineland-Palatinate.
- **12 June 1973** — Elected national chairman of the CDU.
- **1 October 1982** — Elected Chancellor of Germany via a constructive vote of no confidence.
- **22 September 1984** — Meets French President François Mitterrand at Verdun, symbolizing reconciliation.
- **9 November 1989** — Berlin Wall falls; Kohl moves decisively toward reunification.
- **3 October 1990** — German reunification is completed.
- **7 February 1992** — Chairs the G7 summit in Munich.
- **7 February 1992** — Signs the Maastricht Treaty with Mitterrand, establishing the EU.
- **27 October 1998** — Defeated in federal election by Gerhard Schröder; ends chancellorship.
- **16 June 2017** — Dies in Ludwigshafen.

## Body

### Early Life and Political Rise
Born to a conservative Catholic family, Kohl joined the CDU in 1946. He earned a PhD in history in 1958 and entered politics, becoming the youngest member of the Rhineland-Palatinate parliament in 1959. He served as the state's Minister-President from 1969 to 1976, where he enacted reforms like abolishing school corporal punishment. Initially seen as a party progressive, he was elected CDU national chairman in 1973 and later became a more conservative figure.

### Chancellorship and European Integration
Kohl became Chancellor in 1982 after the liberal FDP withdrew support from Helmut Schmidt's government. He was a staunch Atlanticist and committed to European integration. His close partnership with French President François Mitterrand was crucial for projects like the Eurocorps and the Maastricht Treaty, which created the European Union and the euro currency. His government also pushed for international recognition of newly independent Balkan states during the Yugoslav Wars.

### German Reunification
Following the collapse of the East German regime in 1989, Kohl seized the historic opportunity for reunification. He presented a ten-point plan and secured Soviet leader Mikhail Gorbachev's agreement for a unified Germany to choose its alliances. Reunification was achieved on 3 October 1990 under Article 23 of the Basic Law, with East Germany acceding to the Federal Republic. Kohl later affirmed Germany's final borders with Poland and the Czech Republic.

### Domestic Policy and Legacy
Domestically, Kohl's policies focused on integrating the former East Germany, a process that included a controversial 1:1 currency exchange rate. He moved the federal capital back to Berlin. His chancellorship saw various social policy measures, including child-rearing allowances and early retirement schemes. He was defeated in the 1998 election. His reputation was later damaged by the CDU donations scandal, though he remained an honored figure internationally. He was posthumously honored with a European act of state.

## Terms
- **Constructive vote of no confidence**: A procedure in the German Basic Law allowing parliament to remove a chancellor only by simultaneously electing a successor.
- **Ostpolitik**: A policy of détente and normalization of relations between West Germany and Eastern Europe, begun by previous SPD-led governments.
- **Article 23 of the Basic Law**: The legal mechanism used for reunification, allowing new states to join the Federal Republic by a simple majority vote.
- **Maastricht Treaty**: The 1992 treaty that established the European Union and paved the way for the euro currency.
- **CDU donations scandal**: A party financing scandal revealed in 1999, involving illegal donations during Kohl's leadership, which damaged his domestic reputation.
- **Ordoliberalism**: A German school of economic thought emphasizing a strong state role in ensuring a competitive market order, which influenced Kohl's early economic policies.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Reunification Policy**: The decision to allow a 1:1 exchange rate for wages and savings between the East and West German marks is debated for its long-term economic impact on the new federal states.
- **Political Style**: Kohl was often criticized for a lack of charisma and a provincial image, yet his personal networks and tenacity were key to his political success.
- **Post-Chancellorship Controversies**: His 2008 marriage to Maike Richter while incapacitated led to a public family feud and questions about his final years, including his estrangement from his sons.

# Emperor Wu of Han

## Overview
Emperor Wu of Han (r. 141–87 BC) was the seventh and one of the most consequential emperors of the Han dynasty. His 54-year reign, the longest for an ethnic Han emperor until the Kangxi Emperor, transformed China through vast territorial expansion, the establishment of a strong centralized state, and the adoption of Confucianism as state philosophy. His policies and cultural patronage had an enduring influence on Chinese civilization and its neighbors.

## Timeline
- **156 BC** — Liu Che (future Emperor Wu) is born.
- **141 BC** — Ascends the throne at age 15 after Emperor Jing's death.
- **140 BC** — Launches the Jianyuan Reforms, promoting Confucianism and centralizing power.
- **139 BC** — Dispatches envoy Zhang Qian to seek an alliance with the Yuezhi in Central Asia.
- **135 BC** — Death of Grand Empress Dowager Dou, removing the last major restraint on his power.
- **133 BC** — Battle of Mayi initiates open warfare against the Xiongnu.
- **127–119 BC** — Generals Wei Qing and Huo Qubing defeat the Xiongnu, securing the Northern Silk Road.
- **111 BC** — Conquest and annexation of the Nanyue kingdom.
- **108 BC** — Conquest of Gojoseon in the Korean Peninsula.
- **104 BC** — Implements the Taichu calendar reform.
- **91 BC** — Crown Prince Ju's revolt and subsequent suicide.
- **87 BC** — Emperor Wu dies; succeeded by his young son, Emperor Zhao.

## Body

### Early Reign and Consolidation of Power
Liu Che became crown prince through a political marriage alliance orchestrated by his mother, Consort Wang, and Princess Guantao. Upon ascending the throne, he attempted ambitious reforms (the Jianyuan Reforms) to centralize power and promote Confucianism, but these were swiftly defeated by the conservative faction led by his grandmother, Grand Empress Dowager Dou. After her death in 135 BC, he gained full control. He created an "insider court" of loyal officials to counter the traditional bureaucracy and began using military intervention to assert imperial authority, as seen in the 138 BC rescue of Dong'ou from Minyue.

### Imperial Expansion and Foreign Policy
Emperor Wu dramatically expanded the Han empire through military campaigns. He ended the policy of appeasement (*heqin*) toward the nomadic Xiongnu, launching offensive cavalry warfare that expelled them from the Ordos Desert and opened the Northern Silk Road. He conquered the southern kingdoms of Minyue and Nanyue, the Korean kingdom of Gojoseon, and the southwestern kingdom of Dian. Diplomatic missions, most famously Zhang Qian's journey to Central Asia initiated in 139 BC, established Han contact with the Western Regions, leading to increased trade and cultural exchange.

### Governance, Culture, and Religion
Emperor Wu adopted Confucianism as the state ideology and founded the Imperial University in 136 BC to train bureaucrats. He also employed harsh Legalist methods of control, executing thousands for treason or witchcraft. His reign saw significant cultural developments, including the establishment of the Imperial Music Bureau (*yuefu*) around 120 BC, which collected folk songs and promoted new poetic forms. He was deeply interested in religion and immortality, patronizing shamans and performing the Feng and Shan sacrifices at Mount Tai in 110 BC.

### Late Reign and Succession Crisis
His later years were marked by paranoia, extravagant spending, and the devastating Witchcraft Persecutions (starting 96 BC). These culminated in the tragic Crown Prince Ju's Revolt of 91 BC, where his heir was framed for witchcraft, rebelled, and was killed. In his final years, Emperor Wu expressed regret for his harsh policies in the Repenting Edict of Luntai (92 BC). He died in 87 BC, having arranged for his young son Liu Fuling (Emperor Zhao) to succeed him under the regency of Huo Guang.

## Terms
- **Heqin**: A policy of appeasement involving marriage alliances and gifts to the Xiongnu to maintain peace.
- **Confucianism**: A philosophical system emphasizing morality, social harmony, and proper governance, which became the state ideology under Emperor Wu.
- **Legalism**: A philosophy advocating strict laws and harsh punishments to maintain order, which influenced Emperor Wu's governing style.
- **Western Regions (Xiyu)**: The area of Central Asia east of the Pamir Mountains, with which the Han established diplomatic and trade relations.
- **Imperial Music Bureau (Yuefu)**: A government office established to collect folk music and compose ritual and court music.
- **Feng and Shan sacrifices**: Ancient imperial rituals performed at Mount Tai to worship heaven and earth.
- **Taichu calendar**: A reformed calendar implemented in 104 BC that set the first month as the start of the year.
- **Insider court (Neichao)**: A group of officials appointed directly by the emperor to bypass the traditional bureaucracy.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The extent to which Emperor Wu was personally aware of or influenced by Buddhism, given the cultural exchanges via the Silk Road during his reign.
- The precise motivations and sequence of events leading to the Crown Prince Ju's revolt, and whether Emperor Wu initially intended to depose him.
- The historical assessment of his legacy, balancing his immense expansion and cultural achievements against the human and economic costs of his later reign.

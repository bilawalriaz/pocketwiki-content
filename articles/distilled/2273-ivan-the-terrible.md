# Ivan the Terrible

## Overview
Ivan IV Vasilyevich, known as Ivan the Terrible, was the first Tsar of all Russia, reigning from 1547 to 1584. His rule marked Russia's transformation from a medieval state into a multi-ethnic empire, but this expansion came at a devastating human and economic cost. His reign established the model of autocratic tsarist power and set the stage for the political instability that followed his death.

## Timeline
- **25 August 1530** — Born, eldest son of Grand Prince Vasili III.
- **1533** — Succeeds his father as Grand Prince of Moscow at age three.
- **16 January 1547** — Crowned Tsar of all Russia at age 16, the first Russian monarch to receive this title.
- **1550** — Issues the Sudebnik, a revised legal code.
- **1552** — Conquers the Kazan Khanate, bringing the Volga River under Russian control.
- **1556** — Conquers the Astrakhan Khanate, securing the entire Volga.
- **1558** — Launches the Livonian War for Baltic access; establishes the Moscow Print Yard.
- **3 December 1564** — Abdicates temporarily, then returns with absolute power, creating the *oprichnina*.
- **1570** — Orders the massacre of Novgorod by the *oprichniki*.
- **1571** — Crimean Tatars burn Moscow.
- **1572** — Abolishes the *oprichnina* after its military failure.
- **1580** — Cossack leader Yermak begins the conquest of Siberia.
- **19 November 1581** — Kills his eldest son and heir, Ivan Ivanovich, in a rage.
- **28 March 1584** — Dies, leaving the throne to his ineffectual son Feodor.

## Body

### Rise to Power and Early Reforms
Ivan IV became Grand Prince at age three after his father's death. His mother, Elena Glinskaya, served as regent until her death in 1538, after which feuding boyar families controlled the regency, fostering Ivan's lifelong distrust of the nobility. Crowned Tsar in 1547, he initially ruled with a reformist "Chosen Council." This period saw the creation of the *Zemsky Sobor* (a representative assembly), the *streltsy* (a standing army), and the *Sudebnik* legal code. He also strengthened the Orthodox Church and introduced local self-government.

### Conquests and the Oprichnina
Ivan's reign saw massive territorial expansion. He conquered the Kazan and Astrakhan Khanates, securing the Volga River and establishing Russia as a multi-ethnic empire. However, after personal losses and perceived betrayals, his rule turned repressive. In 1564, he created the *oprichnina*, a state-within-a-state under his personal control, policed by the *oprichniki*. This system was used to purge the nobility, culminating in the 1570 massacre of Novgorod. The *oprichnina* caused severe economic disruption and social terror before its abolition in 1572.

### Foreign Policy and the Livonian War
Ivan sought access to the Baltic Sea, triggering the Livonian War (1558–1583). The prolonged conflict against Poland-Lithuania, Sweden, and others drained Russia's resources and ended in defeat, depriving Russia of Baltic ports. His reign also saw the first major Russo-Turkish conflict and devastating Crimean Tatar raids, including the burning of Moscow in 1571. Conversely, he began the Russian conquest of Siberia via Cossack proxies and established trade ties with England.

### Personal Life and Legacy
Ivan's personal life was marked by multiple marriages and volatile behavior. He is widely believed to have killed his heir, Ivan Ivanovich, in 1581. His death left the throne to his weak son Feodor, whose childless reign ended the Rurik dynasty and led to the Time of Troubles. Ivan's legacy is the establishment of absolute tsarist autocracy, but his policies left the economy in ruins and the state on the brink of collapse.

## Terms
*   **Tsar**: The title adopted by Ivan IV, signifying an absolute monarch with divine authority, equivalent to a Caesar or emperor.
*   **Oprichnina**: A separate territory and system of government created by Ivan in 1564, under his direct control and policed by the *oprichniki*, used to terrorize the nobility.
*   **Oprichniki**: The personal guard and enforcers of the *oprichnina*, granted special privileges and land.
*   **Zemsky Sobor**: A representative assembly of feudal estates convened by the tsar, considered an early form of Russian parliament.
*   **Streltsy**: The first Russian standing army, established by Ivan IV.
*   **Sudebnik**: A revised legal code issued by Ivan in 1550.
*   **Boyars**: The highest rank of the feudal Russian aristocracy, whose power Ivan sought to curtail.
*   **Khanate**: A state ruled by a khan, such as the successor states of the Golden Horde that Ivan conquered (Kazan, Astrakhan).

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **The Kurbsky Letters**: The authenticity of the famous correspondence between Ivan and the defector Andrey Kurbsky is debated. While most scholars accept them, some, like Edward Keenan, argue they are later forgeries.
*   **The Epithet "Terrible"**: The Russian word *grozny* is more accurately translated as "formidable" or "awe-inspiring." The modern negative connotation of "terrible" was popularized by later historians and Western propaganda.
*   **Ivan's Mental State**: Contemporary accounts describe him as intelligent but prone to paranoia and rage. Historians debate the extent to which his violent actions, including the killing of his son, were products of mental instability versus calculated policy.
*   **The Massacre of Novgorod**: While the event is undisputed, casualty figures vary widely, with modern estimates ranging from 2,000 to 3,000 victims, given the city's depleted population at the time.

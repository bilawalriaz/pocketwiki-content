# History of India (1947–present)

## Overview
Independent India emerged on 15 August 1947, following the end of British rule. Its post-independence journey is defined by the monumental challenge of forging a unified, democratic nation from a partitioned subcontinent, navigating complex internal conflicts, and transforming from a protectionist, state-led economy into a global economic powerhouse. India's sustained democracy, despite numerous crises, and its rise as a major world power make its story a significant case study in nation-building and development.

## Timeline
- **15 August 1947** — India and Pakistan gain independence; Partition causes massive migration and violence.
- **30 January 1948** — Mahatma Gandhi is assassinated.
- **26 January 1950** — The Constitution of India comes into effect, establishing the Republic.
- **1962** — India suffers a defeat in the Sino-Indian War with China.
- **1971** — India intervenes in the Bangladesh Liberation War, leading to the creation of Bangladesh.
- **1974** — India conducts its first nuclear test (Pokhran-I).
- **1975-1977** — The Emergency is declared, suspending civil liberties.
- **1991** — Economic liberalisation reforms are initiated, ending the "Licence Raj."
- **1998** — India conducts a series of nuclear tests (Pokhran-II).
- **2014** — Narendra Modi and the BJP-led NDA win a landslide election victory.
- **5 August 2019** — Article 370, granting special status to Jammu and Kashmir, is revoked.
- **2020-2021** — India is severely impacted by the COVID-19 pandemic.

## Body

### Partition and Integration (1947-1950)
British rule ended with the Partition of India, dividing the subcontinent into Hindu-majority India and Muslim-majority Pakistan. This triggered one of history's largest mass migrations, with 14-18 million people moving and around one million dying in communal violence. Prime Minister Jawaharlal Nehru's government faced integrating over 500 princely states. While most acceded through negotiation led by Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel, difficult cases like Hyderabad were resolved by military action, and the accession of Jammu and Kashmir led to the first Indo-Pakistani War (1947-48). The 1950 Constitution established India as a sovereign, secular, democratic republic with a parliamentary system.

### The Nehruvian Era (1950s-1960s)
Under Nehru, India pursued non-alignment in the Cold War while implementing socialist-inspired economic planning through Five-Year Plans, focusing on heavy industry and public sector enterprises. The States Reorganisation Act of 1956 redrew state boundaries along linguistic lines. This period saw India's first general elections and Congress party dominance. However, India faced major setbacks, including the defeat in the 1962 war with China and the ongoing Kashmir dispute with Pakistan.

### Political Shifts and Crises (1970s-1980s)
The 1970s were marked by the decisive victory in the 1971 war with Pakistan, leading to Bangladesh's independence, and India's first nuclear test in 1974. Domestically, Prime Minister Indira Gandhi declared a state of Emergency from 1975 to 1977, suspending civil liberties. Her defeat in the subsequent election led to a brief period of non-Congress rule. The 1980s saw the rise of insurgencies in Punjab and the Northeast, the controversial Operation Blue Star at the Golden Temple, and the assassination of Indira Gandhi in 1984. Her son, Rajiv Gandhi, became Prime Minister and initiated early economic reforms and investments in technology.

### Liberalisation and Coalition Politics (1990s-2000s)
A balance-of-payments crisis in 1991 led to landmark economic liberalisation under Prime Minister P.V. Narasimha Rao and Finance Minister Manmohan Singh, dismantling the "Licence Raj" and opening the economy. This era saw the rise of coalition politics, with the BJP emerging as a major national force. The period was marked by communal tensions, notably the demolition of the Babri Mosque in 1992, and continued conflict in Kashmir. India conducted nuclear tests in 1998 and fought the Kargil War with Pakistan in 1999. The 2008 Mumbai attacks by Pakistani terrorists severely strained relations.

### The Modi Era (2014-Present)
Narendra Modi and the BJP won a decisive mandate in 2014, ending a decade of Congress-led coalition rule. The government pursued initiatives like "Make in India" and implemented major reforms like the Goods and Services Tax (GST). In 2019, the government revoked the special status of Jammu and Kashmir. The period has been marked by heightened tensions with Pakistan, including the 2019 Balakot airstrike, and with China, culminating in fatal clashes in the Galwan Valley in 2020. The COVID-19 pandemic caused a severe economic contraction and a devastating second wave in 2021. The BJP retained power in the 2024 election but lost its single-party majority, leading to a coalition government.

### Economic Transformation
Post-independence India followed a dirigiste model with heavy state regulation and import substitution. The 1991 reforms shifted the economy towards liberalisation, privatisation, and globalisation, unleashing rapid growth and transforming India into one of the world's largest economies. The information technology sector became a global leader. However, growth has been uneven, and challenges like poverty, unemployment, and agrarian distress persist. The economy suffered a major contraction during the COVID-19 pandemic but has since rebounded.

## Terms
*   **Partition of India:** The 1947 division of British India into the independent dominions of India and Pakistan, leading to massive communal violence and migration.
*   **Princely States:** Semi-sovereign territories under British paramountcy whose rulers were given the choice to accede to India or Pakistan upon independence.
*   **Non-Aligned Movement:** A forum founded in 1961 for states not formally aligned with any major power bloc during the Cold War, which India helped establish.
*   **Licence Raj:** The elaborate system of licenses, regulations, and red tape required to set up and run businesses in India before the 1991 economic liberalisation.
*   **Green Revolution:** The period of technology transfer and agricultural modernisation from the 1960s that dramatically increased food grain production, making India self-sufficient.
*   **Emergency:** A 21-month period from 1975 to 1977 when Prime Minister Indira Gandhi declared a state of emergency, suspending civil liberties and elections.
*   **Economic Liberalisation (1991):** The reforms that dismantled the "Licence Raj," reduced tariffs, and opened India to foreign investment and global trade.
*   **Article 370:** A provision in the Indian Constitution that granted special autonomous status to the state of Jammu and Kashmir, revoked in 2019.
*   **Naxalism:** A Maoist-inspired communist insurgency in parts of rural India, described by the government as its greatest internal security challenge.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Democratic Backsliding:** Scholars and critics debate whether India's democratic institutions and freedoms have eroded under recent governments, particularly regarding press freedom, judicial independence, and treatment of minorities.
*   **Economic Model:** There is ongoing debate about the success of India's development model, focusing on whether post-1991 growth has been inclusive or has exacerbated inequality between urban and rural areas.
*   **Kashmir Conflict:** The causes, nature, and potential resolution of the conflict in Kashmir remain deeply contested, with differing narratives between India, Pakistan, and Kashmiri groups.
*   **Impact of Partition:** Historians continue to assess the long-term political, social, and psychological consequences of the Partition and the communal violence it unleashed.
*   **Effectiveness of Social Policies:** The efficacy of policies like reservations (affirmative action) and poverty alleviation programs in addressing deep-rooted caste and economic inequalities is frequently debated.

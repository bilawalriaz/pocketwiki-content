# Juan Manuel Fangio

## Overview
Juan Manuel Fangio was an Argentine racing driver who dominated the first decade of Formula One, winning a record five World Drivers' Championship titles between 1950 and 1958. His career is defined by exceptional skill, longevity, and a record for the highest winning percentage in the sport's history, making him a foundational figure in motorsport.

## Timeline
- **24 June 1911** — Born in Balcarce, Argentina.
- **1938** — Debuts in Argentine stock car racing (Turismo Carretera).
- **1940** — Wins the Grand Prix International Championship; becomes Argentine TC champion.
- **1947–1949** — Competes in Europe, achieving success in Grand Prix races.
- **1950** — Makes Formula One debut; wins first F1 World Championship with Alfa Romeo in 1951.
- **1952** — Suffers severe neck injury in a crash at Monza.
- **1953** — Wins the Carrera Panamericana in Mexico.
- **1954** — Wins second World Championship, driving for both Maserati and Mercedes-Benz.
- **1955** — Wins third World Championship with Mercedes-Benz; wins the Argentine Grand Prix in extreme heat.
- **1956** — Wins fourth World Championship with Ferrari.
- **1957** — Wins fifth and final World Championship with Maserati; achieves legendary victory at the German Grand Prix.
- **23 Feb 1958** — Kidnapped in Havana, Cuba, before the Cuban Grand Prix; released unharmed.
- **1958** — Retires from Formula One after the French Grand Prix.
- **17 July 1995** — Dies in Buenos Aires, Argentina, aged 84.

## Body

### Early Life and Argentine Racing
Born to Italian immigrant parents, Fangio left school at 13 to work as an auto mechanic. After military service, he opened a garage and began racing in 1936. He became a dominant force in Argentine long-distance road racing, winning the grueling 1940 Gran Premio del Norte and the Argentine Turismo Carretera championship in 1940 and 1941. His success, backed by the Argentine government, led to a move to Europe in 1948 to compete at the highest level.

### Formula One Career and World Championships
Fangio debuted in the inaugural 1950 F1 season with Alfa Romeo. He won his first World Championship in 1951. After a year without a competitive car and a serious crash in 1952, he returned with Maserati. He then won four consecutive titles (1954-1957), becoming the only driver to win championships with four different teams: Alfa Romeo, Maserati, Mercedes-Benz, and Ferrari. His driving style emphasized preserving the car to ensure a finish, a crucial strategy given the unreliability of 1950s machinery.

### Key Victories and Driving Conditions
Fangio's victories often came under extreme conditions. In the 1955 Argentine Grand Prix, he drove the entire three-hour race alone in 40°C heat, setting the fastest lap. His final win at the 1957 German Grand Prix is considered one of the greatest drives in history; after a disastrous pit stop, he drove with unprecedented risk to catch and pass the leaders. He also excelled in endurance sports car racing, winning events like the 1953 Carrera Panamericana and the 12 Hours of Sebring.

### Later Life and Legacy
After retiring, Fangio remained involved in motorsport as an executive and ambassador. He was kidnapped briefly in 1958 by Cuban revolutionaries seeking to embarrass the Batista regime. He was posthumously confirmed to be the father of three sons through DNA testing. His record of five World Championships stood for 46 years until broken by Michael Schumacher. He is revered as "El Maestro" (the master) and is consistently ranked by statistical analysis as one of the greatest Formula One drivers of all time.

## Terms
*   **Formula One (F1):** The highest class of international auto racing for single-seater formula racing cars, as defined by the Fédération Internationale de l'Automobile (FIA).
*   **World Drivers' Championship (WDC):** The premier competition in Formula One, awarded to the most successful driver of the season based on points.
*   **Grand Prix:** A major international motor racing event, especially a Formula One race.
*   **Turismo Carretera (TC):** A popular Argentine stock car racing series.
*   **Pole Position:** The starting position at the head of the grid, awarded to the driver with the fastest qualifying time.
*   **Endurance Racing:** A form of motorsport where cars compete over long distances, often requiring multiple drivers and pit stops (e.g., Mille Miglia, Carrera Panamericana).
*   **Shared Drive:** A practice in early F1 where a driver could take over a teammate's car during a race if their own failed, sharing the championship points earned.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Greatest of All Time (GOAT):** While statistical analyses often rank Fangio as the best driver in F1 history due to his winning percentage and era-adjusted performance, debates persist about comparing drivers across vastly different technological and safety eras.
*   **Paternity and Legacy:** The posthumous confirmation of his three sons through DNA testing added a complex and previously unknown chapter to his personal legacy, leading to legal battles over his estate.

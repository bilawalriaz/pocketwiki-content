# Lake Balkhash

## Overview
Lake Balkhash is a large, sickle-shaped lake in southeastern Kazakhstan and one of Asia's largest. It is a vital but fragile ecosystem and economic resource, currently threatened by significant water level decline and pollution due to human activity, drawing comparisons to the environmental disaster of the Aral Sea.

## Timeline
- **c. 103 BC–8th century** — Known to Chinese as Yibohai (夷播海) or Bugu/Buku/Busu.
- **1759** — Marked the northwestern limit of Qing suzerainty.
- **1864** — Ceded to the Russian Empire under the Treaty of Tarbagatai.
- **1910** — Lake area estimated at 23,464 km².
- **1930** — Systematic fish breeding begins.
- **1946** — Lake area shrinks to 15,730 km².
- **1970** — Kapshagay Hydroelectric Power Plant built on the Ili River.
- **1987** — Water level reaches a modern minimum after reservoir filling.
- **1991** — Becomes part of independent Kazakhstan.
- **2000** — "Balkhash 2000" conference addresses ecosystem management.
- **2024** — Referendum resolves to build a nuclear power plant near the lake.

## Body

### Geography and Formation
Lake Balkhash lies in the Balkhash-Alakol Basin, a closed (endorheic) depression formed during the Neogene and Quaternary periods. It is fed primarily by the Ili River (73–80% of inflow) and other rivers like the Karatal, but has no outflow. The lake is divided by the Saryesik Peninsula into two distinct parts connected by the Uzynaral Strait. The western half is larger, shallower, and fresh, while the eastern half is deeper and saline. Historically, the lake was much larger and part of an ancient sea.

### Hydrology and Water Level
The lake's water level fluctuates naturally but has declined significantly due to human intervention. The construction of the Kapshagay Reservoir on the Ili River in 1970 drastically reduced inflow, causing the water level to fall by 2.2 metres between 1970 and 1987. While levels partially recovered by 2005 due to high precipitation, long-term shrinkage continues. The lake's salinity varies greatly, from about 0.74 g/L in the west to 3.5–6 g/L in the east.

### Ecology and Climate
The region has a continental climate with high evaporation. The lake's ecosystem includes riparian forests, reed beds, and diverse fish species, including native marinka and perch. However, biodiversity has declined since 1970 due to water quality deterioration, habitat loss in the Ili delta, and pollution. The delta area has shrunk, and numerous smaller surrounding lakes have disappeared. The lake is a habitat for 120 bird species, including several endangered ones.

### Economy and Infrastructure
The largest city on the lake is Balkhash (pop. ~66,000), centered on mining and metallurgy. The fishing industry, once significant, has collapsed from 30,000 tonnes annually in the 1960s to 6,600 tonnes by the 1990s. Navigation exists on the lake and connected waterways. Energy projects, including the Kapshagay dam and a proposed nuclear plant, are major features of the regional economy.

### Environmental Threats
The primary threat is the diversion of water from feeder rivers, especially the Ili, for irrigation and industry, leading to shallowing and increased salinity. Industrial pollution from mining and metallurgy, particularly from the Balkhash plant, deposits heavy metals into the lake. Transboundary issues with China, which consumes significant Ili River water, exacerbate the problem. Desertification and dust storms from dried lakebeds are growing concerns.

## Terms
- **Endorheic basin:** A closed drainage basin that retains water and allows no outflow to other external bodies of water.
- **Orogeny:** The process of mountain formation through large-scale structural deformation of the Earth's crust.
- **Neogene and Quaternary:** Geological periods spanning from about 23 million years ago to the present.
- **Sill:** A shallow ridge or strait connecting two larger bodies of water.
- **Salinity:** The concentration of dissolved salts in water.
- **Delta:** A landform created by deposition of sediment that is carried by a river as the flow leaves its mouth and enters slower-moving or stagnant water.
- **Evaporation rate:** The rate at which water turns into vapor, a key factor in lake water balance.
- **Endemic:** Native and restricted to a certain place (e.g., a plant species found only at Lake Balkhash).

## Debates and open questions
- The long-term sustainability of the lake's water level and ecosystem is debated, with comparisons to the Aral Sea disaster.
- The environmental impact of proposed energy projects, particularly the nuclear power plant, is a subject of concern and opposition.
- The effectiveness of international cooperation, especially with China, to manage shared water resources like the Ili River is uncertain and politically sensitive.

# Globe

## Overview
A globe is a spherical model of Earth or the celestial sphere, serving as a three-dimensional alternative to flat maps. Unlike maps, globes do not distort the shape or size of large features, making them uniquely accurate for representing planetary surfaces. They have been used for millennia for education, navigation, and astronomical study.

## Timeline
- **c. 150 BC** — The Globe of Crates, the first known globe, is described by Strabo.
- **2nd century AD** — The Farnese Atlas, the oldest surviving celestial globe, is carved in the Roman Empire.
- **1276** — The Persian astronomer Jamal ad-Din introduces a terrestrial globe to China.
- **1492** — Martin Behaim creates the Erdapfel, the oldest surviving terrestrial globe.
- **c. 1510** — The Hunt–Lenox Globe is made, possibly the source of "Hic Sunt Dracones."
- **1507** — Martin Waldseemüller creates a facsimile globe showing the Americas.
- **1570s** — Taqi ad-Din constructs a "remarkably modern-looking" terrestrial globe in Constantinople.
- **1802** — Globe-making methods are illustrated in *The English Encyclopedia*.
- **1851** — Wyld's Great Globe, a hollow 18.39 m diameter structure, opens in London.
- **1961** — The Globus IMP navigation instrument is first used in Soviet spacecraft.
- **1998** — Eartha, the world's largest rotating globe (12 m diameter), is constructed.
- **2001** — The Soyuz spacecraft replaces the Globus IMP with a digital map.

## Body

### Terrestrial and Planetary Globes
A globe is the only representation that avoids the distortion inherent in flat map projections. Most globes are scaled models of Earth, commonly with a circumference of one metre (1:40 million scale) or a diameter of one foot (1:42 million scale). They often feature raised relief to exaggerate topography, as true-scale elevations would be invisible. Globes typically display parallels, meridians, country boundaries, and sometimes an analemma—a diagram showing the Sun's apparent annual motion. They are usually mounted with a 23.5° axial tilt to visualize Earth's seasons.

### Celestial Globes
Celestial globes map the positions of stars and constellations but exclude the Sun, Moon, and planets due to their variable locations. A key challenge is that a globe viewed from the outside presents a reversed image compared to the sky's perspective from Earth. Historically, these globes reflected geocentric models like Ptolemy's, which placed a static Earth at the center of rotating celestial spheres. This view persisted until Copernicus proposed a heliocentric system.

### History
The concept of a spherical Earth was established by Greek astronomy in the 3rd century BC, leading to the first terrestrial globe by Crates of Mallus in the mid-2nd century BC. No ancient terrestrial globes survive. Early globes were also made in the Islamic world. In medieval Europe, no known globes were made before the 15th century. The oldest extant terrestrial globe is Martin Behaim's 1492 Erdapfel. The first seamless celestial globe was built by Mughal scientists. In the 20th century, electro-mechanical Globus IMP devices were used for navigation in Soviet and Russian spacecraft until 2001.

### Manufacture
Traditionally, globes were made by gluing printed paper gores (tapered strips) onto a sphere, often of wood. More gores reduced stretching and crumpling. Modern globes are often made from thermoplastic; a flat, printed plastic disk is molded into a hemisphere, and two halves are joined. Globes are typically mounted with a 23.5° axial tilt. In the 1800s, small pocket globes were status symbols.

## Terms
- **Terrestrial globe**: A spherical model of Earth.
- **Celestial globe**: A spherical model of the celestial sphere, showing star positions.
- **Gores**: Long, tapered strips of paper used to map a flat image onto a sphere.
- **Analemma**: A diagram showing the Sun's apparent motion in the sky over a year.
- **Topography**: The arrangement of physical features on land.
- **Bathymetry**: The measurement of depth in bodies of water.
- **Geocentric model**: A model of the universe with Earth at the center.
- **Heliocentric model**: A model of the solar system with the Sun at the center.
- **Globus IMP**: An electro-mechanical navigation instrument used in Soviet/Russian spacecraft.

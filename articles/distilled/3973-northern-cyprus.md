# Northern Cyprus

## Overview
Northern Cyprus, officially the Turkish Republic of Northern Cyprus (TRNC), is a de facto state occupying the northern third of the island of Cyprus. It is recognized only by Turkey, while the international community considers it territory of the Republic of Cyprus under Turkish military occupation. Its existence stems from a 1974 Turkish invasion following a coup, and its lack of recognition creates significant political, economic, and social challenges.

## Timeline
- **August 1960** — Cyprus gains independence from the UK with a power-sharing constitution for Greek and Turkish Cypriots.
- **1963** — Intercommunal violence erupts after President Makarios proposes constitutional changes; Turkish Cypriots withdraw from government.
- **15 July 1974** — A coup d'état backed by the Greek military junta overthrows President Makarios.
- **20 July 1974** — Turkey invokes the Treaty of Guarantee and invades Cyprus, capturing the northern third.
- **2 August 1975** — A population exchange agreement is signed, leading to the movement of ~196,000 Greek Cypriots south and ~42,000 Turkish Cypriots north.
- **15 November 1983** — The Turkish Federated State of Cyprus unilaterally declares independence as the TRNC.
- **2004** — A UN-brokered reunification plan (Annan Plan) is rejected by Greek Cypriots in a referendum but accepted by Turkish Cypriots. Cyprus joins the EU as a whole, with EU law suspended in the north.
- **2011** — Turkish Cypriots protest against economic reforms.
- **October 2020** — Ersin Tatar, favoring independence over reunification, is elected president of the TRNC.

## Body

### History and Conflict
Cyprus's 1960 independence was designed as a partnership between Greek Cypriots (who favored *enosis*, or union with Greece) and Turkish Cypriots (who favored *taksim*, or partition). The partnership collapsed into intercommunal violence in 1963, leading to the displacement of Turkish Cypriots into enclaves. A 1974 coup by Greek Cypriot nationalists seeking *enosis* prompted a Turkish military invasion, which resulted in the de facto division of the island, a massive population exchange, and the displacement of Greek Cypriots from the north. The TRNC declared independence in 1983, a move rejected by UN Security Council Resolution 541. Reunification efforts, notably the 2004 Annan Plan, have failed, primarily due to rejection by Greek Cypriots.

### International Status and Politics
The TRNC is a semi-presidential republic but is a member of no major international organization except as an observer under names like "Turkish Cypriot State." Its sole recognition comes from Turkey, which maintains a large military force (30,000–40,000 troops) there, considered an illegal occupation force by the UN and international community. This reliance on Turkey leads some experts to label the TRNC a "puppet state," though others dispute this, citing its independent elections and government. The economy is heavily dependent on Turkish support and is hampered by an international embargo, as the Republic of Cyprus (the recognized government) has closed the north's ports and airports to international shipping.

### Geography and Demographics
Northern Cyprus covers 3,355 km², featuring the Kyrenia mountain range and the Mesaoria plain. Its population, last officially counted at 294,906 in 2011, is predominantly Turkish-speaking and Sunni Muslim, though society is largely secular. The population includes indigenous Turkish Cypriots and settlers from Turkey, a demographic mix that is politically contentious. Small Greek Cypriot and Maronite communities remain, primarily in the Karpaz Peninsula. The economy is service-based, with tourism and education as key sectors, but is constrained by its lack of international recognition and infrastructure links.

## Terms
- **De facto state**: A political entity that functions as an independent state but lacks widespread international legal recognition.
- **Enosis**: The political movement for the union of Cyprus with Greece.
- **Taksim**: The political movement for the partition of Cyprus into separate Greek and Turkish zones.
- **Green Line**: The UN-patrolled buffer zone dividing the island, running through Nicosia.
- **Treaty of Guarantee**: A 1960 treaty that gave Greece, Turkey, and the UK the right to intervene to protect Cyprus's constitutional order.
- **Annan Plan**: A 2004 UN proposal for reunifying Cyprus, which was rejected by Greek Cypriots in a referendum.
- **Embargo**: The international restriction on direct trade and travel to Northern Cyprus, enforced because the Republic of Cyprus (the recognized government) has closed its ports and airports.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Puppet State vs. Independent Entity**: Whether Northern Cyprus is a puppet state of Turkey due to its military and economic dependence, or an independent political entity with its own democratic processes.
- **Population Demographics**: The disputed ratio of indigenous Turkish Cypriots to settlers from Turkey, which has significant political implications.
- **Reunification Prospects**: The ongoing failure to resolve the Cyprus problem and the future of the island's division, especially given the election of leaders in the north who favor independence over reunification.
- **International Embargo**: The impact of the international embargo on Northern Cyprus's development and whether pledges by the EU to ease isolation will be fulfilled.

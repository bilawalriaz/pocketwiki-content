# Thomas Aquinas

## Overview
Thomas Aquinas was a Dominican friar, priest, and one of the most influential thinkers in the history of Catholic theology and Western philosophy. He is considered the father of Thomism, a school of thought that encompasses both theology and philosophy. Aquinas' works have had a profound impact on modern virtue ethics, aesthetics, and cognitive theory. His philosophy is characterized by the integration of faith and reason, and he is known for his commentaries on Aristotle's works.

## Body
### Early Life and Education
Thomas Aquinas was born in the family castle of Roccasecca, near Aquino, Italy, around 1225. He began his early education at Monte Cassino and later studied at the University of Paris, where he met Dominican scholar Albertus Magnus. Thomas followed Albertus to Cologne, where he taught as an apprentice professor and wrote several commentaries on Aristotle's works.

### Academic Career
Thomas taught in Paris as an apprentice professor and later became a baccalaureus Sententiarum. He wrote several works, including the Summa Theologiae, Summa contra Gentiles, and commentaries on Christian Scripture and Aristotle. Thomas was involved in the dispute between secular masters and masters of the mendicant orders and wrote several works in defense of the mendicant orders.

### Later Career and Death
Thomas took leave from the University of Paris and established a studium generale in Naples. He worked on the third part of the Summa Theologiae and preached to the people of Naples every day in Lent of 1273. Thomas died on March 7, 1274, while giving commentary on the Song of Songs.

### Legacy and Veneration
Thomas Aquinas was canonized as a saint in 1323 and declared a Doctor of the Church in 1567. His feast day is celebrated on January 28th, and he is honored as the patron saint of all Catholic educational establishments. Thomas' works have had a profound impact on Catholic theology and Western philosophy, and he remains one of the most influential thinkers in the history of Catholic theology.

## Terms
* **Thomism**: a school of thought that encompasses both theology and philosophy, founded by Thomas Aquinas.
* **Dominican Order**: a Catholic order of priests and brothers, founded by St. Dominic, to which Thomas Aquinas belonged.
* **Scholasticism**: a philosophical and theological movement that emerged in the Middle Ages, characterized by the use of reason and Aristotelian philosophy to understand Christian doctrine.
* **Aristotelianism**: a philosophical movement that emphasizes the use of reason and observation to understand the world, based on the works of Aristotle.
* **Virtue ethics**: a philosophical approach that emphasizes the development of character traits (virtues) as the foundation of moral behavior.

## Debates and Open Questions
* **The relationship between faith and reason**: Thomas Aquinas' integration of faith and reason has been the subject of ongoing debate and discussion in the history of Catholic theology and Western philosophy.
* **The nature of God**: Thomas' concept of God as a simple, perfect, and infinite being has been the subject of ongoing theological and philosophical debate.
* **The problem of evil**: Thomas' discussion of the nature of evil and its relationship to God's existence has been the subject of ongoing philosophical and theological debate.

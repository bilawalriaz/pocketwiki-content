# Zhang Heng

## Overview
Zhang Heng (AD 78–139) was a Chinese polymath of the Eastern Han dynasty whose work spanned astronomy, mathematics, seismology, engineering, and literature. He is celebrated for inventing the world's first seismoscope and water-powered armillary sphere, and for his influential poetry and philosophical writings. His career illustrates the integration of science, technology, and statecraft in ancient China, and his inventions laid groundwork for later Chinese mechanical engineering.

## Body

### Early Life and Education
Zhang Heng was born into a distinguished but not affluent family; his grandfather Zhang Kan had supported the restoration of the Han dynasty. After his father's death, he was raised by his mother and grandmother. At age 17, he left home to study in the capitals, becoming well-versed in the classics and befriending scholars like Cui Yuan and Ma Rong. He initially declined government appointments, preferring to study. At 23, he returned to Nanyang as an Officer of Merit, gaining experience in official writing. He began his astronomical studies at age 30.

### Official Career and Political Stance
Zhang Heng served two terms as Chief Astronomer (115–120, 126–132) and later as Palace Attendant. His career was marked by intellectual integrity; he opposed reforming the calendar based on apocryphal teachings in 123, which made him controversial and blocked his promotion to Grand Historian. As Palace Attendant to Emperor Shun, he warned the emperor about the threat of court eunuchs, which led to political rivalry and his temporary retirement to Hejian Kingdom. He was recalled to the capital in 138 and died there in 139.

### Scientific and Technological Achievements
Zhang Heng made significant contributions across multiple fields. In mathematics, he improved calculations for pi, approximating it as the square root of 10 (≈3.162). In astronomy, his *Ling Xian* theorized a geocentric universe, catalogued 2,500 stars, and explained solar and lunar eclipses as the Moon blocking sunlight. He invented the world's first water-powered armillary sphere (c. 125), using hydraulic power to rotate an astronomical instrument, a design that influenced later clockmakers. He also improved the inflow water clock by adding a compensating tank to regulate water pressure. His most famous invention was the seismoscope (132), a bronze device that could detect the direction of distant earthquakes, as verified when it indicated a quake in Gansu province.

### Literary and Philosophical Works
Zhang Heng was a renowned poet and scholar. His *fu* rhapsodies, such as "Western Metropolis Rhapsody" and "Eastern Metropolis Rhapsody," critiqued the luxury of the elite and warned of moral decline. His poem "Four Sorrows" is among the earliest heptasyllabic *shi* poetry. He also wrote "Responding to Criticism," an early example of the *shelun* (hypothetical discourse) genre, where he defended his choice of virtue over political power. His works displayed deep knowledge of classics, philosophy, and history, and he compiled a commentary on Yang Xiong's *Taixuan*.

### Cartography and Other Inventions
Zhang Heng is credited with establishing the rectangular grid system in Chinese cartography, which allowed for precise map measurements. He presented a physical geography map (*Dixingtu*) in 116. He is also associated with the invention of the odometer (a distance-measuring carriage) and the south-pointing chariot (a non-magnetic directional vehicle using differential gears), though these inventions may have evolved from earlier models.

## Terms
- **Polymath**: A person with wide-ranging knowledge and expertise in multiple fields.
- **Fu (rhapsody)**: A form of classical Chinese literary composition, often elaborate and descriptive.
- **Shi poetry**: A major form of Chinese lyric poetry.
- **Armillary sphere**: An astronomical instrument representing the celestial sphere with rings for celestial coordinates.
- **Clepsydra**: A water clock used for timekeeping.
- **Seismoscope**: An instrument that records the motion of the ground during an earthquake, but not the time record.
- **Geocentric model**: A model of the universe where the Earth is at the center.
- **Rectangular grid system**: A cartographic method using a grid of squares for accurate mapping.
- **Shelun**: A Chinese literary genre of hypothetical discourse or dialogue.

## Debates and Open Questions
Scholars debate the exact mechanics of Zhang Heng's seismoscope, with different reconstructions proposed (e.g., suspended vs. inverted pendulum). Some, like Anthony Barbieri-Low, suggest Zhang designed but did not personally craft his inventions, collaborating with artisans. Comparisons to Ptolemy highlight a key difference: while Zhang's celestial model resembled Ptolemy's geocentric theory, Chinese astronomy did not develop a similarly structured theoretical model, focusing instead on algebraic patterns of planetary motion. The authenticity and preservation of some inventions, like the south-pointing chariot, are also questioned.

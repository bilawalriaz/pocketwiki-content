# Yaroslav the Wise

## Overview
Yaroslav I Vladimirovich (c. 978–1054), known as Yaroslav the Wise, was Grand Prince of Kiev from 1019 to 1054. He is a pivotal figure in Kievan Rus' history, remembered for uniting the principalities, establishing a legal code, promoting Orthodox Christianity and culture, and forging international alliances that shaped Eastern European politics.

## Timeline
- **c. 978** — Born, son of Vladimir the Great and Rogneda of Polotsk.
- **987–1010** — Prince of Rostov.
- **1010** — Transferred to rule Novgorod after his brother's death.
- **1014** — Refuses to pay tribute to Kiev, straining relations with his father.
- **1015** — Vladimir the Great dies; Yaroslav begins war for the throne against Sviatopolk.
- **1016** — Defeats Sviatopolk in first battle; Sviatopolk flees to Poland.
- **1019** — Defeats Sviatopolk decisively, becomes Grand Prince of Kiev. Marries Ingegerd Olofsdotter of Sweden.
- **1024** — Defeated by his brother Mstislav; they divide Kievan Rus' between them.
- **1030** — Captures Tartu (Estonia), renames it Yuryev.
- **1036** — Decisive victory over the Pechenegs near Kiev.
- **1037** — Sponsors construction of Saint Sophia Cathedral in Kiev.
- **1043** — Leads a naval campaign against Constantinople; defeated by storm.
- **1051** — Has Hilarion of Kiev proclaimed metropolitan, challenging Byzantine tradition.
- **1054** — Dies; entombed in Saint Sophia Cathedral.

## Rise to the Throne
Yaroslav's early life is obscure. He was one of many sons of Vladimir the Great, likely by Rogneda of Polotsk, though his exact parentage is debated. He was sent to rule northern lands around Rostov before being transferred to Novgorod in 1010. His relationship with his father was strained, culminating in his refusal to pay tribute in 1014. After Vladimir's death in 1015, Yaroslav fought a bloody war for the Kievan throne against his half-brother Sviatopolk I, who was supported by Polish Duke Bolesław I the Brave. Several brothers were killed during this conflict. Yaroslav prevailed in 1019, rewarding his Novgorodian allies with privileges that laid the foundation for the Novgorod Republic. He likely promulgated the *Russkaya Pravda*, the first legal code in the East Slavic lands, during this period.

## Reign and Policies
**Power Struggles and Division:** Yaroslav's reign was not free of family conflict. He imprisoned his youngest brother Sudislav. In 1024, his brother Mstislav defeated him, leading to a division of Kievan Rus' east of the Dnieper River, with Mstislav ruling from Chernigov until his death in 1036.

**Foreign Policy and Alliances:** Yaroslav pursued a foreign policy centered on a Scandinavian alliance to counter Byzantine influence. He married Ingegerd Olofsdotter of Sweden in 1019. He conducted successful military raids, capturing Tartu (Estonia) in 1030 and the Cherven cities from Poland in 1031. He later allied with Polish King Casimir I the Restorer. A Swedish expedition under Ingvar the Far-Travelled (c. 1035) likely assisted him against Byzantines and Pechenegs. His 1043 naval campaign against Constantinople ended in a storm-driven defeat.

**Defense and Culture:** To protect against nomadic Pechenegs, Yaroslav built a line of southern forts. His victory over them in 1036 was commemorated by the construction of Saint Sophia Cathedral in 1037. He was a major patron of learning, sponsoring the construction of churches and monasteries. In 1051, he had the Slavic monk Hilarion of Kiev made metropolitan, a challenge to Byzantine ecclesiastical control. Hilarion's discourse is considered the first work of Old East Slavic literature.

## Family Life and Legacy
Yaroslav married Ingegerd Olofsdotter in 1019. Their daughters married into European royal houses: Elisiv to Harald Hardrada of Norway, Anastasia to Andrew I of Hungary, and Anne to Henry I of France (she became regent). Their six sons ruled various parts of Kievan Rus', with three—Iziaslav I, Sviatoslav II, and Vsevolod I—succeeding each other as Grand Prince of Kiev. Yaroslav exhorted his sons to live in peace. He founded several towns, including Yaroslavl and Yuryev (now Bila Tserkva). His legacy includes numerous monuments and institutions named after him. He was venerated as a saint by Adam of Bremen in 1075 and was formally added to the calendars of the Ukrainian and Russian Orthodox Churches in the 21st century.

## Terms
- **Grand Prince of Kiev:** The supreme ruler of the Kievan Rus' federation, holding power from the capital city of Kiev.
- **Kievan Rus':** A medieval East Slavic state and cultural entity, centered in Kiev, from which modern Russia, Ukraine, and Belarus trace their origins.
- **Pechenegs:** A semi-nomadic Turkic people who were a major threat to the southern borders of Kievan Rus'.
- **Russkaya Pravda:** The earliest legal code of the East Slavs, traditionally attributed to Yaroslav the Wise.
- **Metropolitan:** The chief bishop of a major ecclesiastical province in the Eastern Orthodox Church.
- **Veche:** A popular assembly in medieval Slavic cities, particularly influential in Novgorod.
- **Varangians:** The Old Norse term for Vikings, who served as mercenaries and traders in Eastern Europe.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Parentage:** There is scholarly debate about Yaroslav's mother. While traditionally considered the son of Rogneda of Polotsk, some historians argue he was the son of Vladimir's later wife, Anna Porphyrogenita, based on naming patterns and his later involvement in Byzantine affairs.
- **Role in Brothers' Deaths:** The Primary Chronicle accuses Yaroslav's rival Sviatopolk of murdering their brothers Boris and Gleb, but some interpretations of the Norse saga *Eymundar þáttr hrings* suggest Yaroslav's own Varangians may have been involved.
- **Fate of His Remains:** Yaroslav's sarcophagus in Saint Sophia Cathedral was opened multiple times. The 2009 opening revealed only a female skeleton, leading to the conclusion that his male remains were lost or stolen, possibly during the 20th century, with theories suggesting they were hidden from the Nazis or taken to the United States.

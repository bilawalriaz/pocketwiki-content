# Hồng Bàng dynasty

## Overview
The Hồng Bàng period is the legendary first era of Vietnamese statehood, spanning from the mythical founding of Văn Lang in 2879 BC to its conquest in 258 BC. It is foundational to Vietnamese national identity, establishing the lineage of the Hùng kings and the origins of a centralized, rice-based civilization in the Red River Delta.

## Timeline
- **c. 2879 BC** — Lộc Tục (Kinh Dương Vương) founds the kingdom of Xích Quỷ.
- **c. 2524 BC** — The kingdom is renamed Văn Lang, with its capital at Phong Châu.
- **c. 2000 BC** — Development of sophisticated agricultural societies and early bronze casting.
- **c. 1200 BC** — Emergence of the Đông Sơn culture, known for its elaborate bronze drums.
- **c. 6th century BC** — Development of tidal irrigation systems for rice paddies.
- **258 BC** — Thục Phán (An Dương Vương) conquers Văn Lang, ending the Hồng Bàng period.
- **258 BC** — Thục Phán establishes the Âu Lạc kingdom, uniting Âu Việt and Lạc Việt tribes.

## Body

### Origin and Name
The name "Hồng Bàng" is the Sino-Vietnamese pronunciation of "鴻龐," meaning a mythical giant bird. The kingdom's original name, Văn Lang, is part of a Southeast Asian word-family (*-ra:ŋ) meaning "human being," with cognates in various regional languages. The earliest historical mentions of Văn Lang appear in Tang dynasty (7th-9th century) Chinese documents referring to the Phong Châu area.

### Founding and Political Structure
According to the 15th-century chronicle *Đại Việt sử ký toàn thư*, the period began when Lộc Tục, a descendant of the mythical Shennong, consolidated tribes into the kingdom of Xích Quỷ ("Red Demon") in 2879 BC. He became the first Hùng king, an absolute monarch. The state was organized into fifteen *bộ* (regions), each ruled by a *lạc tướng* (often a royal family member), who oversaw agricultural hamlets led by male elders. This structure is described as a primitive sovereign state formed for hydraulic cooperation and defense.

### Economy and Culture
The economy was based on irrigated rice cultivation, supplemented by hunting, fishing, and handicrafts. By 2000 BC, a sophisticated agricultural society had developed, with tidal irrigation systems emerging by the 6th century BC. This required tight-knit village communities. The period is archaeologically defined by the Đông Sơn culture (c. 1200 BC), famous for its bronze drums depicting houses, clothing, and customs. Pottery evolved through distinct phases (Phùng Nguyên, Đồng Đậu, Gò Mun) with increasing sophistication in form and decoration before simplifying in the Đông Sơn period.

### End of the Period
The Hồng Bàng epoch concluded in 258 BC when Thục Phán, leader of the Âu Việt tribes, conquered Văn Lang and overthrew the last Hùng king. He united the Lạc Việt and Âu Việt to form the Âu Lạc kingdom, establishing a new capital at Cổ Loa Citadel.

## Terms
- **Hùng Vương (Hùng king):** The title for the legendary absolute monarchs of the Hồng Bàng period.
- **Văn Lang:** The name of the kingdom from c. 2524 BC until 258 BC, centered at Phong Châu.
- **Xích Quỷ:** The original name of the kingdom founded in 2879 BC, meaning "Red Demon."
- **Đông Sơn culture:** An archaeological culture (c. 1200 BC) known for advanced bronze casting, especially drums.
- **Lạc tướng:** Regional governors (*bộ*) appointed by the Hùng kings, often from the royal family.
- **Bộ:** One of the fifteen administrative regions of Văn Lang.
- **Cổ Loa Citadel:** The capital and fortress built by An Dương Vương after his conquest in 258 BC.
- **Âu Lạc:** The kingdom formed by the unification of Âu Việt and Lạc Việt tribes after 258 BC.

## Debates and Open Questions
The historicity of the Hồng Bàng period is heavily debated. The chronology and existence of the Hùng kings are based on later legends and chronicles, not contemporary records. Archaeological evidence confirms advanced Bronze Age cultures like Đông Sơn in the region, but a direct, proven link to the legendary dynasty is not established. The claimed borders of Văn Lang (e.g., to Hunan) are considered unverified by archaeological research.

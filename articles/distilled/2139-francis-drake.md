# Francis Drake

## Overview
Sir Francis Drake (c. 1540–1596) was an English sea captain, privateer, and explorer whose career defined England's early naval rivalry with Spain. He completed the second circumnavigation of the globe (1577–1580), was a key commander against the Spanish Armada in 1588, and was a pioneer of English privateering and involvement in the slave trade. His exploits made him a national hero in England but a pirate ("El Draque") to the Spanish, and his actions were instrumental in the outbreak of the Anglo-Spanish War.

## Timeline
- **c. 1540** — Born at Crowndale Farm, Tavistock, Devon.
- **1562–1569** — Participates in four English slaving voyages to West Africa and the Spanish Indies with John Hawkins.
- **1572–1573** — Leads first independent privateering expedition, raiding the Spanish Main and capturing the Silver Train at Nombre de Dios.
- **1575** — Present at the Rathlin Island massacre in Ireland.
- **1577–1580** — Leads the "Famous Voyage," circumnavigating the globe and claiming "Nova Albion" in California.
- **1581** — Knighted by Elizabeth I aboard the *Golden Hind*; becomes Mayor of Plymouth.
- **1585** — Commands the "Great Expedition to America," attacking Spanish colonies from Santo Domingo to San Agustín.
- **1587** — "Singes the King's beard" by raiding Cádiz, delaying the Spanish Armada.
- **1588** — Serves as vice admiral in the English fleet that defeats the Spanish Armada.
- **1589** — Co-leads the disastrous English Armada against Spain.
- **1595–1596** — Fails in final expedition against Spanish America; dies of dysentery off Portobelo, Panama.

## Body

### Early Career and the Slave Trade
Drake began his seafaring training as an apprentice to his relative, Captain William Hawkins. In the 1560s, he participated in four English slaving voyages organized by his cousin, Sir John Hawkins. These voyages, which involved capturing Africans and selling them in the Spanish Indies, were highly profitable but ended in disaster at the Battle of San Juan de Ulúa (1567–1569). Drake's departure during the battle led to a bitter falling-out with Hawkins and redirected his career toward attacking Spanish possessions.

### Privateering and the Isthmus of Panama
In 1572, Drake led his first independent privateering mission to the Spanish Main. His most famous exploit was the capture of the Spanish Silver Train near Nombre de Dios on 1 April 1573, seizing over 200,000 pesos' worth of treasure. This expedition, aided by Cimarrón (escaped slave) allies, made Drake wealthy and famous in England. It also marked the first time he and his men saw the Pacific Ocean, vowing to sail its waters.

### Circumnavigation (1577–1580)
Drake's circumnavigation was financed by a private syndicate and tacitly supported by Queen Elizabeth I. After disciplining his crew, including executing his co-commander Thomas Doughty for mutiny, Drake navigated the Strait of Magellan. He then sailed north along the Pacific coast of the Americas, raiding Spanish ports and capturing the treasure ship *Nuestra Señora de la Concepción* ("Cacafuego"). In 1579, he landed on the coast of California, claimed the land for England as "Nova Albion," and careened his ship, the *Golden Hind*. He completed the circumnavigation by crossing the Pacific, rounding the Cape of Good Hope, and returning to Plymouth on 26 September 1580 with immense riches.

### Naval Command and the Spanish Armada
Drake's circumnavigation inaugurated an era of open conflict. In 1585, he commanded a major expedition that attacked Spanish colonies in the Americas. In 1587, his raid on Cádiz delayed the Spanish Armada's invasion by a year. In 1588, as vice admiral of the English fleet, he played a crucial role in the defeat of the Armada, capturing the flagship *Nuestra Señora del Rosario* and fighting at the Battle of Gravelines. His subsequent command of the English Armada in 1589 was a costly failure, leading to a temporary loss of royal favor.

### Final Years and Death
Drake's later career was marked by declining success. In 1595, he failed to capture Las Palmas and was defeated at the Battle of San Juan, Puerto Rico. His final attempt to attack Panama was repulsed by the Spanish. Drake died of dysentery on 28 January 1596 and was buried at sea near Portobelo.

## Terms
- **Privateer**: A private ship licensed by a government (via a "letter of marque") to attack and capture enemy shipping during wartime.
- **Circumnavigation**: The act of sailing all the way around the world.
- **Spanish Main**: The mainland coast of the Spanish Empire in the Americas, bordering the Caribbean Sea.
- **Cimarrón**: An African slave who had escaped from Spanish owners and lived independently in the Americas.
- **Armada**: A fleet of warships, specifically referring to the Spanish fleet sent to invade England in 1588.
- **Nova Albion**: The name Drake gave to the region he claimed on the California coast in 1579.
- **Golden Hind**: The name of Drake's galleon during his circumnavigation, originally called the *Pelican*.
- **Scurvy**: A disease caused by vitamin C deficiency, common on long sea voyages.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Birth Date**: Drake's exact birth year is uncertain, with estimates ranging from c. 1539 to 1544 based on conflicting contemporary sources.
- **Nova Albion's Location**: The precise location of Drake's landing in California is debated, with some fringe theories suggesting it was further north, possibly on Vancouver Island.
- **Legacy and Historical Interpretation**: Scholarship has shifted from Victorian-era hero worship to a more critical view that emphasizes his failures, his role in the slave trade, and his actions as a privateer. His legacy is now contested, leading to the renaming of landmarks in California.

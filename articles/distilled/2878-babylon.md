# Babylon

## Overview
Babylon was an ancient city in southern Mesopotamia (modern-day Iraq) that served as the principal cultural and political center of the Babylonia region. It rose to prominence as the capital of two major empires—the Old Babylonian and Neo-Babylonian—and remained a significant urban and religious hub for millennia. Its legacy, including the legendary Hanging Gardens and its role in biblical narratives, has made it one of the most famous cities of the ancient world.

## Timeline
- **c. 2217–2193 BC** — Earliest known mention of Babylon as a small town under Akkadian ruler Shar-Kali-Sharri.
- **c. 1894–1595 BC** — Old Babylonian Empire period, with its peak under King Hammurabi (r. 1792–1750 BC).
- **1595 BC** — City sacked by the Hittite Empire, leading to the rise of the Kassite dynasty.
- **911–609 BC** — Period of Neo-Assyrian domination, including the city's destruction by Sennacherib in 689 BC.
- **626–539 BC** — Neo-Babylonian Empire, with major rebuilding under Nebuchadnezzar II (r. 605–562 BC).
- **539 BC** — Conquered by Cyrus the Great of Persia, becoming part of the Achaemenid Empire.
- **331 BC** — Captured by Alexander the Great, entering the Hellenistic period.
- **11th century AD** — Last known habitation, referred to as the "small village of Babel."
- **2003** — US-led coalition forces establish a military base on the site, causing significant damage.
- **2019** — Babylon is inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

## Body

### Names and Etymology
The name "Babylon" derives from the Akkadian *Bābilim*, meaning "gate of the god(s)," a folk etymology for an original name of unknown meaning, possibly *Babilla*. The city was also known by other spellings, such as *TIN.TIR.KI*. In the Hebrew Bible, it appears as *Babel*, interpreted as meaning "confusion."

### Archaeology and Sources
The site covers about 1,000 hectares, bisected by a branch of the Euphrates. Major mounds include Kasr (site of the ziggurat Etemenanki) and Amran (site of the Esagila temple). Scientific excavation began with Robert Koldewey's German team (1899–1917). Key sources include cuneiform tablets, biblical references, and classical accounts from writers like Herodotus, which are often contradictory.

### Early History and Old Babylonian Empire
Babylon was a minor town under the Akkadian and Ur III empires. It became an independent city-state in the early second millennium BC. The Amorite king Hammurabi founded the Old Babylonian Empire, conquering all of southern Mesopotamia and making Babylon its capital. After his death, the empire fragmented, and Babylon entered a long period of domination by Kassites, Assyrians, and Elamites.

### Middle Babylonian and Assyrian Periods
Following a Hittite sack in 1595 BC, the Kassite dynasty ruled for over four centuries. Babylon was later weakened and fell under the control of the Middle Assyrian Empire. During the Neo-Assyrian Empire, the city was repeatedly rebellious and was completely destroyed by King Sennacherib in 689 BC, though it was later rebuilt.

### Neo-Babylonian Empire
Babylon regained independence under Nabopolassar, who helped destroy the Assyrian Empire. His son, Nebuchadnezzar II, initiated a massive rebuilding program, constructing the Ishtar Gate and possibly the Hanging Gardens. This era also saw the Babylonian exile of the Jews. The empire fell to Cyrus the Great of Persia in 539 BC.

### Persian, Hellenistic, and Later Periods
Under Persian rule, Babylon remained a major administrative and scholarly center. It was captured by Alexander the Great in 331 BC but declined after his death. The city was later ruled by Parthian and Sassanid empires before its final decline following the Muslim conquest in the 7th century AD.

### Modern Era and Damage
The site was excavated extensively in the early 20th century. In the modern era, Saddam Hussein's government conducted controversial reconstructions. During the 2003 Iraq War, the US military established "Camp Alpha" on the site, causing irreparable damage to archaeological layers and monuments. It was designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2019.

## Terms
- **Ziggurat:** A massive terraced structure of ancient Mesopotamia, believed to be a link between heaven and earth.
- **Cuneiform:** The earliest known system of writing, using wedge-shaped marks on clay tablets.
- **Amorite:** A Semitic people who founded several dynasties in Mesopotamia, including the First Dynasty of Babylon.
- **Kassite:** A people who ruled Babylon for over 400 years after the fall of the Old Babylonian Empire.
- **Neo-Babylonian Empire:** The last native Mesopotamian empire, also called the Chaldean Empire, which flourished under Nebuchadnezzar II.
- **Achaemenid Empire:** The first Persian Empire, which conquered Babylon in 539 BC.
- **Hanging Gardens:** One of the Seven Wonders of the Ancient World, purportedly built in Babylon; their existence is debated.
- **Esagila:** The main temple of the god Marduk in Babylon.
- **Etemenanki:** The ziggurat in Babylon, possibly the inspiration for the biblical Tower of Babel.

## Debates and open questions
- The historical existence and location of the Hanging Gardens of Babylon are disputed; some scholars, like Stephanie Dalley, argue they were actually in Nineveh.
- The interpretation of the name "Babylon" as "gate of the god" is increasingly seen as a later folk etymology for an original name of unknown meaning.
- The accuracy of classical descriptions, particularly those of Herodotus, is debated, with some viewing them as authentic accounts and others as Greek folklore.
- The extent of damage caused by the 2003 US military occupation and the long-term impact on the archaeological record remain subjects of concern and study.

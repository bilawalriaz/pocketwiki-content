# Ramanuja

## Overview
Ramanuja (c. 1077–1157) was an Indian Hindu philosopher and social reformer who became the chief proponent of the Vishishtadvaita (qualified non-dualism) school of Vedanta. He challenged the dominant non-dualism of Adi Shankara, providing an intellectual foundation for the devotional (bhakti) movement by arguing that liberation is achieved through loving devotion to a personal God (Vishnu), not through metaphysical realization of an impersonal absolute.

## Timeline
- **c. 1077–1157** — Modern scholarly estimate for Ramanuja's lifespan (traditional date is 1017–1137).
- **Early life** — Born in Sriperumbudur, Tamil Nadu, into a Tamil Brahmin family under the Chola Empire.
- **Early career** — Studies under guru Yādava Prakāśa; they disagree on Vedic interpretation and separate.
- **c. 1100s** — Becomes a priest at the Varadharāja Perumal temple in Kānchipuram, teaching his philosophy.
- **Mid-career** — Inducted into Sri Vaishnavism by Periya Nambi (Mahapurna) after the death of leader Yamunāchārya.
- **Mid-career** — Faces persecution from a Chola king (possibly Kulothunga II); flees to the Hoysala kingdom for 14 years.
- **During exile** — Converts Jain king Bitti Deva (later Vishnuvardhana) to Hinduism; helps build a temple at Melukote.
- **Later life** — Returns to Tamil Nadu after the Chola king's death; reorganizes rituals at Srirangam temple.
- **1157** — Traditional date of death; legacy continued by disciples and split sects (Vadakalai and Thenkalai).
- **2022** — Statue of Equality dedicated to Ramanuja inaugurated in Hyderabad, India.

## Body

### Life and Context
Ramanuja lived during the stable, pluralistic rule of the Chola dynasty, where Vaishnava, Shaiva, Buddhist, and Jain traditions coexisted. He was born into a Tamil Brahmin family and studied under the Advaita Vedanta scholar Yādava Prakāśa, but their frequent disagreements over interpreting the Upanishads led to a separation. Ramanuja then aligned with the existing Sri Vaishnava tradition, following the lineage of the Tamil Alvars and scholars Nāthamuni and Yamunāchārya. Hagiographies, written centuries after his death, describe his life, including miraculous events and persecution by a Chola king, which forced him into exile in the Hoysala kingdom. Modern scholarship suggests his historical life is difficult to reconstruct with certainty.

### Philosophical System: Vishishtadvaita
Ramanuja's core philosophy, Vishishtadvaita, is a form of qualified non-dualism. It posits three eternally real and distinct categories: the material world, countless individual souls (jivas), and the transcendent Brahman (God). Unlike Shankara's Advaita, Ramanuja rejected the idea that the world is an illusion (maya). He argued that souls and matter are the "body" of Brahman, eternally distinct yet inseparably connected to and dependent on Him. Brahman is not an impersonal absolute but a personal God (Vishnu) with attributes like omnipotence and infinite love. Liberation (moksha) is not the merging of the soul into an undifferentiated absolute, but the eternal, joyful contemplation of God's perfection, achieved through devotion (bhakti).

### Epistemology and Scriptural Interpretation
Ramanuja's epistemology is realist, accepting perception, inference, and scriptural testimony as valid sources of knowledge. He uniquely argued that bhakti (devotion) is itself an epistemic state—a direct awareness of Brahman's nature. His hermeneutic method insisted that the Vedas must be interpreted as a unified, doctrinally consistent corpus. He criticized Shankara's school for selectively using passages that supported monism while ignoring those that indicated plurality. For Ramanuja, since the scriptures mention both oneness and difference, the truth must be a qualified non-dualism that incorporates both.

### Social Reform and Legacy
Ramanuja reformed the Sri Vaishnava order by challenging caste discrimination. He admitted people from non-Brahmin castes, whom he called "Tirukulattar" (of noble descent), into the community and temple worship. This liberal policy enhanced the social status of groups like weavers. He organized a network of temples and centers of study, giving bhakti an intellectual basis that made it a major force in Hinduism. His influence is compared to that of Thomas Aquinas in Western Christianity. After his death, the Sri Vaishnava community split into the Vadakalai (Sanskritic) and Thenkalai (Tamil) sects.

## Terms
- **Vishishtadvaita**: Ramanuja's philosophy of "qualified non-dualism," asserting that souls and matter are real, distinct from yet eternally dependent on Brahman.
- **Bhakti**: Devotion to a personal God, which Ramanuja saw as both the path to and the essence of liberation.
- **Moksha**: Spiritual liberation; for Ramanuja, the eternal, joyful contemplation of God's perfection.
- **Brahman**: The ultimate reality; in Ramanuja's view, a personal God (Vishnu) with attributes who is the inner controller of all.
- **Jiva (Atman)**: The individual soul, which is real, eternal, and distinct from Brahman but shares its essential nature of bliss.
- **Maya**: Illusion; Ramanuja rejected Shankara's concept, affirming the reality of the material world.
- **Advaita**: Shankara's philosophy of absolute non-dualism, which Ramanuja opposed.
- **Dvaita**: Madhvacharya's philosophy of dualism, which shares Ramanuja's theism but asserts an eternal, unbridgeable difference between souls and God.
- **Panchasamskaras**: The five sacraments of initiation into the Sri Vaishnava tradition.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Historical Dates**: Ramanuja's lifespan is debated; traditional hagiographies give 1017–1137, while modern scholars based on temple records suggest 1077–1157.
- **Hagiography vs. History**: The reliability of later hagiographies, which contain miraculous accounts, is questioned by scholars who note the difficulty of reconstructing his "historical life."
- **Persecution**: The identity and motives of the Chola king who persecuted Ramanuja (Kulothunga II vs. Adhirajendra/Virarajendra) are debated, as is whether the persecution was religious or personal.
- **Personal Life**: Evidence is uncertain on whether Ramanuja led a married life or was a renunciate monk.
- **Scriptural Interpretation**: A core scholarly debate is between Ramanuja's method of interpreting the Vedas as a unified corpus versus Shankara's method of identifying a core essence within them.

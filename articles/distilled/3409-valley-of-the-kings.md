# Valley of the Kings

## Overview
The Valley of the Kings is an archaeological site in Egypt that served as the principal burial ground for pharaohs and powerful nobles of the New Kingdom (c. 1539–1075 BC). Its rock-cut tombs, decorated with religious texts, provide crucial insights into ancient Egyptian funerary practices and beliefs about the afterlife. The site's exploration, culminating in the discovery of Tutankhamun's tomb, has made it one of the most famous archaeological locations in the world.

## Timeline
- **c. 1539–1075 BC** — Period of use for primary royal burials, from the Eighteenth to the Twentieth Dynasty.
- **c. 1539 BC** — First royal tombs constructed, possibly for Amenhotep I and Thutmose I.
- **c. 1353–1336 BC** — Amarna Period: Royal burials temporarily moved to Amarna under Akhenaten.
- **c. 1336–1327 BC** — Return to Thebes; burial of Tutankhamun (KV62).
- **1817** — Giovanni Belzoni discovers the tomb of Seti I (KV17).
- **1827** — John Gardner Wilkinson establishes the modern tomb numbering system (KV/WV).
- **1922** — Howard Carter discovers the largely intact tomb of Tutankhamun (KV62).
- **1979** — The Valley of the Kings becomes a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
- **2005** — Discovery of a new chamber, later designated tomb KV63.
- **2011–2012** — Excavation and description of tomb KV64.

## Body

### Geology and Hydrology
The Valley is cut into over 1,000 feet of limestone and sedimentary rock. The quality of the rock was inconsistent, which sometimes forced builders to alter tomb plans. The area is subject to infrequent but violent flash floods, which have historically deposited tons of debris into tombs. A major flood at the end of the Eighteenth Dynasty buried several tombs under metres of debris, leading later dynasties to level the valley floor.

### History and Use
The site was chosen for its isolation, the pyramid-shaped peak of al-Qurn, and its reduced access, which aided guarding. It was used for primary burials from approximately 1539 BC to 1075 BC. Despite its name, it also contains tombs of nobles and royal family members. The workers who built and decorated the tombs lived in the nearby village of Deir el-Medina. By the end of the New Kingdom, political decline led to widespread tomb robbery. During the Twenty-first Dynasty, priests moved many royal mummies to caches for protection, such as the Deir el-Bahari cache (TT320).

### Tomb Architecture and Decoration
Tombs evolved from an early "Bent Axis" layout with sharp turns to a later "Straight Axis." A common feature was a "well," initially a flood barrier that later took on symbolic meaning. Decoration primarily featured religious texts guiding the deceased through the underworld, such as the *Amduat*, *Book of Gates*, and *Book of Caverns*. The most famous discovery is the tomb of Tutankhamun (KV62), found largely intact in 1922, though he was a relatively minor king.

### Exploration and Tourism
Modern exploration began with Napoleon's expedition in 1799. Key figures include Giovanni Belzoni, who discovered Seti I's tomb in 1817, and Howard Carter, who found Tutankhamun's tomb in 1922. The site is a major tourist destination, with an average of 4,000–5,000 visitors daily. Access to tombs is restricted to preserve their fragile decorations.

## Terms
- **Wadi**: A valley or streambed that is dry except in the rainy season.
- **New Kingdom**: The period in ancient Egyptian history (c. 1550–1070 BC) when the Valley of the Kings was used.
- **KV**: The modern abbreviation for "Kings' Valley," used to number tombs in the East Valley.
- **WV**: Abbreviation for "West Valley," a separate section of the necropolis.
- **Mortuary Temple**: A temple for the cult of a deceased pharaoh, located away from the hidden tomb itself.
- **Deir el-Medina**: The village of workers who constructed and decorated the royal tombs.
- **Amarna Period**: A brief era of religious revolution under Pharaoh Akhenaten, when the capital and royal burials moved to Amarna.
- **Shabtis**: Funerary figurines placed in tombs to perform manual labor for the deceased in the afterlife.
- **Amduat**: A religious text describing the sun god's journey through the underworld, used in early tomb decorations.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The exact identity and location of the first royal tomb in the valley (possibly for Amenhotep I or Thutmose I) remains unclear.
- The purpose and owner of some tombs, such as the Amarna Period cache KV55, are debated.
- The complete extent of the valley is still unknown; new tombs like KV63 and KV64 continue to be discovered.
- The precise reasons for the shift from "Bent Axis" to "Straight Axis" tomb layouts are not fully understood.

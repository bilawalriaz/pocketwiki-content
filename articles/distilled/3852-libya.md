# Libya

## Overview
Libya is a North African country with a long history of successive civilizations and foreign rule, from ancient Berber tribes and Phoenician colonies to Roman, Arab, Ottoman, and Italian empires. Its modern history is defined by the discovery of vast oil wealth, the 42-year rule of Muammar Gaddafi, and a devastating post-2011 political collapse that has left the country divided between rival governments and militias. Libya's stability and future are critical for regional security and global energy markets.

## Timeline
- **c. 3000 BC** — Berber tribes inhabit the region; name "Libya" first appears in Egyptian inscriptions.
- **630 BC** — Greeks colonize Cyrenaica in eastern Libya.
- **74 BC** — Rome annexes Cyrenaica; Tripolitania later becomes a prosperous Roman province.
- **647** — Arab armies begin the conquest of Libya, introducing Islam.
- **1551** — Ottoman Empire establishes control over Tripoli.
- **1911** — Italy defeats the Ottoman Empire in the Italo-Turkish War and occupies Libya.
- **1943** — Allied forces take Libya from Italy during World War II.
- **1951** — Libya declares independence as a constitutional monarchy under King Idris I.
- **1969** — Muammar Gaddafi leads a military coup, overthrowing King Idris.
- **2011** — A civil war, part of the Arab Spring, leads to the overthrow and death of Gaddafi.
- **2014** — A second civil war begins, splitting the country between rival governments in Tripoli and Tobruk.
- **2020** — A permanent ceasefire is signed between the main warring factions.

## Body

### Ancient and Medieval History
The coastal plain has been inhabited since the Neolithic period. The region saw Phoenician trading posts, Greek colonies like Cyrenaica, and Roman rule that made Tripolitania and Cyrenaica prosperous provinces. Christianity was an early faith. Arab conquests from the 7th century onward brought Islam and led to centuries of Arab migration, which shifted the demographic and cultural landscape. The region later saw rule by various Islamic dynasties before the Ottoman Empire took control in the 16th century.

### Ottoman and Italian Rule
Ottoman control, often indirect, lasted from 1551 until 1911. The Italo-Turkish War (1911–1912) resulted in Italian colonization. Italy unified the regions of Tripolitania, Cyrenaica, and Fezzan into the colony of "Libya" in 1934. Italian rule was marked by brutal suppression of resistance, led by figures like Omar Mukhtar, and significant infrastructure development. During World War II, Libya was a major battleground in the North African Campaign, after which it came under British and French administration until independence.

### Independence and the Gaddafi Era
Libya became an independent kingdom in 1951 under King Idris I. The discovery of oil in 1959 transformed the economy but also fueled discontent. In 1969, Colonel Muammar Gaddafi seized power in a bloodless coup. He ruled for 42 years, establishing a unique political system he called the "Jamahiriya" (state of the masses). His regime was characterized by internal repression, support for international militant groups, and periods of isolation and conflict with the West. Despite oil wealth, political dissent was illegal, and the state maintained extensive social programs.

### Civil Wars and Fragmentation (2011–Present)
The 2011 Arab Spring protests escalated into a civil war, with NATO intervention helping to topple Gaddafi's regime. The subsequent power vacuum led to the rise of rival militias. A second civil war erupted in 2014 between the internationally recognized government in Tripoli and the House of Representatives in Tobruk, backed by General Khalifa Haftar. A 2020 ceasefire led to a unity government, but political rivalries persist. As of 2024, two rival governments operate simultaneously, creating a state of dual power.

### Economy and Geography
Libya's economy is overwhelmingly dependent on oil, which accounts for over half of GDP and 97% of exports. It holds Africa's largest proven oil reserves. The country is mostly desert, with the Libyan Desert being one of the most arid places on Earth. The population of about 7 million is heavily concentrated along the Mediterranean coast. The Great Man-Made River is a massive irrigation project that pumps fossil water from a vast aquifer to coastal cities.

## Terms
- **Jamahiriya**: The "state of the masses" political system established by Gaddafi in 1977, officially described as direct democracy.
- **Senussi**: A Sunni Sufi Islamic order and political movement that was a major force in Cyrenaica before and during the independence era.
- **Barbary Wars**: A series of conflicts in the early 19th century between the United States and the North African states of Tripoli, Algiers, and Tunis over piracy and trade.
- **Great Man-Made River**: A network of pipelines that pumps fossil water from the Nubian Sandstone Aquifer System in the south to the populated northern coast.
- **National Transitional Council (NTC)**: The de facto government established in 2011 by anti-Gaddafi forces during the First Libyan Civil War.
- **Government of National Unity (GNU)**: The interim unity government formed in 2021, based in Tripoli and internationally recognized.
- **House of Representatives (HoR)**: The parliament based in Tobruk, which is part of the rival eastern-based government.
- **HDI**: Human Development Index, a composite statistic of life expectancy, education, and per capita income indicators.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The primary ongoing debate concerns the path to holding national elections and forming a unified, permanent government to replace the current dual-power structure.
- The role and influence of external foreign powers and militias in perpetuating Libya's division remains a critical issue for stability.
- The management and equitable distribution of Libya's vast oil wealth amid political fragmentation is a central challenge for any future unified state.

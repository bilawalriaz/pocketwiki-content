# Scandinavia

## Overview
Scandinavia is a subregion of Northern Europe, most commonly comprising Denmark, Norway, and Sweden, which share strong historical, cultural, and linguistic ties. The term's meaning is fluid, sometimes including Iceland, the Faroe Islands, and Finland due to broader Nordic connections. The region's history is marked by the Viking Age, shifting political unions, and the development of a modern, prosperous "Nordic model" of welfare states.

## Timeline
- **c. 793–1066 AD** — **Viking Age:** Scandinavian peoples engage in large-scale raiding, conquest, and exploration, reaching North America.
- **1397** — The **Kalmar Union** unites Denmark, Norway, and Sweden under a single monarch.
- **1523** — **Sweden leaves the Kalmar Union** under King Gustav I, leading to the formation of separate kingdoms.
- **1536** — The **Kingdoms of Denmark and Norway** enter a personal union, which lasts until 1814.
- **1645–1660** — **Treaties of Brömsebro, Roskilde, and Copenhagen** establish the modern borders between Denmark-Norway and Sweden.
- **1814** — The **Treaty of Kiel** dissolves the Denmark-Norway union; Norway is ceded to Sweden but gains its own constitution.
- **1873** — The **Scandinavian Monetary Union** is established, lasting until World War I.
- **1905** — The **Union between Sweden and Norway is peacefully dissolved**; Norway becomes an independent kingdom.

## Body

### Geography and Climate
Scandinavia's geography is varied, featuring Norwegian fjords, the Scandinavian Mountains, the flat lowlands of Denmark, and numerous lakes and archipelagos in Finland and Sweden. The southern regions have a temperate climate and are the most populous. The climate ranges from a marine west coast in the south and west to subarctic and tundra climates in the north and at high elevations. The Gulf Stream moderates temperatures, making the region relatively mild for its latitude.

### The Concept of "Scandinavia"
The term's etymology is debated, likely deriving from a Proto-Germanic compound meaning "dangerous island," referring to the treacherous sandbanks around Scania (Skåne). It was reintroduced in the 18th century and popularized in the 19th century by the **Scandinavist movement**, which promoted cultural and political unity. In English, "Scandinavia" is often used synonymously with "Nordic countries," but in local usage, it typically refers only to Denmark, Norway, and Sweden. The inclusion of Finland and the Sámi people is a point of political and cultural contestation.

### Languages
Two main language families coexist: the **North Germanic languages** (Danish, Norwegian, Swedish, Icelandic, Faroese) and the **Uralic languages** (Finnish, Sámi). The Continental Scandinavian languages (Danish, Norwegian, Swedish) are mutually intelligible and form a dialect continuum. Finnish and the Sámi languages are unrelated to the Scandinavian languages. Finland is officially bilingual (Finnish and Swedish), and Swedish is a recognized minority language in Finland.

### History: From Vikings to Modern Unions
The Viking Age (c. 793–1066 AD) saw Scandinavian expansion and settlement across Europe and the North Atlantic. Following Christianization, three kingdoms—Denmark, Sweden, and Norway—were forged. They were united in the Kalmar Union (1397), but Sweden seceded in 1523. Denmark and Norway remained in a union until 1814, when Norway was transferred to Sweden after the Napoleonic Wars. The Sweden-Norway union lasted until its peaceful dissolution in 1905. Numerous wars, particularly in the 17th century, shaped the modern borders.

### Economy and Society
In modern times, the Nordic countries have strong economies and are known for their generous welfare systems, high taxation, and significant state involvement. This combination of economic and social policies is often referred to as the **"Nordic model."**

## Terms
*   **Scandinavia:** A subregion of Northern Europe, most commonly defined as Denmark, Norway, and Sweden.
*   **Nordic countries:** A broader political and cultural region including Scandinavia, Finland, Iceland, and their associated territories.
*   **Kalmar Union:** A personal union that joined the three Scandinavian kingdoms under one monarch from 1397 to 1523.
*   **Viking Age:** The period from approximately 793 to 1066 AD, characterized by Scandinavian exploration, trade, and raiding.
*   **North Germanic languages:** The branch of Germanic languages that includes Danish, Norwegian, Swedish, Icelandic, and Faroese.
*   **Uralic languages:** A language family unrelated to the Germanic languages, which includes Finnish and the Sámi languages.
*   **Nordic model:** The economic and social system of the Nordic countries, characterized by a comprehensive welfare state and collective bargaining.
*   **Scandinavism:** A 19th-century political and cultural movement advocating for unity among Scandinavian nations.

## Debates and Open questions
*   **Definitional boundaries:** There is ongoing debate and political contestation over whether Finland, Iceland, and the Faroe Islands should be considered "Scandinavian," with usage differing between English and local languages.
*   **Ethnic vs. demonymic identity:** The term "Scandinavian" can refer to an ethnic/cultural identity (speakers of North Germanic languages) or a demonym (all inhabitants of Scandinavian countries). The inclusion of the Sámi people in the latter category is controversial due to historical assimilation policies.
*   **Etymology of the name:** The origin of the name "Scandinavia" is debated, with theories linking it to words for "danger," "shadow," or pre-Germanic roots.

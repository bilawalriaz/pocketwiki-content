# Saint Petersburg

## Overview
Saint Petersburg is Russia's second-largest city and a major cultural and economic hub, founded by Tsar Peter the Great in 1703 as a "Window to Europe." It served as the capital of the Russian Empire from 1712 until 1918, and its history is deeply intertwined with Russia's modernization, revolution, and resilience, most notably during the devastating Siege of Leningrad in World War II.

## Timeline
- **27 May 1703** — Tsar Peter the Great founds the city on Zayachy (Hare) Island.
- **1712** — Peter moves the capital from Moscow to Saint Petersburg.
- **1914** — City renamed Petrograd to remove Germanic elements during WWI.
- **1917** — February Revolution ends the monarchy; October Revolution sees Bolsheviks seize power.
- **1924** — City renamed Leningrad after Vladimir Lenin's death.
- **1941–1944** — Siege of Leningrad lasts 872 days, causing over one million civilian deaths.
- **1991** — Citywide referendum restores the name Saint Petersburg.
- **2019** — Lakhta Center, Europe's tallest skyscraper, is completed.

## Body

### Founding and Imperial Capital
Peter the Great founded the city to secure a Baltic seaport and modernize Russia. Built by conscripted peasants and Swedish prisoners of war, it became the capital in 1712. The city's layout was planned by architects like Domenico Trezzini, and its early architecture featured Petrine Baroque. After a brief move to Moscow (1728–1730), it remained the imperial capital until 1918. The city was a center of culture and industry, but also of political unrest, including the Decembrist revolt (1825) and the Revolution of 1905.

### Revolution, Soviet Era, and World War II
The February Revolution of 1917 began in the city, leading to the abdication of Tsar Nicholas II. The Bolsheviks' October Revolution later that year transferred power to the Soviets. The government moved to Moscow in 1918, and the city was renamed Leningrad in 1924. During Stalin's rule, many historic buildings were destroyed, and the population suffered during the Great Purge. The city's most traumatic period was the Siege of Leningrad (1941–1944), a 872-day German blockade that resulted in over a million civilian deaths from starvation and bombardment, making it one of history's most lethal sieges.

### Post-Soviet Revival and Modern City
Following the 1991 referendum, the city regained its original name. The post-Soviet transition brought economic hardship, including food rationing, but conditions improved in the 21st century. The city has faced challenges in preserving its UNESCO-listed historic center from controversial modern development, such as the initially proposed Okhta Center skyscraper. Today, it is a federal city hosting key government institutions like the Constitutional Court, and is a major tourist destination known for its architectural heritage, including the Hermitage Museum and the Lakhta Center.

## Terms
- **Petrine Baroque:** An architectural style developed under Peter the Great, characterized by its use of classical elements and Western European influences.
- **Siege of Leningrad:** The prolonged military blockade of Leningrad by German forces during WWII, resulting in catastrophic civilian casualties.
- **Kommunalka:** A type of communal apartment in the Soviet Union where multiple families shared a single dwelling and common facilities.
- **White Nights:** A natural phenomenon in high-latitude cities where the sky does not get fully dark for several weeks around the summer solstice.
- **UNESCO World Heritage Site:** A location officially recognized for its cultural or natural significance, warranting preservation for future generations.
- **Federal City:** A city in Russia that is a federal subject of its own, equal in status to a region or republic.

## Debates and Open Questions
The article highlights ongoing tension between preserving Saint Petersburg's historic architectural ensemble and allowing modern development. A key controversy was the proposed Okhta Center skyscraper, which was ultimately relocated to Lakhta after public outcry and UNESCO concerns. The demolition of older buildings in the historic center since 2005 remains a point of contention for preservationists.

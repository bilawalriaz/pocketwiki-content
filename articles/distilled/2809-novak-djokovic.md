# Novak Djokovic

## Overview
Novak Djokovic is a Serbian professional tennis player widely regarded as the greatest of all time. He holds the records for the most men's singles Grand Slam titles (24) and the most weeks ranked as world No. 1 (428), dominating the sport for over a decade and reshaping its historical benchmarks.

## Timeline
- **22 May 1987** — Born in Belgrade, SR Serbia, SFR Yugoslavia.
- **2003** — Begins professional career.
- **2008** — Wins first Grand Slam title at the Australian Open.
- **2011** — Ascends to world No. 1 for the first time; wins three majors.
- **2015** — Has most successful season, winning three majors and a record 10 Big Titles.
- **2016** — Completes Career Grand Slam and non-calendar year Grand Slam ("Nole Slam").
- **2018** — Returns from injury to win Wimbledon and US Open; completes Career Golden Masters.
- **2021** — Wins three majors; loses US Open final, missing calendar-year Grand Slam.
- **2022** — Deported from Australia ahead of Australian Open due to COVID-19 vaccine status.
- **2023** — Wins record-extending 24th Grand Slam title at the US Open.
- **2024** — Wins Olympic gold medal, completing Career Golden Slam and Career Super Slam.

## Body

### Early Life and Career Beginnings
Born in Belgrade during the Yugoslav Wars, Djokovic began playing tennis at age four. He trained under Jelena Genčić and later moved to Nikola Pilić's academy in Germany in 1999. He turned professional in 2003 and broke into the top 10 by 2007, reaching his first Grand Slam final at the US Open.

### Rise to Dominance and the "Big Three" Era
Djokovic won his first major at the 2008 Australian Open, disrupting the Federer-Nadal duopoly. By 2011, he ascended to No. 1, winning three majors and establishing himself as the third member of the "Big Three." His 2015 season is considered one of the greatest in history, culminating in a non-calendar year Grand Slam in 2016.

### Injury, Comeback, and Record-Breaking
After an elbow injury sidelined him in 2017, Djokovic returned to win Wimbledon and the US Open in 2018, completing the Career Golden Masters. He later surpassed Federer and Nadal's Grand Slam records, winning his 24th major at the 2023 US Open. His career was interrupted by his deportation from Australia in 2022 over COVID-19 vaccination status.

### Playing Style and Achievements
An aggressive baseline player, Djokovic is renowned for his exceptional return of serve, flexibility, and court coverage. He is the only man to hold all four majors simultaneously across three different surfaces and the only player to complete a triple Career Grand Slam. In 2024, he won Olympic gold to complete the Career Golden Slam.

### Off-Court Activities and Legacy
Djokovic founded the Novak Djokovic Foundation and co-founded the Professional Tennis Players Association (PTPA). He is a UNICEF Goodwill Ambassador and has been named one of *Time*'s 100 most influential people. His rivalry with Rafael Nadal is the most prolific in men's tennis history, and he holds a winning record against both Nadal and Roger Federer.

## Terms
- **Grand Slam:** The four major annual tennis tournaments: Australian Open, French Open, Wimbledon, and US Open.
- **Career Grand Slam:** Winning all four Grand Slam tournaments at least once in a player's career.
- **Career Golden Masters:** Winning all nine ATP Masters 1000 tournaments at least once in a career.
- **Career Golden Slam:** Winning all four Grand Slams and an Olympic gold medal in singles over a career.
- **Career Super Slam:** Winning all four Grand Slams, an Olympic gold medal, and the year-end championships in a career.
- **Big Titles:** A category including Grand Slams, ATP Finals, and Masters 1000 events.
- **ATP Masters 1000:** A series of nine prestigious tennis tournaments on the ATP Tour.
- **PTPA (Professional Tennis Players Association):** A player-led organization co-founded by Djokovic to advocate for player interests.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Greatest of All Time (GOAT):** While widely considered the greatest male player due to his records, debate continues, often centering on comparisons with Federer and Nadal.
- **Impact of COVID-19 Stance:** His refusal to vaccinate led to missed tournaments and deportation, with some arguing it affects his legacy, while others praise his principle of personal choice.
- **Rivalry with Carlos Alcaraz:** Their emerging rivalry, with a 5–5 head-to-head, is seen as a potential passing of the torch, though Djokovic remains competitive at an advanced age.

# Dagestan

## Overview
Dagestan is a republic of Russia in the North Caucasus, bordering the Caspian Sea, Azerbaijan, and Georgia. It is one of the most ethnically and linguistically diverse regions in the world, with over 30 ethnic groups and 14 official languages. Its history is marked by successive waves of rule from Persian, Arab, Mongol, and Russian empires, and it remains a region of complex cultural identity and geopolitical significance.

## Timeline
- **643** — Arab conquest of Derbent; Dagestanis become the first people in current Russian territory to convert to Islam.
- **1236–1239** — Mongols conquer Derbent and surrounding areas.
- **1813** — Treaty of Gulistan: Persia cedes southern Dagestan to Russia.
- **1828** — Treaty of Turkmenchay consolidates Russian control over Dagestan.
- **1828–1859** — Imamate of Dagestan leads uprisings against Russian rule during the Caucasian War.
- **1921** — Dagestan Autonomous Soviet Socialist Republic (ASSR) is proclaimed.
- **1991** — Dagestan declares sovereignty within the Russian Federation.
- **1999** — Islamist invasion from Chechnya is repelled by Russian and local forces.
- **2003** — Constitution of the Republic of Dagestan is adopted.
- **2006** — The post of Chairman of the State Council is abolished; the first President of Dagestan is appointed.
- **2022** — Dagestan becomes a center of protests against Russian mobilization for the invasion of Ukraine.

## Body

### Geography and Climate
Dagestan covers 50,300 square kilometers in the North Caucasus, making it Russia's southernmost republic. Its landscape is predominantly mountainous, with the Greater Caucasus in the south and the highest peak, Mount Bazardüzü, at 4,446 meters on the Azerbaijani border. The eastern border is the Caspian Sea. The climate is continental and warm, with precipitation ranging from 250 mm in the northern plains to 800 mm in the mountains.

### History and Rule
The region was part of Caucasian Albania and later a satrapy of the Sasanian Empire, which built the citadel of Derbent. Following the Arab conquest in 7th century, Islam spread from urban centers like Derbent into the highlands. Control later passed to Seljuk Turks, Mongols, and Timurids. From the 16th century, rule alternated between Persian Safavids and, briefly, Ottomans. Russian control was solidified after the Russo-Persian Wars, formalized by the Treaties of Gulistan (1813) and Turkmenchay (1828). This led to the Caucasian War, where highlanders rose under the Muslim Imamate. After the 1917 revolution, Dagestan briefly formed part of the Mountainous Republic of the Northern Caucasus before becoming a Soviet ASSR in 1921. Post-Soviet instability included a 1999 Islamist invasion.

### Demographics and Languages
Dagestan's population exceeds 3.1 million, with Northeast Caucasian peoples (Avars, Dargins, Lezgins, etc.) comprising nearly 75%, Turkic peoples (Kumyks, Azerbaijanis, Nogais) 21%, and Russians 3.3%. Over 30 languages are spoken, mostly from the Nakh-Daghestanian family. Russian is the primary lingua franca. Historically, Kumyk served as a regional lingua franca until the 1930s.

### Religion and Culture
Islam is the dominant religion, practiced by 83% of the population, primarily Sunni of the Shafi'i school, with Shia communities in coastal areas like Derbent. Sufi orders like the Naqshbandiya have been influential. The region has a strong tradition of martial arts, producing numerous champions in freestyle wrestling, boxing, and mixed martial arts (MMA), including UFC champions Khabib Nurmagomedov and Islam Makhachev. Culture includes epic literature, folk dances like the lezginka, and a national dish called khingal-bat.

### Politics and Human Rights
Dagestan is a republic within Russia with a parliament (People's Assembly) and a State Council representing 14 ethnicities. Governance has been marked by corruption and weak institutions. Human rights organizations report abuses including arbitrary detention, torture by security forces, violence against women under customary practices (adat), and systemic discrimination. LGBTQ+ individuals face severe persecution.

## Terms
- **Lingua Franca**: A common language used for communication between people who do not share a native language; in Dagestan, this is Russian.
- **Imamate**: A state governed by an Imam, a religious and political leader; the Imamate of Dagestan led 19th-century resistance to Russian rule.
- **Adat**: Customary law or traditional practices of a community, which in Dagestan can conflict with state law, particularly regarding women's rights.
- **Sufi Orders (Tariqas)**: Mystical branches of Islam focused on inner spiritual development; the Naqshbandiya and Qadiriya are prominent in Dagestan.
- **Nakh-Daghestanian**: A language family spoken in the Northeast Caucasus, including most of Dagestan's indigenous languages.
- **ASSR**: Autonomous Soviet Socialist Republic, a type of federal subject in the USSR; Dagestan was an ASSR from 1921.
- **Raion**: An administrative district; Dagestan is divided into 41 raions.
- **MMA**: Mixed Martial Arts, a combat sport; Dagestan is a renowned global source of elite fighters.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The extent of Persian cultural influence ("Persianisation") in southern Dagestan over centuries is a subject of historical analysis.
- The causes and dynamics of the 1999 invasion and the subsequent insurgency in the North Caucasus involve complex debates about local support, ideology, and Russian policy.
- The effectiveness of Russian governance and anti-corruption measures in Dagestan, given its persistent economic challenges and human rights issues, is an ongoing concern.

# Menelik II

## Overview
Menelik II (1844–1913) was the Emperor of Ethiopia who expanded the empire to its greatest historical extent and secured its independence by defeating Italy at the Battle of Adwa in 1896. He is widely regarded as the founder of modern Ethiopia, centralizing its government and initiating modernization, though his legacy is controversial due to the brutal methods of his southern conquests.

## Timeline
- **17 August 1844** — Born as Sahle Maryam in Ankober, Shewa.
- **1855** — Captured and taken to Magdala by Emperor Tewodros II after his father's death.
- **1 July 1865** — Escapes from Magdala and returns to Shewa.
- **1866** — Proclaimed King (Negus) of Shewa after defeating rival Bezabeh.
- **20 March 1878** — Signs Treaty of Wadara, submitting to Emperor Yohannes IV in exchange for territory.
- **3 November 1889** — Crowned Emperor of Ethiopia following Yohannes IV's death.
- **2 May 1889** — Signs Treaty of Wuchale with Italy.
- **1891** — Formally rejects the Treaty of Wuchale after discovering the Italian version's deceptive clause.
- **1 March 1896** — Leads Ethiopian forces to victory against Italy at the Battle of Adwa.
- **1906** — Suffers a major stroke, leading to his incapacitation.
- **12 December 1913** — Dies in Addis Ababa.

## Body

### Early Life and Captivity
Born Sahle Maryam, he was the son of the Negus (King) of Shewa. After his father's death in 1855, the 11-year-old prince was captured by Emperor Tewodros II and held at the fortress of Magdala. Despite his captivity, Menelik received an education and was treated favorably by Tewodros. He escaped in 1865 as Tewodros's power waned and returned to Shewa, where he was acclaimed king in 1866.

### King of Shewa and Imperial Rivalry
As King of Shewa, Menelik built his power base through alliances, such as with the Oromo general Ras Gobena Dache, and by acquiring modern weapons via European contacts like the Swiss engineer Alfred Ilg. He expanded southward, conquering territories rich in ivory, coffee, and gold, which funded his military. His imperial ambitions brought him into conflict with Emperor Yohannes IV. After a failed alliance with Egypt against Yohannes, Menelik was forced to submit to him via the Treaty of Wadara in 1878, receiving the title of Negus of Shewa in return.

### Emperor and the Battle of Adwa
Following Yohannes IV's death in 1889, Menelik was crowned Emperor. He signed the Treaty of Wuchale with Italy, but upon discovering the Italian version falsely implied a protectorate, he repudiated it in 1891. This led to the First Italo-Ethiopian War. Menelik mobilized a vast, well-armed army and decisively defeated the Italians at the Battle of Adwa on 1 March 1896. The victory secured Ethiopia's sovereignty and full recognition from European powers.

### Modernization and Expansion
Menelik continued to expand the empire, particularly southward, incorporating groups like the Gurage, Sidamo, and Kaffa. He initiated modernization, establishing a cabinet of ministers, a postal system, schools, and hospitals. He granted a concession for the Addis Ababa–Djibouti Railway and reformed the tax and monetary systems. However, his health declined after a series of strokes beginning in 1906, leading to a regency under his wife, Taytu Betul, and later Ras Tessema Nadew.

### Personal Life and Succession
Menelik married three times: first to Altash Tewodros (divorced 1865), then to Bafena Wolde Michael (divorced 1882), and finally to Taytu Betul in 1883. He had no legitimate children but recognized three natural children, including daughters Shoaregga and Zewditu. His grandson, Lij Iyasu (son of Shoaregga), was named heir but was later deposed in favor of Zewditu, who was succeeded by Ras Tafari Makonnen (Haile Selassie).

## Terms
- **Negus**: A royal title meaning "King," used for regional rulers like the King of Shewa.
- **Solomonic dynasty**: The imperial dynasty of Ethiopia, claiming descent from King Solomon and the Queen of Sheba.
- **Katama**: Fortified garrison towns established during Menelik's southern expansion to control new territories.
- **Neftenya**: Soldiers or settlers from the highlands who were granted land in conquered lowland areas, often fearing malaria.
- **Treaty of Wuchale (1889)**: A treaty with Italy whose differing Italian and Amharic versions led to conflict; the Italian version implied a protectorate.
- **Battle of Adwa (1896)**: The decisive Ethiopian victory over Italian forces that preserved Ethiopian independence.
- **Gebbar maderia**: The reformed, centralized tax system introduced by Menelik, linking taxation directly to military needs.
- **Rinderpest**: A viral cattle disease that caused a devastating famine (*Kifou Qen*) in the 1890s, prompting further southern expansion.

## Debates and Open Questions
Menelik's legacy is deeply contested. While celebrated as a symbol of anti-colonial resistance and the architect of modern Ethiopia, he is also criticized for the brutality of his southern conquests. Historians debate whether the atrocities committed during these campaigns, which included massacres and forced assimilation of groups like the Oromo, constitute genocide. His expansionist policies are seen by some as a primary cause of later ethnic tensions within Ethiopia.

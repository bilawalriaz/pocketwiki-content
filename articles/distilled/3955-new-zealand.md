# New Zealand

## Overview
New Zealand is an island country in the southwestern Pacific Ocean, comprising two main landmasses (North and South Islands) and over 700 smaller islands. A developed nation with a population of around 5.3 million, it is known for varied topography shaped by tectonic activity, unique biodiversity from long isolation, and a bicultural foundation from Māori and British colonial history. It is a constitutional monarchy with a parliamentary democracy, a high-income economy, and consistently ranks highly in international measures of quality of life, human rights, and low corruption.

## Timeline
- **c. 1280–1350** — Polynesians settle the islands, developing Māori culture.
- **1642** — Dutch explorer Abel Tasman becomes the first European to sight New Zealand.
- **1769** — British explorer Captain James Cook maps the coastline and becomes the first European to set foot on the islands.
- **1840** — The Treaty of Waitangi is signed between the British Crown and Māori chiefs.
- **1841** — New Zealand becomes a separate Crown colony.
- **1893** — New Zealand becomes the first nation to grant all women the right to vote.
- **1907** — New Zealand is proclaimed a Dominion within the British Empire.
- **1947** — New Zealand adopts the Statute of Westminster, gaining full statutory independence.
- **1975** — The Waitangi Tribunal is established to investigate breaches of the Treaty of Waitangi.
- **1996** — The country adopts the mixed-member proportional (MMP) voting system for elections.

## Body

### Etymology
Dutch explorer Abel Tasman named the islands "Staten Land" in 1642. In 1643, Dutch cartographers renamed it "Nova Zeelandia," later anglicised to New Zealand. The Māori name "Aotearoa" (often 'land of the long white cloud') originally referred only to the North Island. The names "North Island" and "South Island" were formalised in 2013, with their Māori equivalents, Te Ika-a-Māui and Te Waipounamu, also officially recognised.

### History
Polynesian settlers arrived between about 1280 and 1350, developing a distinctive Māori culture. European contact began with Tasman in 1642, followed by Cook's mapping in 1769. The subsequent introduction of the potato and musket transformed Māori agriculture and warfare, leading to the intertribal Musket Wars (1801–1840). The Treaty of Waitangi (1840) paved the way for British sovereignty and the Colony of New Zealand (1841). A series of conflicts, the New Zealand Wars, followed, resulting in the confiscation of large amounts of Māori land. New Zealand gained representative government in 1852 and became self-governing in 1856. It became a Dominion in 1907 and achieved full statutory independence in 1947. A Māori protest movement from the 1960s led to the establishment of the Waitangi Tribunal and ongoing treaty settlements.

### Geography and Climate
New Zealand's topography is shaped by its position on the boundary of the Pacific and Indo-Australian tectonic plates. The South Island is dominated by the Southern Alps, while the North Island is marked by volcanism, including the Taupō Volcanic Zone. The climate is predominantly temperate maritime, with conditions varying sharply from wet on the West Coast to semi-arid in Central Otago. The country has extensive marine resources, with one of the world's largest exclusive economic zones.

### Biodiversity
Geographic isolation for 80 million years led to unique flora and fauna, with about 82% of indigenous vascular plants being endemic. Forests, once covering 80% of the land, were heavily reduced after human arrival. The lack of native land mammals allowed birds like the kiwi and kākāpō to evolve flightlessness. The introduction of humans and invasive mammals caused the extinction of many species, including the moa. Conservation efforts include island sanctuaries and pest control.

### Government and Politics
New Zealand is a constitutional monarchy and parliamentary democracy. The monarch, currently Charles III, is represented by a governor-general. Legislative power is held by the unicameral Parliament, and executive power by the Government led by the prime minister. Since 1996, elections have used the mixed-member proportional (MMP) system. The judiciary is independent. The Realm of New Zealand includes the Cook Islands, Niue, Tokelau, and the Ross Dependency in Antarctica.

### Economy
New Zealand has an advanced, high-income market economy. Historically reliant on agricultural exports to Britain, it underwent major economic liberalisation in the 1980s. The service sector is now the largest, followed by manufacturing and agriculture. Key exports include dairy products, meat, and wood. The country is heavily dependent on international trade and tourism. Wealth is concentrated, and child poverty, particularly among Māori and Pasifika children, is a significant issue.

### Demographics and Society
The population is predominantly urban and concentrated in the North Island. The 2023 census recorded 62.1% as New Zealand European, 17.8% as Māori, with significant Asian and Pasifika minorities. English is the predominant language, with Māori and New Zealand Sign Language also official. The population is increasingly multicultural due to immigration, particularly from Asia. Christianity is the largest religion, but over half the population now reports no religion.

### Culture
New Zealand culture reflects Māori, early British, and more recent global influences. Māori arts, such as carving and weaving, have experienced a resurgence. The country has a distinctive literary and film industry, with *The Lord of the Rings* films boosting international recognition. Rugby union is the national sport, and the national team, the All Blacks, perform the traditional Māori haka. Cuisine is described as "Pacific Rim," featuring local produce and seafood.

## Terms
- **Māori**: The indigenous Polynesian people of mainland New Zealand.
- **Treaty of Waitangi**: A foundational document signed in 1840 between the British Crown and Māori chiefs, establishing British sovereignty and Māori rights.
- **Waitangi Tribunal**: A permanent commission of inquiry established in 1975 to investigate breaches of the Treaty of Waitangi.
- **Mixed-member proportional (MMP)**: A voting system where voters cast two votes—one for a local electorate candidate and one for a party—used in New Zealand since 1996.
- **Dominion**: A self-governing territory within the British Empire; New Zealand was proclaimed a Dominion in 1907.
- **Realm of New Zealand**: The area over which the New Zealand monarch is sovereign, including New Zealand, the Cook Islands, Niue, Tokelau, and the Ross Dependency.
- **Exclusive economic zone (EEZ)**: A maritime zone extending 200 nautical miles from a coast, where the state has special rights to explore and use marine resources.
- **Kiwiana**: Items of popular culture considered unique to New Zealand.

## Debates and Open questions
- The precise date of initial Polynesian settlement is debated, with evidence suggesting a range between 1250 and 1350.
- The interpretation and application of the Treaty of Waitangi, particularly regarding Māori sovereignty and land rights (such as the foreshore and seabed), remain subjects of ongoing political and legal debate.
- Structural socioeconomic disparities between Māori and the European-descended population, including in health, education, and the justice system, are a persistent issue.

# Panama City

## Overview
Panama City is the capital and largest city of the Republic of Panama, serving as the nation's political, administrative, and economic hub. Situated at the Pacific entrance of the Panama Canal, its strategic location has made it a critical center for global trade, banking, and commerce throughout its history.

## Timeline
- **15 August 1519** — City founded by Spanish conquistador Pedro Arias Dávila.
- **28 January 1671** — Original city destroyed by fire during an attack by privateer Henry Morgan.
- **21 January 1673** — City formally reestablished on a new peninsula site (now Casco Viejo).
- **1855** — Panama Railroad begins full operation, greatly increasing city prosperity.
- **3 November 1903** — Panama declares independence from Colombia.
- **1903** — Hay-Bunau-Varilla Treaty grants the U.S. a 10-mile-wide Canal Zone.
- **1914** — Panama Canal construction is completed.
- **9 January 1964** — "Martyrs' Day" events erupt over tensions with the U.S. in the Canal Zone.
- **1989** — U.S. invades Panama to depose General Manuel Noriega.
- **1999** — United States transfers full control of the Panama Canal to Panama.

## Body

### Founding and Colonial Era
Panama City was founded on 15 August 1519 by Pedro Arias Dávila. It quickly became a vital launching point for the Spanish conquest of the Inca Empire in Peru and a key stopover on the trade route for gold and silver shipped to Spain. The city was destroyed by fire in 1671 after an attack by the privateer Henry Morgan and was rebuilt two years later on a new site, now known as Casco Viejo.

### The Panama Canal and U.S. Influence
Following Panamanian independence in 1903, the Hay-Bunau-Varilla Treaty granted the United States control over a 10-mile-wide Canal Zone to build the Panama Canal, completed in 1914. The construction brought major infrastructure and public health improvements, notably the eradication of yellow fever by 1905 under chief sanitary officer William Gorgas. However, it also created social tensions due to the importation of Caribbean laborers. U.S. control of the Canal Zone was a source of ongoing friction, culminating in the 1964 Martyrs' Day riots. Full control of the canal was transferred to Panama in 1999.

### Modern Economy and Development
Panama City's economy is service-based, dominated by banking, commerce, and tourism linked to the canal and port facilities. The city produces approximately 55% of Panama's GDP. It became a major international banking center in the late 1970s and 1980s, though this also attracted money-laundering concerns. The 1989 U.S. invasion to depose dictator Manuel Noriega caused destruction in the El Chorrillo neighborhood. Recent growth is fueled by the canal's expansion, real estate investment, and a booming tourism sector, with hotel rooms increasing tenfold from 1997 to 2013.

### Geography and Climate
The city lies between the Pacific Ocean and tropical rainforest. Its tropical savanna climate (Köppen Aw) features constant temperatures averaging 27°C (81°F) and distinct wet (May-November) and dry (December-April) seasons. The surrounding tropical forests are vital for the Panama Canal's water supply and have been largely preserved.

### Culture and World Heritage Sites
Panama City contains two UNESCO World Heritage Sites: the ruins of the original city, Panamá Viejo, and the historic district, Casco Viejo. Casco Viejo, built after the 1671 destruction, features a mix of architectural styles and is undergoing gentrification and restoration. The city is culturally diverse, with a population that is predominantly mestizo.

## Terms
*   **Corregimiento:** An administrative division of a district in Panama.
*   **Mestizo:** A person of mixed Caribbean and European/Amerindian ancestry.
*   **Panamá Viejo:** The archaeological site and ruins of the original 1519 Spanish city.
*   **Casco Viejo (or Casco Antiguo):** The historic walled district of Panama City, built after 1671.
*   **Canal Zone:** The 10-mile-wide strip of land granted to the U.S. in 1903 for the Panama Canal's construction and administration.
*   **Martyrs' Day:** Refers to the 9 January 1964 events involving clashes over sovereignty in the Canal Zone.
*   **Cinta Costera:** A coastal highway and parkland development project in the city.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   The construction of the Cinta Costera 3 viaduct around Casco Viejo sparked controversy and protests over the potential loss of its UNESCO World Heritage status, though UNESCO decided in 2012 not to place it on the List of World Heritage Sites in Danger.
*   Panama City's status as an international banking center has historically brought undesirable attention as a locale for money laundering, leading to the implementation of visible controls on cash flow.

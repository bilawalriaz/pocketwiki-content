# Ted Turner

## Overview

Ted Turner (1938–2026) was an American businessman, media proprietor, and philanthropist who transformed the cable television industry. He founded CNN, the first 24-hour cable news channel, pioneered the "superstation" concept with WTBS, and created TNT, TCM, and Cartoon Network. He also owned the Atlanta Braves, founded the charitable Goodwill Games, and gave $1 billion to create the United Nations Foundation. His career reshaped how Americans consumed news and entertainment, and his controversial statements earned him nicknames like "The Mouth of the South" and "Captain Outrageous."

## Body

### Early life and entry into business

Turner was born November 19, 1938, in Cincinnati, Ohio, and raised in Savannah, Georgia. He attended Brown University, initially majoring in classics—a choice his father wrote made him "appalled, even horrified"—before switching to economics. He was expelled for having a female student in his dormitory room and later received an honorary B.A. in 1989. He joined the Coast Guard Reserve, which he described as getting "deployed to some pretty sweet places."

After his father's suicide in March 1963, Turner became president of Turner Advertising Company. The business prospered during the Vietnam War era, holding "virtual monopolies" in several Southern cities. In the late 1960s, he sold his radio stations to buy a struggling Atlanta UHF station in 1970, betting that viewers would want more than a few channels. The station initially ran old movies, cartoons, and reruns, including Gilligan's Island and I Love Lucy.

### The superstation and CNN

In 1976, the FCC allowed WTCG to transmit via satellite to cable providers nationwide, creating the first superstation. Turner bought the Braves and Hawks partly to supply programming. By 1978, he acquired the WTBS call sign from MIT's student radio station for $50,000, renaming his company Turner Broadcasting System.

In 1978, Turner contacted media executive Reese Schonfeld about launching a 24-hour news channel. Schonfeld said it could be done with 300 staff using an all-electronic newsroom and satellites, requiring $15–20 million initially. CNN launched June 1, 1980, from lower-cost, non-union Atlanta. Before debut, Turner famously said the network would cover the end of the world live, and he commissioned a video of a military band playing "Nearer, My God, to Thee" tagged in CNN's database as "[Hold for release] till end of world confirmed."

### Expansion and the Time Warner merger

Turner bought MGM/UA in 1986 for $1.5 billion, amassing enormous debt. He kept MGM's pre-1986 film library and syndicated its 2,200 films, airing colorized versions of black-and-white classics. This sparked opposition from film critics; Roger Ebert called colorizing Casablanca "one of the saddest days in the history of the movies." The controversy contributed to the Library of Congress establishing the National Film Registry.

In 1988, Turner purchased Jim Crockett Promotions, renaming it WCW, which became the main competitor to Vince McMahon's WWF in the "Monday Night War." He launched TNT in 1988 and TCM in 1994. In 1991, he bought Hanna-Barbera, whose library formed the core of Cartoon Network, launched October 1, 1992.

Turner Broadcasting merged with Time Warner on October 10, 1996. In January 2001, Time Warner was purchased by AOL; the dot-com bubble's burst dragged down the stock, and Turner—the biggest individual shareholder—lost an estimated $7 billion. He resigned as vice chairman in 2003 and from the board in 2006.

### Sports and philanthropy

Turner owned the Atlanta Braves, who won the 1995 World Series. In 1977, he was suspended one year by Commissioner Bowie Kuhn for tampering while pursuing free agent Gary Matthews; the suspension was appealed and draft picks reinstated. He briefly managed the team himself for one game before being ordered to stop, citing rules barring managers from owning stock.

Turner founded the Goodwill Games in 1986 to ease Cold War tensions. He gave $1 billion to create the United Nations Foundation and co-founded the Nuclear Threat Initiative in 2001. In 2020, Forbes awarded him a perfect philanthropy score of 5, placing him among just ten Forbes 400 members who gave away at least 20% of their fortune. In 2010, he joined The Giving Pledge.

### Turner Enterprises and landholdings

Turner Enterprises, founded in 1976, manages his land holdings—about two million acres across the U.S. and Argentina. His bison herd of approximately 51,000 animals on 15 ranches is the largest private herd in the world. His biggest ranch, Vermejo Reserve in New Mexico, is the largest privately owned contiguous tract in the U.S. He was the largest private landowner in America until John C. Malone surpassed him in 2011.

### Political views and controversies

Turner's controversial statements included calling Christianity "for losers" (he later apologized), accusing Israel of terrorism in 2002 (also apologized), and suggesting in 2008 that global warming would leave survivors as "cannibals." He advocated for universal healthcare and curbing population growth, putting $125 million into the Turner Foundation for that cause. In 2018, he revealed he had once considered running for president while married to Jane Fonda, who said she would leave him if he did.

### Personal life and death

Turner married three times: Judy Nye (1960–1964), Jane Shirley Smith (1965–1988), and Jane Fonda (1991–2001). He had five children. A lifelong sailor, he won the America's Cup in 1977 and the 1979 Fastnet Race. In 2018, he revealed his diagnosis of Lewy body dementia. He died May 6, 2026, at his Avalon Plantation in Lamont, Florida, at age 87.

## Terms

- **Superstation**: A local TV station transmitted via satellite to cable providers nationwide
- **UHF**: Ultra high frequency; stations that initially struggled against VHF competitors
- **Colorization**: Computerized adding of color to black-and-white films, which Turner aired
- **Monday Night War**: The 1990s ratings rivalry between WCW and WWF
- **The Giving Pledge**: Warren Buffett and Bill Gates's initiative committing billionaires to donate most of their wealth
- **Lewy body dementia**: A progressive neurological disease Turner was diagnosed with in 2018
- **Goodwill Games**: An international sports competition Turner founded in 1986 to ease Cold War tensions

## Debates and open questions

Turner's colorization of classic films remains controversial; critics argued it vandalized cinematic history, while Turner defended it as making old films accessible. His legacy is mixed: Slate called him "the Alexander the Great of broadcasting" but noted his programming "fell short of inspiring," built on reruns and old movies. His philanthropy was widely praised, but his controversial statements—on religion, terrorism, and population control—drew repeated criticism and forced apologies throughout his career.

# Khanate of Kazan

## Overview
The Khanate of Kazan was a Tatar state that ruled the former Volga Bulgaria region from 1438 to 1552. As a successor state to the Golden Horde, it was a multi-ethnic, Islamic polity whose capital, Kazan, was a major trade and cultural center. Its conquest by Ivan the Terrible's Russia marked a pivotal expansion of the Tsardom and led to the suppression of its independent political and cultural institutions.

## Timeline
- **c. 1438** — Ulugh Muhammad founds the Khanate, usurping the throne of Kazan.
- **1487** — Ivan III of Moscow occupies Kazan and installs a puppet khan, making it a protectorate.
- **1521** — Kazan breaks free from Moscow, allies with the Crimean Khanate, and attacks Muscovy.
- **1545** — Pro-Russian nobles depose Khan Safa Giray, beginning a decade of instability.
- **1551** — Lands west of the Volga are ceded to Russia; the child khan and his regent mother are sent to Moscow.
- **August 1552** — Ivan the Terrible besieges Kazan from the fortress of Sviyazhsk.
- **October 3, 1552** — Russian forces capture Kazan, executing the khan and slaughtering much of the population.
- **1552-1556** — Resistance to Russian rule continues in rebel enclaves until its final suppression.

## Body

### Geography and Population
The Khanate encompassed the territories of former Volga Bulgaria, centered on the Volga, Kama, and Vyatka rivers. The majority population were Kazan Tatars, whose identity was often tied to Islam. The state was multi-ethnic, ruling over Chuvash, Mari, Mordvins, Udmurts, and Bashkirs. The feudal nobility included local Bulgar elites and steppe Tatar (Kipchak and later Nogai) families. The southern steppe border was inhabited by nomadic Nogais, who frequently raided settled areas.

### History and Downfall
Founded by Ulugh Muhammad, the Khanate was politically unstable, with frequent succession struggles. Its history was marked by conflict and shifting alliances with Muscovy, the Crimean Khanate, and the Nogai Horde. Internal divisions between pro-Russian and anti-Russian factions weakened the state in its final decades. In 1552, Tsar Ivan the Terrible besieged and captured Kazan, ending the Khanate's independence. The conquest led to the execution of its leadership, the forced resettlement of Tatars, the settlement of Russians on former Tatar lands, and the beginning of forced Christianization.

### Society and Administration
Society was hierarchical, with the Khan advised by a council (Diwan). The nobility included ranks like *bäk* and *morza*, while the military class consisted of *uğlan* and *bahadir*. The Muslim clergy (*ulema*) held judicial and educational roles. The majority of the population were *qara xalıq* (free people) and *çura* (serfs). The state was divided into five administrative regions (*daruğa*). The military numbered between 20,000 and 60,000, supplemented by allies.

### Economy and Culture
The economy was based on agriculture, crafts, and trade with Russia, Central Asia, and the Caucasus. Landownership followed the *söyurğal* system. Culturally, the Khanate inherited traditions from Volga Bulgaria and the Golden Horde. Kazan was a center of Islamic learning with literate urban populations, madrasas, and libraries. Old Tatar literature flourished, and architecture featured white-stone and wood-carving styles.

## Terms
- **Golden Horde**: The Mongol and later Turkicized state that succeeded the Mongol Empire in the western steppe, from which the Kazan Khanate emerged.
- **Daruğa**: An administrative division or "direction" of the Khanate, replacing earlier duchies.
- **Diwan**: The cabinet council that advised the Khan on governance.
- **Qara xalıq**: The free Muslim commoner population of the Khanate.
- **Çura**: Serfs who worked on feudal estates.
- **Söyurğal**: A system of conditional land grants or estates.
- **Yasaq**: A tribute or tax levied on the population.
- **Ulema**: The Muslim scholarly and judicial clergy.
- **Nogais**: A nomadic Turkic people of the steppe who were sometimes allies, sometimes raiders of the Khanate.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The exact status and degree of independence of the Kazan region within the disintegrating Golden Horde before Ulugh Muhammad's usurpation is unclear.
- The scarcity of surviving documents from the Khanate itself, due to their destruction during the Russian conquest and the Time of Troubles, limits historical understanding.

# Enzo Ferrari

## Overview
Enzo Ferrari (1898–1988) was an Italian racing driver and entrepreneur who founded the Scuderia Ferrari racing team and the Ferrari automobile marque. His leadership made Ferrari a dominant force in motorsport, winning multiple World Championships, and his management style and personal life became legendary in automotive history.

## Timeline
- **18 February 1898** — Born in Modena, Italy.
- **1908** — Witnessed Felice Nazzaro's win at the Circuito di Bologna, inspiring his racing ambition.
- **1919** — Competitive debut at the Parma-Poggio di Berceto hillclimb; joined Alfa Romeo's racing department.
- **1929** — Founded Scuderia Ferrari as Alfa Romeo's racing division.
- **1939** — Left Alfa Romeo; founded Auto-Avio Costruzioni.
- **1947** — Founded Ferrari S.p.A. to produce cars bearing his name.
- **1950** — Ferrari entered the inaugural Formula One World Championship.
- **1951** — First World Championship Grand Prix win with José Froilán González at Silverstone.
- **1961** — "Great Walkout" of key personnel to form rival ATS.
- **1969** — Sold 50% of Ferrari to Fiat S.p.A., retaining control of racing.
- **14 August 1988** — Died in Maranello at age 90.

## Body

### Early Life and Racing Career
Born to a metalwork shop owner, Enzo Ferrari had little formal education and preferred working in his father's workshop. After witnessing a 1908 motor race, he aspired to become a racing driver. His father and brother died in the 1916 flu pandemic. Ferrari began his career as a test driver for CMN in 1919, then joined Alfa Romeo's racing department in 1920. He won his first Grand Prix in 1923 and achieved a successful season in 1924. Following the birth of his son Alfredo ("Dino") in 1932, he retired from driving to focus on team management, founding Scuderia Ferrari in 1929. The team's emblem, the prancing horse, originated from a World War I Italian fighter pilot, Francesco Baracca.

### Building the Ferrari Empire
After a dispute with Alfa Romeo, Ferrari founded Auto-Avio Costruzioni in 1939. Despite a contractual clause restricting car design, he produced two cars for the 1940 Mille Miglia. During World War II, his factory was relocated from Modena to Maranello after Allied bombing. In 1947, he founded Ferrari S.p.A. to build his own cars. The team's first major victory came at the 1949 24 Hours of Le Mans. Ferrari entered Formula One in 1950, winning its first Drivers' Championship with Alberto Ascari in 1952 and 1953. To finance racing, the company began selling sports cars. A tragic accident at the 1957 Mille Miglia, which killed nine spectators, led to a manslaughter prosecution against Ferrari that was dismissed in 1961.

### Management and the Fiat Partnership
Ferrari's autocratic management style involved pitting drivers against each other to improve performance. In 1961, a major dispute led to the "Great Walkout," where key personnel left to form the rival Automobili Turismo e Sport (ATS). Ferrari responded by promoting junior engineers and hiring new drivers, leading to renewed success, including John Surtees' 1964 World Championship. By the late 1960s, financial pressures led Ferrari to sell 50% of his company to Fiat in 1969, with the condition he retained 100% control of racing. He stepped down as managing director of road cars in 1971. The team continued to win championships under drivers like Niki Lauda and Jody Scheckter.

### Final Years and Legacy
Ferrari's final years saw both triumph and tragedy, including the deaths of drivers Gilles Villeneuve (1982) and Didier Pironi's career-ending accident. The team won the Constructors' Championship in 1982 and 1983. Ferrari died of leukaemia on 14 August 1988 in Maranello. His death was reported two days later per his wishes. He witnessed the launch of the Ferrari F40 shortly before his death. The Scuderia Ferrari team remained successful after his passing, winning multiple championships in the 2000s.

## Terms
- **Scuderia Ferrari**: The racing team founded by Enzo Ferrari in 1929, initially as Alfa Romeo's racing division.
- **Prancing Horse (Cavallino Rampante)**: The iconic emblem of Ferrari, derived from a World War I Italian fighter pilot's personal insignia.
- **Formula One (F1)**: The highest class of international auto racing for single-seater formula racing cars.
- **Grand Prix**: A major international motor racing event, especially for Formula One cars.
- **Mille Miglia**: An open-road endurance race held in Italy from 1927 to 1957.
- **24 Hours of Le Mans**: An annual endurance sports car race held in France.
- **Fiat S.p.A.**: An Italian industrial conglomerate that acquired a 50% stake in Ferrari in 1969.
- **Cavaliere del Lavoro**: An Italian order of merit awarded to those who have distinguished themselves in industry, commerce, and agriculture.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **The "Great Walkout" Cause**: A 1998 interview with former manager Romolo Tavoni suggested the 1962 exodus was not voluntary but an ousting following a disagreement with Ferrari over the role of his wife in the company.
- **Driver Safety and Responsibility**: Ferrari's management coincided with a high number of driver fatalities (eight between 1955-1971). While contemporary Stirling Moss defended him, citing no mechanical failures, the Vatican newspaper compared him to Saturn consuming his sons.
- **Personal Motivations**: Biographies, such as Brock Yates', portray Ferrari's personal life as complex, suggesting his marriage was for appearances and that his primary loves were racing and his son Dino.

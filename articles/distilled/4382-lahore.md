# Lahore

## Overview
Lahore is the capital and largest city of Pakistan's Punjab province, serving as a major industrial, educational, and cultural hub. Its history spans over two millennia, rising to prominence as a medieval capital and reaching its zenith under the Mughal Empire. The city's trajectory was dramatically altered by the 1947 Partition of British India, which made it part of Pakistan and led to significant demographic and economic shifts.

## Timeline
- **c. 326 BCE** — Alexander the Great's historians make no mention of a city at Lahore's location.
- **982 CE** — First documented mention of Lahore by name in the *Hudud al-'Alam*.
- **1020–1027** — Sultan Mahmud of Ghazni conquers Lahore, making it part of the Ghaznavid Empire.
- **1584** — Lahore becomes the capital of the Mughal Empire under Emperor Akbar.
- **1739** — Nader Shah of the Afsharid dynasty captures Lahore.
- **1799** — Ranjit Singh seizes Lahore, making it the capital of the Sikh Empire.
- **1849** — The British annex Punjab, making Lahore the capital of British Punjab.
- **1940** — The All India Muslim League passes the Lahore Resolution, demanding a separate Muslim state.
- **1947** — Following Partition, Lahore is awarded to Pakistan and becomes the capital of Punjab province.
- **2020** — The Orange Line metro train, Lahore's first, begins commercial operations.

## Body

### Origins and Medieval Era
Lahore's early history is ambiguous. It was not noted by Alexander the Great's historians in 326 BCE, but a Chinese pilgrim described a prosperous city in the region in 630 CE. The first named mention is in 982 CE. The city rose to prominence as the capital of the Hindu Shahis before being conquered by the Ghaznavids (c. 1020–1027). Under Ghaznavid and later Delhi Sultanate rule (Mamluk, Tughluq dynasties), Lahore became a cultural and academic center, though it was repeatedly sacked by Mongol invasions, leading to periods of decline and obscurity.

### Mughal Empire
Lahore reached its peak under the Mughal Empire, serving as its capital from 1584. Emperors Akbar, Jahangir, Shah Jahan, and Aurangzeb lavished the city with architectural marvels, including the Lahore Fort, Shalimar Gardens, and Badshahi Mosque. The city's population and suburbs expanded greatly. Following Aurangzeb's death in 1707, succession wars weakened Mughal control, and Lahore fell into decline, vulnerable to invasions by Nader Shah (1739) and the Durrani Afghans.

### Sikh and British Rule
After a period of instability and Durrani invasions, Ranjit Singh captured Lahore in 1799, establishing the Sikh Empire's capital. His rule restored some grandeur but also involved the plundering of Mughal monuments for materials. After the Anglo-Sikh Wars, the British annexed Punjab in 1849, making Lahore their capital. They developed a new "Civil Station" south of the Walled City, built infrastructure like the railway station, and repurposed Mughal monuments. Lahore became a center of the Indian independence movement, hosting the 1929 Declaration of Indian Independence and the 1940 Lahore Resolution.

### Partition and Modern Era
The 1947 Partition of British India was traumatic for Lahore. Despite a Muslim majority (64.5% in 1941), its economy was dominated by Hindus and Sikhs, who left en masse amid horrific riots. The city was awarded to Pakistan and became its Punjab capital, but its economy weakened, and Karachi was chosen as the national capital. Post-independence, Lahore slowly recovered, becoming the capital of all West Pakistan (1955–1970) and a cultural and economic hub. Major developments include the Minar-e-Pakistan (1968), the Orange Line metro (2020), and ongoing restoration of the Walled City.

## Terms
- **Walled City:** The historic fortified interior of Lahore, its original core.
- **Subah:** A province of the Mughal Empire.
- **Misl:** A sovereign state of the Sikh Confederacy.
- **Indo-Saracenic:** A British colonial architectural style blending Victorian and Islamic elements.
- **Charbagh:** A Persian-style garden layout with four quadrants, symbolizing paradise.
- **Urs:** An annual festival at a Sufi shrine commemorating a saint's death anniversary.
- **Basant:** A traditional Punjabi spring festival, historically celebrated with kite-flying in Lahore.
- **Haveli:** A traditional mansion or townhouse, common in the Walled City.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The exact origin and etymology of Lahore's name are unclear, with theories linking it to the Ravi River, a blacksmith, or the legendary Prince Lava.
- The city's early history is debated due to a lack of definitive records before the 10th century.
- The population demographics of Lahore during the 1947 Partition are disputed, with figures affecting the city's award to Pakistan.
- Contemporary debates include the status of the Punjabi language in education and administration, and the accurate census of the city's Pashtun population.

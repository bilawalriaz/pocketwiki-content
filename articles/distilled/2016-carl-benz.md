# Carl Benz

## Overview
Carl Benz (1844–1929) was a German engineer and inventor credited with creating the first practical, modern automobile. His work laid the foundation for the global automotive industry, and his company eventually merged to form Daimler-Benz, the maker of Mercedes-Benz.

- **4 April 1929** — Died in Ladenburg at age 84.

## Body

### Early Life and Education
Born to a locomotive driver, Benz studied at the Karlsruhe polytechnical school, graduating in 1864. After several years of professional training in various engineering firms, he co-founded his first factory in Mannheim in 1871. His fiancée, Bertha Ringer, bought out his unreliable partner's share using her dowry, securing the business. In the late 1870s, Benz focused on developing a reliable petrol engine, patenting a two-stroke engine in 1880 and key components like the spark plug and carburetor.

### The Benz Patent-Motorwagen
In 1883, Benz co-founded Benz & Cie., which provided the resources to pursue his passion for a "horseless carriage." Using bicycle technology, he created a three-wheeled vehicle with a four-stroke engine. He received a patent for this "automobile fueled by gas" in 1886, marking the birth of the first practical automobile. The vehicle was first sold commercially in 1888. A pivotal moment came in 1888 when Bertha Benz, without her husband's knowledge, drove a Model 2 on a 104 km trip to demonstrate its feasibility, inventing brake pads along the way and suggesting the addition of a third gear for hill climbing.

### Company Expansion and Innovation
Benz & Cie. grew rapidly, becoming the world's largest automobile company by 1899. The company introduced successful models like the Victoria and the Velo, the latter being an early mass-produced car. Benz also pioneered commercial vehicles, designing the first internal combustion engine truck and motor bus in 1895, and patented the flat (boxer) engine in 1896. Competition from Daimler Motoren Gesellschaft (DMG) intensified after DMG introduced the Mercedes 35 hp in 1902. Benz countered with the high-speed Parsifal model but retired from design management in 1903 after DMG hired French designers without consulting him.

### Later Years and Legacy
Benz continued as a director at Benz & Cie. and founded a separate family company, C. Benz Söhne, in 1906. The economic crisis of the 1920s forced a merger between his company and DMG. On 28 June 1926, they formed Daimler-Benz, with all automobiles branded as Mercedes-Benz. Benz remained on the board until his death in 1929. He is widely regarded as "the father of the car" and the "father of the automobile industry."

## Terms
- **Patent-Motorwagen**: The first practical, modern automobile, patented by Carl Benz in 1886.
- **Two-stroke engine**: An internal combustion engine completing a power cycle in one piston stroke, which Benz patented in 1880.
- **Four-stroke engine**: The standard engine cycle (intake, compression, combustion, exhaust) used in the Patent-Motorwagen.
- **Carburetor**: A device that mixes air and fuel for an internal combustion engine, patented by Benz.
- **Flat engine (boxer engine)**: An engine design with horizontally opposed pistons, patented by Benz in 1896.
- **Benz & Cie.**: Carl Benz's primary automobile manufacturing company, founded in 1883.
- **Daimler-Benz**: The company formed in 1926 from the merger of Benz & Cie. and Daimler Motoren Gesellschaft.
- **Mercedes-Benz**: The brand name adopted by Daimler-Benz for all its automobiles after the 1926 merger.

# Hermann Göring

## Overview
Hermann Göring was a leading figure in Nazi Germany, serving as a World War I flying ace, a key architect of the Nazi state, and Supreme Commander of the Luftwaffe. He was the second most powerful man in Germany for much of the Third Reich, responsible for economic rearmament, the creation of the Gestapo, and significant roles in the Holocaust. His power declined during World War II due to military failures, and he was convicted of war crimes at Nuremberg before committing suicide.

## Timeline
- **12 January 1893** — Born in Rosenheim, Bavaria.
- **1914–1918** — Served as a World War I fighter pilot ace, finishing with 22 victories.
- **8–9 November 1923** — Wounded in the failed Beer Hall Putsch; began morphine addiction.
- **1 March 1923** — Appointed head of the Nazi Party's SA (Sturmabteilung).
- **27 February 1933** — First to arrive at the Reichstag fire, using it to justify a crackdown on Communists.
- **18 October 1936** — Named Plenipotentiary of the Four Year Plan to mobilize the economy for war.
- **1 September 1939** — Designated by Hitler as his official successor.
- **19 August 1940** — Promoted to Reichsmarschall, the highest military rank in Germany.
- **22 April 1945** — Arrested for treason after sending a telegram to Hitler requesting to assume leadership.
- **30 September 1946** — Sentenced to death at the Nuremberg trials.
- **15 October 1946** — Committed suicide by cyanide capsule the night before his scheduled execution.

## Body

### Early Life and World War I
Born to a former colonial governor, Göring was raised with a romantic militarism. He became a decorated World War I fighter pilot, eventually commanding the elite Jagdgeschwader 1 ("Flying Circus") after the death of Manfred von Richthofen. He embraced the "stab-in-the-back myth," which blamed Germany's defeat on civilian traitors.

### Rise in the Nazi Party
Göring joined the Nazi Party in 1922 and led its paramilitary SA. After being wounded in the 1923 Beer Hall Putsch, he developed a severe morphine addiction that required asylum treatment. He re-entered politics as a Reichstag deputy, using his position to gain influential supporters. As President of the Reichstag from 1932, he helped facilitate Hitler's rise to power.

### Consolidation of Power
After Hitler became Chancellor in 1933, Göring held multiple key posts, including Minister-President of Prussia. He created the Gestapo (Secret State Police) but later ceded control to Heinrich Himmler. He was central to the 1934 "Night of the Long Knives," which purged the SA leadership. As Plenipotentiary of the Four Year Plan from 1936, he controlled economic rearmament and pushed for the annexation of Austria. He also played a role in the Blomberg–Fritsch Affair, which led to a military leadership reshuffle.

### World War II and Decline
As Supreme Commander of the Luftwaffe, Göring's forces achieved early successes in Poland and France. However, his prestige collapsed after the Luftwaffe's failure in the Battle of Britain and its inability to supply the surrounded German army at Stalingrad. His standing with Hitler and the public plummeted as Allied bombing devastated German cities. He increasingly withdrew from duties to amass art and property looted from Jewish victims.

### Downfall and Trial
In April 1945, as the war was lost, Göring sent a telegram to Hitler requesting permission to take over leadership. Hitler, influenced by Martin Bormann, considered this treason, stripped Göring of all offices, and ordered his arrest. After the war, he was the highest-ranking official tried at Nuremberg. Found guilty on all counts of conspiracy, crimes against peace, war crimes, and crimes against humanity, he was sentenced to death. He avoided hanging by committing suicide with a hidden cyanide capsule.

### Role in the Holocaust
Though perhaps less ideologically antisemitic than others, Göring was instrumental in the Holocaust's economic and administrative aspects. He supported the Nuremberg Laws, ordered the registration of Jewish property, and in 1941 issued the memo to Reinhard Heydrich to organize the "Final Solution." He directed anti-partisan operations that murdered thousands of Jews and civilians.

## Terms
*   **Pour le Mérite:** A prestigious Prussian military order, often called the "Blue Max," awarded to Göring for aerial victories in WWI.
*   **Sturmabteilung (SA):** The original paramilitary wing of the Nazi Party, known as the "Brownshirts."
*   **Gestapo:** The Geheime Staatspolizei (Secret State Police), created by Göring in 1933 to suppress political opposition.
*   **Four Year Plan:** A 1936 economic initiative led by Göring to make Germany self-sufficient and ready for war within four years.
*   **Reichsmarschall:** The highest military rank in Nazi Germany, created for Göring in 1940.
*   **Luftwaffe:** The aerial warfare branch of the German military, commanded by Göring.
*   **Night of the Long Knives:** The 1934 purge in which Hitler, with help from Göring and Himmler, eliminated SA leaders and other political rivals.
*   **Final Solution:** The Nazi plan for the genocide of Jews during World War II, which Göring authorized Heydrich to prepare.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Reichstag Fire:** While the consensus is that Marinus van der Lubbe acted alone, some historians accuse the Nazis of staging a false flag attack. At Nuremberg, a witness testified Göring admitted responsibility, which Göring denied.
*   **Göring's Knowledge of the Holocaust:** At his trial, Göring claimed ignorance of the extermination camps, stating he only intended to remove Jews from positions of influence. Evidence presented, including the minutes of a post-Kristallnacht meeting he chaired, contradicted this claim.
*   **Source of Suicide Capsule:** How Göring obtained the cyanide is debated. Theories include a hidden stash, a gift from a U.S. Army guard (Herbert Lee Stivers) who claimed he was tricked, or assistance from a deceased officer (Jack G. Wheelis).

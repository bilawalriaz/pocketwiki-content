# Arabian Peninsula

## Overview
The Arabian Peninsula is the world's largest peninsula, located in West Asia. It is the historical and cultural heartland of the Arab world and the birthplace of Islam. In the modern era, its vast oil reserves have made it a region of critical global geopolitical and economic significance.

## Timeline
- **c. 300,000–500,000 years ago** — Possible hominin migration through a "Green Arabia."
- **c. 90,000 years ago** — Oldest human fossil outside Africa/Levant found at Al Wusta.
- **Early 1st millennium BC** — Historical record begins with the introduction of writing systems.
- **106 AD** — Northwestern Arabia becomes the Roman province of Arabia Petraea.
- **c. 570–632 AD** — Life of the Islamic prophet Muhammad; founding of Islam.
- **632 AD** — Unification of Arabian tribes under Islam; creation of the first Islamic state.
- **661–750 AD** — Umayyad Caliphate expands Arab-Muslim power far beyond the peninsula.
- **1744** — Alliance between Muhammad bin Saud and Muhammad ibn Abd al-Wahhab founds the first Saudi state.
- **1916–1918** — Arab Revolt against Ottoman rule.
- **1932** — Foundation of the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia under King Abdulaziz Ibn Saud.
- **1930s** — Discovery of vast oil reserves begins.
- **1990–1991** — Gulf War following Iraq's invasion of Kuwait.

## Body

### Etymology and Historical Geography
In antiquity, "Arabia" was a broader concept than the modern peninsula. The Romans divided it into three regions: *Arabia Petraea* ("Stony Arabia," a province in the northwest), *Arabia Deserta* ("Desert Arabia," the central interior), and *Arabia Felix* ("Fortunate Arabia," the fertile south, mostly modern Yemen). Medieval Muslim geographers later divided the peninsula into four main regions: the Central Plateau (Najd), South Arabia, Al-Bahrain (Eastern Arabia), and the Hejaz.

### Prehistoric and Pre-Islamic Arabia
The peninsula was among the first regions occupied by modern humans after their migration from Africa. Archaeological evidence shows human presence from at least 90,000 years ago. The historical record begins in the early 1st millennium BC. For much of ancient history, the region was sparsely populated by Arab tribes and home to various local kingdoms (e.g., Saba, Himyar, Dilmun) and foreign religious practices, with limited political control from empires like Rome.

### Rise of Islam and the Caliphates
The 7th century AD saw the peninsula's unification under the prophet Muhammad and the religion of Islam. After his death in 632, the Rashidun and Umayyad Caliphates rapidly expanded Arab-Muslim power, creating a vast empire from Iberia to India. While the political center shifted to newly conquered territories, Mecca and Medina remained the spiritual heart of the Islamic world, central to the Hajj pilgrimage.

### Medieval to Early Modern Period
Politically, Arabia became a peripheral region of the Islamic world, with major states based in Damascus, Baghdad, and Cairo. The Hashemite Sharifs of Mecca maintained a state in the Hejaz, often under the suzerainty of larger empires like the Abbasids and Mamluks. The Ottoman Empire controlled the western and eastern coasts but never the central Najd region. In 1744, the alliance between the House of Saud and the Wahhabi religious movement began the rise of the Saudi state.

### Modern History and Geopolitics
The 20th century brought the collapse of the Ottoman Empire after the Arab Revolt (1916–1918) and the subsequent foundation of modern states, most notably Saudi Arabia (1932). The discovery of oil in the 1930s transformed the region's economy and global importance. Conflicts have included the North Yemen Civil War (1962–1970), the Gulf War (1990–1991) after Iraq's invasion of Kuwait, and the ongoing Yemeni civil war that began in 2014.

### Geography and Environment
The peninsula is dominated by the Arabian Desert, an extension of the Sahara. It features a central plateau (Najd), major deserts like the Nefud and Rub' al Khali, and mountain ranges along the western and southern coasts. The climate is extremely hot and arid, with most areas drained by ephemeral riverbeds called *wadis*. Agriculture is limited to oases and coastal plains, though Yemen's fertile soils support terraced farming. The surrounding seas host rich coral reef ecosystems. The Arabian Aquifer System is the world's most over-stressed aquifer.

### Demographics and Politics
The peninsula is home to nine countries, with Saudi Arabia being the largest. The six Gulf states form the Gulf Cooperation Council (GCC). The population is characterized by rapid growth, a youthful demographic, and a heavily skewed sex ratio due to migrant labor. In several states, South Asian expatriates outnumber the native population.

## Terms
- **Arabian Plate:** The tectonic plate on which most of the Arabian Peninsula sits.
- **Hajj:** The annual Islamic pilgrimage to Mecca, a religious duty for Muslims who can undertake it.
- **Caliphate:** A state under the leadership of an Islamic ruler considered a successor to the prophet Muhammad.
- **Wadis:** Dry riverbeds that contain water only during periods of heavy rain.
- **Petrocracy:** A state whose political power and economy are heavily dependent on oil revenue.
- **Bedouin:** Traditionally nomadic Arab tribes of the desert.
- **Oasis:** A fertile area in a desert, typically surrounding a spring or well.
- **Wahhabism:** A strict, puritanical form of Sunni Islam founded in the 18th century.
- **Haram:** An Islamic term for a forbidden or sacred site; the Masjid al-Haram is the Grand Mosque in Mecca.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The exact timing and routes of early human migrations into and out of the peninsula are subjects of ongoing archaeological and genetic research.
- The historical extent and precise boundaries of ancient regions like *Arabia Deserta* are debated among scholars.
- The long-term sustainability of the region's water resources, particularly the over-stressed Arabian Aquifer System, poses a critical future challenge.

# Svetlana Alexievich

## Overview
Svetlana Alexievich is a Belarusian investigative journalist, essayist, and oral historian who writes in Russian. She was awarded the 2015 Nobel Prize in Literature "for her polyphonic writings, a monument to suffering and courage in our time." Her work is a form of "documentary literature," using collages of witness testimonies to create an emotional history of the Soviet and post-Soviet individual, focusing on major traumas like World War II, the Afghan War, and the Chernobyl disaster.

## Timeline
- **31 May 1948** — Born in Stanislav, Ukrainian SSR (now Ivano-Frankivsk, Ukraine).
- **1972** — Graduated from Belarusian State University.
- **1985** — Published her first book, *War's Unwomanly Face*, which sold over two million copies.
- **1989** — Published *Zinky Boys*, about the Soviet-Afghan War, leading to controversy and trials.
- **1992-1996** — Tried multiple times in Minsk on charges of "defamation" related to *Zinky Boys*.
- **2000** — Left Belarus due to political persecution by the Lukashenko administration.
- **2011** — Moved back to Minsk.
- **2013** — Published *Secondhand Time: The Last of the Soviets*.
- **26 October 2015** — Awarded the Nobel Prize in Literature.
- **2020** — Became a member of the Coordination Council of Sviatlana Tsikhanouskaya; left Belarus for Germany in September.
- **2021** — Her book *The Last Witnesses* was excluded from the Belarusian school curriculum.

## Body

### Method and Influences
Alexievich's literary method is to construct narratives from the collages of individual testimonies, aiming to "reduce history to the human being." She describes her work as capturing "a chorus of individual voices and a collage of everyday details." Her primary influence was the Belarusian writer Ales Adamovich, who advocated for recording witness testimonies to describe 20th-century horrors. She also cites writers like Ryszard Kapuściński, Hanna Krall, and Varlam Shalamov as influences.

### Major Works and Themes
Her books document the emotional history of Soviet and post-Soviet life through specific catastrophes. *War's Unwomanly Face* (1985) and *The Last Witnesses* (1985) present World War II through the previously untold perspectives of women and children. *Zinky Boys* (1992) uses testimonies from the Soviet-Afghan War (1979–1989) to reclaim the "small, the personal, and the specific" from state narratives. *Voices from Chernobyl* (1997) is an oral history of the nuclear disaster. *Secondhand Time* (2013) explores the collapse of the Soviet Union and its aftermath, including the suicides of those unable to accept the new order.

### Political Persecution and Activism
Alexievich has faced persistent political pressure in Belarus. *Zinky Boys* led to trials from 1992 to 1996, widely seen as an attempt to preserve old ideology. After 1993, her books were not published by state-owned Belarusian houses. During the 2020 Belarusian protests, she joined the Coordination Council of the opposition. Following criminal proceedings against the council and threats to her safety, she left Belarus for Germany in September 2020. In 2021, her work was removed from the school curriculum. She has also publicly condemned Russia's annexation of Crimea and Belarus's involvement in the 2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine.

## Terms
*   **Oral History:** A historical method that uses recorded interviews with people who have personal knowledge of past events.
*   **Documentary Literature:** A genre Alexievich uses, described as an artistic rendering of real events using testimonies and documents, with some poetic license.
*   **Polyphonic:** A term from the Nobel citation meaning "many-voiced," referring to her method of weaving together multiple individual testimonies.
*   **Coordination Council:** A body formed during the 2020 Belarusian protests to facilitate a peaceful transfer of power, of which Alexievich was a member.
*   **Zinc Coffins:** The metal coffins used to transport the bodies of Soviet soldiers killed in the Afghan War, a central image in her book *Zinky Boys*.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Genre Classification:** Alexievich rejects the label of journalist, preferring "documentary literature." Her work sits at the intersection of journalism, sociology, psychology, and literature, sparking debate about its precise genre.
*   **Accuracy and Purpose:** Her book *Zinky Boys* led to trials where she was accused of "distorting and falsifying" testimonies. The Belarus League for Human Rights claimed such cases were politically motivated attempts to preserve old ideology.
*   **Political Impact:** Her exile in 2020 and the subsequent removal of her work from school curricula highlight the ongoing conflict between her documentation of state-sponsored trauma and the Belarusian government's narrative.

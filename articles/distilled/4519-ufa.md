# Ufa

## Overview
Ufa is the capital and largest city of the Republic of Bashkortostan, Russia. Situated at the confluence of the Belaya and Ufa rivers near the southern Ural Mountains, it is a major industrial, educational, and cultural center with a population of over 1.1 million. Its economy is heavily based on oil refining and petrochemicals, and it is an ethnically diverse city with a significant Bashkir and Tatar population.

- **1781** — Became the seat of a separate Ufa Governorate.

- **1870** — Connected to the European part of Russia via the Belaya River Waterway.

- **1989** — Ufa train disaster occurs ~75 km from the city, killing 575 people.
- **2015** — Hosted separate summits of BRICS and the Shanghai Cooperation Organisation.

## Body

### Etymology and Early History
The city's name derives from the Ufa River. The river's name origin is disputed: linguist Aleksandr Matveyev proposed an Iranian root meaning "water." A 2014 hypothesis by Karimov and Khabibov suggests an ancient Turkic ritual settlement named "Upe" or "Ufe" existed before the Russian fortress. Early history of the area dates to the Paleolithic. Medieval maps (e.g., 1367, 1375) show a town called "Pascherti" (Bashkort) near the Belaya River, which some scholars associate with Ufa's location.

### Founding and Administrative History
The official founding is 1574, when a fortress (originally named Tura-Tau) was built by order of Ivan the Terrible. Town status was granted in 1586. The city was initially under Orenburg's jurisdiction. It became the capital of its own governorate in 1781, was reunited with Orenburg in 1796, and became the center of the Orenburg Governorate in 1802. Today, it is the capital of Bashkortostan and an administrative center separate from Ufimsky District.

### Economy and Industry
Ufa's economy is dominated by oil refining, chemistry, and mechanical engineering. Key enterprises include subsidiaries of the petroleum company Bashneft (Ufimsky petrochemical, Novo-Ufimsky, and Ufimsky refinery plants) and the Ufa Engine-Building Production Association. The city is a major transportation hub, connected to Moscow by two federal highways (M7 and M5) and on the Trans-Siberian Railway. It has an international airport, and a metro system has been planned since the 1980s but remains delayed.

### Demographics and Geography
The population exceeded one million in 1980. As of the 2021 Census, the ethnic composition is a Russian majority with substantial Bashkir and Tatar minorities. The city covers 707.93 km² on the Ufa Plateau. It has a warm summer continental climate (Köppen: Dfb) with harsh winters; recorded temperatures range from −48.5 °C to 39.4 °C.

### Governance and Society
Ufa is governed by a Council of 35 deputies and a Head of Administration. The city faces ecological issues, notably periodic chemical odors and air pollution from suspended solids, with monitoring gaps in some residential areas. Major religions are Sunni Islam and Russian Orthodoxy; the city hosts the Central Spiritual Administration of Muslims in Russia. Education includes Bashkir State University and several specialized technical and medical universities.

## Terms
- **Ufa Plateau:** A hilly region west of the southern Ural Mountains where the city is situated.
- **Belaya River:** Also called Agidel; one of the two rivers at whose confluence Ufa lies.
- **Ufa Governorate:** A historical administrative division of the Russian Empire, with Ufa as its capital.
- **Bashneft:** A major petroleum company headquartered in Ufa, with several subsidiary plants in the city.
- **Trans-Siberian Railway:** A historic rail line connecting Moscow to the Russian Far East, on which Ufa is located.
- **Warm summer continental climate (Dfb):** A Köppen climate classification characterized by warm summers and cold, snowy winters.
- **CEMS (Continuous Emission Control System):** A system for monitoring air pollution emissions, noted as lacking in Ufa.
- **Central Spiritual Administration of Muslims in Russia:** A major Islamic religious administrative body located in Ufa.

## Debates and open questions
- The etymology of the river name "Ufa" is disputed, with competing Iranian and Turkic-origin hypotheses.
- The existence and exact location of a pre-Russian medieval settlement (Pascherti) on the site are debated among historians.
- The effectiveness and coverage of air quality monitoring in Ufa's residential areas are questioned, with reports of pollution exceedances and gaps in monitoring near some neighborhoods.

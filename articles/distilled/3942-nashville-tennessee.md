# Nashville, Tennessee

## Overview
Nashville is the capital and most populous city of Tennessee, located on the Cumberland River. It is a major center for the music industry, particularly country music, earning it the nickname "Music City." The city is also a significant hub for healthcare, publishing, and automotive manufacturing, and has experienced rapid population growth in the 21st century.

## Body

### Founding and Early Growth
Nashville was founded in 1779 as Fort Nashborough on the site of an earlier French trading post called French Lick. It was chartered in 1784 and named for Revolutionary War general Francis Nash. The city grew quickly due to its strategic location as a port on the Cumberland River and later as a railroad center. By 1806, it was incorporated and became the county seat of Davidson County. In 1843, it was named Tennessee's permanent capital.

### Civil War and Reconstruction
Nashville's importance as a transportation hub made it a key military target. It fell to Union forces in February 1862, becoming the first Confederate state capital to be occupied. The city was occupied for the remainder of the war. The Battle of Nashville in December 1864 was a decisive Union victory. After the war, the city reclaimed its stature as a trade and manufacturing center. The Fisk Jubilee Singers, formed in 1871, brought international acclaim to Nashville's African American musical culture.

### 20th Century: Music and Civil Rights
The establishment of the Grand Ole Opry in 1925 cemented Nashville's status as a country music hub. The "Nashville Sound" of the 1950s broadened the genre's appeal. The city was a center for civil rights activism, with the 1960 sit-ins leading to the desegregation of lunch counters. In 1963, the city and county governments consolidated. The latter half of the century saw urban renewal, suburbanization, and the arrival of professional sports teams like the Tennessee Titans (1998) and Nashville Predators (1997).

### 21st Century: Growth and Challenges
Nashville has experienced significant population growth and economic expansion, becoming a top job market. However, this has brought challenges like rising housing costs, homelessness, and gentrification. The city has faced natural disasters, including a devastating flood in 2010 and tornadoes in 2020 and 2023. In 2023, a mass shooting at The Covenant School killed six people. The city continues to invest in infrastructure, with a 2024 referendum passing to fund transportation improvements.

## Terms
- **Consolidated city-county government:** A system where a city and its surrounding county merge into a single governing body, as Nashville did with Davidson County in 1963.
- **Nashville Sound:** A subgenre of country music developed in the 1950s, characterized by smooth strings and choruses, which broadened the music's appeal.
- **Grand Ole Opry:** A weekly country music stage show in Nashville, the world's longest-running live radio show, established in 1925.
- **Music Row:** An area in Nashville that is the center of the city's music industry, housing record labels, studios, and related businesses.
- **Metropolitan Government:** The official governing structure of Nashville and Davidson County, featuring a mayor and a 40-member Metropolitan Council.
- **Highland Rim:** A geophysical region of hilly land that encircles the Nashville Basin, influencing the city's topography.
- **Fisk Jubilee Singers:** An African American a cappella ensemble from Fisk University that gained international fame in the 1870s for performing Negro spirituals.

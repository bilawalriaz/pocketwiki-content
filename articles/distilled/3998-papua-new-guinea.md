# Papua New Guinea

## Overview
Papua New Guinea (PNG) is a highly diverse island nation in Oceania, comprising the eastern half of New Guinea and offshore islands. Its extreme linguistic and cultural diversity, rugged geography, and resource-dependent economy make it a unique case study in post-colonial development. The country's history is marked by early human settlement, colonial division, World War II, and a complex path to independence in 1975, with ongoing challenges in governance, social cohesion, and economic development.

## Timeline
- **c. 42,000–45,000 years ago** — Modern humans first arrive in New Guinea and Australia.
- **c. 7000 BC** — Agriculture independently developed in the New Guinea highlands.
- **1884** — Eastern New Guinea divided between German New Guinea (north) and British New Guinea (south).
- **1914** — Australia occupies German New Guinea during World War I.
- **1942–1944** — Fierce fighting during the New Guinea campaign of World War II.
- **1949** — Australian-administered territories of Papua and New Guinea formally united.
- **16 September 1975** — Papua New Guinea gains independence as a Commonwealth realm.
- **1988–1998** — Bougainville conflict, a secessionist civil war.
- **2001** — Bougainville Peace Agreement signed, promising autonomy and a future referendum.
- **2019** — Bougainville votes overwhelmingly (98.31%) for independence in a non-binding referendum.
- **March 2025** — Constitution amended to adopt Christianity as the official state religion.

## Body

### Etymology and History
The name "Papua" derives from a local term, while "New Guinea" was coined by Spanish explorer Yñigo Ortiz de Retez in 1545 due to a perceived resemblance to people in Guinea, Africa. Archaeological evidence shows modern humans arrived around 42,000–45,000 years ago. Agriculture was independently developed in the highlands around 7000 BC. Austronesian-speaking Lapita peoples reached the Bismarck Archipelago by 3,300 years ago.

European influence began in the 16th century. The eastern half of the island was divided in 1884 between Germany (north) and Britain (south). Britain transferred its territory to Australia in 1902, renaming it the Territory of Papua. After World War I, Australia administered the former German territory as a League of Nations mandate. World War II brought fierce fighting, including the Kokoda Track campaign, and led to a joint military administration. The two territories were formally united in 1949 as the Territory of Papua and New Guinea. Independence was granted on 16 September 1975, with Michael Somare as the first Prime Minister.

### Government and Politics
PNG is a Commonwealth realm with a parliamentary system under a Westminster model. The unicameral National Parliament elects the Prime Minister, who forms the cabinet. Politics are characterized by unstable coalition governments, frequent votes of no confidence, and a lack of strong, ideologically distinct parties. A significant factor is the "wantok" system, a network of reciprocal obligations within kinship groups, which influences patronage and expectations of public service delivery. MPs control substantial discretionary Rural Development Funds, which can foster corruption but are also seen as essential for local representation. Over 97% of land is held under customary tenure by communities.

### Society and Culture
The population is highly rural and extremely diverse, with 840 known languages, making it the world's most linguistically diverse country. English, Tok Pisin, and Hiri Motu are common languages. The 2011 census found 95.6% of the population identified as Christian. Traditional "big man" social structures, where status is earned through merit and generosity, influence modern politics and economics. Cultural practices are diverse, with strong local identities often taking precedence over a national one. Rugby league is a major unifying national sport.

### Economy and Geography
The economy is developing and heavily dependent on natural resource extraction (minerals, oil, gas), which made up 28% of GDP in 2018. Agriculture employs about 85% of the workforce, with nearly 40% of the population being subsistence farmers. The country is geographically rugged, with mountains covering at least 72% of the land. It lies on the Pacific Ring of Fire, resulting in active volcanoes and frequent earthquakes. The terrain makes infrastructure development challenging, with air travel being a key mode of transport. PNG is a megadiverse country, hosting about 5% of global species.

## Terms
- **Wantok system**: A social network of reciprocal obligations among people who share a language or kinship group, influencing politics and expectations of patronage.
- **Big man**: A traditional Melanesian social role where leadership is earned through merit, oratory, and the generous distribution of wealth, rather than being hereditary.
- **Tok Pisin**: An English-based creole language and the primary lingua franca of Papua New Guinea.
- **Hiri Motu**: A pidgin language based on the Motu language, historically used as a lingua franca in the southern region.
- **Customary land**: Land held and governed by indigenous communities according to traditional laws and practices, covering over 97% of PNG's land area.
- **Limited Preferential Vote (LPV)**: An electoral system where voters rank at least three candidates in order of preference.
- **Commonwealth realm**: A sovereign state that recognizes the British monarch as its head of state, represented locally by a Governor-General.
- **Bougainville Autonomous Region**: An autonomous region of PNG that fought a civil war (1988–1998) and voted overwhelmingly for independence in a 2019 referendum.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Population size**: Official estimates are around 11.8 million, but a 2022 UN report using satellite data suggested a figure closer to 17 million, creating significant uncertainty.
- **Bougainville's future**: The 2019 referendum was non-binding. Negotiations between the PNG national government and the Bougainville Autonomous Government on the path to potential independence are ongoing.
- **Governance and corruption**: The interplay between the wantok system, patronage politics, and formal anti-corruption measures creates a persistent challenge, with cultural expectations often clashing with legal norms.
- **Land tenure**: The vast majority of land is customary and unregistered, which complicates large-scale development projects and economic formalization.

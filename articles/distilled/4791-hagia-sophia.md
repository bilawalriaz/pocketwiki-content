# Hagia Sophia

## Overview
Hagia Sophia is a monumental architectural landmark in Istanbul, Turkey, that has served as a Christian cathedral, an Ottoman mosque, a secular museum, and a mosque again. Its significance lies in its role as the world's largest interior space for nearly a millennium, its pioneering use of the pendentive dome, and its status as a symbol of the shifting religious and political powers of the Byzantine and Ottoman empires.

## Timeline
- **360** — First church (Magna Ecclesia) consecrated under Emperor Constantius II.
- **415** — Second church inaugurated by Emperor Theodosius II.
- **532–537** — Current structure built by Emperor Justinian I after the Nika riots.
- **558** — Eastern semi-dome collapses; rebuilt higher by Isidore the Younger in 562.
- **1054** — Excommunication of Patriarch Michael I Cerularius delivered, starting the East–West Schism.
- **1204** — Sacked by Crusaders during the Fourth Crusade; converted to a Catholic cathedral.
- **1261** — Restored to the Eastern Orthodox Church after the Byzantine recapture of Constantinople.
- **1453** — Conquered by the Ottomans; converted into a mosque by Sultan Mehmed II.
- **1935** — Secularized and converted into a museum by Mustafa Kemal Atatürk.
- **2020** — Reclassified as a mosque by a Turkish court decision.

## Body

### Early Churches and Construction
The site has hosted three successive churches. The first, the Magna Ecclesia, was consecrated in 360 and largely burned down in 404. The second, built by Theodosius II, was inaugurated in 415 but destroyed in the Nika riots of 532. The current basilica was commissioned by Emperor Justinian I and designed by Anthemius of Tralles and Isidore of Miletus. Completed in 537, it became the world's largest church and the episcopal see of the Ecumenical Patriarch of Constantinople. Its innovative pendentive dome, which allows a circular dome to sit atop a square base, made it a masterpiece of Byzantine architecture.

### Byzantine Era and Latin Interlude
Hagia Sophia was the spiritual center of Eastern Orthodox Christianity for nearly a millennium. It was damaged by earthquakes in 553, 557, and 558, leading to a major reconstruction of the dome in 562. The church was stripped of icons during the Byzantine Iconoclasm (726–843). In 1054, the ceremony delivering the excommunication that formalized the East–West Schism took place within its walls. During the Fourth Crusade in 1204, the church was ransacked and converted into a Catholic cathedral under the Latin Empire. It was restored to Orthodox control in 1261 but fell into disrepair.

### Ottoman Conversion and Modifications
After the fall of Constantinople in 1453, Sultan Mehmed II immediately converted the building into a mosque. Minarets were added, and Christian mosaics were gradually covered with plaster or whitewash. Major structural reinforcements were added by the architect Mimar Sinan in the 16th century, including exterior buttresses and additional minarets. The interior was adorned with Islamic calligraphy, mihrabs, and minbars. The complex was expanded into a *külliye* (social complex) with a medrese, library, and mausoleums (*türbes*) for sultans.

### Museum and Reversion to Mosque
In 1935, Mustafa Kemal Atatürk secularized the building, turning it into a museum. This allowed for the uncovering and restoration of many Byzantine mosaics. In 2020, Turkey's Council of State annulled the 1934 museum decision, and President Erdoğan signed a decree reclassifying Hagia Sophia as a mosque. The move was controversial, drawing condemnation from UNESCO, the World Council of Churches, and international leaders, while being welcomed by many Muslim leaders. Christian mosaics are now covered by drapes during prayer times but remain visible to visitors outside of those hours.

## Terms
- **Pendentive**: A structural element that allows a circular dome to be placed over a square room, transferring the dome's weight to the supporting piers.
- **Narthex**: An enclosed porch or entrance hall at the western end of a church.
- **Apse**: A semicircular recess, typically at the eastern end of a church, housing the altar.
- **Mosaic**: A picture or pattern produced by arranging small colored pieces of stone, glass, or ceramic (tesserae).
- **Minaret**: A tall tower attached to a mosque, from which the call to prayer is issued.
- **Mihrab**: A niche in the wall of a mosque that indicates the direction of Mecca.
- **Waqf**: An inalienable religious endowment in Islamic law, typically donating a building or land for religious or charitable use.
- **Külliye**: A complex of buildings associated with a mosque, often including a school, hospital, and soup kitchen.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Architectural Origins**: The exact form of the first two churches on the site is debated, with theories ranging from a U-shaped basilica to a conventional three-aisled plan.
- **Mosaic Dating**: The precise date of the famous apse mosaic of the Virgin and Child is contested, with scholarly proposals ranging from the late 8th to the 14th century.
- **UNESCO Status**: Following the 2020 reconversion, UNESCO has expressed "grave concern" and requested conservation reports, questioning whether the change affects the site's "outstanding universal value."

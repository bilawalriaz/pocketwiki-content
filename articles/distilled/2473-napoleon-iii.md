# Napoleon III

## Overview
Napoleon III (1808–1873) was the first elected president of France and its last monarch, ruling as Emperor from 1852 to 1870. He established the Second French Empire, a period of significant modernization, infrastructure expansion, and increased French influence abroad, which ended with his defeat in the Franco-Prussian War.

## Timeline
- **20 April 1808** — Born Charles-Louis Napoléon Bonaparte in Paris.
- **1836 & 1840** — Leads two failed coups against the July Monarchy; imprisoned after the second.
- **10 December 1848** — Elected first President of the French Second Republic with 74.2% of the vote.
- **2 December 1851** — Seizes power in a coup d'état, dissolving the National Assembly.
- **2 December 1852** — Proclaimed Emperor of the French, founding the Second Empire.
- **1853–1856** — Allies with Britain to defeat Russia in the Crimean War.
- **1859** — Leads France to victory against Austria in the Second Italian War of Independence.
- **1860** — Annexes Savoy and Nice following referendums.
- **1862–1867** — Intervention in Mexico ends in failure with the execution of Emperor Maximilian I.
- **19 September 1870** — Captured with his army at the Battle of Sedan during the Franco-Prussian War.
- **4 September 1870** — The Second Empire falls; the Third Republic is proclaimed.
- **9 January 1873** — Dies in exile in Chislehurst, England.

## Body

### Rise to Power
Born the nephew of Napoleon I, Louis-Napoleon spent his youth in exile. He became the Bonapartist heir in 1832 and developed a political philosophy blending Bonapartism with republicanism and popular sovereignty. After two failed coups (1836, 1840) and imprisonment, he escaped to London. The 1848 Revolution allowed his return; he was elected president in a landslide, appealing to peasants, workers, and conservatives. When the National Assembly blocked his re-election, he executed a successful coup in December 1851, followed by a plebiscite approving his actions. He proclaimed himself Emperor in 1852, ending the Second Republic.

### Domestic Policy and Modernization
Napoleon III's reign focused on economic modernization and infrastructure. His government stimulated investment through new banks (e.g., Crédit Mobilier), expanded the railway network from 3,500 km to 20,000 km, and supported the Suez Canal. He oversaw the massive reconstruction of Paris, directed by Prefect Georges-Eugène Haussmann, which created broad boulevards, new parks, and modern sanitation. Social reforms included granting workers the right to strike (1864) and organize (1866), and expanding education for girls and women. Economic policies lowered tariffs, notably through the 1860 Cobden–Chevalier Treaty with Britain, which modernized French industry but also sparked opposition.

### Foreign Policy and Imperial Expansion
Napoleon III aimed to restore French influence through a "principle of nationalities," supporting new states like Italy. He allied with Britain to defeat Russia in the Crimean War (1853–56), boosting French prestige. He assisted Italian unification against Austria, gaining Savoy and Nice in return, but later defended the Papal States against Italian annexation to appease French Catholics. His colonial empire doubled in size, with acquisitions in Asia and Africa. However, his attempt to install a French-backed monarchy in Mexico ended in total failure. His later years were dominated by the rising threat of Prussia.

### Downfall
From 1866, Napoleon III failed to counter the growing power of Prussia and its minister-president, Otto von Bismarck. He found no effective allies against German unification. In 1870, following the manipulated Ems Dispatch, he reluctantly declared war on Prussia. The French army was rapidly defeated, and Napoleon III was captured at Sedan on 2 September. He was deposed, and the Third Republic was proclaimed. He died in exile in England in 1873.

## Terms
- **Bonapartism**: A French political ideology supporting the rule of a Bonaparte emperor, blending authoritarianism with plebiscitary legitimacy and social reform.
- **Plebiscite**: A direct vote by the electorate on a particular proposal, often used by Napoleon III to legitimize his rule.
- **Haussmannization**: The massive urban renewal of Paris under Prefect Georges-Eugène Haussmann, characterized by wide boulevards and uniform architecture.
- **Principle of Nationalities**: Napoleon III's foreign policy doctrine supporting the creation of nation-states based on ethnic or linguistic identity.
- **Cobden–Chevalier Treaty**: An 1860 free-trade agreement between France and Britain that significantly lowered tariffs.
- **Salon des Refusés**: An 1863 exhibition of artworks rejected by the official Paris Salon, authorized by Napoleon III, which showcased avant-garde artists.
- **Ems Dispatch**: A telegram from King Wilhelm I of Prussia, edited by Bismarck to appear insulting, which provoked France into declaring war in 1870.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Legitimacy and Illegitimacy**: His birth was rumored to be illegitimate, a claim supported by some genetic studies but contested by others.
- **Historical Reputation**: Historians debate whether he was a "vulgar adventurer" or a modernizing statesman. His domestic reforms are often praised, while his foreign policy is seen as a series of miscalculations culminating in disaster.
- **Role of Coup d'état**: The 1851 coup is analyzed as a pivotal moment that ended the Second Republic, with debates on its popular mandate versus its authoritarian nature.
- **Economic Impact**: While credited with modernizing France's economy and infrastructure, some argue his policies primarily benefited the bourgeoisie and created unsustainable debt.

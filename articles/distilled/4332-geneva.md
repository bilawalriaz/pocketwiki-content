# Geneva

## Overview
Geneva is a global city in southwest Switzerland, situated where the Rhône exits Lake Geneva. As the capital of the Canton of Geneva, it is a major international financial center and a worldwide hub for diplomacy, hosting the highest number of international organizations in the world, including many UN agencies. This unique role has earned it the nickname "Peace Capital."

## Body

### History and Political Status
Geneva was an Allobrogian border town when captured by Rome in 121 BC. In the Middle Ages, it was ruled by a count under the Holy Roman Empire until gaining self-governance in the late 14th century. The Protestant Reformation in the 16th century, led by John Calvin, caused religious strife and led to an alliance with the Swiss Confederacy. After a period of French influence and a brief annexation by Revolutionary France (1798), Geneva recovered its independence and officially joined Switzerland in 1815. The city's international vocation grew in the 20th century, hosting the League of Nations and later becoming the European headquarters of the United Nations.

### Geography and Metropolitan Area
Geneva is located at the southwestern tip of Lake Geneva, surrounded by Jura mountain ranges. The city municipality is small (16 km²), but its metropolitan area extends significantly into France. The "Grand Genève" is a transnational cooperation zone covering 1,996 km² with a population of over 1 million, of which 58.3% live in Switzerland and 41.7% in France. This cross-border urban area is managed through formal cooperation bodies established since the 1970s.

### Demographics and Society
The city of Geneva had a population of 203,856 in 2021. The metropolitan area is one of Europe's fastest-growing, with a population of 1,053,436 in 2021. French is the official language. The population is highly international, with 48% being resident foreign nationals as of 2013. Historically a Protestant city, Geneva's religious landscape has diversified due to immigration and secularization; as of 2023, 51.1% of the population had no religious affiliation or an unknown one, while 38.5% identified as Christian.

### Economy
Geneva's economy is service-driven, dominated by finance, particularly commodity trading (oil, sugar, grains), trade finance, and wealth management. The canton is a major center for watchmaking, hosting luxury brands like Patek Philippe and Rolex. It hosts the headquarters of numerous international organizations, including the UN, WHO, WTO, and Red Cross, which are major employers. Agriculture, notably wine production, exists in the hinterlands.

### International Organizations
Geneva hosts the European headquarters of the United Nations and the headquarters of many of its specialized agencies (WHO, ILO, ITU, WIPO, WMO). It is also home to the World Trade Organization, the International Committee of the Red Cross, the World Economic Forum, and CERN, the world's largest particle physics laboratory. This concentration makes it a global center for diplomacy, humanitarian work, and scientific research.

## Terms
- **Romandy**: The French-speaking part of Switzerland.
- **Calvinism**: A major branch of Protestantism based on the theological teachings of John Calvin.
- **Grand Genève**: A transnational cooperation zone encompassing the Canton of Geneva, parts of Vaud, and French territories.
- **CERN**: The European Organization for Nuclear Research, a particle physics laboratory near Geneva.
- **Geneva Conventions**: International treaties establishing humanitarian standards for the treatment of non-combatants and prisoners of war.
- **Bise**: A cold, northerly wind that can affect Geneva in winter.
- **Pierres du Niton**: Two large rocks in Lake Geneva that define the city's altitude reference point.
- **Léman Express**: A cross-border commuter rail network serving the Geneva metropolitan area.

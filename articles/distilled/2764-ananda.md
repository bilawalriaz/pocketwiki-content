# Ānanda

## Overview
Ānanda was the primary attendant and cousin of the historical Buddha, renowned for his exceptional memory. He is pivotal to Buddhism because he is credited with reciting the Buddha's discourses at the First Buddhist Council, forming the core of the Sutta Piṭaka (the collection of discourses). His role as the "Treasurer of the Dhamma" (Buddha's teaching) and his advocacy for the establishment of the bhikkhunī (nun) order make him a central figure in the preservation and early organization of the Buddhist community.

## Timeline
- **c. 5th–4th century BCE** — Ānanda is born, likely as the Buddha's first cousin.
- **c. 20 years into Buddha's ministry** — Ānanda is chosen as the Buddha's permanent attendant.
- **c. 5 years later** — Ānanda persuades the Buddha to ordain women, establishing the bhikkhunī order.
- **c. 483 or 400 BCE (debated)** — The Buddha dies; Ānanda is present but not yet enlightened.
- **Shortly after Buddha's death** — Ānanda attains enlightenment just before the First Buddhist Council.
- **At the First Buddhist Council** — Ānanda recites the Buddha's discourses, facing criticism from Mahākassapa.
- **c. 20 years after Buddha's death** — Ānanda dies, passing the teaching lineage to his pupils.

## Body

### Role as Attendant
Ānanda served as the Buddha's personal attendant for the last 25 years of the Buddha's life. He accepted the role on the condition that he receive no material benefits, but could accept invitations on the Buddha's behalf, ask questions, and have teachings repeated. He acted as a secretary, intermediary with laypeople and the saṅgha (monastic community), and a mouthpiece. His devotion was profound; he once risked his life to protect the Buddha from a rampaging elephant sent by the monk Devadatta.

### Establishing the Nun's Order
A key event in Ānanda's life was his role in founding the bhikkhunī order. When the Buddha's foster-mother, Mahāpajāpatī Gotamī, requested ordination, the Buddha initially refused. Ānanda interceded, arguing that women could achieve enlightenment and reminding the Buddha of Mahāpajāpatī's care for him. The Buddha relented but imposed the *garudhamma* (heavy rules), making the nun's order subordinate to the monks. Scholars debate whether the Buddha's reluctance was genuine or a test, and some suggest Ānanda's role may be symbolic of a later institutional development.

### The Buddha's Death and the First Council
Ānanda was with the Buddha during his final journey and death. He failed to ask the Buddha to prolong his life, a lapse attributed to distraction by Māra (the personification of evil). After the Buddha's death, the First Buddhist Council was convened to preserve the teachings. Only enlightened monks (*arahants*) were allowed to attend. Ānanda, not yet enlightened, was initially banned. He attained enlightenment on the eve of the council, reportedly in a moment between lying down and standing up. He then recited the discourses, verifying each with the assembly. During the council, he was charged with offenses, including enabling women's ordination and failing to ask the Buddha to live longer. He confessed to prevent schism, though he defended his actions.

### Character and Legacy
Ānanda is depicted as compassionate, erudite, and devoted, but also as a foil to the Buddha—worldly, attached, and slow to attain enlightenment. He was praised by the Buddha for his memory, conduct, and resoluteness. His primary legacy is as the living memory who preserved the Buddha's discourses. He passed the teaching lineage to pupils like Sāṇavāsī and Majjhantika, who played roles in the Second and Third Councils. He is honored in bhikkhunī traditions for establishing their order and is credited with designing the monk's robe and introducing certain rituals.

## Terms
- **Sutta Piṭaka**: The collection of the Buddha's discourses, part of the Pāli Canon.
- **Dhamma (Dharma)**: The Buddha's teaching or doctrine.
- **Saṅgha**: The Buddhist monastic community.
- **Bhikkhunī**: A Buddhist nun.
- **Garudhamma**: The "heavy rules" imposed on the bhikkhunī order, making it subordinate to the bhikkhu (monk) order.
- **Arahant**: A fully enlightened disciple who has attained Nirvana.
- **Sotāpanna (Śrotāpanna)**: A "stream-enterer," one who has attained the first stage of enlightenment.
- **Māra**: The Buddhist personification of evil or temptation.
- **Paritta**: Protective chants or recitations in Theravāda Buddhism.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Historicity of the First Council**: Many scholars doubt the traditional account that the entire canon was recited at a single council shortly after the Buddha's death, suggesting it may be a later idealized narrative.
- **Ānanda's Role in the Bhikkhunī Order**: Scholars debate whether the account of Ānanda persuading the Buddha is historical or a later literary device to connect the nun's order to the Buddha. Some argue the order may have been established after the Buddha's death.
- **Charges Against Ānanda**: The offenses leveled against Ānanda at the council are seen by some scholars as reflecting later sectarian tensions between schools emphasizing monastic discipline versus those emphasizing discourses.
- **Ānanda's Enlightenment**: The dramatic story of his last-minute enlightenment before the council is viewed with skepticism by some historians as a legendary motif to legitimize his role.

# Bobby Fischer

## Overview
Robert James "Bobby" Fischer (1943–2008) was an American chess prodigy and the eleventh World Chess Champion. He is widely regarded as one of the greatest chess players of all time, whose dominance in the early 1970s and his 1972 World Championship victory over Boris Spassky—a Cold War spectacle—ignited a global chess boom. His legacy is complex, marked by extraordinary chess achievements, a reclusive and controversial personal life, and significant contributions to chess theory and culture.

## Timeline
- **March 9, 1943** — Born in Chicago, Illinois.
- **1956** — Wins first US Junior Championship at age 13; plays "The Game of the Century."
- **1957/58** — Wins first US Championship at age 14, becoming the youngest US Champion.
- **1958** — Becomes the youngest Grandmaster (age 15) and Candidates qualifier.
- **1962** — Wins Stockholm Interzonal; accuses Soviet players of collusion at Candidates Tournament.
- **1963/64** — Achieves a perfect 11–0 score in the US Championship, the only one in tournament history.
- **1970–71** — Wins Interzonal and Candidates matches, defeating Taimanov and Larsen each 6–0.
- **1972** — Defeats Boris Spassky in Reykjavík to become World Chess Champion.
- **1975** — Refuses to defend title against Anatoly Karpov; forfeits championship by default.
- **1992** — Re-emerges to win unofficial rematch against Spassky in Yugoslavia, violating US sanctions.
- **2004** — Arrested in Japan for traveling on a revoked US passport.
- **January 17, 2008** — Dies in Reykjavík, Iceland, where he had been granted citizenship.

## Body

### Early Life and Prodigious Talent
Fischer learned chess at age six and quickly became engrossed, playing thousands of games and studying intensely. His talent was recognized early, leading to mentorship and rapid advancement. He dropped out of high school at 16 to focus on chess, having already become the youngest US Champion and Grandmaster. His early career was marked by a meteoric rise through US and international tournaments, including winning the US Championship a record eight times, with a perfect 11–0 score in 1963/64.

### Rise to the World Championship
Fischer's path to the title was marked by both brilliance and controversy. He won the 1962 Interzonal but accused Soviet players of collusion at the Candidates Tournament, leading to FIDE format changes. After periods of semi-retirement, he returned in 1970 to dominate the chess world. He won the 1970 Interzonal and then swept through the 1971 Candidates matches, defeating Mark Taimanov and Bent Larsen by unprecedented 6–0 scores before beating Tigran Petrosian 6½–2½. This set up the 1972 World Championship match against Boris Spassky in Reykjavík, Iceland.

### World Champion and Title Forfeiture
The 1972 match, billed as a Cold War confrontation, became a global media sensation. After losing the first two games (one by forfeit), Fischer won the match 12½–8½ to become champion. He made lasting contributions to chess, including his book *My 60 Memorable Games* and patenting the Fischer chess clock, which added a time increment after each move. In 1975, he refused to defend his title against challenger Anatoly Karpov, objecting to FIDE's rejection of his proposed match conditions (first to 10 wins, no game limit, and a 9–9 draw clause favoring the champion). Consequently, Karpov was named World Champion by default.

### Later Life and Legacy
Fischer disappeared from public life for nearly 20 years after 1975. He re-emerged in 1992 for an unofficial rematch against Spassky in embargoed Yugoslavia, leading to a US arrest warrant. He lived as an émigré, making controversial antisemitic statements and praising the 9/11 attacks. Arrested in Japan in 2004, he was granted Icelandic citizenship and lived there until his death. Fischer invented Fischerandom (Chess960), a variant with randomized starting positions. His play was characterized by deep opening preparation, flawless endgames, and an intimidating psychological presence that often unnerved opponents.

## Terms
- **Grandmaster (GM):** The highest title a chess player can attain, awarded by FIDE for exceptional skill.
- **Interzonal:** A tournament in the World Championship cycle where players competed to qualify for the Candidates Tournament.
- **Candidates Tournament/Matches:** The stage of the World Championship cycle where players competed to become the challenger for the world title.
- **FIDE:** The Fédération Internationale des Échecs, the international governing body of chess.
- **Elo rating:** A system for calculating the relative skill levels of players, used by FIDE.
- **Fischer clock:** A chess clock that adds a small time increment after each move, now standard in top play.
- **Fischerandom (Chess960):** A chess variant invented by Fischer where the initial positions of pieces are randomized to one of 960 possible setups.
- **Poisoned Pawn Variation:** A sharp line in the Najdorf Sicilian opening where Black's queen captures a pawn, a line Fischer helped prove sound.
- **Fischer Endgame:** A term for the endgame of rook, bishop, and pawns versus rook, knight, and pawns, due to Fischer's instructive wins.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Soviet Collusion:** Fischer's 1962 accusation that Soviet players made quick draws amongst themselves to conserve energy against him is widely considered to have been correct and led to FIDE format changes.
- **1975 Title Forfeiture:** Whether Fischer or FIDE was more at fault for the match not happening is debated. Fischer's demands (especially the 9–9 clause) were seen by some as reasonable for a champion and by others as unsporting.
- **Fischer vs. Karpov:** The hypothetical outcome of a 1975 match is a subject of speculation. Opinions are divided, with a slight majority believing Fischer would have won.
- **Psychological Condition:** Fischer was never formally diagnosed, but speculation exists about possible paranoid personality disorder or other conditions, based on his extreme views, reclusiveness, and erratic behavior.
- **Antisemitism:** Fischer's virulent antisemitism, despite his Jewish ancestry, is a major and disturbing aspect of his legacy, with ongoing discussion about its origins and impact.

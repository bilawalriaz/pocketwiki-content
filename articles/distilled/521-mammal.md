# Mammal

## Overview
Mammals (class Mammalia) are vertebrate animals defined by milk-producing mammary glands, a neocortex brain region, fur or hair, and three middle ear bones. They are the only living members of the Synapsida clade, which diverged from the Sauropsida (reptile/bird) lineage over 300 million years ago in the Carboniferous. Roughly 6,640 extant species exist across 27 orders, dominated by rodents, bats, and eulipotyphlans. Mammals originated from cynodont therapsids in the Late Triassic to Early Jurassic but remained small and largely nocturnal until the Cretaceous–Paleogene extinction (66 Ma) removed non-avian dinosaurs, triggering a rapid Cenozoic diversification into dominant terrestrial, aquatic, and aerial niches. Humans domesticated many mammals during the Neolithic Revolution, enabling sedentary agriculture and civilization, but now drive a major extinction crisis through habitat destruction and overexploitation.

## Timeline
- **~323–300 Ma (Pennsylvanian)** — Synapsida splits from reptile lineage; first amniotes appear.
- **~265 Ma (Middle Permian)** — Therapsids evolve, become dominant land vertebrates.
- **~252 Ma (Permian–Triassic)** — Mass extinction ends carnivorous therapsid dominance; archosaurs rise.
- **~225 Ma (Late Triassic)** — First mammals (per Kemp's definition, e.g., *Tikitherium*) appear from cynodonts.
- **~195 Ma (Early Jurassic)** — *Hadrocodium* shows definitive mammalian jaw joint (dentary-squamosal).
- **~167 Ma (Middle Jurassic)** — Crown group Mammalia estimated origin (Rowe definition); *Ambondro*, *Amphilestes* fossils.
- **~164 Ma (Mid-Jurassic)** — Earliest clear evidence of fur/hair (*Castorocauda*, *Megaconus*).
- **~160 Ma (Late Jurassic)** — Oldest known eutherian (*Juramaia sinensis*).
- **~125 Ma (Early Cretaceous)** — Oldest metatherian (*Sinodelphys*) and eutherian (*Eomaia*) with epipubic bones.
- **~120 Ma (Early Cretaceous)** — Early monotreme (*Teinolophos*) in Australia.
- **66 Ma (K–Pg extinction)** — Non-avian dinosaurs extinct; mammals undergo exponential diversification and body size increase.
- **~55 Ma (Early Eocene)** — Earliest known primate ancestor (*Archicebus achilles*).
- **~10,000 years ago** — Neolithic Revolution: domestication of mammals transforms human societies.

## Body

### Evolutionary Origins and Early History
Synapsids, the clade containing mammals and their extinct relatives, originated in the Pennsylvanian subperiod (~323–300 Ma) when they split from the sauropsid lineage. Early synapsids ("pelycosaurs") like *Dimetrodon* dominated the early Permian. Therapsids evolved in the Middle Permian (~265 Ma), developing larger skulls and equal-sized incisors, and became the dominant land vertebrates. The therapsid lineage progressed through stages: gradual development of a bony secondary palate; abrupt acquisition of endothermy among Mammaliamorpha (30–50 million years before true mammals); slow, erratic progression toward erect limb posture (monotremes retain semisprawling limbs); and the dentary bone gradually becoming the main lower jaw bone, with former jaw bones (articular, quadrate) repurposed as middle ear bones (malleus, incus).

The Permian–Triassic extinction (~252 Ma) ended therapsid dominance. Archosaurs filled large carnivore niches for 35 million years, though large cynodonts persisted. The first mammals (sensu Kemp) appeared ~225 Ma in the Late Triassic (*Tikitherium*). They expanded from a nocturnal insectivore niche ("nocturnal bottleneck") in the mid-Jurassic; *Castorocauda* (164 Ma) shows adaptations for swimming and digging. Mesozoic mammals were mostly multituberculates, eutriconodonts, and spalacotheriids.

### Classification and Phylogeny
No classification system is universally accepted. Living mammals split into **Monotremata** (5 egg-laying species: platypus, echidnas) and **Theria** (live-bearing). Theria divides into **Marsupialia** (pouched young, short gestation, epipubic bones) and **Placentalia** (long gestation via placenta, no epipubic bones). Placentalia contains the six most species-rich orders: Rodentia, Chiroptera, Eulipotyphla, Primates, Cetartiodactyla (whales + even-toed ungulates), and Carnivora.

Molecular studies (early 21st century) reveal three basal placental lineages diverging in the Cretaceous (105–120 Ma): **Afrotheria** (elephants, tenrecs), **Xenarthra** (sloths, armadillos), and **Boreoeutheria**. Boreoeutheria splits into **Euarchontoglires** (primates, rodents) and **Laurasiatheria** (bats, carnivores, ungulates). Relationships among the three basal groups are contentious (Atlantogenata, Epitheria, or Exafroplacentalia hypotheses). Crown group Mammalia is defined phylogenetically (Rowe, 1988) as the last common ancestor of monotremes and therians plus all descendants (~167 Ma, Middle Jurassic). Kemp's broader morphological definition (dentary-squamosal jaw joint, molar occlusion) places the origin at ~225 Ma (*Tikitherium*).

### Anatomy and Physiology
**Distinguishing fossil features:** Dentary-squamosal jaw joint (replacing articular-quadrate); three middle ear bones (malleus, incus, stapes) derived from ancestral jaw bones; diphyodonty (single tooth replacement) or monophyodonty/polyphyodonty; prismatic enamel; two occipital condyles (vs. one in other tetrapods). Nearly all mammaliaforms possess epipubic bones (lost/converted to baculum in placentals).

**Integument:** Hair is a definitive class characteristic. Types include definitive hair, vibrissae (whiskers), pelage (guard/underfur), spines, bristles, velli (down fur), and wool. Primary function is thermoregulation; density matters more than length. Arctic mammals (musk ox) have dense underfur for -40°C survival; desert mammals (camels) use dense fur to block solar heat. Coloration derives from melanin (eumelanin: brown/black; pheomelanin: yellow/red). Functions: camouflage (seasonal change in arctic species), aposematism (skunk, honey badger), sexual selection, thermoregulation.

**Locomotion:** Basic quadrupedal plan adapted for cursorial (digitigrade/unguligrade), fossorial (fusiform body, strong forelimbs), arboreal (prehensile tails, grasping hands/feet, mobile ankles), aerial (bats only; wing membrane with Merkel cells for airflow sensing), and aquatic (cetaceans/sirenians: tail fluke propulsion, flipper steering; pinnipeds: fore-flipper oscillation).

**Organ Systems:**
*   **Circulatory:** Four-chambered heart; enucleate red blood cells maximize hemoglobin.
*   **Respiratory:** Bellows lung driven by diaphragm; rib cage assists.
*   **Digestive:** Herbivores use multichambered stomachs (ruminants) or enlarged cecum (perissodactyls) for microbial cellulose fermentation; carnivores have simple stomachs and carnassial teeth; omnivores vary.
*   **Excretory:** Ureotelic (ammonia → urea via liver urea cycle); bean-shaped kidneys with renal pelvis/pyramids, cortex/medulla, loops of Henle; cloaca retained only in monotremes, afrosoricids, some shrews; marsupials have trace external cloaca.
*   **Nervous:** All possess neocortex; placentals have corpus callosum. Seven cervical vertebrae (exceptions: manatee/two-toed sloth = 6; three-toed sloth = 9).

**Reproduction:** Internal fertilization; gonochoric. Ancestral condition: birth of undeveloped young (constrained by epipubic bones preventing torso expansion). *Kayentatherium* (Jurassic) produced 38 perinates. Monotremes lay leathery eggs; young lick milk from mammary patches. Marsupials: short gestation, altricial young develop in marsupium (pouch); epipubic bones present. Placentals: long gestation, developed young; placenta enables nutrient transfer; epipubic bones lost/converted, allowing torso expansion. Milk composition differs: placental = lactose dominant; marsupial/monotreme = oligosaccharide dominant. Marsupial mammary glands can supply different milks simultaneously.

**Endothermy:** Near-universal; requires high food intake. Naked mole-rat is a poikilothermic exception. Endothermy likely evolved in non-mammalian therapsids.

**Sensory/Communication:** Larynx produces sound; vocal folds enable complex vocalizations. Infrasound (elephants, baleen whales) travels kilometers; ultrasound (rodents, bats, toothed whales) used for mother-pup communication and echolocation (bats use larynx; toothed whales use melon). Vocal learning rare: humans, seals, cetaceans, elephants, possibly bats.

**Intelligence:** Indicated by learning, behavioral flexibility, tool use (sea otters, chimpanzees), self-awareness (mirror test passed by great apes, humans >18 mo, bottlenose dolphins, orcas, false killer whales, Asian elephants). Encephalization quotient (brain size vs. allometric expectation) used as proxy. Sperm whales have largest absolute brain (~8,000 cm³, 7.8 kg).

### Behavior
**Social Structure:** Range from solitary/territorial (avoid competition) to fission-fusion societies (primates, elephants, hyenas, lions, dolphins—group size/composition changes adaptively) to hierarchies (despotic: wolves/lemurs; linear pecking orders: hyenas) to harems (one/few males monopolize females). Eusociality (overlap generations, reproductive division, cooperative care) occurs only in naked mole-rats and Damaraland mole-rats among mammals. Monogamy rare (wolves, beavers, otters); polygyny estimated in ~90% of mammals.

**Feeding:** High metabolic cost of endothermy demands nutritious diet. Small mammals (<510 g) mostly insectivorous (high surface-area-to-volume ratio); large insectivores only eat colonial insects (ants/termites). Herbivores require microbial fermentation (stomach/cecum). Carnivores have simple guts. Omnivores categorized: hypocarnivory (≤50% meat), mesocarnivory (50–70%), hypercarnivory (≥70%). Some physiological specialists consume atypical items for nutrients (ungulates gnaw bones; cats eat grass). Hibernation (cold) and aestivation (heat/drought) suppress metabolism.

**Lifespan:** Maximum lifespan ranges from 1 year (yellow-sided opossum) to 211 years (bowhead whale). Strong correlation between longevity and DNA repair capacity (excision repair, double-strand break recognition, Ku80 protein, PARP enzyme activity).

### Human Interactions
**Domestication & Culture:** Neolithic Revolution driven by mammal domestication (cattle, sheep, pigs, goats) ~10 kya, enabling sedentary farming, civilization, and radical biosphere restructuring. Livestock (2009: 1.4B cattle, 1B sheep, 1B pigs) provide meat, dairy, labor (80% power on small third-world farms, 20% global transport), leather, wool. Mammals are primary model organisms in research (millions of mice/rats annually; knockout mice for genetics; non-human primates for biomedical similarity). Cultural roles: pets, art (Paleolithic caves to Dürer/Stubbs), sport (racing, hunting), mythology, religion.

**Threats & Conservation:** Humans drive defaunation. Wild mammal biomass declined 82% since civilization began; wild mammals now 4% of mammalian biomass (humans + livestock = 96%). Vertebrate populations down 68% since 1970 (WWF 2020). Primary drivers: habitat destruction (deforestation for meat production), overexploitation (hunting, poaching, ivory trade), bycatch (65k–86k cetaceans/year). Quaternary megafauna extinctions coincide with human arrival. IUCN Red List (2008: 5,488 species assessed); Convention on Biological Diversity (Rio Accord, 189 signatories) coordinates conservation. ~80 mammal species extinct since 1500; others extinct in wild (Père David's deer), ecologically extinct (Florida panther), or locally extirpated (Atlantic gray whales).

**Hybrids:** Interspecific (lion × tiger), intergeneric (sheep × goat), intraspecific (Bengal × Siberian tiger). Natural hybrids in contact zones. "Breeding back" (e.g., Heck cattle for aurochs) creates look-alikes but original gene pool lost. Genetic pollution from domesticates threatens wild relatives (wild water buffalo by domestic buffalo).

## Terms
- ****Synapsida**** — Clade containing mammals and all extinct relatives closer to mammals than to reptiles/birds; characterized by single temporal fenestra in skull.
- ****Therapsida**** — Advanced synapsid group (from ~265 Ma) with larger skulls, equal incisors; ancestors of mammals.
- ****Cynodontia**** — Derived therapsid group from which mammals originated in Late Triassic/Early Jurassic.
- ****Crown group Mammalia**** — Phylogenetic definition (Rowe 1988): last common ancestor of living monotremes and therians + all descendants (~167 Ma).
- ****Epipubic bones**** — Paired bones projecting forward from pelvis in non-placental mammals; stiffen body wall during locomotion but constrain torso expansion, limiting gestation; lost/converted to baculum in placentals.
- ****Diphyodonty**** — Single tooth replacement pattern (deciduous → permanent) typical of most mammals.
- ****Carnassial teeth**** — Modified upper premolar and lower molar forming a shearing blade for meat processing; characteristic of Carnivora.
- ****Bellows lung**** — Mammalian lung ventilated by diaphragm and rib cage expansion/contraction, moving air down pressure gradient.
- ****Fission–fusion society**** — Social system where a permanent "parent group" frequently splits into subgroups (fission) and merges (fusion) to adapt to conditions.
- ****Encephalization quotient (EQ)**** — Ratio of actual brain size to expected brain size for body mass (allometric scaling ~2/3 or 3/4 exponent); used as intelligence proxy.

## Debates and open questions
*   **Placental root:** Relationships among Afrotheria, Xenarthra, and Boreoeutheria remain unresolved; three competing hypotheses (Atlantogenata, Epitheria, Exafroplacentalia) have support.
*   **Mammal definition timing:** Crown-group (Rowe, ~167 Ma) vs. broader morphological (Kemp, ~225 Ma) definitions yield different origin dates; *Tikitherium* (225 Ma) may actually be Neogene.
*   **Placental fossil gap:** Molecular clocks suggest placental orders diverged 100–85 Ma, but no undisputed placental fossils exist before the K–Pg boundary (66 Ma); earliest confirmed placentals are Paleocene.
*   **Endothermy origin:** Uncertain when true endothermy arose; likely in non-mammalian therapsids, but monotremes have lower/variable temperatures, and some placentals (afrotheres, xenarthrans) secondarily reduced theirs.
*   **Lifespan mechanisms:** DNA repair capacity correlates strongly with maximum lifespan across species, but causal pathways and relative importance of specific repair pathways (excision, double-strand break, PARP, Ku80) remain under investigation.
*   **Hybridization vs. conservation:** Genetic pollution from domesticates threatens wild species (e.g., water buffalo), but some gene flow is natural; defining management thresholds is difficult.

# Hyder Ali

## Overview
Hyder Ali (c. 1720–1782) was the de facto ruler of the Kingdom of Mysore in southern India, rising from a military commander to become its sultan. He is significant for transforming Mysore into a major regional power, challenging the British East India Company in the Anglo-Mysore Wars, and pioneering military rocket technology. His reign laid the foundation for the empire later expanded by his son, Tipu Sultan.

## Timeline
- **c. 1720** — Born in Budikote, Kolar district, Karnataka.
- **1749** — Distinguishes himself during the Siege of Devanahalli Fort in the Second Carnatic War.
- **1758** — Captures Bangalore from the Marathas.
- **1759** — Granted the title "Nawab Hyder Ali Khan" by Krishnaraja Wodeyar II.
- **1761** — Becomes de facto ruler of Mysore as Sarvadhikari (Chief Minister).
- **1766** — Defeats the Zamorin of Calicut, expanding Mysore into Malabar.
- **1767–69** — Fights the First Anglo-Mysore War, ending with the Treaty of Madras.
- **1770–79** — Engages in prolonged warfare with the Maratha Confederacy.
- **1780–84** — Leads Mysore in the Second Anglo-Mysore War against the British.
- **7 December 1782** — Dies of cancer at his camp near Chittoor; succeeded by his son Tipu Sultan.

## Body

### Rise to Power
Born to a military commander in the service of the Wodeyar Rajas, Hyder Ali entered Mysore's army and rose through the ranks during the Carnatic Wars, where he fought alongside French allies. He gained control of the Mysorean army by 1759 and, after a period of political intrigue and exile, returned to seize Srirangapatna in 1761. He deposed the chief minister, made the king Krishnaraja Wodeyar II a prisoner, and assumed the title of Sultan, establishing himself as the absolute ruler.

### Expansion and Conflicts
Hyder Ali's rule was marked by constant warfare. He expanded Mysore northward, capturing territories like Sira and Bednore from the Marathas. His campaigns in Malabar subdued local rulers like the Zamorin of Calicut. He fought two major wars against the British East India Company. The First Anglo-Mysore War (1767–69) ended in a stalemate with the Treaty of Madras. The Second Anglo-Mysore War (1780–84) began after the British captured the French port of Mahé, a key supply point for Mysore. Hyder initially achieved a major victory at the Battle of Pollilur (1780) but was later defeated by British General Eyre Coote at Porto Novo (1781).

### Administration and Innovations
Despite being illiterate, Hyder Ali was known for his prodigious memory and financial acumen. He developed a system of checks and balances to manage state finances and plunder. He established a small navy on the Malabar coast and cultivated relations with overseas powers like the Sultan of Muscat and the ruler of Persia. A key military innovation was the significant improvement and large-scale deployment of iron-cased military rockets, which were used effectively against British forces.

## Terms
- **Dalavayi/Sarvadhikari**: Titles for the chief minister and military commander-in-chief of Mysore, positions Hyder Ali held before becoming ruler.
- **Jagir**: A land grant given by a ruler to a subordinate in exchange for military service or revenue collection.
- **Sepoy**: An Indian soldier serving under European (often British or French) command.
- **Nawab**: A title of nobility, often used for Muslim rulers or governors in India.
- **Carnatic Wars**: A series of 18th-century conflicts in southern India between European powers (Britain and France) and their local allies for regional dominance.
- **Rocket artillery**: A military technology using self-propelled projectiles, which Hyder Ali's forces significantly improved and deployed in large numbers.
- **Status quo ante bellum**: A Latin phrase meaning "the way things were before the war," referring to a peace treaty that restores pre-war conditions.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Ancestry**: Historical sources debate Hyder Ali's ethnic origins, with traditions suggesting Arab, Punjabi, or other lineages.
- **Economic Impact**: The severity and long-term effects of his economic policies and military campaigns on regions like Tanjore, where output reportedly fell by 90%, are subjects of historical analysis.
- **Naval Capabilities**: The documentary record on the Mysore navy is fragmentary, leading to debates about its true size, composition, and effectiveness.

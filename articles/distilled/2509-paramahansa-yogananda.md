# Paramahansa Yogananda

## Overview
Paramahansa Yogananda (1893–1952) was an Indian Hindu monk, yogi, and guru who became a pioneering spiritual teacher in the West. He founded the Self-Realization Fellowship (SRF) to disseminate the teachings of Kriya Yoga and is widely regarded as the "Father of Yoga in the West" for his lifelong work demonstrating the unity between Eastern and Western religions.

## Timeline
- **Jan 5, 1893** — Born Mukunda Lal Ghosh in Gorakhpur, India.
- **1910** — Meets his guru, Swami Sri Yukteswar Giri, at age 17.
- **1915** — Takes formal monastic vows, choosing the name Swami Yogananda Giri.
- **1917** — Founds a school in Dihika, West Bengal, combining modern education with yoga training.
- **Aug 1920** — Departs for the United States; arrives in Boston.
- **1920** — Founds the Self-Realization Fellowship (SRF) in the U.S.
- **1925** — Establishes SRF international headquarters in Los Angeles.
- **1935–36** — Returns to India; visits guru Sri Yukteswar, who dies in March 1936.
- **1946** — Publishes his seminal work, *Autobiography of a Yogi*.
- **1949** — Becomes a naturalized U.S. citizen.
- **Mar 7, 1952** — Dies in Los Angeles after giving a speech at a banquet.

## Body

### Early Life and Training
Born into a Bengali family, Mukunda Lal Ghosh showed spiritual inclinations from childhood. After seeking various teachers, he met his guru, Swami Sri Yukteswar Giri, in 1910 and trained under him for a decade. Sri Yukteswar informed him he had been chosen by the great guru Mahavatar Babaji for a special mission. In 1915, Yogananda took monastic vows and later founded a school that became the Yogoda Satsanga Society of India (YSS).

### Teaching in America
In 1920, Yogananda arrived in the U.S. as India's delegate to a religious congress in Boston. He founded the Self-Realization Fellowship and embarked on successful lecture tours, attracting thousands. He settled in Los Angeles in 1925, establishing its headquarters. He was the first prominent Indian teacher to settle long-term in the West, facing challenges including FBI surveillance and racial prejudice. In 1928, he was barred from holding events in Miami due to threats and racial tensions.

### Return to India and Later Years
In 1935, Yogananda returned to India, where he was greeted by large crowds and met notable figures like Mahatma Gandhi. His guru Sri Yukteswar died in 1936, after which Yogananda reported a supernatural encounter with his resurrected form. Returning to the U.S. in late 1936, he continued his work, writing his *Autobiography of a Yogi* at a hermitage in Encinitas, California. He became a U.S. citizen in 1949 and spent his final years in seclusion, focusing on his writings.

### Teachings and Kriya Yoga
Yogananda's core teaching was Kriya Yoga, an ancient meditation technique for spiritual union. He taught that the purpose of life is the evolution of consciousness toward God-realization. He emphasized the harmony between the original teachings of Jesus Christ and the yoga of Krishna, advocating for "plain living and high thinking." His organization, SRF/YSS, was established to disseminate these techniques and principles worldwide.

### Death and Legacy
Yogananda died on March 7, 1952, after speaking at a banquet in Los Angeles. The official cause was a heart attack, though his disciples believe he entered *mahasamadhi* (a yogi's conscious exit). His body was reported to show no signs of decay for weeks after death. His *Autobiography of a Yogi* (1946) became an international bestseller, inspiring millions. SRF/YSS continues his work, with centers in over 175 countries.

## Terms
- **Kriya Yoga**: The foundational meditation technique taught by Yogananda, described as "union with the Infinite through a certain action or rite."
- **Self-Realization Fellowship (SRF)**: The religious organization Yogananda founded in 1920 to disseminate his teachings worldwide.
- **Yogoda Satsanga Society of India (YSS)**: The Indian branch of Yogananda's organization, founded in 1917.
- **Mahasamadhi**: The yogic term for a master's conscious exit from the body at the time of death.
- **Paramahansa**: A title meaning "supreme swan," indicating the highest spiritual attainment, given to Yogananda by his guru in 1936.
- **Swami**: A Hindu monastic title.
- **Guru**: A spiritual teacher or guide.
- **Ashram**: A spiritual hermitage or monastery.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Cause of Death**: The medical verdict was "acute coronary occlusion" (heart attack), while his disciples maintain he entered *mahasamadhi*.
- **Bodily Incorruptibility**: The mortuary director reported an "unparalleled" absence of decay for over 20 days, though the body was embalmed, leading some to question the term "incorruptibility."
- **Paternity Allegations**: A paternity claim made in the 1990s was definitively settled by DNA tests in 2002, which showed no biological connection between Yogananda and the claimant.

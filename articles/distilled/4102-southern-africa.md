# Southern Africa

## Overview
Southern Africa is the southernmost region of the African continent, significant for its varied geography, mineral wealth, and complex history of kingdoms, colonialism, and modern nation-states. Its definition is contested, but it is generally recognized for its relatively developed economy and infrastructure compared to other African regions, alongside persistent challenges like inequality, crime, and high HIV/AIDS rates.

## Timeline
- **c. 350,000–260,000 years ago** — Modern humans (Homo sapiens) are believed to have arisen in East and Southern Africa.
- **c. 1075–c. 1220** — Kingdom of Mapungubwe flourishes as an early class-based state.
- **c. 1220–1450** — Kingdom of Zimbabwe succeeds Mapungubwe, with Great Zimbabwe as its capital.
- **1430–1760** — Kingdom of Mutapa emerges in the north after Zimbabwe's decline.
- **Late 18th century** — Mthethwa Paramountcy consolidates in present-day KwaZulu-Natal.
- **Early 19th century** — Zulu Kingdom rises under Shaka, leading to regional conflicts.
- **1969** — Southern African Customs Union (SACU) is created.
- **1980** — Southern African Development Community (SADC) is established; Zimbabwe gains independence.
- **1990** — Namibia achieves independence.
- **1994** — Democratic elections in South Africa end the Apartheid regime.

## Body

### Definitions and Usage
There is no single agreed-upon definition of Southern Africa. The most restrictive definition, used by the UN geoscheme and SACU, includes only Botswana, Eswatini, Lesotho, Namibia, and South Africa. A physical geography definition includes countries south of the Cunene and Zambezi Rivers. The broader SADC membership includes 16 nations, from Angola to Tanzania. In cultural geography, Madagascar is often excluded due to its distinct heritage.

### Geography and Climate
The terrain varies from deserts (Namib, Kalahari) to mountains (Drakensberg) and coastal plains. The region holds the world's largest resources of platinum, chromium, vanadium, and cobalt, along with significant gold, diamonds, and copper. Climate ranges from subtropical in the north to temperate in the south, with the Tropic of Capricorn dividing the region. Rainfall is highly variable, with most areas experiencing a summer wet season, except the Western Cape's winter rainfall zone. Climate change projections suggest a hotter, drier future.

### History
The region is a cradle of early human evolution. Early state societies included the Kingdom of Mapungubwe and the Kingdom of Zimbabwe, whose economies were based on cattle, farming, and gold. The 19th century saw the rise of the Zulu Kingdom and conflicts with Boer settlers. Colonial rule by European powers was followed by a post-WWII decolonization process, culminating in the independence of states like Zimbabwe (1980), Namibia (1990), and the end of Apartheid in South Africa (1994). South Africa emerged as the dominant economic power.

### Economy
Southern Africa has a more developed economy than other African regions, driven by a robust mining sector and manufacturing, particularly in South Africa and Zimbabwe. However, since the 1990s, industries have struggled with globalization and competition. Key challenges include poverty, corruption, HIV/AIDS, and a "brain drain" of skilled workers. South Africa is the dominant economy, with most global banks basing their regional offices in Johannesburg. Regional integration is pursued through SADC and the Southern Africa Power Pool.

### Demographics and Society
The region was initially populated by San and Khoikhoi peoples. The Bantu expansion led to the predominance of Bantu-speaking ethnic groups like the Zulu, Xhosa, and Shona. Colonization created smaller populations of European and Asian descent. Christianity is the majority religion, with sizeable Muslim communities in Mozambique and Malawi. Crime rates are notably high in several countries, including South Africa, Lesotho, and Eswatini.

### Food Security
Food security is threatened by political instability, drought, poverty, and poor governance. While South Africa is a major food producer, urban food insecurity is a growing crisis; a study of 11 cities found only 17% of households were food-secure. This is linked to climate change, migration, and a shift toward processed foods, leading to a dual burden of undernutrition and obesity.

## Terms
- **SADC:** Southern African Development Community, an intergovernmental organization established in 1980 to foster regional cooperation and integration.
- **SACU:** Southern African Customs Union, created in 1969, comprising Botswana, Eswatini, Lesotho, Namibia, and South Africa.
- **Bantu expansion:** A major historical migration and cultural shift where Bantu-speaking peoples spread across central, eastern, and southern Africa.
- **Veldt:** Open, uncultivated country or grassland in Southern Africa, divided into the Bushveld (lower altitude) and Highveld (higher altitude).
- **Fynbos:** A distinctive belt of natural shrubland or heathland vegetation in the Western Cape of South Africa, known for its biodiversity.
- **Great Dyke:** A prominent geological feature in Zimbabwe, a linear band of rocks containing significant mineral deposits, especially chromium and platinum.
- **Brain drain:** The emigration of highly trained or intelligent people from a particular country, a significant issue for South Africa and Zimbabwe.
- **FEWS NET:** Famine Early Warning Systems Network, an organization that provides data on food security and agricultural production.

## Debates and Open Questions
The primary debate is the **definition of the region itself**, with no consensus on which countries belong. Scholarly discussions also focus on the **causes of deindustrialization** in countries like Zimbabwe, weighing domestic factors against global economic pressures. The **effectiveness of regional bodies like SADC** in achieving true economic and political integration remains an open question, as does the long-term impact of **climate change** on the region's water resources and food security.

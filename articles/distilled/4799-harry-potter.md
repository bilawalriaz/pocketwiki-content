# Harry Potter

## Overview
The Harry Potter series is a seven-book fantasy saga by J.K. Rowling that chronicles the life of young wizard Harry Potter and his struggle against the dark wizard Lord Voldemort. Published between 1997 and 2007, the series became a global cultural phenomenon, transforming children's literature and spawning a vast multimedia franchise. Its exploration of themes like death, love, and prejudice, combined with its commercial success, has made it one of the most influential literary works of the modern era.

## Body

### Plot and World
The series follows Harry Potter, an orphan raised by his cruel aunt and uncle, who discovers he is a wizard at age 11. He attends Hogwarts School of Witchcraft and Wizardry, where he befriends Ron Weasley and Hermione Granger. The central narrative arc details Harry's conflict with Lord Voldemort, a dark wizard who murdered his parents and seeks immortality and domination over both the magical and non-magical ("Muggle") worlds. The story progresses through Harry's school years, with each book centered on a school year and a major mystery or threat connected to Voldemort. The final novel, *Deathly Hallows*, largely takes place outside Hogwarts as Harry and his friends hunt Voldemort's Horcruxes—objects containing fragments of his soul—before returning for a climactic battle.

### Style and Themes
The novels blend fantasy with elements of mystery, adventure, and the British boarding school story. Rowling juxtaposes an ordinary contemporary world with a hidden magical society. The overarching theme is death, explored through Harry's loss of his parents and his own confrontation with mortality. Love is presented as a powerful protective force, exemplified by his mother's sacrifice. The series also emphasizes that good and evil are defined by choices, not inherent nature, as seen in the complex character of Severus Snape. Other major themes include prejudice, corruption, and the importance of questioning authority.

### Development and Publishing
Rowling completed the first manuscript in 1995. After rejection from twelve publishers, Bloomsbury accepted it, and the first book was published in 1997. The American publisher, Scholastic, changed the title to *Sorcerer's Stone* for the US market. The series was published in English by Bloomsbury (UK) and Scholastic (US), with the final four books setting records as the fastest-selling in history. As of 2023, the books have sold over 600 million copies and been translated into more than 80 languages, including Latin and Ancient Greek.

### Reception and Legacy
The series achieved immense commercial success, making Rowling the first billionaire author and spawning an eight-film franchise, a stage play, and a planned television series. The brand is estimated to be worth $25 billion. Critical reception was initially positive but became mixed, with some scholars criticizing its literary style and others praising its narrative craft. The books have been both celebrated for encouraging literacy and criticized by religious groups for promoting witchcraft. Culturally, the series introduced terms like "Muggle" into common usage and inspired real-world adaptations like the sport of Quidditch. Its success revitalized the children's fantasy genre and had a profound impact on publishing and popular culture.

## Terms
*   **Muggle:** A non-magical person.
*   **Horcrux:** An object in which a Dark wizard has hidden a fragment of their soul to attain immortality.
*   **Death Eater:** A follower of Lord Voldemort.
*   **Pensieve:** A stone basin that allows a wizard to view memories stored in silvery substance.
*   **Patronus:** A magical guardian conjured by a charm, often taking the form of an animal, used to repel Dementors.
*   **Dementor:** A dark creature that feeds on human happiness, causing despair and cold.
*   **Triwizard Tournament:** A magical competition between three wizarding schools.
*   **The Order of the Phoenix:** A secret society founded to fight against Voldemort and his followers.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Literary Merit:** Critics like Harold Bloom and A.S. Byatt have questioned the series' literary style and originality, while supporters like Stephen King have praised its storytelling and imagination.
*   **Thematic Interpretation:** Scholars debate the coherence of the series' social messages, particularly regarding the portrayal of house-elves and the inherent superiority of wizards over Muggles, which some argue undermines its anti-prejudice themes.
*   **Influence on Literacy:** While widely believed to have boosted childhood reading, research from the National Endowment for the Arts found no evidence of a sustained increase in reading rates among children attributable to the series.
*   **Author's Intent vs. Reception:** Following J.K. Rowling's public statements on gender identity from 2019 onward, many former fans have rejected the series, creating a complex debate about separating art from the artist.

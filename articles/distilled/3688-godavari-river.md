# Godavari River

## Overview
The Godavari is India's second-longest river after the Ganges, draining the third-largest basin in the country. It is considered the largest river in peninsular India by length, catchment area, and discharge, earning it the title "Dakshina Ganga" (Southern Ganges). The river is vital for irrigation, drinking water, and religious practices across several states, but faces significant ecological challenges from damming and pollution.

## Timeline
- **c. 3000 BC** — Possible intensification of deforestation and erosion in the Deccan Plateau, indicated by sediment changes in the Bay of Bengal.
- **15th-16th C.** — Construction of the Laxmi Narasimha Swamy temple at Antarvedi in the Godavari delta.
- **1852** — Sir Arthur Cotton Barrage built across the river.
- **1876-1897** — Construction of the Old Godavari Bridge (Havelock Bridge).
- **1970-1974** — Construction of the Godavari Rail-cum-road Bridge.
- **1986** — The river experiences a recorded flood of 3.6 million cusecs.
- **1997** — Completion of the Godavari Arch Bridge.
- **2013** — The river reaches an all-time low water level in Nizamabad district, Telangana.
- **2015** — Godavari water is linked to the Krishna River via the Polavaram right bank canal and Pattiseema lift scheme.

## Body

### Course and Basin
The Godavari originates in the Western Ghats at Trimbakeshwar, Maharashtra, 80 km from the Arabian Sea. It flows 1,465 km east across the Deccan Plateau before turning southeast to empty into the Bay of Bengal. Its 312,812 km² drainage basin, nearly one-tenth of India's area, is divided into Upper (source to Manjira confluence), Middle (Manjira to Pranhita), and Lower (Pranhita to mouth) sections. The basin drains parts of Maharashtra, Telangana, Andhra Pradesh, Chhattisgarh, and Odisha.

### Tributaries and Delta
Major left-bank tributaries (Purna, Pranhita, Indravati, Sabari) contribute 59.7% of the basin's catchment, while right-bank tributaries (Pravara, Manjira, Manair) contribute 16.1%. The Pranhita is the largest tributary by drainage area. At Rajamahendravaram, the river splits into distributaries forming a large delta. The seven traditional distributary mouths are sacred to Hindus, though some original branches no longer exist.

### Hydrology and Infrastructure
The river has India's highest flood flows, with 1.0 million cusecs being a normal annual flood. Its mean annual water inflow is about 110 billion cubic meters, of which nearly 50% is harnessed. The basin has more dams than any other in India, including the Gangapur and Jayakwadi dams in Maharashtra and the Sriram Sagar Dam in Telangana. Water allocation among states is governed by the Godavari Water Disputes Tribunal.

### Geology and Ecology
The basin's geology is dominated by Deccan basalt (~50% of the area). The river carries the largest sediment load among peninsular Indian rivers. The Coringa mangrove forests in the delta are the country's third-largest mangrove formation. The basin is a nesting site for endangered olive ridley sea turtles and home to the endangered fringed-lipped carp.

### Ecological Concerns
Frequent drying of the river in lean months is a major concern, attributed to indiscriminate damming and sugarcane irrigation in Maharashtra. The delta is at risk as sediment deposition no longer keeps pace with relative sea-level rise, with suspended sediment load declining three-fold between the 1970s and 2000s. High alkalinity from coal-fired power stations and natural basalt weathering further degrades water quality.

## Terms
- **Distributaries**: Channels that branch off from a main river and flow independently to the sea.
- **Catchment area**: The area of land where all water drains into a common outlet, like a river.
- **Cusecs**: Cubic feet per second, a unit of flow rate.
- **Tributary**: A river or stream that flows into a larger river.
- **Delta**: A landform created by sediment deposition at the mouth of a river.
- **Barrage**: A type of dam with gates that can be opened or closed to control water flow.
- **Jyotirlinga**: A sacred shrine of the Hindu god Shiva.
- **Pushkaram**: A Hindu festival dedicated to the worship of rivers, held every 12 years.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The **Babli project** in Maharashtra is controversial, with concerns about its impact on downstream water availability in Telangana.
- The **Polavaram Project** is described as gigantic and politically driven, raising questions about environmental clearance, displacement, dam design safety, and loss of forest cover.
- The **unutilized water** flowing to the sea (averaging 2490 tmcft annually) presents a challenge and opportunity; a freshwater coastal reservoir is proposed as a feasible solution, but new inland reservoirs are limited by land submergence and population displacement.

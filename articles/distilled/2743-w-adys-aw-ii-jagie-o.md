# Władysław II Jagiełło

## Overview
Władysław II Jagiełło (c. 1352/1362–1434) was the Grand Duke of Lithuania and the first King of Poland from the Jagiellonian dynasty. His reign was pivotal in forging the Polish–Lithuanian union, a major European power, and in the Christianization of Lithuania. His victory at the Battle of Grunwald (1410) against the Teutonic Order secured the alliance's dominance in the region.

## Timeline
- **1377** — Becomes Grand Duke of Lithuania after his father Algirdas's death.
- **1382** — Defeats his uncle Kęstutis in a civil war, securing sole rule in Lithuania.
- **14 Aug 1385** — Confirms the Union of Krewo, promising to convert to Catholicism and marry Queen Jadwiga of Poland.
- **15 Feb 1386** — Baptized as a Catholic in Kraków; crowned King of Poland on 4 March.
- **1387** — Converts Lithuania to Catholicism.
- **1392** — Treaty of Ostrów makes his cousin Vytautas Grand Duke of Lithuania under his overlordship.
- **1399** — Queen Jadwiga dies, leaving him as sole King of Poland.
- **1401** — Union of Vilnius and Radom confirms Vytautas as Grand Duke and solidifies the Polish-Lithuanian alliance.
- **15 Jul 1410** — Allied Polish-Lithuanian forces win the decisive Battle of Grunwald against the Teutonic Order.
- **1411** — Peace of Thorn ends the war; Lithuania regains Samogitia.
- **1413** — Union of Horodło permanently ties Lithuania to Poland and grants Lithuanian Catholic nobles equal status with Polish *szlachta*.
- **1422** — Treaty of Melno ends the Teutonic wars with Lithuania, defining a border that lasted for centuries.
- **1 Jun 1434** — Jagiełło dies; his sons succeed him in Lithuania and Poland.

## Body

### Early Life and Accession
Little is known of Jogaila's early life; his birth year is uncertain (c. 1352 or 1362). He was the son of Algirdas, Grand Duke of Lithuania, and succeeded him in 1377. The Grand Duchy was a complex entity of ethnic Lithuania and vast Ruthenian territories. His early reign was marked by challenges from his brothers and a civil war with his uncle Kęstutis, whom he defeated and imprisoned in 1382. He also faced constant incursions from the Teutonic Knights, a crusading order fighting pagan Baltic tribes.

### Conversion and the Polish-Lithuanian Union
To end the Teutonic crusades and secure an alliance, Jogaila accepted a Polish proposal to convert to Catholicism and marry the child-queen Jadwiga. The Union of Krewo (1385) outlined these terms. His baptism and coronation as King Władysław II Jagiełło in 1386 triggered the Christianization of Lithuania. He co-ruled Poland with Jadwiga until her death in 1399, after which he was sole king. His rule in Lithuania was initially challenged by his cousin Vytautas, but the Treaty of Ostrów (1392) established Vytautas as Grand Duke under Władysław's overlordship, creating a durable, if complex, partnership.

### Conflict with the Teutonic Order
The alliance with Lithuania made the Teutonic Knights Władysław's primary adversary. After years of intermittent war, a major conflict erupted in 1409. On 15 July 1410, their combined forces won the overwhelming victory at the Battle of Grunwald, annihilating the Order's army and killing its Grand Master. Despite this, Władysław hesitated to capture the Order's capital, Marienburg, and the subsequent Peace of Thorn (1411) was seen by Polish nobles as a failure to fully exploit the victory. This dissatisfaction fueled internal opposition.

### Consolidation and Later Reign
Władysław worked to solidify the union through agreements like the Union of Horodło (1413), which granted Lithuanian Catholic nobles privileges equal to the Polish *szlachta* (nobility). He fought further wars against the Teutonic Order, culminating in the Treaty of Melno (1422), which permanently settled Lithuania's borders and ended the Order's claims to Samogitia. His reign saw the rise of the Jagiellonian dynasty, which ruled both states until 1572. He married four times, securing heirs who succeeded him: his sons Władysław III and Casimir IV became kings of Poland.

## Terms
- **Grand Duchy of Lithuania**: A vast medieval state, at the time of Jogaila's rule comprising ethnic Lithuania and large Ruthenian (modern Ukrainian, Belarusian) territories.
- **Teutonic Order**: A German crusading military order founded to convert and fight pagan Baltic tribes; a major rival to Poland-Lithuania.
- **Union of Krewo (1385)**: The agreement where Jogaila promised to convert to Catholicism, marry Jadwiga, and unite Lithuania with Poland.
- **Szlachta**: The privileged noble class of Poland, whose support was crucial for the king.
- **Boyars**: The nobility of the Grand Duchy of Lithuania, whose status was elevated through unions with Poland.
- **Samogitia**: A region in western Lithuania, a persistent point of conflict with the Teutonic Order until secured by Lithuania in 1422.
- **Gediminid dynasty**: The ruling dynasty of Lithuania to which Jogaila belonged; it became the Jagiellonian dynasty in Poland.
- **Peace of Thorn (1411)**: The treaty ending the Great War after Grunwald; it restored Samogitia to Lithuania but left most Teutonic territory intact.

## Debates and Open questions
- **Interpretation of the Union of Krewo**: Historians debate whether the clause *terras suas Lithuaniae et Russiae Coronae Regni Poloniae perpetuo applicare* meant a personal union, a dynastic union, or a complete incorporation of Lithuania into Poland.
- **Reasons for the failed siege of Marienburg (1410)**: The sources do not explain why Władysław II hesitated after Grunwald. Theories include the fortress's impregnability, high Lithuanian casualties, a desire to keep the Order as a weakened buffer, or a lack of resources.
- **Assessment of the Peace of Thorn (1411)**: It is debated whether the treaty was a strategic failure for Poland, as it did not translate the military victory into significant territorial gains, frustrating the Polish nobility.

# Erbil

## Overview
Erbil (also called Hawler) is the capital of the Kurdistan Region of Iraq and one of the oldest continuously inhabited cities in the world, with settlement dating back to the 5th millennium BC. Its ancient Citadel is a UNESCO World Heritage Site, and the city has served as a major cultural, economic, and political hub for millennia, passing through the control of numerous empires and civilizations.

## Timeline
- **c. 5th millennium BC** — Earliest evidence of human settlement at the Erbil Citadel.
- **c. 3000 BC** — Region under Sumerian domination; city known as Urbilum.
- **2150 BC** — Captured by Erridupizir, king of Gutium.
- **331 BC** — Battle of Gaugamela fought ~100 km west; Darius III flees to Erbil.
- **Mid-2nd century BC – early 2nd century AD** — Capital of the tributary state of Adiabene.
- **1258** — Mongols capture the citadel after a siege.
- **1310** — Ilkhanate troops and Kurdish tribesmen capture the citadel, massacring defenders.
- **1397** — City destroyed by the forces of Timur.
- **1851** — Erbil taken by the Ottoman Empire.
- **1970** — Parliament of Iraqi Kurdistan established in Erbil.
- **2003** — Celebrations after the fall of the Ba'ath regime.
- **2014** — Citadel of Erbil inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

## Body

### Ancient and Medieval History
Erbil is one of the world's oldest continuously inhabited cities. Its earliest historical reference is from the Third Dynasty of Ur (c. 3000 BC). The city was an independent power in the 3rd millennium BC, was conquered by the Gutians, and later came under Assyrian control. It subsequently formed part of the Median, Achaemenid, Macedonian, Seleucid, Parthian, Roman, and Sasanian empires. From the mid-second century BC to the early 2nd century AD, it was the capital of the tributary state of Adiabene, whose royal family converted to Judaism. The population gradually converted to Christianity between the 1st and 4th centuries, and the city became a center of Eastern Syriac Christianity.

Following the Muslim conquest of Persia, the region saw an influx of Arab and Turkic peoples. Erbil was ruled successively by the Umayyads, Abbasids, Seljuks, and the Turkmen Begtegīnid Emirs (notably Gökböri). The Mongols captured the citadel in 1258, and a massacre of its Assyrian inhabitants occurred in 1310. The city was later destroyed by Timur in 1397. It was eventually incorporated into the Ottoman Empire in 1851.

### Modern History and Politics
The modern town stands on the ancient tell. The parliament of Iraqi Kurdistan was established in Erbil in 1970 but was effectively controlled by Saddam Hussein until the 1991 Kurdish uprising. The city was captured by the Kurdistan Democratic Party (KDP) in 1996 with Iraqi government assistance, leading to a split with the Patriotic Union of Kurdistan (PUK). During the 2003 invasion of Iraq, U.S. special forces were headquartered near the city. Erbil has since faced sporadic attacks, including major bombings in 2004 and 2005. The Erbil International Airport opened in 2005. The city is now the capital of the Kurdistan Region and hosts a large population of refugees and internally displaced persons (IDPs).

### Archaeology and Culture
The heart of the city is the ancient Citadel of Erbil, a tell rising 25–32 meters. It is claimed to be the oldest continuously inhabited town, with occupation from the 5th millennium BC. Major restoration began in 2007, and it was inscribed as a World Heritage Site in 2014. The Mudhafaria Minaret, dating to the late 12th century, is another key landmark. The Erbil Archaeological Museum houses a large collection of pre-Islamic Mesopotamian artifacts. The wider plain contains promising sites like Tell Baqrta, and the Erbil Plain Archaeological Survey began in 2012.

### Demographics and Economy
The city is predominantly Kurdish, with minorities of Turkmen, Assyrians, Arabs, and Chaldeans. The Ankawa district is mainly populated by Christian Assyrians. Historically agricultural, Erbil's economy now relies on tourism, oil, and gas. The Erbil 2050 Master Plan, developed with Japan's JICA, guides urban development. The Erbil Green Belt Project, launched in 2025, aims to encircle the city with vegetation to combat climate change and improve the urban environment.

## Terms
- **Tell**: An artificial mound formed by the accumulated debris of successive human settlements.
- **Satrapy**: A province or administrative region of an ancient Persian empire.
- **Adiabene**: An ancient Assyrian kingdom and later a Roman province, with Erbil as its capital.
- **Ilkhanate**: A Mongol and later Turkicized khanate established in the 13th century.
- **Begtegīnid Emirs**: A Turkmen dynasty that ruled Erbil from the 12th to 13th centuries.
- **Kurdistan Region of Iraq (KRG)**: A federal autonomous region of Iraq, with Erbil as its capital.
- **Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs)**: People forced to flee their homes but who remain within their country's borders.
- **Green Belt Project**: A large-scale urban forestry initiative to create a protective vegetative zone around a city.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The claim that Erbil is the "oldest continuously inhabited town in the world" is noted but is a subject of ongoing scholarly discussion.
- The exact nature and extent of the city's population shifts, particularly the conversion from ancient Mesopotamian religion to Christianity and later to Islam, involve complex historical processes.
- The demographic impact and integration of large numbers of refugees and IDPs in the modern city present ongoing social and economic challenges.

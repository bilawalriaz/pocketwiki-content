# Bhubaneswar

## Overview
Bhubaneswar is the capital and largest city of the Indian state of Odisha, known as the "Temple City" due to its numerous historical temples. It is a major hub for sports, tourism, and information technology in eastern India, forming part of the "Golden Triangle" tourist circuit with Puri and Konark. The modern city was planned in 1948, but the area's history dates back to the 1st century BCE.

## Timeline
- **c. 262-261 BCE** — The Kalinga War occurs near Dhauli, leading to the Mauryan annexation of Kalinga.
- **1st century BCE** — The area's history begins, with the Hathigumpha inscription of King Kharavela carved at Udayagiri and Khandagiri caves.
- **7th century CE** — The Somavamshi or Keshari dynasty establishes their kingdom and constructs temples.
- **8th–12th centuries CE** — Most of the older temples in Bhubaneswar are built under Shaiva influence.
- **1568** — The Afghan-origin Karrani dynasty gains control of Odisha, destroying many temples.
- **13 April 1948** — The foundation of the new capital is laid, replacing Cuttack.
- **1949** — The Legislative Assembly of Odisha is shifted from Cuttack to Bhubaneswar.
- **12 March 1979** — A municipality is established for Bhubaneswar.
- **14 August 1994** — The Bhubaneswar Municipal Corporation is established.
- **2018** — Bhubaneswar hosts the Odisha Hockey World Cup.
- **2023** — Bhubaneswar co-hosts the FIH Hockey World Cup, becoming the first city to host two consecutive tournaments.

## Body

### History and Etymology
The city's name derives from "Tribhubaneshwara," meaning "Lord of the Three Worlds," referring to Shiva. The area's ancient history includes the Kalinga War (c. 262-261 BCE) and rule by dynasties like the Mahameghavahanas, Somavamshis, and Eastern Gangas. Many temples were built between the 8th and 12th centuries. Later control passed to the Karrani dynasty (1568), the Mughals, and the Marathas, before British colonial rule began in 1803. The modern city was designed by German architect Otto Königsberger in 1946 and formally established as the capital in 1948.

### Urban Structure and Geography
Bhubaneswar is located in the Khordha district on the eastern coastal plains. The city is divided into the old "Temple Town," the planned capital area (with units for government and residential use), and newer added areas. The Bhubaneswar Municipal Corporation area covers 186 km². The city has a tropical savanna climate with temperatures ranging from 11 to 44 °C. It receives most of its annual rainfall of 1,657.8 mm from the southwest monsoon between June and September.

### Economy
Bhubaneswar is an administrative, IT, education, and tourism center. The economy shifted from retail and small-scale manufacturing to IT and services after the 1990s economic liberalization. It is a major IT hub with parks like Infocity and hosts companies such as Infosys and Tech Mahindra. Traditional handicrafts, including silver filigree and stone carving, also contribute significantly. As of 2001, 95.67% of the workforce was employed in the tertiary (service) sector.

### Demographics and Governance
As per the 2011 census, Bhubaneswar had a population of 837,737, with a literacy rate of 93.15%. The primary language is Odia. Hindus form the majority (over 95%). The city is governed by the Bhubaneswar Municipal Corporation (BMC), established in 1994. It is the seat of the Odisha state government and home to the Odisha Legislative Assembly.

### Culture and Sports
Bhubaneswar is a center for Odia culture, including classical Odissi dance and music. Key festivals include the Rukuna Ratha procession for Lingaraja and the Durga Puja. The Kalinga Stadium is a major sports venue, hosting international hockey and athletics events. The city is home to the Kalinga Lancers (hockey) and Odisha FC (football). It is emerging as a "sports capital of India."

### Education and Transport
The city is an education hub with institutions like IIT Bhubaneswar, AIIMS Bhubaneswar, and Utkal University. Transport includes the Biju Patnaik International Airport, Bhubaneswar railway station (headquarters of East Coast Railway), and the Mo Bus public transport system. National Highway 16 connects it to other major cities.

## Terms
- **Kalinga architectural style:** A temple style featuring a curving spire (shikhara) over the sanctum and a pyramidal hall.
- **Tribhubaneshwara:** Meaning "Lord of the Three Worlds," a name for Shiva from which Bhubaneswar derives its name.
- **Swarna Tribhuja:** The "Golden Triangle" tourist circuit of Bhubaneswar, Puri, and Konark.
- **Mo Bus:** The public bus service in Bhubaneswar operated by Capital Region Urban Transport (CRUT).
- **Mo Cycle:** A public bicycle-sharing scheme in the city.
- **Kalinga War:** A major conflict (c. 262-261 BCE) between the Mauryan emperor Ashoka and the Kalinga kingdom.
- **Hathigumpha inscription:** An inscription of King Kharavela from the 1st century BCE, located at the Udayagiri and Khandagiri caves.
- **Odissi:** One of the eight classical dance forms of India, with roots in Bhubaneswar's temples.
- **Bhubaneswar Municipal Corporation (BMC):** The civic body governing the city, established in 1994.

## Debates and open questions
- The source notes that the city's rapid growth has outstripped its original planning process, leading to issues like unplanned development, floods, and waterlogging in low-lying areas.
- The demographic changes from IT and education sector growth have caused concerns about infrastructure strain.

# Eddy Merckx

## Overview
Eddy Merckx is a Belgian former professional cyclist, widely regarded as the most successful rider in history. His unparalleled dominance across all road racing forms—from one-day classics to three-week Grand Tours—defined an era and set enduring records. His career was a study in relentless attacking and comprehensive victory, though marked by controversies and a dramatic crash that altered his physical condition.

## Body

### Early Life and Amateur Career
Born in 1945, Merckx grew up in Woluwe-Saint-Pierre. He focused on cycling from a young age, imitating idol Stan Ockers. He began racing at sixteen, winning his first race in October 1961. His results improved rapidly, leading him to drop out of school to focus on cycling. His amateur career culminated in a victory at the 1964 UCI Road World Championships amateur road race; he finished twelfth at the Tokyo Olympics later that year. He turned professional in April 1965 after winning 80 amateur races.

### Professional Career: Rise to Dominance (1965–1970)
Merckx turned professional with Solo–Superia but left after one season due to poor treatment from teammates. With Peugeot–BP–Michelin, he won his first Monument, Milan–San Remo, in 1966. In 1967, he won his second Milan–San Remo and his first professional World Championship. He then signed with Faema, winning his first Giro d'Italia in 1968. In 1969, he achieved a historic season, winning the Tour of Flanders, Milan–San Remo, Liège–Bastogne–Liège, and the Tour de France, where he took all four main classifications. His season was interrupted by a severe crash at a track event in Blois, which fractured his pelvis and caused lasting pain. He recovered to win the Giro and Tour in 1970, the first of his three Giro–Tour doubles.

### Professional Career: Peak and Later Years (1971–1978)
After Faema folded, Merckx joined Molteni. In 1971, he won his third consecutive Tour de France and a second World Championship, completing the set of all five Monuments by winning the Giro di Lombardia. In 1972, he won the Giro and Tour again and broke the hour record in Mexico City. In 1973, he skipped the Tour to win the Vuelta a España and a fifth Giro d'Italia. In 1974, he won the Giro and Tour for a fifth time each and a third World Championship, becoming the first rider to complete the Triple Crown. From 1975, his form declined; he finished second at the Tour de France in 1975 and did not win a stage at the 1976 Giro. After two less successful seasons with Fiat France and C&A, he retired in May 1978, citing medical advice.

### Post-Racing Career and Legacy
Merckx founded the Eddy Merckx Cycles bicycle brand in 1980. He managed the Belgian national cycling team for eleven years (1986–1996) and was instrumental in creating the Tour of Qatar (2002–2016). He is universally regarded as the greatest cyclist of all time, holding the records for most Grand Tour victories (11), most Monument victories (19), and most professional road race wins (525). His aggressive, attacking style earned him the nickname "The Cannibal." His legacy is complicated by three doping positives during his career, though he consistently maintained his innocence or blamed medical staff.

## Terms
- **Monuments:** The five most prestigious one-day classic races in cycling: Milan–San Remo, Tour of Flanders, Paris–Roubaix, Liège–Bastogne–Liège, and Giro di Lombardia.
- **Grand Tours:** The three major multi-stage races: the Tour de France, Giro d'Italia, and Vuelta a España.
- **Triple Crown of Cycling:** Winning the Tour de France, Giro d'Italia, and the men's road race at the UCI World Championships in a single calendar year.
- **UCI Hour Record:** The distance a cyclist can ride in one hour on a velodrome, a benchmark of endurance and speed.
- **Giro–Tour Double:** Winning both the Giro d'Italia and the Tour de France in the same year.
- **La course en tête:** French for "the race in the lead," describing Merckx's signature aggressive, attacking racing style.
- **Domestique:** A rider who works for the benefit of their team leader, sacrificing their own chances for victory.
- **Kermis:** A type of local, often criterium-style race common in Belgium.
- **Derny:** A small motorized vehicle used to pace cyclists in track events.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Greatest of All Time:** While Merckx is almost universally regarded as the greatest cyclist, he himself believes cross-generational comparisons are impractical due to the increased specialization of modern riders.
- **Doping Controversies:** Merckx's career was marred by three positive tests (1969 Giro, 1973 Giro di Lombardia, 1977 Flèche Wallonne). He claimed innocence in the first case, blamed a prescribed cough medicine in the second, and in the third admitted he was wrong to trust a doctor. These incidents remain a point of historical discussion.
- **Impact of the Blois Crash:** Merckx stated he was "never the same again" after the 1969 crash that fractured his pelvis, which required constant seat adjustments and likely contributed to later physical decline.

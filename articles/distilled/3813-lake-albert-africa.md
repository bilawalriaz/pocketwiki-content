# Lake Albert (Africa)

## Overview
Lake Albert is a large freshwater lake on the Uganda–Democratic Republic of the Congo border, part of the Albertine Rift. It is a critical component of the upper Nile system and supports significant biodiversity and fisheries, though it has fewer endemic species than other African Great Lakes due to its hydrological connections.

## Timeline
- **Pre-colonial** — Known as Mwitanzige ("locust killer") by the Banyoro and Nam Ovoyo Bonyo by the Alur.
- **1864** — Explorers Samuel Baker and Flóra von Sass find the lake and rename it after Prince Albert.
- **20th century** — Zairian President Mobutu Sese Seko temporarily renames the lake after himself.
- **1930** — The cargo/passenger ship SS *Robert Coryndon* is built for colonial shipping services.
- **1962 or 1964** — The SS *Robert Coryndon* is scuttled or sinks at Butyaba landing site.
- **21st century** — Major oil finds are announced in the Lake Albert basin by Heritage Oil and Tullow Oil.
- **March 2014** — A boat carrying Congolese refugees capsizes, killing over 250 people.
- **26 December 2016** — A boat carrying a football team and fans capsizes, killing at least 30 people.
- **24 December 2020** — A boat capsizes while travelling to avoid COVID-19 restrictions, killing 30 people.

## Body

### Geography and Hydrology
Lake Albert is the northernmost lake in the Albertine Rift, the western branch of the East African Rift. It is about 160 km long and 30 km wide, with a maximum depth of 51 metres. It is part of the upper Nile system; its main inflows are the White Nile from Lake Victoria and the Semliki River from Lake Edward. Its outlet is the Albert Nile, which later becomes the Mountain Nile in South Sudan. The lake's water is relatively stable in temperature (27–29 °C) and oxygenated even at depth, with a high pH and conductivity.

### Ecology and Fisheries
The lake hosts hippos, crocodiles, and numerous water birds, including the rare shoebill. It contains 55 fish species. The native Nile perch is the largest predator. Unlike other Rift lakes, Lake Albert has few endemic fish species because its connections to the main Nile (via the Albert Nile) and isolation from Lake Edward (by Semliki rapids) and Lake Victoria (by Murchison Falls) allow widespread riverine species to dominate. The only endemic fish are four haplochromine cichlids, the small cyprinid *Engraulicypris bredoi*, and the endangered *Albert lates*. The lake provides up to 30% of Uganda's fish production.

### History and Human Activity
Local names like Mwitanzige ("locust killer") derive from legends of a locust plague. European explorers renamed it in 1864. Colonial powers developed shipping, exemplified by the SS *Robert Coryndon*. The 21st century has seen major oil discoveries in the basin and several fatal boat capsizing disasters. The Kibiro settlement on the lake has cultural and archaeological significance.

## Terms
- **Albertine Rift**: The western branch of the East African Rift system, containing a chain of large lakes.
- **Endemic**: A species found only in a specific, isolated geographic location.
- **Haplochromine cichlids**: A diverse group of fish common in African Rift lakes, with many endemic species.
- **Saline**: Containing salt; Lake Albert's water is less saline than the Victoria Nile's.
- **Surface elevation**: The height of the lake's surface above sea level (619 m for Lake Albert).
- **Upper Nile**: The section of the Nile River system that includes Lake Albert and its inflows/outflows.

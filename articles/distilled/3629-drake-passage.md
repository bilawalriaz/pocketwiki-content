# Drake Passage

## Overview
The Drake Passage is the body of water between Cape Horn, Chile, and the South Shetland Islands, Antarctica, connecting the Atlantic and Pacific Oceans. It is the narrowest choke point around Antarctica and is considered one of the world's most treacherous voyages due to the powerful Antarctic Circumpolar Current (ACC) and massive waves. Its opening, driven by plate tectonics, fundamentally altered global ocean circulation and climate by isolating Antarctica with a continuous ocean current.

## Timeline
- **c. 49–17 million years ago** — Drake Passage opens as Antarctica separates from South America due to plate tectonics.
- **1525** — Spanish navigator Francisco de Hoces is assumed to have discovered the passage after being blown south from the Strait of Magellan.
- **1578** — Sir Francis Drake, during his circumnavigation, is blown south into the passage, demonstrating open water south of South America.
- **1616** — Dutch navigator Willem Schouten becomes the first to sail around Cape Horn and through the Drake Passage.
- **December 25, 2019** — A crew of six explorers becomes the first to successfully row across the Drake Passage.

## Body

### Geography and Discovery
The Drake Passage is the 800-kilometre-wide (500 mi) body of water separating South America from Antarctica. It is named after Sir Francis Drake, whose 1578 voyage proved the existence of open water south of the continent. Spanish sources often call it the Mar de Hoces, after Francisco de Hoces, who may have seen it in 1525. The passage is the shortest crossing from Antarctica to another landmass and is preferred by ships over the narrower, more unpredictable Strait of Magellan and Beagle Channel.

### Oceanographic and Climate Importance
The opening of the Drake Passage allowed the formation of the Antarctic Circumpolar Current (ACC), the world's strongest oceanic current, with an estimated transport of 100–150 Sverdrups (million m³/s). This current connects the Atlantic, Pacific, and Southern Oceans and is the only large-scale exchange between them. The ACC's unimpeded flow, driven by the Roaring Forties and Furious Fifties winds, isolates Antarctica from warmer waters, a process credited with causing its glaciation and global cooling in the Eocene epoch.

The passage's bathymetry (underwater topography) is critical for global climate. Models show that an open Drake Passage is necessary for the existence of the global thermohaline circulation "conveyor belt" and the Atlantic Meridional Overturning Circulation (AMOC). It also influences global surface temperature, cooling the Southern Ocean while warming the high latitudes of the Northern Hemisphere.

### Turbulence and Mixing
The coarse topography of the Drake Passage generates significant diapycnal mixing—the process where different layers of ocean water mix vertically. This mixing is driven by the breaking of internal waves (lee waves) as currents flow over underwater obstacles. The mixing in the Drake Passage is estimated to be about 20 times stronger than in the adjacent Pacific sector of the ACC. This extra mixing is vital for supporting the global thermohaline circulation, as it helps drive the density-driven movement of ocean water.

### Historical and Modern Observation
The Drake Passage's constrained geography makes it a key location for oceanographic study. Before satellite data, scientists measured the ACC by making repeated ship transects across the passage. Its clear boundaries and strong current make it ideal for validating theories of ocean circulation, such as Ekman transport, and for observing features like cold-core rings. Direct observations remain valuable even with modern satellite altimetry.

## Terms
- **Antarctic Circumpolar Current (ACC):** The strongest oceanic current, flowing eastward around Antarctica and connecting the Atlantic, Pacific, and Southern Oceans.
- **Bathymetry:** The measurement of depth and topography of ocean floors.
- **Choke Point:** A narrow passage that significantly influences the flow of traffic or currents.
- **Diapycnal Mixing:** The process by which different density layers in a stratified fluid, like the ocean, mix vertically.
- **Ekman Transport:** The net motion of fluid as the result of a balance between Coriolis and turbulent drag forces, causing water to move at an angle to the wind direction.
- **Sverdrup (Sv):** A unit of flow used in oceanography, equal to one million cubic meters per second.
- **Thermohaline Circulation:** The global density-driven circulation of the oceans, often called the "conveyor belt."

## Debates and Open Questions
- The exact timing of the Drake Passage's opening is debated, with estimates ranging from 49 to 17 million years ago.
- The precise role of the passage's opening in causing Antarctic glaciation and global climate change is a subject of ongoing research and modeling.
- The relative importance of the Drake Passage's bathymetry versus wind forcing in driving the ACC and global circulation is studied through various models.

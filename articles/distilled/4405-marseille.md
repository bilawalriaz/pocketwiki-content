# Marseille

## Overview
Marseille is France's second-largest city and its oldest, founded by Greek settlers around 600 BC. As a major Mediterranean port, it has been a historic gateway for trade and immigration, shaping a uniquely cosmopolitan and multicultural identity. Today, it is a significant economic, cultural, and sporting hub for southern France.

## Timeline

- **49 BC** — Lost independence to Rome after siding against Julius Caesar in his civil war.
- **1720** — Devastated by the Great Plague, losing a significant portion of its population.
- **1792** — Volunteers from Marseille sing "La Marseillaise" in Paris, giving the anthem its name.
- **19th C.** — Experienced a commercial and industrial boom during the colonial era.
- **1943** — Heavily damaged by Nazi destruction during World War II.
- **1990s** — Launch of the Euroméditerranée urban renewal project.
- **2013** — Named European Capital of Culture.
- **2016** — Formation of the Aix-Marseille-Provence Metropolis.
- **2023** — Described as the city's "deadliest year" due to a rise in drug-related violence.

## Body

### History and Identity
Marseille is the oldest city in France, founded c. 600 BC as Massalia. It maintained its independence as a Greek trading port until losing it to Rome in 49 BC. The city remained a premier maritime hub through the Roman, Visigothic, and medieval periods, though it suffered from events like the Black Death and a 1423 sack. Its modern identity was forged by waves of immigration, particularly from Italy, Armenia, North Africa, and former French colonies, making it a "cosmopolitan melting pot." This multiculturalism is a defining feature, though it coexists with significant socioeconomic segregation.

### Economy and Port
Marseille's economy is historically and currently dominated by its port, the first in France and fifth in Europe by cargo tonnage. The port handles 100 million tons of freight annually, with petroleum accounting for 60%. While the Old Port is now a marina and tourist site, the modern Marseille-Fos Port is a major industrial and logistics center. The city has transitioned from heavy industry to a service and high-tech economy, with major companies like CMA CGM and Airbus Helicopters. The Euroméditerranée project has expanded its business districts.

### Culture and Society
Marseille has a distinct cultural identity, celebrated as the European Capital of Culture in 2013. It is known for its unique dialect, cuisine (notably bouillabaisse), and as the birthplace of the Tarot de Marseille deck. The city is a major center for hip-hop music in France and hosts numerous festivals. Its multicultural tolerance is noted, yet challenges persist, including high unemployment and, since the early 2020s, a severe rise in organized crime and drug-related violence. The city is also a major sporting hub, home to the Olympique de Marseille football club.

### Geography and Landmarks
Situated on the Mediterranean coast, Marseille is surrounded by hills and features the Calanques, a rugged coastal area now within a national park. Key landmarks include the Old Port (Vieux-Port), the Romano-Byzantine basilica of Notre-Dame de la Garde, and the MuCEM museum. The city's architecture ranges from ancient sites to modernist buildings like Le Corbusier's Unité d'Habitation. It is served by Marseille Provence Airport and a major railway station with TGV connections.

## Terms
- **Massalia**: The ancient Greek name for Marseille, founded c. 600 BC.
- **Calanques**: A rugged coastal area of fjord-like inlets east of Marseille, part of a national park.
- **Euroméditerranée**: A major economic development and urban renewal project launched in the 1990s.
- **Bouillabaisse**: A traditional Provençal fish stew from Marseille, containing specific local fish and seafood.
- **Tarot de Marseille**: A traditional tarot deck pattern that takes its name from the city.
- **Métro**: Marseille's underground train system, consisting of two lines.
- **Pétanque**: A popular boules game in which Marseille is considered a world capital.
- **Marseillais**: The demonym for a resident of Marseille.

## Debates and open questions
The source highlights ongoing debates about Marseille's social fabric. A key question is how to address the deep economic and geographic segregation between the poorer, majority-Muslim north and the wealthier south. Furthermore, the city's struggle with rising organized crime and drug trafficking since the early 2020s presents a major challenge to its safety and governance, with officials comparing the threat to terrorism.

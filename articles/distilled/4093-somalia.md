# Somalia

## Overview
Somalia, officially the Federal Republic of Somalia, is the easternmost country in continental Africa, located in the Horn of Africa. It is known for its long coastline, historically significant role in regional trade, and a complex modern history marked by colonialism, state collapse, and ongoing efforts at reconstruction. The country is ethnically homogeneous, with about 85% of its population being ethnic Somalis, and is overwhelmingly Sunni Muslim.

## Timeline
- **c. 3000 BC** — Camel domestication believed to have occurred in the Horn region.
- **2nd millennium BC** — Trade with ancient Egypt and Mycenaean Greece; possible location of the Land of Punt.
- **7th century CE** — Islam introduced to the region.
- **13th–16th centuries** — Powerful Somali empires like the Ajuran Sultanate dominate regional trade.
- **1884–1889** — British and Italian protectorates established, forming British and Italian Somaliland.
- **26 June & 1 July 1960** — British and Italian Somaliland unite to form the independent Somali Republic.
- **21 October 1969** — Siad Barre seizes power in a coup, establishing the Somali Democratic Republic.
- **1977–1978** — Ogaden War with Ethiopia ends in Somali defeat, shifting Cold War alliances.
- **1991** — Collapse of the Barre regime and onset of the Somali Civil War; Somaliland declares independence.
- **2006** — Islamic Courts Union (ICU) briefly controls much of southern Somalia.
- **August 2012** — Provisional Constitution adopted, establishing the Federal Government of Somalia.
- **2022–2025** — Federal government launches major offensive against al-Shabaab, with gains later reversed.

## Body

### Ancient and Medieval History
Somalia was a key commercial centre in antiquity, trading with ancient Egypt, Greece, and Rome. The region is a candidate for the ancient Land of Punt. Classical-era city-states like Opone and Malao developed lucrative trade networks. Islam arrived early, and by the Middle Ages, powerful Somali sultanates, including the Ajuran and Adal, dominated regional trade and warfare, with the Adal Sultanate launching a major 16th-century campaign against the Abyssinian Empire.

### Colonialism and Independence
In the late 19th century, European powers colonized the region, creating British Somaliland and Italian Somaliland. After World War II, Italy administered its territory under a UN trusteeship with a mandate to prepare for independence. The two territories united in 1960 to form the Somali Republic. A military coup in 1969 brought Siad Barre to power, whose regime pursued scientific socialism and a failed invasion of Ethiopia in 1977, which triggered major instability.

### Civil War and State Collapse
The Barre regime collapsed in 1991, leading to a protracted civil war and the emergence of a "failed state." Clan-based factions fought for control, particularly in the south. The northwestern region declared independence as Somaliland in 1991, remaining de facto independent but unrecognized internationally. Transitional governments, including the Transitional Federal Government (TFG), struggled to establish authority with international support.

### Insurgency and Reconstruction
In 2006, the Islamic Courts Union (ICU) briefly brought stability to the south before being ousted by a US-backed Ethiopian invasion. This led to an insurgency by the militant group al-Shabaab. A new provisional constitution in 2012 established the Federal Government of Somalia, marking the first permanent central government since 1991. The government, supported by African Union forces, has fought al-Shabaab, which still controls parts of central and southern Somalia. The country faces ongoing political disputes between federal and state authorities.

### Economy and Society
Somalia has one of the world's lowest GDPs per capita, with an economy based on livestock, remittances from the diaspora, and a resilient telecommunications sector. The population is young, with a high fertility rate. The official language is Somali, and Arabic is widely used. The country is a member of the UN, African Union, and Arab League.

## Terms
- **Horn of Africa:** A peninsula in East Africa containing Somalia, Ethiopia, Eritrea, and Djibouti.
- **Clan:** A dominant social and political unit in Somali society, based on patrilineal kinship.
- **Al-Shabaab:** A militant Islamist group and insurgent faction in Somalia, opposing the federal government.
- **Federal Member State:** One of Somalia's semi-autonomous regional states, such as Puntland or Galmudug.
- **Hawala:** An informal value transfer system, or money transfer operator, crucial to Somalia's economy.
- **Provisional Constitution:** The 2012 document that established the framework for the Federal Republic of Somalia.
- **Transitional Federal Government (TFG):** The internationally recognized interim government of Somalia from 2004 to 2012.
- **Xeer:** A system of Somali customary law used for dispute resolution and governance.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The political status of Somaliland, which declared independence in 1991 and is self-governing but lacks international recognition.
- The effectiveness and future of the federal system, with ongoing disputes over power-sharing and resource allocation between the federal government and member states.
- The long-term viability of the state-building project amid persistent insecurity from al-Shabaab and clan-based political competition.
- The accurate measurement of Somalia's economy and population due to decades of conflict and limited formal governance.

# Gulf of Aden

## Overview
The Gulf of Aden is a deep-water inlet of the Indian Ocean situated between the Arabian Peninsula and the Horn of Africa. It is a globally significant maritime corridor, forming a critical link in the Suez Canal shipping route that connects the Mediterranean Sea to the Arabian Sea, making it vital for international trade, particularly the transport of Persian Gulf oil.

## Timeline
- **Antiquity** — Ancient Greeks viewed the gulf as part of the "Erythraean Sea."
- **1st century** — Documented in the *Periplus of the Erythraean Sea* as a trade route.
- **Medieval period** — Known as the Gulf of Berbera, highlighting Berbera's trade importance.
- **15th century** — Navigator Ibn Majid referred to it as the Gulf of Berbera.
- **Late 1960s** — Increased Soviet naval presence in the gulf.
- **1975** — Suez Canal reopened, revitalizing the gulf's importance.
- **First decade of 2000s** — Evolved into a hub of pirate activity, especially during the Somali war.
- **By 2013** — Pirate attacks declined due to private security and international naval patrols.

## Body
### Geography and Formation
The Gulf of Aden is bounded by Yemen to the north, the Arabian Sea to the east, Djibouti to the west, and Somalia/Somaliland to the south. It connects to the Red Sea via the Bab-el-Mandeb strait and narrows into the Gulf of Tadjoura in Djibouti. The Aden Ridge runs along its center, and tectonic activity there causes the gulf to widen by approximately 15 mm per year. Water temperatures range from 15 to 28 °C, and salinity varies from 35.3‰ to 37.3‰.

### Historical and Economic Significance
In antiquity, the gulf was a major trade route between the Roman Empire and regions like India and China. After the Roman economy's collapse, regional ports like Crater (near modern Aden) remained important. The rise of Islam facilitated migrations across the gulf. Its modern economic importance stems from the Suez Canal route; about 21,000 ships cross annually, carrying roughly 11% of the world's seaborne petroleum. Major ports include Aden (Yemen), Djibouti City, Berbera (Somaliland), and Bosaso (Somalia). India, for example, conducts $110 billion in annual trade through these waters.

### Ecology and Environment
The gulf is a geologically young body of water with unique biodiversity, including fish, coral, and seabirds. Historically, it supported populations of whales and dugongs, but commercial hunts in the 1960s and 1970s severely reduced their numbers. The critically endangered Arabian humpback whale was once common, but now only a few large whale species, like Bryde's and sperm whales, are occasionally seen. Environmental groups warn that a lack of coordinated pollution control threatens this ecosystem.

## Terms
- **Exclusive Economic Zone (EEZ):** A maritime zone where a state has special rights regarding exploration and use of marine resources.
- **Hydrography:** The science of surveying and charting bodies of water.
- **Tectonic activity:** The movement of the Earth's crustal plates, causing features like ridges and earthquakes.
- **Periplus of the Erythraean Sea:** A 1st-century Greco-Roman manuscript describing navigation and trade routes.
- **Suez Canal:** An artificial waterway in Egypt connecting the Mediterranean and Red Seas, crucial for global shipping.
- **Bab-el-Mandeb:** The strait connecting the Gulf of Aden to the Red Sea.
- **Aden Ridge:** An underwater tectonic ridge causing the gulf to widen.
- **Erythraean Sea:** An ancient Greek term for the Red Sea and adjacent waters, including the Gulf of Aden.

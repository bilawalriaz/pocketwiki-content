# Tim Berners-Lee

## Overview
Sir Timothy John Berners-Lee (born 1955) is an English computer scientist who invented the World Wide Web, including the core technologies HTML, URL, and HTTP. His creation, proposed in 1989, fundamentally transformed global communication and information sharing, becoming the fastest-growing communications medium in history. He continues to advocate for an open, decentralized web and works on projects to give users greater control over their personal data.

## Timeline
- **8 June 1955** — Born in London to mathematician parents.
- **1976** — Graduates with a first in physics from The Queen's College, Oxford.
- **1980** — Proposes a hypertext project at CERN and builds the ENQUIRE prototype.
- **12 March 1989** — Writes his proposal for an information management system at CERN.
- **Late 1990** — Builds the first web browser (WorldWideWeb) and web server (CERN httpd).
- **20 December 1990** — Publishes the first website, describing the World Wide Web project.
- **6 August 1991** — Posts a public invitation for collaboration on the WorldWideWeb project to Usenet.
- **1994** — Founds the World Wide Web Consortium (W3C) at MIT.
- **2004** — Knighted by Queen Elizabeth II for services to the global development of the Internet.
- **2016** — Receives the Turing Award for inventing the World Wide Web and its fundamental protocols.
- **2017** — Approves the Encrypted Media Extensions (EME) specification as W3C director.
- **2018** — Announces the Inrupt startup to build a commercial ecosystem around the Solid project.

## Body

### Invention of the World Wide Web
Working at CERN in 1989, Berners-Lee saw an opportunity to combine existing hypertext concepts with the Internet's TCP and DNS protocols. His 1989 proposal, initially called "vague, but exciting" by his manager, was developed with partner Robert Cailliau. By late 1990, Berners-Lee had built the first browser, server, and website. He made the technology freely available with no patents or royalties, a decision crucial for its rapid, universal adoption.

### Leadership and Advocacy
In 1994, Berners-Lee founded the W3C to establish open standards for the web. He has since been a leading voice for net neutrality, arguing that Internet service providers should offer "connectivity with no strings attached." He co-founded the World Wide Web Foundation and the Open Data Institute to promote data openness and affordability. His recent work focuses on the Solid project, which aims to give users true ownership and control over their personal data.

### Later Career and Honors
Berners-Lee has held prestigious academic positions at MIT and the University of Oxford. He has received numerous accolades, including a knighthood, the Order of Merit, and the 2016 Turing Award. In 2021, he auctioned the source code of the original World Wide Web as an NFT for over $5.4 million. He published a memoir, *This Is for Everyone*, in 2025.

## Terms
- **World Wide Web**: An information system where documents are identified by URLs, accessed via the Internet using HTTP, and interconnected via hypertext links.
- **HTML (Hypertext Markup Language)**: The standard markup language for creating web pages and applications.
- **URL (Uniform Resource Locator)**: The address used to access a resource on the Internet.
- **HTTP (Hypertext Transfer Protocol)**: The foundational protocol for transmitting hypermedia documents on the Web.
- **Hypertext**: Text displayed on a computer with references (hyperlinks) to other text that the reader can immediately access.
- **W3C (World Wide Web Consortium)**: An international community that develops open standards to ensure the long-term growth of the Web.
- **Net Neutrality**: The principle that Internet service providers should treat all data on the Internet equally, without discriminating or charging differently by user, content, website, or application.
- **Semantic Web**: An extension of the World Wide Web that enables machines to understand and interpret data, a concept Berners-Lee has championed.
- **Solid**: A project led by Berners-Lee aiming to create a decentralized platform for web applications that gives users control over their data.

## Debates and open questions
- **DRM and EME**: Berners-Lee's decision as W3C director to approve the Encrypted Media Extensions (EME) specification, which supports digital rights management (DRM), was controversial. Critics, including the Electronic Frontier Foundation, argued it compromised the web's open philosophy and could force users into specific browsers.
- **Web's Evolution**: Berners-Lee has expressed concern that the web is being misused by those who "exploit, divide and undermine," leading to initiatives like the Contract for the Web to establish protective principles.
- **Centralization vs. Decentralization**: His current work on Solid is a direct response to the growing centralization of data by large corporations, seeking to restore user control and privacy.

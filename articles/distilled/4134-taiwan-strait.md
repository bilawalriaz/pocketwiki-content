# Taiwan Strait

## Overview
The Taiwan Strait is a 180-kilometer-wide body of water separating the island of Taiwan from mainland Asia, connecting the South China Sea to the south and the East China Sea to the north. It is a vital shipping lane for Northeast Asia and a focal point of geopolitical tension between the People's Republic of China (PRC) and the Republic of China (ROC, Taiwan), with its status and control being a central issue in Cross-Strait relations.

## Timeline
- **c. 1661** — Zheng Chenggong (Koxinga) establishes the Kingdom of Tungning in Taiwan after expelling the Dutch.
- **1683** — The Qing Dynasty defeats Koxinga's grandson at the Battle of the Penghu Islands, consolidating control over Taiwan.
- **1895** — Japan gains control of Taiwan and the Penghu Islands after the First Sino-Japanese War.
- **1949** — The ROC government retreats across the Taiwan Strait following the Chinese Civil War.
- **1954-55** — The First Taiwan Strait Crisis occurs.
- **1955** — U.S. General Benjamin O. Davis Jr. defines a theoretical "median line" in the strait.
- **1958** — The Second Taiwan Strait Crisis occurs; Taiwanese aircraft cease frequent crossings of the median line.
- **2008** — The "Three Links" (direct commercial flights, shipping, and post) are established across the strait.
- **2019** — PLA warplanes cross the median line for the first time since 1999.
- **2020** — The PRC Foreign Ministry explicitly denies the existence of a median line.
- **2022** — Chinese military exercises around Taiwan are conducted, further challenging the concept of the median line.

## Body

### Geography and Definition
The strait separates Fujian Province from Taiwan Island. While an international agreement places its waters within the South China Sea, a draft IHO definition classifies it as part of the North Pacific Ocean, delimiting it with specific coordinates. The entire strait lies on the continental shelf and is mostly less than 150 meters deep. Key islands include Kinmen and Matsu (controlled by the ROC) and Xiamen and Pingtan (controlled by the PRC). The Penghu (Pescadores) islands, within the strait, are ROC-controlled.

### The Median Line
A theoretical "median line" (or Davis Line) was proposed in 1955 as a tacit understanding to prevent conflict. The PRC has never recognized it. Taiwanese aircraft crossed it frequently until 1958. After 2019, PRC military aircraft began crossing it, and by 2020, the PRC formally denied its existence, a position reinforced by subsequent military exercises.

### Geology and Sediment
Taiwan's rivers deposit 60 to 150 million tons of sediment annually into the strait. Over the past ten thousand years, approximately 600 billion tons have accumulated, forming sediment lobes up to 40 meters thick in the southern part.

### History and Conflict
For millennia, the strait separated Han Chinese culture from Taiwan's aboriginal populations, though migration and trade occurred. European powers used it for trade and raiding during the Ming Dynasty. Widespread Chinese migration began in the late Ming. Control of the strait was contested during the Qing conquest, the Japanese colonial period, and World War II. The 1949 retreat of the ROC government across the strait led to the establishment of the PRC and subsequent crises (First, Second, and Third Taiwan Strait Crises). In the 21st century, relations stabilized with the establishment of direct links in 2008.

### Strategic and Economic Importance
The strait is a critical shipping gateway for Northeast Asia. Taiwan uses it for strategic depth against the mainland. China has, since 2018, denied that the concept of "international waters" applies to the strait, seeking to regulate activities it views as supporting Taiwanese authorities. This has drawn international concern. Economically, it is a major fishing ground and the site of Taiwan's developing offshore wind farms.

## Terms
- **Strait**: A narrow passage of water connecting two larger bodies of water.
- **Continental Shelf**: The extended perimeter of each continent and associated coastal plain, covered by relatively shallow water.
- **Median Line (Davis Line)**: A theoretical dividing line proposed in 1955 down the center of the Taiwan Strait, never officially recognized by the PRC.
- **Cross-Strait Relations**: The relations between the People's Republic of China (mainland China) and the Republic of China (Taiwan).
- **Three Links**: The establishment of direct postal, transportation, and trade links between mainland China and Taiwan.
- **Exclusive Economic Zone (EEZ)**: A maritime zone extending 200 nautical miles from a state's coast, where it has sovereign rights for resource exploration and use.
- **Innocent Passage**: The right of all ships to pass through the territorial sea of a coastal state in a manner not prejudicial to its peace, good order, or security.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Status of the Waters**: Is the Taiwan Strait considered "international waters"? China denies this, while the U.S. and others assert traditional navigation rights. The UNCLOS definition is ambiguous.
- **The Median Line**: Is the median line a valid boundary for military and political purposes? The PRC rejects it, while Taiwan has historically used it as a defensive reference.
- **Sovereignty and Control**: The fundamental debate over whether the strait is an internal water, territorial sea, or EEZ of one or both sides, or an international strait with transit passage rights, remains unresolved and central to regional security.

# First Sino-Japanese War

## Overview
The First Sino-Japanese War (1894–1895) was a conflict between the Qing dynasty of China and the Empire of Japan, primarily for influence over Korea. It marked a decisive shift in regional power, demonstrating the success of Japan's Meiji Restoration and the failure of China's Self-Strengthening Movement. The war ended with a Japanese victory, leading to the Treaty of Shimonoseki and setting the stage for Japan's emergence as a major imperial power.

## Timeline
- **25 July 1894** — War begins with the Battle of Pungdo; Japan sinks the Chinese transport *Kowshing*.
- **1 August 1894** — Formal declarations of war are issued by both nations.
- **15 September 1894** — Japanese victory at the Battle of Pyongyang forces Chinese retreat from Korea.
- **17 September 1894** — Japanese navy decisively defeats the Chinese Beiyang Fleet at the Battle of the Yalu River.
- **21 November 1894** — Japanese capture the Chinese naval base at Lüshunkou (Port Arthur).
- **12 February 1895** — Japanese capture the Chinese naval base at Weihaiwei.
- **17 April 1895** — Treaty of Shimonoseki is signed, ending the war.
- **23 May 1895** — Republic of Formosa is declared in Taiwan to resist Japanese cession.
- **21 October 1895** — Japanese complete the occupation of Taiwan.

## Body

### Background and Causes
In the late 19th century, Korea was a tributary state of the Qing dynasty, while Japan, following its Meiji Restoration, sought to expand its influence. Both nations had attempted modernization: Japan through successful centralizing reforms, and China through the less effective Self-Strengthening Movement. Tensions escalated in 1894 when China sent troops to Korea to suppress the Donghak Peasant Revolution. Japan, citing a violation of the 1885 Convention of Tientsin (which required mutual notification of military action near Korea), deployed a larger force. Japanese troops seized the Korean emperor and established a pro-Japanese government, which then authorized Japan to expel Chinese forces.

### Military Conflict
The war began with naval and land engagements in Korea. The Japanese navy gained an early advantage by sinking the Chinese transport *Kowshing* on 25 July. After the Battle of Pyongyang (15 September), Chinese forces retreated to Manchuria. The decisive naval Battle of the Yalu River (17 September) destroyed much of the Chinese Beiyang Fleet, giving Japan command of the sea. The Japanese army then invaded Manchuria, capturing Port Arthur (21 November) and Weihaiwei (12 February 1895), which threatened Beijing. The Qing government sued for peace.

### Treaty and Aftermath
The Treaty of Shimonoseki (17 April 1895) required China to recognize Korean independence, cede Taiwan and the Liaodong Peninsula to Japan, pay a large indemnity, and open additional ports. A subsequent Triple Intervention by Russia, Germany, and France forced Japan to return the Liaodong Peninsula in exchange for a larger indemnity. The war's outcome humiliated the Qing dynasty, catalyzing internal reform movements and ultimately contributing to the 1911 Revolution. It established Japan as the dominant power in East Asia and prompted further imperialist encroachment on China by Western powers.

## Terms
- **Meiji Restoration**: The political revolution beginning in 1868 that restored imperial rule to Japan and launched its rapid modernization and industrialization.
- **Self-Strengthening Movement**: A Qing dynasty policy (1861–1895) to adopt Western technology and military techniques to bolster Chinese power, which ultimately failed to prevent defeat.
- **Tributary State**: A state that acknowledges the suzerainty of a larger power (in this case, Korea under China) through ritual submission and tribute.
- **Convention of Tientsin (1885)**: An agreement between China and Japan requiring both to notify the other before sending troops to Korea.
- **Beiyang Fleet**: The most modern of the Qing dynasty's four regional navies, which was largely destroyed in the war.
- **Donghak Peasant Revolution**: A major 1894 uprising in Korea against government corruption and foreign influence, which served as the immediate pretext for Chinese and Japanese military intervention.
- **Treaty of Shimonoseki**: The 1895 agreement that ended the war, imposing heavy terms on China including territorial cessions and a large indemnity.
- **Triple Intervention**: The 1895 diplomatic action by Russia, Germany, and France that forced Japan to return the Liaodong Peninsula to China.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The extent to which the Qing defeat was caused by systemic corruption versus specific military failures remains a point of scholarly analysis.
- The role of the Triple Intervention in shaping Japan's subsequent imperial ambitions and its decision to pursue a more aggressive foreign policy is widely debated.
- Historians continue to examine the long-term impact of the war on the collapse of the Qing dynasty and the rise of Chinese nationalism.

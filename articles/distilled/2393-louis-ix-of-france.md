# Louis IX of France

## Overview
Louis IX (1214–1270), also known as Saint Louis, was King of France from 1226 until his death. He is the only French king canonized as a saint and is recognized as the most distinguished of the Direct Capetians, being the direct ancestor of all subsequent French kings. His reign is often marked as an economic and political zenith for medieval France, and he was widely respected throughout Christendom as a fair and judicious ruler.

- **1239–41** — Acquires the Crown of Thorns relic from Emperor Baldwin II.

- **24 March 1267** — Takes the cross for the Eighth Crusade.
- **25 August 1270** — Dies of dysentery at Tunis during the Eighth Crusade.

## Body
### Early Life and Minority
Louis was born in 1214 and became king at age 12 upon his father's death in 1226. His mother, Blanche of Castile, ruled as regent until he came of age and remained a trusted adviser until her death in 1252. During his minority, Blanche confronted rebellious vassals and ended the Albigensian Crusade in 1229. Louis married Margaret of Provence in 1234; historians believe he began ruling personally that year.

### Reign and Domestic Policy
As king, Louis expanded French territory, annexing parts of Aquitaine, Maine, and Provence. He defeated England's Henry III at the Battle of Taillebourg. His most enduring domestic achievements were legal reforms: he abolished trials by ordeal, introduced the presumption of innocence, and created a royal justice system allowing appeals to the monarch. He established offices of provosts and bailiffs and used *enquêtes* (investigations) to address governmental abuses. The 1254 Great Reform Ordinance set ethical standards for royal officers.

### Religion and Culture
Louis was an extremely devout Catholic, enforcing orthodoxy and enacting harsh laws against blasphemy. He persecuted Jews, ordering them to wear a yellow badge and burning 12,000 copies of the Talmud in 1242. He built the Sainte-Chapelle to house relics like the Crown of Thorns. His patronage inspired Gothic art and architecture, and his court made Paris a European center of arts and learning. The foundations for the Sorbonne were laid around 1257.

### Crusades and Foreign Relations
Keeping a vow made during illness, Louis led the Seventh Crusade (1248–1254) to Egypt. He was captured in 1250, ransomed, and later spent four years fortifying Crusader states in the Holy Land. He died during the Eighth Crusade at Tunis in 1270. Louis was a respected arbitrator in European disputes, mediating between Henry III of England and his barons. He maintained diplomatic relations with Mongol rulers, sending envoys to their courts.

## Terms
- **Direct Capetians**: The main line of French kings descended from Hugh Capet.
- **Regent**: A person who rules in place of a monarch who is too young or absent.
- **Albigensian Crusade**: A 13th-century campaign against the Cathar heresy in southern France.
- **Enquêteurs**: Royal investigators appointed to examine abuses and deliver justice.
- **Presumption of innocence**: The legal principle that a person is considered innocent until proven guilty.
- **Trial by ordeal**: A medieval judicial practice where guilt or innocence was determined by physical tests.
- **Sainte-Chapelle**: A Gothic chapel in Paris built by Louis IX to house Christian relics.
- **Talmud**: The central text of Rabbinic Judaism, containing Jewish law and commentary.
- **Mise of Amiens**: Louis IX's 1264 arbitration that sided with Henry III of England against his barons.
- **mos Teutonicus**: A 13th-century funerary practice of separating a body's flesh from bones for transport.

## Debates and Open Questions
The primary sources for Louis's life—by Jean de Joinville, Geoffrey of Beaulieu, and William of Chartres—are all biased favorably toward the king. Historians debate the extent of his personal rule versus his mother's influence during the early years of his reign. His legacy is also viewed through a modern lens, with his religious persecutions and disastrous crusades contrasting with his reputation as a just and saintly ruler.

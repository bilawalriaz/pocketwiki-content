# Monaco

## Overview
Monaco is a sovereign city-state and microstate on the French Riviera, governed by the House of Grimaldi since 1297. It is the world's second-smallest and most densely populated sovereign state, renowned as a wealthy tax haven and tourist destination with a unique political and economic structure.

## Timeline
- **1297** — Francesco Grimaldi captures the Rock of Monaco, beginning Grimaldi rule.
- **1861** — Franco-Monégasque Treaty recognizes Monaco's sovereignty after it cedes Menton and Roquebrune to France.
- **1869** — Monaco stops collecting income tax from residents, funded by casino revenue.
- **1911** — Constitution adopted, ending absolute rule and establishing a constitutional monarchy.
- **1918** — New Franco-Monégasque Treaty aligns Monaco's foreign policy with France.
- **1943-1944** — Monaco is occupied first by Italy, then Nazi Germany; Jewish population is deported.
- **1956** — Prince Rainier III marries American actress Grace Kelly, bringing global attention.
- **1962** — Constitution amended, abolishing capital punishment and establishing a Supreme Court.
- **1993** — Monaco becomes a full voting member of the United Nations.
- **2002** — New treaty ensures Monaco remains independent even without Grimaldi heirs.
- **2024** — Land reclamation project "Le Portier" opens, expanding Monaco's area.

## Body

### Government and Politics
Monaco is a semi-constitutional monarchy with Prince Albert II as head of state, who holds significant political power. The head of government, the Minister of State, can be Monégasque or French, with the monarch consulting France before appointment. Key judicial positions are held by French magistrates. The unicameral National Council has 24 elected members, though elections often produce lopsided results favoring the pro-monarchist Monegasque National Union. Monaco is not part of the EU but uses the euro and participates in some EU policies like customs.

### Economy and Taxation
Monaco's economy is driven by tourism, banking, and its status as a tax haven. It has no personal income tax (except for French citizens) and low business taxes, attracting wealthy residents and over 30% millionaires. The Monte Carlo Casino, opened in the late 19th century, was pivotal to its financial recovery and tourism boom. Real estate is among the world's most expensive. In 2024, the Financial Action Task Force placed Monaco under increased monitoring for money laundering and terrorist financing concerns.

### History and Sovereignty
Monaco's history begins with ancient Ligurian settlement. The Grimaldi family, from Genoa, seized control in 1297. Its sovereignty was contested for centuries, with periods under French and Sardinian protection. The 1861 treaty with France, after Monaco ceded most of its territory, formally recognized its independence. Monaco was occupied during World War II, leading to the deportation of its Jewish population. Full UN membership came in 1993, and a 2002 treaty with France guarantees its continued independence.

### Geography and Demographics
Monaco is a 2.08 km² semi-enclave on the Mediterranean, bordered by France. It is the world's most densely populated country. The population is diverse, with Monégasques making up about 24% of residents. French is the official language, but Italian and the historic Monégasque (a Ligurian variety) are also spoken. The climate is Mediterranean with mild, rainy winters and warm, dry summers. Land reclamation projects, like the new Le Portier district, are expanding its limited territory.

## Terms
- **Microstate**: A very small sovereign state, like Monaco or Vatican City.
- **Semi-constitutional monarchy**: A system where a monarch shares power with a constitution and elected bodies, but retains significant authority.
- **Tax haven**: A jurisdiction with no or low taxes, attracting foreign wealth and investment.
- **Franco-Monégasque Treaty**: Key agreements (1861, 1918, 2002) defining Monaco's sovereignty and relationship with France.
- **Grimaldi dynasty**: The ruling family of Monaco since 1297.
- **Land reclamation**: The process of creating new land from the sea, used by Monaco to expand its area.
- **Monégasque**: The traditional Ligurian language of Monaco, now spoken by a minority.
- **Minister of State**: The head of government in Monaco, appointed by the Prince.

## Debates and Open Questions
- Monaco's status as a tax haven and its recent placement on the FATF "grey list" for money laundering raise ongoing international scrutiny.
- The political system's democratic deficit is noted, with an unelected head of state and government, and election results that often do not reflect proportional vote share.
- The environmental and social impacts of continuous land reclamation and high-density development are points of concern.

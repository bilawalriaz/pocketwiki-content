# Venezuela

## Overview
Venezuela is a country on the northern coast of South America with a population of approximately 31.8 million. It is a nation defined by its vast oil reserves, a history of political turbulence, and a severe ongoing humanitarian and economic crisis. Once a relatively prosperous democracy, Venezuela has experienced democratic backsliding, hyperinflation, and a massive refugee crisis, culminating in a 2026 U.S. military intervention that has left it described as a puppet state.

- **1811** — Venezuela declares independence from Spain, beginning the War of Independence.
- **1830** — Venezuela separates from Gran Colombia to become a fully sovereign country.
- **1958** — The Puntofijo Pact initiates a period of democratic governments.
- **1998** — Hugo Chávez is elected president, launching the Bolivarian Revolution.
- **2013** — Nicolás Maduro succeeds Chávez as president after his death.
- **2014** — Poverty begins to rapidly increase amid economic crisis.
- **2024** — Presidential election results are widely disputed, sparking protests.
- **2026** — U.S. forces capture President Maduro; Vice President Delcy Rodríguez becomes acting president.

## Body

### History and Politics
Venezuela's territory was colonized by Spain in 1522. It declared independence in 1811 and, after a war, became a sovereign nation in 1830. The 19th century was marked by political turmoil and military dictatorships. A democratic period began in 1958 but was destabilized by economic crises in the 1980s and 1990s, leading to social unrest and political crises. This collapse of confidence in traditional parties paved the way for Hugo Chávez's election in 1998 and the start of the Bolivarian Revolution. Chávez's government implemented populist social policies funded by oil revenues, temporarily reducing poverty. After his death in 2013, Nicolás Maduro continued the policies, but a sharp fall in oil prices in 2014 triggered a severe economic and humanitarian crisis. The government has been accused of authoritarianism, with disputed elections in 2013, 2018, and 2024. In 2026, a U.S. military intervention captured Maduro, and the country is now described as a U.S. puppet state, with its finances and policies overseen by the U.S. Department of State.

### Economy and Resources
Venezuela has the world's largest known oil reserves, and the petroleum sector dominates its economy, accounting for roughly a third of GDP and over half of government revenues. The discovery of oil in the early 20th century transformed the economy from an agricultural exporter to a petro-state. Under Chávez, oil profits funded extensive social programs, but the economy became overly dependent on high oil prices. Following the 2014 oil price collapse and mismanagement, the economy entered a severe depression with hyperinflation, widespread shortages of basic goods, and a collapse in public services. U.S. sanctions, escalating from 2017, have further crippled the economy by targeting the state oil company PDVSA and freezing assets. The crisis has led to a massive refugee exodus, with over 7.9 million Venezuelans fleeing the country.

### Geography and Demographics
Venezuela covers 912,050 km² and features diverse geography, including the northern Andes mountains, the central Llanos plains, the Orinoco River basin, and the Guiana Highlands, which contain Angel Falls, the world's highest waterfall. It is highly urbanized, with about 88.4% of the population living in urban areas, primarily in the north. The population is predominantly mixed-race (pardo), with significant European, Indigenous, and African ancestry. Spanish is the official language, and Roman Catholicism is the dominant religion. The country is rich in biodiversity but suffers from deforestation and environmental degradation.

### Society and Culture
Venezuelan culture is a blend of Indigenous, Spanish, and African influences. The country is a major baseball nation, a rarity in South America. Music and dance, such as the joropo, are central to its cultural identity. The economic crisis has severely impacted society, causing a collapse in the healthcare and education systems, widespread malnutrition, and one of the world's highest homicide rates. Corruption is endemic, and human rights organizations have documented severe abuses, including extrajudicial killings and political repression.

## Terms
- **Bolivarian Revolution:** A left-wing populist political process and social movement led by Hugo Chávez, named after independence hero Simón Bolívar.
- **PDVSA:** Petróleos de Venezuela, the state-owned oil and natural gas company, central to the country's economy.
- **Hyperinflation:** Extremely rapid and out-of-control inflation, which has devastated Venezuela's currency and purchasing power.
- **Petro-state:** A country whose economy is heavily dependent on the export of petroleum.
- **Caudillo:** A type of personalist leader wielding military and political power, common in 19th-century Latin America.
- **Llanos:** The vast tropical plains of central Venezuela, seasonally flooded.
- **Tepui:** The distinctive table-top mountains (plateaus) of the Guiana Highlands.
- **CLAPs:** Local food distribution committees established by the government to manage shortages.
- **Puntofijo Pact:** A 1958 agreement among major political parties to maintain democratic stability.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The extent to which U.S. economic sanctions, versus government mismanagement and corruption, is responsible for the collapse of Venezuela's economy and oil production.
- The legitimacy of the 2024 presidential election results and the subsequent political crisis.
- The long-term implications of the 2026 U.S. intervention for Venezuelan sovereignty and its status as a potential "puppet state."
- The future of Venezuela's vast oil reserves and their role in the global energy transition.

# Joseph Fourier

## Overview
Jean-Baptiste Joseph Fourier (1768–1830) was a French mathematician and physicist whose work fundamentally shaped modern analysis and mathematical physics. He is best known for developing Fourier series and their application to heat transfer, and for being the first to propose the concept of the greenhouse effect. His theories provided essential tools for solving differential equations and analyzing periodic phenomena.

## Timeline
- **21 March 1768** — Born in Auxerre, France.
- **1795** — Appointed to the École Normale; later succeeds Lagrange at the École Polytechnique.
- **1798** — Accompanies Napoleon's expedition to Egypt as scientific adviser.
- **1801** — Appointed Prefect of the Department of Isère in Grenoble by Napoleon.
- **21 December 1807** — Presents his seminal paper "On the Propagation of Heat in Solid Bodies" to the Paris Institute.
- **1822** — Publishes *Théorie analytique de la chaleur* (The Analytical Theory of Heat).
- **1822** — Succeeds Delambre as Permanent Secretary of the French Academy of Sciences.
- **1824 & 1827** — Publishes articles proposing the insulating effect of the atmosphere (greenhouse effect).
- **16 May 1830** — Dies in Paris.

## Body

### Biography and Career
Fourier was orphaned at nine and educated by Benedictines. Barred from military commissions due to his birth, he became a mathematics lecturer. He supported the French Revolution, was briefly imprisoned during the Terror, and later joined Napoleon's 1798 Egyptian expedition. After returning, Napoleon appointed him Prefect of Isère in 1801, where he oversaw infrastructure projects. It was in Grenoble that he began his crucial experiments on heat propagation. He later became Permanent Secretary of the French Academy of Sciences.

### The Analytic Theory of Heat
Fourier's 1822 treatise, *The Analytical Theory of Heat*, revolutionized mathematical physics. He proposed that any function (with certain conditions) could be expanded into a series of sines and cosines—a concept now known as a **Fourier series**. This breakthrough, though initially controversial, laid the foundation for **Fourier analysis**. Physically, he formulated the **heat equation**, a partial differential equation describing conductive heat diffusion, and emphasized the principle of **dimensional homogeneity** in equations. His work inspired later developments like the Sturm–Liouville theory and the study of Bessel functions.

### Mathematical Contributions
Beyond heat theory, Fourier made significant contributions to algebra. He discovered an inductive proof of Descartes' rule of signs and developed a theorem for locating real roots of polynomials. His work on roots, published posthumously in 1831, was closely related to a theorem independently discovered by François Budan. A complete solution to the root-finding problem was later provided by Sturm in 1829.

### Discovery of the Greenhouse Effect
In the 1820s, Fourier calculated that Earth should be much colder based solely on solar radiation. He hypothesized that the atmosphere might act as an insulator, trapping heat. Referencing an experiment by Horace Bénédict de Saussure involving a glass-paneled vase, Fourier suggested that atmospheric gases could create a similar warming effect. While he mistakenly attributed some extra heat to interstellar radiation, his core idea—that the atmosphere insulates the planet—is recognized as the first proposal of the **greenhouse effect**.

## Terms
- **Fourier series**: A mathematical tool that decomposes a periodic function into a sum of simple sine and cosine waves.
- **Fourier analysis**: The branch of mathematics studying how functions can be represented by trigonometric series.
- **Heat equation**: A partial differential equation that describes the distribution of heat in a given region over time.
- **Dimensional homogeneity**: The principle that an equation is physically meaningful only if the dimensions of all its terms are consistent.
- **Greenhouse effect**: The process by which a planet's atmosphere traps thermal radiation, warming its surface.
- **Partial differential equation (PDE)**: An equation involving an unknown function of several variables and its partial derivatives.
- **Parabolic PDE**: A type of PDE (like the heat equation) that describes diffusion processes.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The initial mathematical validity of Fourier's claim that any function could be expanded in a Fourier series was debated. The question of convergence conditions remained fundamental for centuries, with later mathematicians like Dirichlet providing rigorous proofs under specific restrictions.
- Fourier's explanation for Earth's excess warmth included a mistaken contribution from interstellar radiation; the significant role of internal radiogenic heat was not yet understood.

# Bogotá

## Overview
Bogotá is the capital and largest city of Colombia, serving as the nation's primary political, economic, and cultural center. Founded by Spanish conquistadors in 1538 on the site of a Muisca settlement, it has remained the capital through colonial rule, independence, and the formation of the modern Colombian state. Located on a high Andean plateau, it is one of the world's highest capital cities and a major metropolis in South America.

## Timeline
- **c. 12,500 BP** — Earliest evidence of human activity in the region found at El Abra.
- **6 August 1538** — Gonzalo Jiménez de Quesada founds the settlement "Our Lady of Hope."
- **27 April 1539** — Official founding with a town council and street demarcation.
- **27 July 1540** — Spanish Crown grants city status; name changed to Santa Fe.
- **1717** — Santa Fe becomes capital of the Viceroyalty of New Granada.
- **20 July 1810** — Junta de Santa Fe established, marking the start of independence movements.
- **7 August 1819** — Battle of Boyacá; Simón Bolívar renames the city Bogotá.
- **1948** — Assassination of Jorge Eliécer Gaitán triggers the "Bogotazo" riots.
- **1995** — "Comunidad Segura" security policy adopted, leading to major crime reduction.
- **2000** — First phase of the TransMilenio bus rapid transit system opens.
- **2007** — UNESCO designates Bogotá as World Book Capital.
- **2021** — Construction begins on the Bogotá Metro.

## Body

### Pre-Columbian and Colonial Foundations
The area was inhabited by the Muisca, a Chibcha-speaking people who formed a loose confederation. They were known for agriculture, goldwork, and trade. Spanish conquistador Gonzalo Jiménez de Quesada, seeking a route to Peru, conquered the Muisca and founded a settlement in 1538. It was officially named Santa Fe in 1540 and became the seat of the Spanish Royal Audiencia in 1550 and later the Viceroyalty of New Granada in 1717.

### Independence and Modern Capital
Political unrest led to the establishment of a junta in 1810. After a period of civil conflict ("la Patria Boba") and Spanish reconquest, Simón Bolívar's forces won the Battle of Boyacá in 1819. Bolívar renamed the city Bogotá to honor the indigenous Muisca and symbolize emancipation from Spain. Bogotá became the capital of Gran Colombia and later the Republic of Colombia. The 19th century saw slow growth, political instability, and civil wars, including the Thousand Days' War (1899-1902).

### 20th Century to Present
The 20th century brought rapid urbanization, industrialization, and social upheaval. The 1948 assassination of liberal leader Jorge Eliécer Gaitán sparked the "Bogotazo" riots, destroying downtown and leading to a decade of civil conflict known as "La Violencia." From the 1990s, the city implemented significant urban reforms, including a participatory security policy that drastically reduced homicide rates. Major infrastructure projects like the TransMilenio BRT system and an extensive network of bike paths (ciclorrutas) were developed. The city continues to grow as a global business hub, though it faces challenges with inequality and public transport demand, prompting the construction of its first metro line.

## Terms
*   **Muisca Confederation:** A loose egalitarian society of indigenous chiefs (caciques) in the Altiplano Cundiboyacense before the Spanish conquest.
*   **Altiplano Cundiboyacense:** A high plateau in the Eastern Cordillera of the Andes where Bogotá is located.
*   **Viceroyalty of New Granada:** A Spanish colonial administrative territory, with Santa Fe (Bogotá) as its capital from 1717.
*   **Gran Colombia:** A short-lived republic (1819-1831) that included modern-day Colombia, Venezuela, Ecuador, and Panama, with Bogotá as its capital.
*   **TransMilenio:** A bus rapid transit (BRT) system using articulated buses on dedicated lanes, opened in 2000.
*   **Ciclorrutas:** An extensive network of dedicated bicycle paths in Bogotá, one of the largest in the world.
*   **Bogotazo:** The massive riots and destruction in Bogotá following the assassination of Jorge Eliécer Gaitán in 1948.
*   **La Violencia:** A period of intense civil conflict in Colombia (c. 1948–1958) sparked by the Bogotazo.
*   **Estrato:** A socioeconomic stratification system (1-6) used in Colombia to classify residential properties for public service billing and social programs.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   The exact meaning of the Muisca name "Bacatá" is debated, with translations including "walling of the farmland," "The Lady of the Andes," or the name of a cacique.
*   The traditional founding date is 6 August 1538, but the official founding with a town council occurred on 27 April 1539.
*   The city's rapid growth and urbanization have led to ongoing debates about the effectiveness of public transport solutions, the pace of metro construction, and managing social inequality between northern and southern districts.

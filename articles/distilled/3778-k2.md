# K2

## Overview
K2, also known as Mount Godwin-Austen, is the world's second-highest mountain at 8,611 metres (28,251 ft). Located in the Karakoram range on the Pakistan-China border, it is widely considered a more difficult and dangerous climb than Mount Everest due to its steep, exposed faces and severe weather. Its first successful ascent was in 1954, and it was the last of the world's 14 eight-thousanders to be climbed in winter, in 2021.

## Timeline
- **1856** — First surveyed by British team; named "K2" by Thomas Montgomerie.
- **1902** — First serious climbing attempt reaches 6,525 m (21,407 ft).
- **1909** — Duke of the Abruzzi's expedition reaches ~6,250 m (20,510 ft) on the Southeast Spur.
- **1954** — First successful summit by Italians Lino Lacedelli and Achille Compagnoni via the Abruzzi Spur.
- **1986** — First woman, Wanda Rutkiewicz, summits; 13 climbers die in the 1986 K2 disaster.
- **2008** — 11 climbers die in the 2008 K2 disaster, the deadliest single incident on the mountain.
- **2018** — Record 62 summits in a season; first ski descent from summit to base camp.
- **2021** — First winter ascent by a Nepalese team led by Nirmal Purja and Mingma Gyalje Sherpa.
- **2022** — Record 145 summits in a single day and 200 in a season.

## Body

### Name and Geography
The designation "K2" originated from the Great Trigonometrical Survey of British India, where "K" stands for Karakoram. The mountain lacks a widely accepted local name, though "Chogori" (Balti for "big mountain") has been suggested. It is officially known as Mount Godwin-Austen in Pakistan and Qogir in China. K2 is a steep, pyramid-shaped peak with immense local relief, rising over 3,000 m (10,000 ft) above its glacial valleys. It is the most prominent peak in the Karakoram range.

### Geology
K2 is composed of metamorphic rock known as the K2 Gneiss, part of the Karakoram Metamorphic Complex. This gneiss formed from the metamorphism of pre-collisional Cretaceous granite during the collision of the Indian and Asian plates. The mountain was later uplifted along major faults.

### Climbing History: Early Attempts
The first serious attempt was in 1902. The 1909 expedition by the Duke of the Abruzzi established the route that would become the standard path. After several failed American attempts, the first successful summit was achieved on 31 July 1954 by an Italian expedition led by Ardito Desio. The ascent was controversial due to a dispute over camp placement that left two climbers, Walter Bonatti and Amir Mehdi, exposed overnight above 8,000 m (26,000 ft).

### Climbing History: Success and Records
The second ascent was in 1977. The first woman to summit was Wanda Rutkiewicz in 1986. The mountain has seen increasing traffic, with record seasons in 2018 and 2022. The first winter ascent was finally achieved on 16 January 2021 by a team of Nepalese climbers. Notable disasters occurred in 1986, 1995, 2008, and 2021.

### Climbing Routes and Difficulties
The standard route is the Abruzzi Spur, used by 75% of climbers. It features technical rock and ice sections, including the "Black Pyramid" and the dangerous "Bottleneck" couloir below a serac ice cliff. Other routes, like the North Ridge on the Chinese side, are rarely climbed due to technical difficulty and access challenges. The East and North Faces remain unclimbed. All routes present extreme altitude, severe storms, and difficult retreats.

## Terms
- **Eight-thousander**: A mountain exceeding 8,000 m (26,247 ft) in height. There are 14 in the world.
- **Abruzzi Spur**: The standard climbing route on K2's southeast ridge, first attempted in 1909.
- **Serac**: A large, unstable block or column of glacial ice.
- **Death zone**: The altitude above 8,000 m (26,247 ft) where oxygen is insufficient to sustain human life for extended periods.
- **Topographic prominence**: A measure of a mountain's independent stature, calculated as the vertical distance between its summit and the lowest contour line encircling only it and a higher peak.
- **Gneiss**: A coarse-grained metamorphic rock formed from pre-existing rocks under high heat and pressure.
- **Alpine style**: A style of climbing where climbers carry all their gear and supplies in a single push, without fixed ropes or high camps.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The 1954 first ascent remains controversial. It was long alleged that climbers Lacedelli and Compagnoni deliberately moved their high camp to force Bonatti and Mehdi into a dangerous situation. While Bonatti was initially blamed for Mehdi's frostbite amputations, later investigations exonerated him.
- The ethics and impact of increasing commercialization and traffic on K2 are ongoing concerns, especially given its history as a deadly, expert-only climb.

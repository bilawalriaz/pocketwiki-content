# Palermo

## Overview
Palermo is the capital and largest city of Sicily, Italy, with a 2,700-year history shaped by successive conquests. Its strategic location on the Tyrrhenian Sea made it a crossroads of Mediterranean cultures, resulting in a unique architectural and cultural blend, particularly the Arab-Norman style. Today, it is Sicily's economic, cultural, and tourism hub, though it faces challenges including organized crime and economic inequality.

## Timeline
- **c. 734 BC** — Founded by Phoenicians as Ziz ("flower").
- **5th century BC** — Greeks name the settlement Pánormos ("wide haven").
- **254 BC** — Conquered by the Roman Republic.
- **831 AD** — Conquered by Arabs, becoming the capital of the Emirate of Sicily.
- **1072** — Conquered by Normans after a siege.
- **1130** — Becomes capital of the Kingdom of Sicily under Roger II.
- **1860** — Conquered by Giuseppe Garibaldi's Expedition of the Thousand.
- **1943** — Heavily bombed and captured by Allied forces during WWII.
- **1946** — Declared seat of the Regional Parliament of the Special Status Region.

## Body

### History and Conquest
Palermo's history is defined by a succession of rulers. Founded as a Phoenician colony, it became a major Carthaginian trading post. After the Punic Wars, it was part of the Roman Empire for over a millennium. Following the fall of Rome, it passed through Vandal and Ostrogothic hands before being taken by the Byzantine Empire in 535. Arab rule from 831 to 1072 was transformative; Palermo became the capital of Sicily and a major Mediterranean center. The Norman conquest in 1072 began a new era, with Palermo as the capital of the wealthy Kingdom of Sicily. Later rule by the House of Habsburg, the Bourbons, and finally unification with Italy in 1860 shaped its modern identity.

### Culture and Religion
Palermitan culture is deeply influenced by its layered history. Roman Catholicism is central, with Saint Rosalia as the patron saint, celebrated in the annual Festino on July 15. The city's architecture is a hallmark of its past, featuring Arab-Norman, Gothic, Baroque, and Art Nouveau styles. Key UNESCO World Heritage Sites include the Palazzo dei Normanni with the Cappella Palatina and the Cathedral. The city is renowned for its vibrant markets (Vucciria, Ballarò, Capo) and its distinctive gastronomy.

### Modern Challenges and Economy
In the post-WWII era, Palermo experienced rapid, often unregulated growth known as the "Sack of Palermo," which destroyed historical buildings. The city has long struggled against the Sicilian Mafia (Cosa Nostra), which has infiltrated the legal economy and public life. Economically, Palermo relies on tourism, services, commerce, and agriculture, but suffers from high unemployment and corruption. It is undergoing redevelopment to re-establish itself as a major Euro-Mediterranean city.

## Terms
- **Emirate of Sicily:** An Islamic state that ruled Sicily from 831 to 1072, with Palermo as its capital.
- **Kingdom of Sicily:** A state that existed from 1130 to 1816, with Palermo as its first capital.
- **Arab-Norman:** A unique architectural style blending Arab, Byzantine, and Norman elements, characteristic of 12th-century Palermo.
- **Cosa Nostra:** The Sicilian Mafia, a criminal syndicate with significant historical influence in Palermo.
- **Sack of Palermo:** A term for the speculative and destructive urban development that occurred in Palermo from the 1950s to the 1980s.
- **Festino:** The annual religious procession and festival honoring Palermo's patron saint, Rosalia.
- **Vucciria, Ballarò, Capo:** Famous historic open-air markets in the center of Palermo.
- **Teatro Massimo:** One of Europe's largest opera houses, opened in 1897, renowned for its acoustics.

## Debates and Open Questions
The source notes scholarly debate about the origins of Palermo's earliest inhabitants, with the Sicani people possibly arriving from the Iberian Peninsula. The extent and nature of the city's economic recovery and its success in combating Mafia influence remain ongoing challenges.

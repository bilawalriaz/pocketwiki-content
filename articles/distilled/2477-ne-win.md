# Ne Win

## Overview
Ne Win (1911–2002) was a Burmese general and politician who dominated Burma's (Myanmar's) political landscape for decades. He served as head of government from 1958–1960 and 1962–1974, and as head of state from 1962–1981. As the country's military dictator, he founded the Burma Socialist Programme Party (BSPP) and established a one-party socialist state following a 1962 coup. His rule was marked by isolationism, economic stagnation, and superstition, ending with his resignation during the 1988 pro-democracy uprising.

## Body

### Early Life and Independence Struggle
Born Shu Maung into an ethnic Burman family, Ne Win attended Rangoon University before being expelled in 1931. He joined the nationalist Dobama Asiayone (We Burmans Association) and, in 1941, was one of thirty young men chosen for military training by the Japanese, forming the Burma Independence Army (BIA). He adopted the nom de guerre Bo Ne Win ("Commander Radiant Sun"). During World War II, the BIA entered Burma with the Japanese. In 1945, the Burma National Army turned against the Japanese, and Ne Win quickly established links with the British. After independence in 1948, he rose to become Chief of Staff of the Armed Forces in 1949, restructuring the military along Socialist Party lines.

### The 1962 Coup and the Burmese Way to Socialism
On 2 March 1962, Ne Win overthrew the democratic government of U Nu in a "bloodless" coup, declaring parliamentary democracy unsuitable for Burma. He founded the Burma Socialist Programme Party (BSPP), which became the sole legal party in 1964. His regime implemented the "Burmese Way to Socialism," a mix of nationalism, Marxism, and Buddhism, and pursued "Burmanization" policies that centralized Burmese language, culture, and Buddhism, often suppressing ethnic minorities. The government nationalized the economy and pursued autarky (economic self-sufficiency), expelling foreigners and restricting aid. This led to economic decline, with a thriving black market filling the void.

### Economic Policies and Superstition
Ne Win's economic policies were disastrous. In 1985 and 1987, he abruptly demonetized currency notes, wiping out public savings. The 1987 decision, reportedly based on an astrologer's advice that the number nine was auspicious, rendered 75% of the currency reserves useless. His rule was also characterized by superstition (*yadaya*), such as performing rituals to avert misfortune and abandoning a state visit after seeing a motorcycle accident as a bad omen.

### Repression and the 8888 Uprising
Ne Win's government faced sporadic student and worker protests throughout his rule, often met with force. The most significant challenge was the nationwide 8888 Uprising, which began on 8 August 1988. At its height, Ne Win resigned as BSPP chairman on 23 July 1988, warning that the army would "shoot straight to hit." The military brutally suppressed the uprising, killing hundreds or thousands. On 18 September 1988, a new military junta, the SLORC, seized power. It is widely believed Ne Win orchestrated this coup from behind the scenes.

### Final Years and Death
Ne Win remained a shadowy influence on the junta for about a decade. After 1998, his influence waned. He died under house arrest on 5 December 2002. His death was unannounced by state media, and he received no state funeral or monument. Only thirty people attended his hastily arranged funeral.

## Terms
- **Burmese Way to Socialism**: The official ideology of Ne Win's regime, combining elements of nationalism, Marxism, and Buddhism.
- **BSPP (Burma Socialist Programme Party)**: The sole legal political party in Burma from 1964 to 1988, founded by Ne Win.
- **Autarky**: A policy of economic self-sufficiency and isolation from the global market.
- **Burmanization**: Policies promoting the centrality of the Burmese language, culture, and Buddhism, often at the expense of ethnic minorities.
- **Tatmadaw**: The armed forces of Myanmar (Burma).
- **8888 Uprising**: The nationwide pro-democracy protests that began on 8 August 1988.
- **SLORC (State Law and Order Restoration Council)**: The military junta that seized power in September 1988.
- **Yadaya**: Traditional Burmese rituals performed to ward off misfortune, to which Ne Win was devoted.
- **Thirty Comrades**: The group of thirty Burmese nationalists, including Ne Win, who received military training from the Japanese in 1941.

## Debates and open questions
- **Exact birth date**: Sources conflict, with dates given as 10 July 1910, 14 May 1911, and the widely cited 24 May 1911. Evidence from a 1998 book and his family's obituary suggests 1910 may be correct.
- **Responsibility for the 1962 Student Union bombing**: Ne Win denied ordering the dynamiting of the student union building, blaming his deputy Brigadier Aung Gyi, though he took responsibility for the subsequent "sword with sword" speech.
- **Role in the 1988 coup**: While publicly retired, Ne Win is widely believed to have orchestrated the SLORC's September 1988 coup from behind the scenes.

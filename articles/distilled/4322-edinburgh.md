# Edinburgh

## Overview
Edinburgh is the capital city of Scotland, serving as the seat of the Scottish Government and Parliament. It is a major cultural, financial, and educational centre, renowned for its historic Old and New Towns (a UNESCO World Heritage Site) and its world-famous annual festivals. The city's economy is driven by tourism, financial services, and the public sector.

## Timeline
- **c. 8500 BC** — Earliest known human habitation in the area at Cramond.
- **638** — Siege of Din Eidyn (Castle Rock) by Northumbrian forces begins centuries of Germanic influence.
- **c. 1124–1127** — First documentary evidence of the medieval royal burgh, founded by King David I.
- **1582** — Royal charter permits the establishment of a university (now the University of Edinburgh).
- **1706–1707** — Acts of Union uniting the Scottish and English parliaments; riots in Edinburgh.
- **1766** — Competition won to design the New Town, a planned Georgian extension.
- **1947** — Edinburgh International Festival and Film Festival first held.
- **1995** — Old and New Towns listed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
- **1999** — Devolved Scottish Parliament established in Edinburgh.
- **2014** — Edinburgh Trams system becomes operational.
- **2023** — Edinburgh becomes the first European capital to sign the Plant Based Treaty.

## Body

### History and Etymology
The city's name derives from "Eidyn," the Brittonic Celtic name for the region, with the stronghold of Din Eidyn believed to be Castle Rock. Control passed to the Angles in 638 and later to the Scots around 950. The royal burgh was founded by King David I in the early 12th century. Recognised as Scotland's capital by the 15th century, it was the centre of the Scottish Enlightenment in the 18th century, earning the nickname "Athens of the North." The 18th-century New Town was built to alleviate overcrowding in the medieval Old Town. The city was the seat of the pre-union Parliament of Scotland and, after the 1707 Union, remained a centre of law and administration. A devolved Scottish Parliament was re-established in 1999.

### Geography and Climate
Edinburgh lies on Scotland's southern shore of the Firth of Forth, built on seven hills including Castle Rock and Arthur's Seat, a volcanic formation. The landscape was shaped by volcanic activity and glaciation. The city has a cool temperate maritime climate, milder than other locations at similar latitudes due to its coastal position. It is known for its wind, with prevailing south-westerlies, and can experience "haar," a coastal fog from easterly winds.

### Governance and Politics
The City of Edinburgh Council is a unitary authority governing the council area. The city is represented in the Scottish Parliament by six constituencies and in the UK House of Commons by five constituencies. It is the seat of the Scottish Government and the highest courts in Scotland.

### Economy
Edinburgh has the second-strongest economy of any UK city after London, with a high proportion of degree-holders. Key sectors include financial services (it is the UK's second-largest financial centre), public administration, tourism, and education. In 2023, its GDP per capita surpassed London's for the first time. A 5% visitor levy on overnight stays was introduced in 2026.

### Culture and Festivals
The city is a major cultural hub, hosting the Edinburgh Festival Fringe (the world's largest arts festival), the Edinburgh International Festival, and the Military Tattoo each August. It is home to the National Museum of Scotland, National Library of Scotland, and national art galleries. The Old Town's medieval layout and the New Town's Georgian grid are key architectural features. The city's literary heritage led to its designation as the first UNESCO City of Literature in 2004.

### Education and Sport
Edinburgh is a major centre for education, with four universities, including the ancient University of Edinburgh (founded 1582). It has a strong sporting tradition, hosting the Scottish national rugby team at Murrayfield Stadium and professional football clubs Heart of Midlothian and Hibernian. The city has hosted the Commonwealth Games and will host the 2027 Tour de France Grand Départ.

## Terms
- **Burgh**: A type of Scottish town or city with specific historical rights and privileges, granted by royal charter.
- **Closes and Wynds**: Narrow alleyways or lanes leading off the main streets of the Old Town.
- **Edinburgh Enlightenment**: An 18th-century intellectual movement in the city, involving figures like David Hume and Adam Smith.
- **Firth**: A narrow inlet of the sea, especially in Scotland (e.g., Firth of Forth).
- **Haar**: A cold sea fog that affects the east coast of Britain, including Edinburgh.
- **Tenement**: A multi-occupancy residential building, a common form of housing in Scottish cities.
- **Unitary Authority**: A type of local government in Scotland responsible for all local services in its area.

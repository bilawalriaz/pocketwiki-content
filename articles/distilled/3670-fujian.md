# Fujian

## Overview
Fujian is a culturally and linguistically diverse province in southeastern China, bordering the Taiwan Strait. Its history of migration, maritime trade, and unique geography has made it a major source of the global Chinese diaspora, particularly to Taiwan and Southeast Asia. Today, it is an economically dynamic region with strong cross-strait ties.

## Timeline
- **c. 6000–3500 BC** — Neolithic cultures (Keqiutou, Tanshishan) flourish on the coast.
- **306 BC** — Royal family of the State of Yue flees to Fujian, founding the Minyue kingdom.
- **111 BC** — Han dynasty conquers Minyue, incorporating the region into the empire.
- **Early 4th century** — First major wave of northern immigration during the Jin dynasty collapse.
- **945–978** — Independent Kingdom of Min and Qingyuan Circuit exist during the Five Dynasties period.
- **985** — Area reorganized as "Fujian Circuit," the first use of the name "Fujian."
- **1683** — Qing dynasty conquers Taiwan and annexes it as part of Fujian Province.
- **1885** — Fujian and Taiwan are split into separate provinces.
- **1949** — People's Republic of China takes control of mainland Fujian; ROC retains Kinmen and Matsu.
- **2008** — Direct transport links with Taiwan commence.
- **2023** — PRC designates Fujian as a demonstration zone for cross-strait integration.

## Body

### Name and Geography
The name "Fujian" combines the characters for its historical cities Fuzhou and Jianzhou (now Nanping). The province is predominantly mountainous, described as "eight parts mountain, one part water, and one part farmland." It has a rugged coastline facing the Taiwan Strait, which separates it from Taiwan by 180 km. The Wuyi Mountains form its border with Jiangxi. Fujian has a subtropical climate and is the most forested province in China.

### History
Fujian's recorded history begins with the Minyue kingdom, which was conquered by the Han dynasty in 111 BC. Due to its rugged terrain, it remained relatively undeveloped until major waves of northern Chinese migration during periods of turmoil, such as the collapse of the Western Jin dynasty (early 4th century) and the end of the Tang dynasty (9th century). These migrations established the province's dominant Han population and surnames.

During the Five Dynasties period, an independent Kingdom of Min was established. The name "Fujian" first appeared as an administrative region in 985. The province became a major source of emigration, especially to Taiwan, which was annexed by the Qing dynasty in 1683 and administered as part of Fujian until 1885. After the Chinese Civil War, the PRC took control of the mainland, while the ROC government retained the islands of Kinmen and Matsu.

### Culture and Demographics
Fujian is one of China's most culturally diverse provinces. The population is predominantly Han Chinese, but speaks a variety of mutually unintelligible Min Chinese dialects (including Hokkien and Fuzhou dialect) and Hakka Chinese. This linguistic diversity is a result of its mountainous terrain isolating communities and successive waves of migration. Due to emigration, Southern Min (Hokkien) is widely spoken in Taiwan, Singapore, Malaysia, Indonesia, and the Philippines.

The province has a strong tradition of education and research. Its culture includes distinct forms of opera, cuisine (one of the eight great Chinese culinary traditions), and tea production. Fujian is the origin of oolong, white, and black tea processing techniques.

### Economy
Fujian is an affluent coastal province with a 2022 GDP of CN¥5.31 trillion, ranking 8th nationwide. Its GDP per capita is the second highest among Chinese provinces. Historically hampered by its position opposite Taiwan, development accelerated after 1978 with significant investment from overseas Fujianese, Taiwan, and foreign firms. Key industries include tea, sportswear manufacturing, and electronics. The Minnan Golden Triangle (Xiamen, Quanzhou, Zhangzhou) accounts for 40% of the province's GDP. Xiamen was one of China's first Special Economic Zones.

## Terms
- **Min Chinese**: A broad group of Chinese dialects spoken in Fujian, subdivided into Eastern, Southern, and other regional varieties. They are mutually unintelligible with Mandarin.
- **Hokkien**: A Southern Min dialect spoken in southeastern Fujian and widely in the Chinese diaspora of Southeast Asia and Taiwan.
- **Hakka**: A distinct Chinese ethnolinguistic group with their own dialect, living primarily in southwestern Fujian.
- **Minyue**: An ancient kingdom located in Fujian, founded by refugees from the State of Yue in 306 BC.
- **Fujian Tulou**: Unique, large, circular earthen communal dwellings built by the Hakka people in southwest Fujian, listed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
- **Taiwan Strait**: The 180 km-wide body of water separating Fujian from Taiwan, a key geopolitical and economic corridor.
- **Special Economic Zone (SEZ)**: A designated area where business and trade laws differ from the rest of the country to attract investment; Xiamen was an early example.

## Debates and open questions
- The political status of Taiwan and the islands of Kinmen and Matsu remains a core issue. The PRC administers most of Fujian but claims the ROC-held islands, while the ROC administers them as part of its "Fujian Province."
- The PRC's 2023 plan to make Fujian a "demonstration zone for cross-strait integration" aims to boost economic and social ties with Taiwan, the success and implications of which are ongoing.

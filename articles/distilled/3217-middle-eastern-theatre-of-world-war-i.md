# Middle Eastern theatre of World War I

## Overview
The Middle Eastern theatre of World War I (1914-1918) was a vast and complex conflict involving the Ottoman Empire and its allies against the Allied Powers. It was significant for its enormous geographic scope, the use of both conventional and asymmetrical warfare, and its role in the dissolution of the Ottoman Empire, which reshaped the political map of the modern Middle East.

## Timeline
- **2 Aug 1914** — Ottoman Empire signs secret alliance with Germany.
- **29 Oct 1914** — Ottoman warships shell Russian Black Sea ports, triggering Allied declarations of war.
- **Nov 1914** — British forces land at Fao and capture Basra in Mesopotamia.
- **Dec 1914** — Battle of Sarikamish begins in the Caucasus.
- **Apr 1915** — Allied amphibious assault on Gallipoli begins.
- **24 Apr 1915** — Ottoman government orders the deportation of Armenians (Tehcir Law).
- **1916** — Arab Revolt begins in the Hejaz, led by Sharif Hussein.
- **Feb 1916** — Russian forces capture Erzurum in the Caucasus.
- **Apr 1916** — Siege of Kut begins in Mesopotamia.
- **Dec 1916** — Siege of Kut ends with British surrender.
- **Mar 1917** — British capture Baghdad.
- **Dec 1917** — Armistice of Erzincan ends Russo-Ottoman hostilities.
- **Mar 1918** — Treaty of Brest-Litovsk ends Russian participation in the war.
- **Jun 1918** — Treaty of Batum signed between Ottoman Empire and First Republic of Armenia.
- **Sep 1918** — Battle of Megiddo leads to the collapse of Ottoman forces in Palestine.
- **30 Oct 1918** — Armistice of Mudros signed, ending Ottoman participation in the war.

## Body

### Objectives and Alliances
The Ottoman Empire joined the Central Powers via a secret alliance with Germany in August 1914. Its primary objectives were to recover territories lost to Russia in 1877-78 (like Kars and Batum) and to threaten British interests in Egypt and the Suez Canal. Germany sought to cut off Russian access to Caspian oil and promote subversion in British India. The Allies' main goals were to protect oil supplies in Persia and the Caucasus, secure communications with India, and, for Russia, to eventually seize Constantinople and the Straits. Local groups like the Armenians sought an independent state, while the Arab Revolt aimed for self-rule from Ottoman control.

### The Caucasus Campaign
This front saw major fighting between Ottoman and Russian forces. The disastrous Ottoman offensive at the Battle of Sarikamish (Dec 1914-Jan 1915) was followed by a successful Russian advance, capturing Erzurum (Feb 1916) and Trabzon (Apr 1916). Russian participation ended with the 1917 Revolution and the Treaty of Brest-Litovsk (Mar 1918), which returned Kars and Batum to the Ottomans. The region then saw conflict between the Ottomans and newly formed states like the First Republic of Armenia, culminating in the Treaty of Batum (Jun 1918).

### The Mesopotamian and Palestine Campaigns
British Indian forces advanced up the Tigris and Euphrates rivers, capturing Basra (1914) but suffering a major defeat with the Siege and surrender of Kut (1916). Baghdad was finally taken in March 1917. In Palestine, an initial Allied naval attack on the Dardanelles (1915) failed, as did the subsequent Gallipoli Campaign. After a period of stalemate at Gaza, General Allenby's forces captured Jerusalem (Dec 1917) and decisively defeated the Ottomans at the Battle of Megiddo (Sep 1918), leading to the capture of Damascus.

### Asymmetrical Warfare and Local Forces
Both sides employed irregular forces. The Arab Revolt, supported by the British and advised by T.E. Lawrence, used guerrilla tactics to disrupt Ottoman supply lines. The Armenian resistance and volunteer units fought alongside Russia, later forming the core of the Armenian Corps. The Assyrians, following their genocide, allied with the Russians and British. The Ottoman Empire relied on Kurdish tribal units, some loyal (like the Hamidiye) and some in revolt, and faced internal insurgencies that diverted significant resources.

### Forces and Casualties
The Ottoman Army, though reformed, was hampered by limited industrial capacity, poor infrastructure, and a population base where non-Muslims were exempt from conscription. It relied heavily on German equipment and advisers. Allied forces included British, French, and Russian regular armies, along with colonial troops from India, Australia, and elsewhere. Casualties were immense. Allied military losses were around 1,000,000. Ottoman military casualties are estimated between 975,000 and 1,680,701, with disease being a major killer due to collapsed medical services. Total Ottoman losses, including civilians, may have reached 25% of its population.

## Terms
*   **Asymmetrical Warfare:** Conflict between belligerents having markedly different military power and strategy, often involving guerrilla tactics by the weaker side.
*   **Tehcir Law:** The Ottoman law of 27 May 1915 ordering the forced deportation of Armenians from war zones, leading to the Armenian Genocide.
*   **Armistice:** A formal agreement of warring parties to stop fighting.
*   **Treaty of Brest-Litovsk:** The peace treaty (3 Mar 1918) that ended Russian participation in WWI, ceding territory to the Central Powers.
*   **Treaty of Sèvres:** The post-WWI treaty (10 Aug 1920) imposed on the Ottoman Empire, later annulled during the Turkish War of Independence.
*   **Treaty of Lausanne:** The 1923 treaty that replaced Sèvres, establishing the modern borders of Turkey.
*   **Dunsterforce:** An Allied military force of under 1,000 troops sent to the Caucasus in 1917 to secure oil supplies and oppose Ottoman/German forces.
*   **Hamidiye:** A mostly Kurdish elite cavalry division of the Ottoman army, noted for its role in the Armenian Genocide.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   The strategic wisdom of the Allied Dardanelles/Gallipoli Campaign is heavily debated: was it a sound concept poorly executed, or fundamentally flawed?
*   The extent of pre-war Armenian revolutionary activity and its role as a pretext for the Ottoman government's actions remains a point of scholarly contention.
*   The precise Ottoman military casualty figures are uncertain due to the collapse of bureaucratic records, with estimates varying widely.
*   The motivations and degree of agency of local groups (Kurds, Arabs, Assyrians) are complex, ranging from nationalist aspirations to opportunism or coercion.

# Great Victoria Desert

## Overview
The Great Victoria Desert is Australia's largest desert, spanning 348,750 km² across Western Australia and South Australia. It is a significant ecoregion characterized by unique flora and fauna adapted to harsh conditions, with a long history of Indigenous habitation and modern conservation efforts.

## Timeline
- **1875** — Ernest Giles becomes the first European to cross the desert, naming it after Queen Victoria.
- **1891** — David Lindsey's expedition travels across the area from north to south.
- **1903–1908** — Frank Hann explores the area searching for gold.
- **1950s–1960s** — UK nuclear weapons trials at Maralinga and Emu Field cause radioactive contamination.
- **1960s** — Len Beadell explores the area.
- **2007** — The Central Ranges taipan is discovered in the desert.

## Body

### Location and Climate
The Great Victoria Desert is over 700 km wide, stretching from Western Australia's Eastern Goldfields to South Australia's Gawler Ranges. It consists of sandhills, grassland plains, gibber plains (areas with a closely packed pebble surface), and salt lakes. The climate features low, irregular annual rainfall of 200–250 mm, with 15–20 thunderstorms per year. Summer temperatures range from 32 to 40 °C, while winter temperatures are 18 to 23 °C.

### Habitation and Human Activity
The majority of residents are Indigenous Australians, including the Kogara, Mirning, and Pitjantjatjara groups. Despite its isolation, the desert is crossed by rough tracks like the Connie Sue and Anne Beadell Highways. Human activity has included mining and nuclear testing, which left areas contaminated with plutonium-239.

### Flora and Fauna
Only hardy plants survive, including eucalyptus and mulga (Acacia aneura) shrubs scattered among resilient spinifex grasses (Triodia basedowii). Wildlife includes large birds and mammals like emus and red kangaroos, but the desert is particularly rich in reptiles, such as the vulnerable great desert skink and the Central Ranges taipan. Many animals, like the southern marsupial mole, survive by burrowing. Predators include the dingo and large monitor lizards like the perentie.

### Conservation
With limited agriculture, many habitats remain undisturbed. 31% of the desert is protected, including the Mamungari Conservation Park, a UNESCO World Biosphere Reserve. Key protected areas include multiple Indigenous Protected Areas and nature reserves in both states. The primary threat is historical radioactive contamination from nuclear tests.

## Terms
- **Ecoregion:** A large area of land or water that contains a distinct assemblage of natural communities.
- **Gibber plains:** Desert pavement; a surface closely packed with pebbles and small stones.
- **Spinifex:** A type of resilient, spiky grass common in Australian deserts.
- **Mulga:** A common name for several Australian acacia shrubs or small trees.
- **Indigenous Protected Area:** A protected area owned and managed by Indigenous communities.
- **Biosphere Reserve:** A UNESCO designation for areas balancing conservation with sustainable use.
- **Plutonium-239:** A radioactive isotope used in nuclear weapons, posing a long-term contamination hazard.

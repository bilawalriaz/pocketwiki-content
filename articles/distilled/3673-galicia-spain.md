# Galicia (Spain)

## Overview
Galicia is an autonomous community and historic nationality in northwest Spain, distinguished by Celtic and Roman heritage, its own language (Galician), and a long history of semi-autonomous governance. Its identity is shaped by Atlantic geography, a rural economy, and a persistent movement for self-government within the Spanish state.

## Timeline
- **c. 3000 BC** — Megalithic culture expands; thousands of tumuli are built.
- **137–136 BC** — Roman legions first enter under Decimus Junius Brutus.
- **19 BC** — Incorporated into the Roman Empire after the Cantabrian Wars.
- **411** — The Suebi establish a kingdom, the first medieval kingdom in Europe.
- **585** — Visigothic King Leovigild conquers the Suebic kingdom.
- **740** — Incorporated into the Christian Kingdom of Asturias.
- **1063** — Kingdom of Galicia granted to García II by Ferdinand I of Castile.
- **1072** — Forcibly annexed by Alfonso VI of León.
- **1833** — Kingdom administratively dissolved into four provinces.
- **1936** — Statute of Autonomy granted, annulled after Franco's coup.
- **1981** — Current Statute of Autonomy ratified, establishing the modern community.

## Body

### Toponymy and Early History
The name "Galicia" derives from Latin *Gallaecia*, linked to the Celtic Gallaeci tribe. Human presence dates to the Middle Paleolithic. The area was shaped by Megalithic, Bell Beaker, and Bronze Age cultures with rich metallurgy. The Iron Age Castro culture, characterized by hillforts (*castros*), flourished until Roman conquest. Romans, seeking mineral resources like gold, established control, built infrastructure like the Tower of Hercules, and made Galicia a province in the 3rd century AD.

### Medieval Kingdom and Integration with Castile
After Rome's decline, the Suebi established a kingdom (411), later conquered by Visigoths (585) and integrated into the Kingdom of Asturias (740). The cult of Saint James at Santiago de Compostela gave Galicia major symbolic importance. The Kingdom of Galicia was occasionally independent but most often united with León and later Castile, maintaining its own laws and assembly (*Cortes* or *Xunta*). Centralizing reforms by Isabella I in the late 15th century subdued local nobility and established a royal tribunal (*Real Audiencia*).

### Early Modern Period and the "Dark Centuries"
From the 16th century, Galicia was governed through the *Xunta*, an assembly of city representatives. The kingdom contributed to the Crown's finances but suffered from Atlantic wars and piracy. The written Galician language entered a long decline (*Séculos Escuros*) as Spanish became dominant in administration and literature, though Galician remained the spoken language.

### 19th and 20th Centuries: Nationalism and Autonomy
The 1833 territorial division abolished the kingdom, creating four provinces and sparking nationalist and federalist movements. A cultural revival (*Rexurdimento*) in the 19th century recovered the Galician language. In the early 20th century, political nationalism grew. During the Second Spanish Republic, Galicia was granted autonomy in 1936, revoked after Franco's Civil War victory. Franco's dictatorship suppressed Galician institutions and language. After democracy's restoration, the 1981 Statute of Autonomy established the current self-governing community.

### Geography and Climate
Galicia's interior is hilly, with mountain ranges rising to 2,000 m in the east. Its Atlantic coast is defined by *rías* (fjord-like inlets) and islands, including the Atlantic Islands of Galicia National Park. The climate is temperate and rainy, generally oceanic with mild winters and drier summers. The landscape is shaped by agriculture and forestry, though environmental issues like deforestation, eucalyptus plantations, and oil spills (e.g., the *Prestige* in 2002) are concerns.

### Government and Politics
Galicia is a self-governing community with its own parliament, executive (*Xunta de Galicia*), and judiciary. It is divided into four provinces and 314 municipalities. Politically, it has been dominated by the conservative Partido Popular (PP) since the 1990s, though a coalition of Galician Socialists (PSdeG) and the nationalist Bloque Nacionalista Galego (BNG) governed from 2005 to 2009. Independence support is low, at 10-15%.

### Economy and Demographics
The economy is based on services, industry (notably Citroën's plant in Vigo), fishing, and agriculture. Coastal areas are more prosperous, while the rural interior relies on small landholdings (*minifundios*). Galicia has low population density and has experienced significant emigration since the 19th century, first to Latin America and later to Europe. The population is aging with a low fertility rate.

### Language and Culture
Galician and Spanish are both official languages. Galician, a Romance language closely related to Portuguese, was standardized in the 20th century and is resurgent in education and media. Culture is marked by Celtic influences, bagpipe music, and a cuisine featuring seafood, empanadas, and octopus. Key symbols include the chalice coat of arms and the anthem "Os Pinos."

## Terms
- **Autonomous Community:** A first-level political and administrative division of Spain with varying self-governance.
- **Castro Culture:** Iron Age archaeological culture of northwestern Iberia, characterized by fortified hillforts (*castros*).
- **Rías:** Fjord-like coastal inlets formed by drowned river valleys, a defining feature of Galicia's coastline.
- **Xunta de Galicia:** The executive branch of the Galician autonomous government.
- **Rexurdimento:** 19th-century cultural and literary movement to revive the Galician language.
- **Séculos Escuros:** The "Dark Centuries" (c. 16th-18th) when written Galician declined under Spanish dominance.
- **Minifundio:** A small agricultural landholding, typical of Galicia's rural economy.
- **Real Audiencia:** A royal tribunal and governing body established in Galicia in 1500.
- **BNG (Bloque Nacionalista Galego):** A left-wing coalition advocating for Galician political status as a nation.
- **Denominación de Orixe (D.O.):** A system regulating the quality and geographical origin of Spanish products, including Galician wines and foods.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The precise etymology of "Galicia" is debated, with proposals linking it to Celtic words for "hill," "forest," or "stone."
- The degree of Romanization in Galicia is considered lesser than in other provinces due to its remoteness and the decline of gold mining.
- The political status of Galicia remains a topic, with the BNG advocating for nationhood, though widespread support for independence is low.
- The environmental impact of eucalyptus plantations and hydroelectric dams is a subject of ongoing concern and debate.

# History of Israel

## Overview
The history of Israel covers the Southern Levant from prehistoric migrations to the modern state, tracing the rise and fall of ancient kingdoms, successive imperial conquests, and the emergence of Judaism, Christianity, and Islam. This history is foundational to understanding the ongoing Israeli-Palestinian conflict and the region's profound religious and cultural significance.

## Timeline
- **c. 1209 BCE** — First recorded mention of "Israel" in the Egyptian Merneptah Stele.
- **c. 900–850 BCE** — Kingdoms of Israel and Judah are established.
- **720 BCE** — Kingdom of Israel falls to the Assyrians; population deported.
- **587/586 BCE** — Babylonians destroy the First Temple and exile Judeans.
- **515 BCE** — Second Temple completed under Persian rule.
- **164 BCE** — Maccabean revolt leads to Hasmonean dynasty and Jewish independence.
- **63 BCE** — Roman Republic conquers Judaea.
- **70 CE** — Romans destroy the Second Temple in the First Jewish–Roman War.
- **635 CE** — Arab armies conquer the Levant, introducing Islamic rule.
- **1099** — Crusaders capture Jerusalem, establishing the Kingdom of Jerusalem.
- **1517** — Ottoman Empire conquers the region, ruling for four centuries.
- **1917** — Balfour Declaration supports a Jewish national home in Palestine.
- **1947** — UN Partition Plan (Resolution 181) proposes separate Jewish and Arab states.
- **1948** — State of Israel declares independence; 1948 Arab–Israeli War begins.

## Body

### Prehistory and Bronze Age
The region shows evidence of human presence from 1.5 million years ago. The Natufian culture (c. 10,000 BCE) marks a transition to agriculture. By the Middle Bronze Age (2100–1550 BCE), Canaanite city-states emerged. The Late Bronze Age saw Canaan as Egyptian vassals until a civilizational collapse (c. 1200 BCE) ended Egyptian control, paving the way for the Iron Age.

### Iron Age: Israelites and Kingdoms
The earliest Israelites appeared in the highlands during Iron Age I, likely branching from Canaanite peoples with a distinct monotheistic religion centered on Yahweh. By the 10th century BCE, the kingdoms of Israel (north) and Judah (south) formed. Israel was more powerful but fell to Assyria in 720 BCE, leading to the "Ten Lost Tribes" concept. Judah survived until 587/586 BCE, when Babylonians destroyed the First Temple and exiled the population, creating the Babylonian diaspora.

### Second Temple Period
Persian rule (538–332 BCE) allowed Jewish return and Temple rebuilding. Hellenistic rule (333–64 BCE) led to the Maccabean revolt and Hasmonean independence. Roman conquest (63 BCE) installed the Herodian dynasty. Jewish–Roman wars (66–135 CE) culminated in the Second Temple's destruction (70 CE) and the Bar Kokhba revolt, ending Jewish political autonomy and shifting the demographic center to Galilee and the diaspora.

### Byzantine to Early Muslim Period
Byzantine rule (4th–7th centuries) saw Christianity become dominant, with Jews losing majority status. The 7th-century Muslim conquest introduced Islamic caliphates, building the Dome of the Rock. Demographics shifted through Islamization, urban decline, and migration, with the Muslim population growing steadily.

### Crusades to Ottoman Rule
The Crusades (1099–1291) established Christian states, later defeated by Saladin. Mamluk rule (1258–1516) followed, destroying coastal cities to prevent Crusader return. The Ottoman Empire ruled from 1516, with Jewish immigration continuing, especially after the 1492 Spanish expulsion.

### Zionism and British Mandate
19th-century Zionism drove Jewish immigration (aliyah). After World War I, Britain governed under the Mandate, issuing the Balfour Declaration (1917). Tensions led to the 1947 UN Partition Plan and civil war. Britain's 1939 White Paper restricted Jewish immigration during the Holocaust, fueling illegal immigration and insurgency.

### State of Israel
Israel declared independence in 1948, sparking the Arab–Israeli War and Palestinian displacement. Subsequent wars (1967, 1973) led to occupation of territories. Peace treaties with Egypt (1979) and Jordan (1994), and the Oslo Accords (1993), have not resolved the conflict.

## Terms
- **Aliyah**: Jewish immigration to the Land of Israel.
- **Hasmonean dynasty**: Independent Jewish dynasty (164–63 BCE) after the Maccabean revolt.
- **Mandate for Palestine**: League of Nations mandate (1923–1948) administered by Britain.
- **Balfour Declaration**: 1917 British statement supporting a Jewish national home in Palestine.
- **UN Partition Plan (Resolution 181)**: 1947 UN proposal to divide Palestine into Jewish and Arab states.
- **Oslo I Accord**: 1993 agreement establishing the Palestinian Authority.
- **Yishuv**: The Jewish community in Palestine before 1948.
- **Haganah**: Main Jewish paramilitary organization in Mandate Palestine.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The existence and scale of the United Monarchy of Saul, David, and Solomon are debated between archaeological maximalists and minimalists.
- The causes of the demographic decline and Islamization after the Muslim conquest are analyzed through migration, conversion, and economic factors.
- The interpretation of the 1948 Palestinian displacement ( Nakba) remains contested, involving war events, voluntary flight, and expulsion.
- The ultimate resolution of the Israeli-Palestinian conflict, including borders, refugees, and Jerusalem, is unresolved.

# Medan

## Overview
Medan is the capital of North Sumatra, Indonesia, and the largest city on the island of Sumatra. It serves as a major economic, financial, and transportation hub for western Indonesia, with its growth historically tied to the colonial-era plantation economy and its strategic location near the Strait of Malacca.

## Timeline
- **c. 13th-16th century** — The area is part of the Kingdom of Aru, founded by the Karo people.
- **16th century** — Guru Patimpus founds Medan village at the confluence of the Deli and Babura rivers.
- **1632** — The Aceh Sultanate expands to include Medan.
- **1669** — The Deli Sultanate, including Medan, is declared independent from Aceh.
- **1860s-1870s** — Dutch merchants establish tobacco plantations, founding the Deli Company and transforming Medan into an economic hub.
- **1873-1924** — Sultan Ma'mun Al Rashid Perkasa Alamyah moves the capital to Medan and builds the Maimun Palace and Great Mosque.
- **1942** — Japanese forces occupy Medan during World War II.
- **1945** — Indonesia declares independence; Medan becomes part of the new republic.
- **1946-1947** — The Battle of Medan occurs between Allied and Indonesian forces.
- **1947** — The Dutch establish the State of East Sumatra with Medan as its capital.
- **1950** — The State of East Sumatra is dissolved into the Republic of Indonesia.
- **1998** — Riots occur in Medan following a student's death during protests against the Asian financial crisis.
- **2005** — Mandala Airlines Flight 091 crashes shortly after takeoff from Medan's old airport.
- **2013** — Kualanamu International Airport opens, replacing the old Polonia Airport.

## Body

### Etymology and Founding
The name "Medan" may derive from the Batak Karo word *madan*, meaning 'healed' or 'blessed', associated with the city's founder, Guru Patimpus. Another theory suggests a Malay origin meaning 'field'. The city was founded by Guru Patimpus, a Karonese man who converted to Islam, and it became part of the Deli Sultanate in 1632.

### Colonial Development and Economy
In the late 19th century, Dutch entrepreneurs, attracted by the region's potential, established large-scale tobacco plantations. The founding of the Deli Company and the opening of the Suez Canal in 1869 made Medan a global center for the tobacco trade, earning it the nickname "Het Land Dollar." The Deli Railway was built to transport commodities like tobacco, rubber, and palm oil to the Port of Belawan for export. This plantation economy drove rapid urbanization and infrastructure development.

### Modern History and Governance
After Japanese occupation in World War II, Medan became part of independent Indonesia. The city was the capital of the short-lived State of East Sumatra (1947-1950) before being incorporated into the republic. Governance has been marked by frequent changes in leadership, with several mayors, including Abdillah, Rahudman Harahap, and Dzulmi Eldin, arrested for corruption. The current mayor is Bobby Nasution.

### Demographics and Culture
Medan is a multicultural metropolis. Its dominant ethnic groups are Batak (including Karo, Toba, and Mandailing subgroups) and Javanese, with significant Malay, Chinese, Minangkabau, Acehnese, and Indian communities. This diversity is reflected in the city's languages, religions, and cuisine. The local Malay-based dialect, "Bahasa Medan," incorporates loanwords from these languages. The population is predominantly Muslim, with substantial Christian and Buddhist minorities.

### Economy and Infrastructure
As a National Strategic Region, Medan's economy is based on trade, agriculture (especially palm oil), and a growing manufacturing sector. It is a major international trade hub for western Indonesia. The city is served by Kualanamu International Airport (with a direct rail link), the Port of Belawan (one of Indonesia's largest container ports), and a network of roads and railways. Public transport includes the *sudako* (share taxis) and bus rapid transit systems.

## Terms
- **Deli Sultanate:** A Malay sultanate in eastern Sumatra, established in 1632, under whose rule Medan developed.
- **Deli Company (De Deli Maatschappij):** A Dutch-founded company that dominated the tobacco and plantation economy in the Deli region in the late 19th century.
- **Port of Belawan:** The main seaport for Medan, originally built for tobacco exports and now a major container terminal.
- **Kualanamu International Airport:** Medan's current airport, opened in 2013, featuring Indonesia's first direct airport rail link.
- **Sudako:** Medan's system of share taxis, minibuses that follow fixed routes.
- **Bahasa Medan:** The local Malay-based slang dialect of Indonesian spoken in Medan, incorporating words from various ethnic languages.
- **Batak:** An ethnic group native to North Sumatra, with several subgroups (Karo, Toba, Mandailing) prominent in Medan.
- **Maimun Palace:** The royal palace of the Deli Sultanate, built in 1888-1891, a major city landmark.

## Debates and Open Questions
The etymology of "Medan" is debated, with competing theories linking it to the Batak Karo word for "healed" or the Malay word for "field." The city's history also involves complex political transitions, such as the brief establishment of the State of East Sumatra by the Dutch, which is a point of historical study regarding post-colonial nation-building.

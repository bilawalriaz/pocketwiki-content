# Khmer Empire

## Overview
The Khmer Empire, known as Kambuja to its inhabitants, was a powerful state in mainland Southeast Asia that lasted from 802 to 1431 AD. Centered on the hydraulic cities of the Angkor region in modern Cambodia, it ruled or vassalized much of the mainland and is renowned for its monumental architecture, complex water management, and profound cultural influence. Its history, known as the Angkor period, represents the zenith of classical Khmer civilization.

## Timeline
- **c. 781** — Jayavarman II establishes Indrapura as his capital.
- **802** — Jayavarman II declares himself *chakravartin* on Phnom Kulen, marking the conventional start of the Khmer Empire.
- **889** — Yasovarman I establishes the first major Angkor capital, Yasodharapura.
- **1113–1150** — Reign of Suryavarman II; construction of Angkor Wat.
- **1177** — Champa raids and sacks Angkor.
- **1181–1219** — Reign of Jayavarman VII; construction of Angkor Thom and the Bayon.
- **1243–1295** — Reign of Jayavarman VIII; pays tribute to the Mongol Yuan dynasty.
- **1296–1297** — Chinese diplomat Zhou Daguan visits and documents life in Angkor.
- **1352–1353** — Ayutthaya besieges and captures Angkor for the first time.
- **1431** — Angkor is abandoned as the capital moves to the Phnom Penh area.

## Body

### Formation and Early Growth
The empire was founded by Jayavarman II, who unified former Chenla territories and declared independence from a place called "Java" (debated as the island of Java or Champa). He established the *devaraja* (god-king) cult, which legitimized royal authority and enabled massive building projects. His successors, like Indravarman I and Yasovarman I, expanded the territory and developed the Angkor region's core infrastructure, including the first major temple-mountains and vast water reservoirs (*barays*).

### Golden Age and Zenith
The 12th century saw both conflict and peak achievement. Suryavarman II built Angkor Wat, the empire's most famous monument. After a period of instability and a Cham raid on the capital, Jayavarman VII (r. 1181–1219) restored Khmer power, conquering Champa and initiating a vast building program that included the new capital Angkor Thom, the Bayon temple, and a network of roads and hospitals. At its peak in the 13th century, the Greater Angkor region had a population of 700,000–900,000, making it one of the world's largest pre-industrial urban complexes.

### Religion and Society
The state religion was initially Hinduism (worshipping Vishnu and Shiva), with the king as a living god. This shifted over time, with Mahayana Buddhism gaining influence under Jayavarman VII. By the 13th–14th centuries, Theravada Buddhism, introduced from Sri Lanka, became dominant among all social classes. Society was hierarchical, based on a Hindu caste model, with rice farming as the economic foundation, supported by an intricate irrigation system managed by the state. Women played a significant role in trade and the economy.

### Decline and Fall
The empire's decline from the 14th century was multifaceted. Constant internal power struggles weakened central authority. The adoption of Theravada Buddhism may have eroded the *devaraja* concept and the labor mobilization it supported. The elaborate water management system suffered from ecological strain, including deforestation, sedimentation, and climatic extremes of drought and flood, reducing agricultural productivity. External pressure intensified from the rising Siamese Ayutthaya Kingdom, which besieged Angkor multiple times. In 1431, King Ponhea Yat abandoned Angkor, moving the capital southwest to the Phnom Penh region.

## Terms
- **Chakravartin**: A Sanskrit title meaning "universal ruler," equivalent to emperor, used to assert supreme authority.
- **Devaraja**: The "god-king" cult, which deified the Khmer monarch as an incarnation of a Hindu deity (like Vishnu or Shiva), justifying their rule.
- **Baray**: A massive, rectangular artificial reservoir used for water management, irrigation, and symbolic purposes.
- **Temple-mountain**: A stepped pyramid temple structure representing Mount Meru, the cosmic home of the gods in Hinduism.
- **Sampot**: A traditional Khmer garment worn by both men and women, consisting of a cloth wrapped around the waist and legs.
- **Prahok**: A fermented fish paste, a staple protein source in the Khmer diet.
- **Theravada Buddhism**: A school of Buddhism emphasizing monastic discipline and individual enlightenment, which became the dominant religion in Cambodia by the 14th century.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The identity of "Java" in Jayavarman II's independence declaration is debated: it could refer to the Indonesian island of Java, the kingdom of Champa, or another location.
- The precise causes of the empire's collapse are contested, with theories ranging from ecological breakdown and infrastructural failure to religious conversion, internal strife, and foreign invasion.
- The extent to which the shift to Theravada Buddhism directly caused a decline in royal authority and state capacity is a subject of scholarly discussion.

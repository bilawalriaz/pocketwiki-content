# Algerian War

## Overview
The Algerian War (1954–1962) was a brutal decolonization conflict between France and the Algerian National Liberation Front (FLN) that resulted in Algerian independence. It was a pivotal event that caused the collapse of the French Fourth Republic, exposed the use of systematic torture by French forces, and led to a massive exodus of European settlers (Pieds-Noirs) and pro-French Algerians (Harkis) to France.

## Timeline
- **1 Nov 1954** — FLN launches coordinated attacks, beginning the war (Toussaint Rouge).
- **Aug 1955** — Philippeville massacre by FLN leads to severe French reprisals and full-scale war.
- **Oct 1956** — French intercept plane carrying FLN leaders (Ben Bella), imprisoning them.
- **1957** — Battle of Algiers; French military victory but revelations of torture damage French morale.
- **13 May 1958** — Army junta seizes power in Algiers, precipitating the fall of the Fourth Republic.
- **1959** — De Gaulle introduces "self-determination" as official French policy.
- **Jan 1960** — "Week of Barricades" in Algiers by pro-French Algeria extremists.
- **22 Apr 1961** — Failed generals' putsch against de Gaulle's negotiations.
- **18 Mar 1962** — Évian Accords signed, establishing a ceasefire.
- **1 Jul 1962** — Algerian referendum votes overwhelmingly for independence.
- **3 Jul 1962** — Algeria declares independence.

## Body

### Background and Nationalism
Algeria was conquered by France starting in 1830 and was legally considered an integral part of France, not a colony. A system of discrimination existed under the *Code de l'indigénat*, while the 1870 Crémieux Decree granted French citizenship to Algerian Jews. After World War II, Algerian nationalism grew, led by figures like Ferhat Abbas and Messali Hadj. The FLN, formed in 1954, adopted an armed struggle strategy inspired by Mao Zedong and Ho Chi Minh, aiming to erode France's political will through guerrilla warfare and terrorism.

### Course of the War
The FLN began with rural guerrilla tactics, but after the 1955 Philippeville massacre, the conflict escalated into a full-scale war. The French army, eventually numbering over 400,000 troops, responded with massive counter-insurgency operations, including the use of helicopters, napalm, and the forced relocation of over 2 million Algerians into regroupment camps. The 1957 Battle of Algiers saw the FLN bring the war to the cities, but French paratroopers under General Massu crushed the urban network, albeit through methods including widespread torture, which later caused a major political scandal in France.

### Political Crisis in France and Internationalization
The war destabilized France, leading to the fall of the Fourth Republic in 1958 and the return of Charles de Gaulle. De Gaulle initially promised "French Algeria" but gradually shifted toward self-determination, alienating the army and the Pieds-Noirs. The FLN successfully internationalized the conflict, gaining support from the Third World and communist bloc at the UN, while France became increasingly isolated. Extremist French settlers formed the Secret Army Organization (OAS) to sabotage peace talks through terrorism.

### End of the War and Exodus
After failed coups and negotiations, the Évian Accords were signed in March 1962, granting independence. A subsequent referendum in Algeria approved it with 99.72% in favor. The war triggered the exodus of about 900,000 Pieds-Noirs to France within months. Approximately 90,000 Harkis (Muslim Algerians who served as auxiliaries in the French army) also fled, while an estimated 50,000 to 150,000 who remained were killed in reprisals.

## Terms
- **FLN (Front de Libération Nationale):** The primary Algerian nationalist movement and political party that led the armed struggle for independence.
- **Pieds-Noirs:** Literally "black feet," the term for European-descended settlers (including Algerian Jews) who lived in Algeria.
- **Harkis:** Muslim Algerians who served as auxiliary soldiers in the French Army during the war.
- **OAS (Organisation armée secrète):** A far-right French paramilitary group formed in 1961 to oppose Algerian independence through terrorism.
- **Quadrillage:** A French counter-insurgency tactic of dividing the country into a grid of sectors, each permanently garrisoned to suppress rebel activity.
- **Regroupment camps:** Fortified villages where the French forcibly relocated over 2 million rural Algerians to isolate them from the FLN.
- **Évian Accords:** The peace agreement signed in March 1962 that ended the war and granted Algeria independence.
- **Toussaint Rouge:** "Red All Saints' Day," the name for the coordinated FLN attacks that began the war on 1 November 1954.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Death Toll:** Estimates vary widely. Algerian sources cite up to 1.5 million Algerian deaths, while French official figures are around 300,000-400,000. The true number remains contested.
- **Use of Torture:** France officially acknowledged in 2018 that torture was systematic and routine, but for decades it was denied or downplayed as isolated acts. The debate over its scale and justification continues.
- **Historical Memory:** The war was known as "la guerre sans nom" (the war without a name) in France for decades. There is still no consensus on commemoration, and memories remain fragmented between French and Algerian perspectives.
- **Legacy of Violence:** Scholars debate how the FLN's use of authoritarian methods and violence during the war shaped the post-independence Algerian state and contributed to the civil war of the 1990s.

# Skanderbeg

## Overview
Gjergj Kastrioti, known as Skanderbeg (c. 1405–1468), was an Albanian nobleman and military commander who led a 25-year resistance against the Ottoman Empire. He is considered Albania's national hero and a major figure in medieval European history, as his campaigns significantly delayed Ottoman expansion into Western Europe.

## Timeline
- **c. 1405** — Born to nobleman Gjon Kastrioti and Voisava in Albania.
- **1423** — Sent as a hostage to the Ottoman court; trained at Enderun School.
- **1440** — Appointed Ottoman sanjakbey (governor) of the Sanjak of Dibra.
- **Nov 1443** — Deserted the Ottoman army during the Battle of Niš and seized Krujë.
- **Mar 1444** — Established the League of Lezhë, uniting Albanian nobles against the Ottomans.
- **1447–1448** — Fought a war with the Republic of Venice over territorial disputes.
- **1450** — Successfully defended Krujë during its first major Ottoman siege.
- **1451** — Signed the Treaty of Gaeta, becoming a formal vassal of the Kingdom of Naples.
- **1460–1461** — Led an expedition to Italy to support King Ferdinand I of Naples.
- **1463** — Earmarked as commander for a papal crusade that never materialized; began fighting alongside Venice in the First Ottoman–Venetian War.
- **1467** — Broke the second Ottoman siege of Krujë.
- **17 Jan 1468** — Died in Lezhë, likely from malaria or poisoning.

## Body

### Early Life and Ottoman Service
Skanderbeg was born into the noble Kastrioti family. His father, Gjon Kastrioti, was a regional lord who paid tribute to the Ottomans. Around 1423, Skanderbeg was sent as a hostage to the Ottoman court, where he was educated at the elite Enderun School and entered the service of Sultan Murad II. He rose through the military ranks, earning the title *sipahi* (cavalry officer) and eventually becoming the Ottoman governor (*sanjakbey*) of Dibra in 1440. The Ottomans gave him the name "İskender bey" (Lord Alexander), which became Skanderbeg.

### Rebellion and the League of Lezhë
In November 1443, Skanderbeg deserted the Ottoman army during the Battle of Niš. He seized the fortress of Krujë using a forged letter from the Sultan and reverted to Christianity. In March 1444, he convened a council of Albanian nobles at Lezhë, forming the League of Lezhë—a military alliance that united the principalities of Albania under his leadership. This marked the beginning of a prolonged guerrilla war against the Ottomans.

### Military Campaigns and Alliances
Skanderbeg's forces, typically numbering 10,000–15,000 men, used mobile tactics and mountainous terrain to defeat consistently larger Ottoman armies in battles like Torvioll (1444) and Albulena (1457). His resistance was funded through a mix of local resources, Ottoman booty, and foreign aid. In 1451, he signed the Treaty of Gaeta, recognizing the de jure sovereignty of the Kingdom of Naples in exchange for military and financial support, though he remained the de facto ruler of his domains. He also fought a war with Venice (1447–48) and later allied with them against the Ottomans from 1463.

### Final Years and Death
Skanderbeg's later years were marked by repeated Ottoman sieges of Krujë (1450, 1466–67) and internal strife. He maintained alliances with Naples and the Papacy, who saw him as a champion of Christian resistance. In 1467, he broke the second siege of Krujë but was unable to capture the new Ottoman fortress at Elbasan. He died in January 1468 after calling a council of nobles to reorganize the League of Lezhë.

## Terms
- **Sanjakbey**: An Ottoman provincial governor.
- **League of Lezhë**: A 1444 military alliance of Albanian nobles led by Skanderbeg against the Ottomans.
- **Devşirme**: The Ottoman system of recruiting Christian boys, converting them to Islam, and training them for state service.
- **Timar**: A grant of land revenue given to an Ottoman soldier or official in exchange for military service.
- **Treaty of Gaeta (1451)**: An agreement where Skanderbeg recognized the sovereignty of the Kingdom of Naples over his lands in exchange for protection and subsidies.
- **Guerrilla warfare**: A form of irregular warfare where small, mobile forces use tactics like ambushes and raids against larger, conventional armies.
- **Arbëresh**: The Italo-Albanian communities formed by refugees who fled to Southern Italy after the Ottoman conquest of Albania.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Birthplace and early life**: The exact birthplace of Skanderbeg is debated among historians, with several villages claimed.
- **Mother's origin**: His mother Voisava's ethnic background is disputed, with theories linking her to Serbian, Bulgarian, or Albanian noble families.
- **Role in the Battle of Kosovo (1448)**: Historians disagree on why Skanderbeg did not join the Hungarian-led crusade at Kosovo Polje, with some blaming the Serbian despot Đurađ Branković.
- **Nature of the Treaty of Gaeta**: Scholars debate whether the treaty made Skanderbeg a true vassal or if he retained full independence, with evidence suggesting he remained the de facto ruler.
- **Cause of death**: While malaria is the likely cause, rumors of poisoning by Venetian agents persist.

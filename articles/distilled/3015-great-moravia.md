# Great Moravia

## Overview
Great Moravia was the first major West Slavic state, emerging in Central Europe in the early 9th century. It is historically significant as the cradle of Slavic literary culture and the site of the pivotal mission of Saints Cyril and Methodius, who introduced the Glagolitic alphabet and Old Church Slavonic liturgy. Its political and cultural legacy influenced the development of neighboring Slavic nations and the Kingdom of Hungary.

## Timeline
- **c. 830s** — Prince Mojmír I unites Moravian tribes and annexes the Principality of Nitra.
- **846** — East Frankish King Louis the German installs Rastislav as duke of Moravia.
- **863** — Byzantine Emperor Michael III sends Saints Cyril and Methodius to Moravia at Rastislav's request.
- **870** — Svatopluk I, with Frankish help, seizes power from his uncle Rastislav.
- **874** — Peace of Forchheim: Svatopluk acknowledges Frankish suzerainty and pays tribute.
- **880** — Pope John VIII issues the bull *Industriae tuae*, confirming Methodius's authority and Old Church Slavonic as a liturgical language.
- **885** — Death of Methodius; his disciples are later expelled from Moravia by Svatopluk I.
- **894** — Death of Svatopluk I; his empire begins to disintegrate.
- **c. 902–907** — Great Moravia collapses and is overrun by the Hungarians.

## Body

### Origins and Formation
Great Moravia arose from Slavic tribes settled north of the Danube, following the collapse of the Avar Khaganate in the late 8th century. The first certain reference to Moravians is in 822. The state's formation is linked to Prince Mojmír I, who ruled from the 820s/830s and expanded by annexing the Principality of Nitra in the early 830s. The core territory was centered on the Morava River valley in present-day Czech Republic and Slovakia.

### Struggle for Independence and the Cyrillo-Methodian Mission
Under Rastislav (r. 846–870), Moravia fought for independence from East Francia. To counter Frankish political and ecclesiastical influence, Rastislav requested a "teacher" from Byzantine Emperor Michael III. In 863, the brothers Cyril and Methodius arrived, creating the Glagolitic alphabet and translating liturgical texts into Old Church Slavonic. This mission established the first Slavic literary culture. Despite Frankish military pressure, Rastislav consolidated his realm.

### The Reign of Svatopluk I
Svatopluk I (r. 870–894) allied with the Franks to overthrow Rastislav but later drove them out, achieving significant autonomy. He expanded Moravia to its greatest territorial extent, possibly including parts of Poland, Pannonia, and Bohemia. His reign saw the peak of Moravian power but also internal tensions. He initially supported Methodius but later favored the Latin rite and expelled Methodius's disciples in 886, ending the Slavic literary boom in Moravia.

### Decline and Fall
After Svatopluk I's death in 894, internal conflicts between his sons and the secession of subjugated tribes (like the Bohemians) weakened the state. The Hungarians, migrating into the Carpathian Basin, became a major threat. They defeated Moravian forces and, by the early 10th century (between 902 and 907), overran the territory, ending the Great Moravian state.

### State, Society, and Economy
Great Moravia was an early feudal monarchy ruled by the House of Mojmir. It was organized around fortified centers like Mikulčice (a likely capital), Staré Město, and Nitra, which served administrative, military, and economic functions. The economy was based on agriculture, crafts (especially ironworking), and trade along routes like the Amber Road. Luxury goods were imported, and slave trading is documented. The population was West Slavic, with a social structure ranging from a princely elite and aristocracy to freemen and slaves.

### Culture and Legacy
Great Moravia saw the development of stone sacral architecture, including basilicas and rotundas, influenced by Frankish, Byzantine, and other styles. The Cyrillo-Methodian mission produced the first Slavic legal codes and literature. After the state's fall, its cultural legacy was carried by Methodius's disciples to Bulgaria, where the Cyrillic alphabet was developed, and to other Slavic regions. The territory was gradually integrated into the Kingdom of Hungary, though some local aristocracy may have persisted. Great Moravia later became a symbol of national identity for Czechs and Slovaks.

## Terms
- **Glagolitic alphabet**: The first alphabet created for a Slavic language, devised by Saint Cyril in the 9th century.
- **Old Church Slavonic**: The first literary Slavic language, used by Cyril and Methodius for religious translations.
- **Suzerainty**: A relationship where a ruler (the suzerain) has control over the foreign affairs of a subordinate state, which retains internal autonomy.
- **Appanage (údelné kniežatsvo)**: A territory given to and ruled by the heir to the throne, often the ruler's sister's son.
- **Družina**: The princely retinue or standing army of professional warriors in early Slavic states.
- **Optimates/Primates**: The leading men or aristocracy in Great Moravian society.
- **Veligrad**: The historical name for the capital of Great Moravia, whose exact location is unidentified but often associated with Mikulčice.
- **Blatnica-Mikulčice horizon**: An archaeological culture of metalwork from the 9th century, showing a synthesis of Late Avar and Carolingian artistic styles.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Location and Extent**: The exact core territory and borders of Great Moravia are debated. The traditional view places it in the Morava River valley, but alternative theories locate its core further south, near the Danube in present-day Serbia.
- **Name**: The meaning of "Great" in "Great Moravia" is debated; it may mean "old," refer to a territory beyond Byzantium, or follow a convention for nomadic homelands.
- **Political Structure**: Scholars disagree on whether Moravia was a unified state or a dualistic entity with separate "Realms of Rastislav" and "Svatopluk."
- **Christianization**: The stages and primary influences (Frankish vs. Byzantine) of the initial Christianization of Moravia before 863 are still researched.
- **Fate of the Aristocracy**: It is unclear what happened to the Great Moravian nobility after the state's fall—whether they were displaced, assimilated into the Hungarian nobility, or persisted locally.

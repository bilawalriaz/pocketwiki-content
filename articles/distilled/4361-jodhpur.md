# Jodhpur

## Overview
Jodhpur is the second-largest city in the Indian state of Rajasthan, serving as the historic capital of the Kingdom of Marwar. Founded in 1459, it is a major tourist destination known as the "Blue City" and is strategically located near the Thar Desert, making it a key base for military and trade.

## Timeline
- **1459** — Founded by Rao Jodha, a Rajput chief of the Rathore clan.
- **1581** — Kingdom annexed by Mughal Emperor Akbar after the death of Rao Chandrasen Rathore.
- **c. 1679** — State briefly sequestrated by Mughal Emperor Aurangzeb.
- **1707** — Maharaja Ajit Singh restored to the throne, ending a 30-year Rathore rebellion.
- **1755** — Maratha leader Jai Appa Scindia killed near Nagaur by Jodhpur volunteers.
- **1857** — Major revolt by Rathore nobles led by Thakur Kushal Singh of Auwa, defeated by the British.
- **11 August 1947** — Maharaja Hanwant Singh signs the Instrument of Accession, merging Jodhpur state into India.
- **30 March 1949** — Becomes part of the newly formed state of Rajasthan.
- **21 June 1949** — Rajasthan High Court established in Jodhpur.
- **2021** — City boundaries expanded, increasing population to 1,663,000.

## Body

### Founding and Early History
Jodhpur was founded in 1459 by Rao Jodha, a Rathore Rajput chief, who conquered territory from the Delhi Sultanate to establish the Kingdom of Marwar. The city was strategically located on the trade route between Delhi and Gujarat, profiting from opium, copper, silk, and other goods. It succeeded the earlier capital of Mandore.

### Mughal and Maratha Periods
After 1581, Marwar became a Mughal vassal, benefiting from new art and architectural styles. Internal strife following the Mughal decline led to Maratha intervention in the 18th century. A notable event was the 1755 killing of Maratha leader Jai Appa Scindia by Jodhpur volunteers. The state's wealth was eventually dissipated, leading it to seek British protection.

### British Colonial and Post-Independence Era
During the British Raj, Jodhpur was the largest state in Rajputana by area. A major revolt occurred in 1857. After Indian independence in 1947, Maharaja Hanwant Singh, initially hesitant, was persuaded by Vallabhbhai Patel to accede to India. Jodhpur became part of Rajasthan in 1949.

### Geography and Climate
Jodhpur has a hot desert climate (Köppen BWh) with extreme temperatures, routinely exceeding 40 °C from March to June. The highest recorded temperature was 48.8 °C on 20 May 2016. Average rainfall is 362 mm, mostly from June to September, but it fluctuates greatly. The Jojari River flows through the urban area.

### Demographics and Economy
As of the 2011 census, the municipal population was 1,056,191. After a 2021 expansion, the city's population is 1,663,000. The economy contributes approximately $4 billion to Rajasthan's economy and is a center for the $200 million handicraft industry. It is also a major tourist destination and hosts a significant test preparation industry.

### Governance and Administration
Jodhpur is administered by two municipal corporations (North and South), each with 80 wards. The Jodhpur Development Authority (JDA) oversees urban development. The city is the seat of the Rajasthan High Court, established in 1949.

### Strategic Location and Transportation
Located about 250 km from the Pakistan border, Jodhpur is a key base for the Indian Army and Air Force, hosting the strategically vital South Western Air Command. It has well-established rail, road, and air networks. Jodhpur Airport is primarily a military airbase with civilian flights. The city is connected by three national highways and ten state highways.

### Culture and Tourism
Known as the "Blue City" for its old town's blue-painted buildings, Jodhpur's major attractions include Mehrangarh Fort, Umaid Bhawan Palace, and Jaswant Thada. Its cuisine features specialties like Mirchi Bada and Mawa Kachori. The city's historic locations have been featured in numerous films.

## Terms
- **Rajput**: A member of a Hindu warrior caste, historically ruling parts of northern India.
- **Instrument of Accession**: A legal document executed by Indian princely state rulers to accede to the Dominion of India upon independence.
- **Köppen BWh**: A climate classification for hot deserts, characterized by very high evapotranspiration.
- **Subsidiary Alliance**: A treaty system used by the British East India Company to maintain control over allied Indian states.
- **Thar Desert**: A large arid region in northwestern India and eastern Pakistan.
- **Marwari**: A community of merchants and traders from the Marwar region; also a language/dialect.
- **JDA (Jodhpur Development Authority)**: The agency responsible for urban planning and development in Jodhpur.
- **South Western Air Command (SWAC)**: A key operational command of the Indian Air Force, headquartered in Jodhpur.

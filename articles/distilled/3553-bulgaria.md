# Bulgaria

## Overview
Bulgaria is a Southeastern European country with a history spanning millennia, established as the First Bulgarian Empire in 681 AD. Its strategic Balkan location has made it a crossroads of civilizations. Today, it is a parliamentary republic and a member of the European Union and NATO.

## Timeline

- **AD 45** — The Roman Empire conquers the region.
- **681** — The First Bulgarian Empire is established by Khan Asparuh.
- **1018** — The First Bulgarian Empire is conquered by the Byzantine Empire.
- **1185** — A revolt establishes the Second Bulgarian Empire.
- **1396** — The Second Bulgarian Empire falls, beginning Ottoman rule.
- **1878** — The Treaty of San Stefano creates an autonomous Bulgarian principality.
- **1908** — Bulgaria declares full independence from the Ottoman Empire.
- **1946** — Bulgaria becomes a socialist republic under Soviet influence.
- **1989** — The Communist Party gives up its monopoly on power.
- **2007** — Bulgaria joins the European Union.
- **2026** — Bulgaria adopts the euro, joining the eurozone.

## Body

### Early History and Empires
The region has been inhabited since prehistory. After Roman conquest in AD 45, Slavs settled in the Balkans. In the late 7th century, Bulgars invaded and, with the Slavs, formed the First Bulgarian Empire, recognized in 681. This empire became a major medieval power, especially under Tsar Simeon the Great, and was instrumental in spreading the Cyrillic script and Orthodox Christianity. It was conquered by the Byzantine Empire in 1018.

A revolt in 1185 created the Second Bulgarian Empire, which peaked under Ivan Asen II. Internal strife and Ottoman conquest ended it by 1396. Ottoman rule lasted nearly five centuries, suppressing Bulgarian culture. National consciousness was preserved in monasteries and revived during the 18th-19th century National Awakening.

### The Third Bulgarian State
The Russo-Turkish War of 1877–78 liberated Bulgaria, but the Treaty of Berlin created a smaller principality, fueling irredentism. After WWII, Bulgaria became a Soviet-aligned socialist state. The Communist Party maintained power until the 1989 revolutions, after which Bulgaria transitioned to democracy. It joined NATO in 2004 and the EU in 2007.

### Politics and Government
Bulgaria is a centralized parliamentary republic. The prime minister is head of government, while the president's powers are limited. The 240-seat National Assembly is elected by proportional representation. The system is marked by instability and coalition governments. Freedom House notes deterioration in democratic governance since 2009, citing corruption and reduced media independence.

### Economy
Bulgaria has an advanced, high-income economy with a large private sector. It transitioned from a planned economy after 1989. Key sectors include services, manufacturing, mining, and agriculture. It is a major producer of lavender and rose oil. Despite growth, it has the EU's lowest average salary and struggles with corruption and a large informal economy. Bulgaria adopted the euro on 1 January 2026.

### Demographics and Society
Bulgaria faces a severe demographic crisis with negative population growth and an aging population. The population is 84.6% ethnic Bulgarian, with Turkish (8.4%) and Roma (4.4%) minorities. Bulgarian is the official language. Eastern Orthodox Christianity is practiced by about two-thirds of the population, followed by Islam (10.8%). The country has high gender equality but a weak healthcare system.

### Culture
Bulgarian culture blends Thracian, Slavic, and Ottoman influences. Folk traditions and rituals like *kukeri* are prominent. The country has nine UNESCO World Heritage Sites, including the Rila Monastery. The Cyrillic script was developed in the First Bulgarian Empire. Notable figures include writer Elias Canetti and artist Christo. Football is the most popular sport.

## Terms
*   **Autocephalous**: A self-governing church independent of external authority.
*   **COMECON**: The Soviet-led economic organization (1949–1991).
*   **Cyrillic script**: An alphabet developed in the First Bulgarian Empire.
*   **Devshirme**: The Ottoman practice of collecting Christian boys for state service.
*   **Irredentism**: Advocating annexation of territories with related ethnic groups.
*   **Lev**: Bulgaria's former currency, pegged to the euro until 2026.
*   **Millet**: Ottoman autonomous religious communities.
*   **National Revival**: The 18th-19th century Bulgarian cultural awakening.
*   **Roma**: An ethnic minority often facing social marginalization.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   The etymology of "Bulgar" is debated, with theories linking it to Proto-Turkic words.
*   The exact size of the Roma population is uncertain due to undercounting.
*   The long-term impact of the demographic crisis is a major concern.
*   The effectiveness of efforts to combat corruption remains critical.

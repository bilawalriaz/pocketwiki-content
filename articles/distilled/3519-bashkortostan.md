# Bashkortostan

## Overview
Bashkortostan is a republic of Russia located between the Volga River and the Ural Mountains. It is the most populous republic in Russia and holds significant economic importance due to its vast natural resources, particularly oil. Its history is marked by early autonomy, integration into the Russian state, and the reassertion of sovereignty following the Soviet Union's dissolution.

## Body

### History and Political Status
The territory has been inhabited since the Paleolithic, with permanent settlements established by the Bronze Age Abashevo culture. The Bashkir people are first described in the 10th century. After the fall of the Kazan Khanate, Bashkir tribes joined the Russian state in the mid-16th century, though this led to later rebellions over violated agreements. Following the 1917 Revolution, Bashkir leaders proclaimed autonomy, leading to the creation of the Bashkir ASSR in 1919—the first autonomous republic in the Russian SFSR. After the Soviet Union's collapse, Bashkortostan declared sovereignty in 1990 and signed autonomy compacts with Russia in 1992 and 1994. The 1994 agreement, which granted significant self-rule, was abolished by the Russian federal government in 2005. The republic's constitution defines it as a sovereign state within Russia.

### Geography and Natural Resources
Bashkortostan covers 143,600 km² and contains part of the southern Ural Mountains, with its highest point being Mount Yamantau (1,638 m). The republic is rich in natural resources, including major oil, natural gas, coal, and metal ore deposits. It is a leading oil-producing region in Russia. Over one-third of its territory is forested, and it contains numerous rivers and lakes. The Asselian Age of the Permian geological period is named after the Assel River in the republic.

### Economy
Bashkortostan has one of Russia's most developed regional economies. Its most important industry is chemical processing, and it produces about 26 million tons of oil annually, supplying a significant portion of Russia's gasoline and diesel fuel. The economy also includes a large agricultural sector, machine-building, and metallurgy. In 2016, its gross regional product was 1.34 trillion rubles. The republic has a positive trade balance and is noted for low economic risk.

### Demographics and Culture
As of the 2021 census, the population is 36.89% Russian, 31.01% Bashkir, and 23.82% Tatar. The Bashkir language is spoken by 23% of the population. Islam is the majority religion among Bashkirs and Tatars, while most Russians are Orthodox Christians. The republic has a rich cultural life with numerous theaters, museums, and folk festivals. The Bashkir School of Dance is internationally renowned, and ballet dancer Rudolf Nureyev began his career in Ufa.

## Terms
*   **Bashkir ASSR:** The Bashkir Autonomous Soviet Socialist Republic, the first autonomous republic in the Russian SFSR, formed in 1919.
*   **Shuro:** A council; the Bashkir Regional Shuro proclaimed national-territorial autonomy in 1917.
*   **Qoroltay:** A traditional convention or assembly of the Bashkir people.
*   **GRP (Gross Regional Product):** The total value of goods and services produced in a region; Bashkortostan's was 1.34 trillion rubles in 2016.
*   **Selsoviet:** A rural local government form in Russia and some other post-Soviet states.
*   **Maktabeh and Madrasah:** Traditional Islamic educational institutions; primary schools and higher learning institutions, respectively.
*   **Rodnovery:** A Slavic native faith or neopagan movement.
*   **Tengrism:** A Central Asian shamanistic and animistic religion.

## Debates and Open questions

The exact nature and extent of the republic's sovereignty within the Russian Federation remain a dynamic issue, highlighted by the unilateral abolition of the 1994 autonomy compact.

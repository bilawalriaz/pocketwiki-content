# Hungary

## Overview
Hungary is a landlocked country in Central Europe, spanning much of the Carpathian Basin. Its history is marked by a medieval Christian kingdom, centuries of Ottoman and Habsburg rule, significant territorial losses after World War I, a communist era, and a modern transition to democracy. The country is known for its unique language, cultural contributions, and a recent period of democratic backsliding under Viktor Orbán, followed by a political shift in 2026.

## Timeline
- **c. 895** — Hungarian tribes, led by Árpád, conquer the Carpathian Basin.
- **1000** — King Stephen I is crowned, establishing the Christian Kingdom of Hungary.
- **1526** — Defeat at the Battle of Mohács leads to Ottoman conquest and the tripartite division of Hungary.
- **1867** — Austro-Hungarian Compromise creates the dual monarchy of Austria-Hungary.
- **1920** — Treaty of Trianon establishes modern borders, resulting in severe territorial and population losses.
- **1949** — The Hungarian People's Republic is established as a Soviet satellite state.
- **1956** — The Hungarian Revolution is crushed by Soviet intervention.
- **1989** — Hungary peacefully transitions from communism to a democratic parliamentary republic.
- **2004** — Hungary joins the European Union.
- **2010** — Viktor Orbán and Fidesz win a parliamentary supermajority, beginning a period of democratic backsliding.
- **2026** — Péter Magyar and the Tisza Party win a supermajority, ending Fidesz's 16-year rule.

## Body

### Early History and the Medieval Kingdom
Before the Hungarian state, the territory was settled by Celts, Romans, Huns, Avars, and Slavs. The Hungarian state was founded in the late 9th century by the Magyar tribes under Grand Prince Árpád. His descendant, King Stephen I, was crowned in 1000, establishing a Christian kingdom integrated into Western Europe. The medieval kingdom reached its height under the Angevin and later Matthias Corvinus, but was devastated by the Mongol invasion of 1241–1242 and internal strife.

### Ottoman Rule and Habsburg Domination
The Kingdom of Hungary was defeated by the Ottoman Empire at the Battle of Mohács in 1526. For over 150 years, the territory was split between the Ottoman-controlled central region, the Habsburg-ruled Royal Hungary in the northwest, and the semi-independent Principality of Transylvania. The Ottomans were expelled by the early 18th century, and Hungary came under firm Habsburg control. This period saw significant demographic changes and a series of failed uprisings for independence.

### 19th Century to World War I
The 19th century was marked by reform and revolution. The failed Revolution of 1848–1849 was suppressed with Russian help. The Austro-Hungarian Compromise of 1867 created a dual monarchy, granting Hungary internal autonomy and leading to economic modernization. The empire was a major European power until its defeat in World War I.

### Interwar Period and World War II
The Treaty of Trianon (1920) stripped Hungary of 71% of its territory and a third of its ethnic Hungarian population. The interwar period saw political instability and the rise of a national-conservative regime under Regent Miklós Horthy. Hungary joined the Axis powers in World War II, suffering massive destruction and participating in the Holocaust. By war's end, the country was occupied by the Soviet Union.

### Communist Era and Transition to Democracy
From 1949, Hungary was a Soviet satellite state. The Stalinist Rákosi regime was followed by the 1956 Revolution, which was violently suppressed. Under János Kádár, the regime liberalized slightly, earning Hungary the nickname "the happiest barrack" in the Eastern Bloc. Economic stagnation in the 1980s led to a peaceful transition to democracy in 1989.

### Modern Hungary
Hungary joined NATO (1999) and the EU (2004). In 2010, Viktor Orbán's Fidesz party began a period of democratic backsliding, characterized as an "illiberal democracy." This era ended in 2026 with a decisive election victory for the opposition Tisza Party led by Péter Magyar.

## Terms
*   **Magyar:** The Hungarian name for the Hungarian people and language.
*   **Árpád dynasty:** The ruling dynasty of Hungary from the state's foundation until 1301.
*   **Treaty of Trianon (1920):** The peace treaty that defined Hungary's modern borders after World War I, resulting in major territorial losses.
*   **Austro-Hungarian Compromise (1867):** The agreement that created the dual monarchy of Austria-Hungary, granting Hungary internal self-government.
*   **ÁVH:** The Hungarian secret political police during the communist era, responsible for repression.
*   **Orbán era:** The period of Viktor Orbán's leadership (2010–2026), marked by democratic backsliding and illiberal policies.
*   **Tisza Party:** The political party led by Péter Magyar that won the 2026 parliamentary election.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   The exact number of ethnic Hungarians left outside Hungary's borders after the Treaty of Trianon is debated, with estimates ranging from 2 million to 5 million.
*   The extent of the Horthy government's complicity in the Holocaust remains a point of historical controversy.
*   The characterization of Hungary under Viktor Orbán—whether as an illiberal democracy, hybrid regime, or other form—is a subject of ongoing political and academic debate.

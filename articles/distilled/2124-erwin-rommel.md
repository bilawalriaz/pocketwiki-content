# Erwin Rommel

## Overview
Erwin Rommel (1891–1944) was a German field marshal whose tactical brilliance in World War II, particularly in North Africa, earned him the nickname "The Desert Fox." His career illustrates the complex relationship between military prowess and political allegiance in Nazi Germany, as he was both a celebrated commander for the regime and a figure implicated in the plot to assassinate Hitler. His legacy remains contested, shaped by wartime propaganda, post-war myth-making, and ongoing historical debate about his knowledge of Nazi crimes.

## Timeline
- **15 Nov 1891** — Born in Heidenheim, German Empire.
- **1912** — Commissioned as a lieutenant in the Imperial German Army.
- **1917** — Awarded the Pour le Mérite for actions at Caporetto in WWI.
- **1937** — Published his book *Infantry Attacks*.
- **10 Feb 1940** — Given command of the 7th Panzer Division.
- **12 Feb 1941** — Arrives in Tripoli to command the Afrika Korps.
- **22 Jun 1942** — Promoted to Generalfeldmarschall after the fall of Tobruk.
- **23 Oct-4 Nov 1942** — Commands Axis forces in the Second Battle of El Alamein.
- **17 Jul 1944** — Severely wounded in an Allied air strafing attack in Normandy.
- **14 Oct 1944** — Dies by suicide after being implicated in the 20 July plot.

## Body

### Early Career and World War I
Rommel served with distinction in World War I, earning the Pour le Mérite for his leadership at Caporetto (1917). He pioneered infiltration tactics, using surprise and rapid advances to outmaneuver larger forces. His experiences informed his 1937 book *Infantry Attacks*, which became a military classic.

### World War II: France and North Africa
In 1940, Rommel commanded the 7th Panzer Division ("Ghost Division") during the invasion of France, earning the Knight's Cross. In 1941, he was sent to North Africa, where his aggressive tactics against British forces earned him the nickname "Desert Fox." He captured Tobruk in June 1942 but was decisively defeated by Montgomery at El Alamein later that year. His retreat across Libya and a final defeat in Tunisia ended the Axis campaign in Africa.

### Italy, Normandy, and the Plot Against Hitler
After brief commands in Italy and Greece, Rommel was tasked with strengthening the Atlantic Wall in France. He argued for defeating the Allied invasion on the beaches, clashing with other German commanders. Gravely wounded on 17 July 1944, he was implicated in the 20 July plot to kill Hitler. Given the choice between suicide and a trial that would disgrace his family, he took cyanide on 14 October 1944.

### Military Style and Reputation
Rommel was a charismatic, front-line commander who valued surprise and mobility. His leadership inspired troops but sometimes led to overextension and strained supply lines. His reputation for chivalry and a "clean war" in North Africa was promoted by both Nazi and Allied propaganda, though its accuracy is debated.

### Relationship with Nazism and the "Rommel Myth"
Rommel was not a Nazi Party member but benefited from Hitler's patronage. Historians debate the depth of his political awareness and when, if ever, he fully broke with the regime. After the war, his image was rehabilitated in West Germany and among former Allies as the "good German," forming the core of the "Rommel myth"—a narrative of an apolitical, brilliant soldier victimized by Nazism.

### Conduct of War and Atrocities
Rommel's forces operated in a theatre with fewer civilians than the Eastern Front, contributing to a perception of a comparatively "clean" war. However, historians note that crimes against North African Jewish populations occurred in areas under his command. Evidence is unclear on his direct knowledge or involvement; he is known to have refused some illegal orders, like Hitler's Commando Order, but also issued harsh directives during the disarmament of Italian forces.

## Terms
*   **Generalfeldmarschall:** The highest military rank in the German Army, equivalent to Field Marshal.
*   **Afrika Korps (DAK):** The German expeditionary force in North Africa, commanded by Rommel from February 1941.
*   **Panzer Division:** An armoured division, the key offensive unit in Blitzkrieg tactics.
*   **Atlantic Wall:** An extensive system of coastal defences built by Nazi Germany along the coast of occupied Europe.
*   **20 July Plot (Operation Valkyrie):** The failed attempt by German military officers to assassinate Adolf Hitler on 20 July 1944.
*   **Pour le Mérite:** Prussia's highest military order, awarded to Rommel for WWI heroism.
*   **Knight's Cross of the Iron Cross:** Nazi Germany's highest military decoration at the time.
*   **Rommel Myth:** A term used by historians to describe the post-war portrayal of Rommel as an apolitical, chivalrous commander and victim of Nazism.
*   **Infiltration Tactics:** A form of manoeuvre warfare using small, fast-moving units to penetrate enemy lines, a precursor to Blitzkrieg.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Extent of Political Awareness:** Did Rommel know about the Holocaust and the full criminal nature of the Nazi regime? Evidence is fragmentary and contradictory.
*   **Role in the 20 July Plot:** How deeply was Rommel involved in the conspiracy? Was he a passive supporter or an active participant planning to lead a post-Hitler government?
*   **The "Clean War" Myth:** Was the North African campaign truly a "war without hate," or did Rommel's forces commit or enable war crimes, particularly against Jewish populations and prisoners?
*   **Military Genius or Overrated?:** Was Rommel a strategic genius, or primarily a gifted tactician whose operational and strategic shortcomings contributed to Germany's defeat in Africa?
*   **The "Rommel Myth":** To what degree has his historical reputation been shaped by post-war political needs for West German rearmament and reconciliation rather than a balanced assessment of his career?

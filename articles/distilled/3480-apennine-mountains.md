# Apennine Mountains

## Overview
The Apennines are a 1,200 km mountain range forming the backbone of peninsular Italy, extending from the Ligurian Alps in the northwest to Reggio di Calabria in the southwest. They are a geologically young and active system, crucial for understanding Mediterranean tectonics, and host significant biodiversity, including refuges for large predators like the Italian wolf and Marsican brown bear.

## Body

### Etymology and History
The name "Apennines" likely derives from the Celtic *penn* ("mountain"), possibly assigned during Celtic domination in the 4th century BC, though its etymology remains debated. The "Apennine culture" was a Bronze Age (15th–14th centuries BC) technology complex of alpine cattle herdsmen who lived in small hamlets and temporary camps. Their distinctive incised pottery has been found across central Italy and on islands like Ischia and Lipari, dating it to the Late Bronze Age via association with Aegean pottery.

### Geography and Subdivisions
The range is divided into three main sectors: Northern, Central, and Southern. The **Northern Apennines** include the Ligurian, Tuscan–Emilian, and Umbrian chains, with key passes like Bocchetta di Altare and Cisa. The **Central Apennines** are geologically complex, split into an inner and outer arc. They include the Umbria-Marche Apennines (home to Monte Vettore) and the rugged Abruzzi Apennines, which contain the highest peak, Corno Grande (2,912 m). The **Southern Apennines** break into smaller groups like the Samnite, Campanian, Lucan, and Calabrian Apennines, ending at the Aspromonte massif. The system officially extends to the Sicilian Apennines.

### Geology and Orogeny
The Apennines formed from the Apennine orogeny, which began about 20 million years ago and continues today. They are not part of the older Alpine system. Their formation is a paradoxical combination of simultaneous compression and extension. On the eastern side, the Adriatic lithosphere subducts under Italy, creating a fold-and-thrust belt (the "Apennine-Adriatic Compressional Zone"). On the western side, crustal stretching under the Tyrrhenian Sea creates fault-block mountains (the "Tyrrhenian Extensional Zone"). This dual process causes instability, with the eastern flanks being particularly prone to landslides.

### Environment and Ecology
The Apennines preserve intact ecosystems with high biodiversity. Vegetation zones range from Mediterranean forests to montane grasslands above the tree line (1,600–2,000 m). They are a last refuge for endemic species like the Italian wolf and Marsican brown bear. The fauna includes numerous endemic amphibians, reptiles, and fish. Historically, transhumance (seasonal livestock movement) was practiced from the Abruzzi to lowland pastures. The range is protected by twelve national parks, including Gran Sasso e Monti della Laga and Pollino.

## Terms
- **Apennine orogeny:** The mountain-building process that formed the Apennines, involving simultaneous compression and extension.
- **Transhumance:** The seasonal movement of livestock between mountain pastures and lowland valleys.
- **Endemic:** A species native and restricted to a certain place, like the Italian wolf.
- **Tree line ecotone:** The transitional zone between closed forest and open alpine grassland.
- **Fold-and-thrust belt:** A sequence of sedimentary rocks that have been deformed by compression into a series of folds and faults.
- **Fault-block mountains:** Mountains formed by the cracking and displacement of the Earth's crust along faults, creating raised blocks (horsts) and sunken blocks (grabens).
- **Subduction:** A geological process where one tectonic plate sinks beneath another into the mantle.
- **Landslide Index (LI):** The ratio of landslide area to total area in a region, used to measure terrain instability.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The precise etymology of "Apennines" is uncertain, with multiple linguistic derivations proposed but none universally accepted.
- The paradox of how compressional and extensional forces can act simultaneously to build the same mountain range is described as a "fundamental and largely unresolved problem in continental dynamics."
- The exact nature and boundaries of the "Adriatic plate" or lithosphere involved in the subduction are subjects of ongoing research.

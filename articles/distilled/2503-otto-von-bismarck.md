# Otto von Bismarck

## Overview
Otto von Bismarck (1815–1898) was a Prussian statesman who masterminded the unification of Germany in 1871 and served as its first Chancellor until 1890. His legacy is defined by his ruthless *Realpolitik*—a pragmatic approach to politics based on power and practical objectives rather than ideology—which secured German unity through "blood and iron." He then became the architect of a complex European alliance system that maintained peace for two decades, while also pioneering the world's first modern welfare state to undercut socialist opposition.

## Timeline
- **1815** — Born into a Junker family in Schönhausen, Prussia.
- **1862** — Appointed Minister President and Foreign Minister of Prussia.
- **1864** — Leads Prussia and Austria to victory over Denmark in the Second Schleswig War.
- **1866** — Defeats Austria in the Austro-Prussian War, dissolving the German Confederation.
- **1867** — Becomes Chancellor of the North German Confederation.
- **1870-71** — Orchestrates the Franco-Prussian War, leading to the proclamation of the German Empire.
- **1871** — Appointed first Imperial Chancellor of the German Empire.
- **1871-78** — Launches the *Kulturkampf* against Catholic Church influence.
- **1878-85** — Organizes the Berlin Conference, formalizing the Scramble for Africa.
- **1883-89** — Implements pioneering social insurance laws (sickness, accident, old-age).
- **1890** — Forced to resign by Kaiser Wilhelm II.

## Body

### Unification of Germany
Bismarck rose to power as Prussia's Minister President in 1862, resolving a constitutional crisis by governing without a parliamentary budget. He famously declared that the great questions of the day would be settled not by speeches but by "iron and blood." He engineered three short, decisive wars to achieve unification under Prussian leadership, deliberately excluding Austria. After defeating Denmark (1864) and Austria (1866), he provoked a war with France in 1870 by editing the Ems Dispatch to inflame nationalist sentiment on both sides. The resulting German victory led to the proclamation of the German Empire at Versailles in 1871, with Wilhelm I as Kaiser and Bismarck as Chancellor.

### Domestic Policy: *Kulturkampf* and Socialism
As Chancellor, Bismarck pursued two major domestic campaigns. First, he launched the *Kulturkampf* ("culture struggle") in the 1870s, a series of anti-Catholic laws aimed at reducing the influence of the Church and the new Centre Party. This backfired, strengthening Catholic political organization, and Bismarck abandoned it by 1878 to secure Centre Party support against his primary foe: the growing socialist movement. After the Anti-Socialist Laws (1878) failed to crush the Social Democrats, he pioneered the modern welfare state in the 1880s. This "state socialism" included sickness insurance (1883), accident insurance (1884), and old-age pensions (1889), designed to foster worker loyalty to the state and undermine socialist appeal.

### Foreign Policy and the Alliance System
After 1871, Bismarck shifted from war-maker to peacemaker, dedicating himself to maintaining Germany's security and the European balance of power. His primary goal was to diplomatically isolate France, which remained vengeful over the loss of Alsace-Lorraine. He constructed a complex web of alliances, including the League of the Three Emperors (with Austria-Hungary and Russia), the Dual Alliance (with Austria-Hungary), and the Triple Alliance (adding Italy). His masterpiece was the secret Reinsurance Treaty (1887) with Russia, which prevented a Franco-Russian encirclement of Germany. He skillfully managed colonial disputes, organizing the Berlin Conference (1884-85) to regulate the Scramble for Africa, though he personally viewed colonies as a costly distraction.

### Downfall
Bismarck's authority was undermined by the accession of the young, ambitious Kaiser Wilhelm II in 1888. Conflicts over foreign policy and social legislation escalated. The final break came in 1890 when Bismarck, seeking a new parliamentary majority, attempted to negotiate with the Catholic Centre Party without the Kaiser's knowledge. Wilhelm II, asserting his authority, demanded Bismarck's resignation, which he accepted on 18 March 1890. He spent his retirement writing his memoirs and died in 1898.

## Terms
*   **Realpolitik**: Politics based on practical and material factors rather than theoretical or ethical objectives.
*   **Junker**: A member of the Prussian landowning aristocracy, from which Bismarck originated.
*   **Kulturkampf**: The "culture struggle" of the 1870s, Bismarck's campaign against Catholic Church influence in Germany.
*   **Reichstag**: The parliament of the German Empire, which Bismarck often sidelined.
*   **Kartell**: A conservative-liberal parliamentary coalition Bismarck relied on in the 1880s.
*   **Weltpolitik**: The "world policy" of expansionism and global power pursued by Kaiser Wilhelm II after Bismarck's fall.
*   **Reinsurance Treaty**: A secret 1887 treaty between Germany and Russia, a key part of Bismarck's alliance system to isolate France.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Master Planner or Opportunist?** Historians debate whether Bismarck had a long-term master plan for German unification or was a brilliant opportunist who seized chances as they arose.
*   **Annexation of Alsace-Lorraine:** Scholars question whether Bismarck willingly annexed the territories to create a permanent buffer or was pressured into it by German military and public opinion.
*   **Colonial Motives:** The exact reasons for his sudden reversal and acquisition of colonies in the 1880s are debated, with theories ranging from domestic political maneuvering to responding to elite and popular pressure.
*   **Legacy for the 20th Century:** Historians like Henry Kissinger argue that Bismarck's undermining of liberalism and reliance on personal rule created a fragile political system that contributed to the rise of extremism after his death.

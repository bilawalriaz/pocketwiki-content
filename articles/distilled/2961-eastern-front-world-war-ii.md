# Eastern Front (World War II)

## Overview
The Eastern Front (1941–1945) was the largest and deadliest theatre of World War II, fought between Nazi Germany and its Axis allies against the Soviet Union and its allies. It was a war of annihilation driven by Nazi ideology, resulting in over 30 million deaths and ultimately determining the defeat of Nazi Germany.

## Timeline
- **22 June 1941** — Germany launches Operation Barbarossa, invading the Soviet Union.
- **Sept–Dec 1941** — German advance stalls at the Battle of Moscow; Soviet counter-offensive begins.
- **28 June 1942** — Germany launches Fall Blau (Case Blue) offensive towards the Caucasus and Stalingrad.
- **19 Nov 1942** — Soviet Operation Uranus encircles German 6th Army at Stalingrad.
- **2 Feb 1943** — German 6th Army surrenders at Stalingrad, a major turning point.
- **5 July 1943** — Battle of Kursk begins, the largest tank battle in history; German offensive capability is crippled.
- **22 June 1944** — Soviet Operation Bagration begins, destroying Germany's Army Group Center.
- **27 Aug 1944** — Romania defects to the Allies, switching sides.
- **16 Apr–2 May 1945** — Battle of Berlin; Soviet forces capture the city.
- **8 May 1945** — Germany signs unconditional surrender, ending the war in Europe.

## Body

### Background and Ideology
The conflict stemmed from Nazi Germany's ideological goal of acquiring *Lebensraum* (living space) in Eastern Europe. Hitler viewed the war as a racial struggle against "Jewish Bolshevism" and the Slavic *Untermenschen* (subhumans). The Molotov–Ribbentrop Pact (1939) was a temporary non-aggression agreement that secretly divided Eastern Europe, enabling the initial invasions of Poland and the Baltic states. Germany invaded the USSR on 22 June 1941, catching Stalin's forces largely unprepared despite warnings.

### The War of Annihilation (1941–1943)
The initial German *Blitzkrieg* made deep advances, encircling millions of Soviet troops. The war was characterized by extreme brutality, with Nazi policies like Generalplan Ost calling for the extermination and ethnic cleansing of over 100 million Eastern Europeans. Key turning points were the Soviet victories at the **Battle of Stalingrad** (1942–43), where the German 6th Army was destroyed, and the **Battle of Kursk** (1943), which permanently crippled German offensive power.

### Soviet Advance and Collapse of Germany (1943–1945)
Following Kursk, the Red Army launched continuous offensives westward. Operation Bagration (1944) shattered Army Group Center, leading to the liberation of Belarus and the defection of Axis allies like Romania and Bulgaria. The Soviets advanced through Poland, entered Germany, and captured Berlin in April–May 1945. The war in Europe ended with Germany's unconditional surrender on 8 May 1945.

### Foreign Support and Industrial Output
The Soviet war effort was critically aided by Western **Lend-Lease** supplies, which provided 58% of its high-octane aviation fuel, 93% of railway equipment, and significant food and raw materials. Soviet industrial output, relocated east of the Urals, outproduced Germany in tanks and aircraft by 1943. Germany exploited the resources of conquered Europe but was outmatched in total war production.

### Casualties and Atrocities
The Eastern Front was the deadliest conflict in history, with approximately 30 million deaths, including 9 million children. Soviet losses were catastrophic: an estimated 26.5 million war-related deaths, including 10.6 million military and 15.9 million civilian. German military deaths on the front were around 4 million. Atrocities were systematic, including the Holocaust, the murder of Soviet POWs (where 45–57% died in captivity), and scorched-earth policies by both sides.

## Terms
*   **Lebensraum**: Nazi ideology demanding territorial expansion into Eastern Europe for German settlement.
*   **Vernichtungskrieg**: "War of annihilation," the Nazi policy of total destruction and genocide in the East.
*   **Generalplan Ost**: The Nazi plan for the ethnic cleansing, deportation, and enslavement of Eastern European populations.
*   **Blitzkrieg**: "Lightning war," a tactic of rapid, concentrated armored and air attacks to encircle and destroy enemy forces.
*   **Lend-Lease**: A U.S. program providing Allied nations with war materials; crucial to sustaining the Soviet war effort.
*   **Stavka**: The Soviet high command during the war.
*   **Operation Bagration**: The massive 1944 Soviet offensive that destroyed Germany's Army Group Center.
*   **Untermenschen**: "Subhumans," a Nazi racial term for Slavic peoples, used to justify their enslavement and extermination.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Stalin's Preparedness**: Historians debate whether Stalin's refusal to believe invasion warnings was due to fear of provoking Hitler, a belief war would come later, or a combination of both.
*   **Impact of Lend-Lease**: The precise importance of Western aid is debated; while critical for logistics and high-tech materials, the Soviet Union bore the brunt of ground combat and industrial production.
*   **The "Clean Wehrmacht" Myth**: Scholarship has thoroughly debunked the notion that the regular German army was not complicit in Nazi atrocities; the Wehrmacht was deeply involved in war crimes and the Holocaust on the Eastern Front.

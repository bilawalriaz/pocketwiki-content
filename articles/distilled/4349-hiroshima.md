# Hiroshima

## Overview
Hiroshima is a major Japanese city and the capital of Hiroshima Prefecture. It is globally significant as the first target of a nuclear weapon, an event that defines its modern identity. Today, it is a large urban center and a symbol of peace, advocating for nuclear abolition.

## Timeline
- **1589** — Founded as a castle town by warlord Mōri Terumoto.
- **1868** — Meiji Restoration begins; Hiroshima transforms into an urban/industrial center.
- **1889** — Officially gains city status.
- **1894-95** — Serves as temporary government headquarters during the First Sino-Japanese War.
- **Aug 6, 1945** — Atomic bomb "Little Boy" is dropped by the United States.
- **Sep 17, 1945** — Devastated by the Makurazaki Typhoon.
- **1949** — Proclaimed a "City of Peace"; Hiroshima Peace Memorial City Construction Law passed.
- **1955** — Hiroshima Peace Memorial Museum opens.
- **1996** — Hiroshima Peace Memorial (A-Bomb Dome) designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
- **May 27, 2016** — First visit by a sitting U.S. president, Barack Obama.

## Body
### Early History and Feudal Period
The area was originally a fishing village. In 1589, warlord Mōri Terumoto established Hiroshima as a castle town on the Ōta River delta. After the Battle of Sekigahara, control passed to the Fukushima clan and, from 1619 to 1871, to the Asano clan.

### Imperial Era and Military Growth
Following the Meiji Restoration in 1868, Hiroshima rapidly industrialized. The construction of Ujina Harbor in the 1880s and the extension of the San'yō Railway in 1894 made it a key port and military hub. It played significant roles in the First Sino-Japanese War (1894-95), the Russo-Japanese War (1904), and World War I. By World War II, it housed major army headquarters and supply depots.

### The Atomic Bombing
On August 6, 1945, at 8:15 a.m., the U.S. B-29 *Enola Gay* dropped the atomic bomb "Little Boy" on Hiroshima. The blast directly killed at least 70,000 people. By the end of 1945, deaths from injury and radiation reached between 90,000 and 166,000. About 70% of the city's buildings were destroyed. Information about the attack was censored during the Allied occupation until 1951.

### Postwar Reconstruction and Peace
Hiroshima was rebuilt with national assistance, formalized by the 1949 Hiroshima Peace Memorial City Construction Law. The Hiroshima Peace Memorial Park was designed, and the preserved ruin of the Industrial Promotion Hall became the A-Bomb Dome. The city was proclaimed a "City of Peace" in 1949 and has since become a center for international peace conferences and advocacy for nuclear abolition.

## Terms
- **Daimyō**: A powerful feudal lord in pre-modern Japan.
- **Han**: A feudal domain in Japan, abolished during the Meiji Restoration.
- **Genbaku Dome**: The "Atomic Dome," the preserved ruin of the Hiroshima Prefectural Industrial Promotion Hall, part of the Peace Memorial.
- **Hiroden**: Hiroshima's extensive light rail and streetcar system, nicknamed the "Moving Streetcar Museum."
- **Okonomiyaki**: A savory pancake; Hiroshima-style is layered rather than mixed.
- **Peace Pagoda**: A Buddhist pagoda built in 1966 in Hiroshima, made of steel.
- **San'yō Railway**: A major railway line connecting Osaka and Shimonoseki, extended to Hiroshima in 1894.
- **Shinkansen**: Japan's high-speed rail network; the San'yō Shinkansen serves Hiroshima.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The exact death toll from the atomic bombing remains debated, with estimates ranging from 90,000 to 166,000 by the end of 1945.
- The decision to use the atomic bomb and its military necessity is a subject of ongoing historical analysis.
- The city's postwar identity is defined by the tension between its traumatic past and its present role as a global advocate for peace and nuclear disarmament.

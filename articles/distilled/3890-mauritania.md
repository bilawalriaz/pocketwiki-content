# Mauritania

## Overview
Mauritania is a large, predominantly desert country in West Africa's Maghreb region, straddling the Arab and African worlds. Its history is marked by ancient Berber and Arab-Islamic kingdoms, French colonialism, a post-independence struggle with ethnic tensions and military coups, and the persistent challenge of modern slavery. The economy relies on agriculture and fishing despite natural resource wealth, and the society is multiethnic with a dominant Arab-Berber Moorish culture.

## Timeline
- **c. 3000 BC** — Berber tribes occupy the region.
- **Late 7th century** — Arab tribes migrate, bringing Islam and Arabic culture.
- **11th century** — Almoravid movement forms, expanding an empire from the Sahara to Iberia.
- **1644–1674** — Char Bouba War: indigenous peoples unsuccessfully resist Maqil Arab invasion.
- **1901–1912** — France colonizes the territory, incorporating it into French West Africa.
- **1960** — Mauritania gains independence from France.
- **1976** — Mauritania annexes part of Western Sahara with Morocco.
- **1979** — Mauritania withdraws from Western Sahara after military losses.
- **1989–1991** — Mauritania–Senegal Border War leads to mass expulsions of sub-Saharan Mauritanians.
- **2005** — Military coup ends President Ould Taya's 21-year rule.
- **2008** — Military coup led by General Mohamed Ould Abdel Aziz.
- **2019** — Mohamed Ould Ghazouani elected; first peaceful transfer of power since independence.

## Body

### History and Politics
Mauritania's modern history began with French colonization in the early 20th century, which ended interclan warfare but also altered power balances. Independence in 1960 was followed by a one-party state under President Moktar Ould Daddah, who was ousted in a 1978 coup after a disastrous war over Western Sahara. The country experienced a series of military governments until 1991, when a new constitution ended formal military rule, though elections were often disputed.

A 2005 coup led to a brief democratic transition, but was reversed by another coup in 2008 led by General Mohamed Ould Abdel Aziz. He won subsequent presidential elections in 2009 and 2014. In 2019, General Mohamed Ould Ghazouani was elected in what was considered the country's first peaceful transition of power since independence. The political landscape has been shaped by recurrent coups, ethnic tensions, and the contentious issue of slavery.

### Society and Demographics
Mauritanian society is multiethnic. The Haratin (black Moors) comprise about 40% of the population, the Bidhan (white Moors) about 30%, and various sub-Saharan groups (like the Halpulaar, Soninke, and Wolof) the remaining 30%. Hassaniya Arabic is the dominant language, though Arabic, Pulaar, Soninke, and Wolof are national languages. The state religion is Islam, with nearly all inhabitants being Sunni Muslims.

A profound social issue is the perpetuation of descent-based slavery, primarily affecting the Haratin. Despite being officially abolished in 1905, 1981, and 2007, it persists. The 2018 Global Slavery Index estimated about 90,000 enslaved people (2.1% of the population). The country also has a poor human rights record, including restrictions on religious freedom and reports of torture.

### Economy and Geography
Mauritania is the 11th-largest country in Africa, with 90% of its territory in the Sahara Desert. Most of the population lives in the temperate south. The economy is based on agriculture and fishing, despite extensive iron ore deposits (accounting for nearly 50% of exports) and oil discovered in 2001. The country remains poor, with recurrent droughts and economic mismanagement contributing to foreign debt. A major recent issue is migration, with the EU signing a deal in 2024 to reduce passage of African migrants through Mauritania to the Canary Islands.

## Terms
*   **Bidhan (White Moors):** An ethnic group of mixed Arab-Berber descent, historically the dominant elite.
*   **Haratin (Black Moors):** A distinct ethnic group, often descendants of ancient Saharans, who are disproportionately affected by modern slavery.
*   **Hassaniya:** A Bedouin Arabic dialect named for the Beni Hassan tribe, it is the dominant language among the largely nomadic population.
*   **Almoravid movement:** A 11th-century nomadic Berber confederation that formed an empire stretching from the Sahara to the Iberian Peninsula.
*   **Char Bouba War (1644–1674):** An unsuccessful effort by indigenous peoples to repel invading Maqil Arabs, led by the Beni Hassan tribe.
*   **Polisario Front:** A Sahrawi nationalist movement seeking independence for Western Sahara, which fought Mauritania and Morocco.
*   **Leblouh:** A traditional practice of forced feeding to encourage weight gain in women, based on cultural beauty standards.
*   **T'heydinn:** A cycle of epic poems central to Moorish oral heritage and musical performance.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Prevalence of Slavery:** Estimates vary widely. While the 2018 Global Slavery Index estimated 90,000 enslaved people, a 2012 CNN report suggested 10-20% of the population (340,000-680,000), a figure considered by several academics to be grossly overstated. A government minister in 2012 stated slavery "no longer exists."
*   **Ethnic Tensions and National Identity:** The country's "prevailing Arab identity" exists alongside a multiethnic society. Efforts to "arabize" law and education have been a reaction to French colonialism but have also exacerbated tensions with southern-based ethnic groups. Models for maintaining cultural diversity have not been successfully implemented.
*   **Political Stability:** Despite the 2019 peaceful transition, Mauritania's history is marked by recurrent coups and military dictatorships. The long-term stability of its democratic institutions remains an open question.

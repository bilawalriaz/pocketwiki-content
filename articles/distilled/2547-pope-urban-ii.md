# Pope Urban II

## Overview
Pope Urban II (c. 1035–1099) was the head of the Catholic Church from 1088 to 1099. He is most significant for convening the Council of Clermont in 1095, where his speech calling for a military expedition to the Holy Land ignited the First Crusade. His papacy was defined by the struggle for authority against the antipope Clement III, the implementation of Gregorian reforms, and the mobilization of Christendom for a holy war.

## Timeline
- **c. 1035** — Born as Odo of Châtillon in France.
- **c. 1080** — Named cardinal-bishop of Ostia by Pope Gregory VII.
- **March 1088** — Elected pope at a meeting in Terracina.
- **March 1095** — Receives a plea for help from Byzantine Emperor Alexios I Komnenos at the Council of Piacenza.
- **November 1095** — Delivers his famous speech at the Council of Clermont, calling for the First Crusade.
- **1097** — Antipope Clement III is removed from Rome by a French Crusader army.
- **1098** — Grants extraordinary rights to Roger I of Sicily.
- **29 July 1099** — Dies, 14 days after the fall of Jerusalem but before news of the victory reached him.

## Body

### Struggle for Authority
Urban's papacy began in conflict with the antipope Clement III, who held Rome with the support of Holy Roman Emperor Henry IV. Continuing the policies of Pope Gregory VII, Urban was often based in northern Italy and France. He held synods that reinforced bans on simony (the selling of church offices) and lay investiture (appointment of clergy by secular rulers). He skillfully built alliances, such as facilitating the marriage of Matilda of Tuscany to Welf II of Bavaria and supporting the rebellion of Prince Conrad against his father, Emperor Henry IV. Despite needing French support, he upheld the excommunication of King Philip I of France over his bigamous marriage.

### The First Crusade
In 1095, Urban received an embassy from Byzantine Emperor Alexios I asking for aid against Turkish tribes in Anatolia. At the Council of Clermont, he delivered a speech summoning nobles and people to reclaim the Holy Land and aid Eastern Christians. The speech, for which no exact transcription exists, promised remission of sins to participants. It proved highly effective, sparking the First Crusade. Urban's own letters reveal his primary focus was liberating the "Eastern churches," with Jerusalem mentioned less prominently than in later chroniclers' accounts. The crusade's most direct benefit for Urban was the removal of Antipope Clement III from Rome in 1097.

### Relations with Spain and Sicily
Urban viewed the fight against Muslims in Iberia as part of the same crusading effort as the eastern campaign. He granted indulgences (remission of temporal punishment for sins) to Catalan nobles fighting in Spain and offered the same divine rewards as for the Holy Land. In Sicily, he secured vital Norman support by granting Roger I extraordinary rights, including lay investiture and control of church revenues, making Roger a powerful papal ally.

## Terms
*   **Antipope:** A claimant to the title of Pope who is in opposition to the canonically elected pope.
*   **Gregorian Reforms:** A movement within the 11th-century Catholic Church to assert its independence from secular rulers and end practices like simony and lay investiture.
*   **Indulgence:** A remission of the temporal punishment due for sins that have already been forgiven.
*   **Lay Investiture:** The appointment of clergy to church offices by a secular ruler.
*   **Papabile:** A cardinal or other cleric who is considered a possible candidate to become pope.
*   **Simony:** The buying or selling of ecclesiastical privileges, such as pardons or the appointment to an office.
*   **Seljuks:** A Turkic dynasty that conquered much of the Middle East in the 11th century, including formerly Byzantine Anatolia.

## Debates and Open questions
*   **Urban's Motives:** Historians debate his primary goal for the Crusade: reuniting the Eastern and Western churches after the Great Schism of 1054, gaining legitimacy against the antipope, or creating a unified Christian defense against the Seljuks.
*   **The Speech at Clermont:** The exact content of Urban's speech is unknown, as the five surviving versions were written later and differ widely. It is also disputed whether the famous slogan "God wills it" was used at the council or created later as propaganda.
*   **Focus on Jerusalem:** Urban's own letters emphasize liberating the "Eastern churches" broadly, while later chroniclers place greater emphasis on reconquering Jerusalem itself.

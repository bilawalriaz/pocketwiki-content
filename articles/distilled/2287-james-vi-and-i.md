# James VI and I

## Overview
James VI and I (1566–1625) was the first monarch to rule both Scotland and England, uniting the crowns in 1603. His reign, known as the Jacobean era, was marked by a push for political union, the beginning of British colonization, and a flourishing of literature. He is remembered for his intellectual pursuits, his theory of divine-right monarchy, and his complex relationships with Parliament and religious factions.

## Timeline

- **24 July 1567** — Crowned King of Scotland at 13 months old after his mother's abdication.
- **1583** — Assumes full control of the Scottish government.
- **23 November 1589** — Marries Anne of Denmark in Oslo.
- **24 March 1603** — Succeeds Elizabeth I, becoming King of England and Ireland.
- **1604** — Assumes the title "King of Great Britain."
- **5 November 1605** — Gunpowder Plot to destroy Parliament is discovered.
- **1607** — English colony of Jamestown, Virginia, is founded.
- **1611** — The King James Version of the Bible is published.
- **1617** — Makes his only return visit to Scotland.
- **27 March 1625** — Dies at Theobalds House, succeeded by his son Charles I.

## Body
### Early Life and Rule in Scotland
James was the son of Mary, Queen of Scots, and Henry Stuart, Lord Darnley. He became king as an infant after his mother was forced to abdicate in 1567. Raised Protestant, he was governed by regents until he assumed personal control in 1583. His reign in Scotland was the longest of any Scottish monarch, during which he established effective royal government, promoted literature, and wrote political treatises like *The True Law of Free Monarchies* (1598) and *Basilikon Doron* (1599), which advocated the divine right of kings. He also authorized the "Gentleman Adventurers of Fife" to colonize the Isle of Lewis and enacted the Statutes of Iona (1609) to suppress Gaelic culture.

### Union of the Crowns and Early English Reign
In 1603, James succeeded his cousin Elizabeth I, uniting the Scottish and English crowns in a personal union. He moved to England, returning to Scotland only once in 1617. He pursued a closer political union, styling himself "King of Great Britain," but faced opposition from both English and Scottish Parliaments. His early English reign saw the end of the Anglo-Spanish War (1604) and the discovery of the Gunpowder Plot (1605), a Catholic conspiracy to assassinate him.

### King and Parliament
James's reign was marked by persistent conflict with the English Parliament over finances and royal prerogative. He dissolved Parliament multiple times, including the "Addled Parliament" of 1614, and ruled without one for years, raising money through sales of titles and other means. The "Spanish Match," a proposed marriage between his son Charles and the Spanish Infanta, was a major point of contention, as Parliament favored war with Catholic Spain. James's reliance on favorites like Robert Carr and George Villiers, Duke of Buckingham, and scandals such as the Overbury murder, damaged the monarchy's reputation.

### Religion and Legacy
James sought a middle way in religion, enforcing conformity against Puritans while tolerating some Catholics who took an Oath of Allegiance. He sponsored the King James Version of the Bible (1611). In Scotland, he tried to reintroduce episcopacy, causing division. His reign saw the start of the Plantation of Ulster and English colonization in North America. Historians have revised his reputation from that of a "foolish" monarch to a serious, peace-oriented ruler, though his financial irresponsibility and conflicts with Parliament are seen as laying groundwork for the English Civil War.

## Terms
- **Personal Union**: The combination of two or more crowns in one person without their merging into a single state.
- **Divine Right of Kings**: The doctrine that a monarch's authority comes directly from God and is not subject to earthly powers.
- **Jacobean Era**: The period of James VI and I's reign, noted for its literary and cultural achievements.
- **Plantation of Ulster**: The process of colonizing the Irish province of Ulster with Protestant settlers from Britain.
- **King James Version**: The English translation of the Bible commissioned by James in 1604 and published in 1611.
- **Privy Council**: The monarch's personal council of advisors.
- **Prorogued**: The postponement or discontinuation of a session of Parliament.
- **Recusant**: A person who refused to attend Church of England services, typically a Roman Catholic.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The exact nature of James's close relationships with male favorites like the Duke of Buckingham remains debated, with some historians suggesting they were sexual and others interpreting them within the context of 17th-century courtly affection.
- Historians continue to assess the extent to which James's policies and personal failings directly caused the political crises that led to the English Civil War under his son, Charles I.
- The success and impact of his lifelong pursuit of a formal political union between Scotland and England is evaluated differently, with some seeing it as a visionary failure and others as a necessary step toward a future British state.

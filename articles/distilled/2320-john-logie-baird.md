# John Logie Baird

## Overview
John Logie Baird (1888–1946) was a Scottish inventor and engineer who pioneered the practical development of television. He demonstrated the world's first working mechanical television system and later developed early colour television systems, playing a crucial role in the transition from mechanical to electronic television technology.

## Timeline
- **13 August 1888** — Born in Helensburgh, Scotland.
- **2 October 1925** — Successfully transmits the first television picture with a greyscale image.
- **26 January 1926** — Gives first public demonstration of true television images.
- **3 July 1928** — Demonstrates the world's first colour television transmission.
- **1928** — Achieves first transatlantic television transmission (London to New York).
- **14 July 1930** — First drama shown on UK television, *The Man with the Flower in His Mouth*, broadcast on BBC.
- **2 November 1936** — BBC begins alternating Baird's 240-line system with EMI's 405-line electronic system.
- **February 1937** — BBC ceases broadcasts using the Baird system.
- **16 August 1944** — Gives first demonstration of a practical fully electronic colour television display.
- **14 June 1946** — Dies in Bexhill-on-Sea, England.

## Body

### Early Life and Television Experiments
Born to a Church of Scotland minister, Baird studied engineering but did not graduate due to World War I. In 1923, he moved to Hastings for his health and began building a television system from household items. He used the **Nipkow disk**, an 1884 invention by Paul Gottlieb Nipkow, as the core of his mechanical scanning system. In 1925, he transmitted the first greyscale image—a ventriloquist's dummy's head—followed by the first human face.

### Public Demonstrations and Broadcasting
Baird's first public demonstration of moving silhouettes was at Selfridges in 1925. On 26 January 1926, he demonstrated true television images to the Royal Institution. He founded the Baird Television Development Company, which achieved the first transatlantic transmission in 1928. The BBC used Baird's 30-line system for broadcasts from 1929 to 1935. In 1936, the BBC began a trial alternating Baird's 240-line mechanical system with EMI's 405-line electronic system. The Baird system was discontinued in 1937, partly due to a fire at his Crystal Palace facilities and the superior mobility of EMI's electronic cameras.

### Transition to Electronic Television
After mechanical systems became obsolete, Baird contributed to electronic television. He developed a hybrid colour system using a cathode-ray tube and a rotating colour-filter disc. From 1940, he worked on the "Telechrome," a fully electronic colour system using two electron guns and patterned phosphors. In 1944, he demonstrated a 600-line electronic colour display. He proposed a 1000-line Telechrome system to the post-war Hankey Committee, but it was not adopted.

### Other Inventions and Legacy
Baird's other inventions included a glass razor, pneumatic shoes, and the "Phonovision" video recording system. He also worked on fibre-optics, infrared night viewing, and a device called "Noctovision," which is debated as a precursor to radar. He is honoured by Australia's Logie Awards and was inducted into the Scottish Engineering Hall of Fame in 2015.

## Terms
- **Nipkow disk**: A spinning disk with a spiral of apertures used in mechanical television to scan an image into an electrical signal.
- **Mechanical television**: A television system that uses mechanical components like spinning disks for scanning and displaying images.
- **Electronic television**: A television system that uses electronic components like cathode-ray tubes for scanning and displaying images, replacing mechanical systems.
- **Cathode-ray tube (CRT)**: A vacuum tube that uses electron beams to display images on a phosphorescent screen, the basis for early electronic TV receivers.
- **Telechrome**: Baird's proposed fully electronic colour television system using patterned phosphors and electron guns.
- **Noctovision**: Baird's system for creating images from reflected infrared light, distinct from radar as it cannot determine distance.
- **Phonovision**: Baird's early, unsuccessful system for recording television signals onto discs.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Radar contribution**: Baird filed a 1926 patent for a device using reflected radio waves, but his contribution to radar development is disputed. Experts note his "Noctovision" system lacked key radar capabilities like determining distance and 3D coordinates.
- **Reasons for system failure**: The Baird mechanical system was ultimately replaced by EMI's electronic system. Key factors included the immobility of Baird's cameras, a devastating fire at his facilities, and the superior technical performance of the electronic system.

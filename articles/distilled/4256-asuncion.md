# Asunción

## Overview
Asunción is the capital and largest city of Paraguay, located on the eastern bank of the Paraguay River. Founded by the Spanish in 1537, it is one of the oldest cities in South America and served as the launching point for colonial expeditions that founded many other cities in the region. Today, it is the political, economic, and cultural heart of Paraguay, housing all three branches of government and concentrating a significant portion of the nation's GDP.

## Body

### History and Foundation
The site was first visited by Spanish conquistador Juan de Ayolas around 1537. Juan de Salazar y Espinosa later established a fort there on 15 August 1537, naming it for the Feast of the Assumption. Early relations between Spanish settlers and the Guaraní people, shaped by alliances and intermarriage, created a predominantly mixed-race society. After the 1542 destruction of Buenos Aires, Asunción became the capital of a vast colonial province. It was a center of early anti-colonial sentiment, culminating in the 1811 independence movement planned in the city.

### Post-Independence and War
After independence, Asunción saw development under dictators Gaspar Rodríguez de Francia and Carlos Antonio López, who built infrastructure including South America's first railroad. The city's progress was devastated by the Paraguayan War (1864–1870), which killed two-thirds of the country's population and led to a Brazilian occupation of Asunción until 1876. Recovery was slow, aided by European immigration in the late 19th and early 20th centuries.

### Modern Era and Governance
Asunción became the first fully urbanized city in Paraguay by the mid-20th century. In 1991, the Treaty of Asunción was signed in the city, establishing Mercosur. Since 1993, it has been an autonomous capital district. The city has been the site of major political events, including the 1999 protests that led to a president's resignation and the 2017 crisis where protesters set fire to the Congress building.

### Geography and Climate
Asunción is located on the left bank of the Paraguay River, near its confluence with the Pilcomayo River. Its terrain is characterized by seven historical hills. The city has a humid subtropical climate (Köppen: Cfa) with hot, humid summers (average 28°C in January) and mild winters (average 17°C in July). Annual precipitation is high, averaging 1,400 mm.

### Economy and Infrastructure
Asunción is Paraguay's economic center, hosting the national stock exchange and headquarters of major companies. Its economy is dominated by the tertiary sector (commerce and services). The city has experienced rapid real estate growth, attracting regional investment due to low taxes and stability. Infrastructure includes the main river port (now largely replaced by Villeta), Silvio Pettirossi International Airport in suburban Luque, and a network of avenues. Public transportation relies heavily on buses.

### Demographics and Culture
According to the 2022 census, Asunción has 462,241 inhabitants, while its metropolitan area exceeds 2.3 million. The population is predominantly Catholic. The main languages are Spanish and Guaraní, with many speaking the mixed Jopará dialect. Culturally, the city is a hub for music, theater, and museums, including the National Museum of Fine Arts and the Manzana de la Rivera complex. It was declared the "Green Capital of Ibero-America" in 2015.

## Terms
- **Autonomous capital district**: An administrative division that functions as the national capital and is not part of any department.
- **Cuñadazgo**: A Guaraní institution where caciques gave women to newcomers to seal alliances, shaping early colonial society.
- **Gran Asunción**: The metropolitan area of Asunción, including surrounding cities in the Central Department.
- **Jopará**: A dialect mixing Guaraní with Spanish loanwords, spoken by a significant portion of Asunción's population.
- **Mercosur**: The Southern Common Market, a regional trade bloc founded by the 1991 Treaty of Asunción.
- **Rancheadas**: Violent mass seizures of Guaraní women by Spanish colonists, contributing to the city's early demographic mix.
- **Tertiary sector**: The part of the economy based on services, including commerce, finance, and tourism.

# Frederick William I of Prussia

## Overview
Frederick William I (1688–1740), known as the "Soldier King," was the King in Prussia and Elector of Brandenburg from 1713 until his death. He is a pivotal figure in Prussian history for transforming the state from a financially ruined, corrupt entity into a formidable military and administrative power through relentless fiscal and military reforms, laying the essential groundwork for his son Frederick the Great to elevate Prussia to a major European power.

## Timeline
- **14 August 1688** — Born in Berlin to King Frederick I of Prussia.
- **c. 1700** — Political awakening during the Great Northern War plague; exposes corruption of his father's ministers.
- **1713** — Ascends to the throne; dismisses his father's corrupt "Cabinet of Three Counts."
- **1713–1740** — Reorganizes state finances, expands the Prussian Army from 38,000 to 80,000 men.
- **1717** — Revokes the charter of the Brandenburg Africa Company and sells the Brandenburg Gold Coast colony to the Dutch.
- **1732** — Invites 20,000 Salzburg Protestants to settle in depopulated East Prussia.
- **1733** — Begins construction of the Dutch Quarter in Potsdam for skilled craftsmen.
- **1730s** — Introduces the Canton system for military conscription and reforms army training.
- **31 May 1740** — Dies in Berlin; succeeded by his son, Frederick II (Frederick the Great).

## Body

### Early Years and Political Awakening
Born to King Frederick I, Frederick William was raised by a Huguenot governess. His formative political experience came during the Great Northern War, when a plague outbreak in Prussia exposed the corruption and inefficiency of his father's ministers. He led a court faction that brought down the chief minister, Johann Kasimir Kolbe von Wartenberg, for embezzlement. This event cemented his lifelong resentment of corruption and waste, convincing him of the need for institutional reform.

### Military and Administrative Reforms
Upon inheriting a state bankrupted by his father's lavish spending, Frederick William I focused on financial reorganization and military expansion. He doubled the size of the Prussian Army and imposed strict regulations on public servants via a detailed manual of duties. He increased taxes on goods and the nobility, encouraged agriculture and commerce, and established schools and hospitals. His primary goal was to build a powerful state apparatus to support one of Europe's largest and best-trained armies, which he achieved through reforms like the Canton system (a conscription method) and the introduction of the iron ramrod to increase infantry fire rate.

### Colonial Affairs and Domestic Policy
Frederick William I prioritized military spending over colonial ventures. In 1717, he sold Prussia's West African colony, the Brandenburg Gold Coast, to the Dutch to avoid the costs of maintaining it and a navy. Domestically, he promoted settlement to repopulate areas like East Prussia, famously welcoming 20,000 Salzburg Protestants in 1732. He also fostered economic development by inviting Dutch craftsmen to build a new quarter in Potsdam.

### Personal Character and Relationship with His Son
The king was known for his harsh, violent temper, exacerbated by a porphyritic disease causing gout and obesity. His relationship with his intellectual heir, Frederick (Fritz), was deeply hostile. Frederick William despised his son's interest in French culture and music, subjecting him to beatings and humiliation. After Frederick attempted to flee Prussia, the king had his tutor, Hans Hermann von Katte, beheaded before the prince's eyes. Though they later reconciled on civil terms, Frederick did not mourn his father's death.

## Terms
- **Soldier King (Soldatenkönig):** The nickname for Frederick William I, reflecting his obsession with building a powerful military.
- **Canton System:** A Prussian military conscription system where specific districts were assigned to recruit for specific regiments, creating a permanent link between the army and the civilian population.
- **Brandenburg Gold Coast:** Prussia's colony in West Africa, established by his father and sold by Frederick William I in 1717.
- **Absolute Monarchy:** A system of government where the monarch holds supreme, unchecked authority, which Frederick William I practiced with great energy.
- **Porphyritic disease:** A metabolic disorder that afflicted the king, causing symptoms like gout, obesity, and stomach pains.
- **Iron ramrod:** A military innovation introduced to the Prussian army that increased the infantry's rate of fire and durability compared to wooden versions.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The exact intentions of Frederick William I regarding his son Frederick's fate after the failed escape attempt are unclear. While the historian Voltaire claimed the king intended Frederick's execution, the source notes this is not certain, as the Holy Roman Emperor intervened to halt the trial.
- The personal dynamics within the royal family are complex; while the marriage to Sophia Dorothea was unhappy and she resented her husband, she also quietly encouraged their son Frederick's defiance against his father's strict rules.

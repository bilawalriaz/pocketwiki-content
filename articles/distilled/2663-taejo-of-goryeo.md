# Taejo of Goryeo

## Overview
Taejo Wang Kŏn (877–943) was the founder of the Goryeo Dynasty, which ruled Korea from 918 to 1392. He unified the Later Three Kingdoms in 936, creating a single state that encompassed most of the Korean Peninsula and its people, a political unity that lasted until 1948. His reign established the cultural and political foundations of medieval Korea, including the adoption of Buddhism as a state religion and the claim to the heritage of the ancient Goguryeo kingdom.

## Timeline
- **877** — Wang Kŏn is born to a powerful maritime merchant family of Goguryeo descent in Songak (modern Kaesong).
- **895** — Wang Kŏn's father surrenders to the rebel leader Kung Ye; Wang Kŏn enters Kung Ye's service.
- **900** — Wang Kŏn leads a successful campaign in the Chungju area, gaining military fame.
- **913** — Wang Kŏn is appointed prime minister of the kingdom of Taebong, ruled by Kung Ye.
- **918** — Wang Kŏn is crowned king in a coup, founding the Goryeo Dynasty and renaming the kingdom.
- **919** — The capital is moved to Gaegyeong (modern Kaesong).
- **926** — The Khitan Empire destroys the kingdom of Balhae; its crown prince and nobles flee to Goryeo.
- **927** — Kyŏn Hwŏn of Later Baekje sacks the Silla capital; Wang Kŏn is defeated at the Battle of Gongsan.
- **935** — The last king of Silla surrenders his kingdom to Goryeo.
- **936** — Wang Kŏn conquers Later Baekje, unifying the Later Three Kingdoms.
- **942** — Wang Kŏn exiles Khitan envoys and starves their gift camels in retribution for Balhae's destruction.
- **943** — Wang Kŏn dies from disease after ruling for 25 years.

## Body

### Background and Rise to Power
Wang Kŏn was born into a wealthy family of Goguryeo refugees who controlled maritime trade in the northern region of Songak. His family's claimed descent from a Tang emperor is considered hagiographical by modern historians. He began his career in the chaotic Later Three Kingdoms period, serving the rebel leader Kung Ye. Wang Kŏn's military successes, including campaigns against Later Baekje, earned him high rank and popular support. As Kung Ye's rule became increasingly tyrannical and erratic, Wang Kŏn's prestige grew.

### Founding of Goryeo and Unification
In 918, four of Kung Ye's top generals staged a coup, overthrowing and killing Kung Ye to install Wang Kŏn as king. Wang Kŏn renamed the kingdom Goryeo, claiming succession from the ancient Goguryeo state. He promoted Buddhism and sought to unify the peninsula through a combination of military force and strategic alliances. He incorporated the ruling elites of defeated states, including Silla and refugees from the fallen kingdom of Balhae, into his coalition. His final victory over Later Baekje in 936 completed the unification of the Later Three Kingdoms.

### Foreign Policy and Legacy
Taejo's foreign policy was shaped by the destruction of Balhae by the Khitan. He welcomed Balhae refugees, viewing them as kin, and displayed strong animosity toward the Khitans, famously insulting their envoys in 942. He proposed a joint attack on the Khitans with the Chinese Later Jin dynasty. In his "Ten Injunctions" to his descendants, he warned against Khitan aggression. His unification created a single state for the Korean people that endured for over a millennium. The modern name "Korea" derives from "Goryeo," the dynasty he founded.

## Terms
- **Later Three Kingdoms:** The period (c. 892–936) following the collapse of Unified Silla, when three successor states—Later Goguryeo (Taebong/Goryeo), Later Baekje, and Silla—competed for control of the Korean Peninsula.
- **Goryeo:** The dynasty founded by Wang Kŏn in 918, from which the modern name "Korea" is derived.
- **Goguryeo:** An ancient Korean kingdom (37 BC–668 AD) whose heritage and territory Goryeo claimed to succeed.
- **Balhae:** A kingdom (698–926 AD) in Manchuria and northern Korea, considered a successor to Goguryeo, destroyed by the Khitan.
- **Khitans/Liao dynasty:** A nomadic people who founded the Liao dynasty in northern China and destroyed Balhae in 926.
- **Ten Injunctions:** A set of political and moral guidelines Taejo left for his successors, including warnings about the Khitans.
- **Hojok:** Local lords or powerful families who controlled regions during the chaotic Later Three Kingdoms period.
- **Buddhism:** Promoted by Taejo as the state religion of Goryeo, influencing its culture and politics for centuries.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The exact nature of Wang Kŏn's ancestry is debated. While later texts claimed imperial Tang descent, modern historians believe his family were influential Goguryeo refugees who accumulated wealth through trade.
- The historical accuracy of specific legends, such as the dragon woman story involving his grandfather, is dismissed by scholars as hagiographical.
- The degree to which Wang Kŏn's unification was a "complete" unification of the Korean people, as opposed to the earlier Silla unification which excluded Balhae, is a point of historical interpretation.

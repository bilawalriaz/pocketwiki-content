# Mozambique Channel

## Overview
The Mozambique Channel is a major arm of the Indian Ocean separating the island nation of Madagascar from the African mainland country of Mozambique. It is a strategically vital waterway, carrying approximately 30% of the world's tanker traffic, and is a site of ongoing geopolitical disputes over sovereignty and resources.

## Timeline
- **15 November 1939** — German pocket battleship *Admiral Graf Spee* intercepts and sinks the British tanker *Africa Shell* in the channel.
- **1942** — The channel is a clashpoint during the World War II Battle of Madagascar.
- **2011** — The South African Navy begins intermittent anti-piracy patrols in the channel.

## Body

### Geography and Extent
The Mozambique Channel is approximately 1,700 km long and 419 km wide at its narrowest point. It reaches a maximum depth of 3,292 m. The International Hydrographic Organization defines its boundaries: to the north by a line from the Rovuma River estuary to Cape Amber in Madagascar; to the east by Madagascar's west coast; to the south by a line from Cape Sainte-Marie in Madagascar to Ponto do Ouro on the mainland; and to the west by the African coast. A warm southward-flowing current, the Mozambique Current, runs through the channel.

### Islands and Sovereignty
The channel contains several island groups with complex sovereignty. The Comoros archipelago includes Grande Comore, Mohéli, and Anjouan. France holds Mayotte (claimed by Comoros) and the Scattered Islands in the Indian Ocean (Glorioso, Juan de Nova, Europa, and Bassas da India), which are also claimed by Madagascar. Mozambique's islands include the Primeiras and Segundas Archipelago and the Quirimbas Islands. The St. Lazarus Banks, a seamount near the Quirimbas, are noted for marine biodiversity.

### Strategic and Economic Importance
The channel is a critical global shipping lane, carrying about 30% of the world's tanker traffic. Its islands provide exclusive economic zones (EEZs) rich in fisheries and potential hydrocarbons. France maintains a military presence via Réunion to patrol these waters and assert sovereignty over its territories, particularly against Malagasy claims. Since 2011, the South African Navy has conducted anti-piracy patrols due to disruptions from an Islamist insurrection in northern Mozambique.

## Terms
- **Arm of the Indian Ocean:** A portion of the ocean partially enclosed by land.
- **Exclusive Economic Zone (EEZ):** A maritime zone where a state has special rights regarding exploration and use of marine resources.
- **Hydrographic Organization:** An international body that defines maritime boundaries and standards.
- **Mozambique Current:** A warm ocean current flowing southward through the channel.
- **Pocket Battleship:** A type of small, heavily armed warship used by Germany in World War II.
- **Seamount:** An underwater mountain rising from the ocean floor.
- **Sovereignty:** Supreme authority within a territory.

## Debates and Open Questions
The primary debate concerns sovereignty over the Scattered Islands and Mayotte. Comoros claims Mayotte, while Madagascar claims the Scattered Islands, arguing they were Malagasy dependencies that should have reverted to it upon independence, not remained with France. France maintains its sovereignty and a military presence to protect its claimed territories and their associated EEZs.

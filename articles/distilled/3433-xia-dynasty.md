# Xia dynasty

## Overview
The Xia dynasty is the first dynasty in traditional Chinese historiography, said to have been founded by Yu the Great after he successfully controlled catastrophic floods. Its historicity is a major scholarly debate, as there are no contemporaneous written records. Most Chinese archaeologists identify the Xia with the Bronze Age Erlitou culture (c. 1900–1500 BC), while many Western scholars consider it legendary or unproven. The concept of the Xia was crucial for later political philosophy, providing a model for dynastic succession and the Mandate of Heaven.

## Timeline
- **c. 2205–1766** — Traditional Xia reign period (based on Liu Xin's calculations).
- **c. 1953–1555** — Proposed Xia reign period based on five-planet conjunctions (Pankenier/Nivison).
- **2070–1600** — Proposed Xia reign period by the Xia–Shang–Zhou Chronology Project.
- **c. 1920** — Large flood event at Jishi Gorge, possibly the basis for the Yu the Great myth.
- **c. 1900–1500** — Erlitou culture flourishes; many archaeologists identify it with the Xia.
- **c. 1600** — Construction of walled cities at Yanshi and Zhengzhou marks the transition to the Shang dynasty.

## Body

### Traditional Historiography and Founding
Traditional texts like the *Book of Documents*, *Bamboo Annals*, and Sima Qian's *Shiji* describe the Xia as historical. The dynasty was founded by Yu the Great, who succeeded the legendary Shun. Yu's success in controlling floods by digging channels increased his tribe's power. He was granted the throne, and upon his death, passed it to his son Qi, establishing the Hereditary System. The traditional narrative includes an interregnum where the throne was usurped by Hou Yi and later Han Zhuo, before being restored by King Shao Kang. The final king, Jie, was a tyrant overthrown by Tang, who founded the Shang dynasty.

### Traditional Structure and Society
According to the *Book of Documents*, Yu divided the realm into Nine Provinces. The Xia state's direct jurisdiction was limited, with surrounding *fangguo* (tribal polities) maintaining semi-independent rule in exchange for submission and tribute. Traditional accounts describe a prosperous agricultural economy with irrigation, bronze metallurgy, and a developed transport system. Population estimates are highly speculative, with one ancient source claiming over 13.5 million people, while modern scholars have proposed figures around 2 million.

### Modern Studies and the Historicity Debate
The Doubting Antiquity School, led by Gu Jiegang in the 1920s, first systematically questioned the traditional narrative, arguing it was embellished over time. Some historians suggest the Zhou dynasty invented the Xia to justify their own conquest of the Shang by analogy. However, evidence like the *Shu Yi Zhong* bronze inscription (c. 600 BC), which mentions the Shang overthrowing the Xia, contradicts the idea of a complete Zhou fabrication. The debate often centers on different research orientations: Chinese scholars frequently use traditional texts to guide archaeological interpretation, while Western scholars often demand independent, testable evidence.

### Archaeological Discoveries
In 1959, archaeologist Xu Xusheng discovered the Erlitou site, a large Bronze Age urban center with palace foundations. Its refined radiocarbon date range (c. 1735–1530 BC) overlaps with proposed Xia chronologies. Most Chinese archaeologists identify Erlitou with the Xia capital. The Xia–Shang–Zhou Chronology Project assigned all four phases of Erlitou to the Xia, but this required designating the earlier Henan Longshan culture (Xinzhai phase) as the start of the Xia period—a transition not yet clearly seen in the archaeological record.

## Terms
- **Mandate of Heaven**: The political philosophy, promoted by the Zhou, that Heaven grants the right to rule to a virtuous leader, which can be revoked if the ruler becomes corrupt.
- **Hereditary System**: The principle of succession within a family or clan, established when Yu passed the throne to his son Qi instead of the most capable candidate.
- **Fangguo**: Polities or tribal groups outside the Xia clan's direct rule, which could be allies or enemies.
- **Erlitou culture**: A Bronze Age archaeological culture (c. 1900–1500 BC) in the Yellow River valley, widely identified by Chinese archaeologists with the Xia dynasty.
- **Nine Provinces**: The traditional division of China into nine administrative regions by Yu the Great.
- **Doubting Antiquity School**: A scholarly movement in the 1920s that critically re-examined traditional Chinese historical narratives.
- **Xia–Shang–Zhou Chronology Project**: A Chinese government-commissioned project (1996) that proposed a unified chronology for China's early dynasties.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Historicity**: Is the Xia dynasty a historical reality or a later legendary construct? The lack of contemporaneous written records is the core issue.
- **Archaeological Identification**: Is the Erlitou culture definitively the Xia? The identification is contested due to the absence of inscriptions at Erlitou explicitly naming the Xia.
- **Chronology**: What are the accurate dates for the Xia? Proposed ranges vary significantly based on different textual interpretations and scientific dating methods.
- **Cultural Transition**: Is there a clear archaeological transition from the preceding Longshan culture to the Erlitou culture that corresponds to the rise of the Xia state?

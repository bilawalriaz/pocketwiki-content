# Johann Bernoulli

## Overview
Johann Bernoulli (1667–1748) was a Swiss mathematician from the famous Bernoulli family, pivotal in developing and applying infinitesimal calculus. He was a key figure in disseminating Leibniz's calculus across Europe, educated the great mathematician Leonhard Euler, and was central to major scientific debates of his era, including the priority dispute over calculus and the theory of gravitation.

## Timeline
- **1667** — Born in Basel, Switzerland.
- **1690** — Completed medical dissertation *De Motu musculorum*, reviewed by Leibniz.
- **1694** — Married Dorothea Falkner; accepted professorship at University of Groningen.
- **1696** — Presented the brachistochrone curve problem.
- **1696** — Guillaume de l'Hôpital published the first calculus textbook, using Bernoulli's work.
- **1705** — Returned to Basel after his brother Jacob's death, becoming professor of mathematics.
- **1713** — Sided with Leibniz in the Leibniz–Newton calculus debate.
- **1724** — Disqualified from a French Académie competition on hard bodies; received honourable mention.
- **1738** — His son Daniel published *Hydrodynamica*; Johann later published *Hydraulica* with a false earlier date.
- **1748** — Died in Basel.

## Body

### Early Life and Education
Johann Bernoulli was born in Basel to an apothecary. He studied medicine at the University of Basel but pursued mathematics alongside his older brother, Jacob Bernoulli. The brothers were among the first to master and apply the newly developed infinitesimal calculus. Johann's 1690 medical dissertation was reviewed by Gottfried Leibniz, a founder of calculus.

### Academic Career and the Calculus Debate
After graduating, Johann taught differential equations and moved to the University of Groningen in 1694. Following his brother's death in 1705, he returned to Basel to take Jacob's former mathematics professorship. He became a staunch defender of Leibniz's calculus against Isaac Newton's, siding with Leibniz in the 1713 priority dispute. He also promoted Descartes' vortex theory of gravitation over Newton's, delaying the acceptance of Newton's theory on the European continent.

### Major Works and Controversies
Bernoulli's work often led to disputes. He and his brother Jacob had a competitive relationship, culminating in a bitter feud over the brachistochrone curve problem (the path of fastest descent under gravity). Johann posed the problem in 1696; both brothers solved it, but Johann incorrectly presented his brother's derivation as his own. Later, Johann had a contractual agreement with Guillaume de l'Hôpital, who published the first calculus textbook in 1696 using Bernoulli's discoveries, including the rule now named after l'Hôpital. Bernoulli later complained about insufficient credit. In 1724, he was disqualified from a French Académie competition on the motion of hard bodies. His later work on hydrodynamics led to a controversy with his own son, Daniel Bernoulli; Johann falsely backdated his *Hydraulica* (published 1743) to appear before Daniel's *Hydrodynamica* (1738).

## Terms
- **Infinitesimal calculus**: The mathematical study of continuous change, developed independently by Newton and Leibniz in the late 17th century.
- **Brachistochrone curve**: The curve of fastest descent between two points under the influence of gravity alone; its solution is a cycloid.
- **Leibniz–Newton debate**: A major 17th-18th century dispute over who deserved credit for inventing calculus.
- **Vortex theory**: A theory of gravitation proposed by Descartes, suggesting planets are swept around the sun by vortices of subtle matter, later superseded by Newton's law of universal gravitation.
- **L'Hôpital's rule**: A rule in calculus for evaluating limits of indeterminate forms, published in a textbook by Guillaume de l'Hôpital but based on work by Johann Bernoulli.
- **Hydrodynamics**: The study of fluids (liquids and gases) in motion.

## Debates and Open Questions
The source details several key disputes: the bitter rivalry between Johann and his brother Jacob Bernoulli, particularly concerning the brachistochrone problem; the priority and credit controversy with Guillaume de l'Hôpital over the first calculus textbook; and the later conflict with his son Daniel Bernoulli over precedence in hydrodynamics, which involved Johann falsifying the date of his work.

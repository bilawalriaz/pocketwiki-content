# Carpathian Mountains

## Overview
The Carpathian Mountains are a 1,500 km (930 mi) arc-shaped range across Central and Southeast Europe, forming the continent's third-longest mountain system. They are a critical ecological corridor, hosting Europe's largest populations of brown bears, wolves, and lynx, and contain vast tracts of virgin forest, particularly in Romania. The range's geology, shaped by ancient tectonic plates, and its role as a watershed between the northern seas and the Black Sea make it a defining feature of the region's geography and biodiversity.

## Timeline
- **2nd century AD** — Name "Carpates" first recorded by Ptolemy in his *Geographia*.
- **Mesozoic & Cenozoic eras** — Carpathian mountains formed during the Alpine orogeny by tectonic plate movement.
- **Middle Miocene** — Intensive calc-alkaline arc volcanism occurred over a subduction zone.
- **c. 30,000 years ago** — Last volcanic activity at Ciomadul.
- **1211** — Gervase of Tilbury describes the mountains as "Hunic Alps".
- **13th-15th centuries** — Hungarian documents name the mountains Thorchal, Tarczal, or Montes Nivium.
- **Post-Roman conquest of Dacia** — Emperor Trajan brought over 165 tons of gold and 330 tons of silver from the Western Carpathians to Rome.
- **Modern era** — Polish scientists discover a permafrost and glacial area in the Tatra Mountains.

## Body

### Geography and Structure
The Carpathians are not a single chain but a series of distinctive geological groups. They stretch from Austria and the Czech Republic clockwise through Slovakia, Poland, Ukraine, Romania, and Serbia. The range is widest (up to 500 km) in the Transylvanian Plateau and southern Tatra Mountains, where the highest peaks are found. The highest point is Gerlachovský štít in Slovakia (2,655 m / 8,711 ft). The mountains are separated from the Alps by the Danube River and are surrounded on all sides by plains, unlike the Alps.

### Geology and Formation
The Carpathians were formed by the movement of tectonic plates (ALCAPA, Tisza, and Dacia) over subducting oceanic crust during the Alpine orogeny. The structure is a fold and thrust belt. The external northern portion is the Carpathian Flysch Belt, an accretionary wedge of rocks scraped from the sea bottom. The internal zones contain older volcanic massifs and were affected by Miocene volcanism. The area remains the most seismically active in Central Europe.

### Ecology and Resources
The ecology varies with altitude, from lowland deciduous forests (oak, beech) to subalpine spruce forests and alpine meadows. The range is a vital habitat for large mammals, including brown bears, wolves, lynx, and chamois, with the highest concentration in Romania. Romania also holds one-third of Europe's thermal and mineral waters and the second-largest area of virgin forests on the continent (250,000 hectares), mostly in the Carpathians. However, these forests face high rates of loss from clearcutting and illegal logging.

### Divisions and Naming
The Carpathians are divided into Western, Eastern, and Southern sections, with borders defined by geological lines and passes like the Predeal Pass. Geopolitical divisions (e.g., Romanian, Ukrainian Carpathians) reflect national borders but are complex due to overlapping historical traditions. The name "Carpathian" is linked to the ancient Dacian tribe "Carpes" or "Carpi." Its etymology is debated, possibly deriving from a Proto-Indo-European root for "rock" or "mountain," or from a root meaning "to turn," referencing the range's L-shaped bend.

## Terms
- **Orogeny**: The process of mountain formation through tectonic plate movements.
- **Flysch Belt**: A geological formation of sedimentary rocks (turbidites) scraped from an ocean floor and thrust onto a continental plate.
- **Accretionary Wedge**: A mass of sedimentary material scraped off a subducting oceanic plate and added to the overriding continental plate.
- **Krummholz**: Stunted, wind-blown trees found at the treeline in alpine environments.
- **Solifluction**: The slow, downhill flow of water-saturated soil over a frozen layer (permafrost).
- **Virgin Forest**: A forest that has never been logged or significantly disturbed by human activity.
- **Thermal/Mineral Waters**: Naturally occurring groundwater with elevated temperatures or dissolved mineral content, often used for therapeutic purposes.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The precise etymology of the name "Carpathian" is not clearly established, with competing theories linking it to ancient tribes, words for "rock," or a root meaning "to turn."
- The exact geological and biological borders between the Western, Eastern, and Southern Carpathians are debated, with different systems placing them along different rivers, towns, or ecological lines.
- The historical and environmental impact of human activity, particularly deforestation and resource extraction, on the Carpathian ecosystem over centuries is a subject of ongoing research.

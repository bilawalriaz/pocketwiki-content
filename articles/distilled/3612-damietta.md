# Damietta

## Overview
Damietta is a historic port city in Egypt's Nile Delta, serving as the capital of Damietta Governorate. Its strategic location at the mouth of a Nile branch has made it a key site for trade, military conflict, and cultural exchange for over a millennium, from the Crusades to the modern era.

- **853** — Byzantine sack leads Caliph al-Mutawakkil to build city walls.
- **1218–1219** — Siege and capture by Crusaders during the Fifth Crusade; population devastated.
- **1249** — Captured by Louis IX of France during the Seventh Crusade.
- **1250** — Destroyed by the Mamluks; a new town is founded south of the old site.
- **1384, 1412** — Attacked by the Republic of Genoa.

- **1803** — Site of a surrender during an Albanian soldier rebellion.
- **1883** — First cholera case of the Egyptian epidemic reported.
- **2019** — Joins the UNESCO Global Network of Learning Cities.

## Body

### Early History and Islamic Period
Damietta, known as Tamíathis in the Hellenistic period, was conquered by the Arab general Miqdad ibn Aswad. It was repeatedly attacked by the Byzantine Empire between 708 and 968. The city was an important port for the Abbasid Caliphate's trade with India and China. The Sufi order Qalandariyya was founded there in the 13th century.

### Crusader Sieges
Damietta was a primary target for Crusaders due to its control over the Nile. The Fifth Crusade (1217–1221) focused on capturing the city; after a devastating siege, Crusaders took it in 1219, but only 3,000 of its 70,000 inhabitants survived. Francis of Assisi visited to negotiate peace. The Seventh Crusade, led by Louis IX, captured Damietta in 1249, but he was forced to surrender it as ransom in 1250.

### Mamluk and Ottoman Rule
After its destruction by the Mamluks in 1250, Damietta was rebuilt. It was governed by appointed officials, with the position upgraded to a viceroyalty in 1427. The city was used as a place of exile for deposed rulers. Under the Ottomans, it remained a transit point for troops and a center for trade, though its importance waned after the creation of the Mahmudiyya Canal and the Suez Canal.

### Economy and Trade
Changes to the Nile in the 9th–10th centuries diverted trade from Alexandria to Damietta. It became a major rice-growing area and a significant customs port. A textile industry, particularly silk production, flourished in the 19th century. Today, Damietta is renowned for its furniture industry, dairy products (like Domiati cheese), and fishing. The modern SEGAS LNG plant and a new port have revitalized its economy.

### Ecclesiastical History
The ancient city was a Christian bishopric. After the Crusader capture in 1249, it briefly became a Latin Church bishopric. Today, it exists as two titular sees in the Catholic Church: a Latin titular see and a Melkite Catholic titular see.

## Terms
- **Titular see:** A diocese that no longer has an active residential bishop but is maintained as an honorary title in the Catholic Church.
- **Distributary:** A stream that branches off and flows away from a main river channel, common in deltas.
- **Progradation:** The growth of land outward into a sea or lake, here caused by Nile sediment.
- **Caliphate:** An Islamic state led by a caliph, a political and religious successor to the Prophet Muhammad.
- **Crusades:** A series of religious wars sanctioned by the Latin Church in the medieval period.
- **Mamluks:** A class of warrior-slaves who ruled Egypt and Syria from 1250 to 1517.
- **Sufi order (tariqa):** A mystical branch of Islam focused on inner spiritual development.
- **Viceroyalty:** A province or territory governed by a viceroy, a ruler acting for a monarch.

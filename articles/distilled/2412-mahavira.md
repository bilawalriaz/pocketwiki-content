# Mahavira

## Overview
Mahavira (c. 6th–5th century BCE) was an Indian spiritual reformer and the 24th and final Tirthankara (ford-maker or supreme teacher) of Jainism. He is credited with reviving and systematizing the Jain community, establishing its core ethical and philosophical doctrines, and founding the fourfold monastic and lay order that preserved the tradition. His teachings, particularly on non-violence (ahimsa), have had a profound and lasting influence on Indian culture and philosophy.

## Timeline
- **c. 599 BCE (traditional)** — Born as Vardhamana into a royal Kshatriya family of the Naya tribe in Kundagrama, Videha (modern Bihar, India).
- **c. 569 BCE (traditional)** — Renounced worldly life at age 30 to become an ascetic.
- **c. 557 BCE (traditional)** — Attained Kevala Jnana (omniscience) after 12.5 years of severe austerities.
- **c. 557–527 BCE (traditional)** — Preached his philosophy for 30 years, establishing the Jain sangha (community).
- **c. 527 BCE (Śvetāmbara) / 510 BCE (Digambara)** — Attained nirvana (liberation) at Pavapuri, Bihar.
- **1st century BCE–2nd century CE** — Earliest known iconography of Mahavira appears in archaeological sites in Mathura.
- **c. 300 BCE** — Jain canon (Agamas) begins to be lost due to famine and dispersal of monks.
- **5th century CE** — Attempt to reconcile differing recitations of the Agamas fails, solidifying the Śvetāmbara and Digambara sects.
- **1974** — 2,500th anniversary of Mahavira's nirvana celebrated; major Jain sects unite on a common flag and emblem.

## Body

### Historical Life and Dating
While Mahavira's historicity is accepted by scholars, his exact dates are uncertain and debated. Traditional Jain accounts place his life from 599 to 527 BCE (Śvetāmbara) or 510 BCE (Digambara). However, many historians consider these dates too early, often by about a century, as Mahavira was a contemporary of the Buddha. Scholarly estimates for his lifespan range from c. 499–427 BCE to a death around 425 BCE. He is believed to have revived an earlier Jain or proto-Jain community likely led by his predecessor, Parshvanatha.

### Teachings and Philosophy
Mahavira's teachings were compiled by his chief disciple, Indrabhuti Gautama, into the Jain Agamas. His core philosophy centered on the existence of an eternal soul (jiva) trapped in the cycle of rebirth (samsara) by karma. Liberation (moksha) is achieved through self-discipline and the observance of the Five Vows: ahimsa (non-violence), satya (truth), asteya (non-stealing), brahmacharya (chastity), and aparigraha (non-attachment). He taught the doctrine of Anekantavada (many-sided reality), which posits that truth and reality are complex and can be expressed only through partial perspectives (nayas). His emphasis on ahimsa is considered supreme in Jainism, extending to all living beings.

### Community and Legacy
Mahavira established the chaturvidha sangha, a fourfold order of monks, nuns, laymen, and laywomen. This structure allowed Jainism to flourish as both a renunciant and lay tradition. His teachings were transmitted orally and later written down, though the original canon was largely lost by the 1st century CE, leading to the formation of the Śvetāmbara and Digambara sects with their own versions of the Agamas. His legacy is commemorated annually during the festivals of Mahavir Janma Kalyanak (his birth) and Diwali (his nirvana).

## Terms
- **Tirthankara**: A "ford-maker" or supreme teacher in Jainism who founds a tirtha (spiritual community) to help others cross the sea of rebirth.
- **Kevala Jnana**: Omniscience or infinite knowledge, the state of perfect perception attained by a Tirthankara.
- **Ahimsa**: The principle of non-violence or non-injury towards all living beings, the foremost vow in Jainism.
- **Anekantavada**: The doctrine of "many-sided reality," asserting that truth and reality are complex and have multiple aspects.
- **Jiva**: The eternal, individual soul or living entity in Jain metaphysics.
- **Karma**: In Jainism, a physical substance that binds to the soul due to actions and intent, causing rebirth.
- **Moksha/Nirvana**: Liberation from the cycle of birth and death (samsara); the ultimate goal of spiritual practice.
- **Agamas**: The canonical scriptures of Jainism, containing the teachings of Mahavira as compiled by his disciples.
- **Sangha**: The Jain community, traditionally comprising four groups: monks, nuns, laymen, and laywomen.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Exact Dates**: The precise years of Mahavira's birth and death are historically uncertain, with traditional and scholarly dates differing by up to a century.
- **Relationship with Parshvanatha**: While tradition places Parshvanatha 273 years before Mahavira, some scholars suggest they were closer in time, with Parshvanatha possibly active only decades before Mahavira.
- **Sectarian Differences**: The Śvetāmbara and Digambara traditions disagree on details of Mahavira's life, such as whether he married, the location of his nirvana, and the completeness of the surviving Agamas.
- **Gender and Liberation**: A historical dispute exists within Jainism over whether women can achieve spiritual liberation (moksha) through asceticism, with the Digambara sect traditionally holding a more restrictive view.

# Arab–Israeli conflict

## Overview
The Arab–Israeli conflict is a century-long political and military struggle between Israel and its Arab neighbors, rooted in competing national movements—Zionism and Palestinian nationalism—over the same territory. It has shaped regional politics, caused massive displacement and loss of life, and remains a central issue in international diplomacy.

## Timeline
- **1917** — Balfour Declaration supports a Jewish national home in Palestine
- **1920** — League of Nations grants Britain the Mandate for Palestine
- **1947** — UN adopts Partition Plan for Palestine (Resolution 181)
- **1948** — Israel declares independence; 1948 Arab–Israeli War begins
- **1949** — Armistice Agreements establish the Green Line
- **1956** — Suez Crisis: Israel invades Sinai with British/French support
- **1967** — Six-Day War: Israel captures Sinai, Gaza, West Bank, Golan Heights
- **1973** — Yom Kippur War: Egypt and Syria launch surprise attack
- **1979** — Egypt–Israel peace treaty signed
- **1993** — Oslo Accords signed between Israel and PLO
- **2005** — Israeli disengagement from Gaza Strip
- **2023** — October 7 attacks trigger major Gaza war

## Background
### National Movements
The conflict's roots lie in late 19th-century Zionism, which sought a Jewish homeland in Palestine, and Arab nationalism, which viewed Palestine as part of the Arab world. The Ottoman Empire controlled the region until World War I. After its collapse, Britain and France divided Ottoman territories. The 1916 Sykes–Picot treaty and the 1915–16 McMahon–Hussein correspondence (promising Arab independence) set conflicting expectations. The 1917 Balfour Declaration, expressing British support for a Jewish national home, further complicated matters.

### British Mandate and Rising Tensions
The League of Nations granted Britain the Mandate for Palestine in 1920. Jewish immigration increased, especially after the Nazis rose to power in Germany. Arab Palestinians opposed this influx, fearing displacement. Violence erupted periodically, including the 1929 Hebron massacre and the 1936–39 Arab revolt. Britain restricted Jewish immigration via the 1939 White Paper, but illegal immigration continued after the Holocaust. In 1947, the UN proposed partitioning Palestine into Jewish and Arab states (Resolution 181), accepted by Jewish leaders but rejected by Arab states.

## Civil War and 1948 War
Fighting between Jewish and Arab militias escalated into civil war in 1947. On May 14, 1948, as the British Mandate expired, Israel declared independence. The next day, armies from Egypt, Jordan, Syria, Iraq, and Lebanon invaded. Israel won the war, expanding beyond the UN partition borders. About 713,000 Palestinian Arabs were displaced, becoming refugees. The 1949 Armistice Agreements established the Green Line, with Jordan controlling the West Bank and Egypt controlling Gaza.

## Major Wars and Peace Efforts
### 1956–1973
The 1956 Suez Crisis saw Israel invade Sinai after Egypt closed the Straits of Tiran. The 1967 Six-Day War resulted in Israel capturing the Sinai, Gaza, West Bank, East Jerusalem, and Golan Heights. The 1973 Yom Kippur War began with a surprise attack by Egypt and Syria, leading to a U.S.-brokered ceasefire. The Arab League's 1967 "three no's" (no peace, recognition, or negotiations) hardened positions.

### Peace Treaties
Egypt and Israel signed a peace treaty in 1979, returning Sinai to Egypt. Jordan and Israel signed a peace agreement in 1994. The 1993 Oslo Accords established limited Palestinian self-rule but final-status issues remained unresolved.

### Palestinian Uprisings and Conflicts
The First Intifada (1987–93) was a Palestinian uprising against Israeli occupation. The Second Intifada (2000–05) involved widespread violence. Israel withdrew from Gaza in 2005 but maintained a blockade after Hamas took control in 2007. Conflicts in Gaza recurred in 2008–09, 2012, 2014, 2021, and 2023. Israel also intervened in Lebanon's civil war, fighting the PLO and later Hezbollah.

## Recent Developments
The Abraham Accords (2020) normalized relations between Israel and several Arab states. The Syrian civil war (2011–24) saw limited Israeli strikes against Iranian targets. The October 7, 2023, Hamas-led attack on Israel triggered a major war in Gaza with significant casualties and displacement.

## Terms
- **Zionism**: Jewish nationalist movement seeking a homeland in Palestine.
- **Mandate for Palestine**: League of Nations authority granted to Britain to administer Palestine (1920–1948).
- **Partition Plan (Resolution 181)**: UN proposal to divide Palestine into Jewish and Arab states, rejected by Arabs.
- **Green Line**: 1949 armistice border between Israel and neighboring Arab states.
- **PLO (Palestine Liberation Organization)**: Established in 1964 to represent Palestinian interests, recognized Israel in 1993.
- **Intifada**: Palestinian uprisings against Israeli occupation (First: 1987–93; Second: 2000–05).
- **Oslo Accords**: 1993 agreements establishing Palestinian self-rule and mutual recognition between Israel and PLO.
- **Abraham Accords**: 2020 agreements normalizing Israel's relations with UAE, Bahrain, Sudan, and Morocco.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Causes of Palestinian displacement in 1948**: Historians debate whether it was primarily due to Israeli military actions or Arab leadership orders.
- **Responsibility for ongoing conflict**: Disagreements persist over the roles of occupation, settlement expansion, security concerns, and political leadership on both sides.
- **Two-state solution viability**: Scholars question whether a separate Palestinian state remains feasible given current political and territorial realities.
- **Role of external powers**: The influence of the U.S., Soviet Union/Russia, and regional actors like Iran and Saudi Arabia is continually assessed.

# Pelé

## Overview
Pelé (Edson Arantes do Nascimento, 1940–2022) was a Brazilian footballer widely regarded as one of the greatest players in history. He is the only player to win three FIFA World Cups (1958, 1962, 1970) and held the record for most career goals (1,279 in 1,363 games, including friendlies). His global fame and skill helped popularize football worldwide, earning him the nickname "O Rei" (The King).

## Timeline
- **23 Oct 1940** — Born in Três Corações, Minas Gerais, Brazil.
- **1956** — Signs professional contract with Santos FC at age 15.
- **1958** — Wins first FIFA World Cup with Brazil at age 17; becomes youngest World Cup goalscorer.
- **1961** — Brazilian government declares him an "official national treasure."
- **1962** — Leads Santos to first Copa Libertadores and Intercontinental Cup titles.
- **19 Nov 1969** — Scores his 1,000th career goal ("O Milésimo") from a penalty.
- **1970** — Wins third FIFA World Cup with Brazil; scores opening goal in final.
- **1975** — Comes out of retirement to play for New York Cosmos.
- **29 Dec 2022** — Dies in São Paulo at age 82 from multiple organ failure due to colon cancer.

## Body

### Early Life and Development
Born to a footballer father, Pelé grew up in poverty in Bauru, São Paulo. He learned football from his father, often playing with improvised balls. His youth career included playing futsal (indoor football), which he credited for improving his quick thinking and confidence. He was scouted by coach Waldemar de Brito, who took him to Santos FC.

### Club Career at Santos
Pelé debuted for Santos at 15 in 1956 and quickly became a star, leading the team to numerous state and national titles. His performances attracted European clubs, but the Brazilian government declared him a "national treasure" to prevent his transfer. He led Santos to victory in the Copa Libertadores (1962, 1963) and Intercontinental Cup (1962, 1963). He scored his 1,000th goal in 1969 and retired from Santos in 1975 with 643 goals in 659 games.

### International Career with Brazil
Pelé debuted for Brazil at 16 in 1957. At the 1958 World Cup, he scored six goals, including two in the final, becoming the youngest winner. He was injured during the 1962 World Cup but still earned a winner's medal (awarded retroactively in 2007). After a disappointing 1966 tournament, he returned for the 1970 World Cup, where he played a key role in Brazil's victory, scoring four goals and providing six assists. He scored 77 goals in 92 official games for Brazil.

### Later Career and Legacy
After retiring from Santos, Pelé played for the New York Cosmos (1975–1977), significantly boosting football's popularity in the United States. He was named "Athlete of the Century" by the IOC (1999) and "World Player of the Century" by IFFHS (2000). He served as Brazil's Minister of Sport (1995–1998) and was a UNESCO Goodwill Ambassador. He died in 2022 after a battle with colon cancer.

## Terms
- **Copa Libertadores**: The premier club football tournament in South America.
- **Futsal**: A variant of football played on a smaller, hard court, primarily indoors.
- **Intercontinental Cup**: An annual football match between the winners of the Copa Libertadores and the European Cup.
- **Campeonato Paulista**: The top-flight state football league in São Paulo, Brazil.
- **O Rei**: Portuguese for "The King," Pelé's most famous nickname.
- **O Milésimo**: Portuguese for "The Thousandth," referring to Pelé's 1,000th career goal.
- **Gol de placa**: Portuguese for "goal of the plaque," a term for a remarkable goal worthy of commemoration.
- **Drible da vaca**: A signature dribbling move of Pelé's, meaning "cow dribble."
- **Paradinha**: A signature move of Pelé's, meaning "little stop," used to deceive opponents.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Goal Record Discrepancy**: Pelé's official goal tally varies by source. FIFA reports 1,281 goals in 1,363 games, including friendlies and tour matches, while the RSSSF lists 1,301 goals in 1,390 appearances. His Guinness World Record is for 1,279 goals in 1,363 games.
- **1962 World Cup Medal**: Pelé did not receive a winner's medal at the time because he did not play in the final due to injury. FIFA regulations were changed in 1978, and he was awarded the medal in 2007.
- **Nigerian Civil War Ceasefire**: A popular story claims factions in the Nigerian Civil War agreed to a 48-hour ceasefire in 1969 to watch Pelé play an exhibition in Lagos. Pelé was unsure if a ceasefire occurred, and local researchers found no contemporary reports to confirm it.
- **FIFA Player of the Century**: In 2000, Pelé and Diego Maradona were named joint winners. The award was originally based on an online poll that favored Maradona, but a FIFA committee chose Pelé, leading to the shared prize.

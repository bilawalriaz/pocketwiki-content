# Ho Chi Minh

## Overview
Hồ Chí Minh (1890–1969) was a Vietnamese revolutionary and statesman who led the struggle for independence from French colonial rule and later against South Vietnam and the United States. As founder and first president of the Democratic Republic of Vietnam (North Vietnam), he was the central figure in 20th-century Vietnamese history, shaping its path through anti-colonial war, communist revolution, and national reunification.

- **2 Sep 1969** — Dies of heart failure in Hanoi.

## Body

### Early Life and Overseas Activism
Born Nguyễn Sinh Cung (later Nguyễn Tất Thành), his early life details are uncertain, with multiple birth years and pseudonyms used. He received a French education at the Collège Quốc học in Huế. In 1911, he left Vietnam, working on ships and living in France, the UK, the US, and Brazil. In Paris, he joined the Groupe des Patriotes Annamites and petitioned the Versailles peace conference for Vietnamese rights under the alias Nguyễn Ái Quốc. In 1920, he was a founding member of the French Communist Party. He later studied in the Soviet Union and worked in China, where he founded the Vietnamese Revolutionary Youth League in 1925, a precursor to the Indochinese Communist Party (ICP), which he helped establish in 1930.

### Return to Vietnam and the First Indochina War
Returning to Vietnam in 1941, Hồ Chí Minh led the Việt Minh, a communist-led united front, in a guerrilla war against the Japanese occupation. After the Japanese surrender in August 1945, he led the August Revolution, proclaimed Vietnamese independence, and became president of the Democratic Republic of Vietnam. His government was not internationally recognized. After the French returned, fighting broke out, leading to the First Indochina War (1946–1954). The Việt Minh, supported by China and the Soviet Union after 1950, fought a guerrilla campaign. The war ended with the decisive French defeat at Điện Biên Phủ in 1954. The subsequent Geneva Accords temporarily partitioned Vietnam at the 17th parallel, with nationwide reunification elections planned for 1956, which were never held.

### Leadership of North Vietnam and the Vietnam War
As president of North Vietnam, Hồ Chí Minh oversaw a communist one-party state. Between 1953 and 1956, his government implemented a land reform campaign that included the execution of landlords, later acknowledged as having serious errors. In 1959, he approved a "people's war" to support the Việt Cộng insurgency in the South, and the Ho Chi Minh Trail was established to supply them. Although he stepped down as party leader in 1960 in favor of Lê Duẩn, he remained head of state and a key figure. During the Vietnam War, he was the symbolic leader of the North Vietnamese effort, insisting on the unconditional cessation of American bombing as a precondition for peace talks. He died in 1969, before the war's conclusion in 1975.

### Personal Life and Legacy
Hồ Chí Minh was a writer, poet, and polyglot, fluent in multiple languages. He married Chinese midwife Zeng Xueming in 1926, though the relationship ended. He remained a bachelor for most of his life, cultivating an image as a celibate "father of the revolution." He died in 1969, and his body is embalmed in a mausoleum in Hanoi, despite his wish for cremation. His legacy is complex: he is revered in Vietnam as the national liberator, and his cult of personality draws on traditional Confucian ideals of leadership. Internationally, he is remembered as a key anti-colonial leader of the 20th century.

## Terms
- **Việt Minh**: The League for the Independence of Vietnam, a communist-led national front founded by Hồ Chí Minh in 1941 to fight for independence.
- **Indochinese Communist Party (ICP)**: The communist party founded in 1930, which led the Vietnamese revolution until its merger into the Workers' Party of Vietnam in 1951.
- **First Indochina War (1946–1954)**: The war between the French Union forces and the Việt Minh for control of Vietnam following the return of French colonial rule.
- **Geneva Accords (1954)**: The agreements that ended the First Indochina War and temporarily partitioned Vietnam at the 17th parallel.
- **Ho Chi Minh Trail**: A logistical network of roads and paths through Laos and Cambodia used by North Vietnam to supply the Việt Cộng in South Vietnam during the Vietnam War.
- **Việt Cộng**: The political organization and army in South Vietnam, allied with North Vietnam, that fought against the South Vietnamese government and U.S. forces.
- **August Revolution (1945)**: The nationwide revolution led by the Việt Minh that resulted in the abdication of Emperor Bảo Đại and the proclamation of Vietnamese independence.
- **Điện Biên Phủ (1954)**: The decisive battle where Việt Minh forces defeated the French, leading to the end of the First Indochina War.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Nationalist vs. Communist**: Scholars debate whether Hồ Chí Minh was fundamentally a nationalist who used communism as a tool, or a dedicated Marxist-Leninist. Recent scholarship emphasizes his longstanding commitment to communist ideology.
- **Land Reform Casualties**: The number of executions during the 1953–1956 land reform is disputed, with estimates ranging from 13,500 to 100,000, though declassified documents suggest a lower figure.
- **Early Life Details**: Many aspects of his life before 1941, including his activities in the U.S. and UK, are based on his own claims and are difficult to verify independently.
- **Betrayal of Phan Bội Châu**: A controversial hypothesis suggests Hồ betrayed the rival nationalist leader Phan Bội Châu to the French in 1925, though this is rejected by many historians as likely propaganda.

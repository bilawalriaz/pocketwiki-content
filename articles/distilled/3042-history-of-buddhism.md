# History of Buddhism

## Overview
Buddhism is a major world religion founded in the 5th century BCE in northeastern India by Siddhārtha Gautama. It is based on his teachings of the path to enlightenment and the cessation of suffering. The religion's history is marked by its spread across Asia, the development of diverse philosophical schools, and periods of both royal patronage and decline.

## Timeline
- **5th cent. BCE** — Siddhārtha Gautama attains enlightenment and begins teaching in the Gangetic Plains.
- **c. 268–232 BCE** — Reign of Mauryan Emperor Ashoka; Buddhism gains royal support and spreads widely.
- **185–73 BCE** — Shunga dynasty; Buddhist institutions face possible decline in the Gangetic valley.
- **c. 200 BCE – 100 CE** — Mahāyāna Buddhism emerges as a distinct movement.
- **30–375 CE** — Kushan Empire; Gandharan Buddhism flourishes and spreads along the Silk Road.
- **4th–6th cent. CE** — Gupta Empire; Buddhist universities like Nālandā thrive.
- **7th cent. CE** — Buddhism arrives in Tibet; Chinese pilgrim Xuanzang travels to India.
- **8th–12th cent. CE** — Pāla Empire; Vajrayāna Buddhism develops and spreads to Tibet.
- **1193 CE** — Turkic raiders burn the university of Nālandā, marking a decline in North Indian Buddhism.
- **13th–14th cent. CE** — Mongol Yuan dynasty adopts Tibetan Buddhism as state religion.
- **19th–20th cent. CE** — Buddhist modernism arises; Buddhism spreads to the Western world.

## Body

### Origins and Early Community
Buddhism began with the teachings of Siddhārtha Gautama (the Buddha) in the 5th century BCE. After his death, his monastic community (saṅgha) preserved his teachings through oral recitation. The first major schism divided the saṅgha into the Sthavira and Mahāsāṃghika schools, primarily over monastic discipline (vinaya). This schism eventually led to numerous Early Buddhist Schools, which developed distinct Abhidharma (systematic doctrinal) literature.

### Imperial Patronage and Expansion
The Mauryan Emperor Ashoka (268–232 BCE) was a pivotal figure, promoting Buddhism through edicts, stupas (relic mounds), and missions to regions including Sri Lanka and the Hellenistic world. This period saw the first anthropomorphic Buddha images emerge in the Greco-Buddhist art of Gandhāra. The Kushan Empire (1st–3rd centuries CE) later facilitated the spread of Buddhism, particularly the Sarvāstivāda school, along the Silk Road into Central Asia and China.

### Major Traditions and Philosophical Developments
The Mahāyāna ("Great Vehicle") movement, emphasizing the bodhisattva path, emerged between 150 BCE and 100 CE. Later, under the Gupta and Pāla empires, the Vajrayāna ("Diamond Vehicle") tradition developed, incorporating tantric practices. In India, Buddhism's influence waned after the 12th century due to Islamic invasions and Hindu revival, but it had already taken root elsewhere.

### Transmission to Asia
Buddhism spread to China by the 1st century CE, evolving into distinct schools like Chan (Zen) and Pure Land. It reached Korea in the 4th century and Japan in the 6th century, where it profoundly influenced culture. In Southeast Asia, Indian influence brought both Mahāyāna and Theravāda Buddhism, with the latter becoming dominant in mainland countries like Thailand and Myanmar through later missionary efforts from Sri Lanka.

### Modern Era
The modern period brought challenges like colonialism and competition from other ideologies. Buddhist modernism emerged, emphasizing rationality and meditation. The 20th century saw significant destruction of Buddhist institutions under communist regimes in China, Tibet, and Mongolia, but also a global revival and spread of Buddhism to the West through teachers and immigrant communities.

## Terms
- **Saṅgha**: The monastic community of Buddhist monks and nuns.
- **Nirvāṇa**: The ultimate goal; the extinguishing of the "fires" of greed, hatred, and delusion, ending the cycle of rebirth.
- **Abhidharma**: A genre of Buddhist texts that systematizes and analyzes the teachings of the Buddha.
- **Mahāyāna**: A major branch of Buddhism emphasizing the bodhisattva ideal and the pursuit of Buddhahood for the benefit of all beings.
- **Vajrayāna**: A form of Buddhism emphasizing tantric practices and rituals as a path to enlightenment.
- **Theravāda**: The "School of the Elders," a conservative branch of Buddhism that aims for individual liberation as an arhat.
- **Stupa**: A mound-like structure containing relics, used as a place of meditation and veneration.
- **Dharma**: The teachings of the Buddha; also refers to the cosmic law and order.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The historicity and precise details of the first and second Buddhist councils are questioned by modern scholars.
- The exact causes and timing of the first schism in the saṅgha (between Sthavira and Mahāsāṃghika) are debated.
- The extent of Buddhist influence on early Western thought and the historical reality of Buddhist communities in the Hellenistic world are subjects of scholarly discussion.
- The scale and nature of persecution under rulers like Pushyamitra Shunga are disputed, with Buddhist textual accounts often considered hyperbolic.

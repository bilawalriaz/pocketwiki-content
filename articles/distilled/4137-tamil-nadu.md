# Tamil Nadu

## Overview
Tamil Nadu is a southern Indian state, home to the Tamil people and their ancient Dravidian culture. It is a major economic and industrial hub with a history spanning over 5,500 years, from ancient Sangam-era kingdoms to a modern, highly urbanized state. Its significance lies in its continuous cultural heritage, classical language, and its position as one of India's most industrialized and economically powerful states.

## Timeline
- **c. 580 BCE** — Earliest artefacts from the urban settlement at Keezhadi are dated.
- **500 BCE – 300 CE** — The Sangam period, with the Chera, Chola, and Pandya kingdoms.
- **3rd – 9th century CE** — The Pallava dynasty rules, noted for temple architecture.
- **9th – 13th century CE** — The Chola Empire reaches its zenith under Rajaraja I and Rajendra I.
- **1336 CE** — The Vijayanagara Empire is founded, later conquering Tamil country.
- **1639** — The British East India Company obtains a land grant, leading to Fort St. George.
- **1760** — The Battle of Wandiwash marks a key British victory over the French.
- **1806** — The Vellore Mutiny, an early large-scale sepoy uprising, occurs.
- **1947** — India gains independence; the region becomes Madras State.
- **1956** — States are reorganised linguistically, shaping modern Tamil Nadu.
- **1969** — Madras State is renamed Tamil Nadu.
- **2023** — The state's GSDP reaches ₹27.22 trillion (US$280 billion). |

## Body

### History and Etymology
The name "Tamil Nadu" means "Tamil Country." Archaeological evidence suggests habitation over 385,000 years ago, with continuous cultural history for over 5,500 years. The ancient region, Tamilakam, was ruled by the Sangam-era triumvirate of Cheras, Cholas, and Pandyas, followed by the Pallavas and later the Vijayanagara Empire. European colonisation began in the 17th century, with the British eventually controlling the region as the Madras Presidency. After Indian independence in 1947, it became Madras State, renamed Tamil Nadu in 1969.

### Geography and Climate
Tamil Nadu is on India's southeastern coast, bordered by the Western and Eastern Ghats, the Deccan Plateau, and the Bay of Bengal. The Kaveri river bisects the state. It has a tropical monsoon climate, with the northeast monsoon (November–early March) providing most rainfall. The state has a 1,076 km coastline and diverse ecosystems, including the Western Ghats, a UNESCO World Heritage Site and biodiversity hotspot.

### Demographics and Society
As of the 2011 census, Tamil Nadu had a population of 72.1 million, with over 48.4% urban. Tamil is the official language, spoken by 88.4%. The state has a high literacy rate (80.1% in 2011) and a low fertility rate (1.6 children per woman as of 2017). Hinduism is the majority religion (87.6%). The state is noted for progressive LGBT rights, including a transgender welfare policy and a ban on conversion therapy.

### Administration and Politics
The state is governed from its capital, Chennai, and divided into 38 districts. Politics have been dominated by regional Dravidian parties since the 1960s, specifically the DMK and AIADMK, following anti-Hindi agitations. The state has a unicameral legislature and has produced three Indian presidents.

### Economy
Tamil Nadu has the second-largest economy among Indian states (GSDP of ₹27.22 trillion in 2023). The economy is diversified, with services (55%), manufacturing (32%), and agriculture (13%) as key sectors. It is a major industrial hub, particularly for automobiles ("Detroit of India"), textiles, and IT. Chennai is a major IT and SaaS hub. The state is also a leading producer of poultry, eggs, and several agricultural products.

### Culture and Heritage
Tamil Nadu has a distinct Dravidian culture, evident in its architecture, cuisine, and arts. Dravidian architecture, characterized by monumental temple gateways called gopurams, originated with the Pallavas. Traditional attire includes the sari for women and the veshti for men. The cuisine is rice-based, with extensive use of coconut and spices. The state has a 2,500-year-old literary tradition, from Sangam literature to modern works. Bharatanatyam is a classical dance form that originated here. The Tamil film industry, "Kollywood," is one of India's largest. Major festivals include Pongal, a harvest festival, and Thaipusam.

### Infrastructure
Tamil Nadu has an extensive road and rail network, with Chennai as a major transport hub. The state has three major ports and several airports, including the busy Chennai International Airport. It is a leader in renewable energy, with the largest installed wind power capacity in India. The state faces water scarcity challenges, with per capita availability below the national average, and depends heavily on monsoons and inter-state river water.

## Terms
- **Gopuram:** A monumental, ornate tower at the entrance of a Hindu temple in the Dravidian style.
- **Sangam Era:** A period in Tamil history (c. 500 BCE – 300 CE) characterized by the rule of the Chera, Chola, and Pandya kingdoms and flourishing Tamil literature.
- **Dravidian:** Relating to a family of languages spoken mainly in southern India, or to the people who speak them.
- **GSDP (Gross State Domestic Product):** The total value of goods and services produced within a state in a year.
- **Polygar Wars:** A series of early 19th-century conflicts between the British East India Company and local chieftains (Palaiyakkarars) in Tamil Nadu.
- **Bhakti Movement:** A medieval Hindu devotional movement that emphasized personal devotion to a deity, leading to a revival of Shaivism and Vaishnavism.
- **Veshti:** A traditional unstitched garment wrapped around the waist, worn by men in Tamil Nadu.
- **Kollywood:** The nickname for the Tamil film industry, based in Chennai.
- **Pongal:** A major multi-day Tamil harvest festival dedicated to the Sun God.
- **Jallikattu:** A traditional bull-taming event held during the Pongal festival.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The "dark period" of Tamil history under the Kalabhra dynasty (c. 300–600 CE) is poorly documented, with information inferred from later literature and inscriptions.
- The origin and precise etymology of the word "Tamil" itself is unclear, with multiple theories attested.
- The exact borders of the ancient region "Tamilakam" are described in early texts like the Tolkāppiyam but are subject to historical interpretation.

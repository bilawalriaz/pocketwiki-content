# North Korea

## Overview
North Korea, officially the Democratic People's Republic of Korea (DPRK), is a highly centralized, totalitarian state in East Asia. It is a hereditary dictatorship ruled by the Kim family, characterized by a pervasive cult of personality, state-controlled economy, and extreme international isolation. Its political system, ideology, and human rights record make it one of the most unique and controversial nations in the modern world.

## Timeline
- **1910** — Korea is annexed by Japan, beginning a period of colonial rule.
- **1945** — After Japan's surrender, Korea is divided into Soviet and US occupation zones along the 38th parallel.
- **1948** — The Democratic People's Republic of Korea (North) and the Republic of Korea (South) are established.
- **1950-1953** — The Korean War ends in an armistice, solidifying the division and establishing the DMZ.
- **1991** — The dissolution of the Soviet Union ends critical aid, triggering a severe economic crisis.
- **1994-1998** — A devastating famine kills an estimated 240,000 to 420,000 people.
- **2006** — North Korea conducts its first nuclear weapons test.
- **2011** — Kim Jong Il dies; his son Kim Jong Un becomes the new supreme leader.
- **2024** — North Korea formally abandons its goal of reunifying with South Korea.

## Body

### History and Division
The Korean Peninsula was unified under various dynasties until its annexation by Japan in 1910. After Japan's defeat in 1945, the peninsula was divided into Soviet and US occupation zones. Failed reunification talks led to the establishment of two separate governments in 1948. The North Korean invasion of the South in 1950 sparked the Korean War, which devastated the peninsula and killed an estimated 3 million people. The 1953 armistice created a heavily fortified Demilitarized Zone (DMZ) that persists today. Under Kim Il Sung, North Korea developed its own ideology of Juche (self-reliance) and consolidated a totalitarian state, achieving rapid post-war industrialization with Soviet and Chinese aid.

### Governance and Ideology
North Korea is a totalitarian hereditary dictatorship centered on the Kim family, known as the "Mount Paektu Bloodline." The ruling Workers' Party of Korea (WPK) controls all aspects of state and society. The official state ideology is Kimilsungism–Kimjongilism, which incorporates Juche (self-reliance) and Songun ("military first"). The government maintains a comprehensive cult of personality around the Kim family, with Kim Il Sung and Kim Jong Il revered as "eternal leaders." The state exercises absolute control, enforced by the "Ten Principles for the Establishment of a Monolithic Ideological System."

### Economy and Society
North Korea operates a highly centralized, state-run command economy. After initial post-war growth fueled by Soviet aid, the economy stagnated and then collapsed following the Soviet Union's dissolution in 1991, leading to a severe famine in the 1990s. While limited market reforms have occurred, the state retains control over most means of production. Society is stratified by the *songbun* system, which classifies citizens based on perceived loyalty to the regime, affecting access to resources and opportunities. The population is ethnically homogeneous and subject to extreme restrictions on movement, expression, and information.

### Military and Foreign Relations
North Korea maintains one of the world's largest militaries, with a policy of "military first." It possesses nuclear weapons and ballistic missiles with a range up to 15,000 km. Its foreign relations have been marked by isolation, though it maintains close ties with China and Russia. Relations with South Korea have fluctuated between periods of tension and brief diplomatic engagement, such as the Sunshine Policy era. In 2024, North Korea formally abandoned reunification, labeling South Korea a "hostile state."

### Human Rights
North Korea has been widely condemned for systematic, widespread, and gross human rights violations. A 2014 UN inquiry found evidence of crimes against humanity, including arbitrary detention, torture, forced labor, and severe restrictions on freedoms. The government operates a network of political prison camps (*kwalliso*), where an estimated 200,000 prisoners are held. The state denies these allegations, calling them a political smear campaign.

## Terms
*   **Juche:** The official state ideology of North Korea, emphasizing economic self-sufficiency, military self-reliance, and independent foreign policy.
*   **Songun:** A political principle meaning "military first," prioritizing the Korean People's Army in state affairs and resource allocation.
*   **Songbun:** An ascribed social classification system that sorts citizens into loyalty-based classes, determining their access to education, employment, and resources.
*   **Kwalliso:** Political prison camps where individuals accused of political crimes are detained without trial, often with their families.
*   **DMZ (Demilitarized Zone):** A 4-kilometer-wide, heavily fortified buffer zone separating North and South Korea, established by the 1953 armistice.
*   **Workers' Party of Korea (WPK):** The sole ruling political party of North Korea, which controls all state organs and society.
*   **Supreme People's Assembly (SPA):** North Korea's nominal supreme legislative body, though real power is held by the WPK and the Kim family.
*   **State Affairs Commission (SAC):** The supreme state policy-making organ, headed by the supreme leader.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Human Rights:** While international bodies and NGOs condemn North Korea's human rights record as among the world's worst, the government vehemently denies these claims, attributing them to hostile foreign propaganda.
*   **Economic Viability:** Scholars debate the long-term sustainability of North Korea's command economy, especially after the loss of Soviet aid and the growth of informal markets (*jangmadang*).
*   **Nuclear Intentions:** There is ongoing debate about whether North Korea's nuclear program is primarily for regime survival and deterrence or if it has offensive strategic goals.
*   **Reunification:** The future of the Korean Peninsula is uncertain, with North Korea's 2024 abandonment of reunification marking a significant shift in its official stance.

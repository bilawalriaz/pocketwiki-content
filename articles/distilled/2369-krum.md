# Krum

## Overview
Krum (c. mid 8th century – 814) was the Khan of the First Bulgarian Empire whose energetic rule doubled its territory and established it as a major power. He is remembered for his brutal conflicts with the Byzantine Empire, which resulted in the deaths of two Byzantine emperors, and for instituting Bulgaria's first known written law code, which centralized state authority.

## Timeline
- **c. mid 8th century** — Krum is born.
- **796–803** — Krum becomes Khan of the First Bulgarian Empire.
- **c. 805** — Defeats the Avar Khaganate, establishing a common border with the Frankish Empire.
- **807** — Bulgarian forces defeat the Byzantine army in the Struma valley.
- **809** — Krum besieges and captures Serdica (Sofia), slaughtering its garrison.
- **26 July 811** — Krum's army traps and kills Byzantine Emperor Nikephoros I at the Varbitsa Pass.
- **812** — Krum captures Mesembria (Nesebar) after Byzantine Emperor Michael I Rangabe rejects peace.
- **22 June 813** — Krum defeats Michael I at the Battle of Versinikia, leading to the emperor's abdication.
- **813** — Krum advances to Constantinople, performing a pagan ceremony and beginning siege preparations.
- **814** — Krum dies while planning a major assault on Constantinople.

## Body

### Origins and Expansion
Krum's family background is unknown, though he may have been a descendant of Khan Kubrat. His reign began with the destruction of the Avar Khaganate around 805, which expanded Bulgarian territory northward to the Tisza region and created a direct border with the Frankish Empire.

### Conflict with Byzantium
Krum's expansion led to war with the Byzantine Empire. After capturing Serdica in 809, he faced a massive Byzantine expedition led by Emperor Nikephoros I in 811. Nikephoros captured and burned the Bulgarian capital, Pliska, but Krum mobilized a large army, including armed peasants, to ambush the returning Byzantines. In the Battle of the Varbitsa Pass on 26 July 811, the Byzantine army was destroyed and Nikephoros was killed; Krum reportedly made a drinking cup from his skull.

Krum's subsequent conflicts with emperors Michael I Rangabe and Leo V the Armenian further demonstrated his military prowess. He defeated Michael I at the Battle of Versinikia in 813, forcing his abdication. After Leo V attempted to assassinate him during peace talks, Krum retaliated by devastating Eastern Thrace and besieging Constantinople, where he began constructing siege works. He died in 814 before launching a full assault.

### Law and Legacy
Krum instituted the first known written Bulgarian law code. This code established state subsidies for beggars and protection for the poor, while severely punishing offenses like drunkenness, slander, and robbery. His laws helped centralize the state, unifying Slavs and Bulgars under a strict but just legal framework.

## Terms
- **Khan**: The title for a ruler of the Bulgars and other Turkic peoples.
- **First Bulgarian Empire**: The medieval Bulgarian state founded in 681, which reached its territorial peak under Krum.
- **Avar Khaganate**: A nomadic empire in the Carpathian Basin that was destroyed by Krum's forces.
- **Varbitsa Pass**: A mountain pass in the Balkans where Krum ambushed and destroyed the Byzantine army in 811.
- **Law code**: A written set of laws; Krum's code was the first known in Bulgarian history, centralizing state authority.
- **Kavkhan**: A high-ranking official or military commander in the Bulgarian state, second to the Khan.

## Debates and open questions
The exact circumstances of Krum's accession to power and his family lineage are unknown. Scholars speculate he may have been a descendant of Khan Kubrat through his son Kuber, but this is unconfirmed. The precise nature and scale of his siege preparations against Constantinople in 814 are also debated, with sources describing elaborate "turtles," battle towers, and flamethrowers.

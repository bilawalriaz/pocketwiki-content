# Brazzaville

## Overview
Brazzaville is the capital and largest city of the Republic of the Congo, serving as the nation's financial and administrative center. Located on the Congo River directly opposite Kinshasa, capital of the Democratic Republic of the Congo, it is the only place in the world where two national capitals face each other across a river. The city's history is deeply tied to French colonialism and has been marked by periods of conflict and regional importance.

## Timeline
- **10 September 1880** — Founded by French explorer Pierre Savorgnan de Brazza on an existing Teke settlement.
- **1884** — Berlin Conference officially places the area under French control.
- **1904** — Becomes the capital of the French Congo.
- **1910** — Capital of the newly formed French Equatorial Africa federation.
- **1928** — Local population revolts against French rule during construction of the Congo-Ocean Railway.
- **1934** — Congo-Ocean Railway opens, linking Brazzaville to the Atlantic port of Pointe-Noire.
- **1940–1943** — Serves as the de facto capital of Free France during World War II.
- **1944** — Hosts the Brazzaville Conference to redefine France's relationship with its African colonies.
- **1980** — Becomes a "commune" separated from the surrounding Pool Department.
- **1990s** — Experiences civil wars resulting in thousands of deaths and mass displacement.
- **2013** — Designated a City of Music by UNESCO.
- **2016** — Fighting between police and militia kills at least 18 people.

## Body
**Founding and Colonial Era**
Brazzaville was founded by the French colonial empire in 1880 upon an indigenous Bateke settlement. Its establishment was part of the "Scramble for Africa," with French explorer Pierre Savorgnan de Brazza securing a treaty with the local Tio king. The city grew in rivalry with Belgian-controlled Léopoldville (now Kinshasa) on the opposite riverbank. It became the capital of French Equatorial Africa in 1910, a federation that included Gabon, the Central African Republic, and Chad. Major infrastructure, like the Congo-Ocean Railway (opened 1934), was built during this period, often at a high human cost.

**Modern History and Conflict**
During World War II, Brazzaville remained under Free French control and hosted a pivotal 1944 conference aimed at reforming colonial relations. After independence, the city was frequently a staging ground for regional conflicts. Civil wars in the 1990s caused significant civilian casualties and displacement. More recently, it has faced tensions related to refugees from the Democratic Republic of the Congo and internal militia clashes.

**Geography and Urban Context**
Brazzaville is situated on the north bank of the Congo River, just below Pool Malebo. It is 506 km inland from the Atlantic Ocean. The city is notably flat, with an altitude of 317 m. Its unique geographical feature is its direct proximity to Kinshasa; the two capitals are within sight of each other, forming a combined conurbation of about 12 million people, though significant political and infrastructure challenges limit their integration. A proposed bridge project aims to connect them.

**Administration and Economy**
Administratively, Brazzaville is both a department and a commune, divided into nine arrondissements. Its economy is based on its role as a port and industrial center. Industries include textiles, manufacturing, and machine shops. It serves as a key hub for collecting raw materials like rubber and wood for export via the railway to Pointe-Noire. The city hosts regional offices for many international organizations, including the WHO's regional office for Africa.

**Culture and Society**
The city's culture reflects its colonial history and modern urban life. Notable landmarks include the Pierre Savorgnan de Brazza Mausoleum and the Nabemba Tower. The Poto-Poto School of Painting is a significant artistic institution. The population is predominantly Christian, with the Roman Catholic Church being the most prevalent denomination. Education is anchored by the public Marien Ngouabi University, founded in 1971.

## Terms
- **Commune**: A French-style municipality; Brazzaville is a commune contained within a department of the same name.
- **Arrondissement**: A subdivision of a commune; Brazzaville is divided into nine of these boroughs.
- **French Equatorial Africa**: A federation of French colonial states (1910–1960) that included the territory of modern-day Republic of the Congo, with Brazzaville as its capital.
- **Pool Malebo**: A lake-like widening of the Congo River on which Brazzaville and Kinshasa are located.
- **Congo-Ocean Railway**: A railway line connecting Brazzaville to the Atlantic port of Pointe-Noire, bypassing the river's rapids.
- **Free France**: The French government-in-exile during WWII that opposed Nazi occupation; Brazzaville served as its de facto capital from 1940–1943.
- **Brazzaville Declaration**: A 2018 agreement signed by the Republic of the Congo, DRC, and Indonesia to conserve the Cuvette Centrale peatlands.
- **Tio Kingdom**: A precolonial Teke kingdom whose territory included the site of modern Brazzaville.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The proposal for a Brazzaville–Kinshasa Bridge, signed in 2018, faces significant political and infrastructure challenges, leaving the future of meaningful connection between the two capitals uncertain.
- The city's role in regional conflicts is ongoing, with recent accusations from the UN and DRC government regarding the deportation of refugees from the DRC.

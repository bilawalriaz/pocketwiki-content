# Kermanshah

## Overview
Kermanshah is a major city in western Iran, serving as the capital of Kermanshah province. Located in the Zagros Mountains, it is a historically significant urban center with a continuous human presence from the Paleolithic era. The city is a cultural and economic hub for the region, known for its rich archaeological heritage and as the center of the largest Kurdish population in Iran.

## Timeline
- **c. 3000 BC** — Earliest evidence of human settlement in the region.
- **390 AD** — City founded by Sasanian King Bahram IV, who held the title "Kirmanshah" (King of Kerman).
- **226 AD** — Sasanian Empire established; Kermanshah becomes a capital and royal summer resort.
- **629 AD** — City conquered by Arab forces.
- **11th century** — Under Seljuk rule, becomes a cultural and commercial center.
- **1257 AD** — Sacked by the Mongols under Hulegu.
- **1723–1732** — Occupied by the Ottoman Empire.
- **1914–1917** — Occupied by Russian and then Ottoman armies during World War I.
- **1980–1988** — Harshly damaged during the Iran–Iraq War.
- **2016** — Population measured as 946,651 in the national census.

## Body

### Etymology and Founding
The name "Kermanshah" derives from the Sasanian title *Kirmanshah*, meaning "King of Kerman." This title was held by Prince Bahram, son of Shapur III. In 390 AD, after becoming King Bahram IV, he founded the city and applied his title to it.

### Ancient and Prehistoric Significance
The Kermanshah area is a cradle of prehistoric cultures, with evidence of human occupation from the Lower Paleolithic period. Neanderthal remains have been found in local caves, and some of the earliest human settlements in the region, such as Ganj-Darreh, were established between 8,000 and 10,000 years ago. The city contains several archaeological mound sites, including Chogha Kaboud and Chogha Golan.

### Sasanian and Islamic Eras
In Sasanian mythology, the city's construction is attributed to King Tahmuras. Historically, it was a significant Sasanian capital and royal health center. After the Arab conquest in 629 AD, it flourished under the Seljuks as a cultural center. The city was sacked by Mongols in 1257 and later fortified by Safavids. It was occupied by Ottomans in the 18th century and repulsed an Ottoman attack under the Qajars.

### Modern History and Demographics
Kermanshah played a role in the Iranian Constitutional Revolution and was occupied by foreign armies during World War I. It was severely damaged in the Iran–Iraq War (1980–1988). The city's population was 946,651 in 2016, with a 2025 estimate of 1,117,000. The primary languages are Southern Kurdish (specifically the Kermashani dialect) and Persian. While most inhabitants are Shia Muslims, the city also has Sunni Muslim, Christian, and Yarsan communities.

### Climate and Landmarks
Kermanshah has a montane climate with hot, dry summers and cold winters. Its main sights include the UNESCO World Heritage Site **Behistun Inscription**, a crucial multi-lingual cuneiform text of Darius I that was key to deciphering ancient scripts. **Taq-e Bostan** features large Sasanian rock reliefs, including an equestrian figure of Khosrow II. The city also has Qajar-era monuments like the Grand Bazaar and Takyeh Moaven-ol-Molk.

## Terms
- **Kirmanshah**: Sasanian title meaning "King of Kerman," which became the city's name.
- **Behistun Inscription**: A multi-lingual cuneiform inscription by Darius I, crucial for deciphering ancient scripts.
- **Taq-e Bostan**: A site with large Sasanian rock reliefs near Kermanshah.
- **Kermashani**: The variety of Southern Kurdish spoken in Kermanshah.
- **Yarsanism**: A religion followed by some inhabitants of Kermanshah.
- **Montane climate**: A climate typical of mountainous regions, with significant temperature variation.
- **Tekyeh**: A building or hall for religious gatherings, especially during Muharram.
- **Daroongara**: An inward-oriented house design common in traditional Iranian architecture.

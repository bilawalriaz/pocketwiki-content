# Andorra

## Overview
Andorra is a small, landlocked parliamentary co-principality in the Pyrenees mountains between France and Spain. Its unique political system, with two co-princes—the Bishop of Urgell and the President of France—was established in the 13th century. The country's modern identity is shaped by its Catalan culture, mountainous geography, and an economy heavily reliant on tourism and banking.

## Timeline
- **c. 9500 BCE** — La Balma de la Margineda is settled as a seasonal camp.
- **839** — First document mentioning Andorra as a territory (Acta de Consagració i Dotació).
- **988** — Andorran valleys transferred from the Count of Urgell to the Diocese of Urgell.
- **1278** — First Paréage signed, establishing shared sovereignty between the Bishop of Urgell and the Count of Foix.
- **1419** — The Consell de la Terra (General Council of the Valleys), Andorra's parliament, is founded.
- **1607** — Edict establishes the French head of state as co-prince alongside the Bishop of Urgell.
- **1866** — The Nova Reforma (New Reform) modernizes institutions and establishes the tricolor flag.
- **1933** — France briefly occupies Andorra following social unrest.
- **1993** — A new constitution is approved by referendum, establishing a modern parliamentary democracy.
- **1993** — Andorra joins the United Nations and the Council of Europe.
- **2004** — The Madriu-Perafita-Claror Valley becomes Andorra's first UNESCO World Heritage Site.

## Body

### History and Political System
Andorra's origins are traditionally linked to a grant from Charlemagne. Its political form was solidified by the Paréage of 1278, which created a co-principality under the Bishop of Urgell and the Count of Foix (a title later inherited by the French head of state). This system persisted for centuries, with the General Council (parliament) founded in 1419. After periods of relative isolation and social unrest in the 19th and early 20th centuries, Andorra adopted a modern constitution in 1993. This established a parliamentary democracy where sovereignty resides with the people, while the Bishop of Urgell and the President of France serve as ceremonial co-princes with equal constitutional powers.

### Geography and Climate
Andorra is the sixth-smallest state in Europe, covering 468 km² in the eastern Pyrenees. Its terrain is predominantly mountainous, with an average elevation of 1,996 m. The highest point is Coma Pedrosa (2,942 m), and the principal river is the Gran Valira. The climate is high-mountain Mediterranean, with mild summers and long, cold winters. Significant warming has been recorded since 1950, which threatens the winter tourism sector by reducing snowfall.

### Economy and Tourism
Tourism is the mainstay of the economy, accounting for roughly 80% of GDP and attracting about 8 million visitors annually. The country is known for its ski resorts, duty-free shopping, and banking sector. Historically, Andorra had very low unemployment and no income tax. However, following international pressure, it introduced a 10% business tax in 2012 and a sales tax in 2013. While not an EU member, it has a customs union with the EU and uses the euro as its official currency.

### Society and Culture
The Andorran people are a Romance group of Catalan descent. Catalan is the sole official language, though Spanish, Portuguese, and French are widely used due to immigration and proximity. The population is approximately 79,000, with a large foreign resident community. Culturally, Andorra shares strong ties with Catalonia, evident in its language, Romanesque art, and traditions like the *castellers* (human towers). The national holiday is Our Lady of Meritxell Day on 8 September.

## Terms
- **Co-principality**: A system of government where two individuals jointly hold the position of head of state.
- **Paréage**: A feudal treaty establishing shared sovereignty over a territory, in Andorra's case between the Bishop of Urgell and the Count of Foix.
- **General Council (Consell General de les Valls)**: Andorra's unicameral parliament, founded in 1419.
- **Syndic General**: The speaker and president of the General Council.
- **Head of Government**: The prime minister of Andorra, elected by the General Council.
- **Sometent**: A traditional communal militia, now part of the civil protection system.
- **Nova Reforma**: The 1866 reform that modernized Andorra's political institutions and symbols.
- **Catalan**: A Romance language and the sole official language of Andorra.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The etymology of the name "Andorra" is uncertain and debated, with hypotheses linking it to the ancient Iberian tribe Andosini or to pre-Roman water sources.
- The country's relationship with the European Union is evolving. An association agreement was authorized for signature in 2026, which would allow participation in the internal market but requires ratification and a potential consultative referendum in Andorra.
- The impact of climate change on Andorra's crucial ski tourism industry, due to reduced snowfall and shorter seasons, is a significant ongoing concern.

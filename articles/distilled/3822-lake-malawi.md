# Lake Malawi

## Overview
Lake Malawi (also called Lake Nyasa) is a large African Great Lake in the East African Rift system, bordering Malawi, Mozambique, and Tanzania. It is the world's fourth-largest freshwater lake by volume and is globally significant for its extraordinary biodiversity, particularly its hundreds of endemic cichlid fish species, which are a major model for studying rapid evolution. The lake's resources are vital for regional economies but face threats from overfishing, pollution, and climate change.

## Body

### Geography and Geology
Lake Malawi is a long (560-580 km), narrow (75 km wide) rift lake with a surface area of about 29,600 km². It is meromictic, meaning its water layers do not mix, creating a permanent stratification with an oxygen-free zone below about 250 m. The lake's water is alkaline, warm, and clear. Geologically, it formed in the East African Rift valley. While traditionally estimated at 1-2 million years old, evidence suggests its basin began forming about 8.6 million years ago. Water levels have fluctuated dramatically over time, with major low-water periods potentially causing near-complete drying.

### Borders and Transport
The lake's surface is divided between three countries, but the border between Malawi and Tanzania is disputed. Malawi claims the entire surface not in Mozambique, while Tanzania claims a mid-lake boundary. The dispute stems from differing interpretations of the 1890 Heligoland Treaty and colonial administrative changes. The Malawi-Mozambique boundary was set in 1954, with exceptions for Likoma and Chizumulu islands, which remain Malawian. The lake is a key transport route, served by several ferries, including the historic MV Chauncy Maples (1901) and the MV Ilala (1951).

### Wildlife and Fisheries
The lake is a major food source, with fisheries centered on species like chambo (four *Oreochromis* species), the Lake Malawi sardine, and kampango catfish. These stocks are severely threatened by overfishing and pollution. The lake is most famous for its cichlid fish, with at least 700 species (possibly up to 1,000), nearly all endemic. These cichlids, divided into groups like the rock-dwelling mbuna and open-water haps, have undergone rapid evolutionary radiation. Non-cichlid fish include catfish, eels, and sardines. The lake also hosts endemic snails, a single crab species, and famous swarms of lake flies (*Chaoborus edulis*), which are harvested locally for food.

### Modern Issues
A 2015 incident involved a non-radioactive sediment leak from a uranium mine, which government tests found had no environmental effect. The lake's water levels have recently declined, raising concerns about climate change impacts. The lake is also a venue for long-distance swimming, with the 25 km crossing and the full-length swim being notable achievements.

## Terms
- **Meromictic lake**: A lake with layers of water that do not intermix.
- **Cichlid**: A diverse family of fish, many species of which are popular aquarium fish.
- **Endemic**: Native and restricted to a certain place.
- **Mbuna**: A group of rock-dwelling, territorial cichlids in Lake Malawi.
- **Haplochromine (hap)**: A large group of mouthbrooding cichlids in the lake, including open-water and predatory types.
- **Chambo**: A common name for four endemic *Oreochromis* cichlid species important to fisheries.
- **Aufwuchs**: A complex mixture of algae and microorganisms that grows on submerged surfaces, a key food source for mbuna.
- **Thermocline**: The layer in a lake where temperature changes rapidly with depth.
- **Oxic-anoxic boundary**: The depth in a lake where oxygen is no longer present.

## Debates and Open questions
- **Border Dispute**: The exact location of the international boundary between Malawi and Tanzania in the lake remains unresolved, with both countries citing the 1890 Heligoland Treaty.
- **Lake Age**: While traditionally considered 1-2 million years old, newer evidence suggests the basin began forming about 8.6 million years ago, with deep water appearing 4.5 mya.
- **Cichlid Speciation**: The exact number of cichlid species is uncertain (700-1,000) due to undescribed species and complex variation. The timing of their rapid radiations is an active area of study.
- **Bilharzia Transmission**: The endemic snail *B. nyassanus* recently became an intermediate host for bilharzia, possibly due to the decline of snail-eating cichlids from overfishing.

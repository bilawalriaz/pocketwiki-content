# Dennis Ritchie

## Overview
Dennis Ritchie was an American computer scientist whose work at Bell Labs fundamentally shaped modern computing. He created the C programming language and co-developed the Unix operating system with Ken Thompson, tools whose principles and derivatives underpin the vast majority of software today, from operating systems to mobile applications.

## Timeline
- **September 9, 1941** — Born in Bronxville, New York.
- **1963** — Graduated from Harvard University with degrees in physics and applied mathematics.
- **1967** — Began working at Bell Labs Computing Science Research Center.
- **c. 1969-1970** — Developed the B language (with Ken Thompson) and later the C language to create Unix.
- **1973** — Co-authored a paper describing the Unix operating system, written in C.
- **1978** — Co-authored *The C Programming Language* (K&R) with Brian Kernighan.
- **1983** — Received the Turing Award with Ken Thompson.
- **1990** — Received the IEEE Richard W. Hamming Medal with Ken Thompson.
- **1999** — Received the National Medal of Technology with Ken Thompson.
- **2007** — Retired from Lucent Technologies as head of System Software Research Department.
- **c. October 12, 2011** — Found dead at his home in Berkeley Heights, New Jersey.

## Body

### Early Life and Career
Born in 1941, Ritchie was the son of Bell Labs scientist Alistair E. Ritchie. After graduating from Harvard in 1963, he joined Bell Labs in 1967. He began a PhD at Harvard but never officially received the degree, though his lost dissertation was later found in 2020.

### Creation of C and Unix
In the late 1960s, Ritchie and Ken Thompson worked on the Multics project. After Multics was abandoned at Bell Labs, Thompson began developing a new operating system on a PDP-7 machine. To move beyond assembly language, Thompson created the B language. Ritchie then created the C language to replace B, providing a more powerful tool for system programming. Together, they used C to write the Unix operating system, which was designed to be portable across different hardware platforms. This portability was a key innovation, allowing Unix to spread widely.

### Later Work and Legacy
Ritchie continued to contribute to operating systems, including Plan 9 and Inferno. He also collaborated on a classified cryptanalysis project in the 1970s. His work on C and Unix established foundational concepts in computing. C influenced numerous subsequent languages, including C++, Objective-C, C#, and Java. Unix and its derivatives (like Linux and BSD) became the basis for many modern operating systems. Ritchie viewed Linux and BSD as healthy continuations of the Unix philosophy he and Thompson started. He retired in 2007 and died in 2011.

## Terms
- **C programming language**: A high-level, general-purpose programming language created by Ritchie. It is low-level enough to map closely to hardware instructions yet portable, making it foundational for system and application software.
- **Unix**: A multi-user, multitasking operating system co-developed by Ritchie and Thompson. Its design principles and code structure influenced countless operating systems.
- **B language**: A precursor to C, created by Ken Thompson, used in the early development of Unix.
- **Multics**: An influential early time-sharing operating system that Ritchie and Thompson worked on before creating Unix.
- **Portability**: The ability of software (like an operating system or language) to be run on different types of computer hardware with minimal modification.
- **Bell Labs**: The research and development organization where Ritchie worked and where Unix and C were created.
- **K&R**: The common shorthand for *The C Programming Language*, the seminal book on C co-authored by Ritchie and Brian Kernighan.
- **POSIX**: A family of standards for maintaining compatibility between operating systems, based on Unix.

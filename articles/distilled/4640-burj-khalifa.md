# Burj Khalifa

## Overview
The Burj Khalifa is the world's tallest structure, standing at 829.8 m (2,722 ft). Located in Dubai, United Arab Emirates, it was designed as the centerpiece of a large-scale development to diversify Dubai's economy from oil to tourism and services. Its construction and completion during the 2008 financial crisis led to its renaming in honor of the UAE's president, who provided crucial financial support.

## Timeline
- **12 January 2004** — Construction begins with excavation.
- **21 September 2004** — Construction of the structure starts.
- **21 July 2007** — Surpasses Taipei 101 to become the world's tallest building.
- **12 September 2007** — Becomes the world's tallest freestanding structure, surpassing the CN Tower.
- **7 April 2008** — Becomes the tallest human-made structure ever built, surpassing the KVLY-TV Mast.
- **1 September 2008** — Surpasses the Warsaw Radio Mast to become the tallest structure ever built.
- **17 January 2009** — Topped out at its final height of 829.8 m (2,722 ft).
- **1 October 2009** — Exterior completion is announced.
- **4 January 2010** — Officially opens and is renamed from "Burj Dubai" to "Burj Khalifa."
- **10 March 2010** — Certified as the world's tallest building by the Council on Tall Buildings and Urban Habitat.

## Body

### Conception and Design
The tower was conceived as the centerpiece of the "Downtown Dubai" development, a decision based on the government's strategy to diversify Dubai's economy from oil to a service and tourism-based model. Designed by the architectural firm Skidmore, Owings & Merrill (SOM), its Y-shaped tripartite floor plan is derived from Islamic architecture and optimized for residential and hotel space. The design uses a buttressed central core and wings for support, with 27 setbacks in a spiral pattern to minimize wind loading. The structure is primarily reinforced concrete, with some structural steel salvaged from the demolished Palace of the Republic in East Berlin.

### Construction and Engineering
Construction began in 2004 and involved a joint venture of Samsung C&T, BESIX, and Arabtec. The project faced challenges, including weak bedrock and extreme desert temperatures. Concrete was poured at night with ice added to prevent cracking. The foundation uses 192 piles buried over 50 m deep to support the building's weight of approximately 450,000 tonnes. The spire, considered "vanity height," is composed of over 4,000 tonnes of structural steel and houses communications equipment. The cladding system features over 26,000 reflective glass panels designed to withstand Dubai's climate.

### Records and Features
The Burj Khalifa holds numerous world records, including tallest structure, tallest skyscraper, building with the most floors (163), and highest restaurant (At.mosphere on the 122nd floor). It contains 57 elevators, including the world's fastest double-deck elevators, and 900 residential apartments. The exterior is cleaned by 36 workers over three to four months using a system of tracks and cradles. Outside, the Dubai Fountain is the world's largest choreographed fountain system.

### Economic Context and Renaming
The project's completion coincided with the Great Recession, which left Dubai in significant debt. The federal government of the UAE, led by then-President Khalifa bin Zayed Al Nahyan, provided tens of billions of dollars in financial support. As a gesture of gratitude, the tower was renamed from "Burj Dubai" to "Burj Khalifa" during its inauguration. Initially, rents plummeted and most apartments were vacant, but occupancy recovered to about 80% by October 2012.

### Controversies and Incidents
The construction relied heavily on migrant labor from South Asia and East Asia, with reports of low wages and poor working conditions. A 2006 protest by about 2,500 workers over delayed buses resulted in a riot causing significant damage. The building has been the site of several incidents, including a worker's suicide in 2011 and a disputed tourist death in 2015. It has also been a venue for authorized and unauthorized BASE jumping and climbing feats.

## Terms
- **Megatall skyscraper**: A skyscraper over 600 m (1,969 ft) in height.
- **Buttressed core**: A structural system where a central core is supported by radiating wings or "buttresses," allowing for great height.
- **Tripartite floor geometry**: A Y-shaped floor plan with three wings, designed to maximize space and structural stability.
- **Vanity height**: The portion of a building's height that is not usable floor space, often housing antennas or spires.
- **Cathodic protection**: A technique used to control the corrosion of metal (like steel rebar) by making it the cathode of an electrochemical cell.
- **Shamal**: A strong, dry, and dusty northwesterly wind that blows in the Persian Gulf region.
- **Great Recession**: The severe global economic downturn from 2007 to 2009, which heavily impacted Dubai's real estate market.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The classification of the spire as "vanity height" is noted in a Council on Tall Buildings and Urban Habitat study, which states the empty spire "could be a skyscraper on its own."
- The conditions and treatment of the migrant labor force used in construction have been a subject of criticism from reports by the BBC and Human Rights Watch.
- The true cause of the 2015 death of tourist Laura Vanessa Nunes is disputed, with Dubai police and Portuguese embassy documents providing conflicting accounts.

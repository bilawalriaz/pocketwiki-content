# Isfahan

## Overview
Isfahan is a major city in central Iran, historically significant as a capital of the Safavid Empire and renowned for its Persian-Muslim architecture. It is the third-most populous city in Iran and a major cultural and economic hub, though it faces contemporary challenges like water shortages and environmental stress.

## Timeline
- **c. 3000 BC** — Earliest human habitation in the region (Palaeolithic period).
- **642 CE** — Arab conquest; Isfahan becomes capital of al-Jibal province.
- **11th century** — Seljuk Turks make Isfahan their capital; city grows under Malik-Shah I.
- **1387** — Timur sacks the city, reportedly killing 70,000 citizens.
- **1598** — Abbas the Great moves the Safavid capital to Isfahan, beginning its golden age.
- **1722** — Central Asian invaders sack Isfahan, leading to Safavid decline.
- **1971** — First master development program created by a German firm.
- **2015** — Isfahan Metro opens.
- **2018** — Farmers protest over water rights and shortages.
- **2025** — Explosion at an ammunition factory in central Isfahan.

## Body

### Historical Development
Isfahan's history spans from prehistoric times. Under the Achaemenid Empire (538 BCE), it became an early example of religious tolerance, settling Jewish immigrants from Babylon. The city was a regional center under the Medes and Parthians. After the Arab conquest in 642 CE, it grew under the Buyid and Seljuk dynasties. Its golden age came under the Safavid Empire, when Abbas the Great made it the capital in 1598. He rebuilt the city, forcibly resettling craftsmen from across the empire, including Armenians who established the New Julfa quarter. The city was sacked in 1722 and later declined as the capital moved to other cities.

### Geography and Climate
Isfahan is located on the plain of the Zayandeh River at the foothills of the Zagros Mountains. It has a cold desert climate (Köppen BWk) with hot summers and cold winters. The city relies on an ancient network of canals (madi) from the Zayandeh River for water. Severe ecological issues include drought, water diversion, and subsidence, with the aquifer level decreasing by one meter annually. As of 2020, it had the worst air quality among major Iranian cities.

### Economy and Industry
Isfahan's economy is diverse. Historically known as the "Manchester of Persia" for its textile industry, it now produces carpets, steel, and handicrafts. The Mobarakeh Steel Company is the largest steel producer in the Middle East and North Africa. The province hosts significant nuclear facilities, including the Uranium Conversion Facility (UCF), and military installations. Agriculture and aquaculture are also important, though water shortages are a critical issue.

### Culture and Architecture
The city is famed for its Persian-Muslim architecture, including Naqsh-e Jahan Square (a UNESCO World Heritage Site), the Grand Bazaar, and historic bridges like the Si-o-se-pol. The Isfahan School of painting flourished during the Safavid era. Local cuisine includes dishes like *gosh-e fil* and sweets like *gaz*. The city has a rich tradition in music, with the *Bayat-e Esfahan* mode named after it.

## Terms
- **Safavid Empire**: The ruling dynasty of Iran from 1501–1736, which established Isfahan as its capital.
- **Zayandeh River**: The main river flowing through Isfahan, vital for the city's water supply and agriculture.
- **Madi**: An artificial network of canals built during the Safavid era to channel water from the Zayandeh River.
- **Naqsh-e Jahan Square**: A large, historic square in Isfahan, surrounded by mosques and palaces, and a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
- **New Julfa**: An Armenian quarter in Isfahan established during the Safavid era for forcibly resettled Armenians.
- **Cold Desert Climate (BWk)**: A climate classification characterized by low precipitation and large temperature variations.
- **Uranium Conversion Facility (UCF)**: A nuclear facility in Isfahan involved in processing uranium.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The exact origins and early history of Isfahan before the Sasanian dynasty are unclear, with little known.
- The reported Sasanian circular city design of Isfahan has not been archaeologically uncovered.
- Isfahan is suspected of being a primary location for Iran's nuclear weapons development program, a claim Iran disputes.

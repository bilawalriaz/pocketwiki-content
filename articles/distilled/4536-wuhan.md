# Wuhan

## Overview
Wuhan is the capital of Hubei province and the most populous city in Central China. Historically a major port and transportation hub at the confluence of the Yangtze and Han rivers, it has been a pivotal site in modern Chinese history, from the 1911 revolution to the COVID-19 pandemic. Today, it is a leading economic, educational, and research center.

## Timeline
- **c. 1510–1460 BC** — Panlongcheng archaeological site (Erligang culture) established in the area.
- **AD 223** — Foundation of Wuchang; construction of the Yellow Crane Tower begins.
- **1861** — Hankou opens as a treaty port; foreign concessions are established.
- **1889–1907** — Zhang Zhidong modernizes industry and education in Hubei.
- **October 10, 1911** — Wuchang Uprising begins, leading to the fall of the Qing dynasty.
- **1927** — Wuchang, Hankou, and Hanyang merge to form Wuhan City; briefly a national capital.
- **1938** — Battle of Wuhan during the Second Sino-Japanese War.
- **1957** — Wuhan Yangtze River Bridge opens, linking north and south China by rail.
- **1967** — Wuhan Incident (July 20th Incident) during the Cultural Revolution.
- **December 2019** — SARS-CoV-2 virus first identified in Wuhan.
- **January 23, 2020** — Wuhan enters the world's first COVID-19 lockdown.

## Body

### Geography and Climate
Wuhan is located at the confluence of the Yangtze and Han rivers, dividing the city into its historic "Three Towns": Wuchang, Hankou, and Hanyang. The terrain is mostly low-lying alluvial plain, with nearly 200 lakes, making water cover a quarter of the urban area. The climate is humid subtropical with four distinct seasons, historically known as one of China's "Three Furnacelike Cities" for its hot summers.

### History
The area has been settled for 3,500 years. During the Han dynasty, Hanyang became a busy port. The city's modern significance grew in the late 19th century under Qing official Zhang Zhidong, who established heavy industries and modern schools. The Wuchang Uprising of October 10, 1911, launched from Wuhan, overthrew the Qing dynasty and ended 2,000 years of imperial rule. Wuhan served as a provisional national capital in 1927 and again as the wartime capital in 1937–1938. It was the site of the major Battle of Wuhan in 1938 and was heavily bombed by U.S. forces in 1944. After the Communist victory in 1949, the city was rebuilt as an industrial center. The 1957 Yangtze River Bridge was a key infrastructure project. The city experienced significant unrest during the Cultural Revolution (1967) and was the site of student protests in 1989. In December 2019, it became the epicenter of the COVID-19 pandemic, with a strict lockdown imposed from January 23 to April 8, 2020.

### Economy and Industry
Wuhan is a major industrial, commercial, and transportation hub, sometimes called "the Chicago of China." Its economy is driven by automobile manufacturing (headquarters of Dongfeng Motor), iron and steel, and high-tech industries, particularly in optoelectronics and biotechnology. The city hosts numerous national development zones and research institutes. It is a leading center for scientific research output in China and globally.

### Education and Research
Wuhan is a major educational hub with 82 higher educational institutions, including prominent universities like Huazhong University of Science and Technology and Wuhan University. It ranks among the top Chinese cities for scientific research output and is home to over 350 research institutes and the Wuhan Institute of Virology.

### Transportation
Wuhan is a critical national transportation hub. The Wuhan Yangtze River Bridge (1957) was the first bridge to link the Beijing–Hankou and Guangdong–Hankou railways. The city has three major railway stations and is a key node for high-speed rail. The Wuhan Metro, opened in 2004, is a rapid transit system with 11 lines. Wuhan Tianhe International Airport is a major air hub.

## Terms
- **Three Towns of Wuhan**: The historic cities of Wuchang, Hankou, and Hanyang, which merged in 1927 to form modern Wuhan.
- **Wuchang Uprising**: The October 10, 1911, revolt in Wuhan that triggered the Xinhai Revolution and the end of the Qing dynasty.
- **Treaty Port**: A port opened to foreign trade and settlement by treaty, as Hankou was in 1861.
- **Ti-yong ideal**: Zhang Zhidong's theory of "Chinese learning as the basis, Western learning for application," guiding late Qing modernization.
- **Hanyang Arsenal**: A major arms factory established in 1891 by Zhang Zhidong, symbolizing early Chinese industrialization.
- **Xinhai Revolution**: The 1911 revolution that overthrew the Qing dynasty, named after the Chinese calendar year.
- **Wuhan Incident (1967)**: An armed conflict between rival factions during the Cultural Revolution.
- **Nature Index**: A database tracking research output, where Wuhan ranks highly among global cities.
- **Guozao**: A local term meaning "having breakfast," reflecting Wuhan's food culture.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The exact origin of the SARS-CoV-2 virus and the role of the Huanan Seafood Wholesale Market remain subjects of international scientific investigation and debate.
- The historical significance and legacy of the 1927 Wuhan Nationalist Government and its split with the Communists is a complex period in modern Chinese history.
- The effectiveness and long-term impact of the strict COVID-19 lockdown in Wuhan continue to be analyzed.

# Brussels

## Overview
Brussels is the capital of Belgium and the de facto capital of the European Union, hosting major EU and NATO institutions. It is a bilingual (French-Dutch) region of 19 municipalities, serving as a major international political and economic hub. Its history, from a small marsh settlement to a global city, reflects broader European political and cultural shifts.

## Timeline
- **c. 695** — First recorded reference to the settlement as "Brosella."
- **c. 979** — Official founding; Charles, Duke of Lower Lorraine, builds first fortification.
- **c. 1000** — Brussels becomes a commercial centre under the Count of Louvain.
- **1477** — Burgundian rule ends; Brussels enters Habsburg sovereignty.
- **1555** — Charles V abdicates his possessions in Brussels, passing them to Philip II of Spain.
- **1695** — French bombardment destroys the Grand-Place and a third of the city.
- **1830** — Belgian Revolution begins in Brussels; city becomes capital of new Belgium.
- **1867-1871** — The river Senne is covered over, enabling major urban renewal.
- **1958** — Brussels hosts the World's Fair (Expo 58), featuring the Atomium.
- **1989** — The Brussels-Capital Region is established as a bilingual federal region.
- **2016** — ISIL terrorist attacks kill 32 victims at the airport and metro.

## Body

### History and Development
Brussels grew from a small rural settlement on the river Senne. Its strategic location on trade routes between Flanders and Germany spurred its growth as a textile centre in the Middle Ages. It flourished under Burgundian and later Habsburg rule, serving as a princely capital. The city was severely damaged by French bombardment in 1695, leading to a major reconstruction. After the Belgian Revolution of 1830, Brussels became the capital of the new nation. The 19th century saw rapid industrialization and population growth, with major urban renewal projects like covering the Senne river. Since World War II, it has become a major centre for international politics, hosting the EU and NATO.

### Political Status and Administration
The Brussels-Capital Region is one of Belgium's three federal regions, separate from Flanders and Wallonia. It is officially bilingual, though French is the majority language. The region is governed by a parliament and a cabinet, with power shared between French and Dutch linguistic groups. The City of Brussels, one of the 19 municipalities, is the constitutional capital of Belgium and hosts national institutions like the Royal Palace and Parliament. The region's complex governance includes separate commissions for the French and Flemish Communities, reflecting Belgium's federal structure.

### International Role
Brussels is the de facto capital of the European Union, hosting the European Commission and the Council of the European Union. The European Parliament also holds many sessions there. The city is the headquarters of NATO and home to numerous other international organizations, embassies, and lobbying groups. This international presence has made it a major global centre for diplomacy and politics, with a large expatriate community.

### Demographics and Language
Brussels is the most densely populated region in Belgium. Its population is highly diverse, with a majority of residents having foreign origins. Historically Dutch-speaking, the city underwent a language shift to French from the late 19th century. Today, French is the lingua franca, but the region is officially bilingual. English is also widely spoken due to the international community. The historic Brabantian dialect, Brusselian, survives among a small minority.

### Economy and Transport
Brussels is Belgium's economic capital and a top financial centre in Western Europe. Its economy is service-oriented, dominated by international institutions and multinational headquarters. It is a major transport hub, with the country's largest airport (Brussels Airport) and busiest railway stations. The Brussels Metro is Belgium's only rapid transit system. The city is known for its cuisine, including waffles, chocolate, and beer, and is a capital of the comic strip.

## Terms
- **Brussels-Capital Region**: One of Belgium's three federal regions, comprising 19 bilingual municipalities.
- **De facto capital**: The functional capital of the EU, though not officially declared.
- **Lingua franca**: A common language used for communication between people with different native languages; in Brussels, this is French.
- **Brusselisation**: The uncontrolled development of modern buildings that clash with historic architecture.
- **Pentagon**: The historical city centre of Brussels, surrounded by the Small Ring road.
- **North-South connection**: A major railway line linking Brussels' main stations, completed in 1952.
- **COCOF/VGC**: The French and Flemish Community Commissions, which exercise community powers in Brussels.
- **Lambic**: A type of beer spontaneously fermented, traditional to the Brussels region.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Language politics**: The expansion of the bilingual region to surrounding municipalities is a contentious issue between French and Flemish speakers.
- **Municipal structure**: There is debate over merging Brussels' 19 municipalities into a single city council to reduce costs and centralize power.
- **Brusselisation**: The tension between modern development and historic preservation remains a concern.
- **Economic disparity**: While Brussels has the highest GDP per capita in Belgium, it also has the lowest available income per household and high unemployment.

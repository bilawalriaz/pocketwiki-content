# Urban area

## Overview
An urban area is a human settlement characterized by high population density and built infrastructure, contrasting with rural areas. The global shift toward urban living is a defining demographic trend of the modern era, with the majority of the world's population now residing in urban areas, a milestone reached in 2009. This urbanization process, which began with early city formation in the 4th millennium BCE, has profound implications for resource use, environmental impact, and societal organization.

## Timeline
- **c. 4th millennium BCE** — Urban revolution leads to formation of early urban predecessors and human civilization.
- **1950** — 764 million people (30% of world population) live in urban areas.
- **2009** — Global urban population (3.42 billion) surpasses rural population (3.41 billion) for the first time.
- **2014** — 3.9 billion people (53% of world population) live in urban areas.
- **1992-2015** — Global urban areas more than double in size, from 33 million hectares (Mha) to 71 Mha.
- **2020** — U.S. Census Bureau identifies 2,646 urban areas; 80.0% of the U.S. population lives in one.
- **2050 (projected)** — UN predicts 68% of world population will be urban, with 90% of growth from Africa and Asia.

## Body

### Recent Historical Growth and Impact
The 20th and 21st centuries have seen explosive urban growth. In 1950, about 30% of the world's 2.5 billion people were urban. By 2009, the urban population (3.42 billion) outnumbered the rural population (3.41 billion). By 2014, 3.9 billion people (53% of the global total) lived in urban areas. This expansion has consumed significant natural resources; between 1992 and 2015, urban land area more than doubled, using 24 Mha of fertile cropland, 3.3 Mha of forest, and 4.6 Mha of shrubland. The UN projects that by 2050, 68% of the world's population will be urban.

### Definitions and Measurement
Defining an "urban area" is complex and varies by country and purpose. The UN relies on national definitions, as a single global standard is not feasible. Common criteria include population density, land use, and the percentage of the population not engaged in agriculture. For example, the UK defines a "built-up area" as land of at least 20 hectares that is "irreversibly urban in character," with areas less than 200 meters apart linked. The U.S. Census Bureau defines an urban area as a densely settled core with at least 5,000 people or 2,000 housing units. These definitions differ from "metropolitan area," which includes the urban core plus socio-economically connected rural land and satellite cities.

### Urbanization by Region
Urbanization patterns and definitions differ globally. In **Asia**, China's urbanization rate surged from 17.4% to 46.6% between 1978 and 2009, with cities expanding at an average 10% annual rate since 2000. India's 2011 census defined urban areas by a minimum population of 5,000, density of 400 persons/km², and 75%+ male employment in non-agricultural work. In **Europe**, definitions often use contiguous built-up land with minimal gaps (e.g., 200m in the UK and France). The Netherlands' Randstad conurbation has 7 million inhabitants. In **North America**, Canada redesignated urban areas as "population centres" in 2011 to reflect a continuum from small to large. The U.S. had 2,646 urban areas in 2020, with the New York urban area being the largest at over 19 million people. **South America** is highly urbanized; Argentina's Greater Buenos Aires area has about 15 million people.

## Terms
- **Urban Area**: A human settlement with high population density and built infrastructure, defined by specific criteria that vary by country.
- **Urbanization**: The process by which an increasing proportion of a population lives in urban areas.
- **Metropolitan Area**: An urban area plus surrounding rural land and satellite cities that are socio-economically connected to the urban core.
- **Conurbation**: A large urban area formed by the merging of several cities and towns.
- **Built-up Area**: Land that is "irreversibly urban in character," often used in road safety and land-use statistics.
- **Urban Agglomeration**: An integrated urban area consisting of a core town and its contiguous suburbs.
- **Population Centre**: A term used in Canada since 2011 to replace "urban area," emphasizing a continuum of settlement patterns.
- **Densely Inhabited District (DID)**: A Japanese statistical unit for urbanized areas with a density of at least 4,000 inhabitants/km².

## Debates and Open Questions
The primary debate centers on the lack of a universal definition of "urban," making cross-country comparisons difficult. The UN states that due to global differences, a single definition is impossible. This leads to arbitrary national definitions that can change between censuses. Furthermore, the distinction between "urban area" and "metropolitan area" is often confused, with the latter including a broader functional region. The environmental impact of rapid, sometimes unplanned, urban expansion on fertile land and natural ecosystems is a critical ongoing concern.

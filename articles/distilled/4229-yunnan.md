# Yunnan

## Overview
Yunnan is a mountainous, ethnically diverse province in southwestern China, bordering Myanmar, Laos, and Vietnam. It is a global biodiversity hotspot and a historical crossroads of trade and culture, yet remains one of China's less economically developed regions. Its unique geography and demographics make it a critical area for conservation, regional trade, and cultural studies.

## Timeline
- **c. 1.7 million years ago** — Yuanmou Man (Homo erectus) fossil dates to this period.
- **3rd–2nd centuries BC** — Dian Kingdom flourishes in central Yunnan.
- **109 BC** — Han dynasty conquers Dian, establishing Yizhou Commandery.
- **738–937** — Nanzhao kingdom, a major regional power, rules from Dali.
- **937–1253** — Dali Kingdom, ruled by the Duan family, succeeds Nanzhao.
- **1253** — Mongol Empire conquers the Dali Kingdom.
- **1937–1945** — WWII: Yunnan becomes a strategic rear area; the Burma Road is built.
- **1997** — Old Town of Lijiang is designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
- **2003** — Three Parallel Rivers area is designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
- **2012** — Kunming Changshui International Airport opens.

## Body

### History and Etymology
The name "Yunnan" first appeared during the Han dynasty. Theories on its meaning include "south of colorful clouds" or "south of the Yunling Mountains," though the latter is chronologically inconsistent. The region was home to the Dian Kingdom before Han conquest. After periods of local rule, the powerful Nanzhao kingdom (738–937) and later the Dali Kingdom (937–1253) dominated. The Mongol conquest integrated Yunnan into the Chinese empire. Subsequent Ming and Qing rule saw significant Han Chinese migration, especially during WWII when the province became a vital wartime base.

### Geography and Climate
Yunnan is highly mountainous, with elevations ranging from 76.4 m (251 ft) in the Red River Valley to 6,740 m (22,110 ft) at Kawagebo Peak. The terrain is divided into a limestone plateau in the east and north-south mountain ranges in the west. It is the source of major rivers like the Yangtze, Mekong, and Salween, which flow through deep parallel gorges. The climate is generally mild and subtropical, with distinct wet and dry seasons.

### Biodiversity and Natural Resources
Yunnan is China's most biologically diverse province, hosting perhaps 17,000 vascular plant species and 42.6% of the country's protected plant species. It is a "Global Biodiversity Hotspot" and home to rare animals like the Asian elephant and Yunnan snub-nosed monkey. The province is rich in minerals, ranking first in China for zinc, lead, and tin deposits. Its water resources are abundant, supporting significant hydroelectric power.

### Demographics and Culture
Yunnan is China's most ethnically diverse province, with 25 of the nation's 56 recognized ethnic groups, including the Yi, Bai, and Dai. Ethnic minorities comprise about 34% of the population. This diversity is reflected in numerous languages and cultural practices, such as the Naxi Dongba script. The province is the birthplace of tea and a major producer of Pu-erh tea.

### Economy and Infrastructure
Yunnan's economy relies on agriculture (tobacco, tea, flowers), mining, hydropower, and tourism. It is a key trade gateway to Southeast Asia via the Mekong River and developing road/rail links. Infrastructure has expanded rapidly, with a growing expressway network and Kunming as a major air hub. The province faces challenges from poverty and uneven development compared to coastal China.

## Terms
- **Zomia**: A vast highland region of mainland Southeast Asia, including parts of Yunnan, historically inhabited by non-state peoples.
- **Nanzhao**: A powerful kingdom centered in Dali (738–937) that played a key role between the Tang dynasty and Tibetan Empire.
- **Dali Kingdom**: The successor state to Nanzhao (937–1253), ruled by the Duan family before Mongol conquest.
- **Panthay Rebellion**: A 19th-century Muslim revolt in Yunnan against Qing rule, resulting in massive casualties.
- **Burma Road**: A vital WWII supply route from Lashio, Burma, to Kunming, completed in 1938.
- **Pu-erh tea**: A fermented tea from Yunnan, named after the historic trading town of Pu'er.
- **Dongba script**: A pictographic writing system used by the Naxi people, still in limited ceremonial use.
- **Three Parallel Rivers**: A UNESCO site where the Yangtze, Mekong, and Salween flow through deep, adjacent gorges.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The etymology of "Yunnan" is debated, with multiple theories (e.g., "south of colorful clouds" vs. "south of the Yunling Mountains") lacking definitive proof.
- The precise origins and classification of some ethnic groups, like the Mosuo, remain subjects of scholarly and political discussion.
- The impact of large-scale dam construction on the Mekong River's ecology and downstream communities is an ongoing concern.

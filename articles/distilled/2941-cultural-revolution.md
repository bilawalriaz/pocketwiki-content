# Cultural Revolution

## Overview
The Great Proletarian Cultural Revolution (1966–1976) was a sociopolitical movement in the People's Republic of China launched by CCP Chairman Mao Zedong to preserve Chinese communism by purging perceived capitalist and traditional elements from society. It resulted in widespread chaos, violence, and persecution, causing severe setbacks for the country and its people, and its legacy remains a sensitive and complex topic in contemporary China.

## Timeline
- **1 Oct 1949** — Mao Zedong declares the founding of the People's Republic of China.
- **11 Jan 1962** — Seven Thousand Cadres Conference held; Mao and Liu Shaoqi make self-criticisms for the Great Leap Forward.
- **1963** — Mao launches the Socialist Education Movement.
- **Feb 1965** — Mao secretly commissions criticism of the play *Hai Rui Dismissed from Office*.
- **16 May 1966** — The "16 May Notification" is issued, outlining Mao's justification for the Cultural Revolution.
- **25 May 1966** — Nie Yuanzi posts a big-character poster at Peking University, endorsed by Mao.
- **18 Aug 1966** — Mao meets over a million Red Guards in Tiananmen Square, escalating the movement.
- **8 Aug 1966** — The "Sixteen Points" decision defines the Cultural Revolution as a nationwide campaign.
- **Jan 1967** — The "January Storm" in Shanghai sees radicals overthrow the municipal government.
- **20 Jul 1970** — The "Wuhan Incident" occurs, a major armed clash between rebel factions.
- **13 Sep 1971** — Lin Biao dies in a plane crash in Mongolia after an alleged coup attempt.
- **6 Oct 1976** — The "Gang of Four" is arrested following Mao Zedong's death.
- **27 Jun 1981** — The CCP officially declares the Cultural Revolution a "severe setback."

## Body
### Background and Outbreak
The Cultural Revolution emerged from Mao Zedong's dissatisfaction after the Great Leap Forward and his fear of "revisionism" following the Sino-Soviet split. He believed capitalist elements had infiltrated the Party and sought to purge them. The movement was triggered by a literary dispute over the play *Hai Rui Dismissed from Office*, which Mao used to attack political rivals like Peng Zhen. In May 1966, the "16 May Notification" formally launched the revolution, accusing Party leaders of being "counter-revolutionary revisionists." Mao called on the youth to "Bombard the Headquarters," leading to the formation of Red Guard groups who began a campaign to destroy the "Four Olds" (old ideas, culture, customs, and habits).

### Mass Movement and Seizure of Power (1966–1968)
The revolution escalated into nationwide chaos. Red Guards, mobilized by Mao's rhetoric, attacked schools, cultural sites, and perceived class enemies, leading to widespread violence and persecution during events like "Red August" in 1966. In 1967, radicals seized power from local governments and party branches, establishing "revolutionary committees" and dismantling public security and judicial systems. This period saw intense factional fighting, often armed, between radical and conservative groups. The military's role became crucial, with the People's Liberation Army (PLA) sometimes supporting radicals and other times suppressing them. By 1968, Mao moved to end the chaos, purging the Red Guards and launching campaigns like the "Down to the Countryside Movement," which relocated over 10 million urban youth to rural areas.

### Power Struggles and the Gang of Four (1969–1976)
Following Lin Biao's designation as Mao's successor at the 9th Party Congress in 1969, a power struggle ensued. Lin's influence grew with the PLA, leading to tensions with Mao. After an alleged coup attempt (Project 571), Lin Biao died in a plane crash in 1971. This event disillusioned many and weakened Mao's authority. Subsequently, the radical "Gang of Four," led by Mao's wife Jiang Qing, gained influence. They engaged in political campaigns like "Criticize Lin, Criticize Confucius" and opposed moderates like Zhou Enlai and the rehabilitated Deng Xiaoping. After Mao's death in September 1976, the Gang of Four was arrested, marking the end of the Cultural Revolution.

### Aftermath and Legacy
In 1978, Deng Xiaoping became paramount leader and initiated the "Boluan Fanzheng" (bringing order out of chaos) program to reverse Cultural Revolution policies. The CCP's 1981 Resolution officially condemned the movement as responsible for "the most severe setback and the heaviest losses" since the PRC's founding. The revolution caused an estimated 1 to 2 million deaths, the persecution of tens of millions, and the destruction of cultural heritage. It disrupted education, economy, and social order for a decade. Public discussion of the Cultural Revolution remains limited in China today, though its impact on Chinese society and politics is profound.

## Terms
- **Four Olds**: Old ideas, old culture, old customs, and old habits, targeted for destruction by Red Guards.
- **Red Guards**: Paramilitary social organizations of students and young people mobilized by Mao to attack perceived class enemies.
- **Gang of Four**: A radical political faction led by Jiang Qing (Mao's wife) that gained power during the Cultural Revolution.
- **Revolutionary Committees**: New governing bodies that replaced local governments and party branches after power seizures.
- **Down to the Countryside Movement**: A policy relocating urban youth to rural areas to "learn from the peasants."
- **Struggle Session**: A form of public humiliation and torture used to persecute perceived enemies of the revolution.
- **Five Black Categories**: Class labels (landlords, rich peasants, counter-revolutionaries, bad elements, rightists) used to target individuals.
- **Boluan Fanzheng**: "Bringing order out of chaos," the post-Mao policy to reverse Cultural Revolution policies.
- **Little Red Book**: *Quotations from Chairman Mao Tse-tung*, a collection of Mao's sayings that became a revered text.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Death Toll**: Estimates vary widely, from hundreds of thousands to millions, due to secrecy and the difficulty of attributing deaths directly to the movement.
- **Mao's Role**: Scholars debate the extent of Mao's direct responsibility versus the actions of radical factions like the Gang of Four.
- **Lin Biao's Death**: Official accounts state Lin Biao died in a plane crash after a failed coup, but details are disputed, and some question whether a coup was actually planned.
- **Legacy in Contemporary China**: Public discussion is restricted, and perspectives range from seeing it as a necessary revolution to a catastrophic mistake, with ongoing scholarly and political debate.

# Lanzhou

## Overview
Lanzhou is the capital and largest city of Gansu province in northwestern China, situated on the Yellow River. It is a critical regional transportation hub, historically a major link on the Northern Silk Road, and a modern center for heavy industry, particularly petrochemicals. The city is also a significant educational and research center for the region.

## Body

### History and Strategic Importance
Originally in the territory of the Western Qiangs, the area was incorporated into the State of Qin in the 6th century BC. Under the Han dynasty (206 BC–AD 220), it became a commandery and a key point on the Northern Silk Road and a Yellow River crossing. The Great Wall was extended to Yumen to protect the city. After the Han, Lanzhou served as the capital for several tribal states and was briefly the capital of the independent state of Liang in the 4th century. From the 5th to 11th centuries, the region became a center for Buddhist study. The city's name, Lanzhou, was reestablished under the Song dynasty in 1081. It became the capital of Gansu province in 1666. During the Second Sino-Japanese War (1937–1945), Lanzhou was a vital terminus for Soviet supplies via a 3,200 km highway and was heavily bombed by Japanese forces, though it was never captured.

### Geography and Climate
Lanzhou lies at 1,600 meters (5,200 ft) above sea level in a narrow, curved river valley along the southern banks of the Yellow River. It has a semi-arid climate with hot summers and cold, dry winters. The mean annual temperature is 10.9 °C (51.6 °F), and annual rainfall is 309 millimetres (12.2 in). The city's topography contributes to severe air pollution, as it was once listed among the world's ten most air-polluted cities. Authorities have since taken measures to improve air quality, including closing polluting enterprises. The Yellow River at Lanzhou carries a high silt load, and in 2014, benzene contamination forced officials to advise residents not to drink tap water.

### Economy and Industry
Since 1949, Lanzhou has been transformed into a major industrial center. It is the center of Gansu's petrochemical industry, with a large oil refinery linked to fields at Yumen by pipeline. Other key industries include textile mills (especially woolen and leather goods), rubber processing, fertilizer plants, machinery, metallurgy, and the production of locomotives and rolling stock. Lanzhou has been a center for China's nuclear power industry since the 1960s. The city is also a collecting center for agricultural produce and livestock from the region.

### Transportation
Lanzhou is a major railway hub. Lanzhou Railway Station connects the western provinces to the east via the Longhai Railway (completed 1953) and to the northwest via the Lanxin Railway. High-speed rail services connect to the east, west, and north from Lanzhou West Railway Station. The city is a key link on the Eurasian Land Bridge, with freight services to destinations like Hamburg and Almaty. Lanzhou Zhongchuan Airport, opened in 1970, is located 70 km north of the city. The Lanzhou Metro opened its first line in 2019. A bus rapid transit system opened in 2013.

### Culture and Education
Lanzhou is the cultural center of Gansu, home to Han, Hui, and Zang ethnic groups. Prominent cultural forms include Qinqiang opera (Gansu style) and Lanzhou Guzi (drum narrative), a national intangible cultural heritage. The city is famous for Lanzhou beef noodles. Lanzhou is a major educational center, home to Lanzhou University (founded 1909) and numerous other institutions. It is ranked among the top 50 global cities for scientific research output by the Nature Index.

## Terms
- **Silk Road**: Ancient network of trade routes connecting the East and West, for which Lanzhou was a major link.
- **Commandery (jùn)**: A historical administrative division in China, larger than a county.
- **Prefecture**: A historical administrative division, often governing several counties.
- **Semi-arid climate**: A climate with low precipitation, insufficient for dense vegetation.
- **PM10/PM2.5**: Particulate matter of 10 or 2.5 micrometers or less in diameter, key indicators of air pollution.
- **Brucellosis**: A bacterial disease that can infect humans, often from contact with infected animals or their products.
- **Eurasian Land Bridge**: A rail freight route connecting Asia and Europe.
- **Intangible cultural heritage**: Practices, representations, and expressions recognized as part of a place's cultural heritage.

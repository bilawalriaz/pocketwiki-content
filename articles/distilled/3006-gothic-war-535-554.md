# Gothic War (535–554)

## Overview
The Gothic War (535–554) was a major conflict between the Eastern Roman (Byzantine) Empire and the Ostrogothic Kingdom of Italy. It was part of Emperor Justinian I's ambitious campaign to reconquer the lost provinces of the former Western Roman Empire. The war resulted in a devastating but ultimately pyrrhic Byzantine victory that left Italy depopulated and vulnerable to later Lombard invasions.

## Timeline
- **535** — Byzantine general Belisarius invades Sicily, beginning the war.
- **536** — Belisarius captures Naples and enters Rome unopposed.
- **537–538** — First Siege of Rome by the Ostrogoths under King Vitiges.
- **539** — The city of Mediolanum (Milan) is sacked by Goths and Burgundians.
- **May 540** — Belisarius captures the Ostrogothic capital, Ravenna.
- **541** — Totila is proclaimed king of the Ostrogoths, reviving Gothic resistance.
- **543** — Totila captures Naples after a siege.
- **Dec 546** — Totila captures Rome.
- **547** — Belisarius briefly reoccupies Rome.
- **549** — Totila captures Rome again.
- **552** — Byzantine general Narses defeats and kills Totila at the Battle of Taginae.
- **Oct 553** — Narses defeats the last Gothic army at the Battle of Mons Lactarius.
- **554** — Emperor Justinian issues the Pragmatic Sanction to govern reconquered Italy.
- **562** — Byzantines capture the last Gothic strongholds, Verona and Brixia.

## Background
### Italy under the Goths
After the fall of the Western Roman Empire in 476, Italy was ruled by Odoacer and then by the Ostrogoths under Theodoric the Great, who governed as a viceroy of the Byzantine emperor. This dual system, with a Gothic military and Roman civil administration, worked under Theodoric but collapsed after his death in 526. His successor, Athalaric, died in 534, and his regent mother, Amalasuntha, was murdered by her cousin Theodahad in 535. Her death provided Emperor Justinian with a pretext for war.

### Belisarius
Fresh from a swift victory over the Vandals in North Africa (533–534), Justinian appointed his general Belisarius to lead a small force of 7,500 men to reconquer Italy. The campaign began secretly in 535.

## First Byzantine Campaign (535–540)
Belisarius quickly captured Sicily and, after a difficult siege, Naples. He entered Rome in December 536. The new Gothic king, Vitiges, besieged Rome for a year (537–538) but failed to take it. Byzantine command was fractured by rivalry between Belisarius and the eunuch general Narses, which contributed to the loss of Mediolanum in 539. Despite these setbacks, Belisarius advanced on the Gothic capital, Ravenna. In 540, he tricked the Goths into surrendering by feigning acceptance of their offer to make him Western Emperor. He then returned to Constantinople, leaving the war unfinished.

## Gothic Revival (541–551)
Belisarius's departure allowed a Gothic revival under the brilliant king Totila. Exploiting Byzantine disunity, the Plague of Justinian (542), and a new Persian war, Totila reconquered most of Italy. He captured Naples (543) and Rome three times (546, 549). Belisarius was sent back in 544 but was hampered by lack of supplies and reinforcements. His attempts to relieve Rome failed, and he was recalled in 549.

## Byzantine Reconquest (551–554)
Justinian assembled a large army of 20,000–25,000 men, including barbarian allies, and placed it under the eunuch Narses. In 552, Narses defeated and killed Totila at the Battle of Taginae. The last Gothic king, Teias, was defeated and killed at Mons Lactarius in 553. Narses then repelled a major invasion by Franks and Alamanni at the Battle of the Volturnus (554). The last Gothic strongholds surrendered in 555, and the final cities in the north were taken by 562.

## Aftermath
In 554, Justinian issued the Pragmatic Sanction to reorganize Italy. The war left Italy devastated and depopulated. The Byzantine victory was pyrrhic, draining resources needed elsewhere. In 568, just three years after Justinian's death, the Lombards invaded and quickly conquered most of the peninsula, leaving only the Exarchate of Ravenna and parts of the south under Byzantine control.

## Terms
- **Foederati**: Barbarian tribes settled within the empire as allied troops under treaty.
- **Magister militum**: A high-ranking military commander, or "master of soldiers."
- **Pragmatic Sanction**: A 554 edict by Justinian that established the new government for reconquered Italy.
- **Exarchate of Ravenna**: A Byzantine territory in Italy, centered on Ravenna, that survived until 751.
- **Plague of Justinian**: A devastating pandemic (541–542) that killed up to a quarter of the Eastern Empire's population.
- **Chalcedonian Christian**: A mainstream Christian denomination following the Council of Chalcedon (451), opposed by Arian Christians like the Goths.

## Debates and Open Questions
Historians debate whether Justinian's reconquest was a strategic error. Some, like Edward Gibbon, view it as a wasteful distraction that weakened the empire against eastern threats. Others, like Warren Treadgold, argue the true cause of later Byzantine weakness was the unforeseeable Plague of Justinian, not the war itself. The war's outcome permanently fractured the unified political structure of Italy, a division that lasted until the 19th century.

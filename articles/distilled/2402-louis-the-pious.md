# Louis the Pious

## Overview
Louis the Pious (778–840) was the only surviving son of Charlemagne and became sole ruler of the Carolingian Empire in 814. His reign was defined by the challenge of maintaining imperial unity while dividing the realm among his sons, leading to repeated civil wars. Though often compared unfavorably to his father, he faced the distinct problem of managing a large, complex empire through partible inheritance, a custom that ultimately fractured it.

## Timeline
- **778** — Born at Cassinogilum (likely Chasseneuil), son of Charlemagne.
- **781** — Crowned King of Aquitaine as a child.
- **801** — Conquers Barcelona from the Emirate of Córdoba.
- **813** — Crowned co-emperor with Charlemagne.
- **814** — Becomes sole emperor after Charlemagne's death.
- **817** — Issues the *Ordinatio Imperii*, dividing the empire among his three eldest sons.
- **818** — His nephew Bernard of Italy is blinded and dies; Louis later performs public penance for this.
- **823** — Birth of his son Charles the Bald, sparking succession disputes.
- **830** — First civil war; Louis is temporarily captured by his sons.
- **833** — Second civil war; Louis is deposed and performs a second public penance at the "Field of Lies."
- **834** — Restored to power.
- **840** — Dies at Ingelheim, leading to the final civil war and the Treaty of Verdun (843).

## Body

### Early Life and Rule in Aquitaine
Louis was the third son of Charlemagne, given a Merovingian name to suggest dynastic continuity. Crowned King of Aquitaine at age three, he was sent to rule there to secure the southwestern frontier after costly wars. He conquered Barcelona in 801 and asserted Frankish authority over Pamplona and the Basques by 812. As the only surviving legitimate son, he inherited the entire empire in 814.

### Reign and the *Ordinatio Imperii*
Upon becoming emperor, Louis purged the court, exiled female relatives to nunneries, and promoted church reform under Benedict of Aniane. After a near-fatal accident in 817, he issued the *Ordinatio Imperii*, a decree to ensure orderly succession. It made his eldest son Lothair co-emperor and overlord, while giving kingdoms to his younger sons Pepin and Louis. This plan failed because it excluded his nephew Bernard of Italy and, later, his own son Charles.

### Civil Wars and Penance
The birth of Charles in 823, Louis's son by his second wife Judith, destabilized the succession plan. His older sons, Lothair, Pepin, and Louis, repeatedly rebelled, often allying with nobles and clergy. Louis was deposed twice (830, 833) and performed public penance both times, an act that severely damaged his imperial prestige. The second deposition at the "Field of Lies" (833) was particularly humiliating. He was restored in 834 and spent his final years in conflict, but ultimately reasserted control before his death.

### Frontier Wars and Death
Throughout his reign, Louis managed threats on multiple frontiers: Slavic tribes in the east, Basques in the southwest, and Vikings who began raiding the north in the 830s. He died in 840, having just secured a final division of the empire at Worms. His death triggered a three-year civil war among his sons, resolved by the Treaty of Verdun (843), which permanently split the empire into the precursors of modern France and Germany.

## Terms
- **Partible inheritance**: The Frankish custom of dividing an estate or kingdom among all sons, rather than passing it intact to the eldest.
- **Ordinatio Imperii**: Louis's 817 decree attempting to secure an orderly succession by making Lothair co-emperor and granting kingdoms to his younger sons.
- **Co-emperor**: A ruler sharing imperial authority; Louis was crowned co-emperor with Charlemagne in 813.
- **Penance**: A public act of contrition for sins, performed by Louis to atone for political and personal transgressions, which weakened his authority.
- **Marches**: Peripheral border territories (e.g., the Spanish March) vital for defense against external threats.
- **Missi dominici**: Envoys sent by the emperor to enforce his authority in the provinces.
- **Weregild**: A monetary compensation paid to the family of a slain person, a key concept in Germanic law.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The exact location of Louis's birthplace, Cassinogilum, is debated, with Chasseneuil-du-Poitou being the most likely identification.
- Historians debate the degree to which Louis's public penances were sincere religious acts versus political tools, and how they impacted his secular authority.
- The *Ordinatio Imperii* is a modern term; the original document is called *divisio imperii*. Its failure is central to understanding the empire's fragmentation.

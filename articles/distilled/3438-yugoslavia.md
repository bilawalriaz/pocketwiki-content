# Yugoslavia

## Overview
Yugoslavia was a multi-ethnic state in Central Europe and the Balkans that existed from 1918 to 1992. It was the first sovereign union of South Slavic peoples, formed after centuries of foreign rule. Its history is marked by a struggle to balance the competing nationalisms of its diverse population, ultimately leading to its violent dissolution.

## Timeline
- **1918** — Kingdom of Serbs, Croats and Slovenes formed after WWI.
- **1929** — Renamed the Kingdom of Yugoslavia by King Alexander I.
- **1941** — Axis powers invade and occupy Yugoslavia.
- **1943** — Communist-led Partisans gain Allied recognition.
- **1945** — Federal People's Republic of Yugoslavia proclaimed, abolishing monarchy.
- **1948** — Tito-Stalin split; Yugoslavia breaks from Soviet bloc.
- **1961** — Yugoslavia co-founds the Non-Aligned Movement.
- **1963** — Renamed the Socialist Federal Republic of Yugoslavia (SFRY).
- **1974** — New constitution grants major autonomy to republics and provinces.
- **1980** — Death of President Josip Broz Tito.
- **1991** — Slovenia and Croatia declare independence.
- **1992** — Federal Republic of Yugoslavia (Serbia and Montenegro) formed.

## Body

### Formation and Kingdom (1918–1941)
The state was created in 1918 as the Kingdom of Serbs, Croats and Slovenes, merging the Kingdom of Serbia with the provisional State of Slovenes, Croats and Serbs. It was commonly called a "Versailles state." King Alexander I renamed it Yugoslavia in 1929 and established a dictatorship to curb separatism. He was assassinated in 1934. Under regent Prince Paul, Yugoslavia signed the Tripartite Pact with the Axis in 1941, but a coup d'état immediately followed, leading to a full Axis invasion.

### World War II and Communist Takeover (1941–1945)
The Axis powers occupied and dismembered Yugoslavia. Resistance was led by two factions: the royalist Chetniks and the communist-led Partisans under Josip Broz Tito. The Partisans, recognized by the Allies in 1943, became the largest resistance army in occupied Europe. They liberated the country by 1945, with Tito emerging as the undisputed leader.

### Socialist Federal Republic (1945–1980)
In 1945, a communist-led election abolished the monarchy, establishing the Federal People's Republic. Tito ruled until his death in 1980. The state was a federation of six republics (Bosnia and Herzegovina, Croatia, Macedonia, Montenegro, Serbia, Slovenia) with two autonomous provinces within Serbia (Kosovo, Vojvodina). After a 1948 split with Stalin, Yugoslavia pursued an independent "workers' self-management" socialism and co-founded the Non-Aligned Movement. The 1974 constitution granted significant autonomy to the republics and provinces, a system that later paralyzed decision-making.

### Breakup and Wars (1980–1992)
After Tito's death, economic crisis and rising nationalism destabilized the federation. Serbian leader Slobodan Milošević sought to restore Serbian dominance, alarming other republics. The League of Communists collapsed in 1990, and multi-party elections brought nationalist governments to power in several republics. Slovenia and Croatia declared independence in 1991, triggering the Ten-Day War in Slovenia and a full-scale war in Croatia. Macedonia separated peacefully. A referendum for Bosnian independence led to the Bosnian War (1992–1995). The Yugoslav People's Army (JNA), increasingly Serb-dominated, intervened in the conflicts.

### Aftermath and Succession
The rump Federal Republic of Yugoslavia (Serbia and Montenegro) was formed in 1992 and claimed sole succession to the SFRY, a claim rejected internationally. After Milošević's overthrow in 2000, it accepted shared succession. The state was renamed Serbia and Montenegro in 2003 and dissolved peacefully in 2006 when Montenegro became independent. Kosovo declared independence from Serbia in 2008, a move not universally recognized.

## Terms
*   **South Slavs:** A group of linguistically related ethnic peoples including Serbs, Croats, Bosniaks, Slovenes, Montenegrins, and Macedonians.
*   **Banovinas:** Administrative provinces created by King Alexander I in 1929, named after rivers, to replace historic regions.
*   **Partisans:** The communist-led, multi-ethnic resistance movement against the Axis occupation in WWII, led by Josip Broz Tito.
*   **Chetniks:** The royalist, predominantly Serb resistance movement in WWII, which later collaborated with the Axis.
*   **Workers' self-management:** Yugoslavia's unique socialist economic system where factories were managed by worker cooperatives.
*   **Non-Aligned Movement:** An international organization founded in 1961 for states not formally aligned with or against any major power bloc.
*   **Badinter Arbitration Committee:** A body established by the European Community to provide legal opinions on the breakup of Yugoslavia.
*   **Yugo-nostalgia:** Remembrance of the positive aspects of life in the former joint state, particularly its social security.

## Debates and open questions
*   The exact number of WWII victims in Yugoslavia is debated, with official post-war estimates of 1.7 million later revised by historians to around 1 million.
*   The legitimacy of the 1945 elections that abolished the monarchy is disputed, with debate over whether the regency council's actions constituted an abdication.
*   The legal succession to the SFRY was contested, with the Federal Republic of Yugoslavia initially claiming sole successor status before accepting shared succession.
*   The international recognition of Kosovo's 2008 declaration of independence from Serbia remains an ongoing dispute.

# Ryukyu Islands

## Overview
The Ryukyu Islands are a chain of Japanese islands stretching southwest from Kyushu to Taiwan, administratively divided between Kagoshima and Okinawa Prefectures. They are geographically and culturally distinct, with a history marked by the Ryukyu Kingdom, foreign influence, and modern geopolitical disputes, particularly over the Senkaku Islands.

## Timeline
- **601, 607** — Chinese expeditions to the "Country of Liuqiu" (Ryukyu).
- **616** — First Japanese record of the Southern Islands (Nantō) in the *Nihonshoki*.
- **c. 702** — Tane Province established, formally incorporating the northern islands into Japan.
- **1429** — Okinawan polities unified as the Ryukyu Kingdom.
- **1609** — Satsuma Domain invades the Ryukyu Kingdom, establishing suzerainty.
- **1872** — Japanese government establishes Ryukyu han.
- **1879** — Meiji government abolishes Ryukyu han, creating Okinawa Prefecture.
- **1945** — U.S. military occupation of Okinawa begins.
- **1951** — Treaty of San Francisco places islands under U.S. administration.
- **1972** — Administrative rights revert to Japan under the Okinawa Reversion Agreement.

## Body

### Geography and Administration
The Ryukyu Islands, also called the Nansei Islands ("Southwest Islands"), are divided into subgroups. Administratively, the northern Satsunan Islands (Ōsumi, Tokara, Amami) are part of Kagoshima Prefecture, while the southern Ryukyu Islands (Okinawa, Miyako, Yaeyama, Daitō, Senkaku) form Okinawa Prefecture. Geographically, major boundaries are the Tokara Strait and Kerama Gap. The climate ranges from humid subtropical in the north to tropical rainforest in the south, with high precipitation and typhoons.

### Names and Cultural Identity
The name "Ryukyu" is strongly associated with the former Ryukyu Kingdom. In Japanese, it typically refers only to the islands of that kingdom (Okinawa, Miyako, Yaeyama), while in English it often includes the Amami and Daitō Islands. The native population of the Amami, Okinawa, Miyako, and Yaeyama Islands are collectively called Ryukyuan people, though they primarily identify with their specific island (e.g., Uchinaanchu for Okinawans). The Ōsumi and Tokara Islands are culturally part of Kyushu.

### Ancient and Medieval History
Chinese and Japanese historical records mention the islands from the 7th century. The islands were strategically important for Japanese missions to Tang China. By the 10th century, the Amami Islands were a trade hub, evidenced by the Gusuku Site Complex. Control was contested between the southward-expanding Satsuma Domain and the northward-expanding Ryukyu Kingdom. Satsuma's 1609 invasion of Ryukyu brought the kingdom under its suzerainty.

### The Ryukyu Kingdom and Japanese Annexation
The Ryukyu Kingdom, unified in 1429, was a tributary of Ming China. After Satsuma's 1609 invasion, its kings paid tribute to both the Japanese shōgun and the Chinese emperor. In 1879, the Meiji government annexed the kingdom, creating Okinawa Prefecture and forcing the king to relocate to Tokyo. This ended formal tributary relations with China in 1874.

### Modern Era and U.S. Administration
Following World War II, the islands were administered by the United States. The 1951 Treaty of San Francisco granted the U.S. administrative rights over islands south of 29°N. Administrative control returned to Japan in 1972 via the Okinawa Reversion Agreement. The Senkaku Islands, part of Okinawa Prefecture, are claimed by China and Taiwan.

## Terms
- **Ryukyu Kingdom (1429–1879):** A kingdom centered on Okinawa that unified the central and southern islands and was a tributary of China.
- **Satsuma Domain:** A powerful Japanese feudal domain based in southern Kyushu that invaded and controlled the Ryukyu Kingdom from 1609.
- **Nansei Islands (南西諸島):** The official Japanese name for the entire island chain, meaning "Southwest Islands."
- **Satsunan Islands:** The Japanese name for the northern islands (Ōsumi, Tokara, Amami) in Kagoshima Prefecture.
- **Ryukyuan people:** The collective term for the native population of the Amami, Okinawa, Miyako, and Yaeyama Islands.
- **Watase Line:** A major biogeographic boundary separating the Palearctic realm (north) from the Indomalayan realm (south).
- **Senkaku Islands:** A group of uninhabited islands administered by Japan but claimed by China and Taiwan.
- **Okinawa Reversion Agreement (1971):** The agreement that returned administrative control of the Ryukyu Islands from the U.S. to Japan in 1972.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The historical referent of "Liuqiu" in the Chinese *Book of Sui* (636) is debated: it may refer to Taiwan, Okinawa, or both.
- The precise location of the Watase Line biogeographic boundary within the Tokara Islands is disputed.
- There is ongoing discussion about the identity of Ryukyuans, their relationship to mainland Japanese (Yamato), and issues of discrimination and potential secession.
- China has periodically questioned Japanese sovereignty over the Ryukyus, particularly in the context of the Senkaku Islands dispute.

# Leeds

## Overview
Leeds is a major city in West Yorkshire, England, and the largest settlement in Yorkshire. It grew from a small medieval manor into a global centre for woollen cloth production during the Industrial Revolution. Today, it is the UK's largest legal and financial centre outside London, with a diverse economy and a significant student population, making it a key cultural and economic hub for the north of England.

## Body

### Economic Development
Leeds developed as a market town and later a major centre for woollen cloth production, handling one-sixth of England's export trade by 1770. Growth was accelerated by canal and railway construction. During the Industrial Revolution, it became a mill town with flax, engineering, and printing industries. Manufacturing diversified but declined from the 1930s, with a post-war shift to services. The contemporary economy is the most diverse of the UK's main employment centres, with a high ratio of private to public sector jobs. Key sectors include finance, legal services, retail, and the visitor economy. Leeds is the largest legal and financial centre outside London, with the financial and insurance services industry worth £13 billion to its economy.

### Local Government
Leeds was a manor in the West Riding of Yorkshire. The Borough of Leeds was created in 1207, with a full charter of incorporation granted in 1626. It became a county borough in 1889 and gained city status in 1893. In 1974, under the Local Government Act 1972, the county borough was abolished and its area combined with others to form the metropolitan district of the City of Leeds within West Yorkshire. The local authority is Leeds City Council. In 2020, a devolution deal led to the creation of a West Yorkshire Metro Mayor, currently Tracy Brabin.

### Suburban Growth and Geography
Leeds is built around the River Aire in the eastern foothills of the Pennines. The city centre lies at about 63m above sea level, while the district's elevation ranges from 10m to 340m. Over 60% of the district is green belt land. The city expanded significantly in the 19th and 20th centuries, absorbing surrounding villages. Major suburban growth occurred in the 19th century due to industrialisation and later in the 20th century through council housing estates and private developments. The city has a varied geology, from coal measure sandstones in the south to gritstones in the north.

### Demography and Culture
Leeds is a diverse city with over 75 ethnic groups. The 2021 census recorded a district population of 811,953. The city has the UK's fourth-largest student population, served by five universities. This has stimulated a vibrant nightlife and cultural scene. Cultural highlights include the Leeds International Film Festival, the Leeds Festival (a major music event), and the Leeds West Indian Carnival. The city is home to Opera North, Northern Ballet, and numerous museums, including the Royal Armouries. It has a rich sporting heritage, with teams like Leeds United F.C. and Leeds Rhinos, and hosted the 2014 Tour de France Grand Départ.

### Transport
Leeds has extensive road, bus, and rail networks. It is connected to the national motorway network via the M1, M62, and A1(M). Leeds railway station is one of the busiest in the UK. Public transport is coordinated by West Yorkshire Metro. Plans for a light rail or metro system have been repeatedly proposed and cancelled. In 2023, a new tram system for the city and region was announced. Leeds Bradford Airport provides domestic and international flights. The city is also a hub for walking and cycling routes.

## Terms
- **Metropolitan district:** A type of local government district in England, created in 1974, covering a large urban area.
- **Green belt:** A planning designation to restrict urban sprawl and protect countryside.
- **Core Cities:** A group of the UK's largest city economies outside London.
- **GVA (Gross Value Added):** A measure of economic productivity, similar to GDP for a region.
- **Devolution deal:** An agreement transferring certain powers from the UK central government to a local or regional authority.
- **Wapentake:** An administrative division of a historic English county, similar to a hundred.
- **Civil parish:** The lowest tier of local government in England, with limited powers.
- **High Sufficiency city:** A ranking by the Globalization and World Cities Research Network indicating a city with a high level of services for itself and surrounding areas.

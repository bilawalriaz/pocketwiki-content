# Ismail I

## Overview
Ismail I (1487–1524) was the founder and first shah of Safavid Iran, ruling from 1501 until his death. His reign is a pivotal turning point in Iranian history, as he unified the country under native rule for the first time in over eight centuries and established Twelver Shia Islam as the state religion, shaping the modern religious and political identity of Iran.

## Timeline
- **17 July 1487** — Ismail I is born in Ardabil to Shaykh Haydar and Halima Begum.
- **1488** — His father, Shaykh Haydar, is killed in battle against the Shirvanshah and Aq Qoyunlu.
- **1494** — The Aq Qoyunlu capture Ardabil; the seven-year-old Ismail goes into hiding in Gilan.
- **1500** — Ismail rallies ~7,000 Qizilbash troops and begins his conquests, defeating the Shirvanshah.
- **1501** — Ismail is crowned shah in Tabriz and proclaims Twelver Shia Islam the official state religion.
- **1502** — He defeats the Aq Qoyunlu and takes the title "Shah of Iran."
- **1508** — Ismail seizes Baghdad and destroys major Sunni holy sites.
- **1510** — He defeats the Uzbeks at the Battle of Merv, killing their leader Muhammad Shaybani.
- **1514** — The Ottomans decisively defeat Ismail at the Battle of Chaldiran, ending his expansion.
- **23 May 1524** — Ismail I dies and is succeeded by his son, Tahmasp I.

## Body

### Origins and Rise to Power
Ismail was the last hereditary Grand Master of the Safavid Sufi order, a movement his predecessors had transformed into a military force supported by the Qizilbash, Turkoman tribesmen who were mainly Shiite. His ancestry was a mix of Iranian (likely Kurdish), Turkmen, and Byzantine Greek royal lines. After his father's death and a period in hiding, the twelve-year-old Ismail emerged in 1501, backed by the Qizilbash, to begin his conquests. A group of seven advisers, the *ahl-i ikhtisas*, directed the early campaign.

### Conquest and Establishment of the Safavid State
In 1500-1501, Ismail's Qizilbash forces conquered Shirvan and then defeated the Aq Qoyunlu ruler at the Battle of Sharur. After occupying Tabriz in 1501, he crowned himself shah and immediately declared Twelver Shia Islam the official religion, enforcing it by dissolving Sunni institutions. Over the next decade, he conquered the rest of Iran, Mesopotamia, and parts of Anatolia, making Georgia his vassal. His expansion into Eastern Anatolia, where he recruited Ottoman subjects, set the stage for conflict with the Ottoman Empire.

### Conflict with the Ottomans and Later Reign
The Safavid recruitment of Ottoman Turkoman tribes and the spread of Shia ideas alarmed the Sunni Ottoman Empire. After a pro-Safavid rebellion in Anatolia and border incursions, Sultan Selim I invaded. At the Battle of Chaldiran in 1514, the Ottomans' superior artillery and firearms defeated Ismail's more mobile cavalry. Though Ismail recovered most of his territory, the defeat shattered his aura of invincibility. He fell into depression and heavy drinking, withdrawing from military campaigns and leaving state affairs to his vizier. He died in 1524, succeeded by his son Tahmasp I, whose early reign was marked by Qizilbash civil wars.

### Royal Ideology and Cultural Legacy
Ismail cultivated a royal ideology that blended Shia Islamic and pre-Islamic Iranian motifs. He presented himself as a divine incarnation and the reincarnation of Shia figures like Ali, while also claiming the ancient Iranian *farr* (divine light of kingship). He named his sons after heroes of the Persian epic *Shahnameh* to link his dynasty to Iran's legendary past. As a poet under the pen name Khaṭāʾī, he wrote in a southern Turkic dialect (a precursor to Azerbaijani) to appeal to his Qizilbash followers, contributing to the language's literary development. His conquest of the Aq Qoyunlu and Timurids merged their artistic schools, creating a new Safavid imperial style in manuscript painting.

## Terms
- **Safavid Order**: A Sufi order founded in the 14th century that evolved into a militant movement under Ismail's ancestors.
- **Qizilbash**: "Red-heads," the Turkoman Shiite tribesmen who formed the core military support for the Safavid dynasty.
- **Twelver Shia Islam**: The branch of Shia Islam that recognizes twelve divinely appointed imams, made the official religion of Iran by Ismail.
- **Vakil**: "Vicegerent," a high office created by Ismail to serve as the shah's representative in both religious and secular matters.
- **Ahl-i ikhtisas**: A group of seven advisers who directed the Safavid movement during Ismail's early conquests.
- **Farr**: The concept of the divine light or charisma of kingship in ancient Iranian tradition, which Ismail claimed for himself.
- **Khaṭāʾī**: The pen name of Ismail I as a poet, meaning "the wrongful" or "the mistaken one."

## Debates and Open Questions
- The exact ethnic origin of the Safavid family is debated; while some describe them as "Turkamans of remote Kurdish descent," scholars agree the resulting empire was fundamentally Iranian.
- The authenticity of the poems in Ismail's *divan* is questioned, as the collection was compiled after his death and some scholars argue certain works may be by other poets.
- The extent to which Ismail's self-identification as a divine figure was a sincere belief versus a political tool to ensure follower loyalty remains a point of historical analysis.

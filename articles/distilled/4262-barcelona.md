# Barcelona

## Overview
Barcelona is the capital of Catalonia and Spain's second-largest city, a major Mediterranean port and cultural hub. Its history spans from ancient Punic and Roman settlements through medieval prominence in the Crown of Aragon to modern industrialization and its role as a global tourist destination. The city is renowned for its architectural heritage, particularly the works of Antoni Gaudí, and its significant economic and political influence in southwestern Europe.

## Timeline
- **c. 15 BC** — Romans establish the colony of Barcino on Mons Taber.
- **801** — Charlemagne's son Louis conquers Barcelona, making it the seat of the Carolingian "Hispanic March."
- **1137** — Dynastic union between the County of Barcelona and the Kingdom of Aragon.
- **1401** — The Bank or Taula de canvi de Barcelona, a public bank, is established.
- **1850s** — Civil engineer Ildefons Cerdà proposes the Eixample district plan to expand the city.
- **1931** — Barcelona becomes the capital of the autonomous region of Catalonia.
- **1939** — City falls to Francoist forces, ending the Spanish Civil War in Catalonia.
- **1977** — Massive demonstrations in Barcelona call for the restoration of Catalan autonomy.
- **1992** — Barcelona hosts the Summer Olympics, driving major urban regeneration.
- **2017** — An independence referendum is held, leading to a Spanish constitutional crisis.
- **2024** — Barcelona is named UNESCO-UIA World Capital of Architecture for 2024–2026.

## Body

### Historical Development
Barcelona's origins are debated, with traditions linking its founding to Phoenicians or Carthaginians, possibly Hamilcar Barca in the 3rd century BC. Archaeological evidence shows Iberian settlement, and the Romans redrew it as a castrum (military camp) around 15 BC, naming it Barcino. After Roman rule, the city was conquered by Visigoths, then Umayyads, before Charlemagne's son Louis made it the seat of the Hispanic March in 801. The Counts of Barcelona grew increasingly independent, and a 1137 dynastic union with Aragon led to Barcelona becoming the capital of the Crown of Aragon, a major Mediterranean power. It was a leading slave trade center until eclipsed by Valencia in the 15th century.

### Early Modern Period and Industrialization
Following the 1469 dynastic union of Castile and Aragon, Barcelona lost political primacy but maintained a strong economy. The 18th century saw population growth and mercantile expansion, though the city suffered during the Napoleonic wars. Industrialization began in the 1830s, making Catalonia the first industrial region in the Mediterranean. To address severe overcrowding, engineer Ildefons Cerdà designed the Eixample district in the 1850s, a grid plan with wide streets and chamfered corners, though his egalitarian vision was only partially realized. The 19th century also saw Catalan involvement in the Atlantic slave trade, which financed industrialization.

### 20th Century to Present
Barcelona became the capital of autonomous Catalonia in 1931. During the Spanish Civil War, it was a Republican stronghold where anarchists, communists, and government forces vied for control. The city was bombed by Fascist Italy and Nazi Germany in 1938 and fell to Franco's forces in January 1939, leading to the suppression of Catalan autonomy and language. After Franco's death in 1975, massive demonstrations restored Catalan autonomy. The 1992 Summer Olympics catalyzed major urban renewal, transforming industrial seafronts into beaches and boosting the city's global profile. Recent decades have seen population fluctuations due to migration and housing costs, and the city has faced challenges like the 2017 terrorist attack and the 2017 independence referendum crisis.

### Economy and Tourism
Barcelona is a major economic center, with a metropolitan GDP of $177 billion (2009). Its economy is driven by services, tourism, and industry, including automobile manufacturing (SEAT) and technology. The Port of Barcelona is Europe's ninth-largest container port and a leading cruise hub. Tourism is vital but controversial; the city was the 9th most visited globally in 2023, leading to "overtourism" protests and measures like limiting new hotel licenses. The city hosts major events like the Mobile World Congress and was named the 2024–2026 UNESCO-UIA World Capital of Architecture.

### Culture and Society
Barcelona's culture is deeply Catalan, with the language used in government and education. The city is a UNESCO City of Literature and home to iconic architecture, including eight UNESCO World Heritage Sites like the Sagrada Família and Park Güell. It has a vibrant arts scene, with venues like the Gran Teatre del Liceu and festivals such as Sónar. FC Barcelona is a globally renowned sports club. The city is diverse, with significant immigrant communities from Latin America, Europe, and North Africa. It is governed by a city council and is the seat of the Catalan government.

## Terms
- **Crown of Aragon**: A composite monarchy formed in 1137 by the union of the County of Barcelona and the Kingdom of Aragon, which dominated the western Mediterranean.
- **Eixample**: A district designed by Ildefons Cerdà in the 1850s, characterized by a grid plan with chamfered corners, built to expand Barcelona beyond its medieval walls.
- **Generalitat de Catalunya**: The executive and legislative government of the autonomous community of Catalonia, headquartered in Barcelona.
- **Mossos d'Esquadra**: The autonomous police force of Catalonia, responsible for public safety alongside the municipal Guàrdia Urbana.
- **Modernisme**: The Catalan variant of Art Nouveau, an architectural style prominent in Barcelona from 1885 to 1950, exemplified by Antoni Gaudí.
- **Taula de canvi**: Often considered Europe's oldest public bank, established in Barcelona in 1401 by city magistrates.
- **Castrum**: A Roman military camp or fortified settlement, the form of the early Roman town of Barcino.
- **Marca Hispanica**: The Carolingian "Hispanic March," a buffer zone established by Charlemagne with Barcelona as its seat after 801.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Founding Origins**: The traditional attribution of Barcelona's founding to the Carthaginian general Hamilcar Barca is questioned, with some scholars arguing the name derives from the Iberian word *Bárkeno* ("The Place of the Plains").
- **Cerdà's Vision**: The extent to which Ildefons Cerdà's original egalitarian and hygienic plan for the Eixample was realized versus altered by private development and city council decisions is a point of historical analysis.
- **Overtourism Impact**: The economic benefits of tourism versus its social costs, including housing affordability, resident displacement, and cultural identity, is an ongoing debate, highlighted by protests in 2017 and 2024.
- **Independence Movement**: The 2017 independence referendum and its aftermath, including the Spanish constitutional crisis, reflect unresolved political tensions regarding Catalonia's status within Spain.

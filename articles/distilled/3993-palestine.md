# Palestine

## Overview
Palestine is a country in the Southern Levant region of West Asia, encompassing the West Bank (including East Jerusalem) and the Gaza Strip, both occupied by Israel. It is central to the Israeli–Palestinian conflict, a major geopolitical issue rooted in competing national movements and historical claims to the same land. Its political status, borders, and governance are subjects of ongoing international dispute and scholarly debate.

## Timeline
- **1917** — British forces capture Jerusalem, ending Ottoman rule.
- **1920** — League of Nations grants Britain the mandate to govern Palestine.
- **1947** — UN proposes partition into Jewish and Arab states; rejected by Palestinians and Arab nations.
- **1948** — Israel declares independence; 1948 Palestine war leads to the Nakba (forcible displacement of Palestinians).
- **1967** — Six-Day War; Israel occupies the West Bank and Gaza Strip.
- **1988** — Palestine Liberation Organization (PLO) declares the State of Palestine.
- **1993** — Oslo Accords signed, establishing the Palestinian Authority (PA).
- **2005** — Israel unilaterally withdraws from the Gaza Strip.
- **2007** — Hamas takes control of the Gaza Strip after conflict with Fatah.
- **2023** — Hamas-led October 7 attacks on Israel; Israel launches a large-scale military campaign in Gaza.

## Body

### History and Conflict
The Palestine region, at a continental crossroad, has been ruled by various empires. Modern conflict stems from the rise of Zionism and British control during the Mandate period (1920-1948). Increased Jewish immigration led to intercommunal violence. A 1947 UN partition plan was rejected by Palestinians and Arab states, leading to civil war and the 1948 Palestine war. This resulted in Israel's establishment and the Nakba, the forcible displacement of a majority of the Arab population. Israel occupied the West Bank and Gaza Strip in the 1967 Six-Day War. The PLO declared independence in 1988 and signed the Oslo Accords with Israel in 1993, creating the Palestinian Authority. Internal divisions led to a Hamas takeover of Gaza in 2007, splitting governance between the Fatah-led PA in the West Bank and Hamas in Gaza. Following Hamas's October 7, 2023 attacks, Israel's military campaign in Gaza has caused mass displacement, a humanitarian crisis, and a death toll recognized by a growing scholarly consensus as genocide.

### Government and Politics
Palestine operates a semi-presidential system. The Palestine Liberation Organization (PLO) represents the state internationally, while the Palestinian Authority (PA) exercises limited self-governance in parts of the West Bank. The Gaza Strip has been under Hamas control since 2007. The proclaimed capital is Jerusalem, but the administrative center is Ramallah. The West Bank is divided under the Oslo Accords into Areas A (Palestinian civil and security control), B (Palestinian civil, joint security control), and C (full Israeli control). East Jerusalem is claimed by Palestine but annexed by Israel, a move not recognized internationally. Palestine is a UN non-member observer state, recognized by 157 of 193 UN member states.

### Economy and Demographics
Palestine has a middle-income, developing economy heavily impacted by the occupation, blockade, and conflict. Its GDP declined by 35% in early 2024 due to the war. Key sectors include agriculture, manufacturing, and services, but growth is severely hindered by Israeli restrictions on movement, access to resources (like water and Area C), and the blockade of Gaza. The population exceeds five million, with a high literacy rate. Palestinians are predominantly Arab and Muslim, with a Christian minority. A significant portion of the Palestinian population lives in the diaspora as refugees.

### Culture and Society
Palestinian culture is an amalgamation of indigenous, Arab, and imperial influences, with religion playing a central role. Cultural expression, including literature, music (like dabke), and cinema, often serves as a form of resistance. Notable cultural sites include the Old City of Jerusalem and Bethlehem. The country faces challenges to freedom of speech and press. Sports, particularly football, are popular.

## Terms
*   **Nakba:** Arabic for "catastrophe," referring to the 1948 displacement of Palestinians during the war that established Israel.
*   **PLO (Palestine Liberation Organization):** The political organization that represents the Palestinian people internationally and declared the State of Palestine in 1988.
*   **PA (Palestinian Authority):** The interim self-governance body established by the 1993 Oslo Accords to administer parts of the West Bank and Gaza Strip.
*   **Intifada:** Arabic for "uprising," referring to major Palestinian protests against Israeli occupation, notably in 1987 and 2000.
*   **Oslo Accords:** A series of agreements (1993-1995) between Israel and the PLO that created the Palestinian Authority and outlined a framework for peace.
*   **Area C:** The 60% of the West Bank under full Israeli civil and security control per the Oslo II Accord.
*   **Fatah:** A secular Palestinian political party, a dominant faction within the PLO, and the governing party of the PA in the West Bank.
*   **Hamas:** A Palestinian militant and political group that governs the Gaza Strip and is designated a terrorist organization by several countries.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Statehood and Borders:** The legal status of Palestine as a sovereign state is contested. Its final borders, especially regarding the West Bank, Gaza, and East Jerusalem, are unresolved.
*   **Jerusalem:** Both Israel and Palestine claim Jerusalem as their capital. Its legal and diplomatic status is a core issue in peace negotiations.
*   **Right of Return:** The question of whether Palestinian refugees from the 1948 war and their descendants have a right to return to their former homes in what is now Israel is a major point of contention.
*   **Settlements:** Israeli settlements in the occupied West Bank and East Jerusalem are considered illegal under international law by most of the international community, but Israel disputes this.
*   **Governance Division:** The political split between the Fatah-led PA in the West Bank and Hamas in Gaza complicates Palestinian unity and negotiations with Israel.

# Computer graphics

## Overview
Computer graphics (CG) is the field of computer science concerned with generating, manipulating, and displaying visual content using computers. It is a foundational technology for digital photography, film, video games, digital art, and most modern display devices. The discipline has evolved from early military and academic research into a ubiquitous commercial and creative force, revolutionizing animation, movies, advertising, and interactive media.

## Timeline
- **1897** — Invention of the first cathode ray tube (Braun tube), a precursor to electronic displays.
- **1958** — William Higinbotham creates *Tennis for Two*, an early interactive video game with graphics.
- **1960** — The phrase "computer graphics" is coined by Verne Hudson and William Fetter of Boeing.
- **1961** — Steve Russell creates *Spacewar!*, an influential early video game.
- **1963** — E. E. Zajac creates a computer-generated film at Bell Labs.
- **1966** — Ivan Sutherland invents the first head-mounted display (HMD).
- **1968** — Arthur Appel describes the first ray casting algorithm.
- **1969** — ACM initiates SIGGRAPH, a major professional organization for graphics.
- **1974** — Edwin Catmull pioneers texture mapping.
- **1982** — Japan's LINKS-1 system, a supercomputer for realistic 3D rendering, is developed.
- **1986** — David Immel and James Kajiya develop the general rendering equation.
- **1995** — Pixar releases *Toy Story*, the first feature-length computer-animated film.
- **1999** — Nvidia releases the GeForce 256, the first home GPU marketed as such.
- **2001** — *Final Fantasy: The Spirits Within* is the first fully CGI film with photorealistic characters.
- **2020s** — Real-time ray tracing and AI-powered graphics (e.g., DLSS, FSR) become mainstream.

## Body

### History and Development
The precursors to computer graphics were advances in electrical engineering and television in the early 20th century. The field emerged in the 1950s from university research and U.S. military projects like Whirlwind and SAGE, which used CRTs and light pens for interactive displays. A major milestone was Ivan Sutherland's 1963 Sketchpad program, which introduced interactive drawing and object modeling. The 1960s saw the term "computer graphics" coined and the founding of key research centers, notably at the University of Utah, which became a hub for pioneers like Edwin Catmull and James Clark.

The 1970s at Utah produced foundational research in hidden surface determination, shading models (Gouraud, Blinn-Phong), and texture mapping. The 1980s brought commercialization with the first GPUs, intelligent workstations, and the rise of CGI in film (e.g., Industrial Light & Magic) and video games. The 1990s saw 3D graphics become mainstream in games and home computing, with the release of *Toy Story* and the first GPUs for consumers. The 2000s and 2010s were marked by the ubiquity of CGI, advances in shader technology, and the pursuit of photorealism, while the 2020s have focused on real-time ray tracing and AI integration.

### Image Types and Techniques
Computer graphics are broadly categorized as two-dimensional (2D) or three-dimensional (3D). 2D graphics, used in typography and digital art, include pixel art, sprite graphics, and vector graphics. Vector graphics encode shapes and colors mathematically, offering flexibility. 3D graphics use geometric data to create models, which are then rendered into 2D images. Key techniques include texture mapping (adding surface detail), shading (simulating depth and light), and anti-aliasing (smoothing jagged edges). Rendering is the process of generating a 2D image from a 3D model, often using algorithms like ray tracing for photorealism.

### Applications and Impact
Computer graphics are essential in film, video games, television, advertising, and scientific visualization. In cinema, CGI has evolved from special effects to fully animated features and photorealistic characters. In video games, it enables immersive 3D worlds, with real-time rendering driven by powerful GPUs. Beyond entertainment, it is used in medical imaging, engineering design (CAD), education, and data visualization. The field has also given rise to new forms of digital art and generative models that create images from text descriptions.

## Terms
- **Computer Graphics (CG):** The field of generating and manipulating visual content with computers.
- **CGI (Computer-Generated Imagery):** The use of computer graphics to create or enhance images, especially in film and video.
- **GPU (Graphics Processing Unit):** A specialized processor designed to accelerate graphics rendering.
- **Rendering:** The process of generating a 2D image from a 3D model using computer programs.
- **Texture Mapping:** A technique for adding surface detail, color, or texture to a 3D model.
- **Ray Tracing:** A rendering technique that simulates light paths to produce photorealistic images.
- **Shader:** A small program that runs on a GPU to control the appearance of rendered objects.
- **Vector Graphics:** Images defined by mathematical paths (shapes, colors), allowing scalable rendering.
- **Raster Graphics:** Images represented as a grid of pixels, common in digital photography.
- **SIGGRAPH:** The ACM Special Interest Group on Graphics, a leading professional organization and conference.

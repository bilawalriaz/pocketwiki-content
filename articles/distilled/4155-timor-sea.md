# Timor Sea

## Overview
The Timor Sea is a relatively shallow sea in the Indian Ocean, bounded by Timor, Indonesia, and Australia. It is significant for its hydrocarbon reserves, which have led to international disputes and treaties, and for its role in the possible early human migration to Australia. The sea is geologically active, prone to cyclones, and contains important reefs and ecosystems.

## Timeline
- **c. 3000 BC** — Possible timing for early human crossing of the Timor Sea from the Malay Archipelago to Australia.
- **1974** — Woodside-Burmah's Troubadour No. 1 well confirms significant hydrocarbon reserves.
- **1974** — Discovery of the Greater Sunrise gas field.
- **19 February 1942** — Japanese air raids on Darwin from ships in the Timor Sea begin the Battle of Timor.
- **20 May 2002** — Timor Sea Treaty is signed, establishing the Joint Petroleum Development Area (JPDA).
- **2004** — Production commences at the Bayu-Undan petroleum project.
- **2005** — Completion of the Bayu-Undan gas pipeline and Darwin LNG plant.
- **21 August – 3 November 2009** — Montara oil field spill, Australia's largest in 25 years.
- **2013** — Revelation of Australian spying on Timor-Leste during resource negotiations.
- **6 March 2018** — Australia–Timor Leste Maritime Boundaries Treaty is signed.
- **30 July 2018** — First gas achieved from the Ichthys gas field.

## Body

### Geography and Meteorology
The Timor Sea is about 480 km wide, covering 610,000 km². It is mostly shallow, averaging less than 200 m deep over the Sahul Shelf, but its deepest point is the Timor Trough (or Sunda Trench) at over 3,300 m. This trough separates the Australian and Asian continental shelves. The sea is prone to earthquakes and tsunamis north of the trough due to its location on the Ring of Fire, and it experiences major tropical cyclones. The Timor Current, a major contributor to the Indonesian Throughflow of water from the Pacific to the Indian Ocean, runs southwest through the sea.

### Hydrocarbon Resources and Projects
The Timor Sea contains significant oil and gas reserves. The largest project is Bayu-Undan, operated by Santos, which began production in 2004 and supplies gas via pipeline to the Darwin LNG plant. Timor-Leste has earned over $18 billion from Bayu-Undan, though its reserves are predicted to be exhausted by 2023. Other major projects include the Ichthys gas field (first gas in 2018) and the disputed Greater Sunrise field. The Montara oil spill in 2009 was the worst in Australia for 25 years, leaking 400 barrels of oil daily.

### Territorial Disputes and Treaties
Discovery of petroleum led to disputes over the "Timor Gap," an area outside national boundaries. Australia initially claimed the seabed boundary at the Timor Trough, while Timor-Leste (and formerly Portugal) argued for a median line. The 2002 Timor Sea Treaty established the JPDA with a 90:10 royalty split favoring Timor-Leste but did not finalize the maritime boundary. A 2013 spying scandal revealed Australia had bugged Timor-Leste's negotiations. A definitive Maritime Boundaries Treaty establishing a median line was signed in 2018.

## Terms
- **Timor Trough (Sunda Trench):** The deepest point of the Timor Sea (over 3,300 m), marking a major geological boundary between continental shelves.
- **Sahul Shelf:** Part of the Australian continental shelf underlying the shallow southern Timor Sea.
- **Indonesian Throughflow:** A system of ocean currents transporting water from the Pacific Ocean to the Indian Ocean through the Indonesian archipelago.
- **Joint Petroleum Development Area (JPDA):** A zone in the Timor Sea established by the 2002 treaty for shared petroleum resource management.
- **Bathymetric axis:** The line of greatest sea-bed depth, which was the basis for Australia's historical territorial claim.
- **Median line:** A boundary drawn equidistant from the coastlines of two countries, the principle advocated by Timor-Leste.
- **LNG (Liquefied Natural Gas):** Natural gas cooled to a liquid state for easier transport and storage.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The geological interpretation of the Timor Trough is debated: some geologists see it as an extension of the Java Trench, while others view it as a foreland trough to Timor's mountain range.
- The future of the Greater Sunrise gas field remains contested, with Woodside Petroleum proposing a floating platform and Timor-Leste's government preferring a pipeline to its territory.
- Opposition exists to new projects like the Barossa gas field from Indigenous groups and climate justice organizations.

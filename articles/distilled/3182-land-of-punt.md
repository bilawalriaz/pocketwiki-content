# Land of Punt

## Overview
The Land of Punt was an ancient kingdom, known primarily from Egyptian trade records, that exported valuable goods like gold, aromatic resins, and ebony. Its exact location is debated, but modern scholarship and scientific evidence increasingly point to the Horn of Africa, particularly the region of modern Eritrea and Ethiopia. The relationship between Egypt and Punt was significant, with Egyptians viewing it as a source of luxury goods and possibly as a divine ancestral homeland.

## Timeline
- **c. 25th century BC** — Earliest recorded Egyptian expedition to Punt organized by Pharaoh Sahure (Fifth Dynasty).
- **c. 2000 BC** — Officer Hannu organizes voyages to Punt during the reign of Mentuhotep III (Eleventh Dynasty).
- **15th century BC** — Queen Hatshepsut (Eighteenth Dynasty) launches a famous, well-documented expedition to Punt.
- **15th century BC** — Archaeological evidence from Mersa/Wadi Gawasis confirms it as the Egyptian port for Punt trade.
- **12th century BC** — An expedition to Punt is described in the Papyrus Harris I from the reign of Ramesses III (Twentieth Dynasty).
- **Post-New Kingdom** — Punt becomes an "unreal and fabulous land" in Egyptian literature after trade ceases.

## Body

### Egyptian Expeditions and Trade
Egyptian expeditions to Punt began as early as the Fourth Dynasty (c. 2500 BC) and continued intermittently for over a millennium. The most famous expedition was led by Queen Hatshepsut in the 15th century BC, which is extensively documented in reliefs at her mortuary temple at Deir el-Bahri. These records show that Punt was a well-established trading partner, not a conquered territory. The Egyptians navigated the Red Sea from ports like Mersa/Wadi Gawasis to obtain luxury goods for religious and royal use, including incense, ebony, ivory, and live animals. Trade continued into the early 20th Dynasty before ending during Egypt's New Kingdom period.

### Cultural and Religious Significance
The Egyptians sometimes called Punt *Ta netjer*, meaning "Land of the God," referring to its location in the direction of the sunrise (east) and its abundance of temple resources like incense. This name led to older theories that Punt was considered Egypt's ancestral homeland, a view held by scholars like E. A. Wallis Budge. However, other Egyptologists, such as James Breasted, argued the term was applied to various eastern regions. The depiction of Puntites in Hatshepsut's temple shows them with features similar to Egyptians, though their exact ethnic identity remains part of broader scholarly debates about ancient Egyptian and African connections.

### Proposed Locations
The majority of scholars place Punt in the Horn of Africa, based on the products and animals depicted in Egyptian records (e.g., giraffes, baboons, myrrh) which are native to that region. Scientific studies have strengthened this view: oxygen isotope analysis of mummified baboons (2010) and strontium analysis of their tooth enamel (2020) point to a region encompassing modern Eritrea, Ethiopia, and Somalia. A 2023 DNA study of a mummified baboon further suggested Eritrea as the location. A minority view, held by scholar Dimitri Meeks, places Punt on the western coast of the Arabian Peninsula. An older, now-discredited theory suggested a link to Sri Lanka.

## Terms
- **Punt (pwnt):** An ancient kingdom known from Egyptian records as a source of luxury trade goods.
- **Ta netjer (tꜣ nṯr):** An Egyptian name for Punt meaning "Land of the God," referencing its eastern location and sacred resources.
- **Hatshepsut:** An 18th Dynasty female pharaoh who organized a famous, well-documented expedition to Punt.
- **Mersa/Wadi Gawasis:** An archaeological site on the Red Sea coast of Egypt identified as the port from which expeditions to Punt were launched.
- **New Kingdom:** A period of ancient Egyptian history (c. 1550–1070 BC) during which trade with Punt flourished and later ceased.
- **Horn of Africa:** A peninsula in East Africa, the modern region most scholars identify as the location of Punt.
- **Steatopygia:** A medical term for the condition of pronounced fat deposits on the buttocks, a hypothesis once used to explain the appearance of the Queen of Punt in reliefs.

## Debates and Open Questions
The primary debate concerns the precise geographical location of Punt. While the Horn of Africa is the leading hypothesis, supported by recent scientific analysis of animal remains, some scholars argue for the Arabian Peninsula. A secondary debate involves the cultural interpretation of *Ta netjer*—whether it implies Punt was viewed as a divine ancestral homeland or simply a distant eastern land rich in temple goods. The exact nature of the relationship between Egypt and Punt, and the ethnic identity of the Puntites, also remain areas of scholarly discussion.

# Upper Paleolithic

## Overview
The Upper Paleolithic (c. 50,000–12,000 years ago) is the final period of the Old Stone Age, marking a dramatic shift in human behavior known as behavioral modernity. It is defined by the appearance of sophisticated tools, art, and complex social structures, coinciding with the global expansion of *Homo sapiens* and the extinction of Neanderthals. This period laid the cultural foundations for the subsequent Mesolithic and the dawn of agriculture.

## Timeline
- **c. 50,000** — Marked increase in artifact diversity; modern humans expand from Africa.
- **c. 45,000** — Earliest modern humans in Europe; expansion into Siberia (Ust'-Ishim man).
- **c. 44,000–43,000** — Earliest mathematical artifact (Lebombo bone); oldest known mining (Ngwenya Mine).
- **c. 40,000** — Earliest figurative cave art in Europe (Caves of Nerja); Chauvet Cave paintings.
- **c. 35,000** — First notational signs/proto-writing; Aurignacian culture flourishes.
- **c. 25,000–15,000** — Last Glacial Maximum (LGM); human populations refugia in Europe.
- **c. 15,000** — Peopling of the Americas via Beringia; Bølling interstadial begins.
- **c. 14,700–12,900** — Bølling–Allerød warm period; Lascaux cave paintings.
- **c. 12,900–11,700** — Younger Dryas cold period; first pottery in Japan.
- **c. 11,700** — Holocene begins; transition to Mesolithic in Western Eurasia.

## Body

### Behavioral Modernity and Technology
The Upper Paleolithic is characterized by a leap in technological and cultural complexity. Prior to 50,000 years ago, stone tools across regions were relatively uniform and simple. After this point, archaeologists find a diverse toolkit with specialized functions: projectile points, engraving tools, knife blades, and drilling tools. This "Upper Paleolithic Revolution" includes the development of fine blade-based industries, bone and antler tools (burins, racloirs), advanced darts, harpoons, fish hooks, eyed needles, and the oil lamp. These innovations supported more varied diets, including fishing and open-ocean navigation, and enabled complex settlements, some used seasonally and others year-round.

### Art, Symbolism, and Social Change
Artistic expression blossomed, including cave paintings, petroglyphs, and carvings on bone and ivory (e.g., Venus figurines, the Löwenmensch lion-man). Notational signs, possibly proto-writing, appear by c. 35,000 BP, using lines and dots to convey seasonal information about animal behavior. These symbolic and artistic developments, alongside more reliable food sources and specialized tools, likely fostered more complex social groupings and a sense of group identity or ethnicity.

### Climate, Geography, and Human Adaptation
The period was shaped by dramatic climate shifts, most notably the Last Glacial Maximum (c. 25,000–15,000 BP), which covered Northern Europe in ice and forced humans into refugia like the Iberian Peninsula and the Balkans. Subsequent rapid fluctuations—the cold Oldest Dryas, the warm Bølling–Allerød, and the severe Younger Dryas—impacted resources and settlement patterns. As glaciers receded, sea levels rose by over 100 meters, submerging coastal sites and creating land bridges like Beringia, which facilitated the peopling of the Americas.

### Regional Cultures
Distinct archaeological cultures emerged across the Old World. In the Franco-Cantabrian region, the sequence includes the Châtelperronian (associated with late Neanderthals), Aurignacian, Gravettian, Solutrean, and Magdalenian. Other notable cultures include the Aterian in North Africa, the Jōmon in Japan, and the Hoabinhian in Southeast Asia. These cultures are defined by their unique toolkits and artistic styles, reflecting regional adaptations and traditions.

## Terms
- **Behavioral Modernity:** A set of cognitive and cultural traits (symbolic thought, complex language, art) that distinguish modern humans from archaic hominins.
- **Mousterian:** A stone tool industry associated primarily with Neanderthals, preceding the Upper Paleolithic.
- **Aurignacian:** An early Upper Paleolithic culture in Europe and SW Asia, characterized by blade tools and figurative art.
- **Gravettian:** A European culture known for its "Venus" figurines and specialized hunting tools.
- **Solutrean:** A culture in France and Spain noted for finely crafted, leaf-shaped projectile points.
- **Magdalenian:** A late Upper Paleolithic culture famous for its cave art and bone/ivory tools.
- **Last Glacial Maximum (LGM):** The peak of the last ice age, when ice sheets were at their greatest extent.
- **Refugia:** Areas where populations survive during periods of unfavorable climate (e.g., glacial maxima).
- **Epipaleolithic/Mesolithic:** Transitional periods between the Paleolithic and Neolithic, marked by hunter-gatherer adaptations to post-glacial environments.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The cause of the "Upper Paleolithic Revolution" is debated: was it a cognitive change, a demographic shift due to modern human expansion, or a response to climate pressures?
- The extent and nature of interaction (including interbreeding) between modern humans and Neanderthals during their overlap period (c. 45,000–40,000 BP) is actively researched.
- The authorship of some early European cave art (e.g., Caves of Nerja, c. 40,000 BP) is contested, with some theories suggesting Neanderthal involvement.
- The precise timing and routes of the peopling of the Americas remain subjects of ongoing archaeological investigation.

# Eiffel Tower

## Overview
The Eiffel Tower is a wrought-iron lattice tower on the Champ de Mars in Paris, France. Built as the centerpiece of the 1889 World's Fair, it was initially criticized by artists but has since become a global cultural icon of France and one of the most recognizable structures in the world. It is the most visited paid monument globally.

## Timeline
- **May 1884** — Engineers Maurice Koechlin and Émile Nouguier sketch the initial design.
- **1886** — A competition for the 1889 Exposition Universelle centerpiece is altered to favor a 300m metal tower.
- **8 January 1887** — Gustave Eiffel signs the construction contract.
- **14 February 1887** — A petition of artists, "Artists against the Eiffel Tower," is published.
- **28 January 1887** — Construction of the foundations begins.
- **31 March 1889** — Eiffel leads the first ascent to the top to hoist the French flag.
- **6 May 1889** — The tower opens to the public for the Exposition Universelle.
- **1910** — The tower begins transmitting radio signals, saving it from scheduled demolition.
- **1940** — German forces occupy Paris; lift cables are cut by the French.
- **1964** — The tower is designated a *monument historique*.
- **1991** — The tower is named part of a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
- **2000** — Flashing lights are installed for the millennium celebration.

## Body

### Origin and Controversy
The design originated with engineers Koechlin and Nouguier of Gustave Eiffel's company, with architectural embellishments by Stephen Sauvestre. It was selected as the centerpiece for the 1889 Exposition Universelle, celebrating the centennial of the French Revolution. A "Committee of Three Hundred" of prominent artists, including Charles Garnier and Guy de Maupassant, protested the "useless and monstrous" design, fearing it would overshadow Paris's historic monuments. Eiffel defended the tower's aesthetic, comparing it to the Egyptian pyramids.

### Construction
Construction began on 28 January 1887 and was completed in just over two years. The process involved 18,038 prefabricated iron parts joined by 2.5 million rivets. The foundations for the legs near the Seine required compressed-air caissons. The legs were initially built as cantilevers, then joined at the first level. Safety measures were strict; only one worker died during construction. The tower was inaugurated on 31 March 1889, and opened to the public on 6 May.

### Design and Engineering
The tower is made of puddle iron (wrought iron) and stands 330 meters tall, including its broadcasting aerial. Its curved shape was mathematically determined to offer optimal resistance to wind forces. The structure has three levels for visitors, with restaurants on the first and second levels. The top level features a private apartment built for Gustave Eiffel. The tower's original hydraulic lifts were a major engineering challenge, especially for the curved legs leading to the second level.

### Later History and Use
Originally intended to stand for 20 years, the tower was saved by its utility for radio transmission. It played a strategic role in World War I by jamming German communications. During World War II, French forces cut the lift cables to hinder German use, and Hitler ordered its demolition, which was disobeyed. In 1957, a broadcasting aerial was added, increasing its height. The tower has been continuously repainted and maintained, and its image is now a protected copyright for commercial use.

## Terms
- **Puddle iron (wrought iron):** The type of iron used in the tower's construction, known for its durability.
- **Cantilever:** A structural element anchored at only one end, used in the initial phase of building the tower's legs.
- **Compressed-air caissons:** Pressurized chambers used to build foundations underwater or in unstable ground, employed for the legs near the Seine.
- **Rivet:** A metal fastener used to join the tower's 18,038 iron pieces together.
- **Monument historique:** A French legal designation for a protected historical building, granted to the tower in 1964.
- **Calligram:** A poem or text arranged to form a visual image, like the one Guillaume Apollinaire wrote in the shape of the tower.
- **Hydraulic ram:** A type of pump used in the tower's original lift systems to move the cars.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The original artistic controversy: Was the tower a "monstrous" blot on the Parisian skyline or a masterpiece of modern engineering? This debate reflected a broader conflict between traditional architecture and industrial engineering.
- The tower's financing: A portion of the construction funds came from loans to Haiti connected to a controversial indemnity, raising questions about the ethical foundations of the monument.
- Copyright of the illuminated tower: The operating company claims copyright over nighttime lighting displays, making commercial use of such images potentially illegal, a claim that remains legally untested and controversial.

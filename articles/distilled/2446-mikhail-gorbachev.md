# Mikhail Gorbachev

## Overview
Mikhail Gorbachev was the last leader of the Soviet Union, serving as General Secretary from 1985 and as President from 1988 until its dissolution in 1991. His policies of *glasnost* (openness) and *perestroika* (restructuring) aimed to reform the Soviet system but inadvertently accelerated its collapse, ending the Cold War and reshaping global politics. His legacy is deeply contested, praised for ending totalitarianism and criticized for precipitating economic collapse and weakening Russia's global standing.

## Timeline
- **2 March 1931** — Born in Privolnoye, North Caucasus Krai, Russian SFSR.
- **1950** — Joins the Communist Party of the Soviet Union (CPSU).
- **1955** — Graduates from Moscow State University with a law degree.
- **1970** — Becomes First Secretary of the Stavropol Regional Committee.
- **1978** — Appointed Secretary of the CPSU Central Committee, returning to Moscow.
- **11 March 1985** — Elected General Secretary of the CPSU after the death of Konstantin Chernenko.
- **1986** — Launches policies of *glasnost* and *perestroika*.
- **1988** — Becomes head of state as Chairman of the Presidium of the Supreme Soviet.
- **1989** — Declines to intervene militarily as Warsaw Pact regimes fall.
- **March 1990** — Elected first and only executive President of the Soviet Union.
- **August 1991** — Survives an attempted coup by Communist hardliners.
- **25 December 1991** — Resigns as Soviet President; the USSR is formally dissolved the next day.

## Body

### Early Life and Rise in the Party
Born into a peasant family, Gorbachev grew up under Stalin's rule, witnessing the purges and famine that affected his relatives. He joined the Komsomol youth organization in 1946 and the Communist Party in 1950. After graduating from Moscow State University in 1955, he began his career in Stavropol, rising through the ranks of the Komsomol and the party. He became a supporter of Nikita Khrushchev's de-Stalinization reforms. In 1970, he was appointed First Secretary of the Stavropol Regional Committee, where he oversaw agricultural projects like the Great Stavropol Canal. His reliability and reformist inclinations brought him to Moscow in 1978 as a Central Committee secretary, and he joined the Politburo in 1980.

### Path to Leadership
Following the deaths of Leonid Brezhnev (1982) and Yuri Andropov (1984), the aging Konstantin Chernenko became leader. Gorbachev, as a younger Politburo member, became Chernenko's de facto deputy. After Chernenko's death in March 1985, Gorbachev was elected General Secretary. He believed the Soviet system required radical reform to survive. He withdrew troops from the Soviet–Afghan War and pursued summits with U.S. President Ronald Reagan to limit nuclear weapons and end the Cold War.

### Reforms and the Unraveling of the USSR
Domestically, Gorbachev introduced *glasnost* to allow greater freedom of speech and *perestroika* to decentralize the economy. He also pursued *demokratizatsiya* (democratization), creating an elected Congress of People's Deputies that undermined the Communist Party's monopoly on power. In 1989, he refused to use force to stop Marxist-Leninist governments from falling in Eastern Europe. However, nationalist movements within Soviet republics gained strength. In 1990, he became the USSR's first executive president and oversaw the repeal of Article 6, which had enshrined the CPSU as the ruling party. He acquiesced to German reunification within NATO, a decision criticized domestically.

### The August Coup and Dissolution
In August 1991, Communist hardliners launched a coup while Gorbachev was on holiday, placing him under house arrest. The coup failed due to popular resistance led by Boris Yeltsin, the Russian republic's president. After his return, Gorbachev's authority was fatally weakened. Yeltsin and the leaders of Ukraine and Belarus signed the Belavezha Accords in December 1991, declaring the Soviet Union dissolved. Gorbachev resigned on 25 December 1991.

### Post-Soviet Life
After leaving office, Gorbachev founded the Gorbachev Foundation and became a vocal critic of Yeltsin's policies and later of Vladimir Putin's government. He campaigned for social democracy in Russia and remained an international figure, receiving the Nobel Peace Prize in 1990. His wife, Raisa, died in 1999. He died in Moscow on 30 August 2022.

## Terms
- **Glasnost**: Gorbachev's policy of "openness," which expanded freedom of speech, press, and political discourse in the Soviet Union.
- **Perestroika**: Gorbachev's policy of "restructuring," aimed at decentralizing economic decision-making and introducing limited market mechanisms.
- **Demokratizatsiya**: Gorbachev's policy of "democratization," which introduced competitive elections and reduced the Communist Party's constitutional monopoly on power.
- **Congress of People's Deputies**: A new legislative body established in 1989 with some directly elected members, which challenged the authority of the Communist Party.
- **Belavezha Accords**: The agreement signed in December 1991 by the leaders of Russia, Ukraine, and Belarus that formally dissolved the Soviet Union and created the Commonwealth of Independent States (CIS).
- **Nomenklatura**: The elite class of officials in the Soviet Union who held key positions in the Communist Party and state apparatus.

## Debates and Open questions
- **Intentionality of Collapse**: Scholars debate whether Gorbachev intended to dissolve the Soviet Union or if his reforms spiraled out of control. The source notes he "set reforms in motion without understanding where they could lead."
- **NATO Expansion Promise**: There is confusion over whether U.S. Secretary of State James Baker promised Gorbachev that NATO would not expand into Eastern Europe. The source states there was "no oral or written US promise" beyond Germany, and Gorbachev acknowledged only a promise regarding East Germany.
- **Historical Assessment**: Opinions remain deeply divided. In Russia, he is often despised for the USSR's collapse and subsequent economic turmoil. In the West, he is frequently hailed as a visionary statesman who ended the Cold War peacefully. A 2017 Levada Center poll showed only 15% of Russians viewed him positively.

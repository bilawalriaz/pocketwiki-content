# Casimir III the Great

## Overview
Casimir III the Great (r. 1333–1370) was the last king of Poland from the Piast dynasty. His reign transformed a small, weakened realm into a larger, more centralized, and prosperous state through pragmatic diplomacy, extensive legal and administrative reforms, and territorial expansion eastward. He is remembered as one of Poland's most effective medieval monarchs, earning the unique title "the Great."

## Timeline
- **30 April 1310** — Born in Kowal, Kuyavia.
- **16 October 1325** — Marries Aldona of Lithuania.
- **2 March 1333** — Inherits the Polish throne from his father, Władysław I Łokietek.
- **25 April 1333** — Crowned King of Poland at Wawel Cathedral.
- **24 August 1335** — Luxembourg dynasty renounces claim to Polish crown in exchange for Casimir's recognition of Bohemian control over Silesia.
- **November 1335** — Visegrád arbitration court rules Poland must renounce Pomerania and Chełmno Land to the Teutonic Order.
- **1340** — Becomes King of Ruthenia (Galicia–Volhynia).
- **11 March 1347** — Introduces legal reforms at the Sejm in Wiślica, codifying Polish law.
- **1355** — Designates his nephew, Louis I of Hungary, as his successor.
- **1364** — Founds the University of Kraków (Jagiellonian University).
- **5 November 1370** — Dies; succeeded by Louis I of Hungary.

## Body

### Foreign Policy and Expansion
Casimir inherited a kingdom surrounded by threats, notably from the Kingdom of Bohemia and the Teutonic Order. He pursued a pragmatic foreign policy, using diplomacy and arbitration to secure peace. In 1335, he accepted the loss of Silesia to Bohemia in exchange for the formal renunciation of the Bohemian king's claim to the Polish throne. A 1335 arbitration at Visegrád settled a dispute with the Teutonic Order, though it required Poland to renounce Pomerania and Chełmno Land. This stability allowed him to focus on eastward expansion. In 1340, he became King of Ruthenia, annexing Red Ruthenia (Galicia–Volhynia), which significantly increased Poland's territory, population, and access to Black Sea trade.

### Domestic Reforms and State-Building
Casimir's domestic policies centralized the state and strengthened royal authority. He established the *Corona Regni Poloniae* (Crown of the Polish Kingdom), which defined Polish lands as a political entity independent of the ruling dynasty. At the Sejm of Wiślica in 1347, he introduced major legal reforms, sanctioning civil and criminal codes for Greater and Lesser Poland, which earned him the nickname "the Polish Justinian." His reign saw significant infrastructure development, including the construction of about 50 castles and the fortification of nearly 30 towns with stone and brick. He also managed the Black Death through quarantine, minimizing its impact.

### Society and the Jewish Community
Casimir was known as "the Peasants' King" for his efforts to balance the power of the nobility, clergy, and bourgeoisie, often siding with the lower classes. A key aspect of his domestic policy was his protection of Jewish communities. In 1334, he confirmed their privileges, prohibited the kidnapping of Jewish children for forced baptism, and punished the desecration of cemeteries. He encouraged Jewish settlement in great numbers, making Poland a major center of Ashkenazi Jewish life.

### Patronage and Succession
A notable patron of learning, Casimir founded the University of Kraków in 1364, the second university in Central Europe after Prague's Charles University. Despite his achievements, he left no legitimate male heir. In 1355, he had designated his nephew, Louis I of Hungary, as successor. Upon Casimir's death in 1370, Louis became king, ending Piast rule and creating a personal union between Poland and Hungary.

## Terms
- **Piast dynasty:** The first ruling dynasty of Poland, which began with Mieszko I and ended with Casimir III.
- **Corona Regni Poloniae:** The "Crown of the Polish Kingdom," a concept established by Casimir to denote the Polish state as a legal entity separate from the monarch.
- **Sejm:** A Polish legislative assembly or parliament.
- **Magdeburg rights:** A set of town privileges allowing for self-governance, which Casimir granted to many towns to encourage growth.
- **Statutes of Wiślica and Piotrków:** Legal codes issued by Casimir in 1347 that codified Polish law and strengthened royal justice.
- **Red Ruthenia:** The historical name for the region of Galicia–Volhynia, annexed by Poland under Casimir.
- **Teutonic Order:** A German military order that controlled territories along the Baltic coast and was a frequent rival of Poland.
- **Personal union:** A situation where two or more states are ruled by the same monarch but remain legally separate, as occurred between Poland and Hungary after Casimir's death.

## Debates and open questions
- The exact motivations and details behind Casimir's multiple marriages and divorces, which were often considered bigamous, are subjects of historical analysis.
- The legendary figure of his Jewish mistress, Esterka, remains unconfirmed by direct historical evidence.
- The long-term impact of his legal reforms on the development of Polish social structures, particularly the balance between nobility and peasantry, is debated.

# Honduras

## Overview
Honduras is a Central American republic with a history of indigenous civilizations, Spanish colonization, and a modern era marked by political instability, economic inequality, and vulnerability to natural disasters. Its development has been significantly influenced by foreign corporate interests, particularly U.S. fruit companies, and it remains one of the poorest countries in the Western Hemisphere.

## Timeline
- **1502** — Christopher Columbus lands near Trujillo, the first European visit.
- **1524** — Spanish conquest begins with Gil González Dávila.
- **1821** — Honduras gains independence from Spain.
- **1838** — Honduras becomes a fully independent republic.
- **1960** — International Court of Justice transfers the northern Mosquito Coast from Nicaragua to Honduras.
- **1963** — Military coup overthrows the civilian government.
- **1969** — Honduras and El Salvador fight the "Football War."
- **1998** — Hurricane Mitch causes massive destruction, reversing decades of progress.
- **2009** — Military coup ousts President Manuel Zelaya.
- **2021** — Xiomara Castro is elected president, returning the left to power.
- **2025** — Nasry Asfura wins the presidential election amid fraud allegations.

## Body

### Pre-colonial and Colonial History
Before Spanish arrival, Honduras was home to Mesoamerican cultures, most notably the Maya state at Copán, which declined in the 9th century. The Spanish conquest began in 1524, facing resistance from leaders like Lempira. Silver mining drove the colonial economy, which relied on indigenous and later African slave labor. The Spanish controlled the southern Pacific portion but were less successful in the north, where the Miskito Kingdom resisted with British support.

### Independence and Political Instability
Honduras gained independence in 1821 and was part of the First Mexican Empire and then the United Provinces of Central America before becoming a sovereign republic in 1838. The 19th century saw failed attempts at Central American unity and the rise of an export economy based on bananas and other tropical fruits. This era was dominated by U.S. fruit companies, which created an "enclave economy" and led to the coining of the term "banana republic." Political life has been characterized by frequent internal rebellions and military coups, including a 1963 coup and a 2009 coup that ousted President Zelaya.

### 20th and 21st Century Conflicts and Governance
The 20th century involved U.S. military interventions and the 1969 "Football War" with El Salvador. The 1980s saw a U.S. military presence to support Contra operations in Nicaragua. Hurricane Mitch in 1998 caused catastrophic damage, killing about 5,000 people and destroying 70% of crops. The 2009 coup led to international condemnation and a subsequent increase in poverty and inequality. Recent elections in 2013, 2017, and 2025 have been marred by fraud allegations. President Xiomara Castro's government (2021–present) has focused on social spending and re-nationalizing key industries.

### Economy and Society
Honduras has a low middle-income economy heavily reliant on agriculture, making it vulnerable to natural disasters. It has one of the highest levels of income inequality in Latin America. As of 2022, 73% of the population lived in poverty. The society is predominantly Mestizo (83%), with significant Indigenous, Black, and White communities. Spanish is the official language, and while historically Catholic, Protestantism is now the largest religion. Crime and gang violence are rampant, contributing to high emigration rates, primarily to the United States.

## Terms
- **Mestizo**: A person of combined European and Indigenous American descent; the predominant ethnic group in Honduras.
- **Banana Republic**: A term coined by O. Henry to describe a politically unstable, corporate-dominated democracy, originally inspired by Honduras.
- **Enclave Economy**: An economic system where a foreign-controlled industry (like fruit companies) operates with little connection to the local economy.
- **Mosquito Coast**: A historical region along the Caribbean coast, transferred from Nicaragua to Honduras in 1960.
- **Football War**: A brief 1969 conflict between Honduras and El Salvador, triggered by border tensions and social issues.
- **Hurricane Mitch**: A devastating 1998 hurricane that caused massive destruction and loss of life in Honduras.
- **ZEDE (Zonas de empleo y desarrollo económico)**: Autonomous economic zones established in 2013 with their own legal and administrative systems.
- **Human Development Index (HDI)**: A composite statistic of life expectancy, education, and per capita income indicators, used to rank countries by development.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The legality of the 2009 coup remains contested; while the Honduran Supreme Court ruled it legal, a national truth commission concluded it was an illegal coup d'état.
- The effectiveness of international aid and Poverty Reduction Strategies is debated, with critics arguing they have failed to produce lasting economic growth or significantly reduce poverty.
- The impact of U.S. foreign policy and military presence in Honduras, particularly during the 1980s Contra war and in contemporary counter-narcotics efforts, is a subject of ongoing analysis.
- The causes and solutions for Honduras's extremely high levels of crime and violence, linked to drug trafficking and gang activity, are complex and unresolved.

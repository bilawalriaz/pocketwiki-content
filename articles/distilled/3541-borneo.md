# Borneo

## Overview
Borneo is the world's third-largest island, a critical biodiversity hotspot with some of the oldest tropical rainforests on Earth. It is politically divided among three nations—Indonesia, Malaysia, and Brunei—and its history is shaped by ancient human settlement, powerful sultanates, and colonial competition. The island's ecology and indigenous cultures face significant modern pressures from deforestation and economic development.

## Timeline
- **c. 65,000 years ago** — Archaeological evidence of human presence at Niah Cave, Sarawak.
- **4th century** — Hindu influence evidenced by stone pillars in Kutai, East Kalimantan.
- **10th century** — Islam arrives via Muslim traders.
- **14th century** — Majapahit Empire exerts influence; Sultanate of Brunei declares independence.
- **15th-17th centuries** — Golden age of the Sultanate of Brunei, ruling most of the coast.
- **1842** — James Brooke is granted Sarawak by the Sultan of Brunei, establishing the "White Rajahs."
- **1888** — North Borneo, Sarawak, and Brunei become British protectorates.
- **1891** — Southern Borneo becomes a Dutch protectorate.
- **1941-1945** — Japanese occupation during World War II.
- **1963** — Northern Borneo (Sabah, Sarawak) joins the Federation of Malaysia.
- **2019** — Indonesia announces plans to move its capital to East Kalimantan.

## Body

### Etymology and Names
The island is internationally known as **Borneo**, a name derived from European contact with the Brunei kingdom in the 16th century, possibly from the Sanskrit *váruṇa* (water or the god Varuna). In Indonesia, it is called **Kalimantan**, a name derived from the indigenous term *Pulu K'lemantang* recorded by the Portuguese in the 16th century. Historical names include *Nusa Tanjungnagara* in the 1365 Javanese manuscript *Nagarakretagama*.

### Geology and Geography
Borneo formed through Mesozoic geological processes and was once part of the Asian mainland as part of **Sundaland**, becoming isolated by post-ice age sea level rise. It is the largest island in Asia, with Mount Kinabalu (4,095 m) as its highest point. The island is drained by major river systems, including the Kapuas (1,143 km), Barito, and Mahakam. It is surrounded by the South China, Sulu, Celebes, and Java Seas.

### Ecology and Environment
The Borneo rainforest is estimated to be 140 million years old, dominated by **dipterocarp** trees. It is a global centre for biodiversity and endemic species, including the Bornean orangutan, Bornean elephant, and Bornean clouded leopard. The WWF classifies the island into seven ecoregions, from lowland rainforests to montane forests. However, industrial deforestation for timber and palm oil plantations has been severe. Between 2002 and 2019, Indonesian Borneo lost 10.7 million hectares of tree cover. Forest fires, often for land clearance, cause transboundary haze.

### Early History and Trade
Human settlement dates back at least 65,000 years. By the first millennium AD, western coastal cities were established trading ports, exporting gold, camphor, and other goods. The island was a vassal of the Majapahit Empire by the 14th century. The Sultanate of Brunei and later the Sultanate of Sulu dominated the northern coasts, while the interior remained largely independent.

### Colonial Era and World War II
European powers, including the Portuguese, Spanish, British, and Dutch, engaged in trade and territorial disputes. The British established control in the north through figures like James Brooke and the North Borneo Chartered Company, while the Dutch consolidated the south. The **Anglo-Dutch Treaty of 1824** and the **Madrid Protocol of 1885** helped define colonial spheres. During World War II, Japan occupied the island from 1941 to 1945, causing massive suffering and death among prisoners of war and civilians.

### Modern Political Division
Post-war decolonization led to the island's current political division. The southern part became part of Indonesia in 1945. In the north, the formation of Malaysia in 1963 incorporated the British colonies of Sabah and Sarawak, opposed by Indonesia (leading to the **Konfrontasi**) and the Philippines (which claims Sabah). Brunei remained a separate sovereign state. The island's economy relies on oil and gas, agriculture, logging, and mining.

## Terms
- **Sundaland**: A region of Southeast Asia, including Borneo, that was connected to mainland Asia during the last ice age.
- **Dayak**: A collective term for the non-Muslim Indigenous peoples of Borneo, such as the Iban and Kayan.
- **Dipterocarp**: A family of tropical trees that dominate Borneo's lowland rainforests.
- **Wallace's Line**: A faunal boundary line separating the ecozones of Asia and Wallacea (including Sulawesi), running between Borneo and Sulawesi.
- **Majapahit Empire**: A 14th-century Javanese empire that exerted influence over parts of Borneo.
- **Konfrontasi**: The undeclared war (1963-1966) between Indonesia and the newly formed Malaysia.
- **Peat swamp forest**: A waterlogged forest ecosystem found along Borneo's coastlines, with infertile soil.
- **Thalassocracy**: A maritime empire or state with primary influence over the sea, like the Sultanates of Brunei and Sulu.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The precise origins and meanings of the names "Borneo" and "Kalimantan" are debated, with multiple linguistic theories.
- The extent of pre-colonial political control by the Sultanates of Brunei and Sulu over Borneo's interior is unclear.
- The Philippine claim to Sabah, based on the inheritance of the Sultanate of Sulu's territorial rights, remains a point of diplomatic contention.
- The long-term ecological and social impacts of large-scale deforestation and the relocation of Indonesia's capital to the island are subjects of ongoing concern and study.

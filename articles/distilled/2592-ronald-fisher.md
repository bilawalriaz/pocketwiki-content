# Ronald Fisher

## Overview
Ronald Aylmer Fisher (1890–1962) was an English polymath whose work fundamentally shaped modern statistics and population genetics. He is credited with creating the foundations of statistical science and, alongside J.B.S. Haldane and Sewall Wright, founding population genetics, which reconciled Mendelian genetics with Darwinian natural selection to form the modern evolutionary synthesis. His legacy is complex due to his strong advocacy for eugenics and controversial views on race.

## Timeline
- **1890** — Born in East Finchley, London.
- **1912** — Gains First in Mathematics from Gonville and Caius College, Cambridge.
- **1918** — Publishes paper reconciling Mendelian inheritance with continuous variation, introducing the term "variance."
- **1919–1933** — Works at Rothamsted Experimental Station, developing analysis of variance (ANOVA) and maximum likelihood estimation.
- **1925** — Publishes *Statistical Methods for Research Workers*, popularizing the p-value and significance testing.
- **1930** — Publishes *The Genetical Theory of Natural Selection*, a core text of the modern synthesis.
- **1933–1943** — Serves as Galton Professor of Eugenics at University College London.
- **1935** — Publishes *The Design of Experiments*, outlining principles of experimental design and the null hypothesis.
- **1943–1956** — Holds the Balfour Chair of Genetics at the University of Cambridge.
- **1952** — Knighted by Queen Elizabeth II.
- **1957–1962** — Retires to Australia, working at CSIRO in Adelaide.
- **1962** — Dies in Adelaide, Australia.

## Body

### Statistical Foundations
Fisher's work at Rothamsted analyzing crop data led to the development of analysis of variance (ANOVA) and the popularization of maximum likelihood estimation. His 1925 book *Statistical Methods for Research Workers* formalized the use of the p-value, proposing 0.05 as a threshold for statistical significance. He pioneered principles for the design of experiments, including randomization and the use of control groups, and developed key concepts like fiducial inference and sufficient statistics. His 1921 paper on statistical estimation is considered one of the most influential in the field.

### Population Genetics and Evolutionary Biology
Fisher's 1918 paper demonstrated that continuous variation in traits could result from the combined action of many discrete Mendelian genes, bridging the gap between Mendelian genetics and Darwinian natural selection. His 1930 book *The Genetical Theory of Natural Selection* outlined core concepts of population genetics, including Fisher's principle (explaining the 1:1 sex ratio), Fisherian runaway (a model of sexual selection), and the fundamental theorem of natural selection. He also contributed theories on parental investment, the sexy son hypothesis, and the evolution of dominance.

### Eugenics and Controversial Views
Fisher was a founding chairman of the Cambridge Eugenics Society and later the Galton Professor of Eugenics. He viewed eugenics as a scientific solution to social problems, proposing policies like differential family allowances based on income. He formally dissociated from the Eugenics Society in 1941 over its politicization. Fisher publicly disputed the link between smoking and lung cancer, calling it "propaganda." He opposed the 1950 UNESCO Statement on Race, arguing that human groups differ profoundly in their innate capacity for intellectual and emotional development. His association with eugenics has led several institutions to remove commemorations of him.

## Terms
- **Analysis of Variance (ANOVA):** A statistical method Fisher developed to analyze differences among group means in a sample.
- **Maximum Likelihood Estimation:** A method of estimating the parameters of a statistical model by maximizing a likelihood function, so that under the assumed statistical model the observed data is most probable.
- **p-value:** The probability of obtaining test results at least as extreme as the results actually observed, under the assumption that the null hypothesis is correct. Fisher proposed 0.05 as a significance threshold.
- **Null Hypothesis:** A default position that there is no relationship between two measured phenomena, or no association among groups, which is tested against an alternative hypothesis.
- **Modern Synthesis:** The early 20th-century revision of evolutionary theory that combined Darwinian natural selection with Mendelian genetics.
- **Population Genetics:** The study of genetic composition of populations and how it changes over time, founded by Fisher, Haldane, and Wright.
- **Fisher's Principle:** The theory that natural selection acts to equalize the sex ratio at 1:1 in populations.
- **Fisherian Runaway:** A hypothesis explaining how a preference for a trait in one sex and the trait itself in the other sex can lead to an exaggerated, runaway evolution of that trait.
- **Fisher Information:** A measure of the amount of information that an observable random variable carries about an unknown parameter of a distribution.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Interpretation of Fisher's Views on Race:** Historians debate whether Fisher's opposition to the UNESCO statement and his eugenicist beliefs constitute scientific racism. Some, like historian Daniel Kevles, describe him as an "anti-racist conservative," while others, like Richard J. Evans, argue his views were indicative of racism.
- **Flaws in Fisher's Analysis of Mendel's Data:** Fisher's 1936 statistical analysis suggested Gregor Mendel's data was "too perfect," implying possible data adjustment. Later authors have contested Fisher's analysis, proposing alternative statistical or botanical explanations for Mendel's results.
- **Legacy and Institutional Commemoration:** There is ongoing debate about how to reconcile Fisher's monumental scientific contributions with his advocacy for eugenics, leading institutions like Gonville and Caius College and University College London to remove his name from buildings and awards.

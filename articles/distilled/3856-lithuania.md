# Lithuania

## Overview
Lithuania is a Baltic state in Northern Europe with a rich history as a medieval power, a long period under foreign rule, and a modern identity as a democratic, high-income EU member. Its story is defined by the rise and fall of the Grand Duchy, centuries of resistance to Russification, and a successful 20th-century struggle for independence, making it a key case study in national resilience and post-Soviet transition.

## Timeline
- **1253** — Mindaugas crowned King, founding the Kingdom of Lithuania.
- **1386** — Union of Krewo creates a personal union with Poland.
- **1569** — Union of Lublin forms the Polish-Lithuanian Commonwealth.
- **1772-1795** — Partitions of the Commonwealth; Lithuania annexed by the Russian Empire.
- **16 February 1918** — Act of Independence of Lithuania proclaimed.
- **1940** — Soviet occupation begins following a Soviet ultimatum.
- **1944** — Soviet re-occupation after Nazi Germany's defeat.
- **11 March 1990** — Lithuania declares the restoration of its independence, the first Soviet republic to do so.
- **1 May 2004** — Lithuania joins the European Union.
- **1 January 2015** — Lithuania adopts the euro, joining the Eurozone.

## Body

### Early History and Statehood
The territory was settled after the Last Glacial Period, with Baltic tribes forming by the 2nd millennium BC. In the 13th century, Mindaugas united these tribes, establishing the Kingdom of Lithuania in 1253. After his assassination, pagan Lithuania resisted the Teutonic Order's crusades. The Gediminids dynasty later consolidated a hereditary monarchy, expanded into East Slavic territories, and made Vilnius the capital. By the 14th century, the Grand Duchy of Lithuania was Europe's largest state.

### The Commonwealth and Partitions
A dynastic union with Poland in 1385 (Union of Krewo) began a long integration. The Union of Lublin in 1569 formally created the Polish-Lithuanian Commonwealth, a major European power. Internal strife and external pressures led to three partitions (1772-1795) by Russia, Prussia, and Austria, erasing Lithuania from the map. Most territory was annexed by the Russian Empire, which implemented Russification policies, including a ban on Lithuanian-language publications.

### Independence, Occupation, and Restoration
Following World War I, Lithuania declared independence in 1918. It was occupied by the Soviet Union and then Nazi Germany during World War II, before being re-occupied by the Soviets in 1944. Armed resistance to Soviet rule lasted until the early 1950s. The Sąjūdis reform movement in the late 1980s led to the declaration of restored independence on 11 March 1990. Despite a Soviet economic blockade and violent crackdowns in 1991, Lithuania maintained its sovereignty, with the last Russian troops leaving in 1993.

### Modern Lithuania
Since independence, Lithuania has consolidated a semi-presidential democracy and a high-income economy. It rapidly integrated into Western institutions, joining NATO and the EU in 2004, the Schengen Area in 2007, and the Eurozone in 2015. The economy, once termed a "Baltic Tiger," faced a severe contraction in 2009 but has since recovered. Lithuania is a leader in digital infrastructure and laser technology, and it has been a vocal supporter of Ukraine, particularly following the 2022 Russian invasion.

## Terms
- **Grand Duchy of Lithuania:** A vast medieval state that, at its peak in the 14th century, was the largest country in Europe.
- **Polish-Lithuanian Commonwealth:** A dual state formed in 1569 by the Union of Lublin, uniting the Crown of the Kingdom of Poland and the Grand Duchy of Lithuania.
- **Russification:** Policies implemented by the Russian Empire to suppress Lithuanian culture and language, notably including a press ban (1864-1904).
- **Sąjūdis:** The Reform Movement of Lithuania, a political organization that led the struggle for independence from the Soviet Union in the late 1980s.
- **Baltic Way:** A peaceful political demonstration on 23 August 1989, where approximately two million people formed a human chain across the three Baltic states.
- **Semipresidential system:** A form of government where a president coexists with a prime minister and cabinet, answerable to the parliament (Seimas).
- **Seimas:** The unicameral parliament of Lithuania, with 141 members elected to four-year terms.
- **Eurozone:** The group of EU member states that have adopted the euro as their primary currency.

## Debates and Open Questions
The etymology of the name "Lithuania" (Lietuva) is debated. One theory links it to the Lietava stream near Kernavė, while another, proposed by Artūras Dubonis, connects it to the word *leičiai*, a distinct warrior social group in medieval Lithuanian society.

# Prince Henry the Navigator

## Overview
Prince Henry the Navigator (1394–1460) was a Portuguese prince who, despite never sailing on voyages himself, was the central patron and organizer of early Portuguese exploration. He directed the development of the caravel ship and sponsored systematic expeditions along the West African coast and into the Atlantic, initiating the Age of Discovery and establishing the foundations of the Portuguese maritime empire.

## Timeline
- **1394** — Born as third son of King John I of Portugal.
- **1415** — Participates in the conquest of Ceuta, North Africa; knighted and made Duke of Viseu.
- **1418** — Leads a relief expedition to save Ceuta from siege.
- **1420** — Appointed administrator of the Order of Christ, a key source of funding.
- **1434** — Gil Eanes, under Henry's sponsorship, becomes the first European to pass Cape Bojador.
- **1441** — Nuno Tristão and Antão Gonçalves reach Cape Blanco.
- **1444** — Dinis Dias reaches the Senegal River and Cap-Vert peninsula.
- **1455-56** — Alvise Cadamosto explores the coast and discovers the Cape Verde Islands.
- **1458** — Commands the Algarve detachment in the expedition to Alcácer-Ceguer.
- **1460** — Dies in Vila do Bispo, Algarve.

## Body

### Early Life and the Conquest of Ceuta
Henry was the third son of King John I. His early life was marked by the 1415 conquest of the Muslim port of Ceuta, where he played a prominent military role. This victory brought him European renown and, crucially, provided intelligence from Muslim prisoners about trans-Saharan trade routes to gold and the Gambia River, inspiring future maritime exploration.

### Resources and Patronage
Henry secured lasting resources for exploration. In 1420, he became administrator of the Order of Christ, a wealthy religious order. After his father's death in 1433, his brother King Edward granted Henry all profits from trade in areas he discovered and the sole right to authorize expeditions beyond Cape Bojador. He also held a monopoly on tuna fishing in the Algarve. These funds supported his ambitious plans.

### Exploration and the Caravel
Henry's primary objective was to find the source of West African gold and the legendary Christian kingdom of Prester John. To do this, he sponsored the development of the caravel, a lighter, faster ship with lateen sails that could sail into the wind. This technology allowed Portuguese mariners to explore uncharted Atlantic waters. His expeditions, often just one or two ships, gradually pushed south, passing Cape Bojador in 1434 and eventually reaching the Senegal River and Cap-Vert by 1444, circumventing Muslim land trade routes and bringing wealth to Portugal.

### The "School of Sagres" and Later Life
A popular legend holds that Henry founded a "school" of navigation at his villa in Sagres. Modern historians consider this a misconception; while he employed cartographers, there was no formal center of navigation science. In his later years, he participated in the 1458 expedition to Alcácer-Ceguer. He died in 1460, having laid the groundwork for future explorers like Bartolomeu Dias and Vasco da Gama.

## Terms
- **Caravel:** A small, highly maneuverable sailing ship developed under Henry's direction, using lateen sails to sail effectively against the wind.
- **Lateen Sail:** A triangular sail rig common in the Mediterranean, allowing ships to sail closer to the wind than square-rigged vessels.
- **Order of Christ:** A Portuguese religious order, successor to the Knights Templar, which Henry administered and used to fund exploration.
- **Cape Bojador:** A headland on the West African coast long feared by Europeans as a limit to navigation; passed by Gil Eanes in 1434.
- **Volta do mar:** A Portuguese navigational technique involving sailing wide arcs in the Atlantic to catch favorable prevailing winds for the return voyage.
- **Feitoria:** A trading post or factory established by the Portuguese along the African coast for commerce.
- **Quinto:** A 20% tax on profits from trade, collected by the crown or its designees like Henry.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **The "School of Sagres":** The existence of a formal, organized center for navigation science at Sagres during Henry's lifetime is widely discredited by modern historians, though the myth was popularized in the 18th and 19th centuries.
- **Origin of the "Navigator" Nickname:** The epithet "the Navigator" was not used in Henry's lifetime or for centuries after. It was coined by 19th-century German historians and popularized by British biographers.
- **Motivations for Exploration:** While seeking gold and Prester John are cited, the exact balance of religious, commercial, and strategic motives (like countering piracy) remains a subject of scholarly analysis.

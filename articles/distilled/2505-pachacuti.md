# Pachacuti

## Overview
Pachacuti Inca Yupanqui (c. 1400–1471) was the ninth Sapa Inca who transformed the small Chiefdom of Cusco into the expansive Inca Empire (Tawantinsuyu). His reign marked the beginning of systematic imperial conquest, and he is credited with major administrative, religious, and architectural reforms. His historicity is debated, with some scholars viewing him as a historical emperor and others as a mythological archetype for the start of Inca expansion.

## Body

### Rise to Power and the Chanka War
Pachacuti, originally named Cusi Yupanqui, was the son of the eighth ruler, Inca Viracocha. In the early 15th century, the Cusco confederation faced invasion by the Chankas. While Viracocha and his heir Urco fled, Cusi Yupanqui rallied the army and won a decisive victory at Yahuar Pampa. This triumph led to a conflict with his father and brother, after which Cusi Yupanqui was crowned Sapa Inca and took the name Pachacuti ("Earth Shaker" or "turn of the world"). The historicity of this war is debated; some scholars consider it largely mythical, while others see historical elements.

### Imperial Expansion
Pachacuti initiated a period of rapid conquest. He first subdued neighboring groups around Cusco. His armies then conquered the Colla and Lupaca chiefdoms in the Altiplano near Lake Titicaca, reaching possibly as far as Lake Poopó. He sent generals north into Chinchaysuyu, conquering groups like the Huaylas and reaching the Cajamarca chiefdom. His son and successor, Topa Inca Yupanqui, later conquered the powerful Chimú Empire on the coast. Pachacuti used the principle of reciprocity to integrate local lords (kurakas) and employed forced migrations (mitimaes) to stabilize the empire.

### Administrative and Religious Reforms
As ruler, Pachacuti reorganized Cusco into a model imperial city, with sectors representing the four quarters (suyus) of the empire. He rebuilt the Coricancha, the main sun temple, and is linked to the expansion of the cult of Inti (the Sun God), possibly replacing the earlier cult of Viracocha. He established the Inti Raymi festival to celebrate the new year. He also ordered the creation of painted panels and quipu records to codify a state-sanctioned history of previous rulers.

### Succession and Death
Pachacuti first named his son Amaru Yupanqui as co-ruler, but later replaced him with his militarily talented son, Tupac Inca Yupanqui. Pachacuti died in 1471, possibly after abdicating. His death led to a succession crisis that was resolved in favor of Tupac Inca. His mummy was transported to the palace of Patallacta.

## Terms
- **Sapa Inca**: The sole ruler and "emperor" of the Inca Empire.
- **Tawantinsuyu**: The Quechua name for the Inca Empire, meaning "the four regions together."
- **Panaka**: The royal descent group or lineage of a Sapa Inca, which held his mummy and property.
- **Kuraka**: A local chief or lord of an ethnic group within the empire.
- **Mitimaes**: Colonists forcibly relocated by the Inca state to stabilize or control provinces.
- **Quipu**: A recording device made of knotted cords used for keeping records and narratives.
- **Inti**: The Sun God, the principal deity of the Inca state religion.
- **Reciprocity**: A core Andean socio-economic principle of mutual obligation and gift exchange, used politically by the Inca.
- **Hanan/Hurin Cusco**: The "upper" and "lower" moieties (divisions) of Cusco society and city planning.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Historicity**: Scholars debate whether Pachacuti was a historical emperor or a mythological/cosmological figure representing the start of imperial expansion. The Chanka War, his rise to power, is considered by many to be largely legendary.
- **Chronology**: His exact reign dates are uncertain. Rowe's standard chronology (1438–1471) is based on colonial sources, but radiocarbon data suggests an earlier start in the early 15th century.
- **Succession and Co-rule**: The nature of Inca succession is debated. Some historians, like Zuidema, argue for a diarchic system where Pachacuti co-ruled with other figures. The sequence and nature of co-rule with his sons Amaru and Tupac are also contested.
- **Religious Shift**: It is debated whether Pachacuti replaced the cult of Viracocha with that of Inti, or if the association with Viracocha was a later colonial invention.

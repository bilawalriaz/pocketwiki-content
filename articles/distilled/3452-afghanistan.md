# Afghanistan

## Overview
Afghanistan is a landlocked country at the crossroads of Central and South Asia, historically known as the "graveyard of empires" due to its strategic location and rugged terrain. It has been a site of continuous conflict and cultural exchange for millennia, shaped by successive invasions and the rise and fall of empires. The modern state emerged in the 18th century, but since the late 1970s, it has been dominated by warfare, culminating in the Taliban's return to power in 2021.

## Body

### Etymology and Early History
The name "Afghanistan" means "land of the Afghans" (Pashtuns), with the root "Afghan" possibly derived from the Sanskrit *Aśvakan* ("horsemen"). Human habitation dates to the Paleolithic era, with urban centers like Mundigak emerging by c. 3000 BCE. The region was a crossroads for Indo-European migrations and was incorporated into the Achaemenid Persian Empire by the 6th century BCE. It was later conquered by Alexander the Great (330 BCE), the Maurya Empire, and the Greco-Bactrians, becoming a hub for Zoroastrianism, Buddhism, and Hinduism. The Silk Road facilitated trade and cultural exchange.

### Medieval Period and Islamic Conquest
Islam arrived with Arab Muslims in 642 CE, gradually spreading and coexisting with older religions in a syncretic culture. The Ghaznavid dynasty (10th century) and later the Ghurids solidified Islamic rule. The Mongol invasion under Genghis Khan in 1219 CE caused massive destruction. The region later saw rule by the Timurids and the Mughals. The Hotak dynasty (1709) and the Durrani Empire (1747) established the first modern Afghan states.

### The "Great Game" and Independence
In the 19th century, Afghanistan became a buffer state between the British and Russian Empires. After the First Anglo-Afghan War (1839-1842), the British were repelled. The Second Anglo-Afghan War (1878-1880) resulted in British control of Afghan foreign policy. Full independence was achieved after the Third Anglo-Afghan War in 1919. King Amanullah Khan (r. 1926-1929) attempted modernization but faced a civil war and abdication. His successor, Nadir Shah, and later Zahir Shah (r. 1933-1973), pursued gradual modernization while maintaining neutrality in World War II and the Cold War.

### Cold War and Soviet Invasion
In 1973, Daoud Khan overthrew Zahir Shah, ending the monarchy. In 1978, a communist coup (the Saur Revolution) led to the Democratic Republic of Afghanistan. The PDPA's reforms sparked a mujahideen insurgency. In 1979, the Soviet Union invaded to support the communist government, starting a nine-year war that caused up to 2 million Afghan deaths and displaced 6 million. The mujahideen, supported by Pakistan, the US, and others, eventually forced a Soviet withdrawal in 1989.

### Civil War and the First Taliban Era
After the Soviet withdrawal, a civil war erupted among mujahideen factions, destroying Kabul. The Taliban, a movement of Pakistani madrassa students, emerged in 1994, capturing Kabul in 1996 and imposing a harsh interpretation of Sharia law. Their regime was overthrown by a US-led coalition in 2001 after they harbored al-Qaeda.

### The Islamic Republic and the Second Taliban Era
A new government, the Islamic Republic of Afghanistan, was established under Hamid Karzai. Despite international aid and democratic elections, corruption and a persistent Taliban insurgency plagued the country. In 2021, following a US-Taliban deal and NATO withdrawal, the Taliban rapidly recaptured Kabul. The current Islamic Emirate government, led by Hibatullah Akhundzada, is internationally unrecognized and has severely restricted women's rights and education, leading to a humanitarian crisis.

## Terms
- **Loya Jirga**: A traditional Pashtun grand assembly convened for major national decisions.
- **Mujahideen**: Islamic guerrilla fighters who resisted the Soviet invasion and later fought other factions.
- **Durand Line**: The 1893 border agreement between British India and Afghanistan, now the Pakistan-Afghanistan border.
- **Sharia**: Islamic law based on the Quran and Hadith, interpreted and enforced by the Taliban.
- **Pashtunwali**: The traditional Pashtun social code emphasizing hospitality, honor, and revenge.
- **Dari**: A variety of Persian and one of Afghanistan's two official languages, serving as the lingua franca.
- **Buzkashi**: Afghanistan's national sport, a horseback game where players compete to grab a goat carcass.
- **Kabuli Palaw**: The national dish of Afghanistan, a rice pilaf with meat, carrots, and raisins.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The exact origin and meaning of the name "Afghan" and "Afghanistan" remain debated among scholars.
- The founder of the first modern Afghan state is disputed: some credit Ahmad Shah Durrani (1747), while others point to Dost Mohammad Khan.
- The international community's policy toward the unrecognized Taliban government, particularly regarding women's rights and aid, is a major ongoing dilemma.
- The long-term viability of the Taliban's ban on opium cultivation, which reduced production by over 95% by 2023, and its economic and social consequences are uncertain.

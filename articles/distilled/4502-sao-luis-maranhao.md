# São Luís, Maranhão

## Overview
São Luís is the capital and largest city of the Brazilian state of Maranhão, located on São Luís Island in the Baía de São Marcos. It is historically significant as the only Brazilian state capital founded by France and is renowned for its well-preserved 17th-century colonial center, a UNESCO World Heritage Site. The city's economy is driven by major sea ports exporting iron ore and other commodities, and it is a cultural hub known for its unique architecture, reggae music, and traditional festivals.

## Timeline
- **1612** — French settlers found Saint-Louis-de-Maragnan, the colony of France Équinoxiale.
- **1615** — Portugal conquers the settlement in the Battle of Guaxenduba and renames it São Luís.
- **1641-1644** — The Dutch invade and occupy the city for 27 months.
- **1677** — São Luís becomes the seat of the Roman Catholic Diocese of São Luís do Maranhão.
- **c. 18th century** — The Beckman's Revolt occurs, an uprising against Portuguese authorities.
- **Late 18th/19th century** — A cotton boom, driven by demand from the English textile industry, brings wealth and modernization.
- **1997** — The Historic Centre of São Luís is declared a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
- **2018** — The Reggae Museum of Maranhão is founded in the city.

## Body

### History and Founding
São Luís was originally a Tupinambá village. The French established a settlement in 1612, naming it after King Louis XIII. The Portuguese conquered it in 1615. Due to the rapid conquest, the exact founding date is debated. The city was later occupied by the Dutch from 1641 to 1644. After invasions ceased, it became the capital of the state of Grão-Pará e Maranhão. The economy was initially based on agriculture (sugar, cacao, tobacco). A major uprising, the Beckman's Revolt, occurred due to conflicts between Jesuits and landowners over Indian slavery and Portuguese authority. A cotton boom in the 19th century, supplying Britain during the American Civil War, brought significant wealth and modernization, making São Luís the third most populous city in Brazil at the time.

### Economy and Ports
The city's economy is centered on its two major sea ports, Madeira Port and Itaqui Port, which are among the busiest in Brazil. They export iron ore from the Amazon region via the Carajás Railway, as well as soybeans. Key industries include aluminum processing (Alumar) and pelleting (Vale). The city has the largest GDP in the state. The nearby Alcântara Space Center is also a significant facility.

### Geography and Climate
São Luís is located on the island of Upaon-Açu, between the estuaries of the Baía de São Marcos and Baía de São José. The climate is a hot, semi-humid tropical savanna (Köppen: As), with a distinct wet season (January to June) and a short dry season (August to November). The city is connected to the mainland by road and rail bridges across the Strait of Mosquitoes.

### Culture and Heritage
The city is known as "The Tiles City" for the azulejos (ceramic tiles) covering many historic buildings. Its Historic Centre, a UNESCO site, is noted for its grid plan and large collection of Portuguese colonial sobrados (townhouses). Cultural traditions include Tambor de Crioula (a women's drum dance), Tambor de Mina (an Afro-Brazilian religion), and Bumba Meu Boi (a harvest festival pantomime). São Luís is also called the "Brazilian capital of reggae."

## Terms
- **Sobrado**: A Portuguese colonial-style urban town house, typically two or more stories.
- **Azulejo**: A painted tin-glazed ceramic tile, often used decoratively on building facades.
- **France Équinoxiale**: The short-lived French colony established in 1612 in what is now Maranhão.
- **Beckman's Revolt**: An 18th-century uprising in Maranhão against Portuguese colonial authorities.
- **Bumba Meu Boi**: A popular Brazilian folk festival and musical pantomime with a comedic tragedy at its core.
- **Tambor de Crioula**: An Afro-Brazilian cultural manifestation featuring women dancing to a drum rhythm.
- **Tambor de Mina**: A local variant of an Afro-Brazilian religious tradition.
- **Carajás Railway**: A major railway line that transports iron ore from mines in Pará to the ports of São Luís.

## Debates and Open Questions
The exact founding date of São Luís is a subject of controversy. The French built a fort in 1612, but the Portuguese conquered it shortly after in 1615, leaving little time for the French to establish a full city. This leads to debate over whether the official founding should be attributed to the French or the Portuguese.

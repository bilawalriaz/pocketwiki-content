# Louvre

## Overview
The Louvre is a national art museum in Paris, France, housed in the historic Louvre Palace. It is the world's largest and most-visited art museum, holding approximately 500,000 objects, with 35,000 works on display. Its collection spans from prehistory to the 21st century and is organized into eight curatorial departments, making it a central institution for the study and exhibition of Western and global art.

## Body
### Origins and Transformation
The Louvre began as a medieval fortress built by King Philip II in the late 12th century to protect Paris. By the 14th century, Charles V converted it into a royal residence. In 1546, Francis I initiated its transformation into a Renaissance palace, which continued under subsequent monarchs. The royal art collections, started by Francis I, formed the nucleus of the future museum. In 1682, when Louis XIV moved the court to Versailles, the Louvre became a residence for artists and a place to display the royal collection.

### Revolutionary and Napoleonic Eras
The Louvre became a public museum during the French Revolution. The National Assembly decreed it a museum in 1791, and it opened on 10 August 1793, displaying 537 paintings from royal and confiscated church property. Under Napoleon, the museum's collection grew dramatically through looted art from across Europe, and it was renamed the Musée Napoléon. After Napoleon's abdication in 1815, many seized works were returned, but the collections continued to expand under the restored monarchies.

### 19th and 20th Century Developments
The 19th century saw major architectural expansions, particularly under Napoleon III, who added 20,000 artifacts. The museum narrowly escaped destruction during the Paris Commune in 1871. During the Third Republic, collections grew mainly through donations and archaeological excavations. In World War II, the collection was evacuated to protect it from Nazi looting. Post-war, the museum modernized its displays and, in 1981, began the Grand Louvre project under President Mitterrand. This plan, led by architect I.M. Pei, relocated the Finance Ministry, created the iconic glass pyramid entrance (1989), and vastly expanded gallery space.

### 21st Century and Current Status
In the 21st century, the Louvre has focused on expanding its non-Western collections, creating a new Islamic Art department in 2012 and opening the Gallery of the Five Continents in 2025. The museum faced significant challenges from the COVID-19 pandemic, which caused closures and a sharp drop in visitors. In 2025, a major heist of crown jewels occurred. In response to chronic overcrowding and record visitor numbers (over 10 million in 2018), President Macron announced a €1.1 billion renovation and expansion project in 2025, set to begin in 2026, which will include a dedicated suite for the Mona Lisa.

## Terms
- **Grand Louvre**: A major renovation project launched in 1981 to relocate government offices and expand the museum's exhibition space.
- **Cour Napoléon**: The main courtyard of the Louvre Palace, where I.M. Pei's glass pyramid is located.
- **MNR (Musées Nationaux Récupération)**: A collection of artworks recovered after World War II, some of which remain unclaimed and are held by French museums, including the Louvre.
- **Cabinet du Roi**: The original royal art collection, which formed the core of the Louvre's holdings.
- **Salon**: The official art exhibition of the Académie Royale de Peinture et de Sculpture, held at the Louvre for many years.
- **Gypsothèque**: A gallery of plaster casts, managed by the Louvre and located at Versailles.
- **Biens nationaux**: National property, referring to assets confiscated from the Church and émigrés during the French Revolution.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Napoleonic and Nazi Looted Art**: The Louvre is involved in ongoing controversies and restitution claims regarding art seized during the Napoleonic Wars and by the Nazis during World War II. The museum holds 678 unclaimed MNR artworks, and debates continue about their ultimate fate.
- **Cultural Property Repatriation**: The museum faces requests for the return of artifacts, such as the Dendera zodiac from Egypt, which it acquired before export laws were enacted. It participates in UNESCO arbitration sessions on such matters.
- **Labor Practices for Louvre Abu Dhabi**: The construction of the Louvre Abu Dhabi, a separate museum using the Louvre brand, sparked international controversy over alleged poor labor conditions for construction workers on Saadiyat Island.

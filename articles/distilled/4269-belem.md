# Belém

## Overview
Belém is the capital and largest city of the Brazilian state of Pará, serving as the primary gateway to the Amazon River. Founded by the Portuguese in 1616, it was the first European colony on the Amazon and remains a major economic, cultural, and transportation hub for northern Brazil, blending colonial history with modern development.

## Timeline
- **1616** — Founded by Portuguese captain Francisco Caldeira Castelo Branco with the construction of the Forte do Castelo.
- **1655** — Granted city status.
- **1772** — Became the capital of the newly created state of Pará.
- **1866** — Amazon, Tocantins, and Tapajós rivers opened to navigation, boosting its port.
- **1910–12** — Peak of the Amazon rubber boom, after which the city's economic importance declined.
- **1993** — Utinga State Park created to protect the metropolitan water supply.
- **2009** — Hosted the World Social Forum.
- **2015** — Joined the UNESCO Creative Cities Network for gastronomy.

## Body

### History and Foundation
Belém was established in 1616 as a Portuguese military outpost to counter foreign trading expeditions on the Amazon. The initial fort, the "Presépio," became the core of the colony, originally named Feliz Lusitânia. The city's economic importance has fluctuated, first with sugar and cattle, then with the 19th-century rubber boom, which brought significant wealth and modernization. It has remained the principal commercial center for the Amazon valley.

### Geography and Climate
Located about 100 km upriver from the Atlantic on the Pará River, Belém is separated from the main Amazon delta by Marajó Island. It has a tropical rainforest climate (Köppen: Af) with no true dry season, though rainfall is heavier from December to May. The surrounding Amazon rainforest is the world's largest and most biodiverse tropical forest.

### Economy and Infrastructure
The city's economy is based on exporting Amazonian products like aluminium, iron ore, nuts, and hardwoods. It is a major transportation hub with an international airport, a busy port, and highways like BR-316 and BR-010. The Tucuruí Dam provides electricity. The metropolitan area is home to over 2.4 million people.

### Culture and Demographics
Belém's population is predominantly multiracial (Pardo). Its culture is a blend of indigenous, Portuguese, and other influences, most famously celebrated in the Círio de Nazaré, Brazil's largest religious procession, held annually in October. The city is renowned for its Amazonian cuisine, featuring ingredients like cupuaçu and açaí. Colonial architecture, featuring tile-hung façades, coexists with modern skyscrapers.

### Tourism and Landmarks
Key attractions include the historic Ver-o-Peso market (est. 1688), the neoclassical Theatro da Paz, the Museu Paraense Emílio Goeldi, and the Utinga State Park. The Estação das Docas complex repurposes old port warehouses, and the Rodrigues Alves Wood offers a botanical garden in the city center.

## Terms
- **Círio de Nazaré:** The largest religious festival in Brazil, a multi-day procession in Belém honoring the Virgin of Nazareth.
- **Grão-Pará:** The historical name for the captaincy and later the state that included Belém.
- **Marajó Island:** The world's largest fluvial island, located in the Amazon delta near Belém.
- **Presépio / Forte do Castelo:** The original 1616 Portuguese fort that became the nucleus of Belém.
- **Ver-o-Peso:** A historic public market in Belém, established in 1688, known for its vibrant trade in local goods.
- **Pardo:** A Brazilian census term for multiracial people, the largest demographic group in Belém.
- **Tucuruí Dam:** A major hydroelectric dam on the Tocantins River that supplies electricity to the region.

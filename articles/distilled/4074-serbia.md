# Serbia

## Overview
Serbia is a landlocked country in Southeastern and Central Europe, situated in the Balkans and the Pannonian Plain. Its history is marked by medieval kingdoms, centuries of Ottoman and Habsburg rule, a modern revolution for independence, and a complex 20th century within Yugoslavia. Today, it is a parliamentary republic navigating post-Yugoslav transitions, including the status of Kosovo, while pursuing European Union membership.

## Timeline
- **1217** — Serbian Kingdom obtains recognition from the Holy See and Constantinople.
- **1346** — Serbian Empire is proclaimed, reaching its territorial apex.
- **1459** — Fall of Smederevo marks the end of the Serbian state and full Ottoman conquest.
- **1804–1815** — Serbian Revolution establishes the region's first constitutional monarchy.
- **1878** — Formal independence is internationally recognized at the Congress of Berlin.
- **1918** — Kingdom of Serbia unites with Vojvodina and other South Slavs to form Yugoslavia.
- **1941–1945** — Axis invasion and occupation; civil war between Chetniks and Partisans.
- **1991–2001** — Yugoslav Wars; Serbia and Montenegro form the Federal Republic of Yugoslavia.
- **2006** — Montenegro declares independence; Serbia becomes a sovereign state again.
- **2008** — Kosovo unilaterally declares independence; Serbia rejects it.
- **2014** — Serbia begins EU accession negotiations.

## Body

### History
The territory has been continuously inhabited since the Paleolithic. Slavic migrations in the 6th century led to the formation of regional states. The medieval Serbian state, under the Nemanjić dynasty, became a kingdom in 1217 and an empire in 1346. Following its fragmentation and the Battle of Kosovo (1389), the Ottoman Empire gradually conquered all Serbian lands by 1459. Ottoman rule eliminated native nobility and enserfed the peasantry, though the Serbian Orthodox Church was reestablished in 1557. Resistance continued, and the 1804–1815 Serbian Revolution resulted in a semi-independent principality, with full independence recognized in 1878.

In 1918, Serbia united with other South Slavs to form the Kingdom of Serbs, Croats, and Slovenes, later renamed Yugoslavia. After World War II, Serbia became a constituent republic within communist Yugoslavia. The breakup of Yugoslavia in the 1990s led to the Yugoslav Wars. Serbia and Montenegro formed a new federal republic, which dissolved peacefully in 2006. In 2008, Kosovo declared independence, a move Serbia does not recognize. Serbia is currently an EU candidate country, with accession negotiations ongoing since 2014.

### Government and Politics
Serbia is a unitary parliamentary constitutional republic. The President is the head of state, and the Government, led by a Prime Minister, is responsible for policy. The unicameral National Assembly has 250 members. The country has two autonomous provinces: Vojvodina and Kosovo and Metohija (the latter under UN administration since 1999). Serbia formally adheres to military neutrality, a policy contingent on a popular referendum for any change. It is a member of the UN, Council of Europe, and OSCE, and is negotiating EU accession.

### Economy
Serbia has an upper-middle income, emerging market economy. Services dominate (67.9% of GDP), followed by industry and agriculture. Key sectors include automotive, mining (notably copper and coal), food processing, and a growing ICT sector. The country has attracted significant foreign direct investment. Unemployment remains a challenge. Serbia has free trade agreements with EFTA and CEFTA and a preferential regime with the EU.

### Demographics
According to the 2022 census, Serbia (excluding Kosovo) has a population of 6.65 million. The Serbs are the largest ethnic group (80.6%), followed by Hungarians (2.8%) and Bosniaks (2.3%). The official language is Serbian, which uses both Cyrillic and Latin scripts. Eastern Orthodoxy is the predominant religion (81.1%). The country faces a demographic crisis with low fertility, population aging, and net emigration.

### Culture
Serbian culture reflects its position between Byzantine, Central European, and Ottoman influences. It has five UNESCO World Heritage sites, including medieval monasteries. Intangible heritage includes the *slava* (family patron saint day) and *kolo* folk dance. Literature has a strong tradition, from medieval oral epics to Nobel laureate Ivo Andrić. The country is known for its film directors, such as Emir Kusturica, and has a vibrant music scene ranging from traditional folk and brass bands to turbo-folk and rock. Sports, particularly basketball, water polo, and tennis (home to Novak Djokovic), are a major part of national identity.

## Terms
- **Slava**: The celebration of a family's patron saint day, a unique Serbian Orthodox tradition.
- **Kolo**: A traditional collective folk dance performed in a circle.
- **Gusle**: A single-stringed bowed instrument used to accompany epic poetry.
- **Rakija (Rakia)**: A fruit brandy; *šljivovica* (plum brandy) is the national variety.
- **Millet System**: The Ottoman system of governing religious communities autonomously.
- **Autocephalous**: Independent in governance; used to describe the Serbian Orthodox Church.
- **Devshirme**: The Ottoman practice of recruiting Christian boys for conversion and service in the Janissary corps.
- **Turbo-folk**: A controversial popular music genre blending folk with pop and electronic elements.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Kosovo Status**: The 2008 declaration of independence by Kosovo is not recognized by Serbia, which considers it a violation of international law. The issue remains unresolved, with EU-mediated talks ongoing.
- **EU Accession**: While a strategic goal, the timeline and conditions for Serbia's EU membership are subjects of ongoing negotiation and domestic debate.
- **Democratic Backsliding**: International analysts have noted concerns about democratic backsliding, media freedom, and the concentration of power since 2012.
- **Lithium Mining**: A proposed major lithium mine project has sparked significant public debate and protests over environmental concerns.

# Krishnadevaraya

## Overview
Krishnadevaraya (r. 1509–1529) was the third ruler of the Tuluva dynasty and emperor of the Vijayanagara Empire. He is widely regarded as one of India's greatest monarchs, presiding over the empire's political, military, and cultural zenith. His reign marked a period of significant expansion, administrative efficiency, and a flourishing of arts, particularly Telugu literature.

## Timeline
- **1471** — Krishnadevaraya is born.
- **1509** — Ascends the throne after the death of his half-brother Viranarasimha.
- **1509** — Defeats the Deccan sultans, annexes the Raichur Doab, and reunites parts of the Bahmani Sultanate under a puppet ruler.
- **1512** — Begins the siege of the Gajapati fort of Udayagiri.
- **1514** — Captures the Gajapati fort of Kondaveedu after a night attack.
- **1510s** — Establishes friendly relations with the Portuguese in Goa, obtaining guns and horses.
- **1520** — Secures Raichur Fort from Bijapur after a major siege.
- **1524** — Makes his son Tirumala Raya crown prince; the prince is soon poisoned.
- **1529** — Dies on 17 October, succeeded by his brother Achyuta Deva Raya.

## Body

### Military Expansion and Dominance
Krishnadevaraya's reign was defined by military campaigns that made him the dominant ruler of the Indian peninsula. He defeated the sultanates of Bijapur, Golconda, and the Bahmani Sultanate, as well as the Gajapatis of Odisha. Key victories included the annexation of the Raichur Doab in 1512 and the subjugation of Odisha by 1514, culminating in the capture of the Gajapati king's son and a marriage alliance. His most celebrated victory was the capture of Raichur Fort in 1520 from Bijapur, a battle involving a massive army and a Portuguese contingent. He was noted for his tactical flexibility and personally led campaigns. The Mughal emperor Babur later considered him the most powerful ruler in India.

### Administration and Governance
Krishnadevaraya maintained strict control over his ministers and implemented reforms to promote welfare. He abolished unpopular taxes like the marriage fee and invested in irrigation by ordering the construction of tanks and canals to bring new land under cultivation. Foreign travelers like Domingo Paes and Duarte Barbosa praised the efficiency of his administration and the prosperity of his people. He conducted extensive annual tours to personally address grievances and ensure justice, guided by the principle that a king must rule with an eye towards Dharma (righteous duty).

### Patronage of Arts and Literature
His reign is celebrated as a golden age of Telugu literature. Krishnadevaraya was a polyglot and a poet himself, composing the epic Telugu work *Amuktamalyada*. His court hosted the Ashtadiggajas, eight legendary Telugu poets including Allasani Peddana and Nandi Thimmana. He also patronized literature in Kannada, Sanskrit, and Tamil, making his court a major cultural hub. Notable figures like the musician Purandara Dasa and the philosopher Vyasatirtha were associated with his empire.

### Religion and Cultural Policy
Krishnadevaraya was a devout Hindu who respected all sects. He rebuilt temples, including the Virupaksha Temple, and made lavish grants to major shrines like Tirumala Venkateswara, which he visited seven times. His personal religious affiliation is debated; while Sri Vaishnavas claim he was initiated into their tradition, Madhva texts identify the scholar Vyasatirtha as his royal guru. He is honored with titles like "Protector of Cows and Brahmins."

## Terms
- **Vijayanagara Empire:** A powerful South Indian empire that lasted from 1336 to 1646.
- **Tuluva dynasty:** The third dynasty to rule the Vijayanagara Empire, founded by Krishnadevaraya's father.
- **Deccan Sultanates:** A collection of Muslim kingdoms in the Deccan region, including Bijapur and Golconda.
- **Gajapatis:** A Hindu dynasty that ruled Odisha and parts of Andhra and Bengal.
- **Ashtadiggajas:** The eight legendary poets in Krishnadevaraya's Telugu literary court.
- **Amuktamalyada:** An epic Telugu poetic work composed by Krishnadevaraya.
- **Dharma:** A key concept in Indian religions meaning cosmic law, duty, and righteousness.
- **Prabandha:** A genre of Telugu literature that flourished during his reign.

## Debates and Open Questions
The primary scholarly debate concerns Krishnadevaraya's religious affiliation. While his patronage of the Tirumala temple and the content of his *Amuktamalyada* suggest strong Sri Vaishnava leanings, historical texts from the Madhva tradition assert that the scholar Vyasatirtha was his official royal guru. This discrepancy highlights the complex interplay of personal devotion, political strategy, and sectarian patronage in his court.

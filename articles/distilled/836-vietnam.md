# Vietnam

## Overview

Vietnam is a Southeast Asian nation of over 102 million people, bordered by China, Laos, and Cambodia. Historically shaped by a millennium of Chinese rule (111 BC–939 AD), southward expansion (*Nam tiến*), and French colonization (1887–1954), it endured prolonged 20th-century warfare: the First Indochina War against France (1946–1954) and the Vietnam War against the US-backed South (1955–1975). Reunified as a communist state in 1976, Vietnam stagnated under a planned economy and trade embargoes until the 1986 *Đổi Mới* (Renovation) reforms introduced a socialist-oriented market economy. This shift spurred rapid growth, integration into global institutions (WTO, ASEAN), and poverty reduction, though the Communist Party of Vietnam (CPV) maintains strict political control, censorship, and a poor human rights record.

## Body

### Etymology and Early Identity
The name *Việt Nam* (Viet South) appears in a 16th-century poem and on steles from 1558. In 1802, Emperor Gia Long requested the title "King of Nam Việt" from the Qing; the Jiaqing Emperor refused due to the name's association with Zhao Tuo's ancient Nanyue (covering Guangdong/Guangxi) and instead granted "Việt Nam," interpreted classically as "land south of the Viet." Vietnamese understood it as "Viet people's Southern country." The term *Việt* (Yue) originally denoted peoples northwest of the Shang dynasty (c. 1200 BC), later applying to the State of Yue and southern non-Chinese groups (*Bách Việt*, Hundred Yue). The country was internationally known as Annam until 1945.

### Prehistory and Early States
Humans inhabited the region by the Paleolithic; *Homo erectus* fossils date to ~500,000 BC. By 1000 BC, wet-rice cultivation in the Ma and Red River deltas birthed the Đông Sơn culture, famed for bronze drums. Legend holds the Hồng Bàng dynasty (2879 BC) as the first state (Văn Lang), formed by Lạc Việt tribes. In 257 BC, Thục Phán merged Lạc Việt and Âu Việt into Âu Lạc, conquered in 179 BC by Zhao Tuo (Triệu Đà), who founded Nanyue. The Han dynasty annexed Nanyue in 111 BC, imposing Confucianism. Northern Vietnam remained under Chinese rule for a millennium, punctuated by revolts (Trưng Sisters, Lady Triệu) and a brief independent Vạn Xuân (544–602).

### Dynastic Independence and Expansion
In 938, Ngô Quyền defeated the Southern Han at Bạch Đằng River, securing independence in 939. The Đại Việt kingdom emerged by the 960s. The Lý and Trần dynasties (11th–14th c.) marked a golden age: repelling three Mongol invasions and establishing Mahāyāna Buddhism as state religion. After a brief Ming occupation (1407–1427), Lê Lợi restored independence, founding the Lê dynasty. The 15th century, especially under Lê Thánh Tông (1460–1497), was the polity's zenith. From the 11th to 18th centuries, *Nam tiến* gradually absorbed Champa and Khmer lands in the Mekong Delta. From the 16th century, civil strife split the country: the Mạc dynasty challenged the Lê, then power divided between northern Trịnh lords and southern Nguyễn lords (1600–1777). The Tây Sơn rebellion (1770s) toppled both, but Nguyễn Ánh (Gia Long) defeated them, unifying Vietnam under the Nguyễn dynasty in 1802.

### French Colonization and Resistance
European contact began in the 1500s (Portuguese). French missionaries arrived in 1658; naval intervention followed persecution of Catholics (1843). From 1858–1885, France conquered Vietnam, aided by Spain initially. Cochinchina (south) became a colony (1862–67); Annam (central) and Tonkin (north) became protectorates (1884). French Indochina was formalized in 1887. The French imposed a plantation economy (rubber, tea, coffee) and Western education but denied civil rights. Resistance included the royalist Cần Vương movement (1880s), the 1908 Hanoi Poison Plot, and the 1917 Thái Nguyên uprising. Nationalist leaders Phan Bội Châu and Phan Châu Trinh emerged; the 1930 Yên Bái mutiny by the VNQDĐ was crushed, pushing many toward communism. Japan occupied Indochina in 1940, maintaining the Vichy French administration until a full takeover in March 1945, causing a famine killing up to two million.

### First Indochina War and Partition
In 1941, Ho Chi Minh formed the communist-led Việt Minh. After Japan's surrender (Aug 1945), the Việt Minh seized Hanoi/Huế, declaring independence (2 Sept 1945). Allies divided Indochina at the 16th parallel; British/Japanese forces helped France retake the south. The Việt Minh purged rival nationalists. Negotiations failed; full-scale war erupted Dec 1946. France created the State of Vietnam under ex-emperor Bảo Đại (1949). The 1954 Battle of Điện Biên Phủ forced France to the Geneva Conference. The Geneva Accords (July 1954) ended hostilities, affirmed independence, and temporarily partitioned Vietnam at the 17th parallel (DMZ), pending 1956 elections. ~1 million northerners (many Catholics) fled south via US Operation Passage to Freedom. Ngô Đình Diệm refused elections, ousted Bảo Đại (1955), and proclaimed the Republic of Vietnam (South).

### Vietnam War
North Vietnam instituted agrarian reform (1953–56), executing 13,500–100,000. Diệm repressed communists in the South. The pro-Hanoi Việt Cộng guerrilla campaign began late 1950s. The US backed South Vietnam; USSR/China backed North. Diệm's pro-Catholic bias sparked the 1963 Buddhist crisis; a US-backed coup killed Diệm. Political instability followed until Nguyễn Văn Thiệu stabilized the South (1965). The 1964 Gulf of Tonkin incident triggered massive US escalation: 500,000+ troops by 1968, sustained bombing, and the Ho Chi Minh Trail supply line through Laos. The 1968 Tết Offensive failed militarily but shattered US public support; communists massacred 3,000+ at Huế. US withdrawal began early 1970s; Paris Peace Accords (Jan 1973) removed US combat troops. North Vietnam's 1975 offensive captured Phước Long and Saigon (30 April 1975).

### Reunification and *Đổi Mới*
North and South merged as the Socialist Republic of Vietnam (2 July 1976). War deaths: 966,000–3.8 million. No mass executions occurred, but up to 300,000 South Vietnamese sent to reeducation camps. Collectivization and a planned economy failed; millions fled ("boat people"). Vietnam invaded Cambodia (1978), ousting the Khmer Rouge, establishing the People's Republic of Kampuchea (until 1989). China retaliated with a 1979 border incursion, deepening Vietnam's reliance on Soviet aid. At the Sixth CPV Congress (Dec 1986), reformers led by Nguyễn Văn Linh launched *Đổi Mới*: transition to a "socialist-oriented market economy" encouraging private ownership, deregulation, and foreign investment while retaining state control of strategic sectors. Growth averaged ~7–8% (1990s–2000s). US embargo lifted (1994); WTO entry (2007). Leadership transition: Nguyễn Phú Trọng (General Secretary 2011–2024) consolidated power; Tô Lâm succeeded him (Aug 2024); Lương Cường became President (Oct 2024).

### Geography and Climate
Vietnam occupies the eastern Indochinese Peninsula (331,000+ km²). Coastline: 3,444 km. Land boundaries: 4,639 km. Terrain: mostly hilly/forested (40% mountains, 42% tropical forest); level land ≤20%. Narrowest point: 50 km (Quảng Bình). Highest peak: Fansipan (3,143 m). Key features: Red River Delta (north, 15,000 km², densely populated, advancing 60–80 m/year into sea); Mekong Delta (south); Annamite Range; Hạ Long Bay and Phong Nha-Kẻ Bàng (World Heritage); Hang Sơn Đoòng (among world's largest caves, surveyed 2009). Climate varies by latitude/relief: North has humid subtropical/monsoonal (4 seasons); South/Central tropical monsoon (rainy/dry). Avg rainfall 1,500–2,000 mm. Highly vulnerable to climate change (55% population in low-elevation coastal zones).

### Biodiversity and Environment
Vietnam ranks 16th globally in biodiversity (Indomalayan realm), hosting ~16% of world species. 15,986 flora (10% endemic); 840 birds, 310 mammals (100/78 endemic). New species discovered recently (saola, giant muntjac, Tonkin snub-nosed monkey). 126 conservation areas (30 national parks). Major threats: wildlife poaching (rhino horn trade), Agent Orange legacy (4.8 million exposed, birth defects persist; US clean-up began 2012 at Đà Nẵng, Biên Hòa pending), arsenic in groundwater (Mekong/Red River Deltas), unexploded ordnance (UXO) from wars. Government spends ~$44M/year on demining; UXO victims receive rehabilitation. Reforestation (mangroves in Mekong/Cần Giờ) attempts ecosystem restoration. Forest Landscape Integrity Index: 5.35/10 (104th/172).

### Government and Politics
Unitary communist state; CPV sole legal ruler per Constitution. President (head of state, commander-in-chief) elected by National Assembly; General Secretary (party chief) holds most power; Prime Minister heads government (council of 5 deputies, 26 ministries). National Assembly (500 members) is supreme organ, appoints all central officials, oversees judiciary (Supreme People's Court). Only CPV-affiliated orgs (Vietnamese Fatherland Front, unions) contest elections. Death penalty retained. Recent leadership: Three-person collective (2023): President Võ Văn Thưởng, PM Phạm Minh Chính, GS Nguyễn Phú Trọng. Thưởng resigned (Mar 2024, corruption); Tô Lâm elected President (May 2024), then GS (Aug 2024); Lương Cường elected President (Oct 2024). Administrative reform (2025): 27 provinces, 7 municipalities.

### Foreign Relations
Historically dominated by China relations; sovereignty asserted in documents like *Nam quốc sơn hà* (11th c.) and *Bình Ngô đại cáo* (1428). Post-1954: North aligned Eastern Bloc, South Western Bloc. *Đổi Mới* brought diversification: UN, ASEAN, NAM, Francophonie, WTO (178 diplomatic relations by 2010). Policy: independence, self-reliance, multilateralization. US relations normalized 1995; arms embargo lifted 2016. Strategic balancing: close ties with US (countering China in South China Sea disputes) and Russia (military), while managing extensive economic/political ties with China.

### Military
Vietnam People's Armed Forces: Vietnam People's Army (VPA: Ground, Navy, Air Force, Border Guard, Coast Guard), Public Security, Self-Defence Militia. Active manpower ~450,000; total strength (incl. paramilitary) up to 5,000,000. 2015 expenditure: ~US$4.4B (~8% govt spending). Joint exercises with Brunei, China, India, Japan, Laos, Russia, Singapore, US.

### Human Rights
Pre-1986: totalitarian/autocratic. Post-1986: authoritarian. CPV only legal party. Restrictions on association, speech, press, religion. 2009: Lawyer Lê Công Định arrested for "subversion" (Amnesty: prisoner of conscience). Human trafficking persists.

### Economy
Historically agrarian (wet rice). Bauxite mined centrally. Post-1975: centrally planned, collectivized, inefficient. Soviet aid collapse + US embargo forced liberalization. *Đổi Mới* (1986) shifted to socialist-oriented market economy. GDP growth: ~8% (1990–97), ~7% (2000–05), 6.8% (2010). 2022 (IMF): Nominal GDP US$406.5B; per capita $4,086; unemployment 2.3%. Deep poverty (<$1/day) declined significantly; relative poverty lower than China/India/Philippines. Egalitarian land distribution, remote investment, subsidized education/healthcare credited. Sequenced trade liberalization (2000s). Manufacturing, IT, high-tech growing. Oil: 3rd in SEA (318k bpd, 2011). Top export market: US; top import source: China. Tourism: 7.5% GDP; 13M visitors (2017).

### Agriculture and Seafood
Market liberalization made Vietnam top global exporter: #1 cashew nuts (1/3 share), #1 black pepper (1/3), #2 rice (after Thailand), #2 coffee. Agriculture GDP share fell from 42% (1989) to 20% (2006). Fisheries: 5.6M MT (2011) → 6.7M MT (2016), driven by aquaculture expansion.

### Science, Technology, Transport, Energy, Telecom
R&D spending low (0.19–0.45% GDP). Strengths: mathematics (Hoàng Tụy global optimization; Ngô Bảo Châu Fields Medal 2010). VAST (1975) developing space program (Vietnam Space Centre 2018). Robotics (TOPIO humanoid). Zalo messaging app (Vương Quang Khải/FPT). Global Innovation Index: 44th (2025), up from 76th (2012). Publications focus: life sciences, physics, engineering.
Transport: French colonial roots. Road network: 188,744 km (2010); 93,535 km asphalt. Motorbikes dominant (4.7M in Hanoi 2013); rising car ownership causes congestion/pollution. Rail: Reunification Express (1,726 km). High-speed rail (shinkansen, Japan tech) planned but postponed for metro/road priority. 20 civil airports (3 intl: Noi Bai, Da Nang, Tan Son Nhat); 7 more planned by 2025 (Long Thanh: 100M pax capacity). Major seaports; 47,130 km navigable waterways.
Energy: State-controlled EVN (61.4% generation, 25,884 MW). Sources: hydro, coal, oil, gas. Nuclear plan abandoned (2016, public opposition). PetroVietnam dominates LPG (70%); operates renewables. Proven oil reserves: 4.4B barrels (1st SEA, 2015); gas: 0.6 tcm (3rd SEA).
Telecom: VNPT monopoly ended 2003. Top operators: Viettel (military-owned), Vinaphone, MobiFone. Market reforming for foreign investment.

### Water and Sanitation
2,360 rivers; 310B m³ annual discharge (70% rainy season). Urban water capacity exceeds demand but coverage sparse (~1/3 district towns piped). Industrial/domestic wastewater largely untreated. Arsenic in groundwater (deltas). Sanitation: 78% access "improved" (94% urban, 70% rural); 21M lack access (2015). Hi-tech/IT applied to sanitation; challenges: funding, climate change, pollution. Nationwide water inspections from June 2019.

### Demographics
Population ~97.5M (2021); 96.2M (2019 census). 65.6% rural, 34.4% urban (2019). Kinh (Viet) majority: 85.32% (82M), concentrated in deltas/coasts. 54 recognized minorities (Hmong, Dao, Tày, Thái, Nùng, etc.); minorities in highlands (2/3 territory). Central Highlands: originally Degar (Montagnards); South Vietnam resettled Kinh there. Hoa (Chinese) and Khmer Krom in lowlands. Post-1975 nationalization drove many Hoa/wealthy to flee.
Urbanization surged post-1986 *Đổi Mới*. Hanoi/Ho Chi Minh City share of urban pop rose to 15.9%/31%. Urbanization correlates with GDP growth; rural-urban migrants have higher living standards. Structural shift: Agriculture GDP 37.2% (1985) → 18.5% (2008); Industry 26.2% → 43.2%. Electricity access: 14% (1993) → 96%+ (2009). Water access: 12% (2002) → 70%+ (2007). Downsides: traffic, pollution, waste (solid waste +200% 2003–08; industrial 181% of increase). Government promotes waste sorting, public transit.

### Languages
National: Vietnamese (tonal, Vietic, Austroasiatic). Minority languages: Tày, Mường, Cham, Khmer, Chinese (Cantonese), Nùng, Hmong; Montagnard languages (Austroasiatic/Malayo-Polynesian). Sign languages developing in cities.
French: legacy, spoken by educated/older southerners; member of Francophonie.

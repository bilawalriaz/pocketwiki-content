# Broadway theatre

## Overview
Broadway theatre refers to the professional theatrical performances presented in 41 venues with 500+ seats in Midtown Manhattan's Theater District. It represents the highest commercial level of live theatre in the English-speaking world, alongside London's West End. Dominated by musicals, it is a major tourist attraction and cultural institution, with shows grossing over $1.5 billion annually in recent seasons.

## Timeline
- **c. 1750** — First significant NYC theatre established on Nassau Street.
- **1798** — Park Theatre opens, resuming theatre after the Revolutionary War.
- **1866** — *The Black Crook* premieres, considered the first modern musical.
- **1896** — Theatrical Syndicate formed, controlling most U.S. theatres.
- **1919** — Actors' Equity Association strike secures standard contracts.
- **1927** — *Show Boat* premieres, integrating book and score dramatically.
- **1943** — *Oklahoma!* opens, launching a postwar golden age.
- **1969** — Number of Broadway theatres drops to 36 from a 1920s peak of 70-80.
- **1982** — "Save the Theatres" campaign begins to preserve the district.
- **2020** — Broadway shuts down March 12 due to the COVID-19 pandemic.
- **2021** — Performances resume in September with vaccination requirements.

## Body

### Definition and Geography
Broadway theatre is defined by Actors' Equity Association contracts as productions in venues with 500+ seats within the Theater District. While the district is eponymous with Broadway, only three theatres are on the thoroughfare itself. Most are on numbered cross streets between 41st and 53rd Streets, with one at Lincoln Center. Smaller venues are classified as Off-Broadway (100-499 seats) or Off-Off-Broadway (under 100 seats).

### History and Evolution
New York theatre began in the mid-18th century, moving from Lower Manhattan to Midtown by the mid-19th century seeking cheaper real estate. The first "long-run" musical was *The Elves* (1857). The modern musical form is traced to *The Black Crook* (1866). The early 20th century saw the rise of operettas and the "Princess Theatre" shows. The 1920s brought revues and the integration of book and score in *Show Boat* (1927). A golden age followed World War II, epitomized by *Oklahoma!* (1943). The late 1960s and 1970s saw a decline in productions and theatre numbers, leading to preservation efforts in the 1980s. The COVID-19 pandemic caused a historic shutdown in 2020, with a gradual, mandated reopening in 2021.

### Business and Operations
Most shows are commercial, open-ended productions aiming for profit. The length of a run depends on ticket sales, which are influenced by reviews, word-of-mouth, and advertising. Three major organizations—The Shubert Organization, The Nederlander Organization, and ATG Entertainment—own or manage most theatres. The Broadway League is the trade organization that negotiates with unions and co-administers the Tony Awards. Four non-profit companies also operate Broadway venues.

### Audience and Experience
Attending a Broadway show is a major tourist activity. The average audience age in 2022-23 was 40.4, with 35% of tickets sold to locals and 17% to international visitors. Discounted tickets are available through TKTS booths. A standard week includes eight performances, with shows typically dark on Mondays. Successful shows often launch national touring productions, which can be full-scale or scaled-down "bus and truck" tours.

## Terms
- **Broadway theatre**: Professional productions in 500+ seat venues in the Theater District.
- **Off-Broadway**: Theatres with 100-499 seats, offering more experimental work.
- **Open-ended run**: A production with no predetermined closing date, based on commercial success.
- **Theatrical Syndicate**: A late-19th century trust that controlled most U.S. legitimate theatres.
- **Actors' Equity Association**: The union for professional actors and stage managers.
- **The Broadway League**: The trade association for Broadway producers and theatre owners.
- **Tony Awards**: The American theatre industry's highest honor, presented annually since 1947.
- **Matinée**: An afternoon performance, typically on Wednesdays and Saturdays.
- **Bus and truck tour**: A scaled-down national touring production for smaller cities.

It also highlights the historical tension between commercial Broadway and non-profit or experimental Off-Broadway theatre, and the evolving role of star power from film and television in drawing audiences.

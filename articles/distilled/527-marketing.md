# Marketing

## Overview
Marketing is the act of acquiring, satisfying and retaining customers. It is one of the primary components of business management and commerce. The discipline has evolved from a production-focused creative industry into a recognized science drawing on psychology, economics, and neuroscience. It centers on identifying and satisfying consumer needs and wants more effectively than competitors, utilizing frameworks like the marketing mix to align product, price, place, and promotion with target markets. Modern definitions emphasize long-term customer relationships and societal responsibility over simple transactional exchanges.

## Timeline
- **1776** — Adam Smith publishes *The Wealth of Nations*; the marketing concept originates here but does not become widely used until nearly 200 years later.
- **1935** — American Marketing Association (AMA) defines marketing as "the performance of business activities that direct the flow of goods, and services from producers to consumers".
- **1940s (late)** — The origins of the 4 Ps traced to this period; first known mention attributed to James Culliton, Professor of Marketing at Harvard University.
- **1960** — E. Jerome McCarthy proposes the modern 4Ps framework (Product, Price, Place, Promotion) within a managerial approach.
- **1980** — Philip Kotler defines marketing as "satisfying needs and wants through an exchange process" as part of his evolving definition.
- **1990** — Robert F. Lauterborn proposes the consumer-oriented 4Cs model (Consumer, Cost, Convenience, Communication).
- **2008** — AMA adds "society at large" to the official definition of marketing.
- **2018** — Kotler updates definition to emphasize engaging customers, building relationships, and creating customer value to capture value in return.

## Body

### Definition and Evolution
The American Marketing Association (AMA) currently defines marketing as the activity, set of institutions, and processes for creating, communicating, delivering, and exchanging offerings that have value for customers, clients, partners, and society at large. This definition, updated in 2008, expanded the 1935 version—which focused on the performance of business activities directing the flow of goods and services from producers to consumers—by adding stakeholders beyond the buyer-seller dyad. The AMA reviews this definition every three years. Philip Kotler’s definition similarly evolved from a 1980 exchange-process view to a 2018 focus on engagement, relationship building, and mutual value capture. The Chartered Institute of Marketing adds a profitability constraint, defining it as the management process responsible for identifying, anticipating, and satisfying customer requirements profitably. A related definition from sales process engineering views marketing as interconnected processes aimed at achieving customer interest and satisfaction. While historically viewed as a creative industry involving advertising and design, marketing is now widely recognized as a science due to its extensive use of social sciences, mathematics, and neuroscience.

### Core Concepts: Needs, Wants, Demands, and the Marketing Concept
The marketing concept proposes that organizational objectives are best achieved by anticipating and satisfying potential consumers' needs and wants more effectively than competitors. This concept originated from Adam Smith's book *The Wealth of Nations* but would not become widely used until nearly 200 years later. **Needs** are states of felt deprivation essential for survival or well-being (e.g., food, shelter, belonging); unfulfilled needs result in dysfunction or death. **Wants** are culturally shaped desires for specific satisfiers of needs. **Demands** are wants backed by purchasing power. Marketing research identifies unmet needs to drive new product development. **Market segmentation** divides heterogeneous markets into homogeneous groups based on distinct needs, characteristics, or behaviors. **Needs-based segmentation** (benefit segmentation) places customer desires at the forefront of product design and is considered highly effective though difficult to implement.

### Marketing Orientations
Organizations operate under distinct philosophies that guide strategy:
*   **Production Concept:** Focuses on high volume and low cost via economies of scale. Dominant 1860s–1930s; still useful in specific contexts.
*   **Product Concept:** Prioritizes product quality and innovation; largely supplanted except in haute couture and arts marketing.
*   **Selling Concept:** Emphasizes aggressive promotion of existing products, typically for "unsought goods," rather than developing new solutions. A 2011 meta-analysis found the greatest impact on sales performance comes from a salesperson's sales-related knowledge, adaptiveness, role clarity, cognitive aptitude, and motivation.
*   **Marketing Concept:** The dominant contemporary approach; customer-centric, relying on market research and R&D to suit consumer tastes. It includes **Customer orientation** (ascertaining demand is vital for viability), **Organizational orientation** (marketing department guides other departments), and **Societal marketing concept** (social responsibility toward employees, communities, environment, often via triple bottom line reporting). Sustainable or green marketing extends this orientation.

### B2B, B2C, C2B, and C2C Marketing
**B2B (Business-to-Business)** markets products (major equipment, raw materials, services) to organizations: producers, resellers, governments, and institutions. It uses the 7 Ps (adding People, Process, Physical Evidence to the 4Ps). **B2C (Business-to-Consumer)** markets directly to individuals, increasingly online, using emotional appeals. **C2B (Consumer-to-Business)** inverts the model: consumers create value (data, content, naming price) for firms. **C2C (Customer-to-Customer)** facilitates transactions between individuals via third-party platforms (e.g., e-commerce, sharing economy). Key differences include: B2B demand is *derived* from final consumer demand; B2B purchasing volumes are higher but customer count lower; B2B buying is formal, multi-influencer, and negotiable; B2C buying is informal, individual, and usually fixed-price. Reciprocity and leasing are common in B2B but absent in B2C.

### The Marketing Mix: 4Ps and 4Cs
The **marketing mix** is the foundational tool for bringing offerings to market.
**The 4Ps (McCarthy, 1960):**
*   **Product:** Specifications, design, branding, packaging, warranties.
*   **Price:** Monetary or non-monetary cost (time, energy); domain of pricing science.
*   **Place (Distribution):** Channels, intermediaries (wholesalers, retailers), geographic coverage, online vs. retail.
*   **Promotion:** Marketing communications (advertising, PR, sales promotion, personal selling, social media, viral marketing).
*Criticisms:* The 4Ps represent an "inside-out" (seller-centric) view; categories overlap (e.g., personal selling fits Place and Promotion; promotional pricing fits Price and Promotion); lacks strategic framework for uncontrollable external factors.
*Extensions:* **7 Ps** (add People, Process, Physical Evidence) for services marketing (intangibility, perishability, heterogeneity, inseparability).

**The 4Cs (Lauterborn, 1990):** A consumer-oriented ("outside-in") alternative for the shift from mass to niche marketing.
*   **Consumer** (replaces Product): Focus on fulfilling wants/needs.
*   **Cost** (replaces Price): Total sacrifice (monetary + time, effort).
*   **Convenience** (replaces Place): Ease of access (physical and digital).
*   **Communication** (replaces Promotion): Two-way dialogue (social media) vs. one-way advertising.

### Marketing Environment
Decision-making is constrained by three environmental layers:
1.  **Macro-environment (Macromarketing):** Uncontrollable external forces (Political, Economic, Social, Technological, Legal, Ecological) analyzed via **PESTLE**.
2.  **Micro-environment:** Factors with partial control: Customers, Employees, Suppliers, Media.
3.  **Internal Environment:** Controllable internal factors: Labor, Inventory, Policy, Logistics, Budget, Capital Assets.

### Marketing Research
A systematic process: Define problem → Plan research → Research → Interpret data → Implement findings. It supports all marketing activities. **Market research** (subset) gathers data on a specific target market/distribution. **Marketing research** (superset) encompasses distribution, advertising effectiveness, and salesforce effectiveness. Premier academic journals include *Journal of Consumer Research*, *Journal of Marketing*, *Journal of Marketing Research*, and *Marketing Science*.

### Segmentation, Targeting, Positioning (STP)
**Segmentation** splits consumers by Geographic, Demographic (age, gender, income, life-cycle), Psychographic (personality, lifestyle), or Behavioral (loyalty, usage) criteria. **Targeting** evaluates segments via **DAMP**: Discernable, Accessible, Measurable, Profitable. **Differentiation modes:** Undifferentiated (one product for all), Differentiated (modifications per segment), Niche (specialized product for specialized target). **Positioning** establishes a product's mental image relative to competitors, often using **perceptual maps** plotting consumer perceptions of price and quality to tailor communications.

### Product Life Cycle (PLC)
A tool to gauge product progress (sales/revenue over time) based on assumptions: distinct stages (Introduction, Growth, Maturity, Decline); finite product life; strategy must adapt per stage.
*   **Introduction:** High advertising to build awareness.
*   **Growth:** Rising sales; increased competition; sustain communications.
*   **Maturity:** Sales level off; price competition increases; sales promotions used.
*   **Decline:** Demand tapers; discontinue unless niche/complementary value exists or efficiency savings sustain it.

## Terms
- ****Marketing Mix**** — The set of controllable tactical tools (Product, Price, Place, Promotion) used to produce a desired response in the target market.
- ****B2B / B2C**** — Business-to-Business (selling to organizations) vs. Business-to-Consumer (selling to individuals).
- ****Derived Demand**** — B2B demand that originates from the demand for the final consumer product.
- ****4Ps / 4Cs**** — Seller-centric framework (Product, Price, Place, Promotion) vs. Buyer-centric framework (Consumer, Cost, Convenience, Communication).
- ****PESTLE Analysis**** — Framework for scanning the macro-environment: Political, Economic, Social, Technological, Legal, Ecological factors.
- ****Market Segmentation**** — Dividing a heterogeneous total market into homogeneous sub-groups based on needs, characteristics, or behaviors.
- ****DAMP Criteria**** — Target market viability test: Discernable, Accessible, Measurable, Profitable.
- ****Perceptual Map**** — Visual tool plotting competing products by consumer perceptions (typically price vs. quality) to inform positioning.
- ****Product Life Cycle (PLC)**** — Model describing stages (Introduction, Growth, Maturity, Decline) of a product's sales trajectory over time.
- ****Societal Marketing Concept**** — Orientation requiring firms to satisfy customer needs while preserving long-term societal well-being (triple bottom line).

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Definition Scope:** Whether marketing should be defined narrowly as exchange processes or broadly to include societal stakeholders and shareholder value maximization (AMA vs. Kotler vs. Chartered Institute vs. Finance perspectives).
*   **Science vs. Art:** The tension between marketing’s historical identity as a creative industry and its modern classification as a science grounded in social sciences and data analytics.
*   **4Ps Validity:** Ongoing criticism that the 4Ps model suffers from category overlap (e.g., personal selling as promotion vs. place), lacks mutual exclusivity, adopts an "inside-out" bias, and lacks a strategic framework for external uncontrollables.
*   **Orientation Dominance:** While the Marketing Concept is currently dominant, the Production Concept persists in specific industries, and the Societal Marketing Concept challenges the sufficiency of customer-centricity alone.
*   **Research Distinction:** Maintaining the methodological boundary between "market research" (specific target/distribution) and "marketing research" (all marketing activities including advertising/salesforce effectiveness).

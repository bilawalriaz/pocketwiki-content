# Pharaoh

## Overview
The Pharaoh was the monarch of ancient Egypt, serving as both a political and religious leader. The title, originally referring to the royal palace, evolved during the New Kingdom to denote the ruler personally. The Pharaoh was considered a divine intermediary between the gods and humanity, responsible for maintaining cosmic order (Maat), and held absolute authority over the land, laws, and military.

## Timeline
- **c. 3150 BCE** — First Dynasty begins; early kings use titles like Horus and Nesu Bity.
- **c. 2686–2181 BCE** — Old Kingdom; Pharaohs build pyramids and are depicted as divine.
- **c. 1550–1069 BCE** — New Kingdom; the title "Pharaoh" (pr ꜥꜣ) begins to be used for the ruler.
- **c. 1479–1425 BCE** — Reign of Thutmose III; possible early personal use of the title "Pharaoh."
- **c. 1353–1336 BCE** — Reign of Akhenaten; earliest confirmed letter addressing the ruler as "Pharaoh."
- **10th century BCE** — Title "Pharaoh" first attached to a ruler's name (Siamun).
- **8th–7th centuries BCE** — Third Intermediate Period; "Pharaoh" becomes the common epithet for the king.
- **30 BCE** — Annexation of Egypt by the Roman Republic ends Pharaonic rule.

## Body

### Etymology and Usage
The word "pharaoh" derives from the Egyptian *pr ꜥꜣ*, meaning "great house," originally referring to the royal palace. From the Twelfth Dynasty, it appeared in phrases like "Great House, May it Live," but only for the building. During the New Kingdom, it became a form of address for the king. The earliest confirmed use for a ruler is in a letter to Akhenaten (c. 1353–1336 BCE), though an inscription possibly referring to Thutmose III (c. 1479–1425 BCE) may be earlier. By the late Twenty-first Dynasty, "Pharaoh" was added to royal titles, and from the Nineteenth Dynasty, it was used as regularly as "Majesty." The first dated use attached to a ruler's name is Year 17 of Siamun (10th century BCE).

### Functions and Divinity
The Pharaoh was the central figure of the state, acting as the obligatory intermediary between gods and humans. He ensured proper temple rituals and, in return, guaranteed prosperity, defense, and justice. Religiously, he was the supreme officiant, the first of the priests. Politically, he owned all land, enacted laws, collected taxes, and commanded the military. His core duty was to maintain Maat (cosmic order, balance, and justice), which included warfare to defend Egypt or acquire resources. The Pharaoh was considered divine, a concept originating before 3000 BCE. In the Early Dynastic Period, he was the incarnation of Horus. By Djedefre's reign (26th century BCE), his mother was said to be impregnated by the sun god Ra. During the New Kingdom, he received the "living royal ka" of Amun-Re at coronation. This divinity persisted under Persian and Ptolemaic rule.

### Regalia and Crowns
Pharaonic regalia included scepters like the *heqa* (crook) and *was*, and the *uraeus* (rearing cobra) for protection. Crowns symbolized rule over unified Egypt. The *Deshret* (Red Crown) represented Lower Egypt, and the *Hedjet* (White Crown) represented Upper Egypt. After unification, the *Pschent* (Double Crown) combined both. Later headdresses included the *Nemes* (striped headcloth), *Atef*, *Hemhem*, and *Khepresh* (blue crown). Notably, no ancient Egyptian crown has ever been discovered; they were likely ritual objects passed between successors.

### Royal Titulary
By the Middle Kingdom, a Pharaoh held five official names: the Horus name (oldest, in a *serekh* frame), the *Nebty* (Two Ladies) name linking him to protective goddesses, the Golden Horus name, and the *prenomen* and *nomen* (often in cartouches). The *Nesu Bity* (prenomen) name, meaning "King of Upper and Lower Egypt," may have been the birth name.

## Terms
- **Maat (mꜣꜥt):** The ancient Egyptian concept of cosmic order, balance, truth, and justice, which the Pharaoh was responsible for maintaining.
- **Pr ꜥꜣ:** The Egyptian term meaning "great house," the origin of the word "Pharaoh," initially referring to the palace.
- **Serekh:** A rectangular frame representing a palace facade, in which the early Horus name of a king was written.
- **Cartouche:** An oval ring enclosing a royal name, typically the prenomen or nomen, introduced in the Middle Kingdom.
- **Prenomen (Nesu Bity name):** The throne name of a Pharaoh, often incorporating the name of the sun god Re.
- **Nomen:** The birth name of a Pharaoh, often preceded by the title "Son of Re."
- **Uraeus:** A stylized, rearing cobra symbol worn on Pharaonic headdresses, representing divine authority and protection.
- **Pschent:** The Double Crown combining the Red Crown (Deshret) of Lower Egypt and the White Crown (Hedjet) of Upper Egypt.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The exact date when the title *pr ꜥꜣ* was first used to address a Pharaoh personally is debated, with evidence pointing to either Thutmose III or Akhenaten.
- The function and meaning of the Golden Horus name are not well understood.
- The divinity of the Pharaoh is interpreted differently in ancient Egyptian sources versus Classical Greek accounts, with Herodotus claiming Egyptian priests rejected the notion.

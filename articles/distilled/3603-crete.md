# Crete

## Overview
Crete is the largest Greek island and a major Mediterranean crossroads, pivotal to European history as the cradle of the Minoan civilization. Its strategic location and fertile land have made it a contested prize for successive empires, shaping a unique cultural identity that blends ancient, Byzantine, Venetian, and Ottoman influences. Today, it is a vital part of Greece's economy and cultural heritage, renowned for its dramatic landscapes and archaeological wealth.

## Timeline
- **c. 7000 BCE** — Permanent Neolithic settlement begins, with major sites like Knossos.
- **c. 2700–1420 BCE** — Minoan civilization flourishes, centered on palace complexes.
- **c. 1420 BCE** — Mycenaean civilization from mainland Greece overruns the Minoans.
- **69 BCE** — Crete is conquered by Rome, becoming part of the province Creta et Cyrenaica.
- **820s CE** — Andalusian Arabs establish the Emirate of Crete.
- **960–961 CE** — Byzantine general Nikephoros Phokas reconquers Crete, restoring Byzantine rule.
- **1212 CE** — The Republic of Venice secures Crete as a colony following the Fourth Crusade.
- **1669 CE** — The Ottoman Empire conquers Crete after the long Siege of Candia.
- **1898 CE** — Crete achieves autonomy as the Cretan State under Ottoman suzerainty.
- **December 1913** — Crete is formally united with the Kingdom of Greece.
- **May 1941** — The Battle of Crete is fought during World War II, ending in German occupation.

## Body

### Geography and Climate
Crete is elongated, spanning 260 km east-west but only 60 km at its widest. It is predominantly mountainous, defined by six major ranges, including the White Mountains (Lefka Ori) and Mount Ida (Psiloritis), the island's highest point. The landscape features fertile plateaus, deep gorges like the Samaria Gorge (a World Biosphere Reserve), and few freshwater lakes. The climate is primarily hot-summer Mediterranean, with milder, wetter conditions in the west and a semi-arid zone in the south and east. South Crete receives the most sunshine in Greece.

### Human Geography and Economy
The Region of Crete, with its capital at Heraklion, is Greece's most populous island region. The economy is a mix of services, tourism, and agriculture. Crete is self-sufficient in food production, growing olives, grapes, and citrus, and producing 20% of Greek wine. Tourism is a major industry, with 6.3 million visitors in 2023, though this brings pressures like overtourism. The island has a high per capita income relative to Greece. Infrastructure includes international airports at Heraklion and Chania, major ferry ports, and a north-coast highway under upgrade.

### History: From Antiquity to Venetian Rule
Crete was the center of the advanced Minoan civilization, known for its art, writing (Linear A), and palaces. After its subsumption by the Mycenaeans, the island was settled by Dorians and later conquered by Rome in 69 BCE. Following the Roman period, Crete was part of the Byzantine Empire until its conquest by Andalusian Arabs in the 820s. Byzantine rule was restored in 961. After the Fourth Crusade, Venice took control in 1212, fostering a Renaissance and the Cretan School of art, which influenced El Greco.

### History: Ottoman Rule to Modern Greece
The Ottomans conquered Crete in 1669. A significant Muslim population emerged, largely from local Greek converts. Repeated Cretan revolts for union with Greece, such as the 1866–1869 uprising and the Arkadi Monastery Holocaust, drew international attention. Following interventions by the Great Powers, Crete gained autonomy in 1898 as the Cretan State. Political turmoil led to a unilateral declaration of union with Greece in 1908, which was internationally recognized in 1913. During WWII, the island was the site of a fierce 1941 battle and subsequent brutal German occupation.

### Culture and Society
Crete maintains a distinct cultural identity with its own Mantinades poetry, music played on the Cretan lyra, and dances like the Pentozali. Traditional male dress includes black boots and breeches. The island has produced major Greek literary figures like Nikos Kazantzakis and Vitsentzos Kornaros. Cretan society is noted for strong family and clan ties, and a tradition of firearms ownership stemming from resistance periods. Mythologically, Crete is linked to Zeus's birth, the Minotaur, and the labyrinth of Knossos.

### Fauna and Flora
Crete's isolation has led to unique fauna, including endemic species like the vulnerable kri-kri goat and the Cretan shrew. Prehistoric dwarf elephants and hippos once lived there. The island has no animals dangerous to humans. The seas host endangered Mediterranean monk seals, whales, and dolphins. Flora includes over 200 wild orchid species, indigenous herbs like Cretan dittany, and the largest natural palm forest in Europe at Vai. Deforestation has occurred since Minoan times.

## Terms
- **Minoan civilization**: Europe's first advanced civilization, centered on Crete from c. 2700 to 1420 BCE, known for its palace complexes and undeciphered Linear A script.
- **Linear B**: An early form of Greek writing found on Crete, dating to the Mycenaean period (c. 1425–1375 BCE).
- **Thalassocracy**: A state with primary power based on the sea; the Minoans are often described as a thalassocracy.
- **Enosis**: The political union of Crete with Greece, a goal of Cretan revolts in the 19th and early 20th centuries.
- **Cretan State**: The autonomous polity established in 1898 under Ottoman suzerainty, preceding union with Greece.
- **Mantinades**: A traditional form of Cretan rhyming couplet poetry.
- **Cretan lyra**: A three-stringed bowed musical instrument central to Cretan folk music.
- **Kri-kri**: An endangered subspecies of wild goat (*Capra aegagrus cretica*) endemic to Crete and nearby islets.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The exact etymology of the name "Crete" is unknown, with proposals linking it to Luwian or ancient Greek words.
- The precise causes and timeline of the decline and fall of the Minoan civilization remain subjects of scholarly investigation.
- The impact and legacy of the brief Andalusian Arab rule (Emirate of Crete) on the island's subsequent culture and demographics is studied.
- The long-term social and environmental effects of mass tourism on Crete's infrastructure, natural resources, and traditional communities are ongoing concerns.

# Margaret Thatcher

## Overview
Margaret Thatcher was a British stateswoman who served as Prime Minister from 1979 to 1990, the longest-serving PM of the 20th century and the first woman to hold the office. Her tenure, defined by the ideology of "Thatcherism," implemented radical free-market reforms, privatisation, and a reduction in trade union power, fundamentally reshaping Britain's economic and political landscape. Her legacy remains deeply polarising, with debates continuing over the social and economic impact of her policies.

## Body

### Early Life and Career
Born to a Methodist grocer and alderman, Margaret Roberts studied chemistry at Oxford under Dorothy Hodgkin. After a brief career as a research chemist, she qualified as a barrister in 1953. She married businessman Denis Thatcher in 1951. She was first elected as MP for Finchley in 1959, having previously contested the Labour seat of Dartford in 1950 and 1951.

### Rise to Power
Thatcher served as Education Secretary under Edward Heath (1970–74), where she became known as "Milk Snatcher" for abolishing free school milk for older children. After Heath's government fell, she challenged and defeated him for the Conservative leadership in 1975. Influenced by neoliberal thinkers at the Institute of Economic Affairs, she positioned herself against the post-war consensus of state intervention. She became Prime Minister following the Conservatives' victory in the 1979 election, capitalising on public discontent during the "Winter of Discontent."

### Domestic Policy: "Thatcherism"
Thatcher's domestic agenda focused on reversing Britain's economic decline through monetarist policies to curb inflation, privatisation of state-owned industries, and curbing trade union power. Her government faced deep recession and high unemployment in the early 1980s, but she refused to change course, famously declaring, "The lady's not for turning." A key victory was defeating the 1984–85 miners' strike. Privatisation accelerated after 1983, selling off utilities like gas and electricity. Her popularity waned due to the deeply unpopular Community Charge (poll tax) and internal party divisions over Europe.

### Foreign Policy
Thatcher forged a close alliance with US President Ronald Reagan, supporting his Cold War stance against the Soviet Union. Her defining moment was the successful 1982 Falklands War against Argentina. She was initially wary of German reunification and became increasingly Eurosceptic, opposing further political integration within the European Community. She negotiated the 1984 Sino-British Joint Declaration, agreeing to the 1997 handover of Hong Kong to China. In Northern Ireland, she refused to grant political status to hunger-striking prisoners but later signed the 1985 Anglo-Irish Agreement, giving the Republic of Ireland an advisory role in Northern Irish governance.

### Resignation and Later Life
Thatcher's authority was undermined by the poll tax riots, her opposition to European monetary integration, and a challenge from Michael Heseltine. She resigned in November 1990 and was succeeded by John Major. She retired from the Commons in 1992, was made a life peer, and continued to influence political debate until her health declined. She died in 2013 and received a ceremonial funeral.

## Terms
*   **Thatcherism:** A political and economic ideology associated with Thatcher, emphasising free markets, privatisation, deregulation, and a reduced role for the state and trade unions.
*   **Monetarism:** An economic theory (influenced by Milton Friedman) that focuses on controlling the money supply to manage inflation, which Thatcher initially prioritised over combating unemployment.
*   **Privatisation:** The transfer of state-owned industries and assets into private ownership, a central policy of Thatcher's governments.
*   **Community Charge (Poll Tax):** A local tax system introduced by Thatcher, where every adult paid the same amount regardless of income. It was highly unpopular and led to widespread protests.
*   **Winter of Discontent (1978–79):** A period of widespread strikes by public and private sector trade unions in the UK, which contributed to the defeat of the Labour government.
*   **Big Bang (1986):** The sudden deregulation of the London financial markets, removing many restrictions and leading to a boom in the financial sector.
*   **Anglo-Irish Agreement (1985):** An agreement between the UK and Irish governments that gave the Republic of Ireland an advisory role in the governance of Northern Ireland.
*   **Euroscepticism:** Opposition to or criticism of the European Union and further political integration, a stance that became increasingly central to Thatcher's later premiership.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Economic Legacy:** Did Thatcher's policies modernise and revitalise the British economy, or did they cause lasting social damage through deindustrialisation, high unemployment, and regional inequality?
*   **Social Impact:** Was she a necessary reformer who broke the power of vested interests, or a divisive figure who condoned greed and exacerbated social divisions?
*   **Political Realignment:** To what extent did she permanently reshape British politics, forcing even the Labour Party under Tony Blair to accept the core tenets of her economic revolution?
*   **European Policy:** Was her opposition to deeper European integration a principled defence of national sovereignty, or a damaging and isolationist stance?

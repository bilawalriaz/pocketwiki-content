# San Francisco

## Overview
San Francisco is a major U.S. city and county on the West Coast, renowned for its economic power, cultural influence, and iconic landmarks. Its history is defined by rapid growth from the Gold Rush, resilience after the 1906 earthquake, and its role as a center for counterculture, technology, and liberal activism. Today, it is a global hub for finance, technology, and tourism, facing challenges like high costs and homelessness.

## Timeline
- **c. 3000 BCE** — Earliest archaeological evidence of human habitation in the area.
- **November 2, 1769** — First documented European visit to San Francisco Bay by the Portolá expedition.
- **June 29, 1776** — Spanish settlers establish the Presidio of San Francisco and Mission San Francisco de Asís.
- **1847** — The settlement of Yerba Buena is officially renamed San Francisco.
- **1848–1849** — California Gold Rush causes explosive population growth.
- **1856** — San Francisco becomes a consolidated city-county.
- **April 18, 1906** — A major earthquake and subsequent fires destroy over three-quarters of the city.
- **1915** — The city hosts the Panama–Pacific International Exposition, celebrating its rebuilding.
- **1936–1937** — The San Francisco–Oakland Bay Bridge and Golden Gate Bridge are completed.
- **1945** — The United Nations Charter is drafted and signed in San Francisco.
- **1967** — The "Summer of Love" in Haight-Ashbury cements the city's counterculture reputation.
- **1989** — The Loma Prieta earthquake causes significant damage, leading to freeway demolition and waterfront renewal.

## Body

### History and Settlement
The area was originally inhabited by the Yelamu Ohlone. Spanish colonization began in 1776 with the Presidio and Mission. After Mexican rule, the U.S. captured Yerba Buena in 1846 and renamed it San Francisco in 1847. The 1849 Gold Rush triggered massive immigration and growth. The city was devastated by the 1906 earthquake and fire but was rapidly rebuilt. World War II brought a surge of military activity and population. Post-war, San Francisco became a magnet for counterculture movements, including the Beat Generation and hippies, and a pioneering center for the gay rights movement.

### Geography and Climate
San Francisco occupies the tip of a peninsula, characterized by steep hills and a "seven-by-seven-mile square" mainland area. It includes several islands, such as Alcatraz and Treasure Island. The city is near the San Andreas Fault, making earthquakes a persistent threat. Its climate is a warm-summer Mediterranean type, with cool, moist winters and mild, dry summers. The Pacific Ocean and bay strongly moderate temperatures, creating a famously foggy summer, with September typically being the warmest month.

### Demographics and Society
San Francisco is a majority-minority city with a diverse population. As of the 2020 census, Whites (41.3%) and Asians (33.9%) are the largest racial groups, with a significant Hispanic/Latino population (15.6%). The city has a long history of immigration, particularly from Asia, and is home to the oldest Chinatown in North America. It has the highest percentage of gay and lesbian residents among major U.S. cities and is a historic center for LGBTQ+ rights. Homelessness is a chronic and growing issue, with the city spending hundreds of millions annually on services.

### Economy
San Francisco has a diversified, high-income service economy. It is a global financial center, historically known as the "Wall Street of the West." Since the 1990s, it has become a major hub for technology, biotechnology, and venture capital, hosting companies like Salesforce, Uber, and OpenAI. Tourism is a vital industry, with millions of visitors annually drawn to landmarks like the Golden Gate Bridge and Alcatraz. The city has a strong small-business culture, though it faces challenges from high costs and post-pandemic downtown vacancies.

### Culture and Landmarks
The city is known for its eclectic architecture, steep streets, and distinct neighborhoods like Chinatown, the Mission District, and the Castro. Major landmarks include the Golden Gate Bridge, cable cars, and Fisherman's Wharf. It is a center for performing arts, with institutions like the San Francisco Opera and Symphony, and museums such as SFMOMA and the California Academy of Sciences. The city's parks, including Golden Gate Park and the Presidio, are major recreational assets.

### Government and Infrastructure
San Francisco is a consolidated city-county with a mayor and an 11-member Board of Supervisors. It is a sanctuary city and uses ranked-choice voting for local elections. Public transportation is extensive, including the Muni bus and light rail system, BART regional rail, and historic cable cars. The city is served by San Francisco International Airport (SFO). Freeways are limited due to geography and past "freeway revolts," and the city promotes cycling and walking.

## Terms
*   **Consolidated city-county:** A city that is coterminous with a county, combining the functions of both municipal and county government.
*   **Mediterranean climate (Köppen Csb):** A climate characterized by warm, dry summers and cool, wet winters, typical of coastal California.
*   **Gentrification:** The process where wealthier people move into a deteriorating neighborhood, leading to increased property values and the displacement of lower-income residents.
*   **Ranked-choice voting:** An electoral system where voters rank candidates by preference; if no candidate wins a majority, the last-place candidate is eliminated and their votes are redistributed until one achieves a majority.
*   **Vision Zero:** A multi-national road traffic safety project that aims to achieve a highway system with no fatalities or serious injuries.
*   **Point-in-time (PIT) count:** A snapshot count of a city's homeless population on a single night, used for federal reporting and planning.
*   **Formula retail:** A type of business with 11 or more locations nationwide, subject to specific zoning restrictions in San Francisco.
*   **Daylighting (creeks):** The process of uncovering and restoring previously buried or culverted urban streams to their natural state.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Economic "Doom Loop":** There is debate over whether high office vacancies, declining tax revenues, and social issues like homelessness could trigger a self-reinforcing cycle of economic decline for the city's downtown core, though some sources argue the issues are localized.
*   **Homelessness Policy:** The city's approach to homelessness, including spending levels, sanctioned encampments, and the effectiveness of services, is a subject of ongoing political and public debate.
*   **Impact of Tech Industry:** The influx of high-paying tech jobs is credited with economic vitality but also blamed for exacerbating inequality, gentrification, and the high cost of living that displaces long-term residents.
*   **Freeway Removal:** The decision to demolish the Embarcadero Freeway after the 1989 earthquake is seen by many as a success that reclaimed the waterfront, but it remains part of broader discussions about transportation infrastructure and urban design.

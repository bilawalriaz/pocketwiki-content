# Pierre de Coubertin

## Overview
Pierre de Coubertin (1863–1937) was a French educator and historian, best known as the co-founder and second president of the International Olympic Committee (IOC) and the driving force behind the revival of the modern Olympic Games. His work was rooted in a philosophy that sport, particularly organized athletics, was essential for moral and physical education, a belief he sought to implement in French schools and ultimately on an international stage.

## Timeline
- **1 Jan 1863** — Born in Paris into an aristocratic family.
- **1883** — First visit to England; studies physical education at Rugby School.
- **1888** — Publishes *L'Éducation en Angleterre*; founds the Comité Jules Simon.
- **1889** — Develops the idea of reviving the Olympic Games.
- **1894** — Organizes the Sorbonne Congress; the IOC is founded.
- **1896** — Attends the first modern Olympic Games in Athens; becomes IOC president.
- **1900** — Paris Games are overshadowed by the World's Fair; Coubertin is fired from the organizing committee.
- **1912** — Wins Olympic gold medal for literature under a pseudonym.
- **1925** — Steps down as IOC president after the successful 1924 Paris Games.
- **2 Sep 1937** — Dies in Geneva, Switzerland; his heart is interred at Olympia, Greece.

## Body

### Early Life and Educational Philosophy
Born into French nobility, Coubertin was educated in a strict Jesuit system, which fostered his appreciation for ancient Greece and Rome. His career path turned toward education, and a pivotal 1883 visit to England exposed him to the athletic programs of Thomas Arnold at Rugby School. Coubertin became convinced that organized sport was a powerful tool for building character, morality, and social strength, and he advocated for its inclusion in French schools. He romanticized the ancient Greek gymnasium, which he believed achieved a "triple unity" between young and old, different disciplines, and theoretical and practical work. Despite his efforts, his campaign to reform French school curricula largely failed.

### Reviving the Olympic Games
Coubertin's educational reform failure led him to a new idea: reviving the ancient Olympic Games as an international athletic festival. He was influenced by earlier, smaller-scale efforts, notably those of William Penny Brookes in England and the Zappas cousins in Greece. After years of planning, Coubertin organized the 1894 Sorbonne Congress. The congress, which created the IOC, unanimously approved the revival of the Games, with the first to be held in Athens in 1896. Coubertin's philosophy emphasized amateurism, the idea that the struggle was more important than victory, and the potential for sport to promote peace and cross-cultural understanding.

### IOC Presidency and Later Life
Coubertin became the second IOC president in 1896. His tenure saw the early Games struggle for attention, particularly the 1900 Paris and 1904 St. Louis events, which were overshadowed by World's Fairs. He successfully pushed for the 1906 Athens Games to restore prestige. He created the modern pentathlon for the 1912 Olympics and finally achieved his goal of including art competitions. He stepped down after the successful 1924 Paris Games. In his later years, he supported Berlin's bid for the 1936 Games and died in 1937, having depleted his personal wealth to fund the Olympic movement.

## Terms
*   **International Olympic Committee (IOC):** The governing body of the Olympic Movement, founded in 1894 to organize the modern Olympic Games.
*   **Amateurism:** The principle, central to Coubertin's vision, that athletes should compete for honor rather than financial reward.
*   **Triple Unity:** Coubertin's educational ideal, inspired by ancient Greek gymnasia, promoting unity between young and old, different academic disciplines, and theoretical and practical work.
*   **Modern Pentathlon:** A multi-discipline sports event created by Coubertin for the 1912 Olympics, simulating the skills of a 19th-century cavalry soldier.
*   **Citius, Altius, Fortius:** The traditional Olympic motto ("Faster, Higher, Stronger"), proposed by Coubertin in 1894.
*   **Comité Jules Simon:** A French committee founded by Coubertin in 1888 to promote physical exercise.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Ancient Olympic Amateurism:** Scholars debate whether ancient Olympic athletes were amateurs or professionals. Coubertin believed they were amateurs until about 480 BC, a view contested by historians like David C. Young.
*   **Class and Control:** Critics argue that the modern amateur ideal was a tool for upper-class control over sport, excluding working-class athletes. Defenders suggest Coubertin was unaware of these class repercussions.
*   **Historical Accuracy:** Coubertin's romanticized vision of the ancient Games (e.g., the importance of participation over winning, the Games as a direct impetus for peace) is considered by scholars to differ significantly from the historical record.
*   **Role in 1896 Games:** The extent of Coubertin's practical involvement in planning the 1896 Athens Games is questioned, with some accounts suggesting he played a minor logistical role.

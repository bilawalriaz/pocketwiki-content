# Sea of Japan

## Overview
The Sea of Japan is a marginal sea of the Pacific Ocean, enclosed by the Japanese archipelago, Sakhalin, the Korean Peninsula, and the Russian Far East. Its near-total enclosure creates a distinct marine environment with unique hydrological and ecological characteristics. The sea is a vital region for fisheries and regional shipping, though its name is the subject of an ongoing international dispute.

## Timeline
- **Early Miocene** — The Japan Arc begins to form; the Sea of Japan starts to open.
- **Late Miocene** — Orogenesis (mountain-building) begins in the northeastern Japanese archipelago.
- **c. 2.6 million years ago** — Formation of the Tsugaru and Tsushima straits interrupts elephant migration to Japan.
- **c. 60,000–11,000 years ago** — La Pérouse Strait forms, closing the path for mammoths to Hokkaidō.
- **1733–1743** — Russian expeditions map Sakhalin and the Japanese islands.
- **1780s** — Jean-François de Lapérouse travels through the strait later named for him.
- **1803–1806** — Adam Johann von Krusenstern explores the sea during a global voyage.
- **1847–1892** — American, Canadian, and French whaling ships operate extensively in the sea.
- **1928** — The International Hydrographic Organization (IHO) first publishes *Limits of Oceans and Seas* (S-23), using the name "Japan Sea."
- **2020** — The IHO decides to develop a new digital standard (S-130) to replace geographic names in S-23 with unique identifiers.

## Body

### Names and Naming Dispute
The dominant English name is "Sea of Japan." Other names include Nihon kai (Japan) and Donghae (East Sea, South Korea). An ongoing dispute exists between Japan, which asserts "Sea of Japan" has been the international standard since the early 19th century, and Korea, which contends "East Sea" was historically used before Japanese colonial rule. The IHO's 1928 publication S-23 used "Japan Sea," but it has not been updated since 1953. In 2020, the IHO began developing a new digital standard (S-130) that will replace geographic names with unique identifiers.

### Geography and Geology
The sea is bounded by Russia and Sakhalin to the north, Korea to the west, and Japan to the east and south. It is connected to other seas by five shallow straits (under 100 m depth), which hinder water exchange and isolate its marine life. The sea has a surface area of about 1,050,000 km², a mean depth of 1,752 m, and a maximum depth of 4,568 m. It contains three major basins: the deep Japan Basin in the north, and the shallower Yamato and Tsushima Basins in the south. The seabed varies from continental shelves near the coasts to oceanic sediments in the deep basins.

### Climate and Hydrology
The sea's climate features strong monsoons. A winter north-western monsoon brings cold, dry air, causing strong evaporation and heavy snowfall on Japan's western coasts. The northern part freezes for 4–5 months annually. Ocean currents circulate counterclockwise: warm, saline currents (like the Tsushima Current) enter from the south, while cold, fresh currents flow south along the Asian coast. The water forms distinct layers, with a stable cold layer at about 1,000 m depth. Salinity averages 34.09‰, slightly lower than the open ocean. Tides are complex and generally low in amplitude, except in the funnel-shaped Strait of Tartary.

### Flora, Fauna, and Economy
High dissolved oxygen levels support rich biodiversity, with over 3,500 animal species, including fish like saury, mackerel, and salmon, as well as seals and whales. The shallow straits prevent deep-water oceanic fauna from inhabiting the sea. Fishing is the dominant economic activity, focusing on species like herring, sardines, and squid. Shipping intensity is moderate due to political tensions but is increasing with East Asian economic growth. Key ports include Niigata (Japan), Busan (South Korea), and Vladivostok (Russia).

## Terms
- **Marginal sea:** A sea partially enclosed by islands, archipelagos, or peninsulas.
- **Orogenesis:** The process of mountain-building through tectonic plate movements.
- **Hemipelagic sediments:** Marine sediments composed of both terrigenous (land-derived) and biogenic material.
- **Pelagic sediments:** Deep-ocean sediments composed mainly of the shells of microscopic organisms.
- **Continental shelf:** The gently sloping submerged edge of a continent.
- **Salinity:** The concentration of dissolved salts in water, measured in parts per thousand (‰).
- **Von Kármán vortices:** A repeating pattern of swirling vortices caused by the unsteady separation of flow of a fluid around blunt bodies.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The primary debate is the **naming dispute** between Japan ("Sea of Japan") and Korea ("East Sea"). The IHO's move to a digital standard with unique identifiers may sidestep the political issue.
- There is geological controversy over whether, during ice ages, falling sea levels completely dried the straits, turning the sea into a **landlocked freshwater lake**.

# Skin

## Overview
Skin is the soft, flexible outer tissue covering vertebrate animals, functioning as the primary interface with the environment. It serves three main roles: protection against pathogens and water loss, regulation of temperature and fluid balance, and sensation via nerve endings. In mammals, skin is a complex organ of the integumentary system composed of an outer epidermis (waterproof barrier), a deeper dermis (connective tissue, glands, nerves), and an underlying subcutaneous tissue (fat storage, insulation). Skin structure varies significantly across vertebrate classes—fish and amphibians possess permeable, glandular skin often used for respiration, while reptiles and birds have keratinized, scaled or feathered epidermis to prevent desiccation. Skin also forms extraskeletal structures such as horns, antlers, and osteoderms. Fossil evidence indicates skin existed at least 289 million years ago in early reptiles.

## Timeline
- **c. 289 million years ago** — Oldest known fossilized skin discovered (reported 11 Jan 2024), possibly from an ancient reptile.
- **Embryogenesis** — Epidermis splits into periderm (lost) and basal layer (stem cell source for life).
- **Post-embryonic** — Hair, feathers, claws, nails develop from epidermal placodes via reaction-diffusion patterning (Sonic hedgehog / BMP).
- **Adulthood** — Continuous keratinocyte turnover: basal mitosis → differentiation → desquamation (~28-day cycle in humans).
- **Aging** — TGF-β blocks fibroblast-to-fat-cell conversion; photoaging accelerates wrinkles, laxity, malignancies.

## Body

### Structure in Mammals
Mammalian skin comprises two primary layers. The **epidermis** is an avascular, stratified squamous epithelium composed of 95% keratinocytes, plus melanocytes, Langerhans cells, and Merkel cells. It provides waterproofing and infection barrier. Keratinocytes proliferate in the *stratum basale*, migrate upward through *spinosum*, *granulosum*, *lucidum* (palms/soles only), and *corneum*, differentiating into anucleated, keratin-filled corneocytes linked by desmosomes, then shed (desquamation). The **dermis** lies beneath, separated by a **basement membrane** (controls cell/molecule traffic, stores cytokines/growth factors). The dermis is connective tissue rich in collagen, elastic fibers, and proteoglycans (hyaluronan, versican, decorin throughout; biglycan, perlecan epidermal only), providing tensile strength and elasticity. It houses mechanoreceptors, hair follicles, sweat/sebaceous/apocrine glands, lymphatics, and blood vessels nourishing both layers. The dermis has a superficial **papillary region** (loose areolar tissue, dermal papillae interlocking with epidermis) and a deep **reticular region** (dense irregular connective tissue, hair roots, glands, nails). The **subcutaneous tissue (hypodermis)** is not skin proper; it attaches skin to muscle/bone, contains 50% body fat (adipocytes), and supplies vessels/nerves. Skin flora (e.g., *Staphylococcus epidermidis*) recolonizes from hair follicles after disinfection.

### Structure in Fish, Amphibians, Birds, and Reptiles
**Fish/Amphibians:** Epidermis is live cells with minimal superficial keratin; permeable, often a major respiratory organ (amphibians). Dermis lacks tetrapod connective tissue; bony fish have dermal bony scales (lost in tetrapods); cartilaginous fish have tooth-like denticles. Glands: fish have mucus cells (insulation, protection, poison, photophores); amphibians have mucous glands (lubrication, pH, thermoregulation, adhesion, antimicrobial) and granular (venomous) glands. Granular glands are alveolar, clustered, with developed intercalary region (muscular control of duct lumen) and three-layered alveolus (tunica fibrosa, spongy intermediate, tunica propria). Mucous glands cover entire body, simpler structure (epithelium + connective tissue), helical duct lining. Skin color derives from dermal chromatophores (melanin, guanine, carotenoids), not epidermal melanin; some species (chameleons, flounders) change color via chromatophore size adjustment. **Birds/Reptiles:** Epidermis resembles mammals with surface stratum corneum of dead keratinocytes reducing water loss; intermediate layers less distinct. Few glands: reptile pheromone cells, avian uropygial (preen) gland. Unique epidermal derivatives: hair (mammals), feathers (birds), scales (reptiles, pangolins—different origin than fish scales).

### Development
Cutaneous structures (hair, feathers, claws, nails) arise from epidermis. During embryogenesis, epidermis splits into periderm (lost) and basal layer (lifelong stem cell source). Basal layer maintenance requires autocrine TGF-α and paracrine FGF7 (keratinocyte growth factor) from dermis; over-expression in mice causes hypergranulosis and thick skin. Appendage patterning uses a reaction-diffusion system: activator Sonic hedgehog (Shh) and inhibitors BMP4/BMP2 create regular cell clusters. Shh-expressing epidermal cells induce mesodermal condensation; mesodermal clusters signal back to specify structure type. BMPs from epidermis inhibit nearby placode formation. Transplantation experiments (frog/newt) show mesodermal signals are conserved across species, but epidermal response is species-specific: mesoderm instructs position, epidermis executes structure identity.

### Functions
- **Protection:** Anatomical barrier; Langerhans cells (adaptive immunity).
- **Sensation:** Nerve endings detect heat, cold, touch, pressure, vibration, injury (nociceptors, thermoreceptors).
- **Thermoregulation:** Eccrine sweat glands and vasodilation lose heat; vasoconstriction conserves heat; erector pili muscles adjust hair angle to alter insulation.
- **Evaporation control:** Semi-impermeable barrier reduces fluid loss.
- **Storage/synthesis:** Lipids, water; vitamin D/folate production.
- **Absorption:** O₂, N₂, CO₂ diffuse into outer 0.25–0.40 mm (external O₂ supply; negligible total respiration); transdermal drug delivery.
- **Water resistance:** Epidermis/sebum prevent nutrient/oil washout.
- **Camouflage:** Color/pattern via fur, scales, feathers conceals from predators/prey.

### Mechanics
Skin exhibits a **J-curve stress-strain response**: large strain at low stress due to collagen fibril straightening/reorientation. Skin may be pre-stretched (like wetsuits) or under compression regionally. Small punched holes widen, close into ellipses, or shrink circularly depending on local pre-stress.

### Aging
Homeostasis declines as stem/progenitor cells fail to self-renew/differentiate. TGF-β blocks dermal fibroblast conversion to supportive fat cells. Manifestations: wrinkles, discoloration, laxity, malignancies. **Photoaging** (sun exposure) exacerbates all features.

## Terms
- ****Epidermis**** — Outermost skin layer; avascular stratified squamous epithelium (95% keratinocytes) providing waterproofing and barrier function.
- ****Dermis**** — Connective tissue layer beneath epidermis; contains collagen/elastic fibers, glands, hair follicles, nerves, blood vessels; divided into papillary and reticular regions.
- ****Basement membrane**** — Thin fibrous sheet separating epidermis and dermis; controls molecular traffic and stores cytokines/growth factors for repair.
- ****Keratinocyte**** — Principal epidermal cell (95%); produces keratin, forms desmosomes, differentiates from basal layer to anucleated corneocyte.
- ****Stratum basale**** — Deepest epidermal layer (stratum germinativum); site of keratinocyte mitosis and stem cell reservoir.
- ****Stratum corneum**** — Outermost epidermal layer; dead, anucleated, keratin-filled corneocytes providing barrier; continuously shed (desquamation).
- ****Subcutaneous tissue (hypodermis)**** — Loose connective tissue and fat (50% body fat) below dermis; attaches skin to muscle/bone, insulates, supplies vessels/nerves.
- ****Chromatophore**** — Pigment-containing cell in dermis (melanin, guanine, carotenoids) responsible for skin color in fish, amphibians, reptiles.
- ****Granular gland**** — Alveolar amphibian skin gland producing venom/toxins; clustered, with muscular intercalary region controlling secretion.
- ****Reaction-diffusion system**** — Patterning mechanism (activator Shh + inhibitors BMP2/4) generating regular spacing of hair/feather placodes during development.

## Debates and open questions
- **Mesoderm vs. epidermis in patterning:** While mesoderm defines position and epidermis executes structure, the precise molecular dialogue and evolutionary conservation of specific signals remain under investigation.
- **Stem cell dynamics in aging:** Mechanisms by which TGF-β blocks fibroblast-to-adipocyte conversion and how stem/progenitor cell exhaustion drives homeostasis failure are not fully resolved.
- **Fossil skin identity:** The 289-million-year-old fossil skin (reported 2024) is "possibly" reptilian; taxonomic assignment and implications for early amniote integument evolution are pending further study.
- **Mechanical pre-stress variation:** Regional differences in skin pre-stress (tension vs. compression) and their developmental origins are incompletely mapped.

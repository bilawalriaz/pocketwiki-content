# Michael Collins (Irish leader)

## Overview
Michael Collins (1890–1922) was a central figure in the Irish struggle for independence from Britain. He served as a guerrilla leader during the War of Independence, a key negotiator of the Anglo-Irish Treaty, and the first chairman of the Provisional Government of the Irish Free State. His assassination during the Irish Civil War made him a pivotal and tragic figure in the foundation of the modern Irish state.

- **22 August 1922** — Killed in an ambush at Béal na Bláth, County Cork.

## Body

### Early Life and Revolutionary Beginnings
Born in County Cork, Collins moved to London in 1906 to work as a clerk. There, he joined the Gaelic Athletic Association and the Irish Republican Brotherhood (IRB), a secret republican society. He returned to Ireland in 1916 to fight in the Easter Rising, an armed insurrection in Dublin. After the Rising's suppression, he was imprisoned but released in December 1916. In the power vacuum left by the execution of the Rising's leaders, Collins emerged as a key organiser, criticising the military tactics of 1916 and planning for a future conflict.

### War of Independence and Intelligence Leadership
Following the 1918 election, Sinn Féin MPs formed the First Dáil, declaring an Irish Republic. Collins was elected as an MP and appointed Minister for Finance, where he organised a successful "National Loan" to fund the new government. Simultaneously, he became the IRA's Director of Intelligence. He orchestrated a guerrilla campaign against British forces, using a vast spy network and a dedicated assassination unit known as "The Squad." His strategy focused on targeted attacks to cripple British administration, avoiding the large-scale confrontations of 1916. The conflict escalated with British reprisals, including the deployment of the "Black and Tans."

### The Anglo-Irish Treaty and Partition
A truce in July 1921 led to peace talks in London. Collins, despite his reluctance, was part of the Irish delegation. The resulting Anglo-Irish Treaty, signed in December 1921, established the Irish Free State as a dominion within the British Empire, not the independent republic many had fought for. It required an oath of allegiance to the British Crown and confirmed the partition of Ireland, creating Northern Ireland. Collins defended the Treaty as "the freedom to achieve freedom," arguing it was a pragmatic step. The Treaty split the nationalist movement, with Éamon de Valera and a significant portion of the IRA opposing it.

### Provisional Government and Civil War
In January 1922, Collins became Chairman of the Provisional Government, tasked with implementing the Treaty and establishing the Free State. He worked to draft a constitution while attempting to prevent a civil war. However, tensions with anti-Treaty IRA forces, who occupied the Four Courts in Dublin, escalated. Under British pressure, Free State forces attacked the Four Courts in June 1922, sparking the Irish Civil War. Collins, as commander-in-chief of the new National Army, led pro-Treaty forces against his former comrades. He was killed in an ambush by anti-Treaty republicans in his native Cork in August 1922.

## Terms
*   **Irish Republican Brotherhood (IRB):** A secret oath-bound society dedicated to establishing an independent Irish republic through physical force.
*   **Sinn Féin:** The political party that won the 1918 election and whose MPs formed the First Dáil, declaring Irish independence.
*   **Dáil Éireann:** The parliament of the declared Irish Republic, first convened in January 1919.
*   **Anglo-Irish Treaty:** The 1921 agreement that ended the War of Independence and established the Irish Free State as a British dominion.
*   **Provisional Government:** The temporary government of the Irish Free State established in 1922 to implement the Treaty, led by Collins.
*   **National Army:** The military force of the Irish Free State, formed from pro-Treaty IRA units and used to fight in the Civil War.
*   **Plenipotentiary:** A person granted full authority to negotiate and sign a treaty on behalf of their government.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Collins's Role in the Treaty:** Scholars debate whether Collins was a pragmatic realist who secured the best possible deal or whether he was outmanoeuvred by the British. His statement that the Treaty was "the freedom to achieve freedom" is central to this debate.
*   **Responsibility for the Civil War:** The decision to attack the Four Courts is a key point of contention. Some historians argue that Arthur Griffith and the British forced the issue, while others suggest Collins ultimately consented to military action.
*   **Collins's Plans for Northern Ireland:** His private and public statements on partition appear contradictory. He publicly accepted the Treaty's provisions while privately supporting IRA operations in the North and telling associates the Treaty "can go to hell" if political efforts to end partition failed.
*   **The Circumstances of His Death:** The exact details of the ambush at Béal na Bláth remain unclear, with no official inquiry ever held. The identity of the fatal shot is traditionally attributed to Denis "Sonny" O'Neill, but this is not definitively proven.

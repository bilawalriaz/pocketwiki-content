# Rodent

## Overview  
Rodents (order Rodentia) are the largest group of mammals, comprising about 40% of all mammal species. They are defined by a single pair of continuously growing incisors in each jaw, which they wear down by gnawing. Rodents inhabit every continent except Antarctica and thrive in diverse environments, from forests to human settlements. Their ecological roles include seed dispersal and habitat engineering, while some species are significant pests and disease vectors.

## Timeline  
- **Paleocene (c. 66 mya)**: Earliest fossil record of mammals with characteristic rodent dentition appears in Laurasia, shortly after the extinction of the non-avian dinosaurs some 66 million years ago.  
- **Eocene**: Rodents greatly diversified, spreading across continents and reaching both South America and Madagascar from Africa.  
- **Late Eocene**: Hystricognaths invaded Africa, most probably having originated in Asia at least 39.5 million years ago; caviomorphs had arrived in South America by 41 million years ago.  
- **Early Oligocene**: The Castoridae, which includes modern beavers, first appeared in North America in the late Eocene and colonized Eurasia via the Bering Land Bridge in the early Oligocene.  
- **20 mya**: Fossils recognizably belonging to the current families, such as Muridae, could be found.  
- **Miocene–Pliocene**: Species include the Hydromyini, 'old endemics', the first wave of rodents to colonize Australia in the Miocene and early Pliocene, and the true rat (Rattus), 'new endemics', arriving in a subsequent wave in the late Pliocene or early Pleistocene.  
- **5 mya**: Rodents participated in the Great American Interchange that resulted from the joining of the Americas by the formation of the Isthmus of Panama, around 5 million years ago in the Piacenzian age.  
- **1821**: The use of the order name "Rodentia" is attributed to the English traveler and naturalist Thomas Edward Bowdich.  
- **1961**: Guinea pigs have been launched into orbital space flight several times—first by the USSR on the Sputnik 9 biosatellite of 9 March 1961, with a successful recovery.  
- **2016**: The Bramble Cay melomys was the first mammal species to be declared extinct as a consequence of human-caused climate change.

## Body  

### Characteristics  
Rodents are defined by their continuously growing incisors, which have enamel only on the front surface. The rear wears faster, creating a chisel-like edge. They possess 12–28 teeth, no canines, and a diastema separating incisors from cheek teeth. The masseter muscle plays a key role in chewing, making up 60% – 80% of the total muscle mass among masticatory muscles. Three main jaw types—sciuromorphous (squirrels), myomorphous (rats), and hystricomorphous (guinea pigs)—reflect dietary and behavioral adaptations. Most rodents are small (<100g), though capybaras reach 66kg. Limbs vary: some are plantigrade walkers, others are digitigrade runners or bipedal hoppers. Tails range from prehensile to vestigial.

### Distribution and Habitat  
Rodents occupy nearly all terrestrial habitats, excluding Antarctica. They were the only non-human placental mammals to reach Australia naturally. Burrowing species aerate soil and disperse fungal spores, supporting forest health. Prairie dogs and beavers act as keystone species, shaping grasslands and wetlands through their activities.

### Behavior and Life History  
Most rodents are herbivorous, though many opportunistically consume insects or small animals. Food caching is common, especially among squirrels and kangaroo rats. Social structures vary widely: prairie dogs live in large colonies with complex vocal communication, while others like hamsters are solitary. Mating systems include monogamy (beavers), polygyny (ground squirrels), and promiscuity (prairie dogs). Young are either altricial (mice) or precocial (guinea pigs). Parental care includes nursing, grooming, and alloparenting in social species. Infanticide occurs in some populations, often linked to resource competition or male reproductive strategies.

### Communication  
Rodents use multiple sensory channels. Scent marking via urine and glandular secretions conveys identity, reproductive status, and kinship, mediated partly by MHC genes. Vocalizations range from ultrasonic calls in mice to complex alarm systems in prairie dogs, which encode predator type and urgency. Visual signals include UV-reflective urine marks. Seismic communication via foot-drumming warns of predators.

### Evolutionary History  
Rodents evolved from a Paleocene ancestor shared with lagomorphs (Glires clade). They rapidly diversified in the Eocene, colonizing continents through rafting and land bridges. Giant extinct forms like Josephoartigasia monesi (3m, 1000kg) existed until 2 mya. Molecular clocks suggest origins possibly in the late Cretaceous, though fossil evidence supports post-dinosaur emergence. Classification remains debated due to convergent evolution; traditional suborders (Sciuromorpha, Myomorpha, Hystricomorpha) are supplemented by molecular data, with 34 families and over 2,200 species recognized as of 2021.

### Conservation  
168 rodent species face threats, primarily habitat loss and invasive predators. Phylogenetic diversity is at risk since 76% of genera contain only one species. Island rodents are especially vulnerable to climate change and introduced species. Eradication programs using anticoagulants have successfully restored seabird populations on islands like Lundy.

### Interaction with Humans  
Rodents serve as food (guinea pigs, squirrels), pets (hamsters, gerbils), and research models (mice, rats). They provide fur (beaver, chinchilla) and have been used in disease detection (tuberculosis-sniffing rats). However, they are major agricultural pests and disease vectors, transmitting plague, hantavirus, and Lyme disease. Integrated pest management combines trapping, habitat modification, and biological controls.

## Terms  
- **Diastema**: Gap between incisors and cheek teeth in rodents.  
- **Masseter muscle**: Primary jaw muscle in rodents, crucial for gnawing.  
- **Sciuromorphous**: Jaw structure with large deep masseter, seen in squirrels.  
- **Hystricognathous**: Jaw type with enlarged pterygoid muscles, found in guinea pigs.  
- **Altricial**: Underdeveloped newborn, typical of mice and squirrels.  
- **Precocial**: Well-developed newborn, seen in guinea pigs and porcupines.  
- **Eusocial**: Social structure with reproductive division of labor, as in naked mole-rats.  
- **Coprophagy**: Re-ingestion of feces to maximize nutrient absorption.  
- **Baculum**: Penis bone present in many rodent species.  
- **Glires**: Clade uniting rodents and lagomorphs based on shared ancestry.

## Debates and Open Questions  
- **Origins**: Molecular clocks suggest late Cretaceous origins, conflicting with Paleocene fossil evidence.  
- **Multituberculate replacement**: Whether rodents outcompeted multituberculates remains debated.  
- **Metacognition**: Claims of self-awareness in rats are disputed, possibly explained by simpler conditioning.  
- **Classification**: Morphological vs. molecular approaches yield differing suborder arrangements.  
- **Monogamy evolution**: Facultative vs. obligate monogamy in prairie voles lacks consensus on evolutionary drivers.  
- **Climate extinction**: Long-term impacts of warming on montane and island rodent populations are uncertain.

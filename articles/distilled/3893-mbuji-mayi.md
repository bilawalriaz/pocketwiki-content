# Mbuji-Mayi

## Overview
Mbuji-Mayi is the capital of Kasai-Oriental Province in the Democratic Republic of Congo and is considered the country's second-largest city. Its existence and growth are fundamentally tied to its location atop one of the world's largest industrial diamond deposits, making it a critical economic hub despite its remote location and severe infrastructure challenges.

## Timeline
- **1907** — Diamonds first discovered in the area.
- **1913** — True value of the diamond find recognized; mining camp developed.
- **8 August 1960** — Albert Kalonji declares the secessionist Mining State of South Kasai, with Bakwanga (Mbuji-Mayi) as its capital.
- **1 January 1961** — Bakwanga renamed Mbuji-Mayi to signify Luba reconciliation.
- **December 1961** — Central government troops (ANC) take control of the town and arrest Kalonji.
- **4 October 1962** — ANC forces retake Bakwanga, ending the region's independence.
- **1963** — MIBA produces 80% of the world's industrial diamonds; smuggling eclipses official production.
- **1986** — Jonas Mukamba Kadiata Nzemba appointed CEO of MIBA, becoming the region's de facto governor.
- **4 April 1997** — City falls to rebels during the First Congo War.
- **October 1998** — City occupied by Zimbabwean and Chadian troops supporting the DRC government.
- **2015** — Tuberculosis outbreak investigated in the overcrowded Mbuji-Mayi Central Prison.
- **2020** — DRC government launches a $26.2 million project to improve the city's drinking water system.

## Body

### Origins and Colonial Control
The region was a cluster of villages on Bakwanga clan land until diamonds were discovered in 1907. The mining company Societé minière de Bakwanga (MIBA) developed a planned mining camp, strictly controlling growth, movement, and residence to protect its resources. This resulted in slow, company-focused development with limited infrastructure or higher education for the local population until independence.

### Mineral Wealth and the Company Town
Mbuji-Mayi is built on massive diamond deposits, estimated in the 1950s to contain at least 300 million karats. MIBA's dominance defined the city; its operations sometimes required demolishing buildings to access diamonds. While MIBA controlled major mining, diamonds were also easily found in streams. By 1963, MIBA was the source of 80% of the world's industrial diamonds, but smuggling was rampant, with unofficial exports far exceeding official production.

### Secession and Post-Independence Turmoil
Following Congolese independence in 1960, the city grew rapidly with Luba immigration. It became the capital of the secessionist Mining State of South Kasai under Albert Kalonji until central government forces ended the secession in 1962. The city was renamed Mbuji-Mayi in 1961 to promote ethnic reconciliation.

### The Nzemba Era and State Neglect
During the 1980s and 1990s, the central government neglected Mbuji-Mayi. MIBA stepped in, funding infrastructure, utilities, and a university. Its CEO, Jonas Nzemba, became the region's most powerful figure, balancing ties to President Mobutu and opposition leader Étienne Tshisekedi. Despite this, infrastructure remained poor, with spotty electricity and minimal paved roads, leading to deforestation from charcoal use.

### Civil War and Modern Challenges
The city was looted during the First Congo War in 1997. MIBA was forced to make "voluntary contributions" to the new government. The city faced foreign occupation in 1998. In the 21st century, critical issues persist, including a severe water crisis with residents using river water, leading to disease outbreaks. A major government water project was launched in 2020. The city's economy remains centered on the diamond trade, but it is plagued by smuggling and human rights issues, including killings of illegal miners and severe prison conditions.

## Terms
- **Communes**: Municipalities; Mbuji-Mayi is divided into five, each led by a burgomaster.
- **MIBA (Societé minière de Bakwanga)**: The Belgian-founded mining company that controlled diamond extraction and heavily influenced the city's development.
- **Industrial diamonds**: Diamonds used primarily for industrial purposes (e.g., cutting, grinding) rather than jewelry.
- **Karat**: A unit of weight for gemstones, equal to 0.2 grams.
- **Secessionist state**: A region that declares independence from a larger country; South Kasai attempted this from 1960-1962.
- **ANC (Armée Nationale Congolaise)**: The national army of the central Congolese government.
- **Luba**: A major ethnic group in south-central Congo, from which the city's population largely derives.
- **Tshiluba**: The local language from which "Mbuji-Mayi" (Goat-Water) is derived.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The city's exact population is unknown, with estimates ranging from 1.48 million (2010) to 3.5 million (2008).
- The extent of diamond smuggling versus official production has been a persistent issue since independence, with black market activity often eclipsing legal exports.
- The role and power of MIBA and its leaders, like Jonas Nzemba, in governing the region in the absence of a strong state remains a key historical dynamic.

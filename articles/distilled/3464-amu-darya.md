# Amu Darya

## Overview
The Amu Darya, historically known as the Oxus, is a major river in Central Asia. It flows through Afghanistan, Tajikistan, Turkmenistan, and Uzbekistan, originating in the Pamir Mountains and historically emptying into the Aral Sea. Its waters are vital for the region's agriculture and ecology, but extensive irrigation diversions, particularly since the Soviet era, have caused the river to no longer reach the Aral Sea, contributing significantly to its shrinkage.

## Timeline
- **c. 3000 BCE** — A river valley civilization existed in the watershed.
- **Ancient times** — The river was regarded as the boundary between Greater Iran and Turan.
- **c. 985 AD** — The Gurganj Dam began diverting water from the Uzboy distributary to the Aral Sea.
- **1221** — Genghis Khan's troops destroyed the Gurganj Dam.
- **18th century** — The river's flow shifted north, primarily into the Aral Sea.
- **1839** — John Wood's expedition identified Lake Zorkul as the source.
- **1960s-1970s** — The Soviet Union began large-scale irrigation diversions for cotton fields.
- **1970s** — The Caspian tiger was last seen along the river's banks.
- **1990s** — The Soviet Union fell, ending the regional water-sharing system.
- **2007** — Bill Colegrave's expedition identified the Chelab stream as the likely true source.
- **2022** — Construction began on Afghanistan's Qosh Tepa Canal to divert Amu Darya water.
- **2023** — A $720 million gas extraction deal was signed for the Afghan side of the basin. |
- **2025** — An expedition identified Lake Malang as the true source of the Chelab stream and thus the Amu Darya.

## Body

### Names and Identification
The river's classical name, Ōxus, derives from Vakhsh, its largest tributary. The modern name "Amu" comes from the medieval city of Āmul. In medieval Arabic sources, it is called Jeyhoun, derived from the biblical Gihon. Historically, it was also known as the Gozan River.

### Course and Source
The Amu Darya is formed by the confluence of the Vakhsh and Panj rivers. It flows northwest for 2,400 km, forming parts of Afghanistan's borders with Tajikistan, Uzbekistan, and Turkmenistan. The river's water comes almost entirely from glaciers in the Pamir and Tian Shan mountains. The true source was long debated. A 2025 expedition determined it to be Lake Malang, a glacial lake at 4,951 m altitude, which feeds the Chelab stream, the parent stream of the river system.

### Drainage Basin and Hydrology
The river's drainage basin covers 534,739 km². About 61% lies within Tajikistan, Uzbekistan, and Turkmenistan, with 39% in Afghanistan. Only about 200,000 km² actively contribute water, as many tributaries are diverted and the lowlands are arid. The mean discharge is about 97.4 cubic km per year.

### Historical Course Changes
Historical records indicate the river's course has shifted over millennia, flowing into the Aral Sea, the Caspian Sea via the Uzboy distributary, or both. From the 13th to late 16th centuries, it flowed to both seas. In the 18th century, it shifted to flow primarily into the Aral Sea.

### Modern Use and Environmental Impact
Since the 1960s, massive Soviet irrigation projects diverted the river's water for cotton cultivation, which is a major cause of the Aral Sea's shrinkage. After the Soviet Union's collapse, the regional water-sharing system collapsed. In 2022, Afghanistan began building the Qosh Tepa Canal to divert water, raising concerns from downstream countries like Uzbekistan about further environmental damage and agricultural impact.

### Resource Extraction
In 2023, a Chinese company signed a deal with the Taliban government for gas extraction in the Afghan part of the basin, which a 2019 study listed as the world's third-largest potential gas field. The deal was terminated in 2025.

## Terms
- **Confluence**: The junction where two or more rivers meet to form a single channel.
- **Drainage Basin**: The area of land where all water drains into a particular river.
- **Distributary**: A stream that branches off and flows away from a main river channel.
- **Irrigation Diversion**: The artificial redirection of river water for agricultural use.
- **Glacial Lake**: A lake formed by the melting of a glacier.
- **Transoxiana**: The historical name for the region between the Amu Darya and Syr Darya rivers.
- **Basmachi Movement**: An armed resistance movement against Soviet rule in Central Asia in the 1920s and 1930s.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **True Source**: The exact source of the Amu Darya was debated for centuries, with multiple claimants. The 2025 expedition's identification of Lake Malang is a recent determination.
- **Historical Course**: The river's historical course across the Karakum Desert is debated, with evidence suggesting it has shifted between flowing into the Aral Sea, the Caspian Sea, or both.
- **Future of the Aral Sea**: The impact of new diversions like the Qosh Tepa Canal on the already diminished Aral Sea is a major ongoing concern.

# Clovis I

## Overview
Clovis I (c. 466–511) was the first king to unite the Frankish tribes under a single ruler, founding the Merovingian dynasty that would dominate Gaul for over two centuries. His conversion to Nicene (Catholic) Christianity was a pivotal moment, aligning the Franks with the Roman Church and laying the religious and political foundations for what would become France.

## Timeline
- **c. 466** — Born to Childeric I, king of the Salian Franks.
- **481** — Succeeds his father as king.
- **486** — Defeats Syagrius at the Battle of Soissons, ending the last Roman rump state in Gaul.
- **c. 493** — Marries Clotilde, a Burgundian princess and Nicene Christian.
- **496** — Defeats the Alamanni at the Battle of Tolbiac.
- **498–506** — Baptized into Nicene Christianity (Catholicism).
- **507** — Defeats the Visigoths at the Battle of Vouillé, conquering most of Aquitaine.
- **508** — Makes Paris his capital; orchestrates the murders of rival Frankish kings.
- **511** — Dies; his kingdom is partitioned among his four sons.

## Body

### Unification of the Franks
Clovis began his reign as one of several Frankish sub-kings (*reguli*). Through a series of military campaigns and political maneuvering, he systematically eliminated rivals. He first defeated the Roman general Syagrius in 486, then conquered other Frankish kingdoms, the Alamanni, and finally the Visigoths in 507. By 508, after murdering rival kings like Ragnachar and Sigobert, he had united all Frankish tribes under his sole rule.

### Conversion to Catholicism
Born a pagan, Clovis was influenced by his wife Clotilde to convert to Nicene Christianity, which differed from the Arian Christianity practiced by most other Germanic tribes. His baptism, occurring between 498 and 506, was a strategic masterstroke. It won him the support of the Catholic Gallo-Roman clergy and aristocracy, distinguishing him from rival Arian kings and providing a religious justification for his conquests, such as the war against the Visigoths.

### Legal and Administrative Legacy
Under Clovis, the first written codification of Salian Frankish law was created, blending Germanic legal traditions with Roman law and Christian principles. He established Paris as his capital and, shortly before his death, convened the First Council of Orléans (511). This council, attended by 33 bishops, passed decrees that applied equally to Franks and Romans, helping to integrate the conquered Gallo-Roman population.

### Death and Partition
Clovis died in 511 and was buried in Paris, giving the city symbolic importance. His kingdom was divided among his four sons, a practice that inaugurated a tradition of partition and disunity within the Merovingian dynasty. This fragmentation persisted until the dynasty's end in 751.

## Terms
- **Salian Franks**: The specific Frankish tribe from which Clovis descended, which had settled within the Roman Empire.
- **Merovingian dynasty**: The royal dynasty founded by Clovis, named for his supposed ancestor Merovech, which ruled the Franks until 751.
- **Nicene Christianity**: The orthodox Christian faith affirming the Trinity (consubstantiality of Father, Son, and Holy Spirit), later known as Catholicism.
- **Arian Christianity**: A Christian belief system declared heretical, which held that Jesus was a created being subordinate to God the Father. It was prevalent among many Germanic tribes.
- **Regulus**: A diminutive term for a petty king or sub-king, used for Frankish rulers subordinate to a higher king.
- **Salic Law**: The early legal code of the Salian Franks, codified under Clovis, which blended Germanic custom with Roman law.
- **First Council of Orléans (511)**: A church council convened by Clovis that established legal and disciplinary norms, integrating the Frankish monarchy with the Catholic Church.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Date of Baptism**: Traditionally dated to 496, modern scholarship based on Clovis's correspondence suggests it occurred later, around 508.
- **Motives for Conversion**: Scholars debate whether Clovis's conversion was primarily a sincere spiritual decision, a calculated political move to gain Gallo-Roman support, or a combination of both.
- **Historical Sources**: The primary source for Clovis's life is Gregory of Tours, writing in the late 6th century. His chronology is often considered fabricated or schematic, creating uncertainty about precise dates and events.
- **Veneration as a Saint**: Clovis was popularly venerated as a saint in France, particularly in the south, but was never formally canonized by the Pope. The reasons for his cult's promotion and its political uses by later French monarchs are subjects of historical analysis.

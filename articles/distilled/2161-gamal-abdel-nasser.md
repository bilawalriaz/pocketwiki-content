# Gamal Abdel Nasser

## Overview
Gamal Abdel Nasser (1918–1970) was an Egyptian military officer and revolutionary who served as the second president of Egypt from 1954 until his death. He led the 1952 Egyptian Revolution, abolished the monarchy, and became a charismatic symbol of Arab nationalism and anti-imperialism. His presidency was marked by major social reforms, the nationalization of the Suez Canal, and ambitious pan-Arab projects, but also by military defeats and increasing authoritarianism.

## Timeline
- **15 January 1918** — Born in Alexandria, Egypt.
- **23 July 1952** — Leads the Egyptian Revolution, overthrowing King Farouk.
- **18 June 1953** — Monarchy abolished; Republic of Egypt declared.
- **26 July 1956** — Announces nationalization of the Suez Canal Company.
- **29 October 1956** — Suez Crisis begins with Israeli, then British and French, invasion.
- **1 February 1958** — Forms the United Arab Republic (UAR) with Syria.
- **28 September 1961** — UAR dissolves after a Syrian secessionist coup.
- **5 June 1967** — Six-Day War begins; Egypt suffers a major defeat.
- **9 June 1967** — Nasser resigns, but retracts after mass protests.
- **28 September 1970** — Dies of a heart attack in Cairo after an Arab League summit.

## Body

### Early Life and Military Career
Born in Alexandria, Nasser's family moved frequently due to his father's postal work. He became politically active in his youth, joining anti-British protests and the paramilitary Green Shirts. After an initial rejection, he entered the Royal Military Academy in 1937, where he met future allies Abdel Hakim Amer and Anwar Sadat. His experience in the 1948 Arab–Israeli War, where he was besieged at Faluja, deepened his nationalism and desire to overthrow the monarchy.

### The 1952 Revolution and Rise to Power
Nasser founded the secret Association of Free Officers, which launched a successful coup on 23 July 1952. The group initially installed General Mohamed Naguib as a figurehead. After banning political parties and implementing land reform, Nasser sidelined Naguib following a political crisis in early 1954. An assassination attempt by the Muslim Brotherhood in October 1954 allowed Nasser to crush the organization and assume full executive power. He was formally elected president in 1956.

### Suez Crisis and Pan-Arab Leadership
Nasser's nationalization of the Suez Canal in 1956, to fund the Aswan Dam after US and UK funding was withdrawn, triggered the Suez Crisis. Despite a military defeat, international pressure forced the invaders to withdraw, greatly enhancing Nasser's prestige. He adopted a policy of "positive neutralism" in the Cold War, famously securing a major arms deal with Czechoslovakia. His popularity led to the formation of the United Arab Republic with Syria in 1958, though the union collapsed in 1961.

### Domestic Policy and the Six-Day War
Nasser implemented significant socialist reforms, nationalizing industries and expanding social services. His rivalry with his powerful military chief, Amer, created instability. In 1967, amid rising tensions, Nasser closed the Straits of Tiran to Israeli shipping. The ensuing Six-Day War resulted in a catastrophic defeat for Egypt. Nasser resigned but was reinstated after mass demonstrations. He later purged Amer's loyalists from the military.

### Final Years and Legacy
In his final years, Nasser appointed himself prime minister, launched the War of Attrition to reclaim the Sinai, and initiated limited political liberalization. He died of a heart attack in 1970. His legacy is complex: he is revered for advancing social justice, modernization, and Arab dignity, but criticized for authoritarianism, human rights violations, and the military's dominance over civil institutions.

## Terms
- **Pan-Arabism**: An ideology promoting the political, cultural, and social unity of Arab peoples.
- **Positive Neutralism**: A foreign policy of non-alignment with either the US or Soviet bloc during the Cold War, advocated by Nasser.
- **United Arab Republic (UAR)**: A political union between Egypt and Syria from 1958 to 1961.
- **Suez Crisis (Tripartite Aggression)**: The 1956 invasion of Egypt by Israel, the UK, and France following the nationalization of the Suez Canal.
- **Six-Day War**: The June 1967 war in which Israel decisively defeated Egypt, Jordan, and Syria, capturing the Sinai Peninsula, West Bank, and Golan Heights.
- **War of Attrition**: A limited war fought by Egypt against Israel from 1968 to 1970, aiming to reclaim territory lost in 1967.
- **Arab Socialist Union (ASU)**: The sole legal political party in Egypt under Nasser, established in 1962.
- **Free Officers Movement**: The secret military group led by Nasser that executed the 1952 revolution.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Role of Amer**: Scholars debate the extent of Abdel Hakim Amer's independent power and his responsibility for military failures, particularly in 1967.
- **Decision for War in 1967**: Analysis focuses on whether Nasser was maneuvered into war by Amer's actions or made a deliberate, if miscalculated, strategic choice.
- **Economic Legacy**: Assessments vary on whether Nasser's socialist policies laid a foundation for development or created inefficiencies that led to later economic stagnation.
- **Authoritarianism vs. Popular Mandate**: Historians discuss whether Nasser's rule was a necessary phase of state-building or a missed opportunity for democratic development, given his genuine popular support.

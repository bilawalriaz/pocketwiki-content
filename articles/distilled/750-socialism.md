# Socialism

## Overview
Socialism is an economic ideology and political philosophy defined by social ownership of the means of production—encompassing public, cooperative, community, or employee ownership—as an alternative to private ownership. It emerged in the early 19th century as a response to the social dislocation of the Industrial Revolution, evolving from "utopian" visions of ideal communities into a mass political movement shaped by Karl Marx and Friedrich Engels, who framed it as a necessary historical successor to capitalism. By the early 20th century, the movement split into revolutionary communism (aligned with the Soviet model of central planning) and reformist social democracy (pursuing a mixed economy and welfare state via elections). The 1989 collapse of Soviet-bloc regimes discredited the command-economy model, leading many parties to adopt "Third Way" politics or democratic socialism. A resurgence occurred after the 2008 financial crisis, driven by concerns over inequality and ecological crisis.

## Timeline
- **1832** — Pierre Leroux claims first use of term "socialism" in *Le Globe*; associated with Henri de Saint-Simon and utopian socialism.
- **1848** — Marx and Engels publish *The Communist Manifesto*, defining "scientific socialism" on the eve of European revolutions.
- **1872** — Anarchists expelled from the First International, founding the Anti-authoritarian International (St. Imier International).
- **1899** — Australian Labor Party forms world's first socialist government in Colony of Queensland (one week, Dec 1899).
- **1917** — Bolshevik Revolution in Russia; Lenin redefines socialism as a transitional stage between capitalism and communism.
- **1918** — Russian Social Democratic Labour Party renames to All-Russian Communist Party, cementing communism/socialism distinction.
- **1949** — Establishment of the People's Republic of China; state-led industrialization and land redistribution follow.
- **1968** — Vietnam War spawns the New Left, critical of both Soviet authoritarianism and social democratic reformism.
- **1989** — Revolutions across Eastern Europe end communist rule; Soviet Union dissolves in 1991.
- **1990** — São Paulo Forum launched by Brazil's Workers' Party, linking Latin American left parties ("pink tide").
- **2008** — Global financial crisis sparks resurgence in socialist popularity in the 2010s.
- **2013** — Progressive Alliance founded as global network for social-democratic, socialist, and labour parties.

## Body

### Origins and Definitions
The term "socialism" derives from Latin *sociare* (to combine/share). Early 19th-century "utopian socialists"—Saint-Simon (scientific planning), Robert Owen (cooperatives), and Charles Fourier—condemned liberal individualism for ignoring poverty and inequality during the Industrial Revolution. They advocated shared ownership of resources. By the 1860s, "socialism" replaced synonyms like "associationist" and "mutualist." A key early distinction: socialism aimed to socialize production only, while communism aimed to socialize both production and consumption (free access to goods). Marx and Engels used the terms interchangeably; the idea of distinct historical stages (socialism then communism) entered Marxism only after Marx's death, formalized by Lenin in 1917 to justify revolution in economically backward Russia.

### Historical Trajectory
**19th Century:** *The Communist Manifesto* (1848) established "scientific socialism." The First International (1864) fractured over authoritarian vs. libertarian methods (Marx vs. Bakunin/anarchists). The Australian Labor Party briefly held power in 1899, the first socialist government.

**20th Century:** The 1917 Bolshevik Revolution established the USSR, identifying socialism with central planning, state ownership, and single-party rule. The Third International (Comintern) spread this model globally. Meanwhile, reformist social democrats won elections in Europe, building mixed economies with partial nationalization and welfare states (the Nordic model). The 1949 Chinese Revolution introduced a state-led, self-reliant model, later marred by the Great Leap Forward. The 1968 New Left criticized both Soviet bureaucracy and social democratic compromise, favoring decentralized councils or cooperatives. The 1989 revolutions dissolved the Soviet bloc, prompting a neoliberal consensus and "Third Way" politics (abandoning public ownership).

**21st Century:** The 1990 São Paulo Forum coordinated Latin America's "pink tide" (e.g., Venezuela's "Socialism of the 21st Century"). In Europe, mainstream parties suffered "Pasokification" (electoral collapse, e.g., Greece's PASOK fell from 43.9% in 2009 to 4.7% in 2015), creating space for radical left parties (Syriza, Podemos) and left revivals within mainstream parties (Corbyn, Sanders). Few radical parties have won national power; exceptions include Portugal's Socialist Party.

### Major Varieties
*   **Democratic Socialism / Social Democracy:** Both seek economic democracy. *Social democracy* now typically means administering a capitalist mixed economy with a welfare state, progressive taxation, and labor rights (affiliated with Socialist International). *Democratic socialism* formally aims to replace capitalism with a socialist economy, though definitions vary wildly. The split: social democrats humanize capitalism; democratic socialists seek to transcend it.
*   **Marxism-Leninism / Authoritarian Socialism:** Based on vanguard party, democratic centralism, and state ownership. Leninism added "socialism in one country" and the transitional stage concept. Critics (Rosa Luxemburg, Hal Draper) labeled it "socialism from above" (elitist) vs. "socialism from below" (mass democratic). Trotsky opposed Stalin's bureaucracy, advocating decentralized planning and worker control. Many scholars classify the Soviet model as *state capitalism*.
*   **Libertarian Socialism / Anarchism:** Rejects state ownership and wage labor ("wage slavery"). Favors workers' self-management, federalism, and direct democracy (workers' councils, unions). Includes anarcho-syndicalism (general strike, CNT in Spanish Revolution), mutualism (market socialism via labor-credit banks), and council communism. Distinguishes itself from both Leninism and social democracy.
*   **Market Socialism:** Socially owned enterprises (cooperatives or state firms) allocate resources via markets and monetary prices. Profits accrue to society (social dividend) or workers. Models range from the Lange model (neoclassical) to Yugoslavia's worker self-management (post-1953) and China's "socialist market economy" (state commanding heights + private sector, often called state capitalism).
*   **Regional/Religious/Ethical Variants:** *African socialism* (mid-20th c., ~35 countries) roots socialism in traditional communal culture. *Arab socialism* (Michel Aflaq, Ba'athism) fuses pan-Arab nationalism with socialism. *Ethical socialism* grounds advocacy in altruism/morality (official philosophy of many mainstream parties). *Liberal socialism* (Mill, Bernstein, Keynes) targets legal monopolies, supports mixed economy. *Religious socialism* includes Christian socialism and Islamic socialism (Quran/early Medina welfare state as basis).

### Social Movements
Socialism intersects with feminism (Marxist feminism via Engels/Bebel; socialist feminism coined 1972; anarcha-feminism via Goldman), LGBT rights (Fourier on sexual freedom; Carpenter; Hay/Mattachine Society; Soviet decriminalization post-1917), and ecology (Eco-socialism: Marx's "metabolic rift," William Morris, Bookchin; "environmentalism of the poor").

### Economic Theory and Models
**Core Logic:** Production for use-value (utility) rather than exchange-value (profit). Aims to end capitalistic categories (rent, interest, profit, money) via direct allocation in physical units. In a developed socialist economy, balancing inputs/outputs becomes an engineering task.

**Allocation Mechanisms:**
*   *Planned Economy:* Public ownership + economic planning (centralized or decentralized). Barone modeled simultaneous equations for supply/demand. USSR used "command economy" (directives/targets). Kornai identified "soft budget constraints" (enterprises bailed out) causing chronic shortages, hoarding, and repressed inflation (monetary overhang). Trotsky favored decentralized planning to capture tacit local knowledge.
*   *Self-Managed Economy:* Autonomous units (worker cooperatives), decentralized allocation. Goals: eliminate exploitation/alienation. Models: Guild socialism (UK, G.D.H. Cole), Yugoslavia's market socialism (self-management + market allocation), Chile's Project Cybersyn (cybernetics), Participatory Economics (decentralized councils, remuneration for effort/sacrifice), Pat Devine's negotiated coordination.
*   *State-Directed Economy:* State owns "commanding heights" or large industries; technocratic management. UK Labour (Attlee) nationalized coal, rail, steel via compulsory purchase with compensation.
*   *Market Socialism:* Social ownership + market allocation. Mutualism (Proudhon): labor-time equivalence, mutual-credit bank. China/Vietnam: state sector dominance + private commodity production.

**Role of Money:** 19th-century socialists (Marx, Owen, Proudhon, Mill) favored labor vouchers (non-circulating, non-capital). Trotsky argued money must "exhaust its historic mission" before becoming mere bookkeeping receipts.

### Analysis of the Soviet Model
**Achievements (per 1980s official/UN data):** Eradication of class/national oppression, unemployment, hunger, illiteracy. Guaranteed rights to work, rest, education, healthcare, housing, old-age security. High human development: CEE/CIS life expectancy 68 (OECD 75), infant mortality 25/1000 (OECD 13), universal literacy, high tertiary enrollment (2,600 vs 3,550 per 100k). Strong science/tech (40% chemistry PhDs to women in 1960s; leads in energy physics, space, military). Housing: overcrowding not homelessness (15.5 m²/person urban USSR 1990; 15% in communal apartments). No official unemployment; job changers 6–13%/year. GDP per capita (PPP, 1988): USSR $6,304; CEE/CIS avg $6,162 (43% of OECD $14,385; US $20,651).

**Economic Problems (1960s onward):** Shift to "intensive growth" failed; managers preferred routine production. CoCom embargoes blocked tech transfer. "Taut planning" (output targets > capacity) caused resource hoarding, soft budget constraints, chronic shortages, repressed inflation (savings accumulation/black markets). CMEA (Comecon) failed to coordinate specialization or create common planning methodology; little joint ventures/tech transfer. Extensive growth model exhausted; diminishing returns to capital, low innovation, micro-inefficiencies. System in "crisis" by 1980s but not expected to collapse.

### Public Opinion
Significant global support persists. 2018 IPSOS: 50% global agreement socialist values aid progress (range: China 84%, Japan 21%; US 39%). 2021 IEA (UK youth 16–24): 67% want socialist economy, 72% support renationalization. 2023 IPSOS (UK): majority favor public ownership of water (68%), rail (65%), power (63%). US polls: 70% of millennials (2019) would vote socialist; 51% of 18–34 (2021) view socialism positively. 2023 Fraser Institute: ~40% in Canada/UK/Australia view socialism as ideal (US 31%). 2025 Rasmussen/Heartland: 53% of young US voters (18–39) support socialist 2028 candidate; 76% favor nationalizing energy, healthcare, big tech.

### Criticism
*   **Right:** Violates private property rights; enables state oppression (Hayek/Friedman: economic planning = political coercion). Points to mass killings under communist regimes as indictment.
*   **Left (Libertarian/Anarchist):** Central planning cannot maximize economic democracy/self-management (Hahnel). Authoritarian "socialism from above" betrays socialist ideals.
*   **Austrian School (Mises/Hayek):** Economic calculation problem—without market prices for capital goods, rational resource allocation is impossible.
*   **Incentive Critique:** Equal wealth removes material incentive to work, causing stagnation.
*   **Socialist Rebuttal:** Authoritarian crimes are aberrations of specific regimes, not socialism per se; capitalism/colonialism have comparable death tolls. Appeal persists due to inequality, wage labor exploitation, and ecological crisis (Sunkara). Emerging responses: degrowth, eco-socialism, eco-anarchism.

## Terms
- ****Social Ownership**** — Ownership of the means of production by society collectively, taking forms such as public (state), cooperative, community, or employee ownership.
- ****Means of Production**** — The physical, non-human inputs used to produce economic value: factories, machinery, land, infrastructure, raw materials.
- ****Use-Value vs. Exchange-Value**** — *Use-value*: utility of a good for satisfying human wants. *Exchange-value*: market price. Socialism aims to produce for use-value; capitalism produces for exchange-value/profit.
- ****Vanguard Party**** — Leninist concept: a highly organized, disciplined party of professional revolutionaries that leads the working class to revolution.
- ****Democratic Centralism**** — Leninist organizational principle: freedom of discussion, unity of action; decisions binding on all members.
- ****Soft Budget Constraint**** — (Kornai) Enterprises in planned economies face no bankruptcy threat; losses are covered by the state, reducing efficiency incentives.
- ****Monetary Overhang / Repressed Inflation**** — Accumulated household savings due to chronic goods shortages at fixed prices; purchasing power exceeds supply.
- ****Mutualism**** — Anarchist market socialism (Proudhon): individuals/cooperatives own means of production; trade based on labor-time equivalence via mutual-credit bank.
- ****Pasokification**** — Sharp electoral decline of mainstream social democratic parties (named for Greece's PASOK) due to austerity/neoliberal shifts.
- ****Third Way**** — 1990s–2000s political stance: retains welfare/equality goals but abandons public ownership and class-based politics for market-friendly policies.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Reform vs. Revolution:** Can parliamentary reforms accumulate into socialism (reformism), or do they merely stabilize capitalism (Luxemburg, Gorz's "non-reformist reforms")?
*   **Market vs. Plan:** Is market socialism a stable, efficient alternative to capitalism, or does it reproduce capitalist contradictions (profit motive, inequality)? Can planning be decentralized enough to process dispersed knowledge (Hayek/Trotsky vs. Barone/Lange)?
*   **State Role:** Is the state a necessary instrument for transition (Lassalle, Lenin) or an inherently oppressive structure to be abolished immediately (anarchists, libertarian socialists)?
*   **Definition of Socialism:** Does "socialism" require the abolition of wage labor and markets, or does the Nordic model/social democracy qualify? The term's shift from Soviet model to democratic socialism remains contested.
*   **Incentives & Calculation:** Can a socialist economy solve the incentive problem without material inequality? Can non-market allocation achieve rational efficiency (socialist calculation debate)?
*   **Historical Legacy:** Are 20th-century communist regimes' crimes intrinsic to Marxist theory/Leninism, or contingent outcomes of backwardness/war/authoritarianism?
*   **Ecological Compatibility:** Is socialism inherently productivist (dominating nature) or can it realize Marx's "metabolic rift" repair via eco-socialism/degrowth?

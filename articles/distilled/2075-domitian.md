# Domitian

## Overview
Domitian (r. 81–96) was the last emperor of the Flavian dynasty, known for his efficient but autocratic rule. His reign saw significant economic, military, and building programs, but his authoritarian style and conflict with the Senate led to his assassination and a posthumous condemnation of his memory. Modern revisionism often portrays him as a ruthless but effective administrator whose policies laid the groundwork for the peaceful 2nd century.

## Timeline
- **24 October 51** — Domitian born in Rome.
- **1 July 69** — Vespasian proclaimed emperor during the Year of the Four Emperors.
- **21 December 69** — Senate confirms Vespasian as emperor after Vitellius's defeat.
- **14 September 81** — Domitian proclaimed emperor by the Praetorian Guard after Titus's death.
- **82–83** — Campaign against the Chatti in Germania.
- **84** — Battle of Mons Graupius in Britain; Agricola defeats Caledonians.
- **85–88** — Dacian Wars; Roman defeat and subsequent treaty with King Decebalus.
- **1 January 89** — Revolt of Saturninus in Germania Superior, quickly suppressed.
- **18 September 96** — Domitian assassinated by court officials; Nerva succeeds him same day.

## Body

### Early Life and Rise of the Flavians
Domitian was the younger son of Vespasian and grew up in the shadow of his older brother, Titus, who gained military fame. During the chaotic Year of the Four Emperors (69), the Flavian family rose to power. Domitian played a minor role, escaping capture during the siege of Rome in 69. After Vespasian's victory, Domitian held ceremonial titles but no real power, which was concentrated in Vespasian and later Titus. He married Domitia Longina in 70, a politically advantageous match.

### Emperor: Rule and Administration
After Titus's sudden death in 81, Domitian was proclaimed emperor. He openly ruled as an autocrat, moving government from the Senate to the imperial court. He was a hands-on administrator, enforcing strict morality as self-appointed perpetual censor and prosecuting corruption. His building program restored Rome after fires and war, including the Flavian Palace and completion of the Colosseum. Economically, he revalued the coinage and maintained a balanced budget for most of his reign.

### Military Campaigns
Domitian's wars were primarily defensive. He developed the Limes Germanicus frontier defenses. In Britain, his general Agricola conquered much of Caledonia (Scotland) but was recalled in 85, possibly due to jealousy. The most serious conflict was the Dacian Wars (85–88) against King Decebalus. After initial defeats, including the loss of a Praetorian eagle, Domitian negotiated a peace treaty granting Decebalus an annual subsidy, which was criticized as shameful.

### Religious Policy and Opposition
Domitian emphasized traditional Roman religion and the imperial cult, deifying family members. He enforced public morals rigorously, expelling philosophers and punishing Vestal Virgins for breaking vows. His relationship with the Senate deteriorated, leading to executions of perceived enemies, including relatives. In 89, the governor Saturninus revolted but was quickly defeated. Domitian's paranoia increased, culminating in his assassination by court officials in 96.

### Death and Legacy
Domitian was assassinated in a palace conspiracy. The Senate immediately condemned his memory (*damnatio memoriae*), and later senatorial historians like Tacitus and Suetonius portrayed him as a tyrant. The army, however, mourned him. His successor, Nerva, was proclaimed by the Senate. Modern historians often revise this view, seeing Domitian as an efficient autocrat whose administration ensured stability for the next century.

## Terms
- **Flavian dynasty**: The imperial dynasty founded by Vespasian, including his sons Titus and Domitian.
- **Principate**: The system of government established by Augustus, maintaining a facade of republican institutions while the emperor held real power.
- **Damnatio memoriae**: A Roman Senate decree condemning an emperor's memory, erasing their name and images from public records.
- **Limes Germanicus**: The network of fortifications along the Rhine frontier of the Roman Empire.
- **Perpetual censor**: An office Domitian assumed to supervise public morals and conduct.
- **Imperium**: Supreme executive power, held by emperors and certain magistrates.
- **Tribunician power**: A key imperial power granting veto authority and sacrosanctity, symbolizing the emperor's protection of the people.
- **Congiaria**: Donatives or gifts of money distributed to the Roman populace by the emperor.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Assassination conspiracy**: The exact involvement of the Praetorian Guard and whether Nerva was complicit remains unclear.
- **Agricola's recall**: Whether Domitian recalled the successful general Agricola out of jealousy or for strategic reasons (to reinforce the Danube) is debated.
- **Persecution of Christians**: Ancient sources mention persecution, but modern historians find little concrete evidence of systematic targeting under Domitian.
- **Financial legacy**: Whether Domitian left the empire in debt or with a surplus is debated, though evidence suggests a balanced economy for most of his reign.
- **Historical bias**: The reliability of hostile senatorial sources (Tacitus, Suetonius) versus archaeological and numismatic evidence is a central scholarly debate.

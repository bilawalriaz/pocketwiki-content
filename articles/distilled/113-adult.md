# Adult

## Overview

Adulthood is the stage when an organism has reached full growth and sexual maturity, making it capable of reproduction. In humans, the term carries both biological and legal meanings: a person may be physically mature by age 15 or 16, but legal adulthood (the age of majority) typically arrives later, most commonly at 18, though it ranges internationally from 15 to 21. The concept is further complicated by social and psychological dimensions—definitions are often inconsistent, and a person may be biologically adult but legally a minor, or legally adult but lacking adult maturity. Modern societies generally determine legal adulthood by a specified age rather than by demonstrating physical maturity or preparation.

## Body

### Biological adulthood

Historically and cross-culturally, adulthood was determined primarily by the start of puberty—the appearance of secondary sex characteristics such as menstruation and breast development in women, ejaculation, facial hair, and deeper voice in men, and pubic hair in both sexes. In the past, a person typically moved directly from child to adult status, often marked by a coming-of-age test or ceremony. During the Industrial Revolution, children went to work as soon as they could to help provide for their family, with little emphasis on education.

The term "biological adult" clarifies that the original definition—based on physical maturity and reproductive competency—is being used. Puberty timing varies by child, influenced by nutrition, genetics, and environment. Girls generally complete puberty by 15–17 and boys by 16–17.

A recent debate concerns the chronological age of full mental maturity. Media claims since 2005 have suggested an "end-point" of age 25, based on a 2004–2005 brain imaging study by Jay Giedd. However, that study only included participants up to age 21, and Giedd assumed maturation would finish by 25. More recent studies show prefrontal cortex maturation continuing well past age 30, marking the earlier interpretation as incorrect and outdated.

### Legal adulthood

Legally, adulthood means reaching the age of majority, when parents lose parental rights and responsibilities. The age of majority ranges internationally from 15 to 21, with 18 being most common. Nigeria, Mali, Democratic Republic of Congo, and Cameroon define adulthood at 15, though early marriage of girls is common. Exceptions include: 19 in parts of Canada (British Columbia, New Brunswick, Newfoundland and Labrador, Northwest Territories, Nova Scotia, Nunavut, Yukon), Nebraska, Alabama, and South Korea; 21 in Mississippi, Puerto Rico, and Bahrain.

Prior to the 1970s, most western nations set adulthood at 21. In the US, the voting age was lowered to 18 in July 1971 via the 26th Amendment, prompted by young men aged 18–21 being drafted for the Vietnam War—hence the slogan "old enough to fight, old enough to vote." Many states subsequently lowered drinking ages and the age of majority to 18. However, exceptions remain: the Gun Control Act of 1968 prohibits handgun purchases from licensed dealers under 21 (though private purchases may be allowed at 18 if state law permits); the 1984 National Minimum Drinking Age Act mandated age 21 for alcohol, with states risking 10% loss of highway funding for noncompliance; the 2009 Credit Card Act requires those under 21 to have a co-signer or show income proof; the 2010 Affordable Care Act extends parental insurance coverage to age 26; and as of December 2019, tobacco and vaping purchase age is 21. Gambling ages vary from 18 to 21, and rental car companies often impose surcharges for drivers under 25.

In 2020, Quebec raised recreational marijuana age from 18 to 21, citing brain development risks. In March 2021, Washington State's Supreme Court ruled that courts must consider the age of those under 21 before sentencing them to life without parole, amid debates about exempting 18–20-year-olds from the death penalty. Germany courts largely sentence those under 21 under juvenile law to aid reintegration. In May 2021, Texas raised the age for exotic dancers and patrons of sexually oriented businesses from 18 to 21. The UK has proposed raising tobacco age to 21 for a "smoke-free" UK by 2030. These laws reflect growing awareness that young adults remain in a transitional stage between adolescence and full adulthood.

Conversely, some rights are granted before 18: US youth can work part-time at 14 with a permit, obtain driver's licenses at 16, enlist with parental consent at 17 (though not deployed in combat until 18), and vote in local elections at 16 in five localities (four in Maryland: Takoma Park, Riverdale, Greenbelt, Hyattsville) and in Oakland, California school board elections since 2020. Countries with voting age 16 include Austria, Scotland, Argentina, Brazil, Wales, Cuba, and Ecuador. In Germany, beer and wine are legal at 16, spirits at 18; the age of consent is 14 if both partners are under 18.

### Social construction of adulthood

Social scientists conceptualize adulthood as socially constructed, unlike biological aging. The criteria for adulthood are subjective and organized by gender, race, ethnicity, and social class. Historically in the US, adulthood rested on completing education, moving away from family, beginning a career, marrying, and becoming a parent. Contemporary young adults instead emphasize individualistic criteria: gaining a sense of responsibility, independent decision-making, and financial independence.

Jeffrey Arnett, a psychologist at Clark University, argues for a distinct period called "emerging adulthood" between ages 18 and 25, when individuals take some responsibility but do not fully feel adult. He identifies five features: identity exploration (decisions about career, education, love), feeling in between, instability (frequent moving), self-focus (freedom from parental routines), and an age of possibilities where "optimism reigns."

### Religion

Jewish tradition sets adulthood at 13 for boys and 12 for girls (Bar or Bat Mitzvah), with preparation demonstrated through learning the Torah. The Christian Bible and Jewish scripture contain no age requirement for adulthood or marriage. The 1983 Code of Canon Law sets minimum marriage ages at 16 for men and 14 for women. According to Neil Postman's *The Disappearance of Childhood*, the medieval Christian Church considered age 7 the age of accountability, when one could be tried and executed as an adult. Studies cited by the National Library of Medicine show religiosity declining as young people live independently, then increasing as adults marry and have children; the pace varies across cultures and time.

## Terms

- **Age of majority**: The legally specified age at which one is considered an independent adult and parents lose rights and responsibilities.
- **Puberty**: The biological process beginning sexual maturation, marked by secondary sex characteristics.
- **Secondary sex characteristics**: Physical traits appearing at puberty, such as menstruation, breast development, facial hair, and pubic hair.
- **Prefrontal cortex**: A brain region involved in higher cognitive functions; its maturation continues past age 30.
- **Emerging adulthood**: Arnett's proposed developmental stage between ages 18 and 25, distinct from both adolescence and full adulthood.
- **Age of accountability**: A religious/legal concept of when a person can be tried and punished as an adult (age 7 in medieval Christian tradition).
- **Bar/Bat Mitzvah**: Jewish coming-of-age ceremonies at 13 (boys) and 12 (girls).
- **Social construction**: The idea that social categories like adulthood are defined by cultural criteria rather than biology alone.

## Debates and open questions

- Whether a specific chronological age for full mental maturity exists at all; the age-25 claim is now considered outdated, with maturation continuing past 30.
- Whether those aged 18–20 should be exempted from the death penalty, as raised in Washington State's 2021 ruling.
- Whether the voting age should be lowered from 18 to 16, a growing movement in the US and elsewhere.
- Whether legal restrictions for young adults (e.g., tobacco, marijuana, credit, exotic dancing) should be raised to 21, reflecting their transitional status.

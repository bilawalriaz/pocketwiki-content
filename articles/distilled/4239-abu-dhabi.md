# Abu Dhabi

## Overview
Abu Dhabi is the capital and largest emirate of the United Arab Emirates (UAE), serving as the nation's political and industrial center. Its transformation from a pearling settlement into a global metropolis is driven by vast oil and gas reserves, which account for about two-thirds of the UAE's economy. The city is a major cultural and commercial hub, hosting the federal government and significant sovereign wealth funds.

## Body

### History and Foundation
The area shows evidence of ancient civilizations like the Umm Al Nar Culture (c. 3000 BC). The modern settlement began in 1761 after fresh water was discovered by a Bani Yas hunting party led by Sheikh Dhiyab bin Isa Al Nahyan. The Al Bu Falah subsection established the settlement, which grew from a fort and twenty houses to 400 within two years. The fort, Qasr Al Hosn, remains at the city's center. The pearl trade was vital until the mid-20th century. British influence grew through 19th-century treaties to protect trade routes. Oil exploration began in the 1930s, with the first major offshore discovery in 1958. In 1966, Sheikh Zayed became ruler, and in 1971, Abu Dhabi was a founding member of the UAE, with Sheikh Zayed as its first President.

### Geography and Climate
Abu Dhabi city is primarily on a T-shaped island in the Persian Gulf, connected to the mainland by bridges like the Maqta, Mussafah, and the Sheikh Zayed Bridge (opened 2010). The climate is hot desert (Köppen BWh), with extremely hot, humid summers (July-August averages above 40°C) and mild winters. Sandstorms and fog are common.

### Government and Economy
Abu Dhabi is the largest and wealthiest emirate, housing the UAE's federal government and the office of the President, a member of the Al Nahyan family. The emirate owns 95% of the UAE's oil and 92% of its gas reserves, holding 9% of the world's proven oil reserves. The economy is diversifying, with non-oil GDP now 64% of the UAE's total. Major initiatives include the Abu Dhabi Economic Vision 2030 and the Ghadan 21 program. The Abu Dhabi Investment Authority (ADIA) is the world's third-largest sovereign wealth fund.

### Cityscape and Architecture
Planned in 1967 for 40,000 people, the city features a grid-pattern road system and high-density towers. Notable architecture includes the circular Aldar Headquarters and the Emirates Palace. The Sheikh Zayed Grand Mosque, opened in 2007, is a major landmark. The Founder's Memorial honors Sheikh Zayed. The Abrahamic Family House, opened in 2023, is a multi-faith complex. Qasr Al Hosn is the oldest building, now a museum.

### Demographics and Culture
The population is overwhelmingly expatriate (about 80%), with large communities from South Asia and Southeast Asia. Arabic is the official language, but English, Hindi-Urdu, and others are widely spoken. The society is multicultural and generally tolerant, with places of worship for various faiths. Major holidays include Eid al Fitr, Eid ul-Adha, and National Day (2 December). The city hosts numerous cultural institutions and conferences.

### Transportation
The city is connected by major highways and bridges. Zayed International Airport (AUH) is the UAE's second-busiest airport. Public transport includes buses, taxis, water taxis, and ferries. The Etihad Rail network provides freight connections. A toll gate system (Darb) was introduced in 2021. Smart transport initiatives include autonomous vehicles on Yas and Saadiyat Islands.

## Terms
- **Emirate:** A territory ruled by a hereditary monarch called an Emir; the UAE is a federation of seven emirates.
- **Sovereign Wealth Fund:** A state-owned investment fund investing in real and financial assets.
- **Trucial Coast:** The Persian Gulf coast (now UAE) under British protection via treaties in the 19th and 20th centuries.
- **Concession Agreement:** A contract granting a company the right to explore for and extract natural resources.
- **Bani Yas:** The tribal confederation from which the ruling Al Nahyan family descends.
- **Qasr Al Hosn:** The oldest stone building in Abu Dhabi, originally a fort and seat of government.
- **Köppen climate classification BWh:** A climate classification for hot deserts with very low precipitation.
- **Corniche:** A coastal road or promenade; in Abu Dhabi, a 10 km seaside area with gardens and beaches.

## Debates and Open Questions
- Human rights organizations criticize the treatment of foreign workers in Abu Dhabi and the wider UAE, describing it as "endemic to the system."
- Rapid development has created urban challenges, including traffic congestion, parking shortages, and overcrowding, as the population far exceeds original planning estimates.
- The effectiveness and timeline for achieving economic diversification goals outlined in plans like Abu Dhabi Economic Vision 2030 remain subjects of ongoing development.

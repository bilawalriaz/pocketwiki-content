# Sri Lanka

## Overview
Sri Lanka is an island nation in South Asia with a documented history spanning over 3,000 years. Its strategic location in the Indian Ocean has made it a major trading hub since antiquity, influencing its diverse cultural and religious landscape. The country's modern history is marked by colonial rule, a prolonged civil war, and recent severe economic challenges.

## Timeline
- **c. 543 BCE** — Traditional arrival of Prince Vijaya, marking the start of Sinhalese history.
- **250 BCE** — Buddhism introduced to Sri Lanka by Mahinda, son of Emperor Ashoka.
- **29 BCE** — The Pali Canon (Buddhist scriptures) is first written down in Sri Lanka.
- **1017 CE** — Chola invasion leads to the fall of the Anuradhapura Kingdom.
- **1505** — Arrival of the Portuguese, beginning the colonial period.
- **1796** — British Empire occupies coastal areas from the Dutch.
- **1815** — Kingdom of Kandy falls to the British, ending native monarchy.
- **1948** — Independence as the Dominion of Ceylon.
- **1972** — Country becomes a republic, renamed Sri Lanka.
- **1983-2009** — Civil war between the government and the LTTE.
- **2009** — Civil war ends with the defeat of the LTTE.
- **2022** — Severe economic crisis leads to political upheaval and presidential resignation.

## Body

### History and Colonial Rule
Sri Lanka's ancient history is dominated by the Anuradhapura and Polonnaruwa kingdoms, which were major centers of Theravada Buddhism. The island was invaded multiple times by South Indian dynasties like the Cholas. The colonial era began with the Portuguese in 1505, followed by the Dutch in the 17th century, and finally the British, who unified the island by 1815. The British introduced plantation economies for coffee, then tea and rubber, and established modern political institutions.

### Independence and Civil War
Independence in 1948 was followed by rising ethnic tensions between the Sinhalese majority and Tamil minority, exacerbated by policies like the "Sinhala Only Act" of 1956. These grievances fueled the rise of Tamil militancy, leading to a 26-year civil war (1983-2009) between the government and the Liberation Tigers of Tamil Eelam (LTTE). The war concluded with the military defeat of the LTTE.

### Economy and Recent Crisis
Sri Lanka has a mixed economy historically based on agriculture (tea, rubber) and now services. It achieved high human development indicators for South Asia. However, a severe economic crisis began in 2019, driven by foreign debt, tax cuts, and a failed transition to organic farming. This led to the country's first sovereign debt default in 2022, causing mass protests and the resignation of President Gotabaya Rajapaksa.

### Government and Society
Sri Lanka is a democratic republic with a semi-presidential system. Its population is diverse, with Sinhalese (74.8%) and Sri Lankan Tamils (11.2%) as the largest groups. Buddhism is the official religion, practiced by 69.8% of the population. The country has a high literacy rate (92.9%) and a universal free education system.

## Terms
- **Pali Canon:** The standard collection of scriptures in the Theravada Buddhist tradition, first written down in Sri Lanka around 29 BCE.
- **Anuradhapura Kingdom:** The first major kingdom in Sri Lanka, lasting from 377 BCE to 1017 CE, and a center of Buddhist culture.
- **Sinhala Only Act:** A 1956 law that made Sinhala the sole official language, marginalizing the Tamil-speaking minority.
- **LTTE (Liberation Tigers of Tamil Eelam):** A Tamil separatist militant organization that fought a civil war against the Sri Lankan government from 1983 to 2009.
- **Sinhalese:** The largest ethnic group in Sri Lanka, predominantly Buddhist and speaking the Sinhala language.
- **Sri Lankan Tamils:** The second-largest ethnic group, predominantly Hindu and speaking the Tamil language, concentrated in the north and east.
- **Burghers:** A distinct ethnic group of mixed European descent, primarily from Portuguese and Dutch colonists.
- **Vedda:** The indigenous people of Sri Lanka, believed to be the island's earliest inhabitants.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The causes and responsibility for the severe 2022 economic crisis are debated, with factors including policy mismanagement, global shocks, and debt from large infrastructure projects.
- Accountability for human rights violations and alleged war crimes committed by both the government and the LTTE during the final stages of the civil war remains a contentious international issue.
- The effectiveness and social impact of post-war reconciliation efforts and the devolution of power to provincial councils are ongoing political questions.

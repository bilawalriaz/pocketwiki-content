# Yerevan

## Overview
Yerevan is the capital and largest city of Armenia, situated along the Hrazdan River. It is one of the world's oldest continuously inhabited cities, with its origins traced to the founding of the Urartian fortress of Erebuni in 782 BC. As Armenia's primate city, Yerevan serves as the nation's administrative, cultural, and industrial center, a role it has held as the country's capital since 1918.

## Timeline
- **782 BC** — The Urartian fortress of Erebuni is founded by King Argishti I.
- **590 BC** — Erebuni becomes part of the Median Empire after the fall of Urartu.
- **387 AD** — Armenia is partitioned; Erebuni comes under Sasanian Persian rule.
- **658 AD** — The city is conquered during the Arab Islamic invasions.
- **1501–02** — Most of Eastern Armenia, including Yerevan, is conquered by the Safavid dynasty of Iran.
- **1603–05** — The Great Surgun: Safavid Shah Abbas I forcibly deports hundreds of thousands of Armenians to Iran.
- **1679** — A devastating earthquake destroys much of the city.
- **1828** — Yerevan is ceded to the Russian Empire following the Treaty of Turkmenchay.
- **28 May 1918** — Yerevan becomes the capital of the newly independent First Republic of Armenia.
- **2 December 1920** — Yerevan becomes the capital of the Armenian Soviet Socialist Republic.
- **21 September 1991** — Yerevan becomes the capital of the independent Republic of Armenia.

## Body

### Ancient Origins and Early History
The territory of Yerevan has been inhabited since at least the 4th millennium BC, with significant early Bronze Age settlement at Shengavit. The city's recorded history begins with the founding of the Urartian fortress of Erebuni in 782 BC. After the fall of Urartu, the city passed through Median and Achaemenid Persian rule. During the ancient Kingdom of Armenia, Erebuni's importance declined as new capitals like Artaxata were established. Following the Arab conquest in 782 AD, the city became known as Yerevan and grew as a trade center.

### Rule by Empires and Devastation
From the 16th century, Yerevan was a contested territory between the Ottoman and Safavid Empires, ultimately becoming the center of the Erivan Province under Safavid Iran. A pivotal and devastating event was the Great Surgun of 1603–05, when Shah Abbas I forcibly deported the city's Armenian population to Iran, drastically altering its demographics. A major earthquake in 1679 further destroyed the city. In 1828, following the Russo-Persian War, Yerevan was annexed by the Russian Empire, leading to the repatriation of Armenians and a shift in the city's ethnic makeup.

### Capital of Modern Armenia
Yerevan became the capital of the First Republic of Armenia in 1918, receiving tens of thousands of refugees from the Armenian genocide. After a brief independence, it became the capital of the Armenian SSR in 1920. Under Soviet rule, architect Alexander Tamanian's 1924 "General Plan" transformed the city into a modern, radial-circled metropolis, though this involved demolishing many historic structures. Following independence in 1991, Yerevan has undergone a massive construction boom, particularly since 2000, which has further reshaped its urban landscape.

### Modern City and Demographics
As of 2022, Yerevan's population is 1,086,677, over 35% of Armenia's total. The population is overwhelmingly Armenian, a result of 20th-century repatriation and the departure of minorities like Azerbaijanis and Russians. The city is divided into twelve administrative districts. It is a major economic hub, home to the Central Bank of Armenia and the majority of the country's industry and services. The city's climate is a cold semi-arid steppe, with hot, dry summers and cold, snowy winters.

## Terms
- **Primate city**: A city that is disproportionately larger and more influential than any other in the country.
- **Erebuni**: The Urartian fortress founded in 782 BC, considered the birthplace of Yerevan.
- **Great Surgun**: The 1603–05 forced deportation of Armenians from the Ararat Plain to Iran by Safavid Shah Abbas I.
- **Treaty of Turkmenchay (1828)**: The treaty that ended the Russo-Persian War, ceding Eastern Armenia, including Yerevan, to the Russian Empire.
- **Armenian genocide**: The systematic extermination of 1.5 million ethnic Armenians by the Ottoman government during WWI.
- **General Plan of Yerevan**: The 1924 urban design by Alexander Tamanian that shaped the modern city's radial layout.
- **Marz**: An administrative province of Armenia; Yerevan has special status as the capital.
- **Matenadaran**: A library-museum in Yerevan housing one of the world's largest collections of ancient manuscripts.
- **Tsitsernakaberd**: The official memorial complex in Yerevan dedicated to the victims of the Armenian genocide.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The exact origin of the name "Yerevan" is unknown. Theories link it to the Urartian "Erebuni," the name of King Yervand (Orontes) IV, or a legendary exclamation by Noah.
- The scale and impact of the 2000s construction boom are debated, with criticism focused on the destruction of historic 18th and 19th-century buildings.

# Dar es Salaam

## Overview
Dar es Salaam is the largest city and financial hub of Tanzania, located on the Swahili coast. With a population over 7 million, it is East Africa's most populous city and a major economic center, though the national capital was officially moved to Dodoma in 1996. The city's history as a colonial administrative center and its rapid modern growth make it a key case study in African urban development.

## Body

### History and Colonial Legacy
Dar es Salaam began as a coastal village. In 1865 or 1866, Sultan Majid bin Said of Zanzibar founded a new city nearby, naming it "Abode of Peace." After a decline following his death in 1870, the German East Africa Company revived it in 1887. The city grew as the administrative and commercial center of German East Africa, a role it retained under British rule after World War I. Under British indirect rule, European and African areas developed separately. The city remained the capital after Tanganyika's independence in 1961 and the formation of Tanzania in 1964. A 1974 decision moved the capital to Dodoma, completed in 1996, though Dar es Salaam still hosts most government offices.

### Urban Geography and Districts
The city is located on a natural harbor on the Indian Ocean coast. The Dar es Salaam Region is divided into five administrative districts. **Kinondoni** is the most populous, containing high-income suburbs like Masaki and Oyster Bay. **Ilala** is the administrative and commercial core, housing the central business district, government offices, and major transport hubs like the Julius Nyerere International Airport. **Temeke** is the main industrial district and contains the Port of Dar es Salaam. **Ubungo** serves as a key transportation link, and **Kigamboni** is a beachfront suburb on a peninsula.

### Economy and Infrastructure
Dar es Salaam is Tanzania's primary business and financial center. The Dar es Salaam Stock Exchange (DSE) is the country's first stock market. The city is undergoing major construction, including new high-rise buildings like the PSPF Twin Towers. The Port of Dar es Salaam handles 90% of the country's cargo. Transportation infrastructure includes the Julius Nyerere International Airport, the Central Railway Line, and the TAZARA Railway connecting to Zambia. A Bus Rapid Transit system began operation in 2016.

### Climate and Environment
The city has a tropical savanna climate with hot, humid weather and two rainy seasons. It is highly vulnerable to flooding due to its lowland coastal orientation and the Msimbazi River. Climate change projections suggest significant future impacts, including sea-level rise that could cause billions in damages by 2050.

### Demographics and Society
Dar es Salaam is the most populous city in Tanzania, with an estimated population of 8 million in 2020. Muslims make up 70% of the population. The city has a high literacy rate (96%) but also a large population living in informal settlements. It is a center for arts, fashion, and media, with a vibrant music scene featuring styles like *muziki wa dansi* and *Bongo Flava*.

### Education
The city hosts several major universities, including the University of Dar es Salaam, the oldest and second-largest public university in Tanzania, and the Muhimbili University of Health and Allied Sciences.

## Terms
- **Ujamaa**: A socialist policy declared in 1967 that encouraged people to remain in rural villages, which hampered urban growth.
- **Dala dala**: Public minibus share taxis, a common form of local transport.
- **Bodaboda**: Motorcycle transit businesses popular for navigating traffic.
- **Entrepot trade**: The port's role as a hub for goods being imported and then re-exported to landlocked neighboring countries.
- **Tingatinga**: A painting style that originated in Dar es Salaam.
- **Muziki wa dansi**: A long-standing style of live dance music in Tanzania.
- **Bongo Flava**: A broad category of Tanzanian popular music influenced by hip hop and R&B.
- **Human Development Index (HDI)**: A composite statistic of life expectancy, education, and per capita income indicators, used to rank regions.

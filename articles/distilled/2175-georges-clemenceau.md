# Georges Clemenceau

## Overview
Georges Clemenceau (1841–1929) was a dominant French statesman, serving as Prime Minister in 1906–1909 and 1917–1920. A radical republican and fierce anti-clerical, he led France to victory in World War I and shaped the Treaty of Versailles as President of the Paris Peace Conference. His uncompromising leadership and pursuit of a punitive peace against Germany made him a central, controversial figure in 20th-century European history.

## Body

### Early Life and Political Activism
Born into an anti-clerical family, Clemenceau studied medicine before becoming a journalist opposing Napoleon III, leading to exile in the United States (1865–1869). Returning after the Franco-Prussian War, he entered Paris municipal politics. As a deputy from 1876, he championed radical causes, including amnesty for Communards and opposing colonial ventures.

### The Dreyfus Affair and Return to Prominence
After losing his seat in 1893, Clemenceau focused on journalism. He re-entered prominence during the Dreyfus Affair, publishing Zola's "J'Accuse...!" in 1898. Elected to the Senate in 1902, he supported the 1905 law separating church and state.

### First Premiership (1906–1909)
As Minister of the Interior in 1906, he earned the nickname "The Tiger" (*Le Tigre*) for suppressing strikes. As Prime Minister from October 1906, he continued hardline domestic policy and strengthened the *Entente cordiale* with Britain. His government fell in 1909 over a naval policy dispute.

### World War I Leadership
During World War I, Clemenceau criticized the government for insufficient effort. Appointed Prime Minister in November 1917 amid crisis, he rallied France with "war until the end" (*la guerre jusqu'au bout*), visiting troops and cracking down on defeatism. His leadership sustained resolve through the 1918 German spring offensive until the Armistice on 11 November 1918.

### The Paris Peace Conference
As President of the 1919 Paris Peace Conference, Clemenceau sought to permanently weaken Germany, demanding massive reparations, the return of Alsace-Lorraine, strict disarmament, and Allied occupation of the Rhineland. He secured mutual defense guarantees from Britain and the U.S., later nullified by the U.S. Senate's failure to ratify the treaty. He defended the Treaty of Versailles as a necessary, if imperfect, foundation for security.

### Later Years and Legacy
Clemenceau resigned as Prime Minister in January 1920 after failing to be elected President. He spent his final years traveling, writing, and criticizing policies he believed undermined the Treaty of Versailles. He died in 1929, having witnessed the treaty begin to unravel.

## Terms
*   **Independent Radicals:** A French political faction advocating secularism, social reforms, and a strong republican state.
*   **Entente cordiale:** 1904 agreements between the UK and France resolving colonial disputes and forming their WWI alliance.
*   **Paris Peace Conference (1919–1920):** Allied meeting to set peace terms with the Central Powers, resulting in the Treaty of Versailles.
*   **Treaty of Versailles:** 1919 peace treaty ending WWI, imposing reparations, territorial losses, and military restrictions on Germany.
*   **Reparations:** Compensation demanded of a defeated nation for war damage, a major clause in the Treaty of Versailles.
*   **Rhineland:** German territory demilitarized and subject to Allied occupation for up to 15 years as a security buffer.
*   **Saar:** A coal-rich region under League of Nations administration for 15 years, with mines given to France.
*   **Carnet B:** A list of subversives to be arrested upon mobilization at the start of a war.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Was the Treaty of Versailles too harsh or too lenient?** Clemenceau defended it as a necessary compromise. Critics like Marshal Foch argued it was too lenient, while John Maynard Keynes called its reparations economically ruinous.
*   **Did Clemenceau block a potential compromise peace in 1917?** German documents suggest Germany had no serious intention of returning Alsace-Lorraine, making a negotiated peace unlikely.
*   **What was the impact of the U.S. Senate's rejection of the treaty?** The failure to ratify the Treaty of Guarantee nullified the Anglo-American defense pact for the Rhineland, leaving France without essential security and weakening enforcement.

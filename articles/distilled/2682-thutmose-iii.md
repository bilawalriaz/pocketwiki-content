# Thutmose III

## Overview
Thutmose III (r. 1479–1425 BC) was a pharaoh of Egypt's 18th Dynasty, celebrated as a supreme military strategist and conqueror. He expanded Egypt's empire to its greatest territorial extent through numerous victorious campaigns, transforming it into an international superpower. His reign is a defining period of the New Kingdom, marked by monumental building projects and the establishment of Egypt's first navy.

## Timeline
- **1479** — Thutmose III ascends the throne as a child; Hatshepsut becomes regent.
- **1458** — Death of Hatshepsut; Thutmose III becomes sole ruler.
- **1457** — **Battle of Megiddo**: Decisive victory over Canaanite coalition.
- **c. 1450s** — Campaigns 2-4: Tours of Syria/Canaan to collect tribute.
- **c. 1440s** — Campaigns 5-7: Conquest of Syrian cities and Phoenician ports.
- **c. 1440s** — **Campaign 8**: First Egyptian crossing of the Euphrates to attack Mitanni.
- **c. 1430s** — Campaigns 9-16: Further raids and suppression of revolts in Syria.
- **c. 1427** — **Campaign 17**: Final military campaign into Nubia, reaching the 4th cataract.
- **1425** — Death of Thutmose III; succeeded by Amenhotep II.

## Body

### Coregency and Accession
Thutmose III was the son of Thutmose II and a secondary wife, Iset. Upon his father's death, he was too young to rule, and his stepmother and aunt, Hatshepsut, became his regent. She eventually declared herself pharaoh, ruling as senior coregent while Thutmose III remained junior coregent. He was not denied kingship but was relegated to a ceremonial role. Hatshepsut ruled effectively for about 21 years, during which she appointed Thutmose III to command her armies. He became sole ruler upon her death in 1458 BC.

### Military Campaigns and Empire
Thutmose III conducted at least 17 military campaigns, all victorious, which are detailed in the Annals of Thutmose III. His first major campaign was the Battle of Megiddo (1457 BC), where he employed a risky tactical maneuver through the Aruna pass to achieve a decisive victory, gaining control of northern Canaan. Subsequent campaigns secured Syria and Phoenician ports, often by taking hostages to ensure loyalty. In his eighth campaign, he became the first pharaoh since Thutmose I to cross the Euphrates River, launching a surprise attack on the kingdom of Mitanni. His final campaign in Year 50 penetrated Nubia to the fourth cataract of the Nile. These conquests established an empire stretching from Syria to Upper Nubia.

### Building Projects and Artistic Innovations
Thutmose III was a prolific builder, constructing over 50 temples. He focused heavily on the Temple of Amun at Karnak, where he built pylons, a jubilee hall in the basilica style, and a sacred lake. He also commissioned the tallest obelisk ever successfully cut, later moved to Rome. His reign saw artistic innovations, including the first entirely painted tomb walls and new architectural forms like standalone heraldic pillars. He created a botanical garden to document flora and fauna from his campaigns.

### Defacing of Hatshepsut's Monuments
For years, it was believed that Thutmose III ordered the defacement of Hatshepsut's monuments out of vengeance. However, modern research shows the erasures began around Year 46 or 47 of his reign, long after her death. Scholars now suggest this was a political move to ensure a smooth succession for his son, Amenhotep II, by removing the memory of Hatshepsut's female relatives who had a claim to the throne.

### Death and Burial
Thutmose III died in 1425 BC after a reign of 54 years (including coregency). His tomb (KV34) in the Valley of the Kings features a unique oval burial chamber with a starry ceiling and walls decorated in a simple, papyrus-like style. His mummy, discovered in the Deir el-Bahri Cache in 1881, was found in poor condition due to ancient robbery and rough handling.

## Terms
*   **Coregent**: A ruler who shares power with another, often a senior and junior partner.
*   **Annals**: Official records of a ruler's reign, often inscribed on temple walls.
*   **Heliacal Rise of Sothis**: An astronomical event used to anchor Egyptian chronology, but its exact timing is debated.
*   **Pylon**: A monumental gateway to an Egyptian temple.
*   **Basilica style**: An architectural plan with a central nave flanked by aisles, used in Thutmose's jubilee hall.
*   **Damnatio memoriae**: The condemnation of a person's memory, often by erasing their name and images.
*   **Amduat**: An important New Kingdom funerary text describing the sun god's journey through the underworld.
*   **Sed festival**: A jubilee festival celebrating a pharaoh's continued rule.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Chronology**: The exact dates of Thutmose III's reign are debated, with "Low Chronology" (1479–1425 BC) being conventional but "High Chronology" (1504–1450 BC) also used.
*   **Hatshepsut's Defacement**: The motive and timing for the erasure of Hatshepsut's monuments are debated, with current theory favoring political succession for Amenhotep II over personal vengeance.
*   **Campaign Details**: The exact size of armies and the precise outcomes of some later campaigns (e.g., Campaign 10 against Mitanni) are uncertain due to limited or propagandistic records.

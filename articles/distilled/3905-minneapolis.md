# Minneapolis

## Overview
Minneapolis is the most populous city in Minnesota and the county seat of Hennepin County. Situated on the Mississippi River, it is one of the Twin Cities with Saint Paul. The city's history is defined by its water resources, which powered its 19th-century milling industries, and by significant social tensions, including racial disparities and civil unrest that culminated in the 2020 murder of George Floyd.

## Timeline
- **1680** — Cleric Louis Hennepin, likely the first European to see Saint Anthony Falls, renames it.
- **1805** — Zebulon Pike makes the Treaty of St. Peter, purchasing land from the Dakota.
- **1819** — The U.S. Army builds Fort Snelling.
- **1851** — The Minnesota Territorial Legislature meets; leaders lose a bid to move the capital but win the state university.
- **1856** — The territorial legislature authorizes Minneapolis as a town.
- **1862** — The U.S.–Dakota War occurs; 38 Dakota men are hanged, and most Dakota are exiled from Minnesota.
- **1867** — Minneapolis is incorporated as a city.
- **1872** — Minneapolis merges with St. Anthony.
- **1884** — The value of Minneapolis flour milling is the world's highest.
- **1899** — Minneapolis outsells every other lumber market in the world.
- **1934** — A major teamsters' strike leads to federal labor protections.
- **1968** — The American Indian Movement (AIM) is founded in Minneapolis.
- **2020** — The murder of George Floyd by a Minneapolis police officer sparks global protests.

## Body

### Early History and Founding
The area was inhabited by the Dakota and later the Ojibwe. European settlement began near Fort Snelling and Saint Anthony Falls, the only natural waterfall on the Mississippi. The falls' power fostered industrial growth. The city's name combines the Dakota word for water (*mni*) with the Greek for city (*polis*). Minneapolis was incorporated in 1867 and merged with St. Anthony in 1872.

### Industrial Rise and Decline
Minneapolis became the world's lumber and flour milling capital in the late 19th century, earning the nickname "Mill City." The flour industry, led by companies like Washburn-Crosby (later General Mills) and Pillsbury, peaked in 1916 before declining due to soil exhaustion and changing economics. The lumber industry waned after depleting Minnesota's white pine forests. The city later attracted skilled workers and industries, including Thermo King (mobile refrigeration), Medtronic, and early computing firms like Control Data.

### Social Tensions and Civil Rights
The 20th century was marked by corruption, antisemitism, and racial strife. A 1934 teamsters' strike was a pivotal labor event. Restrictive covenants, used from 1910, created severe residential segregation. The Black population grew significantly after World War II, but discrimination persisted, leading to disturbances in 1966-67. The American Indian Movement was founded in 1968. In 2020, the murder of George Floyd by officer Derek Chauvin sparked international protests and ongoing demands for police reform, leading to a consent decree with the Department of Justice.

### Geography and Climate
The city is built on an artesian aquifer and flat terrain, with 13 lakes, wetlands, and a 12-mile segment of the Mississippi River. It has a humid continental climate with cold, snowy winters and hot, humid summers. The highest recorded temperature is 108 °F (42 °C) and the lowest is −41 °F (−41 °C).

### Demographics and Economy
The population peaked at 521,718 in 1950 before declining; the 2020 census recorded 429,954 residents. The city is diverse, with significant White, Black, Hispanic, and Asian populations, and large Somali and Hmong communities. It has stark racial disparities in income, homeownership, and health. The economy is based on trade, services, and finance, with five Fortune 500 companies headquartered in the city.

### Government and Politics
Minneapolis is governed by a mayor-council system and is politically dominated by the Minnesota Democratic–Farmer–Labor Party (DFL). The city uses instant-runoff voting. Following the 2020 unrest, the city council and mayor restructured public safety oversight, creating an Office of Community Safety.

### Parks and Culture
The city's park system, designed by Horace Cleveland, is renowned. The Grand Rounds National Scenic Byway connects parks and lakes. Major cultural institutions include the Minneapolis Institute of Art, the Walker Art Center, and the Guthrie Theater. The city is the birthplace of musician Prince and home to the University of Minnesota.

## Terms
- **Artesian aquifer:** A confined aquifer containing groundwater under positive pressure, causing water to rise without pumping.
- **DFL (Minnesota Democratic–Farmer–Labor Party):** The state affiliate of the U.S. Democratic Party, formed by a merger.
- **Instant-runoff voting:** A voting system where voters rank candidates by preference; the lowest-ranked are eliminated until one has a majority.
- **Restrictive covenant:** A deed provision that prohibits the sale or rental of property to certain groups, often based on race.
- **Consent decree:** A court-enforceable agreement between parties to resolve a dispute, often used for institutional reform.
- **Qualified immunity:** A legal doctrine that shields government officials from liability for civil damages unless their conduct violates clearly established law.
- **YIMBY (Yes In My Backyard):** A movement advocating for increased housing supply and density to address affordability.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The long-term effects of the 2040 Comprehensive Plan, which ended single-family zoning, on housing affordability and racial segregation are still being evaluated.
- The impact of Operation Metro Surge (2025-2026) on the city's economy, immigrant communities, and legal boundaries of federal vs. state authority is contested.
- The effectiveness of police reform measures, including the consent decree and the Office of Community Safety, in reducing crime and addressing systemic racism remains a critical and unresolved issue.

# Sudan

## Overview
Sudan is a large, diverse country in Northeast Africa with a complex history marked by ancient kingdoms, colonial rule, and persistent internal conflict. Its modern history is defined by the struggle between its Arab-Muslim north and non-Arab, non-Muslim south, leading to civil wars, the secession of South Sudan in 2011, and ongoing instability, including a devastating civil war that began in 2023.

- **11 April 2019** — Omar al-Bashir is ousted in a popular uprising and military coup.
- **15 April 2023** — A civil war erupts between the Sudanese Armed Forces (SAF) and the Rapid Support Forces (RSF).

## Body

### Ancient and Medieval History
The region saw early human cultures like the Khormusan and Halfan. By c. 2500 BC, the Kingdom of Kerma emerged, later rivaling Egypt. After a period of Egyptian rule, the independent Kingdom of Kush (c. 785 BC–350 AD) became a major power, even conquering Egypt. Following its decline, three Christian kingdoms—Nobatia, Makuria, and Alodia—arose. From the 14th century, Arab nomads settled the area, and by the 16th century, the Islamic Funj Sultanate dominated central Sudan, while the Keira Sultanate ruled Darfur.

### Colonial Rule and Independence
In 1821, Egypt conquered Sudan. A religious-nationalist revolt, the Mahdist Uprising (1881–1899), established a short-lived Islamic state before being defeated by a British-led force. From 1899, Sudan was jointly administered by Britain and Egypt as a condominium, though effectively a British colony. The Egyptian Revolution of 1952 pressured Britain to withdraw, leading to Sudan's independence on 1 January 1956.

### Civil Wars and Partition
Post-independence politics were dominated by the Arab-Muslim north. A first civil war (1955–1972) ended with a peace agreement granting the south autonomy. This collapsed, leading to a second, more devastating civil war (1983–2005) between the Islamist government and the southern Sudan People's Liberation Army (SPLA). The 2005 Comprehensive Peace Agreement led to a referendum in which the south voted unanimously for secession, creating South Sudan in 2011.

### The Bashir Era and Darfur Genocide
Omar al-Bashir seized power in a 1989 coup, imposing Islamic law and a one-party state. His regime was accused of global terrorism sponsorship and committed widespread human rights abuses. From 2003, conflict in Darfur between government forces/militias (Janjaweed) and rebel groups escalated into a genocide, killing an estimated 300,000–400,000 people. International sanctions followed, and the ICC issued arrest warrants for Bashir.

### Revolution and Current Civil War
Mass protests in 2018–2019 led to Bashir's ouster. A transitional government was formed but was overthrown in a 2021 military coup. In April 2023, a power struggle between army chief Abdel Fattah al-Burhan and RSF leader Hemedti erupted into a full-scale civil war. The conflict has caused a massive humanitarian crisis, with millions displaced, widespread famine, and accusations of war crimes by both sides.

## Terms
*   **Condominium:** A territory under the joint sovereignty of two states, as Sudan was under Britain and Egypt from 1899.
*   **Janjaweed:** Arabic-speaking nomadic militias accused of atrocities in Darfur, later reorganized into the Rapid Support Forces (RSF).
*   **Mahdist Uprising:** A religious-nationalist revolt (1881–1899) that established an Islamic state in Sudan before its defeat by British-led forces.
*   **Rapid Support Forces (RSF):** A powerful paramilitary group in Sudan, evolved from the Janjaweed, involved in the current civil war.
*   **Sharia:** Islamic law; it was imposed as Sudan's state law in 1983 and abolished as the state religion in 2020.
*   **Sudan People's Liberation Army (SPLA):** The primary rebel group representing southern Sudan during the Second Sudanese Civil War.

## Debates and open questions
*   The ethnic and cultural distinction between "Arab" and "non-Arab" in Sudan is debated, with some historians arguing identities are fluid and based on self-claiming rather than strict biology.
*   The precise death toll of the Darfur genocide (estimated 300,000–400,000) and the ongoing civil war (estimates vary widely) remain subjects of investigation and dispute.
*   The future of disputed areas like the Abyei Region, which was to hold a referendum on its status, remains unresolved.
*   The role of external actors, particularly the United Arab Emirates' alleged support for the RSF, is a point of international contention and investigation.

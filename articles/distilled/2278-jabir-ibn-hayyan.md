# Jabir ibn Hayyan

## Overview
Jabir ibn Hayyan (died c. 806–816) is the purported author of a vast corpus of Arabic works on alchemy, chemistry, magic, and Shi'ite philosophy. The authenticity of his historical existence and the authorship of the works are major scholarly debates, with modern analysis dating the corpus to c. 850–950. The writings are foundational for the history of chemistry, containing the earliest systematic classification of substances and the sulfur-mercury theory of metals.

- **c. 806–816** — Traditional date range for Jabir ibn Hayyan's death.
- **c. 850–950** — Modern scholarly dating for the composition of the Jabirian corpus.

## Body

### Historicity and Dating
The existence of Jabir ibn Hayyan as a historical person is uncertain. He is not mentioned in any source before c. 900, and early Shi'ite biographers did not record him. The first known biographical account is by Ibn al-Nadīm (987), who noted that some already denied Jabir's existence. Modern scholarship dates the corpus to c. 850–950 based on philosophical terminology and the translation dates of Greek texts cited within. The works may be based on an earlier 8th-century core, but Jabir's name is generally considered a pseudonym used by a school of Shi'ite alchemists.

### The Jabirian Corpus
About 600 works are attributed to Jabir, with approximately 215 surviving. They are organized into large collections like *The Seventy Books* and *The Books of the Balances*. The majority deal with alchemy, but they also cover magic ("science of talismans"), the "science of specific properties" (hidden powers of substances), and lost works on medicine, philosophy, astronomy, and religion. The corpus contains some of the earliest preserved Shi'ite imamological doctrines.

### Chemical Philosophy
Jabirian alchemy is systematic and less allegorical than its Greek predecessors. It pioneers the use of organic substances (plants, blood) alongside mineral ones. Key concepts include:
*   **Elements and Natures:** Metals are composed of four elements (fire, air, water, earth), each defined by two "natures" (hot, cold, moist, dry). Metals differ by the arrangement of these natures.
*   **The Elixir:** A medicine that can rearrange a metal's natures to transmute it into another, like gold.
*   **Sulfur-Mercury Theory:** Metals form underground from the combination of sulfur and mercury. The quality of sulfur determines the metal, with gold being the most perfect. This theory dominated until the 18th century.
*   **Science of the Balance (ʿilm al-mīzān):** A philosophical theory aiming to reduce all phenomena to quantitative measures and proportions.

## Terms
*   **Jabirian Corpus:** The collection of works attributed to Jabir ibn Hayyan, primarily on alchemy and Shi'ite philosophy.
*   **Elixir (al-iksīr):** The alchemical substance believed capable of transmuting base metals into noble ones like gold.
*   **Sulfur-Mercury Theory:** A mineralogical theory stating that all metals are formed from varying combinations of sulfur and mercury.
*   **Natures (ṭabāʾiʿ):** The fundamental qualities (hot, cold, moist, dry) that compose the four elements and, by extension, all substances.
*   **Science of the Balance (ʿilm al-mīzān):** A philosophical framework seeking to explain all phenomena through measures and quantitative proportions.
*   **Science of Specific Properties (ʿilm al-khawāṣṣ):** The study of the hidden powers of mineral, vegetable, and animal substances and their practical applications.
*   **Theurgy (ʿilm al-ṭilasmāt):** The "science of talismans," a form of magic involving the use of ritual objects.
*   **Sal Ammoniac (nošāder):** Ammonium chloride, a key chemical substance in Jabirian alchemy, derived from both mineral and organic sources.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Historicity:** Did a historical Jabir ibn Hayyan exist, or is "Jabir" a pseudonym for a school of anonymous Shi'ite alchemists?
*   **Dating:** While the corpus is generally dated to c. 850–950, some scholars argue for an earlier 8th-century core, suggesting the works may have been redacted over time.
*   **Authorship:** The sheer volume and diversity of the works make single authorship improbable. The identity and number of authors behind the pseudonym remain unknown.
*   **Relationship to Greek Alchemy:** The extent to which Jabirian alchemy borrowed from versus innovated upon Greco-Egyptian and Byzantine traditions is debated.

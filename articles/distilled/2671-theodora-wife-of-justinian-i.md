# Theodora (wife of Justinian I)

## Overview
Theodora (c. 490/500–548) was the wife of Eastern Roman Emperor Justinian I and a powerful empress in her own right. Rising from humble origins, she became Justinian's chief political adviser and played a crucial role in stabilizing his reign, most notably during the Nika riots of 532. Her life and legacy are central to understanding the politics, religion, and culture of the 6th-century Byzantine Empire, though the primary historical accounts of her are starkly contradictory.

## Timeline
- **c. 490/500** — Born in Constantinople to a bear keeper for the Hippodrome's Green faction.
- **Before 524** — Likely meets Justinian; his first wife dies.
- **524** — Emperor Justin issues an edict allowing prostitutes to marry senators, clearing the way for Theodora's marriage.
- **527** — Justinian becomes emperor; Theodora is crowned augusta (empress).
- **January 532** — The Nika riots erupt; Theodora's speech persuades Justinian to stay and fight, leading to the suppression of the revolt.
- **535** — Justinian's anti-corruption legislation (Novel 8.1) refers to Theodora as his "partner in my deliberations."
- **541** — Theodora helps orchestrate the downfall of praetorian prefect John the Cappadocian.
- **548** — Dies of cancer on 28 June; buried in the Church of the Holy Apostles.

## Body

### Early Life and Marriage
Theodora's early life is uncertain, but she was likely born in Constantinople around 495. Her father was a bear keeper for the Green faction in the Hippodrome. After his death, her family faced poverty. According to the historian Procopius, she worked as a courtesan and actress in her youth, a background that later sources corroborate. She traveled to North Africa as the concubine of an official before returning to Constantinople, where she met Justinian. A law from Constantine's time barred senators from marrying actresses, but Emperor Justin issued an edict in 524 allowing such unions, paving the way for their marriage.

### Role as Empress and the Nika Riots
Crowned empress in 527, Theodora was a formidable partner to Justinian. Her most famous intervention came during the Nika riots of 532, when a violent mob united against the emperor. As Justinian and his officials prepared to flee, Theodora delivered a speech arguing that it was better to die as a ruler than live as an exile. Her resolve steadied the court, and Justinian ordered a massacre of the rioters in the Hippodrome, saving his throne. Historians debate whether Procopius's account of her speech is literal or dramatized, but it underscores her decisive influence.

### Political and Religious Activities
Theodora was deeply involved in state affairs, engaging in court intrigues to remove rivals like John the Cappadocian and influencing ecclesiastical appointments. She was a devout Miaphysite (a Christian who believed Christ had one combined nature), while Justinian was a Chalcedonian (believing Christ had two distinct natures). She actively sheltered Miaphysite leaders and clergy, sometimes directly opposing her husband's policies. She also commissioned public works, including the Hagia Sophia, and worked to end prostitution, founding a convent for former sex workers.

### Historiography and Legacy
The primary source for Theodora's life is Procopius, whose works present conflicting portrayals. In his public histories, he depicts her as courageous and pious. In his posthumously discovered *Secret History*, he portrays her as a shameless, cruel, and lustful manipulator. Modern historians are divided on the accuracy of these accounts, with some seeing the *Secret History* as slander reflecting elite anxiety over her power, and others arguing it contains kernels of truth. Regardless, she left a lasting legacy, inspiring art, literature, and worship as a saint in Eastern Orthodox and Oriental Orthodox Churches.

## Terms
- **Augusta**: The title for a female emperor or empress in the Roman/Byzantine Empire.
- **Nika riots**: A violent uprising in Constantinople in 532 against Emperor Justinian, which he suppressed with great bloodshed.
- **Miaphysite/Monophysite**: A Christian theological position holding that Christ has one united divine-human nature, rejected by the Chalcedonian Orthodox and Catholic churches.
- **Chalcedonian**: Following the Council of Chalcedon (451 AD), which defined Christ as having two distinct natures (divine and human) in one person.
- **Hippodrome**: The large public stadium in Constantinople used for chariot races and political gatherings.
- **Praetorian prefect**: A high-ranking imperial official, often the chief minister of finance and justice.
- **Procopius**: A 6th-century historian and contemporary of Justinian and Theodora, author of the main historical sources about her.
- **Hormisdas Palace**: The former imperial residence that served as Justinian and Theodora's private dwelling before and during their reign.

## Debates and Open questions
- **Reliability of Procopius**: Historians debate whether his *Secret History* is a reliable account of Theodora's character and actions or a biased, exaggerated diatribe reflecting the prejudices of the senatorial class she threatened.
- **The Nika Riots speech**: Scholars question whether Theodora's famous speech as recorded by Procopius is a literal transcript or a dramatized literary device meant to comment on power and morality.
- **Religious sincerity**: Some historians suggest that the apparent conflict between Justinian's Chalcedonianism and Theodora's Miaphysitism was a deliberate political strategy to manage religious factions, rather than a genuine personal disagreement.
- **Early life details**: The exact date and place of her birth, and the full extent of her early career, remain uncertain and are reconstructed from contested sources.

# La bohème

## Overview
*La bohème* is a four-act opera by Giacomo Puccini, composed between 1893 and 1895, with a libretto by Luigi Illica and Giuseppe Giacosa. Based on Henri Murger's 1851 novel *Scènes de la vie de bohème*, it depicts the loves and hardships of young, impoverished artists in Paris around 1830. It is one of the most frequently performed operas in the standard repertoire, renowned for its emotional story and memorable music.

## Timeline
- **1851** — Henri Murger publishes *Scènes de la vie de bohème*.
- **1893–1895** — Puccini composes *La bohème*.
- **1 Feb 1896** — World premiere at Teatro Regio, Turin, conducted by Arturo Toscanini.
- **16 Jun 1896** — First performance outside Italy, in Buenos Aires.
- **22 Apr 1897** — UK premiere in Manchester by the Carl Rosa Opera Company.
- **13 Jun 1898** — French premiere at the Opéra-Comique, Paris.
- **26 Dec 1900** — Metropolitan Opera premiere in New York.
- **29 Nov 1924** — Puccini dies; performance of *La bohème* in Rome is stopped for Chopin's Funeral March.
- **1946** — Toscanini conducts a commemorative radio performance, the only recording of a Puccini opera by its original conductor.
- **1957** — A "missing act" by the librettists is discovered among Illica's papers.
- **1990** — Baz Luhrmann's modernized production premieres in Australia.
- **1996** — Jonathan Larson's musical *Rent*, based on *La bohème*, wins the Pulitzer Prize for Drama.

## Body

### Origin and Libretto
The libretto is based on Murger's novel, a collection of vignettes about Parisian bohemians, but focuses on the love story of Rodolfo and Mimì, ending with her death. The librettists combined two characters from the novel, Mimì and Francine, into a single character. While acts one and four closely follow episodes from the book, much of acts two and three is the librettists' original invention. Puccini faced a dispute with composer Ruggero Leoncavallo, who claimed to have offered his own libretto first; Leoncavallo completed a rival version, now rarely performed.

### Performance History and Reception
The premiere received a subdued audience response and polarized critics, but the opera quickly gained popularity across Italy and internationally. Key early premieres include Buenos Aires (1896), Manchester (1897), Berlin (1897), Paris (1898), and the Metropolitan Opera (1900). Despite its enduring popularity with audiences, some critics have dismissed Puccini's music as insufficiently sophisticated; composer Benjamin Britten called it "cheap" and "empty" in 1951.

### Synopsis
The story is set in Paris around 1830.
*   **Act 1:** On Christmas Eve, the poet Rodolfo meets the seamstress Mimì in their shared garret; they fall in love.
*   **Act 2:** At the Café Momus, the friends celebrate. Musetta, Marcello's former lover, arrives with a wealthy patron and engineers a reunion with Marcello.
*   **Act 3:** At a toll gate in late winter, a coughing Mimì confides to Marcello that Rodolfo's jealousy is a sham; he fears she is dying of consumption (tuberculosis) and wants her to leave him for a wealthier man. The couples quarrel but agree to stay together until spring.
*   **Act 4:** Back in the garret, Rodolfo and Marcello lament their lost loves. Musetta brings the gravely ill Mimì to Rodolfo. As friends leave to pawn belongings for medicine, Mimì and Rodolfo reminisce. Mimì dies peacefully, and Rodolfo realizes what has happened.

### Derivative Works
The opera has inspired numerous adaptations. Jonathan Larson's musical *Rent* (1996) transposes the story to 1980s/90s New York, with AIDS replacing tuberculosis. Baz Luhrmann's 1990 production set the story in 1957 and was later adapted for Broadway. The plot of the 2016 Korean musical *Maybe Happy Ending* begins with a parallel to Mimì and Rodolfo's meeting. Musetta's Waltz was adapted into the 1959 pop song "Don't You Know?".

## Terms
*   **Libretto:** The text of an opera.
*   **Bohemian:** A person, typically an artist or writer, who lives an unconventional, often impoverished lifestyle.
*   **Tessitura:** The range of notes in a part of a musical composition, or for a particular voice.
*   **Consumption:** An archaic term for tuberculosis, a deadly bacterial disease.
*   **Aria:** A self-contained piece for one voice, with or without orchestral accompaniment, in an opera.
*   **Duet:** A musical composition for two performers.
*   **Chorus:** A group of singers and their part in an opera.
*   **Supertitles:** Translations or summaries of an opera's text projected above the stage during performance.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   The initial critical reception was polarized, with some finding the music insufficiently sophisticated, a view echoed by later critics like Benjamin Britten.
*   The "missing act" discovered in 1957 explains Rodolfo's jealousy in Act 3 but was not used by Puccini, raising questions about the intended narrative flow.
*   The dispute with Ruggero Leoncavallo over the source material and libretto led to a rival, now obscure, version of the story.

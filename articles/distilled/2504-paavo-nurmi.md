# Paavo Nurmi

## Overview
Paavo Nurmi (1897–1973) was a Finnish middle- and long-distance runner who dominated athletics in the 1920s. Known as the "Flying Finn," he set 22 official world records and won nine Olympic gold medals, pioneering a scientific, analytical approach to running that transformed the sport.

## Timeline
- **13 June 1897** — Born in Turku, Finland.
- **1912** — Inspired by Hannes Kolehmainen's Olympic feats; began systematic training.
- **1920** — Olympic debut in Antwerp; won gold in 10,000 m and cross country.
- **1923** — First athlete to hold world records in the mile, 5,000 m, and 10,000 m simultaneously.
- **1924** — Won five gold medals at Paris Olympics; set world records for 1,500 m and 5,000 m within an hour.
- **1925** — U.S. tour ended 121-race unbeaten streak; set multiple indoor world records.
- **1928** — Won 10,000 m gold in Amsterdam; took silver in 5,000 m and 3,000 m steeplechase.
- **1931** — Set world record for two miles.
- **April 1932** — Suspended by IAAF council pending amateur status investigation.
- **July 1932** — Barred from 1932 Los Angeles Olympics two days before opening ceremonies.
- **16 September 1934** — Retired after final 10,000 m victory in Viipuri.
- **2 October 1973** — Died in Helsinki; given a state funeral.

## Body

### Early Life and Training
Born to a working-class family, Nurmi left school at 12 to work after his father's death. Inspired by Hannes Kolehmainen's 1912 Olympic success, he began a strict training regimen in 1912. During military service (1919–1920), he innovated training methods like running behind trains and using heavy boots, setting Finnish records that led to his Olympic selection.

### Olympic Career (1920–1928)
Nurmi debuted at the 1920 Antwerp Olympics, winning three gold medals. He pioneered the "even pace" strategy, using a stopwatch to distribute effort uniformly. At the 1924 Paris Olympics, he won five gold medals, including the 1,500 m and 5,000 m within two hours, despite a 45°C heat wave. His 1925 U.S. tour ended a 121-race unbeaten streak. At the 1928 Amsterdam Olympics, he won the 10,000 m but took silver in the 5,000 m and 3,000 m steeplechase.

### Amateur Status Controversy and Later Career
In 1932, the IAAF suspended Nurmi over amateur status allegations, barring him from the Los Angeles Olympics despite his planned marathon entry. The decision, influenced by Swedish official Sigfrid Edström, strained Finland-Sweden relations. Nurmi was never declared a professional but his suspension became definite in 1934. He retired undefeated in the 10,000 m and cross country.

### Later Life and Legacy
After retiring, Nurmi coached Finnish runners, raised funds for Finland during the Winter War, and became a successful businessman. He lit the Olympic Flame at the 1952 Helsinki Games. His analytical training methods and stopwatch pacing influenced future champions like Emil Zátopek and Lasse Virén. He is credited with popularizing running as a major international sport.

## Terms
- **Even pace strategy**: A running tactic of maintaining a consistent speed throughout a race, pioneered by Nurmi using a stopwatch.
- **IAAF**: International Amateur Athletics Federation, now World Athletics, the governing body for track and field.
- **Amateur status**: A requirement for Olympic athletes to not receive payment for competition, central to Nurmi's suspension controversy.
- **Cross country**: A long-distance running event held on open terrain, which Nurmi dominated and which was later removed from the Olympics.
- **3,000 m steeplechase**: A track event combining distance running with obstacles and water jumps.
- **World record**: The best verified performance in a specific athletic event, ratified by the IAAF.
- **Neurasthenic**: A historical term for a condition of nervous exhaustion, which Nurmi identified with.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Amateur status suspension**: The IAAF's 1932 decision to bar Nurmi remains controversial. Evidence came from German promoters alleging payments, but the Finnish federation found no proof. Swedish official Sigfrid Edström's role sparked accusations of political maneuvering.
- **Impact of U.S. tour**: Whether Nurmi's exhaustive 1925 American tour contributed to his later injuries and motivation issues is debated.
- **Legacy of training methods**: While Nurmi's systematic, year-round training was revolutionary, the extent to which his stopwatch-driven approach was uniquely influential versus part of a broader early 20th-century trend is discussed.

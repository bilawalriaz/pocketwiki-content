# War of the Pacific

## Overview
The War of the Pacific (1879–1884) was a conflict between Chile and a Bolivian-Peruvian alliance, primarily fought over control of the resource-rich Atacama Desert. It resulted in a decisive Chilean victory, leading to significant territorial gains for Chile and the loss of coastal territory for Bolivia and Peru, profoundly shaping the geopolitical and economic landscape of South America.

## Timeline
- **Feb 1878** — Bolivia increases taxes on Chilean mining company CSFA, violating the 1874 Boundary Treaty.
- **14 Feb 1879** — Chilean forces occupy the Bolivian port of Antofagasta without resistance.
- **1 Mar 1879** — Bolivia declares war on Chile.
- **5 Apr 1879** — Chile declares war on Peru after its secret alliance with Bolivia is revealed.
- **21 May 1879** — Naval battles of Iquique and Punta Gruesa; Peru loses the ironclad *Independencia*.
- **8 Oct 1879** — Battle of Angamos; Chile captures the Peruvian ironclad *Huáscar*, securing naval supremacy.
- **2 Nov 1879** — Chilean amphibious landing at Pisagua begins the Tarapacá Campaign.
- **26 May 1880** — Battle of Tacna; allied armies are decisively defeated, and Bolivia effectively leaves the war.
- **7 Jun 1880** — Battle of Arica; last Peruvian stronghold in the south falls.
- **13-15 Jan 1881** — Battles of Chorrillos and Miraflores; Chilean forces break Lima's defenses.
- **17 Jan 1881** — Chilean forces occupy Lima.
- **20 Oct 1883** — Treaty of Ancón signed between Chile and Peru, ending the war.
- **1904** — Treaty of Peace and Friendship signed between Chile and Bolivia.

## Body

### Origins and Causes
The war's direct trigger was Bolivia's 1878 tax increase on the Chilean-owned Compañía de Salitres y Ferrocarril de Antofagasta (CSFA), which violated the 1874 Boundary Treaty. Chile's subsequent occupation of Antofagasta led to war. Deeper causes included a long-standing rivalry for regional hegemony, the economic importance of guano and nitrate deposits in the Atacama Desert, and the political and economic instability of Peru and Bolivia compared to Chile. A secret 1873 defensive alliance between Peru and Bolivia, which Peru felt compelled to honor, drew it into the conflict.

### The Naval Campaign
The war began with a struggle for control of the Pacific coast. Chile blockaded Peruvian ports, while Peru used its navy, including the ironclad *Huáscar*, as a raiding force. Key early battles included Iquique and Punta Gruesa (May 1879). Chile's capture of the *Huáscar* at the Battle of Angamos (October 1879) was decisive, granting it naval supremacy. This allowed Chile to transport troops and supplies for land invasions and impose a crippling blockade on Peru.

### Land Campaigns and the Fall of Lima
With naval control, Chile launched amphibious invasions. The Tarapacá Campaign (Nov-Dec 1879) secured the nitrate-rich province. The Campaign of Tacna and Arica (1880) ended with the decisive Battle of Tacna, after which Bolivia withdrew from the war. The final Campaign of Lima (Jan 1881) saw Chilean forces defeat Peruvian defenses at Chorrillos and Miraflores, leading to the occupation of the capital. Peru's government collapsed, and a prolonged guerrilla war in the Andes (the "Campaign of the Breña") continued until 1884.

### Peace Treaties and Territorial Changes
The war concluded with the Treaty of Ancón (1883), where Peru ceded the Tarapacá province to Chile. The provinces of Tacna and Arica were placed under Chilean administration for ten years, after which a plebiscite was to decide their sovereignty. Bolivia signed a truce in 1884 and later the 1904 Treaty of Peace and Friendship, formally ceding its coastal Litoral department to Chile and becoming landlocked. The Tacna-Arica dispute was not resolved until 1929, with Chile keeping Arica and Peru regaining Tacna.

## Terms
- **Nitrate/Saltpeter**: Potassium nitrate and sodium nitrate, key fertilizers and explosives ingredients, mined in the Atacama Desert.
- **Guano**: Accumulated bird droppings, a highly valuable fertilizer.
- **Ironclad**: A type of warship with iron or steel armor, representing advanced naval technology of the era.
- **CSFA (Compañía de Salitres y Ferrocarril de Antofagasta)**: A Chilean mining company whose tax dispute with Bolivia was the immediate cause of the war.
- **Boundary Treaty of 1874**: Agreement setting the Chile-Bolivia border at 24°S and prohibiting tax increases on Chilean enterprises for 25 years.
- **Secret Treaty of Alliance (1873)**: A defensive pact between Peru and Bolivia against Chile, revealed during the crisis.
- **Campaign of the Breña**: The Peruvian guerrilla resistance campaign in the Andes following the fall of Lima.
- **Treaty of Ancón (1883)**: Ended the war between Chile and Peru, ceding Tarapacá to Chile.
- **Treaty of Peace and Friendship (1904)**: Formally ended the war between Chile and Bolivia, ceding the Litoral department.

## Debates and Open Questions
Historians debate the war's primary causes. Some emphasize economic motives, arguing Chilean nitrate interests pressured the government into war. Others stress geopolitical rivalry and the "inevitable" clash between a stable, expanding Chile and weaker neighbors. The role and intentions behind the secret 1873 Peru-Bolivia alliance are also contested—whether it was a defensive pact or an aggressive move to curb Chilean influence. The extent of foreign involvement, particularly alleged British support for Chile, is a persistent myth, though evidence shows European powers maintained official neutrality while selling arms to both sides. The looting of Peruvian cultural artifacts by Chilean forces remains a source of historical grievance.

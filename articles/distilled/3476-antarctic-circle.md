# Antarctic Circle

## Overview
The Antarctic Circle is the most southerly of Earth's five major circles of latitude, marking the northern boundary of the Antarctic region. It is defined by the unique solar phenomenon where the sun remains continuously above or below the horizon for 24 hours at least once per year. Its position is not fixed but drifts southward due to changes in Earth's axial tilt.

## Timeline
- **c. 3000 BC** — Earliest known human habitation in Antarctica (debated).
- **1820** — First confirmed sighting of the Antarctic continent.
- **1898** — First confirmed overwintering on the Antarctic continent.
- **1911** — First expedition to reach the South Pole.
- **1959** — Antarctic Treaty signed, establishing the continent for peaceful scientific research.
- **Present** — The Antarctic Circle is located at approximately 66°33′51.0″ south latitude.

## Body

### Solar Phenomena
The Antarctic Circle is the northernmost latitude in the Southern Hemisphere where the sun's centre can remain continuously above the horizon for 24 hours. Consequently, at any location south of the circle, the sun is visible at local midnight at least once per year (the midnight sun) and is below the horizon at local noon at least once per year (the polar night). Directly on the circle, these events occur exactly at the December and June solstices. However, atmospheric refraction and the sun's apparent disk shape mean the midnight sun can be seen about 50 minutes of arc (90 km) north of the circle, and the polar night can be experienced about 50 minutes of arc south of it at sea level.

### Human Habitation and Geography
There is no permanent human population south of the Antarctic Circle. The region is inhabited only by rotating teams of scientists at various national research stations. Historically, semi-permanent whaling stations existed on the continent. The circumference of the Antarctic Circle is roughly 16,000 kilometres, and the area south of it covers about 20,000,000 km², which is approximately 4% of Earth's surface. Most of the Antarctic continent lies within this circle.

### Position and Drift
The Antarctic Circle's current latitude is approximately 66°33′51.0″ south, but this position is not fixed. Its latitude depends on Earth's axial tilt, which fluctuates by more than 2° over a 41,000-year cycle due to tidal forces from the Moon's orbit. As a result, the Antarctic Circle is currently drifting southwards at a rate of about 14.5 meters per year.

## Terms
- **Antarctic Circle**: The most southerly major circle of latitude, at approximately 66°33′ south.
- **Midnight sun**: The phenomenon where the sun remains visible at local midnight.
- **Polar night**: The phenomenon where the sun remains below the horizon at local noon.
- **Axial tilt**: The angle between a planet's rotational axis and its orbital axis.
- **Refraction**: The bending of light as it passes through the atmosphere, which alters the apparent position of celestial objects.
- **Nutation**: A small, periodic wobble in Earth's axis of rotation.
- **Solstice**: The time when the sun reaches its highest or lowest point in the sky at noon, marking the longest or shortest day.

## Debates and Open questions
The source text notes that the exact current latitude of the Antarctic Circle may be slightly inaccurate because the figure does not account for the effects of astronomical nutation, which can be up to 10″.

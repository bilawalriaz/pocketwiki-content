# Seville

## Overview
Seville is the capital and largest city of Andalusia, Spain, situated on the River Guadalquivir. Its history spans over 2,200 years, from its Phoenician founding to its pivotal role as the gateway to the Spanish Empire's trans-Atlantic trade. Today, it is a major cultural, economic, and tourist center, renowned for its historic architecture, festivals, and flamenco tradition.

## Timeline
- **c. 8th century BC** — Phoenician settlement founded on an island in the Guadalquivir.
- **711–712** — Islamic conquest; the city becomes known as Ishbiliya.
- **1023** — Emergence of the independent Taifa of Seville under the Abbadid dynasty.
- **1248** — Conquest by Ferdinand III of Castile; incorporation into the Crown of Castile.
- **1503** — Designated headquarters of the Casa de Contratación, monopoly for trade with the Americas.
- **1649** — Great Plague of Seville reduces population by nearly half.
- **1717** — The Casa de Contratación is relocated to the Bay of Cádiz.
- **1929** — Hosts the Ibero-American Exposition, spurring major urban development.
- **1936** — Quickly falls to Nationalist forces at the start of the Spanish Civil War.
- **1992** — Hosts the Universal Exposition (Expo '92), leading to significant infrastructure upgrades.

## Body

### Historical Development
Seville's core was founded as the Phoenician town of Hisbaal, later becoming the Roman city of Hispalis. After the Islamic conquest in 711, it was known as Ishbiliya and became a major center, first as a taifa capital and later as the seat of the Almohad Empire in Iberia. Ferdinand III conquered the city in 1248, leading to a mass exodus of its Muslim population and the construction of Gothic churches like the Seville Cathedral.

The city's golden age began in 1503 when it was made the sole port for trade with the Spanish Americas via the Casa de Contratación. This monopoly made Seville one of Western Europe's largest cities. However, silting of the Guadalquivir and the 1649 plague led to a decline, and the trade monopoly was transferred to Cádiz in 1717. The 20th century saw the city suffer during the Spanish Civil War but later experience revitalization through major events like the 1929 Ibero-American Exposition and Expo '92.

### Architecture and Landmarks
Seville's historic center contains a UNESCO World Heritage Site comprising the Alcázar palace complex, the Cathedral, and the General Archive of the Indies. The Cathedral, built on the site of a former Almohad mosque, is the world's largest Gothic cathedral. Its bell tower, the Giralda, was originally the mosque's minaret. The Alcázar is a sprawling palace complex with origins in the 10th century, expanded by successive rulers in Mudéjar, Gothic, and Renaissance styles.

Other significant monuments include the Torre del Oro, an Almohad defensive tower; the Royal Tobacco Factory, now part of the University of Seville; and the Metropol Parasol, a modern wooden structure completed in 2011. The Parque de María Luisa, created for the 1929 exposition, features the Plaza de España, a semi-circular building with tiled alcoves representing Spanish provinces.

### Culture and Festivals
Seville is a global center for flamenco, which originated in the marginalized communities of the Triana district. The city's most famous festivals are Holy Week (Semana Santa), featuring processions by religious brotherhoods, and the April Fair (Feria de Abril), a week-long celebration of folklore, music, and dance.

The city's gastronomy is centered on tapas, with local specialties including fried seafood, spinach with chickpeas, and gazpacho. Bitter Seville oranges, grown on city streets, are exported for marmalade production. Seville is also a major operatic setting, with more operas set there than any other European city.

### Economy and Infrastructure
Seville is the economic hub of Andalusia, with a service-dominated economy. It is home to the only inland river port in Spain, located 80 km from the Atlantic. Major infrastructure improvements for Expo '92 included the AVE high-speed rail link to Madrid, the expansion of San Pablo Airport, and new bridges like the Alamillo Bridge.

The city has a modern public transport network including the Seville Metro, a tram system (MetroCentro), and an extensive bus network. Cycling has grown significantly, supported by the Sevici bike-sharing program. Research and development are focused on renewable energy, aeronautics, and biotechnology.

## Terms
*   **Taifa:** An independent Muslim-ruled principality in the Iberian Peninsula, especially after the collapse of the Caliphate of Córdoba.
*   **Mudéjar:** A style of Iberian architecture and decoration developed under Christian rule but heavily influenced by Islamic artistic traditions.
*   **Casa de Contratación:** The Spanish government agency established in Seville in 1503 to regulate all commerce and navigation between Spain and its overseas empire.
*   **Almohad:** A Berber Muslim reform movement and empire that ruled parts of North Africa and Iberia in the 12th and 13th centuries.
*   **Giralda:** The bell tower of Seville Cathedral, originally built as the minaret of the city's main mosque.
*   **Flamenco:** An art form combining song (cante), dance (baile), and guitar playing (toque), originating in Andalusia.
*   **Semana Santa:** Holy Week, the annual observance of the Passion of Christ in the week leading up to Easter, marked by elaborate processions in Seville.
*   **AVE:** Spain's high-speed rail service, with Seville connected to Madrid and other major cities.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   The exact date and circumstances of the city's initial Islamic conquest in 711–712 are debated, with sources indicating it was taken by Musa ibn Nusayr and then had to be retaken by his son.
*   The establishment of the Archive of the Indies in the 18th century is interpreted by some scholars as a decisive moment marking a shift in Spain's conception of its overseas territories from equal kingdoms to colonies.
*   The origin of the city's motto, "NO8DO" ("She has not abandoned me"), is popularly attributed to King Alfonso X but its historical verification is a matter of legend.

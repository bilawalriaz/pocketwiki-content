# Klemens von Metternich

## Overview
Klemens von Metternich (1773–1859) was a German statesman and diplomat who served as Austrian Foreign Minister and Chancellor. He was the central figure in European diplomacy for three decades, shaping the post-Napoleonic order through the "Concert of Europe" system of international congresses. His conservative policies aimed to maintain the balance of power and suppress liberalism and nationalism, defining an era of relative peace but also earning him criticism as an obstacle to reform.

## Body

### Early Life and Diplomatic Rise
Born in 1773, Metternich received an elite education at Strasbourg and Mainz. His early career was shaped by the French Revolution and Napoleonic Wars, which cost his family their ancestral estates. He married Countess Eleonore von Kaunitz in 1795, gaining entry to Viennese high society. He served as ambassador to Saxony (1801), Prussia (1803), and finally France (1806), where he skillfully navigated the court of Napoleon.

### Foreign Minister and the Napoleonic Wars
Appointed Foreign Minister in 1809, Metternich's first major task was to secure a détente with France, culminating in Napoleon's marriage to Marie Louise. He then masterminded Austria's shift from French ally to coalition partner in the War of the Sixth Coalition. His diplomacy aimed to curb Russian expansion and prevent a unified German state. He was instrumental in the coalition's victory, witnessing the Battle of Leipzig in 1813 and signing the Treaty of Fontainebleau that exiled Napoleon.

### The Congress of Vienna and the "Metternich System"
Metternich presided over the Congress of Vienna (1814–15), which redrew Europe's map after Napoleon's defeat. He secured Austrian gains in Italy (Lombardy-Venetia) and worked to balance Russian and Prussian power. The resulting "Concert of Europe" or "Congress System" used regular meetings of the Great Powers to maintain stability. Domestically, he became Chancellor in 1821, using censorship and a spy network to suppress liberal and nationalist movements, exemplified by the Carlsbad Decrees (1819) in Germany.

### Later Chancellorship and Revolution
As Chancellor, Metternich focused on preserving the Austrian Empire's unity against rising nationalism, particularly in Hungary and Italy. He managed the "Eastern Question" by supporting the Ottoman Empire as a buffer against Russia. His influence waned in the 1840s due to aging, internal Austrian politics, and the rise of rivals like Lord Palmerston. He failed to foresee the Revolutions of 1848; on March 13, popular unrest in Vienna forced his resignation.

### Exile, Return, and Death
Metternich fled to exile in London, Brighton, and Brussels until 1851. He returned to Vienna as an advisor to Emperor Franz Joseph, though his influence was limited. He advocated Austrian neutrality in the Crimean War and opposed the ultimatum that led to the Second Italian War of Independence. He died in 1859, having outlived his political generation.

## Terms
*   **Concert of Europe:** A system of international relations after 1815 where the Great Powers (Austria, Prussia, Russia, Britain, and later France) met in congresses to resolve disputes and maintain the balance of power.
*   **Balance of Power:** A core principle of Metternich's diplomacy, aiming to prevent any single state from becoming dominant in Europe.
*   **Legitimacy:** The conservative principle that only hereditary monarchs had the right to rule, used to oppose revolutionary and nationalist changes.
*   **Carlsbad Decrees:** A set of reactionary measures imposed on the German Confederation in 1819, which Metternich helped draft, to suppress liberal and nationalist activities.
*   **Eastern Question:** The diplomatic and political problems posed by the slow decline of the Ottoman Empire, particularly regarding the ambitions of Russia.
*   **Congress System:** Another term for the Concert of Europe, emphasizing the use of periodic international congresses to manage European affairs.
*   **Nationalism:** A political ideology emphasizing loyalty to a nation based on shared culture, language, or history, which Metternich saw as a threat to multi-ethnic empires like Austria's.

## Debates and Open Questions
Historians debate Metternich's legacy. Critics, especially in the 19th century, saw him as a reactionary who stifled progress and prevented necessary reforms in Austria, potentially contributing to later crises. Sympathetic historians, particularly in the 20th century, praise his diplomatic skill in maintaining a fragile peace for thirty years and correctly identifying the threat of Russian expansion. A key debate concerns his actual power: some, like A.J.P. Taylor, argue he was a "boring" figure with limited influence, while others view him as a master diplomat who shaped his era.

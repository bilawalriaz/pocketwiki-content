# History of atheism

## Overview
Atheism is the rejection of the belief that a deity exists. It has a long history, with philosophical roots in ancient India and Greece, and evolved from a pejorative accusation into a self-avowed position during the Enlightenment. Its development is intertwined with philosophical, political, and social movements, and it remains a significant topic in modern secular discourse.

## Timeline
- **c. 6th–5th century BCE** — Philosophical atheist thought appears in ancient Greece and India (e.g., Cārvāka school).
- **5th century BCE** — Atomists Leucippus and Democritus propose a materialistic philosophy.
- **c. 300 BCE** — Epicurus develops a materialistic philosophy where gods are uninterested in human affairs.
- **1566** — The term "atheist" first appears in English.
- **1674** — Matthias Knutzen publishes the first known explicit atheist writings.
- **1770** — Baron d'Holbach publishes *The System of Nature*, an early explicit atheistic work.
- **1793** — The Cult of Reason is established during the French Revolution.
- **1811** — Percy Bysshe Shelley is expelled from Oxford for writing *The Necessity of Atheism*.
- **1844** — Karl Marx describes religion as "the opium of the people."
- **1966** — Albania becomes a formally declared atheist state.
- **2006** — The "New Atheism" movement gains prominence with publications by authors like Richard Dawkins.

## Body

### Etymology and Early Usage
The English word "atheist" derives from the French *athée*, first used in the 16th century. The underlying Greek term *átheos* originally meant "godless" and was used as a censure for impiety. For centuries, "atheist" was exclusively a pejorative label; no one self-identified as such until the late 18th century.

### Ancient Philosophical Roots
In ancient India, schools like the materialistic Cārvāka (or Lokāyata) rejected the gods and an afterlife, advocating for a prosperous life in this world. In Greece, philosophers like the Sophists and Atomists explained the world through natural processes rather than mythology. While not all were atheists, figures like Epicurus argued that gods, if they existed, did not intervene in human affairs, removing the need for fear of divine punishment.

### Medieval and Renaissance Periods
During the Middle Ages, charges of atheism were often political or heretical accusations. In the Islamic world, freethinkers like Ibn al-Rawandi and Al-Maʿarri criticized religious dogma. In Europe, the Reformation and Renaissance fostered greater criticism of religious institutions, though this did not yet constitute widespread atheism. The term "atheism" was coined in 16th-century France.

### The Enlightenment and 19th Century
The Enlightenment fueled skepticism and secularism. Thinkers like Voltaire promoted deism, while Jean Meslier and Baron d'Holbach wrote explicitly atheistic works. The French Revolution saw the rise of the Cult of Reason. In the 19th century, atheism became linked with political and philosophical movements, including the rationalism of Ludwig Feuerbach, the communism of Karl Marx, and the philosophy of Friedrich Nietzsche, who declared "God is dead."

### 20th Century to Present
Atheism spread through ideologies like Marxism and secular humanism. Several states adopted official atheism, including the Soviet Union, China, and Albania, often involving the suppression of religion. In the West, atheism became more publicly accepted, leading to the "New Atheism" movement in the early 21st century, which advocates for science and secularism. Legal battles in the U.S. have furthered the separation of church and state.

## Terms
- **Positive atheism**: The explicit belief that no deities exist.
- **Negative atheism**: The absence of belief in deities without asserting they do not exist.
- **Deism**: The belief in a creator god who does not intervene in the universe.
- **Materialism**: A philosophical view that everything that exists is material or physical.
- **Cārvāka**: An ancient Indian atheistic and materialistic philosophical school.
- **Impiety**: A lack of reverence for gods or sacred things, often used as a charge against early atheists.
- **State atheism**: The official promotion of atheism by a government, often involving the suppression of religion.
- **New Atheism**: A 21st-century movement advocating for secularism and criticizing religion.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The classification of Buddhism and Jainism as "atheistic" is debated, as they reject a creator god but may include supernatural beings.
- The extent to which ancient Greek philosophers like Epicurus were truly atheist, or employed strategic ambiguity to avoid persecution, is discussed.
- The role of atheism in political revolutions, such as the French Revolution, and its relationship to modern secular governance remains a subject of historical analysis.

# Denmark

## Overview
Denmark is a Nordic country and constitutional monarchy in Northern Europe, forming the core of the Kingdom of Denmark, which also includes the autonomous territories of Greenland and the Faroe Islands. It is a developed, high-income nation known for its advanced welfare state, progressive social policies, and influential role in international affairs. Its history as a maritime power and its evolution into a modern democracy and mixed economy are central to its identity.

## Timeline
- **c. 955–965** — The Jelling stones, erected by Gorm the Old and Harald Bluetooth, are the first recorded use of the name "Denmark" within the country.
- **1397** — Denmark forms the Kalmar Union with Norway and Sweden.
- **1523** — Sweden secedes from the Kalmar Union.
- **1536** — Denmark converts to Lutheranism following the Count's Feud civil war.
- **1658** — The Peace of Roskilde cedes significant territories, including Scania, to Sweden.
- **1849** — The Constitution of Denmark is adopted, ending absolute monarchy and establishing a constitutional monarchy.
- **1864** — Denmark is defeated in the Second Schleswig War and cedes Schleswig-Holstein to Prussia.
- **1920** — Following plebiscites, North Schleswig is reunited with Denmark.
- **1940** — Germany invades and occupies Denmark.
- **1945** — Denmark is liberated after World War II.
- **1973** — Denmark (with Greenland) joins the European Economic Community (now the EU).
- **2009** — Greenland is granted self-determination.

## Body

### Etymology and Early History
The name "Denmark" likely derives from words meaning "flat land" and "borderland." The first recorded use is on the Jelling stones from the 10th century. Archaeological finds date to 130,000–110,000 BC, with agriculture evident by 3900 BC. The tribal Danes arrived during the Roman Iron Age (AD 1–400), assimilating earlier Jutish populations. A unified Danish kingdom emerged by the late 8th century, with the Danevirke fortifications built from the 3rd century onward.

### Viking Age and Medieval Kingdom
From the 8th to 10th centuries, Danish Vikings raided and settled in parts of Britain and France. The monarchy traces its roots to Gorm the Old (early 10th century). His son Harald Bluetooth Christianized Denmark around 965, likely for political reasons to deter the Holy Roman Empire. In 1397, Denmark formed the Kalmar Union with Norway and Sweden, but Sweden repeatedly broke away until its permanent secession in 1523. Denmark then entered a new union with Norway in 1536.

### Early Modern Conflicts and Colonialism
After Sweden's secession, Denmark fought several wars to regain control, losing significant territories in the 17th century, including Scania. The Peace of Roskilde (1658) was a major territorial loss, though Denmark later regained some areas. King Frederick III established absolute monarchy in 1660. Denmark prospered through neutral trade in the 18th century but was drawn into the Napoleonic Wars, leading to British attacks on Copenhagen and the dissolution of the union with Norway in 1814. Denmark retained Greenland, the Faroe Islands, and Iceland, and maintained colonial outposts in India, Ghana, and the West Indies.

### Constitutional Monarchy and Modern Era
A liberal movement led to the 1849 constitution. The defeat in the Second Schleswig War (1864) and loss of Schleswig-Holstein prompted a turn toward neutrality and internal focus. Industrialization began in the mid-19th century. Denmark remained neutral in WWI but was occupied by Germany in WWII (1940–1945). After the war, it joined NATO (1949) and the EEC (1973), negotiating opt-outs like retaining the krone. The Faroe Islands (1948) and Greenland (1979, with further autonomy in 2009) gained home rule.

### Government and Politics
Denmark is a constitutional monarchy with a unicameral parliament (Folketing) elected by proportional representation. The monarch's role is ceremonial. The political system emphasizes consensus, and governments are typically coalitions. The Danish Realm is a unitary state where Greenland and the Faroe Islands are self-governing in domestic affairs. Denmark is a founding member of NATO and the UN, and a member of the EU with opt-outs.

### Economy and Society
Denmark has a developed, high-income mixed economy with a strong welfare state, characterized by the "flexicurity" model (flexible labor markets with high social security). It is a net exporter of food and energy, with major industries in wind turbines, pharmaceuticals, and shipping. The population is over 6 million, predominantly of Danish descent, with growing immigrant communities. The official language is Danish, and the dominant religion is Lutheranism (Church of Denmark). Education is free through university, and the healthcare system is universal and tax-funded.

### Culture and Human Rights
Danish culture is progressive and egalitarian. It was the first country to legalize pornography (1969) and recognize same-sex partnerships (1989), later replacing them with gender-neutral marriage (2012). Notable cultural figures include fairy-tale author Hans Christian Andersen, philosopher Søren Kierkegaard, and filmmaker Lars von Trier. The country has a strong tradition in design, literature, and science.

## Terms
- **Folketing**: The unicameral parliament of Denmark.
- **Kalmar Union**: A personal union of Denmark, Norway, and Sweden from 1397 to 1523.
- **Absolute Monarchy**: A system where the monarch holds supreme authority, established in Denmark in 1660 and ended in 1849.
- **Flexicurity**: A Danish labor market model combining flexible hiring/firing rules with strong social security for the unemployed.
- **Danish Realm**: The unitary state comprising Denmark, Greenland, and the Faroe Islands.
- **Home Rule**: A system of self-government granted to the Faroe Islands (1948) and Greenland (1979).
- **Nordic Model**: A socioeconomic model combining free-market capitalism with a comprehensive welfare state and collective bargaining.
- **Schleswig Plebiscites**: 1920 votes that determined the border between Denmark and Germany in the Schleswig region.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The etymology of "Denmark" and the relationship between the "Danes" and the emergence of a unified kingdom are subjects of continuous scholarly debate.
- The exact meaning of the morpheme "Dan" and the "-mark" ending in the country's name are not definitively settled.
- The historical role and migration patterns of the Jutes versus the Danes in early Denmark are topics of ongoing research.

# Muscle

## Overview
Muscle is a specialized soft tissue and one of the four basic animal tissue types, defined by its ability to contract via the interaction of actin and myosin filaments. In vertebrates, it exists as three distinct types—skeletal, cardiac, and smooth—differing in structure, control mechanism (voluntary vs. involuntary), and location. Skeletal muscle drives voluntary locomotion and posture; cardiac muscle powers the heart’s rhythmic, involuntary pumping; and smooth muscle regulates involuntary functions in hollow viscera and vessels. All muscle derives from paraxial mesoderm during embryonic development (myogenesis).

## Timeline
- **Embryogenesis** — Paraxial mesoderm segments into somites; myotome compartment forms muscle progenitors (myoblasts).
- **Embryogenesis** — Myoblasts migrate along connective tissue frameworks, fusing into multinucleated skeletal muscle fibers.
- **Adult** — Skeletal muscle constitutes ~42% body mass (adult male) / ~36% (adult female).
- **Adult (aging)** — Oxidative DNA damage (8-OHdG) and double-strand breaks accumulate in skeletal muscle.

## Body

### Structure and Classification
Vertebrate muscle tissue is categorized into three types. **Skeletal muscle** is striated, voluntary, and anchored to bone via tendons or aponeuroses; its elongated, multinucleated fibers (10–100 µm wide, mm to inches long) contain parallel myofibrils segmented into **sarcomeres** (contractile units) that create the striped appearance. **Cardiac muscle** is striated and involuntary, found only in the heart wall (myocardium); its cells (cardiomyocytes) are typically uninucleate and connect at branching **intercalated discs**. **Smooth muscle** is non-striated and involuntary, lining hollow organs (esophagus, stomach, intestines, bronchi, uterus, urethra, bladder, blood vessels) and the arrector pili of skin. Invertebrates possess transversely striated, obliquely striated, and smooth muscle types; arthropods lack smooth muscle.

### Skeletal Muscle Fiber Types
Skeletal muscle fibers are classified by contractile speed and metabolic profile:
*   **Type I (slow-twitch/oxidative/red):** High myoglobin, mitochondria, and capillary density; sustains aerobic activity.
*   **Type IIa (fast-twitch oxidative):** Aerobic, rich in mitochondria/capillaries; red when deoxygenated.
*   **Type IIx (fast-twitch glycolytic):** Low mitochondrial/myoglobin density; fastest human fiber type; powers short, anaerobic bursts (fatigue often incorrectly attributed to lactic acid build-up).
*   **Type IIb (fast-twitch glycolytic/white):** Anaerobic, very low mitochondria/myoglobin; predominant in small mammals (e.g., rodents). A *Myh4* gene polymorphism in lab mice reduces Type IIb fiber, causing a "Mini-Muscle" phenotype with ~50% hind-limb mass reduction.
Mammalian skeletal muscle density is ~1.06 kg/L, ~15% denser than adipose tissue (0.9196 kg/L).

### Smooth Muscle Subtypes and Distribution
Smooth muscle divides into **single-unit (unitary)** and **multiunit** types. Single-unit cells contract as a **syncytium** (multinucleate cytoplasmic mass) via gap junctions; multiunit cells are individually innervated, allowing fine, graded control. It is ubiquitous: vascular smooth muscle in the tunica media of arteries/veins; lymphatic vessels; urinary bladder; uterus; reproductive tracts; gastrointestinal and respiratory tracts; arrector pili; ciliary muscle and iris of the eye. Kidney glomeruli contain smooth muscle-like **mesangial cells**. Stimuli vary by organ to enable specific functions (e.g., sealing orifices, peristaltic transport of chyme). Smooth muscle contracts slower than skeletal muscle but generates more sustained force with less energy.

### Cardiac Muscle Physiology
Cardiac muscle forms the myocardium, a thick layer between the epicardium and endocardium. It is self-contracting (autorhythmic) and autonomically regulated, requiring continuous rhythmic contraction for life. Coordinated contractions propel blood through systemic and pulmonary circuits (systole). Cardiomyocytes depend on coronary arteries for oxygen/nutrient delivery and waste removal.

### Development (Myogenesis)
All muscle originates from **paraxial mesoderm**, which segments into **somites** along the embryonic axis. Each somite differentiates into sclerotome (vertebrae), dermatome (skin), and **myotome** (muscle). The myotome splits into **epimere** (epaxial muscles: erector spinae, small intervertebral muscles; innervated by dorsal rami) and **hypomere** (hypaxial muscles: all others, including limbs; innervated by ventral rami). **Myoblasts** (muscle progenitors) either remain in the somite (axial muscles) or migrate along chemical signals following connective tissue frameworks (from somatic lateral plate mesoderm) to form limb/body wall muscles, fusing into elongated skeletal muscle cells.

### Contraction Mechanism and Control
All muscle types use **actin-myosin sliding** for contraction, regulated by **troponin** and **tropomyosin**.
*   **Skeletal:** Stimulated by motor neuron impulses via **acetylcholine** at the neuromuscular junction; voluntary (CNS), though reflexes bypass cortical awareness.
*   **Cardiac & Smooth:** Stimulated by internal pacemaker cells; propagate contractions via cell contacts. Many smooth muscle contractions also use acetylcholine.
*   **Neurotransmitter/Hormone Sensitivity:** Responses to acetylcholine, noradrenaline, adrenaline, and nitric oxide vary by muscle type and anatomical location.

## Terms
- ****Myogenesis**** — Embryonic process of muscle tissue formation from paraxial mesoderm.
- ****Sarcomere**** — The basic contractile unit of striated muscle, composed of overlapping actin and myosin filaments; repeating units create striations.
- ****Myofibril**** — Long, cylindrical organelle within muscle fibers, composed of serial sarcomeres.
- ****Intercalated disc**** — Specialized junction between cardiac muscle cells allowing rapid electrical and mechanical coupling.
- ****Syncytium**** — A multinucleated mass of cytoplasm not divided into individual cells; characteristic of single-unit smooth muscle and skeletal muscle fibers.
- ****Myoblast**** — Embryonic progenitor cell that differentiates and fuses to form skeletal muscle fibers.
- ****Somite**** — Segmental block of paraxial mesoderm giving rise to vertebrae, dermis, and muscle (myotome).
- ****Epaxial / Hypaxial**** — Muscle groups derived from epimere (deep back muscles) and hypomere (all other body/limb muscles) of the myotome, respectively.
- ****Myoglobin**** — Oxygen-binding protein in muscle; high concentration gives Type I/IIa fibers red color.
- ****Mesangial cell**** — Smooth muscle-like cell in kidney glomeruli involved in capillary tone regulation.

## Debates and open questions
*   **Fiber type nomenclature:** Historical confusion exists between Type IIx and Type IIb designations in humans; some older literature refers to the fastest human glycolytic fiber as Type IIB.
*   **Fatigue mechanism:** The attribution of muscle pain during intense anaerobic bursts to lactic acid build-up is noted as incorrect in the source, though the actual mechanism is not specified.
*   **Aging and DNA damage:** Oxidative DNA damage (8-OHdG) and double-strand breaks accumulate with age in rodent skeletal muscle; the causal role in human sarcopenia or functional decline is implied but not detailed.

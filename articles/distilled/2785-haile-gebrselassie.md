# Haile Gebrselassie

## Overview
Haile Gebrselassie is an Ethiopian former long-distance runner and businessman, widely regarded as one of the greatest in history. His career spanned track, cross-country, and road running, where he set 27 world records and won two Olympic gold medals. His achievements earned him the nickname "The Emperor of Distance Running," and he later became a successful entrepreneur contributing to Ethiopia's development.

## Timeline
- **18 April 1973** — Born in Asella, Ethiopia.
- **1992** — Wins 5000m and 10,000m at World Junior Championships.
- **1993** — Wins first of four consecutive 10,000m World Championship titles.
- **1994** — Sets first world record in the 5000m (12:56.96).
- **1995** — Sets world records in the 10,000m (26:43.53) and 5000m (12:44.39).
- **1998** — Sets world records in the 10,000m (26:22.75) and 5000m (12:39.36).
- **2000** — Successfully defends Olympic 10,000m gold in Sydney.
- **2007** — Sets marathon world record in Berlin (2:04:26).
- **28 September 2008** — Sets marathon world record in Berlin (2:03:59).
- **7 November 2010** — Announces retirement after New York City Marathon.
- **10 May 2015** — Officially retires from competitive running after Great Manchester Run.

## Body

### Early and Track Career
Born one of ten children, Haile ran 10 km to and from school daily, developing a distinctive crooked-arm posture. He gained international recognition in 1992, winning junior titles. From 1993 to 1999, he dominated the 10,000m, winning four consecutive World Championship titles. He set his first world record in the 5000m in 1994 and subsequently broke the 10,000m record in 1995. His career featured fierce rivalries, notably with Daniel Komen, with whom he traded the 5000m world record in 1997. In 1998, he reclaimed both the 5000m and 10,000m world records.

### Olympic Success and Transition
Haile won Olympic gold in the 10,000m in 1992 (junior) and defended his title in 1996 and 2000, becoming only the third man to do so. His 2000 victory over Paul Tergat was decided by 0.09 seconds. In 2003, he won silver at the World Championships behind Kenenisa Bekele in a race with a phenomenally fast closing 5000m split. An Achilles injury prevented him from medaling at the 2004 Athens Olympics. After 2004, he transitioned to road racing, debuting at the 2002 London Marathon.

### Marathon Dominance
Haile became a marathon specialist, winning the Berlin Marathon four consecutive times (2006-2009). He set the world record in Berlin in 2007 (2:04:26) and improved it there in 2008 to 2:03:59, a mark that stood for three years. He also set world bests in the half marathon and one-hour run. His later career included wins in Dubai and other major races, though he struggled with injuries and asthma, which led to his withdrawal from the 2008 Beijing Olympic marathon.

### Business and Later Life
Haile began investing in 1995, building a business empire that included Haile Resorts, a Hyundai car dealership, and Marathon Motors, which started assembling electric cars in 2020. He owns hotels, a coffee plantation, and employs thousands. He is an ambassador for the charity "Menschen für Menschen." In 2020, his properties were damaged during the Hachalu Hundessa riots, and in 2021, he pledged to fight in the Tigray War.

## Terms
- **World Record (WR):** The best performance ever ratified by World Athletics for a specific event.
- **World Championship:** The premier global outdoor track and field competition organized by World Athletics.
- **Marathon:** A long-distance road race of 42.195 kilometers (26.2 miles).
- **Golden League:** A former annual series of track and field meetings offering a jackpot prize for athletes winning specific events at all meetings.
- **Masters Age Group:** A category for athletes over a certain age (e.g., 35-39), with separate world records.
- **DNF:** Did Not Finish; indicates an athlete did not complete a race.
- **Exercise-Induced Asthma:** A condition where physical activity triggers airway narrowing and breathing difficulties.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The source notes his 2003 World Championship 10,000m silver medal race featured a closing 5000m split (12:57.24) that was the fastest ever in a global championship 10,000m final at the time, sparking debate about race tactics and the limits of human performance.
- His decision to withdraw from the 2008 Beijing Olympic marathon due to air pollution concerns was later regretted, as conditions were better than feared, raising questions about risk assessment in elite sport.
- Public skepticism existed regarding his potential entry into politics after retirement, given the unfamiliarity of celebrities holding office in Ethiopia.

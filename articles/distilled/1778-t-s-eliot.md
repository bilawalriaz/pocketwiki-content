# T. S. Eliot

## Overview
T.S. Eliot was a renowned poet, essayist, and playwright who revolutionized modernist poetry in the English language. His unique writing style, pessimistic language, and innovative verse structure have had a lasting impact on literature. Eliot's work is characterized by its complexity, depth, and exploration of themes such as disillusionment, spiritual decay, and the search for meaning. His influence extends beyond poetry to the fields of literary criticism, drama, and music. Eliot's legacy is a testament to his innovative and visionary approach to literature, which continues to inspire and influence writers, artists, and thinkers to this day.

## Body
### Early Life and Education
Eliot was born into a prominent Boston Brahmin family in St. Louis, Missouri. His father, Henry Ware Eliot, was a successful businessman, and his mother, Charlotte Champe Stearns, was a social worker and poet. Eliot's early life was marked by a love for literature, which was encouraged by his family. He attended Smith Academy and later studied at Harvard College, where he earned a Bachelor of Arts and Master of Arts in English literature.

### Literary Career
Eliot's literary career began in the early 1910s, when he published his first poems in various magazines and journals. His early work was influenced by the French Symbolist movement and the poetry of Dante Alighieri. In 1915, he published "The Love Song of J. Alfred Prufrock," which gained him widespread recognition. He continued to publish poetry and plays throughout the 1920s, including The Waste Land, "The Hollow Men," and Ash Wednesday.

### Conversion to Anglicanism and British Citizenship
In 1927, Eliot converted to Anglicanism and took British citizenship. This marked a significant turning point in his life and work, as he became increasingly focused on exploring themes of spirituality and faith in his writing.

### Later Life and Work
Eliot's later work includes the play Murder in the Cathedral and the poem Four Quartets, which is considered one of his masterpieces. He was awarded the Nobel Prize in Literature in 1948 and continued to write and publish until his death in 1965.

## Terms
* **Modernist poetry**: a literary movement that sought to break away from traditional forms and explore new ways of expressing the human experience.
* **Verse structure**: the pattern of rhythm, meter, and lineation used in poetry.
* **Allusion**: a reference to a person, place, event, or work of art that is outside the text itself.
* **Symbolism**: a literary movement that emphasized the use of symbols and imagery to convey meaning and emotion.

## Debates and Open Questions
* **Antisemitism**: some critics have accused Eliot of antisemitism due to his depiction of Jews in some of his poems, while others have defended him as a product of his time and cultural context.
* **Influence and legacy**: the extent to which Eliot's work has influenced later writers and artists, and the ongoing relevance of his poetry and plays in contemporary culture.

# Luxor

## Overview
Luxor, formerly ancient Thebes, is a city in Upper Egypt renowned as the "world's greatest open-air museum." It was a political and religious capital of Ancient Egypt during the New Kingdom and remains a major global tourism destination, with its economy heavily dependent on visitors drawn to its unparalleled archaeological heritage.

## Body
### Ancient Thebes
Luxor was the ancient city of Thebes, known in Egyptian as Waset ("city of the sceptre"). It became the political, religious, and military capital of Egypt during the New Kingdom, accumulating wealth from foreign expeditions. The city was the cult center of the god Amun, later syncretized as Amun-Ra, whose great temple at Karnak was Egypt's most important until the end of antiquity. Its political importance faded in the Late Period, but it remained the religious capital until the Greek era.

### Archaeological Discoveries
Luxor's modern identity is defined by continuous archaeological discovery. The city contains the ruins of the Karnak and Luxor temple complexes on the east bank of the Nile. Across the river, the West Bank features the Theban Necropolis, including the Valley of the Kings and Valley of the Queens. Major recent finds include the 3,400-year-old "lost golden city" of Aten (2021) and the tomb of Thutmose II (2025), the first royal tomb discovered since Tutankhamun's in 1922. These discoveries provide insights into both royal and daily life in ancient Egypt.

### Modern City and Economy
Luxor's modern economy is overwhelmingly reliant on tourism, with approximately 5 million visitors annually. The city offers hot air balloon rides and Nile cruises. It also has agriculture, particularly sugarcane, and local crafts like alabaster carving and pottery. The economy has been vulnerable to shocks, such as the 1997 Luxor massacre and the 2011 Arab Spring, which caused significant drops in tourist numbers. A controversial master plan aims to transform Luxor into a vast open-air museum, including the restoration of the Avenue of Sphinxes.

### Geography and Climate
Luxor is located on the Nile in Upper Egypt, about 670 km from Cairo. The city has a hot desert climate (BWh), characterized by extremely low rainfall (less than 1 mm annually), intense sunshine (about 4,000 hours per year), and very high summer temperatures, with average highs above 40 °C (104 °F). The Nile floodplain's expansion around 4,000 years ago increased arable land, supporting ancient prosperity.

## Terms
*   **Thebes:** The ancient name for Luxor, which served as the capital of Egypt during the New Kingdom.
*   **New Kingdom:** The period of Ancient Egyptian history (c. 1550–1070 BC) when Thebes was at its height as the capital.
*   **Amun-Ra:** The chief deity of Thebes, a fusion of the local god Amun with the sun god Ra.
*   **Karnak:** A vast temple complex on Luxor's east bank, primarily dedicated to Amun-Ra.
*   **Theban Necropolis:** The area of mortuary temples and tombs on the west bank of the Nile opposite Luxor.
*   **Valley of the Kings:** A valley in the Theban Necropolis where pharaohs of the New Kingdom were buried.
*   **Ushabti:** Funerary figurines placed in tombs to serve the deceased in the afterlife.
*   **Avenue of Sphinxes:** A 2.7 km processional road that once linked the temples of Luxor and Karnak.

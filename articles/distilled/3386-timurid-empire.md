# Timurid Empire

## Overview
The Timurid Empire was a late medieval Turco-Mongol, culturally Persianate, Muslim empire that dominated Greater Iran and Central Asia in the 14th and 15th centuries. Founded by the conqueror Timur (Tamerlane) in 1370, it became a major center of the "Timurid Renaissance," a flourishing of Persian art, literature, and science. Its legacy directly influenced the later Mughal Empire in India and the Safavid Empire in Iran.

## Timeline
- **1370** — Timur establishes the empire, taking Samarkand as his capital.
- **1398** — Timur invades and sacks Delhi, India.
- **1402** — Timur defeats the Ottoman Empire at the Battle of Ankara.
- **1405** — Death of Timur; succession disputes and civil wars begin.
- **1405–1447** — Reign of Shahrukh Mirza, a period of relative stability and cultural flourishing.
- **1447–1449** — Reign of Ulugh Beg, famed astronomer and mathematician.
- **1469** — Abu Sa'id Mirza is defeated and killed by the Aq Qoyunlu, permanently dividing the empire.
- **1507** — Uzbeks under Shaybani Khan conquer the last major Timurid cities, Samarkand and Herat.
- **1526** — Babur, a Timurid prince, invades India and founds the Mughal Empire.

## Body

### Foundation and Expansion
Timur, a warlord of the Turco-Mongol Barlas tribe, founded the empire in 1370. He saw himself as the restorer of the Mongol Empire of Genghis Khan and used the title "Gurkani" (son-in-law) to legitimize his rule by marrying a Genghisid princess. From his capital Samarkand, Timur launched devastating campaigns westward, conquering Persia, the Golden Horde, the Delhi Sultanate, and the Ottoman Empire, making him the preeminent Muslim ruler of his time.

### Stagnation and Decline
After Timur's death in 1405, the empire was weakened by civil wars among his sons and grandsons. While Shahrukh Mirza (r. 1405–1447) and his son Ulugh Beg (r. 1447–1449) maintained power and patronized a cultural renaissance, the dynasty lost control of Persia to the Aq Qoyunlu confederation by 1469. The remaining Timurid principalities in Central Asia and Khorasan were gradually conquered by the Uzbeks in the early 16th century.

### Culture and Legacy
The Timurid era was defined by a synthesis of Turco-Mongol military traditions and Persianate high culture. Persian was the official language of administration and literature, while Chagatai Turkic was the dynasty's home language. This period saw a golden age in Persian painting, architecture (characterized by large-scale buildings and bulbous domes), and poetry, with figures like the poet Jami. The dynasty's architectural style influenced the later Safavid and Mughal empires. The monetary unit "tanga" introduced by Timur was widely adopted in subsequent regional states.

## Terms
- **Timurid Renaissance**: The cultural and intellectual flourishing in art, literature, and science during the Timurid dynasty, particularly in the 15th century.
- **Persianate**: A society influenced by Persian language, culture, literature, and administration.
- **Barlas**: The Turco-Mongol tribe from which Timur and his dynasty originated.
- **Gurkani**: Meaning "son-in-law," a title used by Timur to legitimize his rule by linking himself to Genghis Khan's lineage through marriage.
- **Chagatai**: A Turkic language that was the native tongue of the Timurid family and a lingua franca in Central Asia.
- **Aq Qoyunlu**: A Turkic tribal confederation that defeated the Timurids and took control of Persia.
- **Mughal Empire**: The empire founded in India by Babur, a Timurid prince, in 1526.
- **Tanga**: A silver coin introduced by Timur that became a standard monetary unit in Central Asia for centuries.

## Debates and Open questions
- **Legitimacy and Lineage**: Scholars debate the authenticity of Timurid genealogical claims linking Timur directly to Genghis Khan. Later texts like the *Tuzuk-e Tīmuri* are considered doubtful by modern scholarship.
- **Cultural Identity**: The empire's identity is analyzed as a synthesis of "Turk" (steppe nomad) and "Tajik" (sedentary Persian) elements, with the terms often representing socio-political roles rather than strict ethnic descent.
- **State Symbols**: The meaning of Timur's "sign" (three circles in a triangle) is uncertain; it may represent rule over three parts of the world or his title "Sahib-Qiran" (ruler of three planets).

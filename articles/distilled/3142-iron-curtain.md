# Iron Curtain

## Overview
The Iron Curtain was the political, ideological, and physical boundary that divided Europe into two opposing blocs from the end of World War II until the Cold War's conclusion. It represented the deepening distrust and separation between the Soviet-dominated Eastern Bloc and the Western democracies, solidifying into a heavily fortified border that restricted movement and symbolized the global ideological struggle.

## Timeline
- **1939** — Molotov-Ribbentrop Pact signed, secretly dividing Eastern Europe between Nazi Germany and the USSR.
- **1945** — WWII ends; Soviet forces occupy Eastern Europe, establishing a buffer zone.
- **5 Mar 1946** — Winston Churchill delivers his "Sinews of Peace" speech in Fulton, Missouri, popularizing the term "iron curtain."
- **1947** — Truman announces the Marshall Plan; Stalin forbids Eastern Bloc participation.
- **1948-49** — Berlin Blockade and subsequent Allied airlift.
- **1955** — Warsaw Pact formed, formalizing the Soviet-led military alliance in the East.
- **1961** — Construction of the Berlin Wall begins.
- **1989** — Pan-European Picnic leads to mass East German emigration; revolutions sweep the Eastern Bloc.
- **9 Nov 1989** — The Berlin Wall falls.
- **3 Oct 1990** — East and West Germany reunify.
- **Dec 1991** — The Soviet Union dissolves.

## Body

### Origins and Definition
The term "iron curtain" predates its Cold War usage, appearing in 1918 and in Nazi propaganda. Its widespread adoption followed Winston Churchill's 1946 speech, which described a Soviet sphere of influence stretching "from Stettin in the Baltic to Trieste in the Adriatic." The metaphor evolved from a literal description of physical barriers to a broader symbol of the ideological, economic, and cultural divide between the communist East and the capitalist West.

### The Eastern Bloc
The Iron Curtain's eastern side comprised states under Soviet hegemony, including the Baltic states (annexed in 1940), Poland, East Germany, Czechoslovakia, Hungary, Romania, Bulgaria, and Albania. These became satellite states with Soviet-installed governments, later allied through the Warsaw Pact (military) and Comecon (economic). Yugoslavia was a notable exception, aligning independently after the Tito-Stalin split.

### The Western Bloc
West of the curtain, nations operated market economies and, with some exceptions, democratic governments. Most aligned with the United States and Canada through NATO. The European Community and EFTA served as Western economic counterparts to Comecon. The division was cemented by events like the Marshall Plan's exclusion of the East and the Berlin Blockade.

### Physical Barrier and Emigration
The Iron Curtain materialized as one of the world's most militarized borders, featuring fences, minefields, watchtowers, and a "death strip." The inner German border and the Berlin Wall were its most infamous sections. Following WWII, over 15 million people emigrated westward, but Cold War restrictions halted this flow. Between 1950 and 1990, migration was limited, with most movement occurring under bilateral "ethnic migration" agreements.

### The Fall
The curtain's collapse began with Mikhail Gorbachev's reforms (glasnost and perestroika) and his abandonment of the Brezhnev Doctrine. A chain reaction started with Hungary dismantling its border fence in 1989, followed by the Pan-European Picnic where hundreds of East Germans fled. This triggered mass protests and revolutions across the Eastern Bloc, culminating in the fall of the Berlin Wall in November 1989 and the dissolution of the Soviet Union in 1991.

## Terms
*   **Eastern Bloc:** The group of communist states in Central and Eastern Europe under Soviet influence or control after WWII.
*   **Warsaw Pact:** The 1955 mutual defense treaty among the Soviet Union and its Eastern Bloc satellite states.
*   **NATO:** The North Atlantic Treaty Organization, the Western military alliance formed in 1949.
*   **Comecon:** The Council for Mutual Economic Assistance, the Eastern Bloc's economic organization.
*   **Satellite State:** A country that is formally independent but under heavy political, economic, and military influence or control by another country.
*   **Brezhnev Doctrine:** The Soviet policy of intervening in socialist countries to preserve communist rule.
*   **Glasnost:** The Soviet policy of increased openness and transparency in government institutions and activities.
*   **Perestroika:** The Soviet policy of restructuring the political and economic system.
*   **Pan-European Picnic:** A peace demonstration held on 19 August 1989 at the Austrian-Hungarian border, which led to a mass escape of East Germans.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   The precise origin of the term "iron curtain" is debated, with earlier uses by figures like Queen Elisabeth of Belgium (1914) and Vasily Rozanov (1918) preceding Churchill's famous speech.
*   The role and intent of the Pan-European Picnic in precipitating the fall of the Iron Curtain are analyzed as either a planned catalyst or an opportunistic event that exposed the weakening resolve of Eastern Bloc regimes.

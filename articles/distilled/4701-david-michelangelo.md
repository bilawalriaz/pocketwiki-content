# David (Michelangelo)

## Overview
Michelangelo's *David* is a colossal marble sculpture created between 1501 and 1504, depicting the biblical hero before his battle with Goliath. It is a masterpiece of the Italian Renaissance and a potent political symbol of the Florentine Republic, representing the defense of civic liberties. Its creation, placement, and enduring legacy reflect the artistic, political, and cultural currents of its time.

## Timeline
- **1464** — Agostino di Duccio commissioned to carve a marble *David* from a large block for Florence Cathedral.
- **1466** — Work halts after Donatello's death; the block is neglected for decades.
- **1500** — The block is described as "badly blocked out and supine."
- **1501** — Michelangelo is commissioned to complete the statue from the existing block.
- **1504** — A committee debates placement; the statue is installed in Piazza della Signoria.
- **1873** — The statue is moved indoors to the Galleria dell'Accademia for protection.
- **1910** — A replica is placed in the original outdoor location.
- **1991** — The statue's left foot is damaged by an attacker.
- **2004** — A major cleaning is performed for the 500th anniversary of its unveiling.

## Body

### Commission and Creation
The project began long before Michelangelo. In 1464, the cathedral authorities (the *Operai del Duomo*) commissioned Agostino di Duccio to carve a young *David* from a massive, flawed block of marble. After Agostino's work halted in 1466, the block lay exposed for 25 years. In 1501, the *Operai* sought an artist to salvage the stone, which they called *il gigante* (the giant). Michelangelo, then 26, secured the commission on August 16, 1501, with a two-year deadline.

Michelangelo's process was guided by a Neo-Platonic belief that the ideal form already existed within the stone. He began carving on September 13, 1501, using a sequence of chisels—from the heavy *subbia* for roughing out to the serrated *gradina* for detailing. He worked directly on the marble without extensive preparatory models, chiseling layer by layer from the main face outward.

### Placement and Political Symbolism
Originally intended for the cathedral roofline, the statue's colossal size (5.17 meters) made that impractical. In January 1504, a committee of artists, including Leonardo da Vinci and Botticelli, debated its placement. It was ultimately installed in June 1504 at the entrance to the Palazzo della Signoria (the town hall), replacing a statue of Judith that also symbolized heroic resistance.

The *David* quickly became a political emblem of the Florentine Republic, representing the city's defiance against more powerful rivals and the Medici family. Its public nudity and location in the civic heart made it a charged symbol of the "body politic." It was attacked by stones in 1504 and had its arm broken during an anti-Medici riot in 1527.

### Artistic Interpretation and Form
Unlike earlier depictions showing David victorious over Goliath, Michelangelo shows him in a moment of tense anticipation before the battle. His pose is a Renaissance interpretation of the classical *contrapposto* (a weight shift creating a dynamic S-curve), conveying potential energy. The statue's proportions are atypical, with an enlarged head and right hand, likely intended to accentuate features when viewed from below on the cathedral roofline.

The tree stump (*broncone*) supporting the right leg is both a structural device and a possible reference to the Medici emblem. The figure's small genitals align with Renaissance conventions, and the presence of a foreskin reflects artistic, not religious, norms.

### Conservation and Legacy
The statue suffered from outdoor exposure, leading to cracks in its legs, possibly exacerbated by an uneven foundation and the weight of a plaster mould made in 1847. It was moved indoors in 1873. A major cleaning in 2004 used distilled water poultices. Scientific monitoring, including 3D scanning and vibration analysis, continues to assess its stability.

The *David* has been widely replicated. A famous plaster cast, made in 1847, was gifted to Queen Victoria and is now at the Victoria and Albert Museum, complete with a detachable fig leaf. A fibreglass replica was temporarily installed on the cathedral in 2010 to show its originally intended context.

## Terms
- **Contrapposto**: A sculptural pose where the figure's weight is shifted onto one leg, creating a dynamic, asymmetrical posture.
- **Operai del Duomo**: The committee of cathedral wardens responsible for commissioning and overseeing the construction and decoration of Florence Cathedral.
- **Non finito**: An artistic technique or state where a work is intentionally left unfinished, as seen in some of Michelangelo's sculptures.
- **Broncone**: The Italian word for the tree-stump support carved on the *David*, also a term for a personal emblem.
- **Socle**: A rectangular pedestal or base for a statue or column.
- **Encaustic**: A painting or coating technique using heated beeswax mixed with pigments or oils.
- **Finite Element Method (FEM)**: A computerized method for analyzing stress and deformation in structures, used to study the statue's cracks.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Original Intent**: Scholars debate whether Agostino di Duccio's initial figure was meant to be clothed, based on a marble node Michelangelo removed.
- **Symbolism of the Stump**: The *broncone* is interpreted both as a structural support and as a possible political reference to the Medici emblem.
- **Cause of Cracks**: The primary cause of the leg cracks is attributed to the statue's slight forward tilt, which developed due to foundation subsidence and the added weight of a plaster mould.
- **Ownership Dispute**: In 2010, the municipality of Florence and the Italian Culture Ministry disputed legal ownership of the statue based on historical documents.

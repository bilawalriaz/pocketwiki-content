# Kunming

## Overview
Kunming is the capital and largest city of China's Yunnan province, known as the "City of Eternal Spring" for its mild climate. It is a major economic, cultural, and transportation hub in Southwest China, serving as a gateway to Southeast Asia. Its strategic location has made it historically significant, particularly during World War II as a vital Allied supply center.

## Timeline
- **109 BC** — Han dynasty incorporates the Dian Kingdom territory into Yizhou Commandery.
- **765** — City founded as Tuodong in the Kingdom of Nanzhao.
- **1253** — City comes under Yuan dynasty control; a new city, Zhongjing, is founded.
- **14th century** — Ming dynasty retakes the city and builds a surrounding wall.
- **1910** — Completion of the Kunming–Haiphong railway connects Yunnan to Vietnam.
- **1937-1945** — WWII transforms Kunming into a major industrial, military, and refugee center.
- **1941-1945** — Kunming serves as the northern terminus for the "Hump" airlift and the Burma Road.
- **1952** — The city's ancient walls are demolished.
- **1999** — Hosts the World Horticultural Exposition, spurring major urban development.
- **2012** — New Kunming Changshui International Airport opens.
- **2014** — High-speed rail service begins with the Shanghai–Kunming line.

## Body

### History and Strategic Importance
Kunming has a nearly 2,400-year history, with early settlements dating to Neolithic times. It was part of the Dian Kingdom until its incorporation by the Han dynasty in 109 BC. The city's modern prosperity began in 1910 with the completion of the French-built Kunming–Haiphong railway. During World War II, Kunming was transformed into a critical military and industrial center after refugees and institutions fled Japanese occupation in eastern China. It became the headquarters for the U.S. Army Forces China-Burma-India Theater and the northern terminus for the vital "Hump" airlift and the Burma Road supply lines. After 1949, it developed as an industrial metropolis and was used for political exile during the Cultural Revolution. Since the 1980s, it has experienced rapid growth, tourism, and foreign investment.

### Geography and Climate
Kunming is situated at an altitude of 1,900 meters (6,234 feet) on the Yunnan–Guizhou Plateau, on the northern shore of Dian Lake. It has a subtropical highland climate (Köppen Cwb) with short, cool, dry winters and long, pleasant, humid summers. The city's annual mean temperature is 16.0 °C (60.8 °F), earning it the nickname "City of Eternal Spring." Its administrative area covers 21,600 square kilometers (8,340 sq mi).

### Economy and Resources
Kunming is a major economic center in Western China, leveraging its position as a transportation hub linking China with Southeast Asia. Key industries include copper, lead, and zinc production; tobacco processing; and the manufacture of machinery, chemicals, and textiles. The city is a significant horticultural center, famous for its flowers, with the Dounan Flower Market being the largest in China. Mineral resources include phosphorus, salt, and copper. As of 2008, it was home to 65 of Yunnan's top 100 enterprises, led by the Hongta Group.

### Transportation
Kunming is the primary rail and air hub for Yunnan. It is served by Kunming Changshui International Airport. Major railways converge here, including the Shanghai–Kunming and Chengdu–Kunming lines. The city is developing a high-speed rail network and is planned as the hub for the "Pan Asia High Speed Network." A comprehensive highway system connects Kunming to neighboring provinces and countries like Thailand, Vietnam, and Laos. An urban metro system with six lines is operational.

### Education and Research
Kunming is a major educational and research center in Southwest China. As of 2024, it was listed among the world's top 100 cities for scientific research output. Key institutions include Yunnan University (founded 1922), Kunming University of Science and Technology, and Yunnan Normal University. The Kunming Branch of the Chinese Academy of Sciences administers several research institutes in botany, zoology, and medical biology.

## Terms
- **Yunnan–Guizhou Plateau:** A large plateau in southwestern China where Kunming is located.
- **Dian Lake:** The largest lake in Yunnan and sixth-largest freshwater lake in China, on whose northern shore Kunming sits.
- **"The Hump":** The name given to the eastern end of the Himalayan air route used by Allied forces to fly supplies from India to China during WWII.
- **Burma Road:** A supply route from Burma (Myanmar) to Kunming, vital to China during WWII.
- **Kunming–Haiphong Railway:** A meter-gauge railway built by the French (completed c. 1911) connecting Kunming to Haiphong in Vietnam.
- **Karst topography:** A landscape formed from the dissolution of soluble rocks such as limestone, characterized by sinkholes and caves, surrounding Kunming.
- **Panthay Rebellion:** A 19th-century (1856–1873) Muslim revolt in Yunnan that caused significant damage to Kunming.
- **Sinicization:** The process of cultural assimilation to Chinese culture, credited to the Yuan dynasty governor Ajall Shams al-Din Omar in Kunming.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The etymology of "Kunming" is debated; a 2009 paper proposes it is a cognate of "Khmer" and "Khmu," originally meaning "people."
- The precise location and nature of the early townships on Lake Dian dated to 279 BC are lost to history.
- The city was prepared as an alternate Chinese National Redoubt during WWII and the Chinese Civil War but was never used for this purpose.

# Jupiter

## Overview
Jupiter is the fifth planet from the Sun and the largest in the Solar System, a gas giant with a mass 2.5 times that of all other planets combined. It formed roughly one million years after the Sun, and its inward migration during the primordial era shaped the architecture of the entire Solar System, clearing the inner region to allow terrestrial planets like Earth to form. Jupiter’s rapid rotation (under ten hours) drives extreme weather, including the Great Red Spot—a persistent anticyclonic storm observed since 1831—and generates the strongest planetary magnetic field in the Solar System. The planet hosts at least 115 moons, including the four large Galilean satellites, and a faint dust ring system. Since 1973, nine robotic probes have visited Jupiter, with the Juno orbiter currently revealing a diffuse core and complex polar cyclones.

## Timeline
- **~4.5 Gya** — Jupiter forms ~1 Myr after the Sun, at or beyond the snow line; reaches 20 M⊕ core, then accretes gas envelope in 3–4 Myr.
- **~4.5–4.0 Gya** — **Grand Tack**: Jupiter migrates inward to ~1.5 AU, captured in 3:2 resonance with Saturn; pair migrates outward to current orbits (~3–6 Myr total).
- **~3.9 Gya** — **Nice Model**: Kuiper belt infall drives Jupiter/Saturn into 1:2 resonance, triggering Late Heavy Bombardment.
- **7th–8th c. BC** — Babylonian astronomers record observations; Chinese "sui star" defines 12-year zodiac cycle.
- **1610** — Galileo Galilei and Simon Marius independently discover four largest moons (Io, Europa, Ganymede, Callisto).
- **1664–1665** — Robert Hooke and Giovanni Cassini possibly observe Great Red Spot (disputed).
- **1831** — Heinrich Schwabe produces earliest known detailed drawing of Great Red Spot; continuous record begins.
- **1892** — E. E. Barnard discovers Amalthea (fifth moon), last moon found by direct visual observation.
- **1955** — Burke & Franklin detect 22.2 MHz radio bursts, yielding precise rotation period.
- **1973** — Pioneer 10 performs first flyby; reveals intense radiation belts and refines mass/size estimates.
- **1979** — Voyager 1 & 2 flybys discover rings, Io’s volcanoes, and confirm Great Red Spot is anticyclonic.
- **1994** — Comet Shoemaker–Levy 9 impacts Jupiter; observed by Hubble and Galileo probe.
- **1995–2003** — **Galileo** orbiter studies system; atmospheric probe descends 150 km (57.6 min) before destruction.
- **2016–present** — **Juno** arrives in polar orbit; reveals diffuse core, polar cyclone clusters, shallow lightning, and Great Red Spot depth (300–500 km).
- **2023–2024** — ESA’s JUICE (launched Apr 2023) and NASA’s Europa Clipper (launched Oct 2024) en route to Jovian system.

## Body

### Formation and Migration
Jupiter is the Solar System’s oldest planet, forming ~1 million years after the Sun at or beyond the water snow line. It first built a solid core (~20 Earth masses, half heavy elements), then rapidly accreted gas once it exceeded ~50 Earth masses, opening a gap in the solar nebula and reaching final mass in 3–4 million years. The **Grand Tack hypothesis** proposes Jupiter formed at ~3.5 AU, migrated inward to ~1.5 AU due to disk interactions, was captured in a 3:2 resonance with Saturn, and both migrated outward to current positions over several hundred thousand years. This migration cleared the inner system of super-Earths, enabling terrestrial planet formation. Competing models place Jupiter’s birth at 18 AU or beyond the N₂ snow line (20–30 AU), with inward migration over ~700,000 years. The **Nice model** suggests later instability: Kuiper belt infall drove Jupiter and Saturn into a 1:2 resonance ~600 Myr after formation, scattering Uranus/Neptune outward and triggering the Late Heavy Bombardment. The **Jumping-Jupiter scenario** posits a fifth gas giant was ejected during this instability.

### Physical Characteristics
**Composition:** Jupiter is a gas giant—primarily hydrogen (76% by mass) and helium (24%), with traces of carbon, oxygen, sulfur, neon, ammonia, water vapor, phosphine, hydrogen sulfide, and hydrocarbons. The interior is denser (~71% H, 24% He, 5% heavy elements). Helium abundance is 80% of solar due to helium rain (droplets precipitating through metallic hydrogen). Neon is depleted to ~1/10 solar abundance.  
**Size and Mass:** Equatorial diameter 142,976 km (11.2 Earth radii); mass 318 Earth masses (2.5× all other planets combined). Barycenter with Sun lies 1.068 solar radii from Sun’s center (outside solar surface). Density 1.326 g/cm³. Jupiter radiates more heat than it receives from the Sun via Kelvin–Helmholtz contraction, shrinking ~1 mm/year. At formation it was hotter and ~2× current diameter.  
**Rotation:** Fastest in Solar System (System I: 9h 50m 30.0s at 7° N–7° S; System II: 9h 55m 40.6s at higher latitudes; System III: 9h 55m 29.7s magnetospheric—official). Axial tilt 3.13° (negligible seasons). Differential rotation: polar atmosphere lags equator by ~5 minutes. Rapid rotation creates oblate spheroid (equatorial radius 7% > polar).  
**Internal Structure:** Juno data reveal a **diffuse core** extending 30–50% of radius (7–25 Earth masses of heavy elements mixed into metallic hydrogen mantle), not a compact core. Possible causes: gradual accretion of solids/gas, or a giant impact (~10 Earth masses) a few Myr after formation. Above the metallic hydrogen layer (electrically conductive fluid), hydrogen transitions to a supercritical fluid (no distinct liquid/gas phase) at >1.3 MPa and 33 K. Helium/neon rain depletes upper atmosphere; diamond rain may occur. Core temperature ~20,000 K, pressure ~4,000 GPa.  
**Atmosphere:** Extends ~3,000 km below cloud tops. Cloud layer ~50 km deep in tropopause: upper thin ammonia-ice deck, lower thick ammonia-ice deck, possible water-cloud layer beneath. Zones (light, rising, ammonia crystals hide chromophores) and belts (dark, sinking, chromophores—phosphorus/sulfur/hydrocarbons—exposed to UV). Zonal jets reach 100 m/s (360 km/h). Lightning up to 1,000× terrestrial; Juno detected "shallow lightning" in ammonia-water clouds producing "mushballs" (water-ammonia slush/ice). Upper-atmosphere "elves/sprites" (blue/pink, 1.4 ms). Convection balances polar/equatorial temperatures despite low axial tilt.  
**Great Red Spot & Vortices:** Persistent anticyclone at 22°S, observed since 1831 (possibly 1665). Counterclockwise rotation, 6-day period, top ~8 km above clouds. Size shrank from ~41,000 km (late 1800s) to ~16,500 × 10,940 km (2015), contracting ~930 km/yr. Juno measured depth 300–500 km. Red color likely from photodissociated ammonia + acetylene. Polar cyclones: North—9 (1 central + 8 ring); South—7 (1 central + 5 large + 1 small). Oval BA ("Little Red Spot") formed 1998–2000 from merger of three 1939–40 white ovals. "Great Cold Spot" in thermosphere (24,000 × 12,000 km, 200 K cooler) is a depleted H₃⁺ ion density region driven by neutral wind–magnetic field interactions.  
**Magnetosphere:** Strongest planetary field (dipole moment 4.170 G, tilted 10.31°). Surface field 2–20 G. Generated by eddy currents in fluid metallic hydrogen core. Bow shock at ~75 Jupiter radii; solar wind elongates magnetosphere on Jupiter's lee side, extending it outward until it nearly reaches the orbit of Saturn. Io’s volcanic SO₂ creates plasma torus; ionization yields S/O ions forming equatorial plasma sheet co-rotating with planet, distorting dipole into magnetodisk. Electron cyclotron radiation produces decametric/decimetric radio bursts (0.6–30 MHz, detectable on Earth). Alfvén waves from Io interaction drive polar aurora and beamed radio emission.

### Orbital Motion and Observation
Orbit: 5.20 AU (778.5 Gm), period 11.86 yr, eccentricity 0.049 (near-circular), inclination 1.30° to ecliptic. Barycenter with Sun outside solar volume (unique among planets). Near 5:2 resonance with Saturn. Visual magnitude −2.94 (opposition) to −1.66 (conjunction); mean −2.20. Angular diameter 30.5–50.1″. Retrograde motion ~121 days near opposition (9.9° loop). Phase angle <11.5° (always near-full from Earth). Visible moons and belts in small scopes; Great Red Spot in 4–6 inch (10–15 cm) aperture.

### Planetary Rings
Faint, dust-dominated system (vs. Saturn’s ice). Three segments: inner **halo** (torus), bright **main ring** (sourced from Adrastea/Metis impacts), outer **gossamer ring** (two components from Thebe/Amalthea). Reddish in visible/near-IR. Possible fourth ring from Amalthea collisional debris. Age unknown, possibly primordial.

### Moons
**115 known** (16 >10 km). **Galilean moons** (visible in binoculars): Ganymede (largest, >Mercury), Callisto, Io, Europa. **Laplace resonance**: Io:Europa:Ganymede = 4:2:1 orbital period ratio. Resonance maintains eccentricities → tidal flexing → internal heating. Io: extreme volcanism (strongest tidal forces). Europa: geologically young surface, likely subsurface ocean. Ganymede: intrinsic magnetic field. Callisto: undifferentiated? **Classification**: 8 inner regular moons (near-circular, equatorial, formed with Jupiter); numerous irregular moons (captured asteroids/fragments, grouped by orbital elements, likely collisional families). Two ungrouped: Themisto, Valetudo.

### Interaction with the Solar System
Jupiter’s gravity dominates Solar System dynamics. Planetary orbits (except Mercury) align closer to Jupiter’s orbital plane than Sun’s equator. **Kirkwood gaps** in asteroid belt caused by Jovian resonances. **Trojan asteroids** at L₄/L₅ (Greek/Trojan camps; >2,000 known; largest 624 Hektor). **Jupiter-family comets** (short-period, semi-major axis < Jupiter’s) sourced from Kuiper belt, perturbed by Jupiter into inner system. **Impact rate**: ~200× Earth’s. 1994 Shoemaker–Levy 9 collision observed globally. Historical impact candidates (1664–1839) largely discredited; Cassini’s 1690 dark spot *may* be impact scar. **Shielding debate**: 2008 simulations suggest Jupiter perturbs comets inward as often as it ejects/accretes them—no net shielding; some argue it draws Kuiper belt comets inward, others that it deflects Oort cloud comets.

### Observation and Exploration History
**Pre-telescopic**: Babylonians (7th–8th c. BC) used trapezoidal integration for Jupiter’s motion (<50 BC). Chinese "sui star" defined 12-year zodiac; Gan De (4th c. BC?) may have seen a moon naked-eye. Ptolemy (2nd c. AD) geocentric model: 4332.38-day period.  
**Ground-based**: Galileo (1610) discovers 4 large moons—supports Copernican heliocentrism. Marius names them (1614). Fontana (1639) sees bands. Cassini (1660s): spots, oblateness, rotation period, differential rotation (1692). Schwabe (1831) draws Great Red Spot. Rømer (1670s) uses moon eclipse timing discrepancies to measure speed of light. Barnard (1892) finds Amalthea (last visual moon discovery). Wildt (1932) identifies ammonia/methane bands. White ovals observed 1938; merge to Oval BA (1998–2000).  
**Radio**: Burke & Franklin (1955) detect 22.2 MHz bursts → precise rotation. Three signal types: decametric (Io-influenced), decimetric (equatorial electron cyclotron radiation), thermal.  
**Spacecraft**:  
- **Flybys**: Pioneer 10/11 (1973/74): first close-ups, radiation belts. Voyager 1/2 (1979): rings, Io volcanism, Great Red Spot anticyclonic, lightning. Ulysses (1992, 1998): magnetosphere (no camera). Cassini (2000): high-res images. New Horizons (2007): gravity assist, Io plasma, Galilean moon studies.  
- **Galileo orbiter** (1995–2003): 7+ years, multiple moon flybys. Atmospheric probe: 150 km descent, 57.6 min, 300°C, 644 km/h winds, 90% H₂. Deliberate deorbit 2003 to protect Europa.  
- **Juno** (2016–present): Polar orbit. Discoveries: diffuse core, polar cyclone clusters, shallow lightning/mushballs, Great Red Spot depth, extended mission to 2025 with Ganymede/Europa/Io flybys. Radiation degrading instruments (JunoCam, UVS).  
- **Future**: ESA JUICE (launched 2023), NASA Europa Clipper (launched 2024). Proposed: CNSA Tianwen-4 (~2035), Interstellar Probe/Express (Jupiter gravity assist).

## Terms
- ****Gas giant**** — A massive planet composed primarily of hydrogen and helium; "gas" refers to planetary-geology classification, not physical state (interior is fluid/metallic).
- ****Snow line**** — Distance from the young Sun where temperature allowed volatiles (water, N₂, argon) to condense into solids, enabling rapid core accretion.
- ****Grand Tack**** — Hypothesis: Jupiter migrated inward to ~1.5 AU, locked in 3:2 resonance with Saturn, then both migrated outward to current orbits.
- ****Nice model**** — Scenario: Kuiper belt infall drove Jupiter/Saturn into 1:2 resonance ~600 Myr post-formation, scattering Uranus/Neptune and causing Late Heavy Bombardment.
- ****Metallic hydrogen**** — Hydrogen compressed to a fluid, electrically conductive state (pressures >50–400 GPa); generates Jupiter’s magnetic field via dynamo action.
- ****Diffuse core**** — Juno finding: heavy elements (7–25 M⊕) mixed into metallic hydrogen mantle out to 30–50% radius, not a distinct compact core.
- ****Helium rain**** — Helium droplets precipitate through metallic hydrogen layer, depleting upper-atmosphere helium to ~80% solar abundance.
- ****Laplace resonance**** — 4:2:1 orbital period ratio of Io:Europa:Ganymede; maintains eccentricities, driving tidal heating.
- ****Magnetodisk**** — Equatorial plasma sheet (from Io’s volcanism) co-rotating with Jupiter, stretching dipole field into a disk shape.
- ****Trojan asteroids**** — Asteroids librating around Jupiter’s L₄/L₅ Lagrange points (60° ahead/behind); >2,000 known.

## Debates and open questions
- **Formation location**: Did Jupiter form at 3–5 AU (Grand Tack), ~18 AU, or beyond N₂/Ar snow lines (20–40 AU)? Composition (noble gas enrichment) favors distant origin, but migration timescales are contested.
- **Core nature**: Is the diffuse core primordial (gradual accretion) or due to a giant impact (~10 M⊕) post-formation?
- **Great Red Spot longevity**: Models predict stability, but observed shrinkage (930 km/yr) may signal dissipation; depth (300–500 km) is shallower than expected.
- **Water abundance**: Galileo probe entered a "hot spot" (dry downdraft); global deep water abundance remains uncertain—key for formation models.
- **Impact shielding**: Does Jupiter protect or endanger inner planets? Simulations show no net decrease in comet flux; debate continues (Kuiper vs. Oort cloud sources).
- **Satellite formation**: Did Galilean moons form in a circumplanetary disk (regular) or via capture? Irregular moon groups suggest collisional breakup of captured bodies, but details are unresolved.
- **Ring age**: Primordial vs. continuously replenished by impacts on inner moons?

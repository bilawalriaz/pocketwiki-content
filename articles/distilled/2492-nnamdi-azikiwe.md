# Nnamdi Azikiwe

## Overview
Nnamdi Azikiwe (1904–1996), known as "Zik of Africa," was a Nigerian statesman, journalist, and revolutionary leader. He is widely regarded as the father of Nigerian nationalism and a key architect of the country's independence in 1960. He served as Nigeria's first indigenous Governor-General (1960–1963) and its first ceremonial President (1963–1966).

## Timeline
- **16 Nov 1904** — Born in Zungeru, Northern Nigeria.
- **1934** — Returns to Africa; becomes founding editor of the *African Morning Post* in Accra, Gold Coast.
- **1937** — Founds the *West African Pilot* in Lagos, a key tool for promoting Nigerian nationalism.
- **1944** — Co-founds the National Council of Nigeria and the Cameroons (NCNC) with Herbert Macaulay.
- **1954** — Becomes Premier of Nigeria's Eastern Region.
- **1 Oct 1960** — Nigeria gains independence; Azikiwe becomes Senate President.
- **Nov 1960** — Inaugurated as the first indigenous Governor-General of Nigeria.
- **1 Oct 1963** — Nigeria becomes a republic; Azikiwe is sworn in as its first President.
- **1966** — Military coup ends the First Republic; Azikiwe's presidency concludes.
- **11 May 1996** — Dies in Enugu, Nigeria, at age 91.

## Body

### Early Life and Education
Born to Igbo parents, Azikiwe learned Hausa, Igbo, and Yoruba, giving him a multi-ethnic perspective. Inspired by Pan-Africanist Marcus Garvey and educator James Aggrey, he pursued higher education in the United States, attending Storer College, Howard University, Lincoln University, and the University of Pennsylvania. He earned multiple degrees, including master's in religion/philosophy and anthropology. His experiences with racial discrimination in the U.S. and colonial service in Nigeria fueled his anti-colonial activism.

### Journalism and Political Activism
Azikiwe's journalism was central to his nationalist mission. As editor of the *African Morning Post* (1934–1937), he promoted radical Pan-Africanism and was tried for sedition (later acquitted). Returning to Nigeria, he founded the *West African Pilot* (1937) and the Zik Group of newspapers, which used sensationalism and human-interest stories to build a mass readership and advocate for independence. His writings attacked colonial injustice and inspired the Zikist Movement, a militant youth wing of his party, the NCNC. The colonial government banned his papers during the 1945 general strike, which he helped lead.

### Political Career and Road to Independence
Azikiwe co-founded the NCNC in 1944, which became a major vehicle for the independence movement. He opposed the Richards Constitution (1947) for its limited African representation, leading a delegation to London. After the Macpherson Constitution (1951) created regional assemblies, he moved to the Eastern Region, becoming its Premier in 1954. In that role, he promoted economic development, founding institutions like the African Continental Bank and the University of Nigeria, Nsukka (1960). His NCNC party won the most votes in the 1959 pre-independence election, but formed a coalition government with the Northern People's Congress (NPC), making Abubakar Tafawa Balewa Prime Minister.

### Governor-General and Presidency
At independence in 1960, Azikiwe became the first indigenous Governor-General, a ceremonial role representing the British monarch. In 1963, Nigeria became a republic, and he was redesignated as its first ceremonial President. His presidency was marked by political instability, including a controversial 1964 federal election marred by boycotts and violence. He refused to call on Balewa to form a government for several days, creating a constitutional crisis. His term ended with the January 1966 military coup.

### Later Life and Legacy
After the coup, Azikiwe initially supported the secessionist state of Biafra during the Nigerian Civil War (1967–1970) but later appealed for negotiation. He remained a revered elder statesman, attempting political comebacks in 1979 and 1983. His legacy is monumental: he is celebrated for uniting diverse Nigerians through his newspapers and political activism, founding key educational and economic institutions, and articulating the philosophy of "Zikism," which called for mental emancipation and African self-determination. His portrait appears on Nigeria's ₦500 banknote.

## Terms
*   **Pan-Africanism:** A worldwide movement that aims to encourage and strengthen bonds of solidarity between all indigenous and diaspora people of African descent.
*   **Zikism:** The political ideology of Azikiwe, advocating for decolonizing the mind through five pillars: spiritual balance, social regeneration, economic determinism, mental emancipation, and risorgimento (resurgence) nationalism.
*   **NCNC (National Council of Nigeria and the Cameroons):** A major political party co-founded by Azikiwe in 1944, which was instrumental in the struggle for Nigerian independence.
*   **Zikist Movement:** A militant, non-violent youth movement formed in 1946 to defend Azikiwe's ideals and advocate for self-government, later banned by the colonial government in 1950.
*   **Richards Constitution (1947):** A British-imposed constitution that increased but still limited African representation in the Legislative Council, opposed by nationalists like Azikiwe.
*   **Macpherson Constitution (1951):** A constitution that replaced the Richards Constitution, establishing elected regional Houses of Assembly and a federal structure.
*   **First Nigerian Republic (1963–1966):** Nigeria's initial republican government, with Azikiwe as ceremonial President and Balewa as Prime Minister, ended by a military coup.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Assassination Plot Allegation:** During the 1945 general strike, Azikiwe claimed there was a colonial plot to assassinate him, which he used to go into hiding. Some contemporaries, particularly Yoruba politicians, believed he fabricated the story to boost his profile and newspaper circulation.
*   **Role in the Biafran War:** Azikiwe's initial support for the secessionist Republic of Biafra and his later shift to advocating for negotiation with the federal government remain points of analysis regarding his motives and influence during the civil war.
*   **Ethnic Politics:** While championing a unified, de-tribalized Nigerian nationalism, Azikiwe's political career was also marked by ethnic tensions, such as his resignation from the Nigerian Youth Movement (NYM) over alleged Yoruba dominance and the subsequent ethnic split in the press between his *Pilot* and the NYM's *Daily Service*.

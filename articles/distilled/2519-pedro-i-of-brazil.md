# Pedro I of Brazil

## Overview
Dom Pedro I (1798–1834) was the founder and first Emperor of Brazil, whose declaration of independence in 1822 created the largest nation in Latin America. He also briefly reigned as King of Portugal, where he later fought to restore liberal constitutional government. His life bridged the transition from absolutism to representative government in both countries, making him a pivotal figure in Atlantic world history.

## Timeline
- **12 October 1798** — Born in Lisbon, Portugal, as fourth child of Prince John (later King John VI)
- **1807** — Royal family flees French invasion, relocates to Brazil
- **17 October 1820** — Liberal Revolution in Portugal; Pedro's father later sends him to rule Brazil as regent
- **9 January 1822** — "Dia do Fico": Pedro vows to remain in Brazil against Portuguese orders
- **7 September 1822** — Declares Brazilian independence ("Independence or Death")
- **12 October 1822** — Acclaimed Emperor Pedro I of Brazil
- **10 March 1826** — Becomes King Pedro IV of Portugal upon father's death
- **2 May 1826** — Abdicates Portuguese throne in favor of daughter Maria II
- **7 April 1831** — Abdicates Brazilian throne in favor of son Pedro II
- **24 September 1834** — Dies of tuberculosis in Portugal after restoring liberal government

## Body

### Early Life and Education
Born into the House of Braganza, Pedro was the grandson of King Peter III and Queen Maria I of Portugal. His parents' unhappy marriage—his mother Carlota Joaquina was Spanish and allegedly unfaithful—created a difficult childhood. When Napoleon invaded Portugal in 1807, the nine-year-old Pedro fled with the royal family to Brazil, Portugal's largest colony. His education was broad but incomplete; historians note he was intelligent but impulsive and never learned self-control. He excelled in physical pursuits like horsemanship and music, composing Brazil's Independence Anthem.

### Path to Brazilian Independence
The Liberal Revolution of 1820 in Portugal threatened Brazil's autonomy. Pedro's father returned to Portugal in 1821, leaving Pedro as regent. When the Portuguese Cortes (parliament) ordered Pedro's return and dissolved Brazil's government, he refused, declaring "I am staying" on 9 January 1822. After Portuguese troops mutinied against him, Pedro rallied Brazilian forces and expelled them. On 7 September 1822, receiving news that Portugal rejected Brazilian self-governance, he declared independence. He was crowned Emperor on 12 October 1822 and defeated remaining Portuguese loyalists by early 1824.

### Constitutional Emperor and Early Crises
Pedro oversaw Brazil's first Constitution in 1824, creating a highly centralized state. He crushed the Confederation of the Equator, a secessionist revolt in the northeast. Portugal recognized Brazilian independence in 1825 through a humiliating treaty that required Brazil to pay reparations and grant Britain favorable trade terms. In 1826, Pedro briefly became King of Portugal but abdicated in favor of his daughter Maria II, hoping to preserve liberal constitutional government there.

### Personal and Political Turmoil
Pedro's reign was marked by the Cisplatine War (1825–1828), which ended with Brazil losing the province of Cisplatina (later Uruguay). His scandalous affair with Domitila de Castro damaged his reputation, while his wife Maria Leopoldina died in 1826 after a miscarriage. In Portugal, his brother Miguel usurped Maria II's throne in 1828, establishing absolutist rule. Pedro married Amélie of Leuchtenberg in 1829 and reformed his behavior.

### Abdication and Later Life
Political struggle between Pedro and the Liberal Party over ministerial control intensified from 1826 to 1831. Despite concessions, including naming a Liberal cabinet in March 1831, Pedro faced street violence and military desertion. On 7 April 1831, he abdicated in favor of his son Pedro II and sailed for Europe. He spent his final years fighting to restore his daughter Maria II to Portugal's throne, defeating absolutist forces in the Portuguese Civil War (1832–1834). He died of tuberculosis on 24 September 1834, having helped establish liberal government in both Brazil and Portugal.

## Terms
- **Cortes**: The Portuguese parliament, summoned to create a constitution after the 1820 Liberal Revolution.
- **Regent**: A ruler who governs in place of a monarch; Pedro served as regent for his father John VI in Brazil.
- **Constitutional monarchy**: A system where a monarch's powers are limited by a constitution and shared with elected representatives.
- **Confederation of the Equator**: A short-lived 1824 secessionist revolt in northeastern Brazil against Pedro's centralized government.
- **Cisplatina War (1825–1828)**: Conflict between Brazil and the United Provinces of the Río de la Plata over the province of Cisplatina, ending with its independence as Uruguay.
- **Absolutism**: A system where a monarch holds unlimited power without constitutional constraints.
- **Liberalism**: In this context, support for constitutional government, individual rights, and representative institutions.

## Debates and Open Questions
- Pedro's personal character remains debated: historians acknowledge his intelligence and energy but note his impulsiveness, emotional volatility, and inability to exercise self-control.
- The extent of his commitment to abolition is questioned; while he expressed anti-slavery views, he took no concrete action against slavery as emperor, partly due to opposition from slave-owning legislators.
- His legacy is contested between those who see him as a heroic liberator who spread liberal ideals and those who emphasize his political failures, personal scandals, and the humiliating terms of Brazil's independence recognition.
- The role of his Portuguese birth in fueling opposition during his Brazilian reign remains a point of historical analysis, as critics used it to question his loyalty.

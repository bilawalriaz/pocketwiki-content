# Sebastião José de Carvalho e Melo, 1st Marquis of Pombal

## Overview
Sebastião José de Carvalho e Melo, the Marquis of Pombal (1699–1782), was the dominant Portuguese statesman during the reign of King Joseph I (1750–1777). He implemented sweeping Enlightenment-inspired reforms to centralize and modernize Portugal and its empire, but his autocratic methods and brutal suppression of opposition made him a highly controversial figure.

## Body

### Rise to Power
Born to minor nobility, Pombal studied law and briefly served in the army before his uncle secured him diplomatic posts. He served as ambassador to Great Britain (1738) and Austria (1745), where he studied foreign commercial and political systems. Upon the accession of Joseph I in 1750, Pombal was appointed Secretary of State for Foreign Affairs. He gained the king's full confidence through his decisive management of the 1755 Lisbon earthquake, becoming the de facto chief minister by 1755.

### The Pombaline Reforms
Pombal, influenced by Enlightenment ideals, enacted ambitious reforms to centralize administration and modernize Portugal's economy. He established royal monopolies and chartered companies, such as the Douro Wine Company, to regulate trade and production. His economic policies aimed to make the empire self-sufficient, which included promoting the Atlantic slave trade to Brazil while banning slave imports to mainland Portugal and India. He also reformed education by expelling the Jesuits in 1759 and creating secular public schools, though these educational reforms were largely ineffective in the short term.

### Consolidation of Power and the Jesuits
Pombal's authority was cemented after the Távora affair of 1758–1759. Following an assassination attempt on the king, Pombal used a special tribunal to convict and brutally execute members of the aristocratic Távora family and the Duke of Aveiro. He accused the Society of Jesus (Jesuits) of involvement, using this as a pretext to expel the order from Portugal and its empire in 1759 and confiscate their assets. He placed the Portuguese Inquisition under his control and used it to persecute opponents, including the Jesuit Gabriel Malagrida, who was executed in 1761.

### Foreign Policy and the Spanish Invasion
Pombal sought to reduce Portuguese dependence on Britain but maintained the Anglo-Portuguese Alliance. When Spain and France invaded Portugal in 1762 during the Seven Years' War, Pombal requested British military aid. The combined Anglo-Portuguese forces repelled the invasion, inflicting heavy casualties on the Bourbon armies. The subsequent Treaty of Paris restored most Portuguese territories, but Pombal grew wary of rising British power.

### Downfall and Legacy
After King Joseph I's death in 1777, his successor Queen Maria I, who loathed Pombal, forced him from office. He was prosecuted for abusing his power, stripped of his titles, and exiled to his estates, where he died in 1782. His legacy remains controversial; he is both celebrated as a modernizing reformer and condemned as a brutal autocrat.

## Terms
*   **Estrangeirado:** A Portuguese intellectual or statesman influenced by foreign, particularly British, ideas and models.
*   **Mercantilism:** An economic system where the state seeks to increase national wealth through strict regulation of all commercial activity, often via monopolies and chartered companies.
*   **Pombaline Reforms:** The series of administrative, economic, and educational reforms implemented by the Marquis of Pombal to centralize and modernize Portugal.
*   **Society of Jesus (Jesuits):** A Catholic religious order that was expelled from Portugal and its empire by Pombal in 1759.
*   **Távora affair:** A 1758–1759 conspiracy and trial in which Pombal crushed aristocratic opposition and used it to justify expelling the Jesuits.
*   **New Christians:** Jews who had converted to Christianity to escape the Portuguese Inquisition; Pombal granted them civil rights.
*   **Anglo-Portuguese Alliance:** The world's oldest active alliance, established in 1373, which was activated during the Seven Years' War.
*   **Pombaline architectural style:** An earthquake-resistant construction method developed for rebuilding Lisbon after the 1755 disaster, using wooden frameworks.

## Debates and open questions
*   **Effectiveness of Reforms:** Modern economists consider Pombal's economic policies to have largely failed in modernizing the Portuguese or Brazilian economy.
*   **Motivations for Anti-Jesuit Campaign:** Historians debate whether Pombal's persecution of the Jesuits was driven by Enlightenment ideals, a desire to seize their wealth and power, or a combination of both.
*   **Legacy:** Pombal's historical reputation is deeply divided, oscillating between that of a visionary reformer and a tyrannical despot.

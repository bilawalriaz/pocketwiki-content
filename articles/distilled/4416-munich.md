# Munich

## Overview
Munich is the capital and most populous city of Bavaria, Germany, and the nation's third-largest city. It is a major global hub for science, technology, finance, and culture, known for its high standard of living, historic architecture, and the annual Oktoberfest. Its history as a ducal, royal, and political capital has shaped its development from a medieval trading post to a modern economic powerhouse.

- **1810** — First Oktoberfest held to celebrate a royal wedding.

- **1992** — New Munich Airport opens.

## Body

### History and Founding
Munich's origins lie in a 10th-century monastic settlement. Its official founding is dated to 1158, when Duke Henry the Lion established a toll bridge on the Isar to control the salt trade. The city received fortification in 1175 and became a ducal residence in 1255. It grew as a center of trade and, later, of the Counter-Reformation. After becoming the capital of the Kingdom of Bavaria in 1806, Munich flourished under King Ludwig I as a center for arts and neoclassical architecture, earning the nickname "Athens on the Isar."

### 20th Century: War and Politics
Munich was a hotbed of political unrest after World War I, seeing the short-lived Bavarian Soviet Republic in 1919. It became the cradle of the Nazi movement, with Hitler staging his 1923 Beer Hall Putsch there. The city was dubbed the "Capital of the Movement" after 1933. During World War II, Munich was heavily bombed, with about 50% of the city destroyed. Post-war reconstruction meticulously restored the historic street grid. The city hosted the 1972 Summer Olympics, which were overshadowed by the terrorist attack on the Israeli team.

### Economy and Demographics
Munich has Germany's strongest economy among major cities and the lowest unemployment rate. It is a global center for high-tech industries, with headquarters for companies like BMW, Siemens, Allianz, and Munich Re. The city is also a major financial and insurance hub. As of 2023, 30.1% of residents were foreigners, with large communities from Turkey, Croatia, Italy, and Greece. The population has grown steadily, surpassing 1 million in 1957 and reaching 1.6 million by 2024.

### Culture and Architecture
Munich's architecture spans Gothic (Frauenkirche), Renaissance (Antiquarium), Baroque (Theatinerkirche), and modern styles. The city is a major cultural center with world-class museums like the Deutsches Museum and the Pinakothek galleries. It has a rich musical heritage, hosting composers like Wagner and Strauss, and was a hub for the Neue Deutsche Welle and electronic music. The annual Oktoberfest is the world's largest Volksfest. The city's dialect is Bavarian, a regional language distinct from Standard German.

### Geography and Climate
Munich lies on the Isar River in the Alpine Foreland, about 50 km north of the Alps. Its climate is oceanic bordering on humid continental, with warm summers and cold, snowy winters. The nearby Alps influence the weather, bringing higher rainfall and occasional warm föhn winds. The city is adapting to climate change, which is expected to bring more heat extremes and altered precipitation patterns.

## Terms
- **Volksfest**: A traditional German folk festival, most famously Oktoberfest.
- **Hauptstadt der Bewegung**: "Capital of the Movement," the Nazi designation for Munich.
- **Jugendstil**: The German name for the Art Nouveau movement.
- **Helles**: A pale, translucent lager beer that is the most popular contemporary Munich beer.
- **Weißwurst**: A traditional Munich white sausage, invented in 1857.
- **Biergarten**: An outdoor area where beer and food are served, central to Munich's social culture.
- **Landtag**: The state parliament of Bavaria, located in Munich.
- **Kunstareal**: An art district in Munich housing several major museums and galleries.
- **Gastarbeiter**: "Guest workers," foreign workers recruited to Germany from the 1960s onward.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The exact founding date of Munich is debated; archaeological evidence suggests a settlement older than the 1158 Augsburg Arbitration.
- The city's future Olympic bid (for 2036, 2040, or 2044) is a current topic, with citizens voting in favor in 2025.
- Munich's ongoing housing crisis and strategies for urban densification (the LaSie plan) are significant policy challenges.

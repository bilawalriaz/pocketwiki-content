# Qinghai Lake

## Overview
Qinghai Lake is China's largest lake, an alkaline salt lake on the Tibetan Plateau. It is a vital ecological and cultural site, serving as a major bird migration hub and a destination for Tibetan Buddhist pilgrimage. Its fluctuating size over recent centuries reflects significant environmental changes and human impact.

## Timeline
- **c. 150,000 years ago** — Lake became isolated from the Yellow River.
- **18,000 years ago** — Lake level was about 30 metres lower than today.
- **c. 5,900 years ago** — Lake level reached a peak a few metres higher than today.
- **1724** — Qing dynasty imposed direct rule after a Mongol rebellion.
- **1928–1949** — During Nationalist rule, Hui Muslims dominated the provincial government.
- **1950s** — Refugees from the Anti-Rightist Movement settled west of the lake.
- **1960s** — 48.9 km² Gahai Lake split off from the main lake.
- **1980s** — Shadao and Haiyan lakes split off; migration to the area increased.
- **1997** — Bird Islands became a protected sanctuary.
- **2001** — "Retire Cropland, Restore Grasslands" campaign launched.
- **2004** — A 96.7 km² daughter lake split off; lake's water level began increasing.

## Body

### Names and Geography
Qinghai Lake (青海, "Blue/Green Sea") is named for its color, as classical Chinese did not distinguish blue and green. It is also known as Koko Nor (Mongolian for "Blue Lake") and Tso ngönpo (Tibetan for "Blue Lake"). Located about 100 km west of Xining on the Tibetan Plateau at 3,205 m elevation, it is an endorheic (closed-basin) salt lake. In 2008, its surface area was 4,317 km², with an average depth of 21 m. Twenty-three rivers feed it, but high evaporation makes it saline (1.4% salt) and alkaline (pH 9.3). The lake's size has fluctuated; it shrank for much of the 20th century but has increased since 2004.

### History and Human Impact
Han Chinese lived in the region during the Han dynasty. In the 17th century, Mongolic tribes migrated to the area. After a 1724 rebellion, the Qing dynasty stripped the region of autonomy and sent Han and Manchu settlers. During Nationalist rule (1928–1949), Hui Muslims held political power. After 1949, migration increased, causing ecological stress. By 2003, 85% of the lake's river mouths had dried up. The lake shrank by 11.7% from 1908 to 2000, leading to the formation of several smaller "daughter lakes" as the water level dropped and the lakebed was exposed.

### Ecology and Culture
The lake is a critical stop on Asian bird migration routes, making it a focal point for avian influenza concerns. The Bird Islands have been a protected sanctuary since 1997. Five native fish species exist, the most abundant being the edible naked carp. Culturally, a western island with a Buddhist temple was historically accessible only in winter when the lake froze. The lake is circumnavigated by Tibetan Buddhist pilgrims, a journey estimated to take about 18 days on horseback.

## Terms
- **Endorheic**: A closed drainage basin that retains water and allows no outflow to other external bodies of water.
- **Alkaline**: Having a pH greater than 7; Qinghai Lake has a pH of 9.3.
- **Calque**: A loanword formed by literal, word-for-word translation from another language (e.g., "Blue Sea" from Mongolian).
- **Daughter lake**: A smaller lake that splits off from a larger one due to falling water levels and exposed lakebed.
- **Circumambulation**: The act of walking around a sacred site as a form of pilgrimage or worship.

## Debates and Open questions
The primary debate concerns the lake's recent size fluctuation. After shrinking for most of the 20th century due to factors like overgrazing and land reclamation, its water level has been rising since 2004. The causes of this reversal and its long-term ecological implications are ongoing areas of study.

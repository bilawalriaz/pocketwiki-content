# John J. Pershing

## Overview
John J. Pershing (1860–1948) was a pivotal American general who commanded the U.S. forces in World War I and became a mentor to the World War II generation of generals. He is the only American promoted to General of the Armies in his lifetime and is remembered for insisting on an independent American army in WWI, though his tactics were later criticized for high casualties.

## Timeline
- **13 Sep 1860** — Born near Laclede, Missouri.
- **1886** — Graduated from West Point; commissioned as a second lieutenant.
- **1898** — Fought in the Spanish-American War; cited for gallantry at San Juan Hill.
- **1906** — Promoted to brigadier general, skipping over 835 senior officers.
- **1916** — Led the Mexican Punitive Expedition against Pancho Villa.
- **10 May 1917** — Appointed Commander of the American Expeditionary Forces (AEF) for WWI.
- **1918** — Commanded AEF in major battles: Cantigny, Belleau Wood, St. Mihiel, Meuse-Argonne.
- **15 Nov 1919** — Promoted to General of the Armies, the highest U.S. Army rank.
- **1921–1924** — Served as Chief of Staff of the U.S. Army.
- **15 Jul 1948** — Died in Washington, D.C.; buried at Arlington National Cemetery.

## Body

### Early Career and Rise to General
Pershing graduated 30th in his West Point class of 1886. He served in the Indian Wars and gained early recognition for bravery in the Spanish-American War and the Philippine-American War. His reputation for stern discipline and leadership grew, earning him the nickname "Black Jack" from his service with the African-American 10th Cavalry. In 1906, President Theodore Roosevelt secured his promotion to brigadier general, a controversial move that bypassed hundreds of senior officers.

### Command in World War I
Appointed AEF commander in 1917, Pershing organized and trained a force that grew to over two million soldiers. He famously resisted Allied demands to integrate American troops as replacements, insisting the AEF fight as a unified unit under U.S. command. The AEF's first major actions came in June 1918 at Cantigny, Chateau-Thierry, and Belleau Wood. Pershing later commanded the successful St. Mihiel offensive and the costly but decisive Meuse-Argonne Offensive, which helped force the German armistice.

### Controversies and Legacy
Pershing's insistence on open-warfare tactics and frontal assaults, even after other armies had abandoned them, led to high American casualties. He was criticized for not halting attacks before the 11 November 1918 armistice, resulting in nearly 3,500 American casualties on the final day. Despite this, he became the most celebrated American leader of the war. He later served as Army Chief of Staff and mentored future WWII leaders like Marshall, Eisenhower, and Patton. He was the only person promoted to General of the Armies during his lifetime.

## Terms
- **American Expeditionary Forces (AEF):** The United States Armed Forces sent to Europe in World War I.
- **General of the Armies:** The highest possible rank in the United States Army, created for Pershing.
- **Open-warfare tactics:** Pershing's preferred strategy emphasizing rifle marksmanship and maneuver over static trench warfare.
- **Meuse-Argonne Offensive:** The largest and bloodiest American military operation in WWI, involving 1.2 million U.S. troops.
- **Buffalo Soldiers:** Nickname for African-American regiments in the U.S. Army, including the 10th Cavalry.
- **Pershing Rifles:** A military drill organization founded in 1894 by Pershing's cadet company at the University of Nebraska.
- **Moro Province:** A former administrative district in the Philippines where Pershing served as governor.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Tactical Criticism:** Historians debate whether Pershing's reliance on costly frontal assaults was a necessary learning experience for the U.S. Army or an avoidable error that caused excessive casualties.
- **Armistice Day Actions:** His decision to continue offensive operations on 11 November 1918 remains controversial; some view it as a breach of orders, while Pershing argued he was following Allied Supreme Commander Foch's directive to maintain pressure.
- **Political Ambitions:** A movement to draft him for the 1920 presidential nomination failed, partly because party leaders saw him as too closely tied to Democratic President Wilson.

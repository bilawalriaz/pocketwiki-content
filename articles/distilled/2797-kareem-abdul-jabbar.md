# Kareem Abdul-Jabbar

## Overview
Kareem Abdul-Jabbar is widely regarded as one of the greatest basketball players in history. His 20-year NBA career, defined by the unstoppable "skyhook" shot, set enduring records for scoring and longevity. Beyond basketball, he became a prominent author, cultural critic, and activist, using his platform to address issues of race, religion, and social justice.

## Timeline
- **April 16, 1947** — Born Ferdinand Lewis Alcindor Jr. in Harlem, New York.
- **1965–1969** — Leads UCLA to three consecutive NCAA championships (1967, 1968, 1969).
- **1968** — Converts to Sunni Islam; boycotts the 1968 Summer Olympics.
- **1969** — Drafted 1st overall by the Milwaukee Bucks.
- **1971** — Leads Bucks to first NBA championship; legally changes name to Kareem Abdul-Jabbar.
- **1975** — Traded to the Los Angeles Lakers.
- **April 5, 1984** — Breaks Wilt Chamberlain's NBA career scoring record.
- **1985** — Wins second NBA Finals MVP at age 38.
- **1989** — Retires as the NBA's all-time career scoring leader.
- **2012** — Appointed U.S. global cultural ambassador.
- **2016** — Awarded the Presidential Medal of Freedom.
- **February 7, 2023** — LeBron James surpasses Abdul-Jabbar's career scoring record.

## Body

### Early Life and College Career
Born in Harlem, Abdul-Jabbar was exceptionally tall from a young age, reaching 6 ft 8 in (2.03 m) by eighth grade. He attended Power Memorial Academy, where he led the team to a 71-game winning streak. He chose UCLA, where he led the Bruins to three straight NCAA titles and was named the tournament's Most Outstanding Player each year. His dominance led to the NCAA banning the dunk from 1967 to 1976, a rule sometimes called the "Alcindor Rule." In the summer of 1968, he converted to Sunni Islam and later boycotted the 1968 Olympics to protest racial inequality in the United States.

### Milwaukee Bucks (1969–1975)
Drafted first overall by the Bucks in 1969, Abdul-Jabbar won Rookie of the Year. In his second season, he won his first MVP award and led the Bucks to their first NBA championship. He publicly adopted his Muslim name in 1971. He won two more MVP awards with Milwaukee but requested a trade in 1975, citing cultural needs. He was traded to the Los Angeles Lakers.

### Los Angeles Lakers (1975–1989)
Abdul-Jabbar won his fourth and fifth MVP awards in his first two seasons with the Lakers. The team's fortunes rose with the 1979 drafting of Magic Johnson, initiating the "Showtime" era. Abdul-Jabbar won a record sixth MVP in 1980 and was a key part of five Lakers championship teams (1980, 1982, 1985, 1987, 1988). He broke Chamberlain's career scoring record in 1984 and won his second Finals MVP in 1985. He retired in 1989 at age 42, holding the records for career points (38,387), games played, and minutes played.

### Player Profile and Legacy
Abdul-Jabbar's signature shot was the skyhook, a high-arcing hook shot released at the peak of his reach, making it nearly unblockable. He was a dominant defensive player, selected to 11 All-Defensive Teams. His longevity was aided by a strict fitness regimen that included yoga. He is a member of the Naismith Memorial Basketball Hall of Fame and was named to the NBA's 35th, 50th, and 75th anniversary teams. ESPN named him the greatest center of all time in 2007.

### Off-Court Life and Career
After retiring, Abdul-Jabbar worked as an assistant coach and scout. He became a best-selling author, writing books on African-American history and fiction. He has been a vocal commentator on social issues, particularly race and religion. He served as a U.S. global cultural ambassador and received the Presidential Medal of Freedom. He has also acted in films and television, most famously in *Airplane!* (1980). In 2009, he was diagnosed with chronic myeloid leukemia, a condition he manages with daily medication.

## Terms
- **Skyhook:** Abdul-Jabbar's trademark shot, a high-arcing hook shot released at the highest point of his arm's motion, making it extremely difficult to block.
- **MVP (Most Valuable Player):** An annual award given to the best player in the NBA regular season. Abdul-Jabbar won a record six.
- **Showtime:** The fast-paced, entertaining style of play used by the Los Angeles Lakers during the 1980s, led by Magic Johnson and Kareem Abdul-Jabbar.
- **Naismith Memorial Basketball Hall of Fame:** The institution that honors players, coaches, and other major contributors to basketball. Abdul-Jabbar was inducted in 1995.
- **All-NBA Team:** An annual honor given to the best players in the NBA at each position. Abdul-Jabbar was selected 15 times.
- **All-Defensive Team:** An annual honor given to the best defensive players in the NBA. Abdul-Jabbar was selected 11 times.
- **Corneal Abrasion:** A scratch on the cornea of the eye. Repeated eye injuries led Abdul-Jabbar to wear protective goggles.
- **Presidential Medal of Freedom:** The highest civilian honor in the United States, awarded by the President. Abdul-Jabbar received it in 2016.

## Debates and Open Questions
The source material presents Abdul-Jabbar's standing in historical rankings as a subject of ongoing discussion. While ESPN ranked him as the greatest center of all time in 2007 and the second-best player ever (behind Michael Jordan) in 2016, other experts and legends like Pat Riley and Julius Erving have at times called him the greatest player of all time. The debate often centers on comparing his sustained excellence and statistical dominance to the peak performances of other legends like Michael Jordan and LeBron James.

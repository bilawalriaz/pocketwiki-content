# Xiamen

## Overview
Xiamen, historically known as Amoy, is a major sub-provincial city in southeastern Fujian province, China, situated on the Taiwan Strait. It is a significant economic, cultural, and transportation hub, notable for being one of China's original Special Economic Zones (SEZs) and for its proximity to the Taiwan-administered Kinmen Islands. The city's development from a historic port to a modern metropolis reflects its strategic importance in trade, cross-strait relations, and China's economic reforms.

## Timeline
- **1387** — Ming dynasty establishes Xiamen Defense Castle, giving the island its name.
- **1650** — Ming loyalist Koxinga occupies Xiamen as an anti-Qing base, renaming it Siming.
- **1842** — Treaty of Nanjing makes Xiamen one of China's first five treaty ports open to British trade.
- **1933** — Xiamen is administratively planned as a city, the first in Fujian province.
- **1949** — Communist forces capture Xiamen and Gulangyu, but fail to take Kinmen.
- **1955-58** — First and Second Taiwan Strait Crises involve shelling between Xiamen and Kinmen.
- **1980** — Xiamen becomes one of China's first four Special Economic Zones.
- **2001** — "Three Mini-Links" restore ferry, commercial, and mail links between Xiamen and Kinmen.
- **2010** — Xiamen SEZ expands to cover the entire city; Xiamen Port incorporates Zhangzhou port.
- **2017** — Gulangyu Island is inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

## Body

### Name and History
The area was historically part of Tong'an County. The name "Xiamen" (meaning "Gate of China") originated from a Ming dynasty fortress built in 1387. The island was renamed "Siming" ("Think Ming") by the loyalist Koxinga in 1650, a name that has persisted as a district. The city became a major treaty port after the First Opium War, leading to significant international trade, particularly in tea, and the establishment of foreign settlements on Gulangyu Island. After 1949, its development was shaped by cross-strait tensions until the "Opening Up" policy designated it a Special Economic Zone in 1980, spurring rapid growth.

### Geography and Climate
Xiamen is centered on Xiamen Island, which was connected to the mainland by a causeway in 1955, and includes Gulangyu Island and parts of the mainland. The Kinmen Islands lie less than 6 km away. The city has a monsoonal humid subtropical climate with hot, humid summers and mild, dry winters. It is known for its relatively low pollution levels.

### Economy and Development
As an early SEZ, Xiamen developed a diverse, export-oriented economy. Key industries include electronic information, equipment manufacturing, tourism, modern logistics, and financial services. The Port of Xiamen is a major global container port. The city hosts the annual China International Fair for Investment and Trade and has numerous development zones, including the Haicang Investment Zone for Taiwanese business and a Pilot Free Trade Zone.

### Transportation
Xiamen is a major transportation hub with extensive connectivity. Xiamen Gaoqi International Airport serves numerous domestic and international destinations. The city is linked by high-speed rail to major Chinese cities. A network of bridges, undersea tunnels, and a subway system connects the island to the mainland. Passenger ferries provide crucial links to Kinmen and the tourist island of Gulangyu.

### Culture and Tourism
Xiamen's culture is rooted in Hokkien (Southern Min) traditions, including the gongfu tea ceremony, Nanyin music, and Gezai Opera. The city is a top tourist destination, famed for the historic architecture of Gulangyu Island, the Nanputuo Temple, and scenic coastal areas. Its cuisine features specialties like satay noodles and oyster omelets.

## Terms
- **Special Economic Zone (SEZ):** A designated area in China with economic regulations more liberal than the rest of the country to attract foreign investment.
- **Hokkien:** A subgroup of Southern Min Chinese, the indigenous language of Xiamen and Fujian.
- **Treaty Port:** A port opened to foreign trade and residence by treaty after military defeat, as per the 19th-century "unequal treaties."
- **Koxinga (Zheng Chenggong):** A Ming dynasty loyalist who used Xiamen as a base to resist the Qing and later expelled the Dutch from Taiwan.
- **Gongfu Tea:** A traditional Chinese tea preparation method emphasizing skill and ritual, central to Xiamen's culture.
- **Nanyin:** An ancient form of Chinese music preserved in Fujian, often called a "living fossil."
- **Gezai Opera:** A traditional Chinese opera form that originated in Taiwan and is popular in Fujian and Southeast Asia.
- **Three Mini-Links:** A 2001 agreement restoring direct ferry, mail, and trade links between mainland China and offshore islands like Kinmen.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The source notes the historical debate over the identity of the "Japanese" pirates who attacked the coast in 1387, stating many were actually disaffected Chinese.
- The city's name has undergone multiple changes reflecting political shifts (e.g., Ming loyalty, Qing administration, Republic era), illustrating how toponyms can embody historical narratives.

# Getúlio Vargas

## Overview
Getúlio Vargas was a Brazilian politician who dominated the country's history for over two decades, serving as president from 1930 to 1945 and again from 1951 until his suicide in 1954. His long and controversial tenure, which included leading a provisional government, a constitutional presidency, and a dictatorship, makes him considered by historians as the most influential Brazilian politician of the 20th century. He guided Brazil through the Great Depression, World War II, and significant social and economic modernization.

## Timeline
- **19 April 1882** — Born in São Borja, Rio Grande do Sul.
- **1930** — After losing the presidential election, rises to power as provisional president following the Revolution of 1930.
- **1934** — Elected president under a new constitution.
- **1935** — Crushes a communist uprising, using it to justify a state of emergency.
- **10 November 1937** — Executes a self-coup, establishing the authoritarian Estado Novo dictatorship.
- **21 August 1942** — Leads Brazil into World War II on the side of the Allies.
- **29 October 1945** — Ousted from power by the military after fifteen years.
- **31 January 1951** — Returns to the presidency after winning the 1950 election.
- **24 August 1954** — Commits suicide amid a political crisis, ending his second presidency.

## Body

### Rise to Power and Provisional Government (1930–1934)
Vargas, from a powerful family in Rio Grande do Sul, built a political career as a state deputy and federal congressman. After serving as Minister of Finance, he became president of his home state. In 1930, he was the opposition's presidential candidate. Following a disputed election and the assassination of his running mate, the Revolution of 1930 overthrew the government, installing Vargas as provisional president. He assumed unlimited power, dissolved legislatures, and appointed federal intervenors to govern the states. His government addressed the Great Depression's impact on coffee by destroying excess stock to sustain prices and introduced innovative labor policies, creating the Ministry of Labor and passing laws to protect workers and incorporate them as supporters.

### Constitutional Presidency and Authoritarian Turn (1934–1937)
Facing resistance, including the 1932 Constitutionalist Revolution in São Paulo, Vargas promised a new constitution. The 1934 Constitution, modeled on Germany's Weimar Constitution, established a federal republic and codified labor rights, a minimum wage, and compulsory primary education. The Constituent Assembly elected Vargas to a four-year term. However, after a communist uprising in 1935, Vargas convinced Congress to declare a state of emergency, suspending civil rights and jailing opponents. This period of exceptional powers set the stage for his next move.

### The Estado Novo Dictatorship (1937–1945)
Using a falsified document, the "Cohen Plan," as a pretext for a communist threat, Vargas executed a self-coup on 10 November 1937. He canceled the 1938 elections, outlawed all political parties, and ruled by decree under a new, corporatist constitution. This "New State" dictatorship suppressed opposition and promoted a state-controlled nationalism called "Brasilidade." During World War II, Vargas navigated between the Axis and Allied powers before declaring war on Germany and Italy in 1942, providing raw materials and troops in exchange for U.S. support, including a steel mill. Growing post-war discontent and military pressure forced his resignation in October 1945.

### Second Presidency and Death (1951–1954)
After serving as a senator, Vargas was re-elected president in 1950. He pursued a nationalist policy, founding the state oil company Petrobras. His second term was consumed by a political crisis stemming from an assassination attempt on a political rival, which implicated Vargas's personal guard. Facing demands for his resignation from the military, Vargas committed suicide on 24 August 1954. His death sparked riots and cemented his legacy as a complex figure who championed the poor and national interests while ruling as an authoritarian.

## Terms
*   **Intervenor:** A federal official appointed by Vargas to administer a state, replacing its elected governor (president).
*   **Estado Novo (New State):** The authoritarian dictatorship established by Vargas in 1937, characterized by rule by decree, corporatism, and the suppression of political parties.
*   **Coffee with Milk Politics:** The oligarchic alliance between the dominant states of São Paulo (coffee) and Minas Gerais (milk) that dominated Brazil's First Republic (1889–1930).
*   **Liberal Alliance (Aliança Liberal):** The opposition coalition formed in 1930 by the states of Minas Gerais, Rio Grande do Sul, and Paraíba to support Vargas's presidential candidacy.
*   **Constitutionalist Revolution:** A 1932 civil war led by São Paulo against Vargas's federal government, demanding a new constitution and an end to his provisional rule.
*   **Cohen Plan:** A falsified document discovered in 1937 that purported to detail a communist uprising plot, used by Vargas as the pretext for his self-coup and the establishment of the Estado Novo.
*   **Brasilidade:** A nationalist cultural policy under the Estado Novo that promoted a unified Brazilian identity, often through the suppression of foreign influences.
*   **Petrobras:** The state-owned petroleum multinational founded by Vargas in 1953 as a cornerstone of his nationalist economic policy.

## Debates and open questions
*   Historians debate the extent to which Vargas's Estado Novo was influenced by European fascism, particularly Italian Fascism's *Carta del Lavoro*, versus being a uniquely Brazilian form of authoritarianism.
*   The interpretation of Vargas's suicide remains contested, with views ranging from a selfless act of a "valiant warrior" for national interests to a final, calculated move by a "crafty and calculating statesman."
*   The effectiveness and enforcement of his social reforms are debated, as many labor laws and minimum wage policies were poorly enforced in rural areas and did not extend to agricultural workers.

# Seven Years' War

## Overview
The Seven Years' War (1756–1763) was a global conflict fought between two major coalitions: Great Britain and Prussia against France, Austria, and others. It reshaped colonial empires, particularly in North America and India, and established Prussia as a dominant European power. The war's origins lie in unresolved tensions from the War of the Austrian Succession and a major diplomatic realignment known as the Diplomatic Revolution.

## Timeline
- **1754** — First clashes in North America (Jumonville Glen, Fort Necessity).
- **1756** — Diplomatic Revolution: France and Austria ally; Britain allies with Prussia (Convention of Westminster).
- **1756** — Prussia invades Saxony, beginning the European war.
- **1757** — Prussia wins at Rossbach and Leuthen, reversing early setbacks.
- **1757** — British capture of Fort Duquesne in North America.
- **1759** — British "Annus Mirabilis": victories at Quiberon Bay (sea) and the Plains of Abraham (Quebec).
- **1760** — Russian and Austrian forces briefly occupy Berlin.
- **1762** — Russia's Peter III ends the war with Prussia; Spain enters the war against Britain.
- **1763** — Treaties of Paris and Hubertusburg end the war.

## Body

### Background and Diplomatic Revolution
The war stemmed from the unsettled aftermath of the War of the Austrian Succession (1740–1748). Austria sought to reclaim the province of Silesia from Prussia. A major diplomatic shift occurred in 1756: long-standing rivals France and Austria formed an alliance (the First Treaty of Versailles), while Britain allied with Prussia (Convention of Westminster). This "Diplomatic Revolution" set the stage for a new European conflict.

### European Theater
The war in Europe centered on Prussia's struggle for survival against Austria, France, and Russia. Prussia, led by Frederick the Great, faced a coalition aiming to dismember it. Key battles included Prussian victories at Rossbach (1757) and Leuthen (1757), and severe defeats at Kunersdorf (1759) and Maxen (1759). The conflict was a stalemate by 1760–1761, with both sides financially exhausted. The 1762 death of Russia's Empress Elizabeth and the accession of the pro-Prussian Peter III saved Prussia from collapse. The Treaty of Hubertusburg (1763) restored the pre-war status quo, confirming Prussian control of Silesia and its rise as a great power.

### Colonial and Global Conflicts
Fought across multiple continents, the war was a decisive struggle for colonial supremacy. In North America (the French and Indian War), Britain captured key French forts and, in 1759, Quebec, leading to the surrender of New France in 1760. In India, British forces under Robert Clive defeated French allies at Plassey (1757) and Wandiwash (1760), effectively ending French power on the subcontinent. Britain also captured French and Spanish colonies in the Caribbean, West Africa, and the Philippines. The Treaty of Paris (1763) granted Britain vast French territories in North America and India, while France chose to retain its lucrative Caribbean sugar islands.

### British Strategy and Finance
Britain's strategy, championed by William Pitt, focused on using naval power to seize French colonies while subsidizing Prussia to tie down French armies in Europe. This "dual strategy" proved highly successful. The war was financed through massive public debt, leaving Britain near bankruptcy by 1763 and leading to new taxes on its American colonies.

## Terms
- **Diplomatic Revolution**: The 1756 realignment where France and Austria allied, ending their long rivalry, while Britain allied with Prussia.
- **Cabinet War**: An 18th-century conflict fought by disciplined, state-supplied regular armies on behalf of the sovereign, with limited civilian atrocities.
- **Reichsarmee**: The Imperial Army of the Holy Roman Empire, mobilized against Prussia on behalf of Austria.
- **Smoothbore flintlock musket**: The standard infantry firearm of the era, effective at short range and used with bayonets.
- **Siege warfare**: A dominant military strategy focusing on capturing key fortifications to control regions and roads.
- **Subsidies**: Financial payments from one power (e.g., Britain) to an ally (e.g., Prussia) to support its war effort.
- **Annus Mirabilis**: "Year of Wonders," referring to Britain's string of major victories in 1759.
- **Pontiac's War**: A 1763 Native American uprising against British rule in the Great Lakes region following the defeat of France.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The war's primary cause is debated: was it driven by colonial rivalries in North America and India, or by the European balance of power and the struggle over Silesia?
- The impact of the Diplomatic Revolution is analyzed as either a direct cause of the war or a framework that made a general conflict more likely.
- The long-term consequences for Britain are contested: did the war's costs and the subsequent need to tax American colonies directly lead to the American Revolution?
- The role of logistics, particularly Russia's chronic supply problems, is seen by historians as a key factor that prevented a decisive Prussian defeat despite multiple battlefield losses.

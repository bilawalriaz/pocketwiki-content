# War of the Spanish Succession

## Overview
The War of the Spanish Succession (1701–1714) was a major European conflict triggered by the death of the childless King Charles II of Spain. The central question was whether his vast empire would be inherited by a French Bourbon (Philip of Anjou) or an Austrian Habsburg (Archduke Charles), a decision that threatened to upset the European balance of power. The war reshaped the continent's political map, leading to the rise of Britain as a leading naval and commercial power and the decline of France and the Dutch Republic.

- **7 Mar 1714** — Treaties of Rastatt and Baden end the war between France and the Holy Roman Empire.

## Body

### Background and Succession Crisis
The death of Charles II of Spain in 1700 without an heir created a succession crisis. The Spanish Empire, though weakened, remained a vast global power. Its acquisition by either France or Austria threatened the European balance of power. Negotiations between Louis XIV of France and Emperor Leopold I to divide the Spanish territories failed, as the Spanish refused to allow it. Charles II's final will named Louis XIV's grandson, Philip of Anjou, as his heir. Louis accepted, and Philip was proclaimed king, triggering war.

### Prelude to War
Louis XIV's subsequent actions made war inevitable. He registered Philip's claim to the French throne, threatened the Dutch "Barrier" fortresses in the Spanish Netherlands, and supported the Jacobite claim to the British throne after the death of James II. These moves united the Grand Alliance (Austria, the Dutch Republic, and Britain) against him. The war was also driven by mercantilist economic theory, which viewed global markets as static, making the conflict expand to colonies in North America, India, and the West Indies.

### Strategic Objectives and Campaigns (1701–1708)
The war was fought on multiple fronts. In Italy, the conflict centered on the Duchy of Milan. In the Low Countries and Rhine, the Allies sought to secure Dutch borders and defeat French forces, achieving major victories at Blenheim (1704) and Ramillies (1706). In Spain and Portugal, the war became a struggle for control of the Iberian Peninsula, with the Allies capturing Gibraltar (1704) and Menorca (1708) to dominate the Mediterranean. By 1708, the Allies had secured their main objectives in the Low Countries and Mediterranean, but the French frontier remained intact and Philip V was firmly established in most of Spain.

### Towards Peace and the Treaties of Utrecht (1709–1714)
By 1709, the war had reached a stalemate. The harsh Allied preliminaries of The Hague, demanding Philip's abdication, were rejected by France. The bloody Battle of Malplaquet (1709) and the failure of Allied campaigns in Spain increased war-weariness. The death of Emperor Joseph I in 1711, which made his brother Archduke Charles the new emperor, changed the strategic calculus for Britain and the Dutch, as a union of Spain and Austria was now as undesirable as one with France. Britain secretly negotiated with France, leading to the Peace of Utrecht (1713). Philip V was confirmed as King of Spain but renounced his rights to the French throne. Spain ceded the Spanish Netherlands, most Italian possessions, Gibraltar, and Menorca. Britain gained key territories and trading rights, including the *Asiento de Negros* (a monopoly on the slave trade to Spanish America). The Holy Roman Empire made separate peace in 1714 via the Treaties of Rastatt and Baden.

## Terms
*   **Balance of Power:** The theory that no single state should become dominant enough to threaten the security of others, a key principle guiding the Grand Alliance.
*   **Grand Alliance:** The coalition formed in 1701 by Austria, the Dutch Republic, and Britain to prevent the union of the French and Spanish crowns.
*   **Mercantilism:** An economic theory that national wealth and power were best served by increasing exports and accumulating precious metals, often through colonial exploitation and trade wars.
*   **Barrier Fortresses:** A line of Dutch-held fortresses in the Spanish Netherlands, established to protect the Dutch Republic from French invasion.
*   **Asiento de Negros:** A monopoly contract granted to Britain at the Peace of Utrecht, giving it the exclusive right to supply enslaved Africans to Spanish American colonies.
*   **Nueva Planta decrees:** A series of laws issued by Philip V after the war that abolished the traditional political institutions of the Crown of Aragon, centralizing power in Castile.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Primary Cause:** Was the war fundamentally about dynastic succession and the balance of power, or were commercial and colonial rivalries the primary driver?
*   **Allied Strategy:** Did the Grand Alliance's insistence on placing Archduke Charles on an undivided Spanish throne make a negotiated peace impossible and prolong the war?
*   **Outcome:** Was the Peace of Utrecht a pragmatic settlement that ensured a lasting balance of power, or did it merely set the stage for future conflicts by leaving key grievances unresolved?

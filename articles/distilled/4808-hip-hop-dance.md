# Hip-hop dance

## Overview
Hip-hop dance is a range of street dance styles primarily performed to hip-hop music or that have evolved as part of hip-hop culture. It originated in the 1970s in the United States and is distinguished by its improvisational, "freestyle" nature, with crews often engaging in competitive "battles." Its evolution from urban neighborhoods to global mainstream entertainment highlights its cultural significance and adaptability.

## Timeline
- **Early 1970s** — Breaking is created in the South Bronx, New York City.
- **1969** — Locking is created in Los Angeles, California by Don "Campbellock" Campbell.
- **1970s** — Funk styles (e.g., popping, boogaloo) develop in California.
- **1973** — DJ Kool Herc popularizes the break beat, foundational for breaking.
- **1980s** — Hip-hop social dances (e.g., Roger Rabbit, Cabbage Patch) emerge.
- **1980s** — Early hip-hop films (*Wild Style*, *Beat Street*, *Breakin'*) gain mainstream exposure.
- **1989** — Buddha Stretch pioneers formal hip-hop dance classes at Broadway Dance Center.
- **2002** — Turfing is created in Oakland, California.
- **2008** — *America's Best Dance Crew* premieres, boosting crew visibility.
- **2009** — Jerkin' is popularized by the New Boyz's song "You're a Jerk."
- **Early 2000s** — Krumping is created in South Central, Los Angeles.

## Body

### Origins and Primary Influences
Hip-hop dance is a fusion genre with roots in 1970s street styles. **Breaking** (or b-boying) emerged in the South Bronx, initially as upright "toprock" before incorporating floor-oriented moves influenced by martial arts and gymnastics. It is considered the first hip-hop dance style. Simultaneously, **funk styles** like locking and popping developed in California. Locking, characterized by freezing in place, was created by Don Campbell and popularized by his crew, The Lockers. Popping involves quick muscle contractions to create a "pop" or "hit" and is an umbrella term for related illusionary styles like waving and tutting. These funk styles were originally danced to funk music and were later adopted into hip-hop culture, partly due to media labeling them as "breakdance."

### Derivative Styles and Social Dance
Decades after the foundational styles, new regional derivatives emerged. **Memphis Jookin'** (1980s, Tennessee) features gliding on tiptoes. **Turfing** (2002, Oakland) emphasizes storytelling and illusion. **Jerkin'** (2009, Los Angeles) spread via viral songs and features moves like the Reject. **Krumping** (early 2000s, Los Angeles) is a highly energetic, battle-focused style. Alongside these, hip-hop **social dancing** (party dancing) continually evolves with new songs, producing fad dances from the Running Man (1990s) to the Dougie (2000s).

### Commercialization and the Dance Industry
The dance industry created commercial, studio-based versions of hip-hop. **New style** (or urban choreography) is the choreographed hip-hop seen in music videos, characterized by hard-hitting movements and isolations. This development involved adding studio techniques like eight-counts, a pivotal shift from street improvisation. **Jazz-funk** is another hybrid style blending hip-hop with jazz dance fundamentals like pirouettes. The industry also saw the rise of hip-hop dance conventions (e.g., Monsters of Hip Hop), specialized dancewear (e.g., Nappytabs), and talent agencies representing street dancers.

### Global Spread and Entertainment
Films, television, and the internet have globalized hip-hop dance. Early films like *Breakin'* and *Wild Style* introduced styles worldwide. Television shows like *Soul Train* provided crucial early exposure for locking and popping. Modern reality competitions such as *America's Best Dance Crew*, *So You Think You Can Dance*, and *World of Dance* have further popularized the art form. Hip-hop dance has also entered theater, with companies like GhettOriginal and Rennie Harris' Puremovement producing stage shows, and online platforms like YAK Films distributing content globally.

## Terms
- **Breaking (B-boying/B-girling):** A hip-hop dance style featuring toprock, downrock, freezes, and power moves.
- **Locking:** A funk style characterized by freezing in a locked position, created by Don Campbell.
- **Popping:** A funk style based on quickly contracting and relaxing muscles to create a "pop" or "hit."
- **Toprock:** Footwork-oriented steps performed while standing up, a foundational element of breaking.
- **Cypher:** A circular dance space formed by spectators for one-on-one breaking battles.
- **New Style:** A commercial, studio-based form of hip-hop dance featuring choreographed routines.
- **Freestyle:** Improvisational dancing, a core characteristic of hip-hop dance.
- **Battle:** A competitive dance-off between individuals or crews.
- **Jazz-funk:** A hybrid dance style combining hip-hop and jazz dance techniques.
- **Krump:** A highly energetic, aggressive street dance style focused on battles.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Authenticity vs. Commercialization:** There is ongoing debate about what constitutes "true" hip-hop dance. Purists argue that commercial "new style" taught in studios is a watered-down form that lacks the improvisational and cultural roots of street styles.
- **Classification of Funk Styles:** Historically, funk styles like locking and popping were created independently of hip-hop and to funk music. Their inclusion under the "hip-hop dance" umbrella is debated, with some scholars and pioneers asserting they are separate genres adopted by hip-hop culture.
- **Pedagogical Standards:** There is concern about the lack of standardized instruction in commercial hip-hop classes, where teachers may not know the foundational elements or history of the art form.

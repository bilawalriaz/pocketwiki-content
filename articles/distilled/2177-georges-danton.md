# Georges Danton

## Overview
Georges Jacques Danton was a leading figure of the French Revolution, rising from an obscure lawyer to a powerful orator and government minister. He played a crucial role in the overthrow of the monarchy and the establishment of the First Republic, but his calls for moderation during the Reign of Terror put him at odds with Maximilien Robespierre, leading to his trial and execution. His legacy is debated, with historians viewing him as either a patriotic giant of the Revolution or a corrupt opportunist.

## Body

### Early Life and Revolutionary Beginnings
Danton was born to a respectable but not wealthy lawyer. A childhood bull attack and smallpox left his face scarred. He studied law, became a barrister in 1783, and married in 1787. His revolutionary career began in 1789 with fiery speeches at the Palais Royal, earning him the nickname "The Thunderer." He became a leader of the Cordeliers district, whose militia participated in the storming of the Bastille, though Danton was not present. He rose to become president of the Cordeliers Club in 1790.

### Rise to Power and the Fall of the Monarchy
Danton's powerful oratory made him a dominant figure in Parisian politics. After the storming of the Tuileries on 10 August 1792, the Legislative Assembly appointed him Minister of Justice. In this role, he organized the defense of France and, on 2 September 1792, delivered a speech calling for daring against foreign enemies. This speech is widely held responsible for inciting the September Massacres, in which over 1,000 prisoners were killed, though direct proof of his organization is lacking. He was elected to the National Convention but was forced to resign from the ministry in October 1792.

### The Committee of Public Safety and the Terror
In 1793, Danton supported the creation of the Revolutionary Tribunal and was appointed to the Committee of Public Safety in April. He was not re-elected in July, and Robespierre joined the committee shortly after. Danton left politics in October but was urged to return in November to lead a moderate faction, the "Indulgents," who demanded an end to the Terror. He and his ally Camille Desmoulins used the newspaper *Le Vieux Cordelier* to attack the Terror and Robespierre's policies. Robespierre saw this as a threat to his "Republic of Virtue" and moved against Danton.

### Downfall and Execution
Danton was arrested on 30 March 1794 alongside Desmoulins and others. The charges included conspiracy, corruption, and royalist sympathies, with the French East India Company scandal providing a key accusation of financial venality. The trial before the Revolutionary Tribunal was irregular; the Convention passed a decree silencing the defendants when they disrupted proceedings. Danton was guillotined on 5 April 1794. His alleged last words to the executioner were, "Show the people my head. It is well worth seeing."

## Terms
*   **Cordeliers Club:** A radical political club in Paris of which Danton was a leading member and president.
*   **September Massacres:** A wave of killings of prisoners in Paris in early September 1792, which Danton's speech is believed to have incited.
*   **Committee of Public Safety:** The executive body of the Convention that directed the government and the Terror; Danton was its first president.
*   **Revolutionary Tribunal:** A court established in 1793 to try political enemies of the Revolution, which later sentenced Danton.
*   **Indulgents:** The moderate faction within the Montagnards, led by Danton, which called for an end to the Terror.
*   **Terror (Reign of Terror):** The period of the Revolution (1793–94) characterized by mass executions of perceived enemies of the state.
*   **Montagnards (The Mountain):** The radical political faction in the Convention, seated on the high benches, which included Danton and Robespierre.
*   **Girondins:** A more moderate political faction in the Convention, rivals to the Montagnards, who were purged in 1793.
*   **Sans-culottes:** The radical working-class militants of Paris who pressured the government for more extreme revolutionary measures.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Role in the September Massacres:** Historians debate whether Danton actively organized the massacres or merely failed to stop them after his inflammatory speech. The source states there is "no proof that the massacres were organized by Danton."
*   **Character and Motives:** Assessments of Danton vary widely. Some historians, like Thiers, see him as a "gigantic revolutionary," while others, like Lamartine, view him as a corrupt "statesman of materialism." A more positive view from Robinet portrays him as a generous patriot.
*   **Reasons for Execution:** While officially charged with corruption and conspiracy, his execution is widely seen as the result of his political challenge to Robespierre and the Committee of Public Safety's monopoly on power during the Terror.

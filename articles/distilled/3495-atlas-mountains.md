# Atlas Mountains

## Overview
The Atlas Mountains are a major mountain range in the Maghreb region of North Africa, stretching approximately 2,500 km through Morocco, Algeria, and Tunisia. They form a natural barrier separating the Sahara Desert from the Mediterranean Sea and Atlantic Ocean. Primarily inhabited by Berber populations, the range is geologically significant for its formation during multiple tectonic phases and is rich in biodiversity and natural resources.

## Timeline
- **~300 million years ago** — First tectonic phase forms the Anti-Atlas during the Paleozoic Era via continental collisions.
- **Before ~66 million years ago** — Second phase during the Mesozoic Era involves crustal extension and sedimentary basin formation.
- **~66 to ~1.8 million years ago** — Third phase in the Paleogene and Neogene Periods uplifts the main Atlas chains due to Africa-Europe collision.

## Body

### Geology and Formation
The Atlas Mountains lie on ancient Precambrian basement rock. Their formation occurred in three main phases. The first created the Anti-Atlas around 300 million years ago as part of the Alleghenian orogeny, when Africa and North America collided. The second phase, during the Mesozoic Era, involved crustal extension that rifted continents and deposited the sedimentary rocks that now form the High Atlas surface. The final and most significant uplift happened between approximately 66 and 1.8 million years ago, driven by the collision of the African and European plates. This event also contributed to the formation of the Alps and Pyrenees. Geologists note the range's relatively small crustal thickening for its height, suggesting deep mantle processes may have aided its uplift.

### Subranges and Geography
The Atlas system is divided into four main regions. The **Anti-Atlas** in southwestern Morocco extends about 500 km and includes volcanic massifs. The **High Atlas** in central Morocco contains the range's highest peak, Toubkal (4,167 m), and is inhabited by Berber communities. The **Middle Atlas**, also in Morocco, is the northernmost main range and is a crucial water catchment area. The **Saharan Atlas** runs through Algeria and Tunisia, marking the Sahara's northern edge. The **Tell Atlas** parallels the Mediterranean coast and has a humid climate, while the easternmost **Aurès Mountains** span Algeria and Tunisia.

### Natural Resources and Climate
The Atlas Mountains are rich in mineral resources, including deposits of iron, lead, copper, silver, mercury, rock salt, phosphate, marble, coal, and natural gas. The climate is generally cool with sunny summers and an average temperature of 25 °C. Precipitation varies, with the Tell Atlas receiving significant rainfall, sometimes exceeding 1,500 mm annually.

### Flora and Fauna
The mountains host diverse plant life, including Atlas cedar, various oaks, and in wetter areas, cork oaks and lavender. Animal species include the Barbary macaque, Barbary sheep, and the endangered Atlas mountain viper. Historically, the range was home to now-extinct species like the Atlas bear and North African elephant. Barbary lions are extinct in the wild but survive in captivity.

## Terms
- **Maghreb**: The region of North Africa west of Egypt, including Morocco, Algeria, Tunisia, Libya, and Mauritania.
- **Berber (Amazigh)**: An ethnic group indigenous to North Africa, primarily inhabiting the Atlas Mountains.
- **Orogeny**: A mountain-building process, especially one due to tectonic plate collisions.
- **Tectonic deformation**: The process of rock layers being bent, broken, or moved by forces within the Earth's crust.
- **Sedimentary basin**: A low area of the Earth's crust where sediments accumulate over time, often forming thick rock layers.
- **Convergent boundary**: A tectonic plate boundary where two plates move toward each other, often causing subduction or continental collision.
- **Subduction zone**: An area where one tectonic plate slides beneath another into the Earth's mantle.
- **Massif**: A compact group of mountains or a distinct section of a mountain range.
- **Hautes Plaines**: The high plateau region situated between the Tell Atlas and Saharan Atlas ranges.

## Debates and Open Questions
The exact nature of the subduction process during the Africa-Europe collision in the Atlas region is unclear due to a lack of evidence. Furthermore, the relatively small amount of crustal thickening and tectonic shortening observed is considered striking given the mountains' significant altitude. Recent studies propose that deep processes rooted in the Earth's mantle may have played a key role in the uplift of the High and Middle Atlas, but this remains an area of active research.

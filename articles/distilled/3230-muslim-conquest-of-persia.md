# Muslim conquest of Persia

## Overview
The Arab conquest of Iran (633–651 CE) was the military campaign by which the Rashidun Caliphate conquered the Sasanian Empire, ending centuries of Persian imperial rule and initiating the Islamization of Iran. This pivotal event reshaped the political and religious landscape of the Middle East, marking the first time since the Neo-Babylonian Empire that Mesopotamia was ruled by a Semitic-speaking power and leading to the gradual decline of Zoroastrianism as the state religion.

## Timeline
- **602–628** — Byzantine–Sasanian War exhausts both empires.
- **628** — Execution of Sasanian shah Khosrow II triggers internal instability.
- **633** — First Rashidun invasion under Khalid ibn al-Walid conquers parts of Mesopotamia.
- **636** — Decisive Battle of al-Qadisiyyah ends Sasanian control west of Iran.
- **642** — Battle of Nahavand, the last major Sasanian defeat, opens Persia proper to invasion.
- **644** — Caliph Umar is assassinated by a Persian slave, Abu Lu'lu'a Firuz.
- **651** — Death of the last Sasanian emperor, Yazdegerd III, near Merv ends organized resistance.

## Body

### Sasanian Empire Before the Conquest
The Sasanian Empire was weakened by the prolonged Byzantine–Sasanian War of 602–628, which drained resources and left the army exhausted. Following the execution of Khosrow II in 628, a rapid succession of ten rulers within four years led to political chaos and the Sasanian Interregnum, a civil war that decentralized power by 632. Socially, the empire was stratified into four classes, with the commoners bearing the sole tax burden. The annexation of its Arab client state, the Lakhmids, in 602 is considered a key factor in its fall, as the Lakhmids later aided the Muslim invaders.

### The Conquest Campaigns
The conquest occurred in several phases. The first invasion in 633, led by Khalid ibn al-Walid, captured parts of Mesopotamia but was later reversed. The second invasion began in 636, culminating in the decisive Battle of al-Qadisiyyah, which ended Sasanian control west of the Zagros Mountains. After the Battle of Nahavand in 642, Caliph Umar ordered a full-scale invasion of the Sasanian heartland. Coordinated multi-pronged attacks conquered central Iran, Fars, Kerman, Sistan, Azerbaijan, and Khorasan. The last Sasanian emperor, Yazdegerd III, was killed in 651, ending the empire.

### Historiography and Scholarly Debate
Traditional narratives relied on Armenian and later Arabic sources. Recent scholarship, notably Parvaneh Pourshariati's 2008 work, challenges the conventional timeline and narrative. Pourshariati argues the Sasanian Empire was a decentralized confederation with the Parthians, who withdrew their support, leaving the Sasanians ill-prepared. She also contends the Arab conquest of Mesopotamia began between 628 and 632, during the Sasanian-Parthian civil war, rather than after 632.

### Aftermath and Administration
Under the Rashidun Caliphate, the administrative system of the late Sasanian period was largely retained. Non-Muslim subjects (dhimmis) paid a poll tax (jizya) in exchange for protection and exemption from military service. The old Sasanian land tax (kharaj) was also adopted. Islamization was a gradual process spanning centuries, incentivized by tax exemptions and social privileges. Zoroastrians faced persecution, and many fled to India. By the Late Middle Ages, Islam was Iran's predominant religion, initially Sunni until the Safavid forced conversion to Shia Islam in the 16th century.

## Terms
- **Rashidun Caliphate**: The first four caliphs (successors) of the Islamic prophet Muhammad, ruling from 632 to 661 CE.
- **Sasanian Empire**: The last Persian imperial dynasty before the Muslim conquest, ruling from 224 to 651 CE.
- **Jizya**: A poll tax levied on non-Muslim subjects (dhimmis) in an Islamic state.
- **Kharaj**: A land tax adopted from the Sasanian administrative system.
- **Dhimmi**: Non-Muslim citizens of an Islamic state who paid the jizya in return for protection.
- **Zagros Mountains**: A major mountain range in western Iran that served as a natural defensive barrier.
- **Dehqan**: A class of Persian landholding nobility or gentry.
- **Confederation**: A union of political entities for a common purpose, as Pourshariati describes the Sasanian-Parthian relationship.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Timeline of the Conquest**: Pourshariati argues the Arab conquest of Mesopotamia began between 628 and 632, during the Sasanian civil war, contradicting the traditional view that it started after 632.
- **Nature of the Sasanian State**: Pourshariati's thesis that the Sasanian Empire was a decentralized confederation with the Parthians, whose withdrawal caused its collapse, challenges the traditional view of a centralized monarchy.
- **Muhammad's Letter to Khosrow II**: The traditional account that the prophet Muhammad sent a letter to Khosrow II inviting him to Islam is disputed by some modern historians as unlikely given the Sasanian court's complexity.

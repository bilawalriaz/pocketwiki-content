# Stephen Báthory

## Overview
Stephen Báthory (1533–1586) was a Hungarian prince who became King of Poland and Grand Duke of Lithuania through election and marriage. He is celebrated as one of the most successful rulers in Polish-Lithuanian history, primarily for his military victory against Russia in the Livonian War, which secured vast territories and a favorable peace treaty. His reign strengthened the Commonwealth's military and judiciary but was marked by internal conflicts with the nobility.

## Timeline
- **27 September 1533** — Born in Somlyó, Transylvania.
- **c. 1549–1550** — Briefly studies in Italy, likely at Padua University.
- **1571** — Elected Voivode of Transylvania after the death of John II Sigismund Zápolya.
- **1573** — Defeats rival Gáspár Bekes, securing control of Transylvania.
- **12 December 1575** — Polish primate declares Habsburg Archduke Maximilian III king, but a faction elects Anna Jagiellon.
- **1 May 1576** — Marries Anna Jagiellon and is crowned King of Poland and Grand Duke of Lithuania.
- **1577** — Defeats the Danzig (Gdańsk) rebellion, securing the city's recognition and payment.
- **1578** — Establishes the Crown Tribunal, a major legal reform.
- **1579–1581** — Leads successful military campaigns against Russia in Livonia, capturing Polotsk and Velikiye Luki.
- **15 January 1582** — Signs the Truce of Jam Zapolski with Russia, gaining significant territory.
- **12 December 1586** — Dies without legitimate heirs, triggering a contested succession.

## Body
**Rise to Power in Transylvania and Poland**
Born into the Hungarian Báthory family, Stephen initially served the Habsburgs against the Ottomans before switching allegiance to John II Sigismund Zápolya. After Zápolya's death in 1571, Báthory was elected Voivode of Transylvania, defeating his rival Gáspár Bekes by 1573. In 1576, he was elected King of Poland and Grand Duke of Lithuania through a complex compromise that saw him marry Anna Jagiellon, the sister of the last Jagiellon king. His election was contested by the Habsburg candidate, Maximilian II.

**Consolidating Rule and Domestic Policy**
Báthory's early reign focused on securing his authority. He accepted concessions to the Lithuanian nobility to gain recognition as Grand Duke. He then suppressed the wealthy city of Danzig's rebellion, defeating its army in 1577 but ultimately reaching a compromise. With the support of Chancellor Jan Zamoyski, he reformed the judiciary by creating legal tribunals and modernized the army, including creating the *piechota wybraniecka* (peasant infantry). He founded the Academy of Vilnius and promoted Catholicism while respecting the Commonwealth's religious tolerance.

**The Livonian War and Legacy**
Báthory's greatest achievement was the war against Ivan the Terrible's Russia, which had invaded Livonia. Leading the army personally, he captured Polotsk (1579) and Velikiye Luki (1580), then besieged Pskov. This forced Russia to the negotiating table, resulting in the Truce of Jam Zapolski (1582). The treaty was highly favorable, with Russia ceding Polatsk, Veliz, and most of Livonia. His death in 1586 left no clear heir, leading to a contested election and a brief interregnum.

## Terms
- **Voivode**: A high-ranking historical title for a governor or military commander, especially in Transylvania and the Polish-Lithuanian Commonwealth.
- **Sejm**: The parliament of the Polish-Lithuanian Commonwealth, with the power to elect kings and approve taxes.
- **Crown Tribunal**: A high court of appeal for the Polish Crown, established by Báthory in 1578 to streamline justice.
- **Piechota wybraniecka**: An infantry formation of peasants established by Báthory to modernize the Commonwealth army.
- **Livonian War**: A prolonged conflict (1558–1583) primarily between the Polish-Lithuanian Commonwealth and Russia for control of Livonia.
- **Truce of Jam Zapolski**: The 1582 treaty ending the Livonian War, which granted the Commonwealth significant territorial gains from Russia.
- **Golden Freedoms**: The legal privileges of the Polish nobility (szlachta) that limited royal authority, a point of tension during Báthory's reign.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Internal vs. External Focus**: Historians debate whether Báthory's primary interest was the Commonwealth or his native Hungary. His plans for a Polish-led liberation of Ottoman Hungary never materialized.
- **Impact on Nobility**: His execution of Samuel Zborowski and centralizing policies were seen by contemporaries as attempts to curtail the nobility's Golden Freedoms, a charge that shaped his early posthumous reputation.
- **Historical Reputation**: His image evolved from being criticized after his death for favoritism toward Hungarians to becoming a celebrated national hero in the 19th century during the partitions of Poland, and remains a complex figure in modern historiography.

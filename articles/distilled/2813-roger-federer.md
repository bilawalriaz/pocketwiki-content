# Roger Federer

## Overview
Roger Federer is a Swiss former professional tennis player, widely regarded as one of the greatest of all time. He was a dominant force in men's tennis for nearly two decades, known for his graceful all-court style, sportsmanship, and remarkable longevity. His career, marked by numerous records and a fierce rivalry with Rafael Nadal and Novak Djokovic, helped define the sport's modern "Golden Age."

## Timeline
- **8 August 1981** — Born in Basel, Switzerland.
- **1998** — Wins Wimbledon Boys' Singles title; turns professional.
- **2001** — Defeats Pete Sampras at Wimbledon; wins first ATP title in Milan.
- **2003** — Wins first Grand Slam title at Wimbledon.
- **2004** — Becomes world No. 1; wins three Grand Slam titles (Australian Open, Wimbledon, US Open).
- **2007** — Wins three Grand Slam titles for the third time in a year.
- **2009** — Completes Career Grand Slam by winning French Open; breaks Pete Sampras's record of 14 major titles at Wimbledon.
- **2012** — Wins seventh Wimbledon title, returning to world No. 1; wins Olympic silver in singles.
- **2014** — Leads Switzerland to first Davis Cup title.
- **2017** — Returns from hiatus to win Australian Open and record eighth Wimbledon title.
- **2018** — Becomes first man to win 20 Grand Slam titles; becomes oldest ATP world No. 1 at age 36.
- **September 2022** — Retires from professional tennis after the Laver Cup.

## Body

### Early Life and Junior Career
Born to a Swiss father and South African mother, Federer holds dual citizenship. He began playing tennis at age three and was coached from age eight by Adolf Kacovsky, who taught him the one-handed backhand. He trained at the Swiss National Tennis Center from age 14. As a junior, he won the 1998 Wimbledon Boys' Singles and Doubles titles and finished the year as the world No. 1 junior.

### Rise to Dominance (2001–2009)
Federer's breakthrough came in 2001 with a win over Pete Sampras at Wimbledon. He won his first Grand Slam in 2003 and ascended to world No. 1 in 2004. From 2004 to 2007, he was the dominant player, winning at least three Grand Slams in three separate seasons (2004, 2006, 2007). His primary rival during this period was Rafael Nadal. In 2009, Federer completed the Career Grand Slam by winning the French Open and surpassed Sampras's record of 14 major titles at Wimbledon.

### Later Career and Comebacks (2010–2022)
After 2009, Federer's dominance was challenged by Novak Djokovic and a resurgent Nadal. He endured injury struggles, including a half-year hiatus in 2016 for knee surgery. He staged a major comeback in 2017, winning the Australian Open and Wimbledon. In 2018, he became the first man to win 20 Grand Slams and the oldest world No. 1. After further knee surgeries in 2020 and 2021, he retired in September 2022.

### Rivalries
Federer's career was defined by rivalries with the "Big Three." He trails Rafael Nadal 16–24 in their head-to-head, with Nadal dominating on clay. He trails Novak Djokovic 23–27, with Djokovic winning multiple Grand Slam finals against him. He leads Andy Murray 14–11 and Andy Roddick 21–3.

### Playing Style and Legacy
Federer was an all-court player known for his fluid movement, powerful forehand, and versatile single-handed backhand. He was celebrated for his grace and sportsmanship, winning a record 13 Stefan Edberg Sportsmanship Awards. He holds numerous records, including 20 Grand Slam singles titles, 310 weeks at world No. 1, and a record eight Wimbledon titles. He is also a philanthropist, founding the Roger Federer Foundation, and a successful businessman.

## Terms
- **Grand Slam**: The four major tennis tournaments: Australian Open, French Open, Wimbledon, and US Open.
- **ATP Tour**: The global men's professional tennis circuit.
- **World No. 1**: The top-ranked player in the Association of Tennis Professionals (ATP) rankings.
- **Career Grand Slam**: Winning all four Grand Slam tournaments at least once in a career.
- **Open Era**: The period in professional tennis starting in 1968, when professionals could compete in Grand Slam tournaments.
- **Masters (or Masters 1000)**: A series of nine prestigious tennis tournaments on the ATP Tour.
- **SABR (Sneak Attack By Roger)**: An aggressive return tactic where Federer charged forward to hit a half-volley on the opponent's second serve.
- **Big Three**: The trio of Roger Federer, Rafael Nadal, and Novak Djokovic, who dominated men's tennis for nearly two decades.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Greatest of All Time (GOAT)**: A persistent debate among fans, players, and analysts. While many considered Federer the greatest for years, the subsequent records of Nadal (22 Grand Slams) and Djokovic (24 Grand Slams) have intensified the discussion. Some, like Richard Gasquet, argue aesthetics and grace should weigh as heavily as title counts.
- **Impact of Rivalries**: Scholars debate how the rivalries with Nadal and Djokovic both challenged Federer's dominance and ultimately elevated his legacy and the sport's popularity.
- **Longevity and Adaptation**: Analysts study how Federer successfully adapted his game and managed his schedule to remain competitive into his late 30s, especially after major knee surgeries.

# Documentary film

## Overview
A documentary film is a nonfiction motion picture intended to document reality for instruction, education, or historical record. It is a cinematic tradition and mode of audience reception without clear boundaries, evolving from documentary photography and early "actuality films." Documentaries serve as journalism, advocacy, or personal expression, and their importance lies in introducing new perspectives not prevalent in traditional media.

## Timeline
- **1898** — Bolesław Matuszewski publishes early texts on cinema's historical and documentary value.
- **1922** — Robert J. Flaherty's *Nanook of the North* embraces romanticism in documentary film.
- **1926** — John Grierson coins the term "documentary" in a review of *Moana*.
- **1920s–30s** — "City symphony" and Kino-Pravda avant-garde film genres emerge.
- **1935** — Leni Riefenstahl's propaganda film *Triumph of the Will* is released.
- **1930s–40s** — The British Documentary Film Movement and U.S. New Deal productions flourish.
- **1960s–70s** — Cinéma vérité and direct cinema movements develop with portable technology.
- **1988** — Errol Morris's *The Thin Blue Line* introduces hybrid documentary forms.
- **2000s** — Social media platforms increase documentary distribution and accessibility.

## Body

### Definition and Origins
The word "documentary" was coined by John Grierson in 1926, defining it as the "creative treatment of actuality." This contrasts with Dziga Vertov's credo of presenting "life as it is." Early film, pre-1900, consisted of short "actuality films" depicting single events like a train entering a station. These evolved from documentary photography, which used singular images to detail historical events.

### Historical Development
Early 20th-century travelogue films, or "scenics," were popular. The 1920s saw romanticized documentaries like Flaherty's *Nanook* and avant-garde "city symphony" films. The 1930s–40s were dominated by propaganda and state-sponsored films, such as Riefenstahl's *Triumph of the Will* and the U.S. *Why We Fight* series. The 1960s–70s introduced cinéma vérité and direct cinema, using portable cameras for intimate, observational filmmaking. Modern documentaries have achieved theatrical success and often use social impact campaigns.

### Modes and Styles
Documentaries employ various modes: expository films use authoritative voice-over to persuade; observational films aim for minimal intervention; participatory films involve the filmmaker as a social actor; and reflexive films draw attention to their own construction. Other forms include docufiction (a hybrid of fiction and documentary), compilation films, and poetic documentaries that use associative editing. Narration styles range from traditional voice-over to hosted narration.

### Modern Trends and Challenges
Digital technology has lowered production costs, aiding filmmakers. However, funding remains challenging, with broadcasters often becoming key funders. Translation of documentaries poses difficulties due to tight deadlines, poor transcriptions, and the need for specialized terminology. The genre overlaps with television forms like reality TV and "making-of" documentaries.

## Terms
- **Actuality film**: Early, brief nonfiction films depicting real events without narrative structure.
- **Cinéma vérité**: A documentary style using portable cameras and sound to capture events as they unfold, often with filmmaker involvement.
- **Direct cinema**: A North American documentary tradition similar to cinéma vérité, emphasizing observational, non-interventionist filming.
- **Docufiction**: A hybrid genre combining elements of documentary and fiction film.
- **Expository documentary**: A mode that uses authoritative commentary to advance an argument or point of view.
- **Hybrid documentary**: A form that blends factual content with fictional techniques, challenging traditional fact production.
- **Kino-Pravda**: A Soviet newsreel series by Dziga Vertov focused on "cinematic truth" through innovative camera techniques.
- **Social impact campaign**: A strategy using media to convert public awareness of social issues into engagement and action.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The definition of documentary remains debated, with tensions between Grierson's "creative treatment of actuality" and Vertov's "life as it is."
- The use of stylized re-enactments and directorial manipulation in films like *The Thin Blue Line* raises questions about whether such works can truly be called documentaries.
- The line between documentary and other forms, such as reality television or "mondo films," is often blurred, leading to critical debate about authenticity and genre boundaries.

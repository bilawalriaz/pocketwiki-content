# Elevation

## Overview
Elevation is the height of a geographic point on the Earth's surface above or below a reference level, typically mean sea level. It is a fundamental measurement in geography, cartography, and aviation, distinct from related terms like altitude (for points above the surface) and depth (for points below). Understanding elevation is crucial for mapping terrain, planning construction, and ensuring safe air travel.

## Timeline
- **c. 1929** — The Sea Level Datum of 1929 (NGVD 29) was established as a vertical datum for the United States.
- **1988** — The North American Vertical Datum of 1988 (NAVD 88) superseded NGVD 29 as the official vertical control datum for North America.
- **Ongoing** — The U.S. Geological Survey (USGS) develops the 3D Elevation Program (3DEP) to provide high-resolution topographic data.

## Body

### Definition and Distinction
Elevation measures a point's height above or below a fixed reference, most commonly a geoid—a mathematical model of Earth's sea level as an equipotential gravitational surface. The term is primarily used for points on the Earth's surface. For points above the surface, such as aircraft, the terms "altitude" or "geopotential height" are used, while "depth" is used for points below the surface. Elevation is not the same as the distance from the Earth's center; due to the equatorial bulge, Mount Everest has the highest elevation, while Chimborazo has the greatest geocentric distance.

### Use in Aviation
In aviation, "elevation" or "aerodrome elevation" is defined by the International Civil Aviation Organization (ICAO) as the highest point of an airport's landing area. This measurement, often in feet, is critical for flight planning and is found on approach charts. It is a distinct concept from an aircraft's altitude or height during flight.

### Representation on Maps and in GIS
Topographical maps depict elevation primarily through contour lines, which connect points of equal height. In Geographic Information Systems (GIS)—computer systems for visualizing and analyzing spatial data—elevation is commonly represented by Digital Elevation Models (DEMs). A DEM is a raster (grid) dataset where each cell contains an elevation value. The USGS 3D Elevation Program (3DEP) aims to provide high-quality, nationwide LiDAR data for the conterminous U.S., Hawaii, and territories, delivered as nationally seamless DEM layers at resolutions of 1/3, 1, and 2 arcseconds.

## Terms
- **Geoid**: A mathematical model of the Earth's sea level, used as the reference surface for measuring elevation.
- **Vertical Datum**: A fixed reference level (like a geoid) from which elevations or depths are measured.
- **Altitude**: The height of a point above the Earth's surface, typically used for aircraft or spacecraft.
- **Depth**: The measurement of a point below the Earth's surface.
- **GIS (Geographic Information System)**: A computer system for capturing, storing, analyzing, and displaying geographic data.
- **Digital Elevation Model (DEM)**: A raster dataset representing terrain surface elevation.
- **Contour Line**: A line on a map connecting points of equal elevation.
- **LiDAR**: A remote sensing method using laser light to measure distances and create high-resolution elevation data.

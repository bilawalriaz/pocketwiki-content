# Syrian civil war

## Overview
The Syrian civil war was a devastating armed conflict that began in 2011 as part of the Arab Spring, evolving from protests into a multi-sided war involving domestic factions and foreign powers. It lasted nearly 14 years, culminating in the fall of the Assad regime in December 2024. The war caused massive humanitarian suffering, reshaped regional geopolitics, and became a major proxy conflict.

## Timeline
- **March 2011** — Protests against the Assad regime begin, marking the start of the Syrian revolution.
- **2012** — Conflict escalates into a full civil war; the Free Syrian Army (FSA) forms.
- **2013** — Iran launches a military intervention to support the Assad government. Rebels capture Raqqa.
- **September 2014** — U.S.-led coalition begins airstrikes against the Islamic State (IS).
- **September 2015** — Russia begins a military intervention in support of Assad.
- **December 2016** — Pro-Assad forces recapture Aleppo after a four-year battle.
- **2017** — Hay'at Tahrir al-Sham (HTS) forms the Syrian Salvation Government in Idlib.
- **March 2020** — A ceasefire is established in Idlib, leading to a period of stalemate.
- **November 2024** — HTS-led coalition launches a major offensive, rapidly capturing Aleppo, Hama, and Homs.
- **December 8, 2024** — Bashar al-Assad flees Damascus; his regime collapses. A transitional government is formed.
- **January 2025** — Ahmed al-Sharaa is appointed President of Syria at the Syrian Revolution Victory Conference.

## Body

### Origins and Escalation
The war began in March 2011 with popular protests against the authoritarian Ba'athist rule of Bashar al-Assad, who succeeded his father Hafez al-Assad in 2000. The regime's violent crackdown on demonstrations led to the formation of armed opposition groups like the Free Syrian Army (FSA). By 2012, the conflict had escalated into a full-scale civil war. The uprising was fueled by long-standing political repression, socioeconomic inequality, and a severe drought from 2006-2011 that displaced farming families to cities.

### Foreign Intervention and Proxy War
The conflict became a major proxy war. The Assad government received critical military and financial support from Iran, Russia, and Hezbollah. Iran intervened directly in 2013, and Russia launched airstrikes and ground operations in September 2015. Opposition groups received arms and training from the U.S., Turkey, Qatar, and Saudi Arabia. The U.S.-led coalition focused its intervention from 2014 on defeating the Islamic State (IS), supporting the Kurdish-led Syrian Democratic Forces (SDF). Turkey also intervened directly, opposing both IS and Kurdish forces, and backing the Syrian National Army (SNA).

### Rise of Extremist Groups and Chemical Warfare
The power vacuum allowed extremist groups to flourish. The Islamic State (IS) seized large territories in eastern Syria and western Iraq in 2014, prompting the U.S.-led coalition campaign. The conflict saw the deadliest use of chemical weapons since the Iran-Iraq War, predominantly by Syrian government forces. The 2013 Ghouta sarin attack was a key atrocity, followed by numerous other confirmed chemical attacks documented by the UN and OPCW, leading to Syria's suspension from the Chemical Weapons Convention in 2021.

### Stalemate and the Fall of Assad
After a 2020 ceasefire in Idlib, the frontlines largely froze, and the war entered a stalemate period from 2020 to 2024. This ended abruptly in November 2024 when a coalition led by Hay'at Tahrir al-Sham (HTS) launched a rapid offensive. The Syrian army collapsed, and HTS captured major cities. On December 8, 2024, Bashar al-Assad fled to Moscow, ending over 60 years of Ba'athist rule. A transitional government was formed, led by former HTS leader Ahmed al-Sharaa.

### Humanitarian Catastrophe and Human Rights Abuses
The war caused one of the 21st century's worst humanitarian crises. Estimates of deaths range from 470,000 to over 650,000. Approximately 6.7 million Syrians became refugees, and over 6 million were internally displaced. All parties were accused of severe human rights violations. The Assad regime was documented as responsible for the vast majority of abuses, including systematic torture, enforced disappearances, and chemical attacks, as detailed in reports like the 2014 Caesar Report. The Islamic State committed widespread atrocities, including public executions.

## Terms
- **Ba'athist regime**: The authoritarian, one-party socialist government led by the Assad family since 1963.
- **Free Syrian Army (FSA)**: An early umbrella term for loosely organized anti-Assad rebel militias.
- **Hay'at Tahrir al-Sham (HTS)**: A Sunni Islamist militant group that became the dominant force in Idlib and led the final offensive against Assad.
- **Syrian Democratic Forces (SDF)**: A multi-ethnic, Kurdish-led coalition that fought IS and controlled northeastern Syria.
- **Autonomous Administration of North and East Syria (AANES/Rojava)**: The de facto autonomous region in northeastern Syria governed by the SDF.
- **Islamic State (IS)**: A jihadist terrorist group that declared a caliphate in 2014 and was a major belligerent.
- **Syrian Salvation Government (SSG)**: The technocratic, Islamist administration formed by HTS to govern Idlib from 2017.
- **Operation Timber Sycamore**: A covert CIA program to arm and train Syrian rebel groups.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Role of the drought**: While the 2006-2011 drought exacerbated socioeconomic tensions, scholars debate its direct causal role in sparking the conflict versus other political and economic factors.
- **Responsibility for chemical attacks**: While multiple investigations attribute the vast majority of chemical attacks to the Assad regime, Russia and the Syrian government have consistently denied responsibility, often blaming opposition groups.
- **Future of Kurdish autonomy**: The status of the Autonomous Administration of North and East Syria (Rojava) within a new Syrian state remains a critical and unresolved issue, with tensions between Kurdish-led forces and the new transitional government.
- **Impact of sanctions**: The renewal of U.S. sanctions (Caesar Act) post-Assad is debated as either a necessary tool for accountability or a major obstacle to Syria's reconstruction and humanitarian recovery.

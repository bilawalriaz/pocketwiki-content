# Lyndon B. Johnson

## Overview
Lyndon B. Johnson (1908–1973) was the 36th U.S. President, serving from 1963 to 1969. He assumed office after President John F. Kennedy's assassination and won a landslide election in 1964. Johnson is remembered for his ambitious domestic agenda, the "Great Society," which included landmark civil rights legislation and anti-poverty programs, but his legacy is heavily overshadowed by his escalation of the Vietnam War.

## Body

### Early Career and Senate Leadership
Born into poverty, Johnson worked as a teacher before entering politics as a congressional aide. He was elected to the House in 1937 and served until 1949. After a controversial 87-vote victory in the 1948 Senate primary, he became a U.S. Senator. Johnson rose to become the Senate Majority Leader in 1954, a position he held with exceptional skill. He was known for his relentless persuasion tactic, "The Johnson Treatment," and was considered one of the most effective majority leaders in history. He shepherded the Civil Rights Acts of 1957 and 1960 through Congress, the first such laws since Reconstruction.

### Vice Presidency and Succession
In 1960, Johnson lost the Democratic presidential nomination to John F. Kennedy but was chosen as his vice-presidential running mate. As Vice President, he felt marginalized by the Kennedy administration but chaired the National Aeronautics and Space Council, recommending the Apollo Moon landing program. After Kennedy's assassination on November 22, 1963, Johnson was sworn in as president aboard Air Force One. He established the Warren Commission to investigate the assassination.

### Domestic Policy: The Great Society
Johnson launched an ambitious domestic agenda called the "Great Society," aimed at eliminating poverty and racial injustice. Key achievements included:
*   **Civil Rights:** He signed the Civil Rights Act of 1964, outlawing discrimination based on race, color, religion, sex, or national origin. The Voting Rights Act of 1965 eliminated barriers to black voting. The Fair Housing Act of 1968 banned discrimination in housing.
*   **War on Poverty:** Programs like the Job Corps, VISTA, and Head Start were created. The Economic Opportunity Act of 1964 established the Office of Economic Opportunity.
*   **Healthcare:** The Social Security Amendments of 1965 created Medicare (for the elderly) and Medicaid (for the poor).
*   **Education:** The Elementary and Secondary Education Act of 1965 and the Higher Education Act of 1965 significantly increased federal funding for schools and created student loan programs.
*   **Other:** He signed the Immigration and Nationality Act of 1965, abolishing the national origins quota system. He also signed over 300 conservation measures, including the Clean Air Act and the Wilderness Act.

### The Vietnam War
Johnson's foreign policy was dominated by the Vietnam War. He escalated U.S. involvement from 16,700 troops in 1963 to over 500,000 by 1968, following the Gulf of Tonkin Resolution (1964) and the start of Operation Rolling Thunder bombing campaign (1965). The war became increasingly unpopular, leading to a "credibility gap" between his statements and reality. The 1968 Tet Offensive, though a military defeat for North Vietnam, turned public opinion decisively against the war. Facing low approval ratings and a divided party, Johnson announced on March 31, 1968, that he would not seek re-election.

### 1968 Election and Post-Presidency
After Johnson withdrew, Vice President Hubert Humphrey won the Democratic nomination but lost the general election to Richard Nixon. Johnson retired to his Texas ranch. He died of a heart attack on January 22, 1973, just days after Nixon's second inauguration.

## Terms
*   **Great Society:** Johnson's domestic program aimed at eliminating poverty and racial injustice through major federal legislation.
*   **War on Poverty:** A set of programs within the Great Society designed to alleviate poverty in the United States.
*   **Credibility Gap:** The growing public skepticism about the Johnson administration's truthful reporting on the Vietnam War.
*   **Gulf of Tonkin Resolution:** A 1964 congressional resolution that granted President Johnson broad authority to use military force in Southeast Asia without a formal declaration of war.
*   **Operation Rolling Thunder:** The sustained aerial bombing campaign against North Vietnam conducted by the U.S. from 1965 to 1968.
*   **Tet Offensive:** A major 1968 military campaign by North Vietnamese and Viet Cong forces that, despite its failure, severely damaged U.S. public support for the war.
*   **The Johnson Treatment:** Johnson's aggressive and persuasive style of political negotiation, often involving physical intimidation and exhaustive personal lobbying.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Legacy Assessment:** Historians consistently rank Johnson highly for his transformative domestic achievements but heavily criticize his foreign policy, particularly the escalation of the Vietnam War. His overall legacy remains deeply contested.
*   **Vietnam War Decision-Making:** Scholars debate the precise factors behind Johnson's decision to escalate the war, including his fear of appearing weak on communism, his trust in military advisors, and the political pressure he felt.
*   **1968 Withdrawal:** Historians have debated the primary reasons for Johnson's surprise decision not to seek re-election, citing factors like his declining popularity, health concerns, a desire to heal the nation, and the influence of the Tet Offensive.

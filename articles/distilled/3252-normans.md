# Normans

## Overview
The Normans were a medieval people who emerged from the fusion of Norse Viking settlers and the local Frankish population in the Duchy of Normandy. Their unique identity, forged in the 10th century, combined a fierce martial spirit with adopted Frankish language, religion, and feudal structures. This blend propelled them to become one of the most influential forces in medieval Europe, conquering England, founding kingdoms in Sicily and Antioch, and leaving a lasting cultural and political legacy across the continent and the Near East.

## Timeline
- **790-800** — First Scandinavian Viking raids target the coasts of northern Gaul.
- **911** — Treaty of Saint-Clair-sur-Epte: Viking leader Rollo swears fealty to King Charles III of West Francia, establishing the Duchy of Normandy.
- **c. 940s** — A distinct Norman ethnic and cultural identity emerges from the intermixing of Norse settlers and local Franks.
- **1017** — Norman warriors first enter southern Italy, beginning their conquest of the region.
- **1066** — William the Conqueror defeats King Harold II at the Battle of Hastings, leading to the Norman conquest of England.
- **1130** — Roger II of Sicily is crowned king, uniting Norman territories in southern Italy and Sicily into the Kingdom of Sicily.
- **1169** — Normans invade Ireland at Bannow Bay, beginning their conquest and settlement.
- **1191** — Richard the Lionheart conquers Cyprus during the Third Crusade.
- **1204** — Philip II of France seizes mainland Normandy from the English crown.
- **1259** — Treaty of Paris: The English king cedes his claim to the Duchy of Normandy, except for the Channel Islands.
- **1402-1405** — Norman noble Jean de Béthencourt leads the conquest of the Canary Islands of Lanzarote, Fuerteventura, and El Hierro.

## Body

### Origins and the Duchy of Normandy
The Normans originated from Norse Viking settlers, primarily from Denmark, Norway, and Sweden, who raided and then settled the northern coast of West Francia. Their settlement was formalized in 911 when their leader, Rollo, agreed to the Treaty of Saint-Clair-sur-Epte. In exchange for the lands between the River Epte and the Atlantic, Rollo swore fealty to King Charles III, accepted baptism, and agreed to defend the region from further Viking raids. This new fief, the Duchy of Normandy, expanded under Rollo's descendants. Within a generation, the Norman elite adopted the Gallo-Romance language (which evolved into Norman French), Christianity, and Frankish feudal customs, while retaining a distinct warrior ethos. This fusion created a formidable and cohesive principality.

### Conquests and Expansion
Driven by a martial spirit and limited prospects at home, Norman adventurers sought land and riches abroad. Their most famous conquest was England in 1066, led by William the Conqueror, which replaced the Anglo-Saxon aristocracy with a Norman ruling class and created an Anglo-Norman culture. In southern Italy and Sicily, families like the Hautevilles and Drengots conquered territories from Byzantines and Saracens, culminating in the founding of the Kingdom of Sicily in 1130. This kingdom was notable for its religious tolerance and multicultural bureaucracy. Normans also played major roles in the Iberian Reconquista, the Crusader states (founding the Principality of Antioch), and conquered Cyprus, parts of North Africa, and the Canary Islands.

### Culture and Legacy
Norman culture was a synthesis of their Norse heritage and adopted Frankish traditions. They spread a distinctive Romanesque architecture, characterized by rounded arches and massive proportions, across their conquered lands. Their legal systems, like the customary law of Normandy, blended Frankish, Carolingian, and Norse influences. The Norman language, Norman French, became an important literary and administrative language, influencing the development of Middle English. Their legacy persists in the regional languages of the Channel Islands, the legal systems of Jersey and Guernsey, and the many place names and family names (e.g., those beginning with "Fitz-") across Europe.

## Terms
- **Duchy of Normandy**: A feudal fief of the Kingdom of France established in 911, ruled by Norman dukes who were vassals to the French king.
- **Feudal system**: A medieval social and legal structure where land was held by a king and granted to nobles in exchange for military service and loyalty, creating a hierarchy of vassals.
- **Romanesque architecture**: A style of medieval architecture characterized by semi-circular arches, massive walls, and sturdy pillars, widely spread by the Normans.
- **Norman French**: The Gallo-Romance language that evolved from the Old Norman dialect, used by the Norman elite and later as a language of law and literature in England.
- **Anglo-Norman**: The dialect of Norman French used in England after the 1066 conquest, which influenced the development of Middle English.
- **Vassal**: A person granted a fief (land) by a lord in exchange for allegiance and military service.
- **Reconquista**: The centuries-long period of Christian reconquest of the Iberian Peninsula from Muslim rule, in which Norman mercenaries participated.
- **Crusader states**: Christian feudal states established in the Levant during the Crusades, such as the Principality of Antioch, founded by the Norman Bohemond I.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The precise ethnic composition of Rollo's original settlers is debated, with sources indicating they included Danes, Norwegians, Norse-Gaels, and Anglo-Danes.
- The degree to which Norman mercenaries in Byzantine service were from Norman Italy versus other parts of France is uncertain, as Byzantine sources often referred to all Western Europeans as "Franks."
- The extent of cultural assimilation versus the retention of a distinct Norman identity in regions like Ireland and England over time is a subject of historical analysis.

# Gerolamo Cardano

## Overview
Gerolamo Cardano (1501–1576) was an Italian polymath whose work in mathematics, medicine, and natural philosophy made him a foundational figure of the Renaissance. He is best remembered for publishing the first solutions to cubic and quartic equations, introducing negative numbers and binomial coefficients to Europe, and writing one of the first systematic treatises on probability. His life was marked by personal tragedy, conflict with authorities, and a relentless curiosity that spanned disciplines from astrology to hydrodynamics.

## Timeline
- **24 Sep 1501** — Born in Pavia, illegitimate son of Fazio Cardano.
- **1520** — Entered the University of Pavia to study philosophy and science.
- **1525** — Graduated with a doctorate in medicine from the University of Padua.
- **1531** — Married Lucia Banderini; began practicing medicine in Piove di Sacco.
- **1536** — Became a sought-after doctor in Milan, quitting his teaching position.
- **1545** — Published *Ars Magna*, revealing solutions to cubic and quartic equations.
- **1550** — Published *De Subtilitate*, a work on natural philosophy.
- **1552** — Traveled to Scotland to successfully treat the Archbishop of St Andrews.
- **c. 1564** — Wrote *Liber de ludo aleae*, the first systematic work on probability.
- **1570** — Arrested by the Inquisition for heresy; lost his professorship.
- **21 Sep 1576** — Died in Rome, having received a papal annuity.

## Body

**Early Life and Medical Career**
Born in Pavia to a mathematician father, Cardano's childhood was marked by illness and conflict. He studied medicine at Padua but was denied a license to practice in Milan due to his illegitimate birth and combative reputation. He settled for a position in a smaller town before eventually gaining recognition in Milan, where he became a highly sought-after physician by the 1530s. He later turned down offers from European royalty to remain in Milan.

**Mathematical Contributions**
Cardano's landmark 1545 book *Ars Magna* made several critical contributions to algebra. It presented the first systematic use of negative numbers in Europe and published the solutions for cubic and quartic equations, crediting earlier mathematicians like Scipione del Ferro and his own student Lodovico Ferrari. A bitter, decade-long dispute with Niccolò Tartaglia arose over the publication of a cubic solution Tartaglia had shared with him. In the work, Cardano also acknowledged the existence of imaginary numbers. In *Opus novum de proportionibus*, he introduced binomial coefficients and the binomial theorem.

**Probability and Other Fields**
Cardano was an accomplished gambler who used games of chance to develop the first systematic theory of probability in his book *Liber de ludo aleae* (written c. 1564, published 1663). He defined odds as the ratio of favorable to unfavorable outcomes. Beyond mathematics, he made contributions to music theory, hydrodynamics, and cryptography (introducing the Cardan grille). His work *De Subtilitate* (1550) noted the properties of steam and condensation, an observation seen as a precursor to the development of the steam engine. He also argued for the education of deaf people.

**Later Years and Legacy**
Cardano's later life was troubled. His favorite son was executed for murder in 1560, and another son stole from him. In 1570, the Inquisition arrested Cardano for heresy, primarily over his astrological writings, including a published horoscope of Jesus. He was imprisoned, lost his position, and his non-medical works were banned. He spent his final years in Rome under papal protection, completing his autobiography. His legacy includes the Cardan shaft (universal joint) and the gimbal, though his exact role in their invention is debated.

## Terms
- **Polymath**: A person of wide-ranging learning and expertise.
- **Cubic equation**: A polynomial equation of the third degree (e.g., ax³ + bx + c = 0).
- **Binomial theorem**: A formula for expanding expressions raised to a power, involving binomial coefficients.
- **Imaginary numbers**: Numbers that are multiples of the square root of -1, which Cardano acknowledged but did not fully understand.
- **Probability**: The measure of the likelihood that an event will occur, which Cardano began to formalize.
- **Gimbal**: A pivoted support that allows the rotation of an object (like a compass) on multiple axes.
- **Cardan grille**: A cryptographic tool for writing secret messages.
- **De Subtilitate**: Cardano's 1550 work on natural philosophy, covering topics from minerals to steam.
- **Inquisition**: A judicial institution within the Catholic Church that combated heresy.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Authorship of inventions**: Cardano is credited with inventions like the gimbal and the Cardan shaft, but the source notes these devices may predate him or were not described by him.
- **The Tartaglia dispute**: The ethical and legal conflict over Cardano's publication of Tartaglia's solution to the cubic equation remains a famous episode in the history of science, involving questions of sworn secrecy and priority.
- **Accuracy of his works**: As noted by later figures like Sir Thomas Browne, Cardano was criticized for sometimes accepting information uncritically, blending rigorous observation with superstition and occult ideas.

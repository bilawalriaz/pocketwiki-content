# Rock (geology)

## Overview

A rock is any naturally occurring solid mass or aggregate of minerals or mineraloid matter, forming Earth's crust and most of its interior. Rocks are categorized into three main groups—igneous, sedimentary, and metamorphic—based on mineral composition, chemical makeup, and formation processes. The study of rocks, through petrology and mineralogy, underpins understanding of Earth's history, human prehistory, and technological development, while also extending to planetary geology.

## Body

### Classification and Composition

Rocks are primarily composed of mineral grains—crystalline solids with orderly atomic structures—or mineraloids, which lack crystallinity (e.g., volcanic glass). Silicate minerals, containing silica tetrahedra, dominate, comprising about 95% of Earth's crust and one-third of known mineral species. Classification depends on mineral and chemical composition, permeability, texture, and particle size, all reflecting the processes that formed the rock.

### Igneous Rocks

Igneous rocks form from the cooling and solidification of magma or lava, derived from partial melts of mantle or crustal material. Melting is driven by increased temperature, decreased pressure, or compositional change. Plutonic (intrusive) rocks cool slowly underground (e.g., granite); volcanic (extrusive) rocks form from surface lava or ejecta (e.g., basalt, pumice). Magma differentiation enriches silica content as it rises, making silica the primary classification criterion. Igneous rocks constitute about 65% of Earth's crust by volume.

### Sedimentary Rocks

Sedimentary rocks form at Earth's surface through accumulation, compaction, and cementation of rock fragments, minerals, or organic remains (diagenesis and lithification). Sediments originate from weathering of source rocks, then are transported by water, wind, ice, or glaciers before deposition. Grain sizes range from clay to boulders. These rocks make up about 7.9% of the crust, with shales (82%), limestone (6%), and sandstone/arkoses (12%) as primary types. They commonly preserve fossils and form layered (stratified) deposits.

### Metamorphic Rocks

Metamorphic rocks form when existing rocks are transformed by high pressure and temperature (greater than 150–200°C and 1500 bars) without melting. The original rock, or protolith, undergoes recrystallization into new mineral forms. Metamorphism occurs via contact (heat from magma intrusion), burial (pressure-dominated), or regional (combined heat and pressure, often in mountain-building zones). Metamorphic rocks compose 27.4% of the crust. They are classified as foliated (banded or layered, e.g., schist, gneiss) or non-foliated (e.g., marble, quartzite).

### Extraterrestrial Rocks

Rocks also constitute celestial bodies throughout the Solar System, including Mars, Venus, Mercury, moons, asteroids, and meteoroids. Meteorites falling to Earth provide direct evidence of extraterrestrial composition and are typically denser than terrestrial rocks. Lunar and Martian samples, along with asteroid material returned by missions like Hayabusa, expand understanding of planetary formation and composition.

### Human Use

Rocks have shaped human civilization for at least 2.5 million years, beginning with lithic (stone) tool technology. Mining extracts metals and minerals essential for advancement, with rates varying by regional resource availability. Soft sedimentary rocks were quarried for construction as early as 4000 BCE. Modern anthropic rocks include concrete (developed since Ancient Rome), artificial stone, and epoxy granite. Rock strength varies widely, from friable materials to quartzites exceeding 300 MPa tensile strength.

## Terms

- **Magma**: Molten or semi-molten rock beneath Earth's surface.
- **Lava**: Magma that reaches the surface.
- **Diagenesis**: Physical and chemical changes in sediments at low temperature and pressure, leading to lithification.
- **Lithification**: Process of turning loose sediment into solid sedimentary rock.
- **Protolith**: The original rock that undergoes metamorphism.
- **Foliation**: Layered or banded texture in metamorphic rocks caused by mineral alignment under pressure.
- **Mafic**: Rock or mineral rich in magnesium and iron, typically dark-colored and low in silica.
- **Felsic**: Rock or mineral rich in feldspar and silica, typically light-colored.
- **Magma differentiation**: Process by which rising magma becomes enriched in silica through crystallization and assimilation.
- **Anthropic rock**: Synthetic or human-restructured rock, such as concrete.

## Debates and Open Questions

The classification of anthropic rock as a potential fourth rock class, proposed by geologist James R. Underwood, remains debated among scholars. Additionally, the boundaries between rock types are not sharply defined; they pass through continuous gradations, making precise categorization sometimes subjective. The relative contributions of temperature versus pressure in different metamorphic environments also continue to be refined through ongoing research.

# São Francisco River

## Overview
The São Francisco River is Brazil's longest river that flows entirely within the country, stretching 2,914 km. Known as the "river of national integration," it connects Brazil's diverse regions, particularly the Southeast and Northeast, and is vital for the semi-arid Northeast, a region historically plagued by drought. Its course has been dramatically altered by hydroelectric dams, impacting navigation, ecology, and local culture.

## Timeline
- **4 October 1501** — Italian explorer Amerigo Vespucci first sees the river on Saint Francis of Assisi's feast day.
- **1865** — British explorer Richard Francis Burton explores the river from its source to Paulo Afonso falls.
- **1955** — Construction of the Paulo Afonso dam begins, harnessing the river's hydroelectric potential.
- **1961** — The Três Marias dam is built in Minas Gerais.
- **1977** — The massive Sobradinho dam and reservoir are completed in Bahia.
- **1988** — The Luiz Gonzaga (Itaparica) dam is built between Bahia and Pernambuco.
- **1994** — The Xingó dam is completed near Piranhas.
- **2005** — The Brazilian government proposes a controversial water diversion project to supply four northeastern states.

## Body

### Course and Geography
The river originates in the Canastra mountain range of Minas Gerais and flows generally north through Minas Gerais and Bahia. It then turns east, forming the border between Bahia and the states of Pernambuco and Alagoas, before emptying into the Atlantic Ocean between Alagoas and Sergipe. Its drainage basin covers over 630,000 km² and includes parts of seven states. The course is divided into four sections: high, upper middle, lower middle, and low.

### Navigation and Dams
The São Francisco is naturally navigable for 1,371 km between Pirapora (MG) and Petrolina/Juazeiro (BA/PE). Historically, passenger steamboats called *gaiolas* navigated this stretch. The construction of large dams, particularly Sobradinho in 1977, severely disrupted navigation. The reservoir's waves and altered river flow, combined with deforestation and agricultural water use upstream, created sandbanks that made *gaiola* travel impossible. As of 2009, only one boat, the *Benjamim Guimarães*, remained for short tourist cruises.

### Ecology and Biodiversity
The river basin hosts over 200 fish species, with about 64% being endemic (found nowhere else). Notable species include the large piranha *Pygocentrus piraya* and the aquarium fish *Hasemania nana*. Dams, which block fish migrations, and pollution are major threats, causing recorded mass fish deaths. The basin is also home to over 40 species of annual killifish.

### Human Use and Culture
Towns along the river, like Pirapora and Januária, are generally small and isolated due to the arid hinterland. Only the irrigated twin cities of Petrolina and Juazeiro have grown into prosperous urban centers. The river is central to regional folklore, celebrated in songs and legends. A key cultural symbol is the *carranca*, a gargoyle once placed on *gaiola* prows to ward off river demons. The dramatic canyon between the Paulo Afonso and Xingó dams is protected as the Rio São Francisco Natural Monument.

### Hydroelectric Power and Diversion Project
Hydroelectric development began in 1955 with the Paulo Afonso dam, which powers much of Northeastern Brazil. Four other major dams followed: Três Marias, Sobradinho, Luiz Gonzaga, and Xingó. The Sobradinho reservoir is one of the world's largest artificial lakes. A controversial diversion project proposed in 2005 aims to pump water from the river at Cabrobó to supply four semiarid states. Environmentalists argue it will cause ecological harm for limited benefit, while the government insists it is a necessary water supply.

## Terms
- **Velho Chico**: Popular nickname for the São Francisco River, meaning "Old Chico" (a diminutive of Francisco).
- **Gaiola**: A type of paddle-wheel steamboat historically used for passenger navigation on the river.
- **Carranca**: A gargoyle-like carving placed on the prow of *gaiolas* to scare away river demons.
- **Endemic**: A species found only in a specific geographic area, like the São Francisco basin.
- **Annual killifish**: Fish species with a life cycle adapted to seasonal drying of their habitat.
- **Hydroelectric dam**: A barrier built to generate electricity from the flow of water.
- **Diversion project**: An engineered system to move water from one area to another.

## Debates and Open Questions
The primary debate concerns the proposed water diversion project. Environmentalists contend it will damage the river's ecology and benefit only large landowners, while the government maintains it is essential for providing water to drought-prone regions. The long-term ecological impact of the existing dams on fish populations and river health remains a significant concern.

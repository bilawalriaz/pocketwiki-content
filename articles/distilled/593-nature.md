# Nature

## Overview
"Nature" refers broadly to the physical world and its laws, elements, and phenomena, including life. Historically, the concept evolved from the ancient Greek *physis* (intrinsic development) through pre-Socratic philosophy to modern scientific and ecological understandings. The term increasingly denotes the non-human, especially wilderness and geology, though debates persist about humanity's place within or apart from nature.

## Timeline
- **c. 6th century BCE** — Pre-Socratic philosophers use *physis* to describe intrinsic natural processes
- **4.54 billion years ago** — Earth forms from the solar nebula
- **c. 4 billion years ago** — Self-replicating molecules emerge
- **c. 750 million years ago** — Supercontinent Rodinia begins breaking apart
- **c. 540 million years ago** — Cambrian explosion of multicellular life
- **66 million years ago** — Cretaceous-Paleogene extinction kills non-avian dinosaurs
- **c. 9th millennium BCE** — Agriculture first adopted
- **1750–1850** — Industrial Revolution reshapes views of nature
- **1859** — Darwin publishes *On the Origin of Species*
- **1950s onward** — Holocene extinction accelerates due to human activity

## Body

### Etymology and Conceptual History
The word "nature" derives from Latin *natura*, meaning "birth" or "innate disposition," and was used to translate the Greek *physis*. Pre-Socratics like Heraclitus emphasized nature's dynamic, self-developing character. Over time, the concept expanded to encompass the entire physical universe. During the Scientific Revolution, nature became viewed as passive reality governed by divine laws. The Industrial Revolution reinforced a separation between human activity and the natural world, though Romantic thinkers like Rousseau idealized nature as sacred. Darwin's theory of evolution reintroduced a vitalist perspective, seeing nature as an active, evolving system.

### Earth and Its Systems
Earth, the third planet from the Sun and the largest terrestrial planet, supports life uniquely within the Solar System. Its surface—71% ocean-covered—is divided into migrating tectonic plates driven by mantle convection. The iron core generates a magnetic field via dynamo action. Life has significantly altered Earth’s atmosphere, creating an ozone layer and stabilizing climate through ecological cycles. Geological evolution involves rock deposition, intrusion, deformation, and metamorphism, shaped by convergent, divergent, and transform plate boundaries.

### Atmosphere, Climate, and Weather
Earth’s atmosphere, held by gravity, consists mainly of nitrogen and oxygen, with trace gases like carbon dioxide. The ozone layer shields life from harmful UV radiation. Weather, a chaotic system in the lower atmosphere, redistributes heat via convection and is influenced by seasonal axial tilt. Climate reflects long-term weather patterns, affected by ocean currents, albedo, greenhouse gases, solar output, and orbital changes. Today, global temperatures are rising, and regional climates are shifting rapidly.

### Water Systems
Water (H₂O) covers 71% of Earth’s surface, primarily in oceans (96.5% of surface water), with minor amounts in glaciers, groundwater, and the atmosphere. The World Ocean is a continuous, interconnected saltwater body divided into five major oceans. Lakes form in basins, fed by rivers or glaciers; ponds are smaller, shallower bodies. Rivers flow toward oceans or lakes, transporting sediment and supporting the hydrological cycle. Streams serve as ecological corridors vital for biodiversity conservation.

### Ecosystems and Wilderness
Ecosystems integrate biotic (living) and abiotic (non-living) components—soil, air, water, sunlight—into energy-flowing, material-cycling systems. Eugene Odum defined ecosystems as units where organisms interact with their physical environment. Microecosystems (e.g., a stone and its undergrowth) scale up to macroecosystems (e.g., ecoregions). Wilderness areas, minimally altered by humans, are crucial for species survival, ecological research, and cultural values. They persist in parks, forests, and urban green spaces.

### Life and Evolution
Life is characterized by organization, metabolism, growth, adaptation, reproduction, and response to stimuli. All known organisms possess DNA or RNA genomes. The earliest life appeared at least 3.5 billion years ago as unicellular forms. Photosynthesis oxygenated the atmosphere, enabling complex eukaryotic cells and eventually multicellular organisms. Five mass extinctions shaped biodiversity; the last, 66 million years ago, ended the age of dinosaurs. Humans evolved from African apes, developing agriculture and civilization, which now drive the Holocene extinction—the fastest mass extinction on record.

### Human Interrelationship
Despite comprising a tiny fraction of Earth’s biomass, humans exert disproportionate environmental impact. A 2020 study found human-made materials exceed all living biomass. Only 3% of land remains ecologically intact. Human activities—including deforestation, pollution, and resource extraction—threaten over 1 million species. Economic systems undervalue natural resources, leading to $4–6 trillion annually in harmful subsidies. Technology enables both exploitation and hazard mitigation, but feedback loops link civilization’s fate to environmental health.

### Aesthetics and Beauty
Nature’s beauty has inspired art across cultures. Chinese landscape painting (Tang Dynasty) and Western Romanticism (Constable, Turner, Wordsworth) celebrated natural scenery. Philosophers explore aesthetics through *mimesis* (imitation) and mathematical patterns in nature. Transcendentalists like Emerson linked nature to spiritual renewal. Beauty remains central to both scientific inquiry and artistic expression.

### Matter, Energy, and the Cosmos
Matter (mass-bearing substance) and energy (capacity to do work) exhibit wave-particle duality at quantum scales. The observable universe comprises 4.9% ordinary matter, 26.8% dark matter, and 68.3% dark energy. Physical laws govern cosmic evolution, expressed through constants whose origins remain mysterious. The anthropic principle suggests these constants permit intelligent life.

### Beyond Earth
Outer space, a near-vacuum of hydrogen-helium plasma, contains cosmic microwave background radiation, magnetic fields, and organic molecules. Structures form hierarchically: stars in galaxies, galaxies in clusters, clusters in superclusters, linked by filaments surrounding voids. Mars may have hosted life historically; subsurface oceans on moons like Europa could harbor it. Exoplanets in habitable zones offer potential for life, though requirements remain uncertain.

## Terms
- **Physis**: Greek term for intrinsic natural development, translated as *natura* in Latin.
- **Tectonic plates**: Crustal and upper mantle slabs whose movement drives earthquakes and volcanism.
- **Dynamo action**: Process generating magnetic fields via convective motion in conductive fluids.
- **Ozone layer**: Atmospheric region absorbing UV radiation, protecting surface life.
- **Thermohaline circulation**: Density-driven ocean currents redistributing global heat.
- **Ecosystem**: Interacting community of organisms and their physical environment.
- **Holocene extinction**: Ongoing sixth mass extinction driven by human activity.
- **Anthropogenic**: Caused by human beings.
- **Mimesis**: Imitation of nature in art and literature.
- **Cosmological principle**: Assumption that the universe is homogeneous and isotropic at large scales.

## Debates and Open Questions
- Whether "nature" should include or exclude human activity remains contested, especially given pervasive anthropogenic influence.
- The extent and trajectory of the Holocene extinction are actively researched, with estimates of species loss varying widely.
- The origin of life and earliest self-replicating molecules are poorly understood.
- The role of dark matter and dark energy in cosmic evolution is unknown.
- Whether life exists beyond Earth, particularly on Mars or icy moons, is unresolved.
- The anthropic principle and fine-tuning of physical constants spark debate about the universe’s apparent design for life.

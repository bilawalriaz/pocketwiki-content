# Sea of Azov

## Overview
The Sea of Azov is a shallow, inland shelf sea in Eastern Europe connected to the Black Sea by the Strait of Kerch. It is the world's shallowest sea, with a maximum depth of 14 metres, and is characterized by low salinity and high biological productivity due to major river inflows. Its strategic location has made it a site of historical conflict and a vital waterway, but its ecology is now threatened by pollution and overfishing.

## Timeline
- **c. 5600 BC** — Theory of a massive flood through the Bosporus, creating the link between the Black and Mediterranean Seas.
- **7th century BC** — Milesian Greek colonization begins in the region.
- **1695–1696** — Russian Azov campaigns under Peter I capture the Turkish fortress of Azov.
- **1711** — Azov is returned to Turkey by the Treaty of the Pruth.
- **1737** — Russia recaptures Azov during the Russo-Austrian-Turkish War.
- **1855** — Allied British and French forces capture Kerch and besiege Taganrog during the Crimean War.
- **1952** — Volga–Don Canal opens, connecting the sea to the Volga River.
- **2003** — Ukraine and Russia agree to treat the sea and Kerch Strait as shared internal waters.
- **2018** — Crimean Bridge opens; Kerch Strait incident sees Russia seize Ukrainian vessels.
- **2022** — Russian invasion of Ukraine leads to full capture of Mariupol and control of the northern coast.

## Body

### Physical Characteristics
The Sea of Azov is 360 km long and 180 km wide, with an area of 39,000 km². It is the shallowest sea in the world, with an average depth of 7 metres and a maximum of 14 metres. The bottom is flat, with depth increasing gradually toward the centre. Major rivers, primarily the Don and Kuban, provide over 90% of water inflow, bringing sand and silt that form numerous spits and bays. This inflow results in low salinity (10–12 PSS in the open sea) and high nutrient levels, which support abundant plankton and fish life. The sea's depth is decreasing due to sediment deposits.

### Names and History
In antiquity, the sea was known as the Maeotis Swamp or Lake Maeotis. The modern name likely derives from a Kipchak Turkish word for "lowlands." The sea was a significant geographical feature for ancient peoples like the Maeotians and was part of the Bosporan Kingdom. Its strategic importance led to repeated conflicts, notably between Russia and the Ottoman Empire in the 17th–18th centuries and during the Crimean War. In the 21st century, tensions between Russia and Ukraine have escalated, culminating in Russia's 2022 invasion and full control of the Ukrainian coastline.

### Ecology and Economy
The sea's high biological productivity, historically yielding annual fish catches of 300,000 tonnes, made it a major fishing ground. However, overfishing, pollution, and reduced river flow have caused declines, including the collapse of anchovy fisheries. The ecosystem features diverse plankton, benthos, and fish species, though some, like the Azov dolphin, are now rare. The economy relies on shipping, fishing, and coastal resorts. The Volga–Don Canal is a key transport route. Pollution incidents, such as the 2007 oil spill in the Kerch Strait, pose significant ecological risks.

## Terms
- **Shelf sea**: A sea over a continental shelf, relatively shallow compared to the open ocean.
- **Salinity**: The salt concentration in water, measured on the Practical Salinity Scale (PSS).
- **Limans**: Lagoons or estuaries formed at the mouths of rivers.
- **Spits**: Narrow sandbanks formed by sediment deposition, extending from the shore.
- **Eutrophication**: Excessive nutrient enrichment in water, leading to algal blooms and oxygen depletion.
- **Anoxic**: Lacking dissolved oxygen, often in bottom waters due to decomposition of organic matter.
- **Volga–Don Canal**: A canal linking the Volga and Don rivers, connecting the Sea of Azov to the Caspian Sea.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The theory of a catastrophic flood through the Bosporus around 5600 BC, which may have inspired flood myths, remains debated among archaeologists and geologists.
- The exact origin of the name "Maeotis" is unclear—whether it was named for the Maeotians or vice versa.
- The impact of the Crimean Bridge on shipping and the ecological consequences of Russia's control over the Kerch Strait are ongoing geopolitical and environmental concerns.

# Mohammad Zahir Shah

## Overview
Mohammad Zahir Shah (1914–2007) was the last King of Afghanistan, reigning from 1933 until his deposition in 1973. His nearly 40-year rule, the longest since the 18th century, was marked by a period of relative peace, modernization efforts, and the establishment of a constitutional monarchy. His overthrow ended over two centuries of continuous monarchical rule, and his later symbolic return as "Father of the Nation" highlighted his enduring role as a unifying figure.

## Timeline
- **15 October 1914** — Born in Kabul, Afghanistan.
- **8 November 1933** — Proclaimed King at age 19 after his father's assassination.
- **1934** — Afghanistan joins the League of Nations and receives U.S. recognition.
- **1944–1947** — A series of tribal revolts occur within Afghanistan.
- **1964** — A new constitution is promulgated, establishing a constitutional monarchy with a parliament and civil rights.
- **17 July 1973** — Deposed in a coup d'état by his cousin, Sardar Mohammad Daoud Khan, while in Italy.
- **August 1973** — Sends a letter from Rome formally abdicating.
- **18 April 2002** — Returns to Afghanistan after 29 years in exile following the fall of the Taliban.
- **2002** — Given the title "Father of the Nation" by President Hamid Karzai.
- **23 July 2007** — Dies in Kabul at age 92.

## Body

### Early Life and Accession
Born into the royal Musahiban family, Zahir Khan received an education in Kabul and France, studying at institutions like the Pasteur Institute and the University of Montpellier. He returned to help his father, Mohammad Nadir Shah, restore order. After his father was assassinated in 1933, the 19-year-old prince was proclaimed King.

### Reign and Modernization
For his first two decades, Zahir Shah ceded effective power to his uncles, who served as Prime Ministers. This period saw Afghanistan expand its international relations, joining the League of Nations and maintaining neutrality during World War II. After the war, he initiated modernization, recruiting foreign advisors and founding the country's first modern university. He sought aid from both the United States and the Soviet Union, maintaining a non-aligned stance. A pivotal moment came in 1964 with a new constitution that transformed Afghanistan into a constitutional monarchy, introducing free elections, a parliament, and civil rights, including for women. His reign was characterized by relative leniency and peace.

### Deposition and Exile
In 1973, while Zahir Shah was abroad for medical treatment, his cousin and former Prime Minister, Sardar Mohammad Daoud Khan, staged a coup. Daoud Khan, who had been forced to resign a decade earlier, established a republic. Zahir Shah formally abdicated from exile in Rome, where he lived for 29 years. His financial support initially came from the deposed Afghan government and later from the Shah of Iran. During the Soviet-Afghan War, various factions, including the Soviet Union and the United States, explored the possibility of his return to lead a neutral government, but these efforts failed due to a lack of consensus among Afghan factions and opposition from Pakistan's ISI.

### Return and Final Years
Following the fall of the Taliban in 2001, Zahir Shah returned to Afghanistan in April 2002 to widespread acclaim. At the 2002 Loya Jirga (a grand assembly), he was prepared to become head of state but publicly renounced the monarchy at the behest of the United States, which feared alienating Pakistan. He was instead given the honorary title "Father of the Nation" by President Hamid Karzai. He spent his final years in Kabul, frail but symbolically important, until his death in 2007.

## Terms
*   **Constitutional Monarchy:** A system of government where a monarch acts as head of state within the parameters of a constitution, with power often shared with a parliament.
*   **Loya Jirga:** A traditional Afghan grand assembly of leaders and elders convened to decide on important matters of national policy.
*   **Musahiban:** The royal family line from which Zahir Shah descended, holding the title "Companions of the King."
*   **Durand Line:** The 19th-century border between Afghanistan and British India (now Pakistan), a perennial source of diplomatic tension.
*   **Coup d'état:** A sudden, illegal seizure of power from a government.
*   **Non-alignment:** A foreign policy of remaining independent from the major power blocs during the Cold War.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   The primary debate surrounding Zahir Shah's return in 2002 was whether he should be restored as monarch. The U.S. and Pakistan opposed this, fearing it would destabilize the region, particularly regarding the Durand Line issue, while many Afghan delegates favored it.
*   The success and legacy of his 1964 constitutional reforms are debated, as they were ultimately undermined by factionalism and political infighting, leading to the coup that ended his reign.

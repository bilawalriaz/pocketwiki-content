# Cape Verde

## Overview
Cape Verde (officially Cabo Verde) is an archipelagic nation in the eastern Atlantic Ocean, off the coast of West Africa. Comprising ten volcanic islands, it was uninhabited until settled by the Portuguese in the 15th century. Its strategic location made it a key hub in the transatlantic slave trade. After independence in 1975, it has developed into a stable, service-based economy and is recognized as one of Africa's most democratic and developed countries, despite having few natural resources.

## Timeline
- **c. 1456** — Archipelago discovered by Genoese and Portuguese navigators.
- **1462** — Portuguese settlers found Ribeira Grande (now Cidade Velha) on Santiago, the first permanent European tropical settlement.
- **16th–17th C.** — Cape Verde prospers as a major center for the Atlantic slave trade.
- **1770** — Capital moves from Ribeira Grande to Praia.
- **1956** — Amílcar Cabral co-founds the PAIGC independence movement in Portuguese Guinea.
- **1975** — Cape Verde gains independence from Portugal on July 5.
- **1991** — Government begins pursuing market-oriented economic policies.
- **2007** — Cape Verde graduates from Least Developed Country to Middle Income Country status.
- **2013** — Country officially requests use of "Cabo Verde" for international purposes.
- **2024** — Cape Verde becomes the third African country to be free of malaria.

## Body

### History and Independence
The volcanic islands were formed 40–50 million years ago and were uninhabited until Portuguese settlement began in 1462. The archipelago's economy initially thrived on the slave trade but declined in the 19th century due to the trade's abolition and drought. It later recovered as a commercial and shipping hub. A nationalist movement, the African Party for the Independence of Guinea and Cape Verde (PAIGC), was founded in 1956. After an armed struggle in Portuguese Guinea, Cape Verde achieved independence in 1975 following a transitional agreement with Portugal.

### Government and Politics
Cape Verde is a semi-presidential representative democratic republic. The president is head of state, and the prime minister is head of government. The constitution, adopted in 1980 and revised in 1992, 1995, and 1999, defines the state's principles. The country is noted for its political stability and is ranked among Africa's most democratic nations. Its foreign policy is non-aligned, and it is a founding member of the Community of Portuguese Language Countries.

### Geography and Climate
The archipelago consists of ten islands divided into the Barlavento (windward) and Sotavento (leeward) groups. The landscape is predominantly volcanic and arid, with less than 2% of land being arable. The climate is moderated by the ocean but remains semi-arid, with most rainfall occurring between August and October. The islands are a known origin point for intense "Cape Verde-type" hurricanes. The country is highly vulnerable to climate change and flooding.

### Economy
Lacking natural resources, Cape Verde's economy is service-oriented, with commerce, transport, and tourism accounting for over 70% of GDP. Agriculture and fishing contribute only about 9%. The country has pursued market-oriented policies and privatization since 1991, attracting significant foreign investment, particularly in tourism. Its currency is linked to the euro. Remittances from a large global diaspora contribute an estimated 20% to GDP.

### Demographics and Society
The population is around 530,000, with a large diaspora (nearly one million) exceeding the resident population. The people are primarily of mixed African and European descent. The official language is Portuguese, but Cape Verdean Creole (Crioulo) is the mother tongue of virtually all inhabitants. The majority of the population is Christian, predominantly Roman Catholic. The country has a high literacy rate and one of Africa's best educational systems.

### Culture
Cape Verdean culture is a blend of African and European influences. The national music is the melancholic *morna*, famously performed by Cesária Évora. Other key genres include *coladeira*, *funaná*, and *batuque*. The cuisine is based on fish, corn, and beans, with *cachupa* as a signature dish. Football is the most popular sport, and the national team qualified for its first FIFA World Cup in 2026.

## Terms
- **Archipelago:** A group of islands.
- **Macaronesia:** A collection of five volcanic island groups in the North Atlantic, including Cape Verde.
- **PAIGC:** African Party for the Independence of Guinea and Cape Verde, the liberation movement that led the independence struggle.
- **Semi-presidential republic:** A system of government with both a president (head of state) and a prime minister (head of government).
- **Cape Verdean Creole (Crioulo):** A Portuguese-based creole language and the mother tongue of most Cape Verdeans.
- **Morna:** A traditional Cape Verdean music genre characterized by melancholic and lyrical songs.
- **Cachupa:** A traditional slow-cooked stew of corn, beans, and fish or meat.
- **Debt-for-nature swap:** An agreement where a portion of a developing nation's debt is forgiven in exchange for local investment in environmental projects.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Language Standardization:** There is an ongoing debate about standardizing Cape Verdean Creole, with proposals for two standards: a northern (Barlavento) standard based on São Vicente Creole and a southern (Sotavento) standard based on Santiago Creole.
- **Renewable Energy Goals:** Cape Verde initially aimed to be 100% reliant on renewable energy by 2025 but has pushed this target back to 2050, with an interim goal of 50% by 2030.
- **Diaspora Size:** The exact size of the Cape Verdean diaspora is debated, as historical immigration under Portuguese passports makes official statistics potentially inaccurate.

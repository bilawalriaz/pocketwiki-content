# Saint Kitts and Nevis

## Overview
Saint Kitts and Nevis is a twin-island federation in the Caribbean and the smallest sovereign state in the Western Hemisphere by both area and population. It is a Commonwealth realm with a parliamentary democracy, historically significant as the site of the first British and French colonies in the West Indies. Its modern economy has transitioned from a sugar monoculture to one based on tourism and financial services, including a pioneering citizenship-by-investment program.

## Body

### Pre-colonial and Colonial History
The islands were inhabited by successive indigenous groups, culminating with the Kalinago (Island Caribs) who named Saint Kitts "Liamuiga" (fertile land). Christopher Columbus sighted them in 1493. The first permanent European settlements were English (1623) and French (1625) on Saint Kitts, which was partitioned between them. After a joint massacre of the Kalinago in 1626, the colonists established slave-based sugar plantations. A Spanish expedition destroyed the colonies in 1629, but they were re-established by 1630. Britain and France fought for control over the islands for a century, with Britain gaining sole possession via the Treaty of Utrecht in 1713. Saint Kitts became the richest British Crown Colony per capita in the Caribbean by the late 18th century.

### Path to Independence
The colony was federated with Nevis and Anguilla in 1882. The 20th century saw the rise of a labor movement, culminating in the 1935 sugar workers' strike and the founding of the Labour Party in the 1940s. After a brief period in the West Indies Federation (1958–62), the islands became an associated state with full internal autonomy in 1967. Anguilla seceded that year, formalized in 1980. To secure Nevis's participation in independence, a constitution was agreed upon granting Nevis a degree of autonomy and the right to secede via a two-thirds majority referendum. Full independence from the United Kingdom was achieved on 19 September 1983.

### Government and Economy
Saint Kitts and Nevis is a federal parliamentary democracy and constitutional monarchy, with the British monarch as head of state represented by a Governor-General. The unicameral National Assembly has 14 members. Nevis maintains its own semi-autonomous assembly. The economy, once dominated by sugar, closed its last sugar company in 2005. It is now driven by tourism, light manufacturing, and offshore banking. The country operates the world's oldest economic citizenship program, established in 1984, which grants citizenship in exchange for qualifying investments in real estate or a government fund.

### Geography and Demographics
The two volcanic islands are separated by a shallow channel called The Narrows. The capital, Basseterre, is on Saint Kitts, which is larger and more populous. The population is approximately 53,000, predominantly Afro-Caribbean, with English as the official language and Saint Kitts Creole widely spoken. Christianity is the dominant religion. High emigration has kept the population relatively stable for decades.

## Terms
- **Commonwealth realm:** A sovereign state where the British monarch is the head of state, represented locally by a Governor-General.
- **Associated state:** A former constitutional arrangement where the UK handled defense and foreign affairs while the islands had full internal self-government.
- **Kalinago:** The indigenous Carib people who inhabited the islands before European colonization.
- **Citizenship-by-investment:** A program allowing foreigners to obtain citizenship through a qualifying financial contribution to the country.
- **The Narrows:** The shallow channel separating the islands of Saint Kitts and Nevis.
- **Liamuiga:** The Kalinago name for Saint Kitts, meaning "fertile land."
- **Saint Kitts Creole:** An English-based creole language widely spoken alongside official English.
- **CARICOM:** The Caribbean Community, a political and economic union of Caribbean nations.

## Debates and open questions
- The exact naming of the islands by Columbus is debated; new studies suggest he may have named Saint Kitts "Sant Yago" and the name "San Cristóbal" was applied to Saba.
- A 1998 referendum in Nevis on secession received 62% support but failed to meet the constitutionally required two-thirds majority, leaving the question of Nevisian independence unresolved.
- The Citizenship-by-Investment program has faced criticism from researchers over risks of corruption, money laundering, and tax evasion.

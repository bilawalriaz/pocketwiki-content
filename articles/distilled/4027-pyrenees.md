# Pyrenees

## Overview
The Pyrenees is a major mountain range in southwestern Europe that forms a natural border between France and Spain, with the microstate of Andorra nestled within it. Stretching nearly 500 km from the Cantabrian Mountains to the Mediterranean, it is geologically older than the Alps and is characterized by its high, rugged peaks, deep valleys, and a distinct lack of low passes, which has historically isolated communities on either side.

## Timeline
- **Paleozoic & Mesozoic Eras** — Sediments that form the Pyrenees are deposited in coastal basins.
- **Ediacaran to Ordovician** — The region is located at the northwest margin of the supercontinent Gondwana.
- **100-150 million years ago** — The opening of the Bay of Biscay pushes Spain against France, beginning the mountain-building process.
- **Eocene Epoch** — The intense uplifting of the Earth's crust culminates, forming the main chain.
- **Late Miocene** — The high-altitude peneplain (low-relief surface) in the upper Pyrenees forms.
- **c. 1878** — Construction begins on the Pic du Midi Observatory.
- **1962-1968** — The Odeillo solar furnace, the world's largest, is built.
- **1996** — Three brown bears are reintroduced from Slovenia to aid the near-extinct native population.
- **1997** — Part of the Pyrenees, including Ordesa y Monte Perdido and Pyrénées National Parks, is inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
- **January 2000** — The Pyrenean ibex subspecies becomes extinct.

## Body

### Etymology and Geology
The name "Pyrenees" likely derives from Greek mythology, referring to a princess named Pyrene, or from the Greek word for fire, *pŷr*, possibly due to ancient wildfires. Geologically, the range is older than the Alps. Its sediments were deposited during the Paleozoic and Mesozoic eras. The mountains were formed between 100 and 150 million years ago when the opening of the Bay of Biscay pushed the Iberian Peninsula against the European mainland, subjecting sedimentary rock layers to intense pressure. This uplift began in the east and progressed westward, culminating in the Eocene Epoch. The range is composed largely of erosion-resistant granite, giving it a massive, unworn character.

### Landscape and Climate
The Pyrenean landscape is marked by the absence of large lakes, the rarity of low passes, and numerous mountain torrents (*gaves*) that form high waterfalls. Semicircular cliff formations called *cirques* are common. The climate varies significantly: the western Pyrenees receive much more precipitation from the Atlantic Ocean than the drier eastern Pyrenees. This results in glaciers existing only in the snowy central and western sections, confined to the northern slopes near the crest. The annual snowline ranges from about 2,700 to 2,800 meters.

### Flora, Fauna, and Protected Areas
Vegetation changes from wooded in the wet west to wild and barren in the granitic east, with flora shifting from central European to Mediterranean character. The range is rich in endemic species, including the Pyrenean desman (a mammal) and the Pyrenean brook salamander. The Pyrenean ibex became extinct in 2000, while the native brown bear was nearly lost but has been supplemented by reintroduction. Key protected areas include Ordesa y Monte Perdido National Park (Spain) and Pyrénées National Park (France).

### Demographics, Culture, and Economy
The region has a varied ethnology, with principal languages including Spanish, French, Catalan, Aragonese, Basque, and Occitan. A traditional rural practice is *transhumance*, the seasonal migration of livestock and families to high mountain pastures for the summer. Historically, the mountains contained iron mines and marble quarries, some opened by Romans. Today, notable economic features include abundant mineral springs (hot and cold) and the Odeillo solar furnace, a major research site. The largest nearby cities are Toulouse, France, and Pamplona, Spain.

### Recreation and Infrastructure
The Pyrenees are a major destination for winter sports and summer high-elevation training. They feature prominently in the Tour de France and Vuelta a España cycling races. Long-distance footpaths like the GR 10 and GR 11 traverse the range. Major roads and railways are limited to the lowlands at the eastern and western ends, with tunnels built under key passes. The Pic du Midi Observatory, operational since the early 20th century, was used for lunar photography for the Apollo missions and is now open to amateur astronomy.

## Terms
- **Peneplain:** A low-relief plain formed by prolonged erosion, representing the final stage of the cycle of erosion.
- **Cirque:** A semicircular, steep-walled mountain basin formed by glacial erosion.
- **Gave:** A local term for a mountain torrent or stream in the Pyrenees.
- **Transhumance:** The seasonal movement of livestock between fixed summer and winter pastures.
- **Endemic:** A species or genus native and restricted to a certain place.
- **Heliostat:** A device that includes a mirror which turns so as to keep reflecting sunlight toward a predetermined target.
- **Physiographic province:** A region with a distinct geological structure and history.

# Vallabhbhai Patel

## Overview
Vallabhbhai Patel (1875–1950), known as Sardar ("Chief") and the "Iron Man of India," was a foundational leader of the Indian independence movement and the first Deputy Prime Minister and Home Minister of independent India. A key lieutenant of Mahatma Gandhi, he was instrumental in organizing peasant satyagrahas (non-violent resistance) and later played the decisive role in the political integration of over 565 princely states into the Indian union, a feat often compared to Otto von Bismarck's unification of Germany.

## Timeline
- **31 October 1875** — Born in Nadiad, Gujarat.
- **1917** — Meets Mahatma Gandhi; becomes secretary of the Gujarat Sabha.
- **1920–21** — Leads the Kheda and Bardoli satyagrahas, earning the title "Sardar."
- **1931** — Elected President of the Indian National Congress; chairs the Karachi session.
- **1942** — Delivers a key speech launching the Quit India Movement; imprisoned until 1945.
- **15 August 1947** — Becomes India's first Deputy Prime Minister and Home Minister.
- **1947–49** — Leads the integration of princely states, including actions in Junagadh and Hyderabad.
- **15 December 1950** — Dies in Bombay (Mumbai).

## Body

### Early Life and Legal Career
Born into a Patidar family in Gujarat, Patel was a successful barrister who studied law in England. He was a skilled lawyer and a fierce advocate, known for his stoic character. His early life was marked by personal tragedy, including the death of his wife Jhaverba in 1909, after which he did not remarry.

### Role in the Independence Movement
Patel became one of Gandhi's earliest and most influential political organizers. He led major non-violent tax resistance campaigns in the Kheda (1918) and Bardoli (1928) regions of Gujarat, mobilizing peasants against British rule. As a senior Congress leader, he was the party's chief fundraiser and strategist for elections in the 1930s. He was a fervent supporter of the Quit India Movement in 1942, delivering a pivotal speech that historians credit with galvanizing nationalist support for the rebellion.

### Partition and Integration of Princely States
As Home Minister after independence in 1947, Patel accepted the partition of India as a practical necessity to avoid civil war. He oversaw the division of public assets and managed the catastrophic refugee crisis, organizing relief and working to restore peace. His most celebrated achievement was the political integration of the princely states. Using a combination of diplomacy, persuasion, and the threat of force, he persuaded almost all 565 states to accede to India. He dealt firmly with holdouts like Junagadh (whose Muslim ruler acceded to Pakistan despite a Hindu majority) and Hyderabad (whose Nizam sought independence), using military action to secure their integration.

### Governance and Legacy
Patel was a key architect of the modern Indian state, playing a major role in shaping the Constitution. He is regarded as the "Father of All India Services" for establishing the administrative "steel frame" of the nation. He clashed with Prime Minister Nehru on several issues, including Kashmir policy and the integration of Hyderabad, but their partnership was crucial to India's early stability. He died in 1950, shortly after suffering a major heart attack.

## Terms
- **Satyagraha**: A form of non-violent civil resistance or civil disobedience, a concept pioneered by Mahatma Gandhi.
- **Sardar**: A title meaning "chief" or "leader," conferred on Patel by the women of Bardoli in 1928.
- **Princely States**: Semi-autonomous regions under British suzerainty, ruled by hereditary monarchs, which had to choose between joining India or Pakistan at independence.
- **Instrument of Accession**: The legal document by which a princely state acceded to the Dominion of India.
- **Quit India Movement**: A mass civil disobedience movement launched by Gandhi in August 1942 demanding an end to British rule.
- **All India Services**: Central civil services that serve both the Union and State governments, established on Patel's initiative.
- **Khadi**: Hand-spun and hand-woven cloth, promoted as a symbol of self-reliance and resistance to British goods.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Patel's Role in Partition**: While he accepted partition as a pragmatic solution, he was criticized by Gandhi, Nehru, and secular Muslims for being overly eager to divide Punjab and Bengal.
- **Relationship with Nehru**: Their partnership was essential but marked by significant policy clashes, particularly over Kashmir, Hyderabad, and the release of funds to Pakistan.
- **Integration Methods**: His use of force in Hyderabad and firm diplomacy with other states is celebrated as decisive but also analyzed as a critical moment where the new state asserted its authority.

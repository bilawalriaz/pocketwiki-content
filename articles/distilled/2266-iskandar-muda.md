# Iskandar Muda

## Overview
Iskandar Muda (r. 1607–1636) was the twelfth Sultan of Aceh, under whom the sultanate reached its greatest territorial extent and became the dominant power in the western Indonesian archipelago and the Strait of Malacca. His reign is defined by extensive military conquests, centralized administration, and Aceh's emergence as a major center of Islamic learning and trade, though it was also marked by brutality and the ultimate failure to permanently seize the Portuguese stronghold of Malacca.

## Timeline
- **c. 1593** — Born to parents from the two main branches of the Acehnese royal dynasty.
- **1606** — Imprisoned after a failed rebellion, but released to fight a Portuguese invasion, gaining esteem.
- **4 April 1607** — Secures the throne upon the death of his uncle, Sultan Ali Ri'ayat Syah III.
- **1612** — Conquers Deli in northern Sumatra.
- **1613** — Conquers Aru and Johor, though Johor later expels the Acehnese garrison.
- **1614** — Defeats a Portuguese fleet at Bintan.
- **1617** — Conquers Pahang on the Malay Peninsula.
- **1619** — Conquers Kedah, bringing its population to Aceh.
- **1620** — Captures Perak, taking 5,000 people to Aceh.
- **1624/5** — Takes the island of Nias.
- **1629** — Launches a massive, devastatingly failed attack on Portuguese Malacca.
- **1636** — Dies; succeeded by his son-in-law, Iskandar Thani, ending the founding dynasty.

## Body

### Military Conquests and Foreign Policy
Iskandar Muda's power was built on a formidable military, including a navy of heavy galleys, an elephant corps, and over 2,000 cannons. He systematically expanded Aceh's control over northern Sumatra and the Malay Peninsula, conquering Deli, Aru, Johor, Pahang, Kedah, Perak, and Nias. These campaigns were driven by the desire to control the lucrative spice trade, particularly pepper, and to challenge Portuguese dominance. His most significant military failure was the 1629 siege of Malacca, where Portuguese sources claim his entire fleet and 19,000 men were destroyed. After this, his expeditions were limited to suppressing revolts in Pahang. While he maintained control over northern Sumatra, he never achieved permanent supremacy over the Strait of Malacca or conquered the pepper-rich regions of Inderapura and Lampung, which were under the rival Sultanate of Banten.

### Centralized Rule and Administration
To fund his campaigns and maintain control, Iskandar Muda implemented a highly centralized administration. He monopolized foreign trade, making the court the sole point of contact for foreigners and suppressing the independent power of the local elite, the *orang kaya* ("powerful men"). He created a new nobility of "war leaders" (*hulubalang* or *uleëbalang*) granted feudal tenure over districts (*mukim*). He also established a network of courts based on Islamic jurisprudence, which became a model for other Indonesian states. His rule was characterized by extreme brutality; he executed disobedient subjects, including his own son, and confiscated their wealth. A French visitor in the 1620s documented daily mutilations for minor offenses.

### Economy and Culture
The economic foundation of the sultanate was the spice trade, especially pepper, cloves, and nutmegs. Iskandar Muda encouraged trade through low interest rates and the use of small gold coins (*mas*). However, the hinterland often failed to produce enough surplus food, leading him to use prisoners-of-war as agricultural slaves. Culturally, his reign saw Aceh become a renowned center of Islamic scholarship, attracting Sufi mystics like Hamzah Pansuri and Syamsuddin of Pasai. The national chronicle, the *Hikayat Aceh*, was likely composed during his rule, praising him and linking his lineage to Alexander the Great (*Iskandar Zulkarnain*) to elevate Aceh's status within the Malay world.

## Terms
- **Orang kaya**: "Powerful men"; the traditional Acehnese elite whose independence Iskandar Muda sought to suppress.
- **Hulubalang / Uleëbalang**: "War leaders"; a new class of nobility created by Iskandar Muda, granted feudal control over districts (*mukim*).
- **Mukim**: A district or territory granted in feudal tenure to a *hulubalang*.
- **Panglima**: Royal officials Iskandar Muda appointed to replace hereditary princes, subject to annual reporting and appraisal.
- **Mas**: Small gold coins used as currency during his reign to stimulate the economy.
- **Hikayat Aceh**: A national chronicle likely written during his reign, which praises him and traces his lineage to Alexander the Great.
- **Sufi mystics**: Practitioners of Sufism, a mystical branch of Islam; scholars like Hamzah Pansuri were favored at his court.

## Debates and Open questions
The exact date of Iskandar Muda's birth is uncertain, given as c. 1583 or c. 1593 in the source. The precise date of composition for the *Hikayat Aceh* is also debated, with some scholars placing it later than his reign. The source notes that his posthumous name, "Iskandar Muda," is sometimes mistakenly thought to be a later addition, but it appears on his coins.

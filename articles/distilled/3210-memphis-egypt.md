# Memphis, Egypt

## Overview
Memphis was the ancient capital of Egypt during the Early Dynastic Period and Old Kingdom, serving as a political, religious, and commercial hub for millennia. Its strategic location at the Nile Delta's apex made it a vital center for trade and administration, and its great Temple of Ptah was one of the most important religious sites in ancient Egypt. The city's decline began with the rise of Alexandria and was completed by the founding of nearby Cairo, leaving behind ruins that are now a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

## Timeline
- **c. 31st century BC** — Legendary founding by King Menes, who united Upper and Lower Egypt.
- **Early Dynastic Period & Old Kingdom** — Serves as the capital of ancient Egypt for over six consecutive dynasties.
- **c. 2055–1640 BCE** — Known as Ankh-Tawy ("Life of the Two Lands") during the Middle Kingdom.
- **c. 1550 BC** — Becomes known as Men-nefer ("enduring and beautiful") at the start of the New Kingdom.
- **671 BC** — Sacked by the Assyrian king Esarhaddon.
- **525 BC** — Conquered by the Persian king Cambyses II; becomes the administrative capital of the Persian satrapy.
- **332 BC** — Alexander the Great is crowned king in the Temple of Ptah.
- **216 & 196 BC** — Decrees of Memphis issued; the Rosetta Stone (196 BC) is created here.
- **641 AD** — Largely abandoned after the Arab conquest and the founding of Fustat (Cairo).
- **1979** — Memphis and its necropolis are inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

## Body

### Names and Location
Memphis had several names over its history. Its original Egyptian name was Inebu-hedj ("the white walls"). It was later called Ankh-Tawy ("Life of the Two Lands") in the Middle Kingdom, stressing its position between Upper and Lower Egypt. At the start of the New Kingdom, it became Men-nefer, which evolved into the Greek "Memphis." The modern village of Mit Rahina lies within its administrative borders, about 20 km south of Cairo on the west bank of the Nile.

### Early History and Golden Age
According to the historian Manetho, Memphis was founded by King Menes, who united the Two Lands. It was the state capital from the First Dynasty. The city reached its peak under the Sixth Dynasty as the center for the worship of the creator god Ptah. Its great temple, Hut-ka-Ptah ("Enclosure of the ka of Ptah"), is the likely origin of the name "Egypt." Memphis thrived as a regional center for commerce, trade, and religion, with its port, Peru-nefer, distributing goods throughout the kingdom.

### Middle Kingdom to New Kingdom
During the Middle Kingdom, the capital moved to Thebes, but Memphis remained a major commercial and artistic center. It was later besieged and captured by the Hyksos around 1650 BC. The New Kingdom saw a revival; Memphis became a center for royal education and a key port. Pharaohs like Amenhotep II, Thutmose IV, and Ramesses II built temples and palaces there. Ramesses II, in particular, adorned the city with colossal monuments, including a famous 10-meter limestone statue.

### Late Period and Foreign Rule
Memphis was repeatedly besieged during the Late Period by Kushites, Assyrians, and Persians. It was sacked by the Assyrians in 671 and 664 BC. After the Persian conquest in 525 BC, Memphis became the administrative capital of the satrapy. It remained a vital city through the Ptolemaic period, where decrees like the Rosetta Stone were issued. However, its political importance waned after the founding of Alexandria.

### Decline and Archaeological Exploration
Memphis lost its status permanently to Alexandria under Roman rule. The rise of Christianity and the founding of Fustat (Cairo) in 641 AD led to its abandonment. The city became a quarry for building materials. Systematic archaeological exploration began with Napoleon's expedition in 1798. Major work by Flinders Petrie in the early 20th century uncovered much of the Temple of Ptah and other structures. The site is now an open-air museum.

## Terms
- **Nome**: An administrative division of ancient Egypt.
- **Ka**: In ancient Egyptian religion, the vital essence or spirit of a person or god.
- **Hut-ka-Ptah**: The great temple of the god Ptah in Memphis; its name is the etymological root of "Egypt."
- **Peru-nefer**: The principal port of Memphis, a hub for workshops and trade.
- **Sed festival**: A jubilee ceremony celebrating the continued rule of a pharaoh.
- **Satrapy**: A province of the ancient Persian Empire.
- **Serapeum**: A temple or necropolis dedicated to the syncretic god Serapis, often containing burials of sacred Apis bulls.
- **Mastaba**: A type of ancient Egyptian tomb in the form of a flat-roofed, rectangular structure with sloping sides.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The identity of the legendary founder Menes is debated; some scholars equate him with the historical king Narmer, while others suggest he may be mythical.
- The exact location of the Temple of Apis within Memphis has not been definitively found, though a mortuary chamber for embalming the bulls was discovered.
- The precise location of the larger Temple of Hathor, believed to exist in Memphis, remains undiscovered.
- The original appearance and full layout of the Great Temple of Ptah are still unclear, as only its perimeter gates and some structures have been excavated.

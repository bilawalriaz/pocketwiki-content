# Norway

## Overview
Norway is a Nordic constitutional monarchy in Northern Europe, known for its extensive coastline, fjords, and significant petroleum reserves. It is a highly developed democracy with a mixed economy, combining free-market activity with large state ownership and a comprehensive welfare state. Its modern prosperity is largely fueled by its oil and gas industry, managed through a sovereign wealth fund.

## Timeline
- **c. 872** — Unification of petty kingdoms into the Kingdom of Norway under Harald Fairhair.
- **1349** — The Black Death kills approximately one-third of Norway's population.
- **1397** — Norway enters the Kalmar Union with Denmark and Sweden.
- **1537** — Norway becomes a province in a union with Denmark, beginning a period of Danish dominance.
- **1814** — Treaty of Kiel transfers Norway to Sweden; Norway adopts its constitution on 17 May.
- **1905** — Peaceful dissolution of the union with Sweden; Prince Carl of Denmark becomes King Haakon VII.
- **1940** — Norway is invaded and occupied by Nazi Germany.
- **1945** — Post-war Labour Party government begins building the modern welfare state.
- **1969** — Discovery of the Ekofisk oil field begins Norway's petroleum era.
- **1972 & 1994** — Norwegian voters reject joining the European Union in referendums.
- **2011** — Terrorist attacks by Anders Behring Breivik kill 77 people.

## Body

### History and Political Development
The unified kingdom was established c. 872. From 1537 to 1814, Norway was in a union with Denmark, a period some refer to as the "400-Year Night." In 1814, it was transferred to Sweden but adopted its own constitution. Norway maintained its own institutions while sharing a monarch and foreign policy with Sweden until a peaceful dissolution in 1905. It remained neutral in World War I but was occupied by Germany in World War II. The post-war era saw the Labour Party dominate politics and build a welfare state. Norway is a founding member of NATO and the UN but has twice rejected EU membership.

### Government and Politics
Norway is a unitary constitutional monarchy. The 1814 constitution establishes a parliamentary system where executive power is exercised by the prime minister and cabinet, while the monarch's role is ceremonial. Legislative power is vested in the unicameral Storting (Parliament), elected via proportional representation. The judiciary is independent. The country is divided into counties and municipalities. The indigenous Sámi people have a degree of self-governance through the Sámi Parliament.

### Economy and Resources
Norway has a prosperous mixed economy. The state has large ownership in key sectors like petroleum (Equinor), hydroelectric power (Statkraft), and banking. Petroleum and natural gas account for a significant portion of GDP and export revenues. The Government Pension Fund Global, the world's largest sovereign wealth fund, is financed by oil revenues and invested abroad. Norway is also a major producer of seafood and hydroelectric power. It participates in the EU single market via the EEA agreement but is not an EU member.

### Geography and Climate
Norway's core territory is the western and northern Scandinavian Peninsula, with a long, fjord-indented coastline. It includes the archipelagos of Svalbard and Jan Mayen. The climate is milder than expected at its latitude due to the Gulf Stream, with significant regional variations from oceanic in the west to subarctic in the north and continental in the east. The country experiences midnight sun in summer and polar night in winter north of the Arctic Circle.

### Demographics and Society
The population is approximately 5.6 million, primarily ethnic Norwegians. The Sámi are the indigenous minority in the north. The official language is Norwegian (in two written forms: Bokmål and Nynorsk). The Church of Norway, a Lutheran denomination, was disestablished from the state in 2017 but remains the "people's church." Society is characterized by egalitarian values, high levels of trust, and a comprehensive welfare system with universal healthcare.

## Terms
- **Constitutional monarchy**: A system of government where a monarch acts as head of state within the parameters of a constitution.
- **Storting**: The unicameral parliament of Norway.
- **Sovereign wealth fund**: A state-owned investment fund that invests in real and financial assets, funded by surplus revenues (e.g., from oil).
- **Mixed economy**: An economic system combining private and public enterprise.
- **EEA (European Economic Area)**: An agreement that allows non-EU members like Norway to participate in the EU's single market.
- **Fjord**: A long, narrow, deep inlet of the sea between high cliffs, formed by glacial erosion.
- **Sámi**: The indigenous Finno-Ugric people inhabiting northern Scandinavia and the Kola Peninsula.
- **Bokmål & Nynorsk**: The two official written standards of the Norwegian language.
- **Nordic model**: A socio-economic model characterized by a comprehensive welfare state, collective bargaining, and a high degree of social mobility.

## Debates and open questions
- **EU Membership**: The question of whether Norway should join the European Union remains a recurring political debate, with referendums held in 1972 and 1994 rejecting membership.
- **Oil Dependency**: There is ongoing debate about the pace of transitioning away from the petroleum industry to meet climate goals, balancing economic interests with environmental commitments.
- **Immigration and Integration**: As immigration has become the main driver of population growth, policies for integration and the balance between multiculturalism and national identity are subjects of political discussion.

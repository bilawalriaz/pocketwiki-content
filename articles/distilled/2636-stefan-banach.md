# Stefan Banach

## Overview
Stefan Banach (1892–1945) was a Polish mathematician and a founder of modern functional analysis, a field of mathematics dealing with vector spaces and operators. His work, particularly the axiomatization of Banach spaces, provided the foundational language for much of 20th-century analysis and remains central to mathematics and its applications.

## Timeline
- **30 March 1892** — Born in Kraków, then part of the Austro-Hungarian Empire.
- **1916** — Encounters Hugo Steinhaus in Kraków, leading to a pivotal collaboration.
- **1919** — Co-founds the Polish Mathematical Society with Steinhaus.
- **1920** — Receives an assistantship at Lwów Polytechnic; his doctoral thesis, containing the axiomatic definition of a Banach space, is accepted.
- **1922** — Becomes a professor at Lwów Polytechnic.
- **1924** — Elected a member of the Polish Academy of Learning.
- **1929** — Co-founds the journal *Studia Mathematica*.
- **1932** — Publishes his major work, *Théorie des opérations linéaires* (Theory of Linear Operations).
- **1939–1945** — During WWII, works as a lice feeder at the Weigl Institute for protection; later helps re-establish the university after Soviet recapture of Lviv.
- **31 August 1945** — Dies of lung cancer in Lviv (formerly Lwów).

## Body

### Early Life and Discovery
Born to a Góral family, Banach was raised by foster family friends after his parents, unable to marry due to military regulations, could not support him. He showed early mathematical talent, solving problems with a school friend. After completing his education, he worked various jobs while attending lectures. His career was launched in 1916 when he met Hugo Steinhaus in a park; Steinhaus overheard Banach discussing advanced mathematics and became his mentor, co-author, and gateway to academic circles.

### Academic Career and the Lwów School
With Poland's independence in 1918, Banach received an assistantship in Lwów. His 1920 doctoral thesis, which axiomatized the concept of a complete normed vector space (later named a Banach space), was foundational. He quickly rose to professor and gathered a group of mathematicians around him. This group, meeting at the Scottish Café, became the renowned Lwów School of Mathematics. In 1932, Banach published *Théorie des opérations linéaires*, the first monograph on functional analysis, cementing the field's structure.

### Wartime and Death
After the 1939 invasion of Poland, Lwów was first under Soviet and then German control. Banach was permitted to keep his position under the Soviets but had to promise to learn Ukrainian. Under the German occupation, universities were closed, and Banach, like many intellectuals, found protection by working as a lice feeder at Rudolf Weigl's Typhus Research Institute. After the city's recapture by the Soviets in 1944, he helped re-establish the university but was preparing to move to Kraków when he was diagnosed with lung cancer. He died in 1945.

### Mathematical Contributions
Banach's primary contribution was the rigorous, axiomatic foundation of functional analysis. His doctoral work and subsequent book defined what are now called Banach spaces—complete normed vector spaces—providing a general framework for studying infinite-dimensional spaces and linear operators. He also proved key results like the Banach fixed-point theorem (applicable in any complete metric space) and the Hahn–Banach theorem, a fundamental tool in analysis. Concepts bearing his name include Banach algebras, the Banach–Tarski paradox, and the Banach–Steinhaus theorem.

## Terms
- **Functional Analysis:** A branch of mathematical analysis dealing with vector spaces and operators acting upon them, often infinite-dimensional.
- **Banach Space:** A complete normed vector space; a central object of study in functional analysis.
- **Norm:** A function that assigns a strictly positive length or size to all vectors in a vector space, except for the zero vector.
- **Complete Space:** A metric space where every Cauchy sequence (a sequence whose elements become arbitrarily close) converges to a limit within the space.
- **Linear Operator:** A mapping between two vector spaces that preserves the operations of vector addition and scalar multiplication.
- **Lwów School of Mathematics:** A group of Polish mathematicians in the interwar period centered in Lwów, known for contributions to functional analysis and set theory.
- **Hahn–Banach Theorem:** A central result that allows the extension of bounded linear functionals defined on a subspace to the whole space.
- **Banach Fixed-Point Theorem:** A theorem that guarantees the existence and uniqueness of a fixed point for certain contraction mappings in complete metric spaces.

## Debates and Open Questions
The source notes that the naming of Banach spaces was not immediate; they were briefly called "Banach–Wiener" spaces, but the name "Banach spaces" became standard due to the limited scope of Norbert Wiener's work on the topic. The development of the theory had antecedents in the work of Frigyes Riesz and contemporaneous contributions from Hans Hahn and Norbert Wiener.

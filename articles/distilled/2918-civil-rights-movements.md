# Civil rights movements

## Overview
Civil rights movements are worldwide political campaigns for legal equality, often using nonviolent resistance, that peaked in the 1960s. They sought to end discrimination against groups like racial minorities, women, and the LGBT community, though many movements faced violence and did not fully achieve their goals.

## Timeline
- **1920** — Partition of Ireland creates tensions in Northern Ireland.
- **1954-1968** — U.S. Civil Rights Movement peaks, targeting racial discrimination.
- **1963** — Campaign for Social Justice (CSJ) launches in Belfast.
- **1966-1975** — Black Power movement emerges in the U.S.
- **1968** — Prague Spring liberalization in Czechoslovakia is crushed by Soviet invasion.
- **1968** — Chicano Movement active, with student walkouts in Denver and East L.A.
- **1969** — Alcatraz Island occupation catalyzes American Indian Movement (AIM).
- **1970** — FLQ kidnappings in Canada lead to invocation of War Measures Act.
- **1972** — Bloody Sunday in Derry, Northern Ireland, kills 14 unarmed marchers.
- **1975** — Helsinki Accords signed, providing human rights clauses for Soviet dissidents.

## Body

### Northern Ireland
The civil rights struggle began with local issues like housing discrimination against Catholics, linked to voting rights. The Northern Ireland Civil Rights Association (NICRA), modeled on the U.S. movement, demanded "one man, one vote" and an end to gerrymandering and sectarian police forces. Protests met Loyalist and police opposition, escalating into the Troubles, with violence from the Provisional IRA and British internment without trial. The Battle of the Bogside (1969) and Bloody Sunday (1972) were key violent turning points. A later peace process reduced violence and implemented protective laws.

### Canada's Quiet Revolution
In Quebec, the 1960s election of Jean Lesage followed the death of Maurice Duplessis, whose corrupt, Church-aligned government was rejected. The new government secularized education and health care, nationalized Hydro-Québec, and strengthened labor rights. These changes fostered a Quebec sovereignty movement, leading to the Parti Québécois enacting French-language laws. A radical faction, the FLQ, used terrorism, culminating in the 1970 kidnappings and murder of Pierre Laporte, which prompted Prime Minister Trudeau to declare martial law.

### United States
The movement targeted Jim Crow laws enforcing racial segregation, disenfranchisement, and violence against African Americans. Early strategies used litigation by the NAACP, but by 1955, leaders adopted nonviolent direct action like boycotts (Montgomery, 1955-56), sit-ins (Greensboro, 1960), and marches (Selma, 1965). The 1963 March on Washington featured Martin Luther King Jr.'s "I Have a Dream" speech. Key achievements included the *Brown v. Board of Education* ruling (1954) and the Civil Rights Acts of 1964 and 1968. By 1967, the Black Power movement, advocating self-determination over integration, gained prominence, symbolized by the 1968 Olympics Black Power salute.

### Other U.S. Movements
The Chicano Movement (1960s-70s) sought empowerment for Mexican-Americans, focusing on education, employment, and police brutality, with roots in the Treaty of Guadalupe Hidalgo. The American Indian Movement (AIM) used confrontational tactics, including the 1969 Alcatraz occupation and the 1973 Wounded Knee standoff. Second-wave feminism (1963-1982) addressed economic, reproductive, and educational equality.

### LGBT Rights
Beginning in the mid-19th century, early homophile groups used civil rights language. The 1960s counterculture spurred Gay Liberation, which rejected assimilation and advocated "coming out" and direct action. The Gay Liberation Front's name echoed anti-colonial movements. By the late 1970s, radicalism gave way to a more formal Gay and Lesbian Rights Movement.

### Soviet Union
In the 1960s, dissidents focused on civil rights like freedom of expression and against punitive psychiatry. They used a "legalist" approach, documenting abuses in samizdat (unsanctioned press) like the *Chronicle of Current Events* (1968). The 1975 Helsinki Accords inspired Helsinki Watch Groups to monitor human rights, despite severe state repression.

### Prague Spring
In Czechoslovakia, leader Alexander Dubček's 1968 "Socialism with a human face" reforms sought political liberalization within the communist system. The Soviet Union, viewing this as a threat to its hegemony, invaded with Warsaw Pact allies on August 20-21, 1968, crushing the movement and arresting Dubček.

### Indigenous Australians
Australia's civil rights push addressed historical injustices, including voting restrictions until 1965 and forced child removals. Protests like the Freedom Ride and Aboriginal Tent Embassy sought land rights and equality. Despite progress, significant disparities in health, education, and imprisonment persist, addressed by the "Closing the Gap" strategy.

## Terms
- **Civil rights**: The rights of citizens to political and social freedom and equality.
- **Nonviolent resistance**: A strategy of protest without physical violence, often using civil disobedience.
- **Gerrymandering**: Manipulating electoral district boundaries to favor a particular party or group.
- **Internment without trial**: Imprisonment of suspected enemies of the state without legal process.
- **Samizdat**: Underground, self-published literature in the Soviet Union used to spread uncensored information.
- **Helsinki Accords**: A 1975 agreement that included human rights provisions, used by dissidents to challenge Soviet authorities.
- **Jim Crow**: State and local laws enforcing racial segregation in the United States, especially in the South.
- **Homophile movement**: Early 20th-century groups advocating for the rights of homosexual people, often through assimilation.

## Debates and Open questions
- The success of the 1963 March on Washington is debated, including criticisms of the marginalization of women speakers.
- The link between civil rights activism and broader nationalist or separatist goals (e.g., in Northern Ireland and Quebec) remains ambiguous.
- The effectiveness of Black Power's self-sufficiency argument versus integration is contested, with some arguing it led to new forms of oppression.
- The long-term impact of the Prague Spring reforms and the Soviet response on later Eastern Bloc revolutions is analyzed.

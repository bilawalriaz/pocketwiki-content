# British Indian Ocean Territory

## Overview
The British Indian Ocean Territory (BIOT) is a British Overseas Territory in the Indian Ocean, comprising the Chagos Archipelago. Its history is defined by the forced expulsion of its native Chagossian population to create a joint UK-US military base on Diego Garcia, and a long-running sovereignty dispute with Mauritius, which culminated in a 2025 treaty to transfer sovereignty, though its ratification is currently suspended.

## Timeline
- **1965** — UK detaches Chagos Archipelago from Mauritius to form BIOT for a US-UK military base.
- **1968-1973** — British government forcibly removes ~2,000 Chagossians to Mauritius and Seychelles.
- **1971** — UK and US sign treaty leasing Diego Garcia for a military base; Chagossian removal from Diego Garcia is completed.
- **2010** — UK declares a Marine Protected Area around BIOT; Mauritius objects.
- **2019** — International Court of Justice advisory opinion states UK's administration is unlawful and must end.
- **2022** — UK and Mauritius begin formal negotiations on sovereignty.
- **2024** — UK and Mauritius announce an agreement for the UK to cede sovereignty, retaining a 99-year lease on Diego Garcia.
- **2025** — Treaty formally signed to transfer sovereignty to Mauritius.
- **2026** — US President Trump opposes the deal, suspending UK ratification; Chagossians attempt a resettlement on Île du Coin.

## Body

### Formation and Expulsion
The Chagos Archipelago was charted by Vasco da Gama and later claimed by France as part of Mauritius. It was settled in the 18th century by enslaved Africans and Indian indentured labourers to work coconut plantations. After the UK captured Mauritius in 1810, the islands remained a plantation colony. In 1965, the UK detached the Chagos Archipelago from Mauritius to form the BIOT, aiming to build a US-UK military base on Diego Garcia. Mauritius was paid £3 million as compensation. Between 1968 and 1973, the entire Chagossian population of about 2,000 people was forcibly removed to Mauritius and Seychelles to make the territory "uninhabited" for the base. The UK informed the UN that BIOT had no indigenous population.

### Sovereignty Dispute and International Rulings
Since the 1980s, Mauritius has claimed sovereignty, arguing the 1965 separation was illegal. The dispute gained international traction, leading the UN General Assembly to request an advisory opinion from the International Court of Justice (ICJ). In 2019, the ICJ ruled the UK's administration was unlawful and must end. The UN General Assembly and the International Tribunal for the Law of the Sea (ITLOS) later issued similar decisions. These rulings created significant diplomatic pressure on the UK.

### The Transfer Agreement and Suspension
Negotiations between the UK and Mauritius began in 2022. In 2024, they reached an understanding for the UK to cede sovereignty while retaining the Diego Garcia base on a 99-year lease. A formal treaty was signed in May 2025. However, in 2026, US President Donald Trump opposed the deal, calling it an "act of great stupidity," and UK ratification was suspended. The UN Committee on the Elimination of Racial Discrimination has criticized the deal for not adequately protecting Chagossian cultural rights and for forbidding their settlement on Diego Garcia.

### Chagossian Rights and Resettlement
Chagossians have consistently fought for their right of return, winning legal victories in UK courts in 2000, 2006, and 2007. The UK initially promised resettlement on outer islands in 2000 but reversed this in 2004. The 2024 agreement allows Chagossians to return to islands other than Diego Garcia. In February 2026, four Chagossians landed on Île du Coin without permission to establish a settlement, leading to a court injunction against their immediate deportation. A rival Chagossian government, formed in 2025, is now based there.

## Terms
- **Chagossian (Ilois):** The people native to the Chagos Archipelago, descended from enslaved Africans and Indian labourers.
- **Diego Garcia:** The largest island in the Chagos Archipelago, site of the joint UK-US military base.
- **Sovereignty:** Supreme authority over a territory; the core of the dispute between the UK and Mauritius.
- **Advisory Opinion:** A non-binding legal opinion issued by the International Court of Justice at the request of the UN.
- **Marine Protected Area (MPA):** A zone where human activities are restricted to protect the natural environment; the UK declared one around BIOT in 2010.
- **Exclusive Economic Zone (EEZ):** A maritime zone where a state has special rights regarding exploration and resource use.
- **Indentured Labourers:** Workers under contract to an employer for a specific number of years, a system that replaced slavery in the British Empire.
- **Decolonization:** The process of a colony gaining independence and becoming a sovereign state.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Legality of the 1965 Separation:** The UK maintains it was lawful, while the ICJ and UN bodies have ruled it violated international law and the territorial integrity of Mauritius.
- **Chagossian Right of Return:** While the 2024 deal permits return to outer islands, Chagossians are excluded from Diego Garcia. The UN has criticized this as insufficient for protecting their cultural rights.
- **Fate of the Transfer Treaty:** The treaty is signed but not ratified. Its future is uncertain due to US opposition and ongoing legal challenges from Chagossian groups.
- **Status of Asylum Seekers:** Sri Lankan Tamils stranded on Diego Garcia since 2021 live in a fenced camp. A court ruled their detention unlawful, but their long-term status remains unresolved as the 1951 Refugee Convention does not apply to BIOT.

# Aegean Sea

## Overview
The Aegean Sea is a large, elongated arm of the Mediterranean Sea situated between the Greek mainland and the Anatolian peninsula of Turkey. It is historically and geographically significant as the cradle of ancient Greek civilization, a vital maritime crossroads connecting Europe and Asia, and a region of ongoing geopolitical dispute between Greece and Turkey.

## Timeline
- **c. 3000–1450 BC** — Minoan civilization flourishes on Crete and other Aegean islands.
- **c. 1600–1100 BC** — Mycenaean civilization dominates mainland Greece and the Aegean.
- **480 BC** — Greek victory over Persians at the Battle of Salamis in the Aegean.
- **4th century BC** — Alexander the Great's conquests unify the Aegean under Macedonian rule.
- **2nd century BC** — Roman Republic gains control of the Aegean through the Macedonian Wars.
- **7th–11th centuries** — Byzantine Empire defends the Aegean against Arab and Bulgarian threats.
- **1204** — Fourth Crusade fragments Byzantine control; Venetians establish the Duchy of the Archipelago.
- **1453–1669** — Ottoman Empire gradually conquers the Aegean coast, except Venetian-held Crete.
- **1829** — Greek War of Independence establishes a Greek state on the Aegean coast.
- **1912–1913** — Balkan Wars result in Greek expansion and Bulgarian access to the northern Aegean.
- **1920s** — Greece and Turkey establish their present-day borders in the Aegean region.

## Body

### Geography and Hydrography
The Aegean Sea covers approximately 214,000 km² and reaches a maximum depth of 2,639 m west of Karpathos. It is connected to the Black Sea via the Dardanelles and Bosphorus straits. The sea contains numerous island groups, including the Cyclades, Dodecanese, and Sporades, with Crete forming its southern boundary. Its waters are characterized by a counterclockwise circulation, with hypersaline Mediterranean water sinking below a less dense Black Sea outflow. The climate is predominantly Mediterranean, with hot, dry summers and mild, wet winters, influenced by the seasonal Etesian winds.

### Ancient Civilizations and Classical Greece
The Aegean was the birthplace of Europe's first advanced civilizations: the Minoans on Crete (c. 3000–1450 BC) and the Mycenaeans on the mainland (c. 1600–1100 BC). Following the Bronze Age collapse and the Greek Dark Ages, the Archaic period saw the rise of city-states like Athens and Sparta. The Aegean was central to the Greco-Persian Wars, culminating in the decisive Greek naval victory at Salamis in 480 BC. Later, the region was unified under Macedonian rule during the conquests of Philip II and Alexander the Great.

### Roman, Byzantine, and Ottoman Rule
The Roman Republic conquered the Aegean in the 2nd century BC. After the empire's division, the Byzantine Empire maintained control for centuries, defending against Arab, Bulgarian, and Seljuk Turkish incursions. The Fourth Crusade (1204) shattered Byzantine authority, allowing Latin states and Venetian colonies to dominate. The Ottoman Empire eventually conquered most of the Aegean coast by the late 14th century, holding it for over 500 years, with the exception of Venetian Crete until 1669.

### Modern Period and Disputes
The Greek War of Independence (1821–1829) created a modern Greek state along the Aegean. The 20th century saw further territorial adjustments after the Balkan Wars and World War I, solidifying the modern borders of Greece and Turkey. Since the 1970s, sovereignty issues—known as the Aegean dispute—have strained Greek-Turkish relations. These disputes concern the delimitation of territorial waters, national airspace, exclusive economic zones, and flight information regions.

## Terms
- **Aegean Civilization**: A collective term for the Bronze Age cultures of the Aegean region, primarily the Minoan and Mycenaean civilizations.
- **Minoan Civilization**: The first advanced civilization in Europe, centered on Crete and flourishing from c. 3000 to 1450 BC.
- **Mycenaean Civilization**: A Bronze Age civilization on mainland Greece (c. 1600–1100 BC), known for its palace-centered states and warrior elite.
- **Linear B**: The syllabic script used by the Mycenaeans, providing the earliest written records of the Greek language.
- **Fourth Crusade (1204)**: A Western European campaign that sacked Constantinople, fragmenting the Byzantine Empire and allowing Latin and Venetian states to control parts of the Aegean.
- **Aegean Dispute**: The ongoing political and territorial disagreements between Greece and Turkey over sovereignty in the Aegean Sea.
- **Exclusive Economic Zone (EEZ)**: A maritime area where a state has special rights to explore and use marine resources, a key point of contention in the Aegean dispute.
- **Etesian Winds**: Strong, dry northerly winds that blow across the Aegean Sea during the summer.

## Debates and Open Questions
The primary open question is the resolution of the **Aegean dispute** between Greece and Turkey. Key debated issues include the legal width of territorial waters, the extent of national airspace, the delimitation of continental shelves and EEZs, and the status of various islands and islets. These unresolved issues remain a major source of tension and a potential flashpoint in the region.

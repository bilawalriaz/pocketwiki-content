# Park

## Overview
A park is a designated area of natural, semi-natural, or planted space set aside for human recreation, enjoyment, or the protection of wildlife and habitats. Parks range from small urban playgrounds to vast national reserves, serving as vital infrastructure for community well-being, physical activity, and biodiversity conservation. Their design and management reflect evolving societal values regarding public health, safety, and environmental stewardship.

## Timeline
- **1634** — Boston Common, a possible early public park, is set aside in Massachusetts.
- **c. 1574** — La Alameda de Hércules, an early urban promenade and park, is built in Seville, Spain.
- **1840** — Derby Arboretum, an early purpose-built public park, opens in England.
- **1843** — Princes Park, Liverpool, opens, influencing later park design.
- **1857** — New York's Central Park is designed, influenced by earlier British parks.
- **1872** — Yellowstone is established as the first "public park or pleasuring-ground" in the U.S.
- **1875** — Mackinac Island becomes the first officially designated U.S. national park.
- **1879** — Australia's Royal National Park becomes the world's second officially designated national park.
- **1974** — Northeast Greenland National Park is established, becoming the world's largest.
- **2016** — A UK report finds 92% of park managers have seen reduced maintenance budgets.
- **2021** — Azerbaijan opens a controversial Military Trophy Park in Baku.

## Body

### History and Evolution
Parks evolved from private, enclosed medieval deer parks used by aristocracy for hunting. From the 16th century, these became landscaped estates around mansions, designed to display wealth. As cities industrialized, private grounds and pasture lands were transformed into public urban parks to provide recreation and a "sense of nature." The Industrial Revolution accelerated this trend, leading to the creation of national parks to protect natural beauty from uncontrolled development.

### Design and Purpose
Park design is shaped by its intended use and audience. Features can include walking paths, playgrounds, sports fields, and seating. Design choices impact usage; for example, research in Vienna suggests semi-enclosed play areas can encourage equal use by boys and girls. Parks are crucial urban infrastructure for social gathering and mental health benefits. Safety is a key concern; studies show perceived safety, influenced by design elements like visibility and lighting, is more critical to park use than actual crime statistics.

### Types and Management
Parks are categorized by scale and management. **National parks** are large wilderness areas protected from development, often with visitor infrastructure. **Sub-national parks** (e.g., state or provincial parks) are managed by regional governments. **Urban parks** are locally maintained green spaces, often resembling savannas. **Linear parks** are long and narrow, like converted rail trails. **Country parks** offer countryside recreation near urban areas. **Private parks** are owned by individuals or businesses, with usage at the owner's discretion.

### Recreation and Conservation
Parks are divided into **active recreation** areas (e.g., ball fields, pools) requiring intensive management, and **passive recreation** areas (e.g., trails, natural habitats) with lower maintenance needs. Parks conserve biodiversity by creating urban habitats for flora and fauna. Vegetation absorbs greenhouse gases, reduces air temperature, and provides shade. The ecological richness of a park is often linked to its age and size.

## Terms
- **Biodiversity:** The variety of life on Earth, including all plants, animals, and microorganisms.
- **CPTED (Crime Prevention Through Environmental Design):** A design approach that uses environmental features to deter criminal behavior and enhance perceived safety.
- **Linear Park:** A park with a much greater length than width, often a converted railway or greenway.
- **Passive Recreation:** Low-intensity park use emphasizing open space and natural habitat, such as walking or birdwatching.
- **Active Recreation:** Intensive, often team-based activities requiring developed facilities like playgrounds or sports fields.
- **National Park:** A large, protected wilderness area, typically owned by a national government, that allows for some public visitation.
- **Sub-national Park:** A park managed by a regional government, such as a state or province.
- **Urban Park:** A green space within a city or town, provided for public recreation.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The optimal design for park safety is debated, as security measures like bars and locks can improve perceived safety but may negatively impact aesthetics and the feeling of public ownership.
- The funding and maintenance of public parks is a significant challenge; in the UK, most parks have no statutory duty for government funding, leading to budget reductions.
- The appropriate balance between active and passive recreation areas within a park to serve diverse community needs is an ongoing consideration.

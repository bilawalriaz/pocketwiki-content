# Jawaharlal Nehru

## Overview
Jawaharlal Nehru was India's first prime minister, serving from 1947 until his death in 1964. He was a central leader of the Indian independence movement and the architect of modern India's parliamentary democracy, secular state, and non-aligned foreign policy. His vision and policies profoundly shaped the nation's political, economic, and social trajectory in the 20th century.

## Timeline
- **14 November 1889** — Born in Allahabad, British India.
- **1912** — Called to the Bar; returned to India to practice law.
- **1919** — Joined Gandhi's non-cooperation movement; first imprisonment.
- **29 December 1929** — As Congress President, hoisted the flag and passed the Purna Swaraj (complete independence) resolution.
- **15 August 1947** — Became the first Prime Minister of independent India.
- **30 January 1948** — Assassination of Mahatma Gandhi.
- **26 January 1950** — Became the first Prime Minister of the Republic of India.
- **1955** — Awarded the Bharat Ratna, India's highest civilian honour.
- **1955** — Co-organized the Bandung Conference, a precursor to the Non-Aligned Movement.
- **1961** — Oversaw the annexation of Portuguese Goa.
- **1962** — India lost the Sino-Indian War; Nehru's "Forward Policy" was discredited.
- **27 May 1964** — Died in office of a heart attack.

## Body

### Early Life and Nationalist Movement
Born into a wealthy Kashmiri Pandit family, Nehru was educated in England at Harrow, Cambridge, and the Inner Temple. He returned to India in 1912, initially practicing law before being drawn into politics. Influenced by his father Motilal Nehru and later by Mahatma Gandhi, he became a key leader in the Indian National Congress. He was imprisoned numerous times between 1921 and 1945 for his activism. As Congress President in 1929, he called for complete independence, a goal formally adopted at the Lahore session. During World War II, he initially supported the Allied war effort but later joined Gandhi's Quit India movement in 1942, leading to his imprisonment until 1945.

### Prime Minister and Nation-Building
Nehru became interim prime minister in September 1946 and was sworn in as the first prime minister of independent India on 15 August 1947. He oversaw the integration of princely states into the Indian Union and the adoption of the Indian Constitution in 1950, which established a sovereign democratic republic. He led the Congress party to electoral victories in 1951, 1957, and 1962. His government focused on modernization through a mixed economy, state-led industrialization, and the establishment of premier educational institutions like the IITs and AIIMS. He championed secularism and social reform, notably through the Hindu Code Bills that expanded women's rights. His tenure ended with the military defeat by China in 1962, which severely impacted his health and reputation.

### Foreign Policy
Nehru was a principal architect of India's foreign policy, holding the external affairs portfolio throughout his tenure. He pioneered the policy of non-alignment, seeking to keep India independent of the two Cold War blocs. He co-founded the Non-Aligned Movement and was a key figure in the 1955 Bandung Conference. His foreign policy was idealistic, emphasizing peaceful coexistence, as codified in the 1954 Panchsheel agreement with China. He maintained good relations with the Commonwealth and was a strong supporter of the United Nations. However, his policy towards China collapsed with the 1962 war, and his handling of the Kashmir dispute with Pakistan remained contentious.

## Terms
*   **Purna Swaraj:** Complete independence from British rule, formally declared by the Indian National Congress in 1930.
*   **Non-Aligned Movement:** A group of nations that did not seek formal alignment with either the US-led Western bloc or the Soviet-led Eastern bloc during the Cold War.
*   **Panchsheel:** The Five Principles of Peaceful Coexistence, a set of principles to govern relations between states, first codified in the 1954 Sino-Indian agreement.
*   **Mixed Economy:** An economic system combining private enterprise with state-owned public sector industries and government planning.
*   **Hindu Code Bills:** A series of laws passed in the 1950s that reformed Hindu personal law, granting greater rights and freedoms to women.
*   **Forward Policy:** India's 1960s strategy of establishing military outposts in disputed border areas with China, which precipitated the 1962 war.
*   **Secularism:** In the Indian context, the principle of equal treatment of all religions by the state, as promoted by Nehru.
*   **Scientific Temper:** A worldview championed by Nehru that emphasizes rational inquiry, skepticism, and the application of science to human problems.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Economic Legacy:** Scholars debate whether Nehru's socialist-inspired, state-controlled economic model (the "License Raj") stifled growth and entrepreneurship or was a necessary foundation for industrializing a newly independent nation.
*   **Secularism and Uniform Civil Code:** Nehru's decision to apply reformed personal law to Hindus while allowing Muslims to retain their own personal laws is criticized as inconsistent secularism. His inclusion of Article 44 (the Directive Principle for a Uniform Civil Code) remains a point of political and legal debate.
*   **Foreign Policy and the 1962 War:** The failure of his non-aligned and peaceful coexistence policies with China, culminating in the 1962 military defeat, is a major point of criticism. Some argue his idealism was naive, while others contend the war was an inevitable betrayal by China.
*   **Kashmir Policy:** His handling of the Kashmir dispute, including the initial promise of a UN-supervised plebiscite (later withdrawn) and the ouster of Sheikh Abdullah in 1953, remains deeply contested between India and Pakistan.

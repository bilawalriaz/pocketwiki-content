# Russo-Turkish War (1877–1878)

## Overview
The Russo-Turkish War (1877–1878) was a conflict between the Ottoman Empire and a coalition led by Russia, including Romania, Serbia, and Montenegro. It was a pivotal event that redrew the map of Southeast Europe, leading to the independence of several Balkan states and the significant reduction of Ottoman territory, while also drawing in the Great Powers of Europe.

## Timeline
- **1875–1876** — Uprisings in Bosnia, Herzegovina, and Bulgaria against Ottoman rule, including the suppression of the Bulgarian Uprising with massacres.
- **30 June 1876** — Serbia and Montenegro declare war on the Ottoman Empire.
- **11 December 1876** — Constantinople Conference of the Great Powers opens; Ottomans later reject its terms.
- **15 January 1877** — Russia and Austria-Hungary sign a secret agreement, securing Austrian neutrality.
- **24 April 1877** — Russia declares war on the Ottoman Empire.
- **10 May 1877** — Romania declares independence from the Ottoman Empire.
- **July–December 1877** — Siege of Plevna, a major defensive victory for the Ottomans that delays the Russian advance.
- **18 November 1877** — Russian forces capture Kars in the Caucasus.
- **31 January 1878** — Ottoman Empire accepts a truce.
- **3 March 1878** — Treaty of San Stefano is signed, creating a large Bulgarian state.
- **June–July 1878** — Congress of Berlin revises the Treaty of San Stefano, reducing Bulgaria's size.

## Body

### Background and Causes
The war stemmed from long-term Ottoman decline and Russian ambitions to recover prestige lost in the Crimean War (1853–1856), re-establish a Black Sea fleet, and support Slavic and Orthodox Christian independence movements in the Balkans. The Concert of Europe, which had maintained a balance of power, fractured after the wars of German unification. Russia, allied with Germany, sought to exploit Ottoman weakness. The immediate trigger was the brutal Ottoman suppression of the 1876 Bulgarian Uprising, which caused international outrage, particularly in Russia and Britain, and provided a pretext for intervention.

### Military Course
The war was fought on two main fronts: the Balkans and the Caucasus. In the Balkans, Russian forces crossed the Danube but were halted at the Siege of Plevna, where Ottoman forces under Osman Pasha held out for 145 days. The siege was only broken with Romanian assistance. After Plevna fell, Russian and Romanian forces advanced, capturing the Shipka Pass and pushing towards Constantinople. In the Caucasus, Russian forces, led by several Armenian generals, captured the key fortresses of Kars and Erzurum. Serbia re-entered the war and captured Niš, while Greek forces briefly entered Thessaly.

### Aftermath and Treaties
The Russian advance to the outskirts of Constantinople prompted British naval intervention. The preliminary Treaty of San Stefano (March 1878) recognized the independence of Romania, Serbia, and Montenegro, and created a large, autonomous Bulgarian state under Russian influence. Alarmed by this expansion of Russian power, the Great Powers convened the Congress of Berlin (June–July 1878). The resulting Treaty of Berlin revised San Stefano, splitting Bulgaria into a smaller principality and the autonomous province of Eastern Rumelia, and returning Macedonia to direct Ottoman control. Russia annexed Kars, Batum, and Ardahan in the Caucasus. The war also internationalized the "Armenian Question," with the Treaty of Berlin (Article 61) calling for Ottoman reforms in Armenian-populated provinces, though these were never implemented.

### Civilian Impact and Atrocities
The war was marked by significant civilian suffering and atrocities committed by both sides. Ottoman forces, including irregular Bashi-bazouks, carried out massacres of Bulgarian civilians, most notably at Stara Zagora. Advancing Russian and Bulgarian forces also committed atrocities against Muslim populations, including massacres and the burning of villages. This led to a massive exodus of Muslims from the territories ceded by the Ottomans, with estimates of refugees and deaths varying widely among historians. The conflict also saw the first use of the Red Crescent emblem by the Ottoman Empire as an alternative to the Red Cross.

## Terms
*   **Bashi-bazouk:** Irregular Ottoman soldiers, often recruited from Circassian or other Muslim communities, known for their lack of discipline and involvement in atrocities.
*   **Redif:** The Ottoman reserve army, consisting of veterans and men who had not been conscripted, called up for the war.
*   **Nizam:** The standing, regular army of the Ottoman Empire.
*   **Treaty of San Stefano (1878):** Preliminary peace treaty that created a large Bulgarian state and recognized the independence of Serbia, Montenegro, and Romania.
*   **Treaty of Berlin (1878):** Revised the Treaty of San Stefano, reducing the size of Bulgaria and confirming Ottoman control over Macedonia.
*   **Pan-Slavism:** The political and cultural movement advocating unity among Slavic peoples, often under Russian leadership, used as a justification for Russian intervention.
*   **Concert of Europe:** The system of diplomatic cooperation among the Great Powers (Britain, France, Austria-Hungary, Russia, Prussia/Germany) established after the Napoleonic Wars, which broke down in the 1860s.
*   **Enosis:** The political movement for union of a region with Greece, as sought by Cretan insurgents.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Scale of Muslim Casualties:** Historians disagree sharply on the number of Muslim civilians killed or forced to flee from territories lost by the Ottoman Empire. Estimates range from around 260,000 deaths and 500,000 refugees (Hupchick, McCarthy) to as high as 1.5 million displaced (Karpat, İpek). The debate is complicated by conflicting census data and accusations of bias.
*   **Responsibility for Atrocities:** While Ottoman forces committed well-documented massacres of Bulgarian civilians, the extent and responsibility for atrocities against Muslim populations are contested. Some historians emphasize Russian and Bulgarian actions, while others point to the role of Circassian irregulars and even Ottoman forces in causing civilian casualties among Muslims.
*   **Legacy of the Armenian Question:** The war's outcome, particularly the unfulfilled reforms promised in the Treaty of Berlin, is seen by many Armenian historians as a critical step that led to the later radicalization of Armenian political movements and the subsequent Armenian Genocide.

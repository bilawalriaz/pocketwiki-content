# Decebalus

## Overview
Decebalus was the last king of Dacia, a region north of the Danube, who ruled from 87 to 106 AD. He is significant for leading his people in a series of wars against the Roman Empire, first securing a period of independence and later fighting two major conflicts against Emperor Trajan. His resistance and eventual defeat are central to the history of Roman expansion and are a foundational element of Romanian national identity.

## Timeline
- **c. 85-86 AD** — Dacian raids into the Roman province of Moesia begin under King Duras.
- **86 AD** — Dacians defeat and kill the governor of Moesia, Oppius Sabinus.
- **87 AD** — Decebalus, likely the commander "Diurpaneus," defeats and kills the Roman general Cornelius Fuscus at Tapae.
- **88 AD** — Decebalus is crowned king after Duras abdicates.
- **88 AD** — Romans under Tettius Julianus defeat Decebalus near Tapae.
- **89 AD** — Peace treaty: Domitian agrees to pay annual tribute to Decebalus.
- **101 AD** — Trajan launches the First Dacian War.
- **102 AD** — Decebalus is defeated, loses territory, and dismantles fortresses but retains his throne.
- **105 AD** — Decebalus initiates the Second Dacian War by attacking Roman territory.
- **106 AD** — Romans capture and destroy the Dacian capital, Sarmizegetusa.
- **106 AD** — Decebalus commits suicide to avoid capture.

## Body

### Rise to Power and War with Domitian
After the death of the great Dacian king Burebista, Dacia fragmented. Decebalus rose to prominence in the court of King Duras. In 86 AD, Dacians raided the Roman province of Moesia, killing its governor. The Roman Emperor Domitian responded, but his general Cornelius Fuscus was ambushed and killed at the mountain pass of Tapae. Decebalus was crowned king after this victory. Although the Romans later won a battle at Tapae, Domitian, facing other threats, agreed to a peace treaty in 89 AD that recognized Decebalus's royal status and provided him with annual tribute.

### Consolidation of Power
Using the peace and Roman engineers, Decebalus centralized his rule, strengthened fortifications, and built war machines. His court became a refuge for Roman deserters and malcontents, forming a center of anti-Roman sentiment. He also forged alliances with neighboring tribes like the Bastarnae and Roxolani, while ensuring others, such as the Quadi and Marcomanni, would not interfere with his plans.

### Conflicts with Trajan
When Trajan became emperor, he resolved to eliminate the Dacian threat. In the First Dacian War (101-102 AD), Trajan invaded Dacia. Despite inflicting serious losses on the Romans, Decebalus was ultimately defeated. He was forced to cede territory, dismantle fortresses, and accept a subordinate position, though he kept his throne.

Decebalus soon violated the treaty by re-arming and attacking Roman-allied tribes. In 105, he initiated the Second Dacian War by attacking Roman-held territory. Trajan responded with a massive invasion. After a prolonged siege, the Romans captured and destroyed the Dacian capital, Sarmizegetusa. Decebalus escaped but was hunted down. To avoid capture and humiliation in Rome, he committed suicide by cutting his own throat.

### Legacy in Romania
Decebalus is a national hero in Romania, a status solidified in the 19th century when he was embraced as a symbol of resistance to imperialism. He is often paired with his conqueror Trajan, representing national identity versus Roman classical values. This heroic image was promoted during the Communist era, particularly under Nicolae Ceaușescu, and remains central to Romanian historical consciousness and the "Dacianism" movement, which views modern Romanians as direct descendants of the Dacians.

## Terms
*   **Dacia:** An ancient region and kingdom located north of the Danube, in modern-day Romania and Moldova.
*   **Client King:** A ruler who maintains his throne under the suzerainty of a more powerful state, in this case, Rome.
*   **Moesia:** A Roman province south of the Danube, frequently raided by Dacians.
*   **Sarmizegetusa:** The capital and major fortress of the Dacian kingdom.
*   **Tapae:** A strategic mountain pass in the Carpathians, site of several key battles between Dacians and Romans.
*   **Hordes of Sesterces:** The annual tribute paid by Rome to Decebalus under their 89 AD treaty.
*   **Protochronism:** A Romanian cultural and historical theory that posits the primacy of Romanian civilization in Eastern Europe.
*   **Dacianism:** A movement emphasizing the Dacian origins of the Romanian nation.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Identity of Diurpaneus:** Ancient sources refer to the Dacian leader in the 86 AD raids as "Diurpaneus." Scholars debate whether this was King Duras himself or the future king Decebalus. Recent sources favor the view that Diurpaneus was Decebalus.
*   **Parentage of Decebalus:** A Dacian pot inscription reading "Decebalus per Scorilo" has led to the theory that his father was named Scorilo. This idea, originally a "scholarly joke," is considered plausible by some historians, who may link Scorilo to a king named Coryllus mentioned by Jordanes.
*   **Ancestry of Regalianus:** The Roman usurper Regalianus claimed kinship with Decebalus. This is sometimes linked to the abduction of Decebalus's sister during the First Dacian War, suggesting she may have been an ancestress of Regalianus.

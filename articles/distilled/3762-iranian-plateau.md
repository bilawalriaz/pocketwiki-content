# Iranian plateau

## Overview
The Iranian Plateau is a vast geological and historical region spanning parts of Central, South, and West Asia. It is a major landmass formed by tectonic plate collisions, serving as a cradle for early human dispersal and the heartland of ancient civilizations like Elam and Persia. Its diverse geography, from high mountains to deserts, has shaped its unique ecology and long history of human settlement.

## Timeline
- **c. 50,000 years ago** — The plateau may have been a "population hub" for early Eurasians, who diverged into Ancient East and West Eurasian lineages.
- **c. 45,000–38,000 years ago** — Two waves of human expansion from the hub during the Initial Upper Paleolithic and Upper Paleolithic periods.
- **Bronze Age** — The civilization of Elam spans the Zagros mountains, connecting Mesopotamia and the Iranian plateau.
- **Classical Antiquity** — The region is known as Persia, after the Persian Achaemenid dynasty originating in Fars.
- **Sasanian Era** — The term *Erān* (Modern Persian *Irān*) begins to be used for the state.

## Body

### Geology and Geography
The Iranian Plateau is a geologically active region formed by the accretion of terranes between the Turan platform and the Zagros fold and thrust belt. It is wedged between the Arabian and Indian plates, which are colliding with the Eurasian Plate. Stretching nearly 2,000 km from the Caspian Sea to the Sulaiman Mountains, it covers about 3.7 million km², encompassing most of Iran, all of Afghanistan, and parts of Pakistan west of the Indus River. Despite its name, the terrain is not flat, containing major mountain ranges and deserts. Its highest point is Noshaq (7,492 m) in the Hindu Kush, and its lowest is the Lut Desert (below 300 m).

The plateau's geography is defined by its boundaries: the Zagros Mountains to the west, the Caspian Sea and Köpet Dag to the north, and the Hindu Kush to the east. The Anti-Taurus region in the northwest is rugged, with peaks over 3,000 m, including Mount Ararat (5,137 m). Major rivers like the Aras, Euphrates, and Tigris originate in these highlands. The central and eastern areas include vast deserts like the Kavir and Lut.

### History and Archaeology
The plateau played a potential major role in early human history, possibly acting as a hub for the expansion of modern humans after the Out of Africa migration. Genetic evidence suggests ancient and modern populations here carry a core "Ancient West Eurasian" lineage, with additional ancestry from Basal Eurasians and Ancient East Eurasians through contact events starting in the Paleolithic.

In the Bronze Age, the Elamite civilization connected Mesopotamia and the plateau. The legendary kingdom of Aratta may have been located in the central plateau. The region became known as Persia in classical antiquity due to the Achaemenid dynasty. The name *Erān* (Iran) came into use for the state during the Sasanian era. Archaeological sites include the Jiroft culture, Shahr-e Sukhteh, and Tappeh Sialk.

### Flora, Fauna, and Economy
The plateau historically supported oak and poplar forests, with trees like aspen, elm, and walnut also present. Wildlife includes leopards, bears, ibex, and gazelles in the mountains, while aquatic birds inhabit the Caspian and Persian Gulf coasts. The region is also home to a variety of reptiles, amphibians, and fish.

Economically, the plateau has been used for harvesting timber and growing a wide range of crops. Fruits include pears, apples, apricots, and dates. Grains like wheat and barley are cultivated, along with cotton, tobacco, and opium. Vegetables such as potatoes, tomatoes, and eggplants are also grown.

## Terms
- **Accretionary terranes:** Fragments of crustal material that have been added to a continent through plate tectonics.
- **Fold and thrust belt:** A series of mountain ranges formed by the shortening and thickening of the Earth's crust during continental collision.
- **Suture zone:** The linear belt of crust marking the boundary where two continents have joined following the closure of an ocean basin.
- **Out of Africa migration:** The major dispersal of anatomically modern humans from Africa to other parts of the world, beginning around 70,000–100,000 years ago.
- **Basal Eurasians:** A hypothesized lineage of early modern humans that diverged from other Eurasians before the main Out of Africa migration.
- **Cuneiform:** An ancient system of writing using wedge-shaped marks, used in Mesopotamia and surrounding regions.
- **Achaemenid dynasty:** The first Persian Empire (c. 550–330 BC), founded by Cyrus the Great.
- **Sasanian era:** The last Persian imperial dynasty before the Muslim conquest (224–651 AD).

## Debates and Open Questions
- The precise role of the Iranian Plateau as a "population hub" for early Eurasian dispersals and the timing of subsequent divergences are subjects of ongoing genetic and archaeological research.
- The exact location of the ancient kingdom of Aratta, known from cuneiform sources, is debated, with the central Iranian plateau being a proposed candidate.

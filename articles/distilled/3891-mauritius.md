# Mauritius

## Overview
Mauritius is an island nation in the Indian Ocean, east of Madagascar. Its strategic location and colonial history have created a diverse, multicultural society. It is known for its political stability, economic development, and unique biodiversity, including the extinct dodo bird.

## Timeline
- **c. 975** — Arab sailors possibly discover the uninhabited island, naming it Dina Arobi.
- **1507** — Portuguese sailors visit and name it Ilha do Cisne (Island of the Swan).
- **1598** — Dutch fleet lands, renames the island Mauritius after Prince Maurice of Orange.
- **1715** — France takes control, renames it Isle de France.
- **1810** — United Kingdom seizes the island; France cedes it and dependencies in the Treaty of Paris (1814).
- **1834** — British begin importing indentured labourers from India after abolishing slavery.
- **1965** — UK detaches the Chagos Archipelago to form the British Indian Ocean Territory (BIOT).
- **1968** — Mauritius gains independence, becoming a constitutional monarchy.
- **1992** — Mauritius becomes a republic within the Commonwealth.
- **2020** — MV Wakashio oil spill causes a major environmental disaster.
- **2025** — UK and Mauritius sign an agreement to transfer sovereignty of the Chagos Archipelago to Mauritius.

## Body

### History and Colonial Rule
Mauritius was uninhabited until Arab sailors possibly reached it around 975. The Portuguese visited in 1507 but did not settle. The Dutch colonized it from 1598, exploiting ebony forests and introducing sugar cane, but abandoned it in 1710. France took control in 1715, developing a plantation economy based on slavery. After the British seized it in 1810, slavery was abolished in the 1830s, leading to the importation of half a million indentured labourers from India between 1834 and 1921. This migration fundamentally shaped the island's demographics.

### Independence and Modern Politics
Mauritius gained independence in 1968 and became a republic in 1992. Its government is a parliamentary democracy modelled on the Westminster system, with a unicameral National Assembly. A unique "Best Loser System" appoints additional members to ensure minority representation. The country is consistently ranked as Africa's only full democracy. A major ongoing issue is the sovereignty dispute over the Chagos Archipelago, which the UK separated from Mauritius in 1965 to create a US military base on Diego Garcia. The local population was forcibly expelled. In 2019, the International Court of Justice ruled the UK's administration unlawful, and in 2025, the UK agreed to transfer sovereignty to Mauritius, with Diego Garcia leased back for 99 years.

### Economy and Society
Mauritius has transformed from a sugar-based economy to a diversified, high-income one, often called the "Mauritian Miracle." Key sectors include tourism, financial services, textiles, and ICT. The country offers free education and healthcare. Its population is multi-ethnic, with Indo-Mauritians as the largest group. Hinduism is the most practiced religion. The official language of the National Assembly is English, but French and Mauritian Creole are widely spoken. The nation is renowned for its biodiversity, though human activity has threatened many endemic species, including the dodo.

## Terms
- **Indentured labourers**: Workers from India who replaced enslaved people on plantations after abolition, bound by a contract.
- **Best Loser System**: A constitutional mechanism to appoint additional parliamentary members to ensure ethnic minority representation.
- **Mascarene Islands**: A group of volcanic islands in the Indian Ocean including Mauritius, Réunion, and Rodrigues.
- **Exclusive Economic Zone (EEZ)**: A maritime area where a nation has special rights to explore and use marine resources.
- **Creole**: A French-based language with influences from other languages, spoken by most Mauritians as a native tongue.
- **Chagos Archipelago**: A group of islands north-east of Mauritius, disputed between the UK and Mauritius.
- **Dodo**: A flightless bird endemic to Mauritius that became extinct shortly after human settlement.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The sovereignty of **Tromelin Island** is disputed between Mauritius and France, as the 1814 Treaty of Paris did not explicitly mention it.
- The **Chagos Archipelago** sovereignty transfer agreement, signed in 2025, awaits ratification and involves complex arrangements for the Diego Garcia military base.
- The environmental and ethical impacts of the **biomedical export of macaques** are debated, balancing population control with animal welfare and religious concerns.

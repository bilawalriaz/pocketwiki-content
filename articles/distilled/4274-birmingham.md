# Birmingham

## Overview
Birmingham is the UK's second-largest city, located in the West Midlands. It evolved from a medieval market town into the "first manufacturing town in the world" during the Industrial Revolution, driven by skilled, small workshops. Today, it is a major international commercial, cultural, and transport hub with a diverse population and vibrant arts scene.

## Body

### Historical Development
Birmingham's history begins with Stone Age settlement around 8000 BC. The modern city was founded by the Anglo-Saxon Beormingas tribe around the 6th or 7th century. Its significant growth began in 1166 with a market charter. The collapse of medieval governing institutions in the 16th century gave the town unusual social and economic freedom, fostering radicalism and innovation. This environment led to the Midlands Enlightenment in the 18th century, where thinkers and manufacturers like those in the Lunar Society exchanged ideas, creating a "chain reaction of innovation" linking the Scientific Revolution to the Industrial Revolution.

Birmingham's industrial expansion was unique, built on a highly skilled, well-paid workforce in small, adaptable workshops rather than large factories. This led to exceptional inventiveness, with Birmingham residents registering over three times as many patents as any other British town between 1760 and 1850. Key innovations included the industrial steam engine (1776), the first cotton mill (1741), and early chemical processes. The city's political influence grew with campaigns for parliamentary reform in the 19th century, led by figures like Thomas Attwood and Joseph Chamberlain.

### 20th Century to Present
Birmingham was heavily bombed during World War II and was the site of critical scientific discoveries, including the Frisch–Peierls memorandum on nuclear weapons and the cavity magnetron for radar. Post-war redevelopment included extensive demolition and the construction of tower blocks. The city's population peaked at 1.1 million in 1951, and its ethnic makeup changed significantly with Commonwealth immigration.

The city's economy, once the most prosperous provincial one, declined after World War II due to government policies and over-reliance on the motor industry, leading to a severe recession in the early 1980s. Since the 21st century, major regeneration projects have transformed the city centre. Birmingham's economy is now dominated by the service sector. In 2023, the city council declared effective bankruptcy due to equal pay liabilities and an IT system cost overrun.

### Geography and Climate
Birmingham is located on the Birmingham Plateau, a relatively high area crossed by Britain's main north–south watershed. It is drained by minor rivers, primarily the River Tame and its tributaries. The city has a temperate maritime climate with average summer highs of 21.3 °C (70.3 °F) and winter lows of 6.7 °C (44.1 °F). It is relatively snowy for a UK city and has experienced notable tornadoes.

### Demographics
The 2021 census recorded a population of 1,144,900. Birmingham is one of Europe's youngest cities, with a median age of 34. It is highly diverse: 48.7% of the population is White, 31% is Asian (with large Pakistani, Indian, and Bangladeshi communities), and 10.9% is Black. The city has the UK's largest Muslim, Sikh, and Buddhist communities outside London.

### Economy and Transport
Birmingham's economy is dominated by the service sector, which accounted for 88% of employment in 2012. It is a major centre for public administration, education, health, and financial services. Manufacturing, including automotive (Jaguar Land Rover) and jewellery (40% of UK output), remains important. The city is a major transport hub. Birmingham New Street is the busiest UK railway station outside London. The city is served by the M5, M6, M40, and M42 motorways and has an extensive canal network. Birmingham Airport is the seventh busiest in the UK.

### Culture
Birmingham has major cultural institutions, including the Birmingham Royal Ballet, the City of Birmingham Symphony Orchestra, and the Library of Birmingham, the largest public library in Europe. The city has a rich musical heritage, being a birthplace of heavy metal (Black Sabbath, Judas Priest) and modern bhangra. It is home to the oldest independent cinema in the UK, The Electric. The Balti curry was invented here, and the city has five Michelin-starred restaurants. It hosted the 2022 Commonwealth Games.

## Terms
- **Midlands Enlightenment:** An 18th-century cultural phenomenon in Birmingham where leading thinkers and manufacturers exchanged philosophical and scientific ideas, fostering innovation.
- **Lunar Society of Birmingham:** An influential club of leading citizens from the Midlands Enlightenment, including manufacturers and scientists.
- **Rational manufacture:** A system pioneered by Matthew Boulton at the Soho Manufactory, combining and mechanising previously separate manufacturing activities under one roof.
- **Birmingham Blitz:** The heavy bombing of Birmingham by the German Luftwaffe from the summer of 1940 to the spring of 1943.
- **Section 114 notice:** A notice issued by a local authority in the UK to declare it cannot meet its financial commitments, effectively declaring bankruptcy.
- **Birmingham Canal Navigations:** The extensive network of canals in and around Birmingham, a legacy of the Industrial Revolution.
- **Brummie:** A demonym for people from Birmingham, derived from the city's old name, Brummagem.
- **Balti:** A type of curry that was invented in Birmingham.

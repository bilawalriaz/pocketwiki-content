# Herat

## Overview
Herat is a major city in western Afghanistan, serving as the capital of Herat Province. Historically a vital hub on the Silk Road, it has been a center of trade, culture, and power for millennia, known as the "Pearl of Khorasan." Its strategic location near the Iranian and Turkmen borders makes it a key economic and political gateway for Afghanistan.

## Timeline
- **c. 550–330 BC** — Part of the Achaemenid Persian Empire as the district of Haraiva (Aria).
- **330 BC** — Captured by Alexander the Great; rebuilt and citadel constructed.
- **650s** — Arab armies conquer the region; Herat submits after initial resistance.
- **870** — Conquered by Ya'qub ibn al-Layth al-Saffar, founder of the Saffarid dynasty.
- **1222** — Devastated by the Mongol Empire after a rebellion; population massacred.
- **1380** — Conquered by Timur (Tamerlane), beginning its Timurid golden age.
- **1469–1506** — Reaches peak cultural glory under Timurid ruler Sultan Husayn Bayqara.
- **1747** — Becomes part of Ahmad Shah Durrani's new Afghan empire.
- **1863** — Reincorporated into Afghanistan by Dost Mohammad Khan after Qajar Iranian invasions.
- **1979** — Site of a major uprising against the communist government, preceding the Soviet-Afghan War.
- **1995** — Captured by the Taliban, becoming their first major Persian-speaking city.
- **2001** — Taken from the Taliban by Northern Alliance forces.
- **2021** — Falls to the Taliban during their nationwide offensive.

## Body

### Ancient and Medieval History
Herat's precise foundation is unknown, but it is recorded in ancient times as Haraiva (Aria) within the Persian Achaemenid Empire. It was rebuilt by Alexander the Great in 330 BC. The city was predominantly Zoroastrian and later had a Christian community before the Arab conquest in the 7th century. After periods under various dynasties, it flourished under the Ghurids in the 12th century. The Mongol invasion in 1222 caused catastrophic destruction. Under the Kartid dynasty and later the Timurids, Herat regained prominence. It became the "Pearl of Khorasan" and the focal point of the Timurid Renaissance, a cultural rebirth whose glory is thought to have matched that of Florence.

### Modern History (1500–2023)
After the Timurids, Herat was contested by Safavid Persia and the Uzbeks. By the early 18th century, it was governed by Abdali Afghans and became part of the Durrani Empire in 1747. In the 19th century, Herat was an independent city-state facing multiple invasions from Qajar Iran until it was conquered by Afghanistan in 1863. The 1979 Herat uprising against the communist government was brutally suppressed, killing thousands. The city suffered damage during the Soviet-Afghan War. After the Taliban captured it in 1995, it was retaken by anti-Taliban forces in 2001. Herat was relatively stable until it fell to the Taliban again in August 2021.

### Geography and Demographics
Herat is situated in a fertile valley of the Hari River, south of the Paropamisus Mountains. It has a cold semi-arid climate with low precipitation. The city has an estimated population of 673,273 settled residents, with Tajiks forming the majority (around 85%). Dari (Persian) is the primary language. The city once had a Jewish community, which disappeared by 1992; its former synagogues have been renovated.

### Economy and Transport
Herat's economy is based on agriculture (notably grapes and saffron), trade, and industry. It has a large industrial park and collects significant customs revenue as a gateway to Iran and Turkmenistan. Transport includes the Khwaja Abdullah Ansari International Airport, a developing rail link with Iran, and key highways connecting it to other Afghan cities and neighboring countries.

## Terms
- **Silk Road**: An ancient network of trade routes connecting East and West Asia, on which Herat was a major hub.
- **Khorasan**: A historical region in Central Asia, of which Herat was a principal city.
- **Timurid Renaissance**: A period of great cultural and artistic flourishing in the Islamic world, centered in Herat under the Timurid dynasty.
- **Dari**: One of the two official languages of Afghanistan, a variety of Persian.
- **Saffarid dynasty**: An Iranian dynasty that ruled parts of eastern Iran and Afghanistan in the 9th century.
- **Kartid dynasty**: A Persian dynasty that ruled Herat and surrounding areas in the 13th-14th centuries.
- **Hippie trail**: An overland travel route from Europe to South Asia popular in the 1960s-70s, of which Herat was a stop.
- **Nahia**: An administrative district within an Afghan city.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The precise date of Herat's foundation is unknown.
- The exact death toll of the 1222 Mongol massacre is debated, with one account claiming 1,600,000 people.
- Casualty figures for the 1979 Herat uprising vary widely, from 3,000 to 24,000 killed.

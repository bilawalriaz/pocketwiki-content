# Christina, Queen of Sweden

## Overview
Queen Christina of Sweden (1626–1689) was a 17th-century monarch renowned for her intellectual pursuits, unconventional lifestyle, and dramatic abdication. Her reign marked Sweden's emergence as a major European power, and her personal conversion to Catholicism and subsequent life in Rome made her a pivotal figure in the Counter-Reformation and a symbol of cultural patronage.

## Timeline
- **1626** — Born in Stockholm, initially mistaken for a boy.
- **1632** — Becomes Queen at age five after her father's death in battle.
- **1644** — Declared an adult; initiates copper currency during the Torstenson War.
- **1648** — Peace of Westphalia ends the Thirty Years' War; Sweden gains territories and indemnity.
- **1650** — Coronation; declares her cousin Charles Gustav as heir.
- **1654** — Abdicates the throne in favor of Charles X Gustav.
- **1655** — Officially converts to Catholicism in Innsbruck.
- **1656** — Arrives in Rome; opens the Academy of Arcadia.
- **1657** — Orders the execution of her master of the horse, Monaldeschi, at Fontainebleau.
- **1689** — Dies in Rome; buried in the Vatican Grottoes.

## Body
### Early Life and Regency
Christina was the daughter of King Gustavus Adolphus. At his death in 1632, she became queen at age five. Her mother, Maria Eleonora, was emotionally unstable and was eventually stripped of parental rights. Christina was educated as a prince, learning languages, philosophy, and politics under Chancellor Axel Oxenstierna. This rigorous education shaped her intellectual ambitions.

### Reign and Abdication
Christina assumed personal rule in 1644. She pursued peace to end the Thirty Years' War, securing favorable terms for Sweden at the Peace of Westphalia. Her lavish spending and creation of new nobles strained state finances. She resisted pressure to marry, instead naming her cousin Charles Gustav as heir. In 1654, she abdicated, citing her desire to convert to Catholicism and her "insurmountable distaste for marriage."

### Patronage of Arts and Learning
Christina aimed to make Stockholm the "Athens of the North." She amassed a vast library and art collection, including treasures looted from Prague. She founded a newspaper and invited leading scholars like Descartes to her court. Her patronage supported theatre, music, and the arts, establishing her as a major cultural figure.

### Exile and Life in Rome
After abdicating, Christina traveled through Europe, converting to Catholicism in 1655. She settled in Rome, where she was a celebrated guest of five popes. She established an academy, patronized artists like Bernini, and opened Rome's first public theatre. Her life was marked by intellectual pursuits, political intrigue, and the controversial execution of her servant Monaldeschi in France.

### Art Collection and Legacy
Christina was a major art collector, acquiring works from the Prague loot and during her Roman exile. Her collection included masterpieces by Titian, Raphael, and Correggio. Upon her death, her collection was inherited by Cardinal Azzolino and later sold, forming the core of the Orleans Collection in France. She is one of only three women buried in the Vatican Grottoes.

## Terms
*   **House of Vasa:** The royal dynasty of Sweden from 1523 to 1654.
*   **Peace of Westphalia (1648):** The series of treaties that ended the Thirty Years' War, redrawing the political map of Europe and granting Sweden territories and influence.
*   **Riksdag of the Estates:** The representative assembly of the four estates (nobility, clergy, burghers, peasants) in Sweden.
*   **Counter-Reformation:** The period of Catholic resurgence and reform in response to the Protestant Reformation.
*   **Academy of Arcadia:** A literary and academic academy founded by Christina in Rome in 1656.
*   **Squadrone Volante ("Flying Squad"):** A loose group of progressive cardinals within the Catholic Church, including Cardinal Azzolino.
*   **Orleans Collection:** A famous art collection formed in the early 18th century, with core paintings acquired from Christina's collection.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Gender and Sexuality:** Christina's physical appearance, masculine dress, and relationships with both men and women have led to enduring debates about her gender identity and sexuality. Scholars have variously described her as lesbian, bisexual, or possibly intersex.
*   **Motives for Abdication:** While her conversion to Catholicism is cited as a primary reason, her abdication was also driven by political discontent with her rule, financial mismanagement, and her refusal to marry and produce an heir.
*   **Execution of Monaldeschi:** The circumstances and justification for her ordering the murder of Gian Rinaldo Monaldeschi in 1657 remain controversial, with accounts differing on the evidence of his betrayal.
*   **Authenticity of Conversion:** Some contemporaries and later scholars have questioned the sincerity of her Catholic conversion, pointing to her continued interest in classical mythology and freethinking ideas.

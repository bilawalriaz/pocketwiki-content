# West Asia

## Overview
West Asia, also called Western Asia or Southwest Asia, is the westernmost subregion of Asia. It is a geographically and culturally significant area that includes the Middle East's core and is considered the birthplace of major world religions and civilizations. Its strategic location, bridging Europe, Africa, and Asia, and its vast energy resources make it a region of global geopolitical and economic importance.

## Timeline
- **Late 18th–early 19th c.** — The term "Western Asia" is used as a geographical descriptor.
- **7th century AD** — Islamic conquests begin, leading to the dominance of Arabic and Turkish languages.
- **Mid-1960s** — The term "West Asia" begins to be used in contemporary geopolitics and world economy.
- **1997** — The first West Asian Games are held.
- **2001** — The West Asian Football Federation is founded.

## Body

### Definition and Boundaries
The term "West Asia" has no single, universally accepted definition. Its typical usage overlaps with, but is not identical to, the terms "Middle East," "Eastern Mediterranean," and "Near East." Different international organizations define the region's borders differently. For example, the UN Statistics Division includes Turkey and Cyprus but excludes Iran, while the UN Industrial Development Organization includes Armenia and Azerbaijan but excludes Israel and Turkey. Geographically, the region is delimited from Europe by the Turkish Straits and the Greater Caucasus watershed, and from Africa by the Isthmus of Suez.

### Geography and Climate
West Asia is a geologically active region where the African, Eurasian, and Arabian tectonic plates converge. Its topography is diverse, featuring the Anatolian Plateau, the Zagros Mountains, and the vast Rub' al Khali desert. The Dead Sea, at 418 m (1,371 ft) below sea level, is the Earth's lowest point. The climate is primarily arid and semi-arid, making water a critical resource. Major aquifers and rivers like the Tigris and Euphrates support agriculture, but water shortages are exacerbated by population growth and pollution. Two notable wind phenomena are the seasonal, dusty *sharqi* and the summer *shamal*.

### Economy and Demographics
The region's economy is diverse but heavily influenced by petroleum, as it holds over half of the world's oil reserves and about 40% of its natural gas reserves. Turkey has the largest economy, followed by Saudi Arabia and Iran. The population was estimated at about 313 million, with Iran, Turkey, Iraq, Yemen, and Saudi Arabia being the most populous countries. The population is predominantly Arab, Persian, and Turkish, with corresponding major languages. The demographic and linguistic landscape was significantly shaped by medieval Islamic conquests.

### Religion and Culture
West Asia is the birthplace of four major religions: Judaism, Christianity, Islam, and the Druze Faith. Islam is the largest religion today. The region is also home to significant ancient Christian communities and is the location of Israel, where Judaism is predominant. Minority religions include the Baháʼí Faith, Yazidism, and Zoroastrianism. Culturally, the region is organized into various sports federations, such as the West Asian Football Federation and the West Asian Basketball Association, which govern regional competitions.

## Terms
- **West Asia**: The westernmost subregion of Asia, with fluid definitions overlapping the Middle East.
- **Middle East**: A geopolitical term for a region that largely overlaps with West Asia but often includes Egypt.
- **Anatolian Plateau**: A large plateau in Turkey, bounded by the Pontus and Taurus Mountains.
- **Zagros Mountains**: A major mountain range along the border of Iran and Iraq.
- **Rub' al Khali**: One of the world's largest sand deserts, located on the southern Arabian Peninsula.
- **Dead Sea**: A salt lake on the border of the West Bank, Israel, and Jordan, noted as the Earth's lowest land point.
- **Sharqi**: A seasonal, dry, and dusty wind from the south/southeast that can cause sandstorms.
- **Shamal**: A strong summer northwesterly wind blowing over Iraq and the Persian Gulf states.

## Debates and Open Questions
The primary open question is the precise definition of "West Asia." There is no consensus among academic, UN, or other institutional bodies on which countries belong to the region. Key points of contention include the inclusion or exclusion of Turkey, Iran, Egypt, Cyprus, and the Caucasus nations (Armenia, Georgia, Azerbaijan), leading to multiple, sometimes contradictory, official definitions.

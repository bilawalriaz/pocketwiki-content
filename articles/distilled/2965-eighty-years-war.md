# Eighty Years' War

## Overview
The Eighty Years' War (c. 1566/1568–1648) was a prolonged revolt of the Habsburg Netherlands against Spanish rule. It began as a rebellion against religious persecution, centralization, and taxation, and evolved into a war for independence. The conflict concluded with the Peace of Münster, which formally recognized the Dutch Republic as a sovereign state, fundamentally reshaping the political map of Europe.

## Timeline
- **c. 1566/1568** — War begins as a revolt against Spanish rule in the Netherlands.
- **1579** — Union of Utrecht formed, uniting northern provinces in resistance.
- **1581** — Act of Abjuration formally renounces Spanish sovereignty.
- **1588** — Dutch Republic established.
- **1609** — Twelve Years' Truce signed, with Spain recognizing the Republic.
- **1621** — Truce expires; fighting resumes as part of the Thirty Years' War.
- **1648** — Peace of Münster ends the war, confirming Dutch independence.

## Body

### Causes and Early Rebellion (1566–1576)
The revolt was triggered by the Reformation, Spanish centralization, excessive taxation, and the defense of traditional Dutch privileges. After initial Spanish repression, widespread army mutinies fueled a general uprising. Under William the Silent, the Pacification of Ghent (1576) sought religious peace and joint opposition to the king, but this broad coalition ultimately failed to sustain itself.

### Formation of the Dutch Republic (1576–1588)
Despite Spanish military successes under the Duke of Parma, resistance continued. The northern provinces, through the Union of Utrecht (1579), solidified their alliance. They formally declared independence with the 1581 Act of Abjuration and established the Calvinist-dominated Dutch Republic in 1588.

### Expansion and the Twelve Years' Truce (1588–1621)
In the decade following its establishment, the Republic expanded its territory and secured diplomatic recognition from France and England in 1596. It also began building a colonial empire, initially by attacking Portuguese overseas territories. Financial strain on both sides led to the Twelve Years' Truce in 1609, a major diplomatic victory for the Republic as Spain formally recognized its independence. The truce period saw internal Dutch religious conflict, culminating in the Synod of Dort and the execution of statesman Johan van Oldenbarnevelt.

### Final Phase and Peace (1621–1648)
When the truce expired in 1621, war resumed within the broader context of the Thirty Years' War. Negotiations for a final peace began in 1646. The resulting Peace of Münster (1648) confirmed Dutch independence and territorial gains, including the closure of the Scheldt to Antwerp. Spain retained the Southern Netherlands. The treaty was ratified by most Dutch provinces, though Zeeland initially refused. The peace also formalized the Republic's independence from the Holy Roman Empire.

## Terms
*   **Habsburg Netherlands:** The collection of states in the Low Countries ruled by the Habsburg dynasty.
*   **Pacification of Ghent (1576):** An agreement seeking religious peace and joint opposition to Spanish rule among the Dutch provinces.
*   **Union of Utrecht (1579):** A treaty uniting the northern provinces of the Netherlands in a closer alliance against Spain.
*   **Act of Abjuration (1581):** A formal declaration renouncing the sovereignty of King Philip II of Spain over the Netherlands.
*   **Dutch Republic:** The state established in 1588 by the northern provinces, also known as the United Provinces.
*   **Twelve Years' Truce (1609):** A ceasefire between Spain and the Dutch Republic, during which Spain formally recognized the Republic's independence.
*   **Synod of Dort (1618–1619):** A national church council that condemned the Remonstrants (Arminians) for heresy.
*   **Peace of Münster (1648):** The treaty that ended the Eighty Years' War and recognized the Dutch Republic as an independent country.

## Debates and Open Questions
The source notes that the general rebellion initially failed to sustain itself, highlighting the challenge of uniting disparate Catholic and Protestant groups. The ratification of the Peace of Münster was contentious, with the province of Zeeland refusing to sign and the treaty being finalized without its consent. The peace was a separate agreement from the broader Peace of Westphalia treaties, which annoyed France and contravened its 1635 alliance with the Republic.

# Red Sea

## Overview
The Red Sea is a narrow, tropical sea between Africa and the Arabian Peninsula, connected to the Indian Ocean via the Bab-el-Mandeb Strait. It is a vital global trade route, home to unique coral reef ecosystems, and a site of significant geological activity as part of the Great Rift Valley. Its strategic importance and fragile environment make it a focal point for international commerce, conservation, and recent geopolitical conflict.

## Timeline
- **c. 2500 BCE** — Ancient Egyptian expeditions to Punt via the Red Sea.
- **6th century BCE** — Persian King Darius I builds a canal linking the Nile to the Red Sea.
- **1st century CE** — The *Periplus of the Erythraean Sea* documents Red Sea trade routes.
- **1869** — The modern Suez Canal opens, linking the Red Sea to the Mediterranean.
- **1967-1975** — The Suez Canal is closed following the Six-Day War.
- **2023-present** — Houthi attacks disrupt global shipping, causing a major environmental and trade crisis.

## Body

### Geography and Oceanography
The Red Sea is an inlet of the Indian Ocean, approximately 2,250 km long and 355 km at its widest. It is relatively shallow, with about 40% of its area less than 100 m deep. The sea is one of the world's warmest and saltiest, with high evaporation and minimal rainfall. Its climate is driven by seasonal monsoon winds, which reverse surface currents. The central Red Sea is nearly tideless, but tidal currents through constrictions can be strong.

### Geology and Resources
The Red Sea formed as the Arabian Peninsula rifted from Africa, a process that continues today, making it a nascent ocean. This active rift contains hot, saline brine pools and metalliferous muds. The seabed holds significant undiscovered oil and gas reserves, primarily in structural traps below Miocene salt deposits. Mineral resources in its sediments include biogenic, volcanogenic, and evaporite components.

### Ecosystem and Biodiversity
The Red Sea hosts over 1,200 fish species (10% endemic) and extensive coral reefs, some of the world's healthiest due to low human impact. Since the Suez Canal opened, over 1,000 marine species have migrated from the Red Sea to the Mediterranean. The ecosystem includes fringing reefs, offshore atolls, seagrass beds, and unique deep-sea brine pools. Key protected areas include Egypt's Ras Mohammed National Park.

### History and Trade
Ancient Egyptians, Persians, Greeks, and Romans used the Red Sea for trade. It became a crucial link in the spice trade during the Middle Ages. The 1869 opening of the Suez Canal cemented its role as a primary global shipping lane, shortening the Asia-Europe route by half. Today, 12% of global trade, including 30% of container traffic, transits the Red Sea.

### The Red Sea Crisis (2023-Present)
Since late 2023, Houthi forces in Yemen have attacked commercial and naval vessels in response to the Gaza war. This has caused a major crisis: shipping volumes through the Suez Canal dropped by 75% in 2024, forcing rerouting around Africa. This increases voyage distances, fuel consumption, and CO₂ emissions. Attacks have caused oil spills (e.g., MV Rubymar) and fertilizer leaks, threatening coral reefs and marine life. The crisis has exposed weak regional environmental governance and increased risks from naval operations and lost shipping containers.

## Terms
- **Rift Valley:** A linear-shaped lowland between several highlands or mountain ranges created by the action of a geologic rift or fault.
- **Endemic:** Native and restricted to a certain place; in biology, a species found only in a specific geographic area.
- **Fringing Reef:** A coral reef that is directly attached to a shore or borders it.
- **Atoll:** A ring-shaped coral reef, island, or series of islets, surrounding a lagoon.
- **Salinity:** The saltiness or amount of salt dissolved in a body of water.
- **Periplus:** An ancient Greek manuscript listing ports and sailing directions for a voyage.
- **Sabkha:** A coastal, supratidal, salt-flat environment common in arid regions.
- **Brine Pool:** An isolated pool of brine (very salty water) on the ocean floor.
- **PERSGA:** The Regional Organization for the Conservation of the Environment of the Red Sea and Gulf of Aden.

## Debates and open questions
- The origin of the name "Red Sea" is debated; theories include seasonal blooms of red algae (*Trichodesmium erythraeum*) or a pre-Indo-European linguistic convention where "red" meant "south."
- The long-term ecological impact of the ongoing Red Sea crisis, including the cumulative effects of oil spills, increased emissions from rerouted ships, and military sonar, is a major concern for scientists and conservationists.
- The effectiveness of regional environmental governance (like PERSGA) in mitigating damage during active conflict is questioned, highlighting a collapse in conservation capacity.

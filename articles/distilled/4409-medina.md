# Medina

## Overview
Medina, officially al-Madinah al-Munawwarah, is the second-holiest city in Islam and the capital of Medina Province in Saudi Arabia. It is considered the "cradle of Islamic culture and civilization," serving as the base from which the early Muslim community expanded. Its significance derives from being the destination of the Prophet Muhammad's migration (Hijrah) and the site of his burial.

## Timeline
- **c. 9th century BCE** — City inhabited, later known as Yathrib.
- **5th century CE** — Jewish tribes lose control to Arab tribes 'Aws and Khazraj.
- **622 CE** — Muhammad and followers migrate from Mecca (Hijrah); city renamed.
- **625 CE** — Battle of Uhud fought near Medina.
- **627 CE** — Battle of the Trench; siege of Medina by Meccans.
- **630 CE** — Muhammad conquers Mecca, but Medina remains the capital.
- **656 CE** — Caliph Uthman assassinated in Medina.
- **660–749** — Umayyad Caliphate period; city sees economic growth.
- **1517–1805** — First Ottoman period; major renovations to Prophet's Mosque.
- **1805–1811** — Brief rule by First Saudi State.
- **1840–1918** — Second Ottoman period; modernization including Hejaz Railway.
- **1916–1919** — Siege of Medina during WWI by Sharifate of Mecca.
- **1925** — Conquered by Ibn Saud, incorporated into modern Saudi Arabia.

## Body

### Pre-Islamic and Early Islamic History
Before Islam, the city was called Yathrib. By the 4th century, it was inhabited by three Jewish tribes and two Arab tribes, 'Aws and Khazraj, who were in a long-standing feud. In 622, Muhammad and approximately 70 Meccan followers (Muhajirun) migrated to Yathrib, an event that transformed the city. The local Arab tribes (Ansar) and some Jews embraced Islam, and Muhammad was agreed upon as leader. The Constitution of Medina established a cooperative community. The city was renamed Madinat al-Nabi ("City of the Prophet").

### Muhammad and the Rashidun Caliphate
Medina became the capital of the nascent Muslim state. Key events include the Battle of Uhud (625), where a Meccan army inflicted heavy losses, and the Battle of the Trench (627), where a defensive trench protected the city from a larger Meccan siege. After the siege, the Jewish tribe Banu Qurayza was judged for breaching the constitution. From Medina, Muhammad launched the conquest of Mecca in 630. The city remained the capital under the first three caliphs (Abu Bakr, Umar, Uthman). Caliph Uthman was assassinated in Medina in 656. The fourth caliph, Ali, moved the capital to Kufa, diminishing Medina's political role.

### Subsequent Islamic Rule
Medina saw numerous changes in rule. The Umayyads (660–749) invested in infrastructure and irrigation. The Abbasids (749–1258) had a variable relationship with the city, which was often governed by local Sharifs. The Mamluks of Egypt (1254–1517) controlled Medina after the fall of Baghdad. The Ottomans ruled in two periods (1517–1805, 1840–1918), building fortifications and modernizing the city, including the Hejaz Railway. The First Saudi State briefly held the city (1805–1811) before Muhammad Ali of Egypt retook it. After WWI, the Sharifate of Mecca besieged Medina (1916–1919). Ibn Saud conquered the city in 1925, and it became part of the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia.

### Modern Era and Heritage
Under Saudi rule, the city has been significantly modernized, with old walls replaced by ring roads and the Prophet's Mosque vastly expanded. The government has also demolished many historical structures, including parts of the al-Baqi' cemetery, citing religious principles against idolatry. The city's economy relies heavily on religious tourism, attracting over 7 million Hajj and Umrah visitors annually. It is served by an international airport and the Haramain High-Speed Railway connecting it to Mecca.

## Terms
- **Hijrah**: The migration of Muhammad and his followers from Mecca to Medina in 622 CE, marking the start of the Islamic calendar.
- **Ansar**: The native citizens of Medina who supported Muhammad and the Muhajirun.
- **Muhajirun**: The early Muslims who migrated with Muhammad from Mecca to Medina.
- **Sahabah**: The companions of Muhammad.
- **Ummah**: The global Muslim community or nation.
- **Qibla**: The direction Muslims face during prayer (towards the Ka'bah in Mecca).
- **Ziyarat**: A pilgrimage to visit holy sites, such as the Prophet's tomb in Medina.
- **Shirk**: The Islamic concept of idolatry or polytheism, considered a grave sin.
- **Jizya**: A tax levied on non-Muslim subjects in an Islamic state.
- **Zakat**: An obligatory alms-giving tax for Muslims.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The nature and exact date of the **Constitution of Medina** are debated; some historians view it as a single treaty, while others see it as a collage of different agreements.
- The extent of **Jewish political independence** in Medina after the Arab tribes' revolt is disputed, with some sources suggesting they became clients while others argue they retained autonomy.
- The **destruction of historical and archaeological sites** in Medina by Saudi authorities is a major point of contention, with critics labeling it "Saudi vandalism" and claiming the loss of hundreds of sites linked to early Islamic history.

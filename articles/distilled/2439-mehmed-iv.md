# Mehmed IV

## Overview
Mehmed IV (r. 1648–1687) was the Ottoman sultan whose reign saw both the empire's greatest territorial expansion in Europe and its catastrophic defeat at the Battle of Vienna. He ascended the throne as a child, and his long rule is defined by the powerful Köprülü family of grand viziers who managed state affairs, leading to a period of military revival and stability before a final, decisive collapse.

## Timeline
- **2 January 1642** — Born at Topkapı Palace, Constantinople.
- **1648** — Ascends to the throne at age six after his father Ibrahim is overthrown.
- **September 1651** — Signs the death warrant for his grandmother and regent, Kösem Sultan.
- **14 September 1656** — Köprülü Mehmed Pasha becomes Grand Vizier, beginning the Köprülü era.
- **4–5 July 1660** — A massive fire destroys two-thirds of Constantinople.
- **1669** — Conquest of Crete from Venice concludes the Cretan War.
- **1672–1673** — Successful campaigns against Poland-Lithuania, acquiring Kamaniçi Castle.
- **12 September 1683** — Ottoman forces are decisively defeated at the Battle of Vienna.
- **1683–1699** — The Great Turkish War, resulting in major Ottoman territorial losses.
- **8 November 1687** — Deposed by the army following military defeats and internal crisis.
- **6 January 1693** — Dies in Edirne Palace, where he had lived after his deposition.

## Body

### Early Life and Accession
Mehmed was born in 1642 to Sultan Ibrahim and the concubine Turhan Sultan. His father, in a fit of rage, threw the infant into a cistern, leaving him with a lifelong head scar. He became sultan in 1648 during a period of intense instability, marked by palace intrigues, naval defeats, and food riots. His grandmother, Kösem Sultan, served as regent but was executed in 1651 after being suspected of plotting against him.

### The Köprülü Era and Military Expansion
To stabilize the empire, Mehmed's mother granted sweeping powers to Grand Vizier Köprülü Mehmed Pasha in 1656. This initiated the Köprülü era, a period of exceptional administrative stability and military success under Köprülü and his successors. The empire recaptured Crete from Venice, defeated Poland, and expanded into Ukraine. This revival culminated in the empire reaching its maximum territorial extent in Europe. However, Mehmed himself was largely disengaged from governance, earning the nickname "the Hunter" (Avcı) for his passion for the sport.

### The Battle of Vienna and Deposition
In 1683, Grand Vizier Merzifonlu Kara Mustafa led a vast army to besiege Vienna, supporting a Hungarian uprising against Austrian rule. The siege ended in a catastrophic Ottoman defeat by a Holy League army led by Poland's King John III Sobieski. This loss triggered the Great Turkish War, in which the Holy League pushed the Ottomans back into the Balkans. Following further defeats, a mutiny erupted in the Ottoman army. On 8 November 1687, Mehmed was deposed by the Janissaries and other troops. He was imprisoned in Topkapı Palace before retiring to Edirne, where he died in 1693.

### Family and Personal Life
Mehmed's primary consort was the Greek-born Gülnuş Sultan, who bore his two successor sultans, Mustafa II and Ahmed III. He had several other consorts, though the historical record for some is disputed. He was known for his piety and was styled *gazi* ("holy warrior") for his reign's conquests. Physically, he was described as stocky with a sun-burnt face and a sparse beard.

## Terms
*   **Gazi**: A title meaning "holy warrior," granted to Mehmed IV for his role in the empire's military campaigns.
*   **Köprülü era**: A period of Ottoman history (1656–1687) dominated by the Köprülü family of grand viziers, known for administrative reform and military success.
*   **Grand Vizier**: The chief minister and commander of the Ottoman Empire, holding executive power.
*   **Janissaries**: Elite infantry units of the Ottoman army, forming the sultan's household troops.
*   **Haseki Sultan**: The primary consort of an Ottoman sultan, often the mother of the heir.
*   **Serdar-ı ekrem**: A title for a commander-in-chief, often held by the Grand Vizier during a campaign.
*   **Holy League**: A coalition of European powers (including Austria, Poland, and Venice) formed to fight the Ottoman Empire after 1683.

## Debates and Open Questions
The historical record regarding Mehmed IV's consorts is uncertain. The existence of several, such as Gülnar Hatun and Güneş Hatun, is controversial, with some historians speculating they may be misspellings of Gülnuş Sultan's name. Similarly, the existence of Gülbeyaz Hatun and Hatice Hatun is disputed, with violent legends about their deaths attributed to Gülnuş.

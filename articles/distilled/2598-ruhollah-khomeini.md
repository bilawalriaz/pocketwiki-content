# Ruhollah Khomeini

## Overview
Ruhollah Khomeini (1900–1989) was an Iranian Shia cleric and leader of the 1979 Iranian Revolution, which overthrew the monarchy and established an Islamic Republic. As its first Supreme Leader, he implemented a theocratic system based on his theory of *velayat-e faqih* (Guardianship of the Jurist), fundamentally reshaping Iran's politics, society, and international relations. His legacy is deeply controversial, encompassing both his role as a revolutionary icon and his implementation of authoritarian rule and severe human rights abuses.

## Timeline
- **17 May 1900** — Born in Khomeyn, Iran.
- **1963** — Leads opposition to the Shah's White Revolution; arrested, sparking the 15 Khordad movement.
- **1964** — Exiled to Turkey, then Najaf, Iraq (1965).
- **c. 1970** — Delivers lectures in Najaf outlining *velayat-e faqih*, later published as *Islamic Government*.
- **1 Feb 1979** — Returns to Iran triumphantly after the Shah's departure.
- **11 Feb 1979** — The monarchy collapses; Khomeini appoints an interim government.
- **31 Mar 1979** — Referendum approves the Islamic Republic with 98% in favor.
- **Nov 1979** — New constitution adopted, instituting the position of Supreme Leader.
- **4 Nov 1979** — Iran hostage crisis begins as students seize the U.S. embassy.
- **Sep 1980** — Iraq invades Iran, starting the Iran–Iraq War.
- **3 Jun 1989** — Khomeini dies; succeeded by Ali Khamenei after a constitutional amendment.

## Body

### Early Life and Education
Born in Khomeyn, Khomeini's father was murdered when he was two. He began religious studies young, becoming a high-ranking Shia cleric (*ayatollah*, *marja'*) in the seminaries of Arak and Qom. His education included Islamic law (*fiqh*), philosophy, mysticism (*irfan*), and poetry. He taught for decades and authored over 40 books, including early works on prayer and philosophy.

### Opposition and Exile
Khomeini emerged as a political leader in 1963, denouncing the Shah's "White Revolution" reforms as an attack on Islam and opposing a 1964 status-of-forces agreement with the U.S. This led to his arrest and, in 1964, exile to Turkey and then Iraq. In Najaf, he developed his key political theory, *velayat-e faqih*, arguing that since Sharia law is perfect, governance must be led by a leading Islamic jurist (*faqih*) to prevent injustice. While publicly emphasizing populism, he privately cultivated this theocratic vision.

### The Revolution and Consolidation of Power
Khomeini became the charismatic leader of a broad opposition coalition. From exile in Paris (1978), he directed the revolution, urging work stoppages and refusing compromise. After returning to Iran in February 1979, he swiftly outmaneuvered secular and moderate allies. He appointed his own interim government, and following a referendum, a new constitution was drafted. His supporters rewrote it to include the post of Supreme Leader (a *velayat-e faqih*), which Khomeini assumed in December 1979. He then systematically suppressed opposition groups, from liberals to leftists.

### Domestic Rule and Policies
Khomeini's rule was characterized by the Islamization of society, including the enforcement of Islamic dress codes, bans on alcohol and Western media, and the replacement of secular laws with Sharia. His government carried out mass executions of political prisoners, notably in 1981–82 and 1988. The Iran–Iraq War (1980–88) cost hundreds of thousands of lives and devastated the economy, which Khomeini famously dismissed as "for donkeys." His regime persecuted minorities, especially the Baháʼí Faith, and suppressed ethnic groups like the Kurds. However, he also issued a fatwa permitting gender reassignment surgery for transgender individuals.

### International Relations and Fatwas
Khomeini branded the U.S. the "Great Satan" and Israel the "Little Satan." The 1979–81 hostage crisis at the U.S. embassy cemented this anti-American stance. He called for the export of Islamic revolution and Muslim unity, establishing Quds Day. In 1989, he issued a fatwa calling for the death of author Salman Rushdie for blasphemy in *The Satanic Verses*. His pursuit of victory in the Iran–Iraq War, including the use of child soldiers, proved futile, and he accepted a UN-mediated truce in 1988, calling it "drinking the cup of poison."

### Death and Succession
Khomeini's health declined in his final years. He died on 3 June 1989 after a series of heart attacks. His funeral drew millions, with chaotic scenes where his coffin was briefly pulled from a helicopter. He had originally designated Grand Ayatollah Hussein-Ali Montazeri as his successor, but ousted him in 1989 after Montazeri criticized the mass executions. A constitutional amendment removed the requirement for the Supreme Leader to be a *marja'*, allowing the Assembly of Experts to elect Ali Khamenei as his successor.

## Terms
*   **Ayatollah/Marja'**: High-ranking titles in Shia Islam; a *marja'* ("source of emulation") is a grand ayatollah considered a living authority on religious law.
*   **Faqih**: An Islamic jurist expert in *fiqh* (jurisprudence).
*   **Velayat-e Faqih (Guardianship of the Jurist)**: Khomeini's political theory that the state should be governed by a leading Islamic jurist to ensure Sharia law is implemented.
*   **Sharia**: Islamic law derived from the Quran and the teachings of the Prophet Muhammad.
*   **Fatwa**: A juristic ruling on a point of Islamic law issued by a qualified cleric.
*   **Mustazafin**: A Quranic term meaning "the oppressed" or "the deprived," used by Khomeini to refer to the masses.
*   **Islamic Cultural Revolution**: The process of purging secular and Western influences from Iran's educational system after 1979.
*   **Mojtahid**: A qualified Islamic scholar capable of independent reasoning in jurisprudence.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Democracy vs. Theocracy**: Scholars debate whether Khomeini intended the Islamic Republic to be democratic. He made contradictory statements, and his theory of *velayat-e faqih* places ultimate authority in an unelected cleric.
*   **Legacy in Iran**: Politics in the Islamic Republic are often defined by competing claims to Khomeini's legacy. His image remains legally "inviolable," and his tomb is a major shrine.
*   **Contact with the U.S.**: Declassified documents suggest Khomeini sent messages to the U.S. before the revolution, pledging non-hostility, which contradicts the official narrative of unwavering defiance.
*   **Human Rights**: The scale of executions during his rule, particularly the 1988 mass killings, remains a major point of contention and has been categorized by a UN report as a genocide of political and religious minorities.

# Eminem

## Overview
Marshall Bruce Mathers III (born October 17, 1972), known professionally as Eminem, is an American rapper, songwriter, record producer, and record executive. Known for his rap flow and conscious rap, which includes political criticism and social commentary, he is widely regarded as one of the greatest and most influential rappers of all time, often credited with popularizing hip-hop in Middle America and the acceptance of white rappers. While much of his transgressive art during the late 1990s and early 2000s made him a controversial figure, Eminem has become a representation of popular angst in lower-income America. With over 220 million records sold, he is one of the best-selling music artists of all time. His career spans from the breakthrough *The Slim Shady LP* (1999) to recent releases like *The Death of Slim Shady* (2024), marked by record-breaking sales, critical acclaim, and cultural impact.

## Body

### Early Life and Influences
Born into poverty in Missouri, Eminem moved frequently before settling in Detroit at age 12. Raised primarily by his mother, he faced bullying and financial hardship. Introduced to hip-hop via the *Breakin’* soundtrack, he began rapping as a teenager under names like M&M. Influenced by artists such as Kool G Rap, Rakim, and Tupac, he developed a dense, multisyllabic rhyme style. His early work with groups like Soul Intent and D12 laid groundwork for his solo career.

### Rise to Fame (1996–2002)
After an unsuccessful debut (*Infinite*, 1996), Eminem created the violent alter ego Slim Shady, leading to the underground hit *Slim Shady EP* (1997). Signed to Aftermath, he released *The Slim Shady LP* (1999), which went triple platinum amid controversy over graphic lyrics. *The Marshall Mathers LP* (2000) sold 1.76 million copies in its first week, setting records. *The Eminem Show* (2002) continued his dominance, becoming the year’s best-selling album globally. That same year, he starred in the semi-autobiographical film *8 Mile*, and "Lose Yourself" won the Academy Award for Best Original Song in 2003, making him the first hip-hop act to ever win the award.

### Hiatus and Comeback (2004–2010)
Following *Encore* (2004), Eminem struggled with prescription drug addiction and withdrew from public life. In August 2005, he canceled the European leg of his tour and announced he had entered drug rehabilitation. He returned in 2009 with *Relapse*, followed by *Recovery* (2010), which topped charts worldwide and was the best-selling album globally in 2010. Collaborations with Jay-Z and Royce da 5’9” revitalized his profile, earning him recognition as a dominant force in modern hip-hop.

### Later Career and Legacy (2013–Present)
Albums like *Kamikaze* (2018) and *Music to Be Murdered By* (2020) maintained his chart presence. *The Death of Slim Shady* (2024) marked a return to form, though met with mixed reviews. Beyond music, he founded Shady Records, launched Shade 45 radio, starred in *8 Mile*, and engaged in political activism. His influence spans generations, with accolades including 15 Grammys, an Academy Award, and Rock Hall induction.

## Terms
- **Alter Ego**: A fictional persona adopted by an artist; Eminem’s Slim Shady expresses anger and violence.
- **Horrorcore**: A subgenre of hip-hop featuring dark, violent, or macabre themes.
- **Multisyllabic Rhyme**: Complex rhyming patterns involving multiple syllables per line.
- **RIAA Certification**: Recognition by the Recording Industry Association of America based on sales thresholds (e.g., Diamond = 10 million units).
- **Billboard 200**: A chart ranking the top albums in the U.S.
- **Conscious Rap**: Subgenre emphasizing social commentary and introspection.
- **Shock Value**: Use of provocative content to generate attention or controversy.
- **Shady Records**: Eminem’s record label, founded in 1999 with Paul Rosenberg.
- **Shade 45**: Sirius XM satellite radio channel launched by Eminem in 2004.
- **Diamond Certification**: RIAA honor awarded for selling 10 million units in the U.S.

## Debates and Open Questions
Eminem’s legacy is debated regarding his lyrical content: critics argue his use of misogyny, homophobia, and violence perpetuates harm, while supporters view it as artistic expression reflecting societal issues. His status as a white rapper succeeding in a Black-majority genre raises questions about privilege and authenticity. Additionally, his political stances—ranging from anti-Bush protests to endorsing Kamala Harris—are scrutinized for consistency and sincerity. Whether his recent work matches his earlier innovation remains a point of contention among fans and critics.

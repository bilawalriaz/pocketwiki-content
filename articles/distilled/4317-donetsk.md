# Donetsk

## Overview
Donetsk is a major industrial city in eastern Ukraine, historically the unofficial capital of the Donbas region. Founded by a Welsh businessman in 1869, it grew into a center for coal mining and steel production. Since 2014, it has been a focal point of the Russo-Ukrainian War, currently administered by Russian-backed forces as the capital of the self-proclaimed Donetsk People's Republic.

## Timeline
- **1779** — First mentioned as the Cossack sloboda of Aleksandrovka.
- **1869** — Founded by Welsh businessman John Hughes as Yuzovka (Hughesovka), with a steel plant and coal mines.
- **1917** — Attains city status.
- **1924** — Renamed Stalin under Soviet rule.
- **1929** — Renamed Stalino.
- **1932** — Becomes the administrative center of the Donetsk region.
- **1941-1943** — Occupied by Nazi Germany during World War II.
- **1961** — Renamed Donetsk during de-Stalinization.
- **1991** — 83.9% of voters support Ukrainian independence in a referendum.
- **7 April 2014** — Pro-Russian activists seize government buildings and declare the "Donetsk People's Republic."
- **11 May 2014** — A self-rule referendum is held, with organizers claiming ~90% approval for independence from Kyiv.
- **30 September 2022** — Russia formally annexs Donetsk and other Ukrainian regions, a move rejected internationally.

## Body

### Foundation and Industrial Growth
The settlement was first documented as Aleksandrovka in 1779. Its modern foundation occurred in 1869 when Welsh engineer John Hughes established a steel plant and coal mines, naming the town Yuzovka. By the early 20th century, it had ~50,000 inhabitants. The city's layout and architecture reflect its British origins, particularly in the "English Colony" district.

### Soviet Era and World War II
Under Soviet rule, the city was renamed Stalin (1924) and then Stalino (1929). Its heavy industry expanded significantly. At the start of World War II, its population was 507,000; after the war, it was reduced to 175,000 due to near-total destruction during the Nazi occupation (1941-1943). The city was rebuilt on a large scale post-war. In 1961, it was renamed Donetsk to distance it from Stalin's legacy.

### Post-Independence and Conflict
After the 1991 Ukrainian independence referendum, Donetsk modernized but faced challenges, including organized crime and industrial accidents like the deadly Zasyadko mine disasters. In 2014, following the ousting of President Yanukovych, pro-Russian separatists seized control, declaring the Donetsk People's Republic (DPR). Heavy fighting ensued, notably around Donetsk International Airport. As of June 2026, Russia has full control of the city, which it has annexed, though this is not recognized by most UN member states.

### Economy and Demographics
Donetsk is a heavily urbanized industrial hub, with an economy based on coal mining, metallurgy, and engineering. Its workforce is skilled but the city faces ecological challenges from heavy industry. According to the 2001 census, the population was predominantly Russian-speaking (87.8% Russian, 11.1% Ukrainian). The city was the fifth-largest in Ukraine before the conflict.

### Sports and Culture
Donetsk is a major sports center, home to the football club Shakhtar Donetsk (which won the UEFA Cup in 2009) and the Donbas Arena stadium, built for UEFA Euro 2012. It has hosted international events in tennis, athletics, and ice hockey. The city features landmarks like the Donetsk Opera and Ballet Theatre, Pushkin Boulevard, and the Forged Figures Park. Due to the war, most professional sports teams no longer play in the city.

## Terms
- **Donbas**: The larger economic and cultural region of the Donets Basin, of which Donetsk is the unofficial capital.
- **Conurbation**: A large urban area formed by the merging of several cities and towns, like Donetsk and Makiivka.
- **DPR (Donetsk People's Republic)**: A self-proclaimed, Russian-backed statelet formed in 2014, administering parts of Donetsk Oblast.
- **De-Stalinization**: The political reforms in the Soviet Union under Nikita Khrushchev aimed at removing Joseph Stalin's cult of personality, leading to the city's 1961 renaming.
- **Zasyadko mine**: A coal mine in Donetsk that was the site of major disasters in 2007 and 2015, killing over 100 workers each time.
- **UEFA Elite category**: A standard for football stadiums set by the Union of European Football Associations, which the Donbas Arena met.
- **Pole Vault Stars**: An annual athletics event in Donetsk founded by Serhii Bubka, where multiple world records were set.
- **Dongorpastrans**: The united municipal company controlling Donetsk's public transport system.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The legitimacy of the 2014 self-rule referendum in Donetsk is disputed; Ukraine, the EU, and the US consider it illegal.
- The ongoing Russo-Ukrainian War has created a divided administration, with outlying territories of Donetsk Oblast controlled by different sides.
- The long-term economic viability of Donetsk is questioned due to its heavy industrial base, safety issues in mining, and the impact of war, with some analysts drawing parallels to the decline of Detroit.

# Ionian Sea

## Overview
The Ionian Sea is a major arm of the Mediterranean Sea, significant for its deep waters, seismic activity, and historical role as a crossroads for ancient Greek colonization and major naval conflicts. It is bounded by Southern Italy, Albania, and Greece, and contains the Ionian Islands, which are all Greek.

## Timeline
- **c. 3000 BC** — Sea is regularly crossed by ancient Greek colonizers establishing colonies in Italy.
- **31 BC** — The Battle of Actium, a major naval engagement between Octavian and Mark Antony, occurs in the sea.
- **1861** — The western section of the Ionian Sea comes under the control of the modern state of Italy.

## Body

### Etymology
The name "Ionian" derives from the Greek word *Ionion* (Ἰόνιον), though its precise etymology is unknown. Ancient Greek writers, such as Aeschylus, linked it to the myth of Io, who swam across the sea. Other legends attribute the name to figures like Ionius, son of Adrias (the eponym for the Adriatic Sea), or Ionius, son of Dyrrhachus, whose body was cast into the water after being accidentally killed by Heracles.

### Geography and Extent
The Ionian Sea is an elongated bay connected to the Adriatic Sea in the north. Its official boundaries, as defined by the International Hydrographic Organization, run from the mouth of the Butrinto River in Albania to Cape Santa Maria di Leuca in Italy in the north, down the Greek mainland to Cape Matapan, across to Cape Passero in Sicily, and up the Italian coast. It contains the Calypso Deep, the deepest point in the Mediterranean at 5,109 m (16,762 ft). The sea is one of the most seismically active areas in the world.

### Islands and Navigation
All major islands in the eastern part of the sea belong to Greece and are collectively called the Ionian Islands, with the main ones being Corfu, Kefalonia, Zakynthos, Lefkada, and Ithaca. Ferry routes connect Greek ports like Patras, Igoumenitsa, and Piraeus with Italian ports such as Brindisi and Ancona.

### Historical Significance
The sea is famed for the seafaring adventures of the Ancient Greek hero Odysseus, king of Ithaca. It was a regular route for Greek colonists from at least the 8th century BC. During the Roman period, it was the site of the pivotal Battle of Actium in 31 BC. Control of the sea and its islands was contested in numerous naval engagements throughout the modern era, culminating in Greek control of the eastern section and Italian control of the western section since 1861.

## Terms
- **Ionian Islands**: A group of Greek islands in the eastern Ionian Sea, including Corfu, Kefalonia, and Zakynthos.
- **Calypso Deep**: The deepest known point in the Mediterranean Sea, located in the Ionian Sea.
- **Seismically active**: An area prone to frequent earthquakes and volcanic activity.
- **Eponymic**: A person after whom something is named; in this context, legendary figures giving their names to geographical features.
- **Battle of Actium**: A decisive naval battle in 31 BC that led to Octavian's victory over Mark Antony and Cleopatra.

## Debates and open questions
The precise etymology of the name "Ionian" is unknown and debated. While ancient myths link it to figures like Io, scholarly analysis notes a spelling discrepancy between the sea's name and the term for the Ionian people, suggesting the popular mythological explanations may not be linguistically accurate.

# Mother Teresa

## Overview
Mother Teresa (1910-1997) was an Albanian-Indian Catholic nun and founder of the Missionaries of Charity, a global religious congregation dedicated to serving "the poorest of the poor." She became an international symbol of charitable work, receiving the Nobel Peace Prize in 1979, but her legacy is complex, encompassing both widespread admiration and significant criticism regarding her methods and philosophy. She was canonized as a saint by the Catholic Church in 2016.

## Body

### Early Life and Religious Calling
Born into a Kosovar Albanian family in Skopje, Mother Teresa was the youngest child. Her father died when she was eight, and she became fascinated with missionary stories by age 12. In 1928, at 18, she left home to join the Sisters of Loreto in Ireland to learn English for missionary work in India. She arrived in India in 1929, began teaching in Calcutta, and took her final vows in 1937. Increasingly disturbed by the poverty around her, especially during the 1943 Bengal famine, she experienced a profound spiritual call in 1946 to leave the convent and serve the poor directly.

### Founding and Growth of the Missionaries of Charity
In 1948, Mother Teresa began her work in the slums, adopting a simple white sari as her habit. After receiving Vatican approval in 1950, she formally established the Missionaries of Charity. The congregation's first major project was the 1952 opening of Kalighat, the Home for the Dying, which provided dignified care for the destitute. The order expanded to include hospices for leprosy patients and orphanages. By the 1960s, it had spread across India and began international expansion in 1965. The congregation grew to include brothers, contemplative sisters, and lay co-workers, eventually operating hundreds of missions in over 100 countries by the 1990s.

### International Recognition and Controversy
Mother Teresa received numerous awards, including the Nobel Peace Prize in 1979, which she accepted on behalf of the poor. Her fame was amplified by Malcolm Muggeridge's 1969 documentary. However, she faced persistent criticism. Critics like Christopher Hitchens and Aroup Chatterjee argued her clinics lacked adequate medical care and pain relief, promoting a "cult of suffering." They accused her of misusing funds and glorifying poverty rather than addressing its causes. Defenders, including her biographer Navin Chawla, stated her homes were never meant to be hospitals but places of comfort for those rejected by society. She also drew criticism for her staunch opposition to abortion and contraception.

### Spiritual Life and Sainthood
Despite her public faith, private letters published posthumously revealed Mother Teresa experienced a profound "dark night of the soul," feeling spiritual emptiness and doubt for nearly 50 years. After her death, the Catholic Church initiated her canonization process. A miracle—the healing of an Indian woman's tumor—was approved for her beatification in 2003. A second miracle, the healing of a Brazilian man, was confirmed in 2015, leading to her canonization by Pope Francis in 2016.

## Terms
*   **Missionaries of Charity:** The Catholic religious congregation founded by Mother Teresa in 1950, dedicated to serving the poorest of the poor.
*   **Canonization:** The official declaration by the Catholic Church that a deceased person is a saint, requiring verification of two miracles.
*   **Beatification:** The step before canonization, recognizing a deceased person as "Blessed" and worthy of public veneration, requiring one verified miracle.
*   **Novitiate:** The period of training and probation for a candidate in a religious order before taking vows.
*   **"Call within the call":** Mother Teresa's term for her 1946 spiritual experience directing her to leave the convent and serve the poor.
*   **Palliative care:** Medical care focused on providing relief from symptoms and stress for serious illnesses, rather than curing the disease.
*   **Spiritual dryness / "Dark night of the soul":** A period of spiritual desolation or feeling of God's absence, experienced by some mystics and saints.

## Debates and Open questions
*   **Quality of Care:** A central debate concerns whether Mother Teresa's homes provided adequate medical treatment and pain relief, or if they intentionally allowed suffering as a spiritual good.
*   **Financial Management:** Critics allege donations were not used to improve facilities or medical care, while defenders state funds supported a vast global network of charity.
*   **Motivations:** Some critics, like Hitchens, argued her primary goal was religious conversion rather than social welfare, a charge strongly denied by her order.
*   **The Miracle for Canonization:** The Vatican-approved miracle for her beatification (the healing of Monica Besra) was disputed by her husband and some medical staff, who attributed her recovery to conventional medicine.

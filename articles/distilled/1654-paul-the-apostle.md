# Paul the Apostle

## Overview
Paul (born Saul of Tarsus, c. 5 – c. 64/65 AD) was a first-century Jewish Pharisee and Roman citizen who became the most influential missionary of the Apostolic Age. Though not one of the Twelve Apostles and having never met Jesus during his lifetime, Paul claimed a direct revelation of the risen Christ (c. 31–36 AD) that transformed him from a persecutor of Hellenistic Jewish Christians into the "Apostle to the Gentiles." He founded communities across Asia Minor and Europe (mid-40s to mid-50s AD) and argued successfully that Gentile converts need not observe Mosaic Law (circumcision, dietary rules) to join the covenant, redefining Christian identity around faith in Christ rather than ethnic boundary markers. Thirteen New Testament letters bear his name; seven are universally accepted as authentic, providing the earliest written Christian theology. His teachings on justification by faith, participation in Christ’s death and resurrection, and the inclusion of the Gentiles shaped Western Christianity (Augustine, Luther, Calvin) and remain central to Christian doctrine and worship.

## Timeline
- **c. 5 BC – 5 AD** — Born in Tarsus (Cilicia) to a devout Pharisaic family; Roman citizen by birth.
- **c. 30s AD** — Educated in Jerusalem under Gamaliel; works as a tentmaker; persecutes Hellenistic ("Hellenist") Jewish Christians.
- **c. 31–36 AD** — Conversion on road to Damascus: vision of Jesus, blindness, healing by Ananias, baptism.
- **c. 35–36 AD** — First Jerusalem visit (15 days with Peter; meets James).
- **c. 45–46 AD** — Famine relief visit to Jerusalem with Barnabas from Antioch.
- **c. 46–49 AD** — First missionary journey (Antioch → Cyprus → southern Asia Minor → Antioch).
- **c. 49 AD** — Council of Jerusalem: Apostles accept Paul’s Gentile mission; circumcision not required.
- **c. 49–52 AD** — Second missionary journey (with Silas, then Timothy; vision calls them to Macedonia; Philippi, Thessalonica, Athens, Corinth).
- **c. 50–51 AD** — 18-month stay in Corinth (Gallio inscription dates proconsulship).
- **c. 53–56 AD** — Third missionary journey (Ephesus ~3 years; Macedonia/Greece; writes Romans).
- **57 AD** — Final Jerusalem visit; arrested after temple riot; held in Caesarea two years (Felix, then Festus).
- **c. 60 AD** — Appeals to Caesar; shipwrecked on Malta; arrives in Rome for house arrest.
- **c. 62–64 AD** — Two years preaching in Rome under guard (Acts ends here).
- **c. 64–67 AD** — Execution in Rome (beheading per tradition) under Nero, likely after Great Fire (64 AD).

## Body

### Names and Identity
Paul’s Jewish name was **Saul** (Hebrew *Sha’ul*), likely honoring King Saul of the tribe of Benjamin. **Paulus** (Latin, "small") was his Roman cognomen, not adopted at conversion but used for Greco-Roman audiences. Acts 13:9 first records the switch to "Paul" on Cyprus. He consistently calls himself Paul in his letters (28 times). As a Roman citizen by birth (Acts 22:28), he likely bore the *tria nomina* (e.g., *Gaius Iulius Paulus*), though his full name is unknown. His dual naming reflects his missionary strategy: becoming "all things to all people" (1 Cor 9:19–23).

### Sources and Historiography
Primary sources are the **Pauline epistles** (seven undisputed: Romans, 1–2 Corinthians, Galatians, Philippians, 1 Thessalonians, Philemon) and **Acts of the Apostles** (c. 80–90 AD). The epistles are preferred for theology and chronology; Acts provides travel narrative but omits Paul’s death. Scholars debate the historical reliability of Acts versus the letters (e.g., F.C. Baur’s "Tübingen School" saw sharp conflict between Paul and the Twelve; modern consensus sees more continuity). Extra-biblical witnesses begin with Clement of Rome (c. 96 AD), Ignatius, Polycarp, and Eusebius (4th c.). Apocryphal Acts (2nd–4th c.) add legendary details (e.g., *Acts of Paul and Thecla*).

### Early Life and Conversion
Born in Tarsus, a major Hellenistic city with a famed academy, Paul was "a Hebrew of Hebrews, a Pharisee" (Phil 3:5). He studied in Jerusalem under **Gamaliel** (Acts 22:3), though not as a Hillelite scholar. He persecuted Greek-speaking "Hellenist" Christians (Acts 6–7), likely due to their anti-Temple stance. His conversion (dated 31–36 AD by his own "revelation of Jesus Christ," Gal 1:12) involved a vision of the risen Jesus on the Damascus road, temporary blindness, and healing by **Ananias of Damascus**. Paul emphasizes the event was pure grace, not human reasoning (Gal 1:15–16). Some scholars (Segal, Boyarin) link his heavenly ascent (2 Cor 12) to Jewish *Merkabah* mysticism; others (Churchill) dispute the fit.

### Missionary Journeys and the Gentile Mission
Paul undertook three major journeys (Acts 13–21), traveling ~12,000 km by land and ~8,000 km by sea.
- **First (c. 46–49 AD)**: Commissioned from Antioch with Barnabas; Cyprus (blinds Elymas), Pisidian Antioch (turns to Gentiles after Jewish opposition).
- **Council of Jerusalem (c. 49 AD)**: "Pillars" (Peter, James, John) endorse Paul’s Law-free Gentile mission (Gal 2:1–10; Acts 15). The **Incident at Antioch** follows: Paul publicly rebukes Peter for withdrawing from table fellowship with Gentiles; Barnabas sides with Peter. Outcome disputed (Paul claims vindication; L.M. White argues Paul left Antioch *persona non grata*).
- **Second (c. 49–52 AD)**: Split with Barnabas over John Mark; Silas joins. Vision of Macedonian man redirects mission to Europe (Philippi, Thessalonica, Athens, Corinth). In Corinth (18 months, dated by **Gallio inscription**), meets **Priscilla and Aquila**, tentmakers and key coworkers.
- **Third (c. 53–56 AD)**: Three years in Ephesus (miracles, riots over Artemis); writes 1–2 Corinthians, Romans. Collects offering for Jerusalem poor.

### Arrest, Trials, and Rome
In 57 AD, Paul delivers the collection in Jerusalem. Accused of bringing Gentiles into the Temple, he is seized, rescued by Roman tribune, and speaks to the crowd. A plot on his life prompts transfer to **Caesarea** under Governor **Felix** (two years), then **Festus**. Paul appeals to Caesar (citizen’s right). Voyage to Rome includes shipwreck on **Malta** (Acts 27–28). In Rome (c. 60–62 AD), he lives under house arrest, preaching freely. Acts ends here; tradition (Clement, Eusebius, Tertullian) places his martyrdom by beheading under Nero (64–67 AD), possibly alongside Peter.

### Writings: Authenticity and Dating
- ****Undisputed (7)**** — Romans, 1–2 Corinthians, Galatians, Philippians, 1 Thessalonians, Philemon Authentic; dictated by Paul c. 48–58 AD.
- ****Disputed "Deutero-Pauline" (2)**** — Colossians, 2 Thessalonians Debated; possibly followers using Paul’s material.
- ****Disputed "Trito-Pauline" / Pastoral (3)**** — 1–2 Timothy, Titus Widely considered pseudepigraphical (late 1st–early 2nd c.).
- ****Ephesians**** — Often grouped with Pastorals; pseudepigraphical.
- ****Hebrews**** — Anonymous; Pauline authorship rejected since Origen (3rd c.).

Authentic letters address specific crises (e.g., Lord’s Supper in Corinth), offering situational theology rather than systematic treatises. They are the earliest Christian documents (predating Gospels).

### Theology: Christ, Atonement, Law, and Eschatology
- **Christology**: Jesus is pre-existent Lord (*kyrios*), Davidic Messiah, Son of God by resurrection (Rom 1:3–4). "Binitarian" devotion: prayer to Jesus, baptism in his name, Lord’s Supper, prophetic oracles (Hurtado).
- **Atonement**: Christ’s death as *expiation* (removes sin) and *propitiation* (appeases God). **Participationist model** (Sanders): believers die/rise with Christ in baptism, escaping sin’s power and receiving the Spirit. **New Perspective** (Sanders, Dunn, Wright): "Justification by faith" primarily solves the *boundary problem*—how Gentiles enter God’s covenant without Torah—rather than individual conscience.
- **Law and Judaism**: Paul upholds "Divine Law" (ethical core) but rejects "Ritual Law" (circumcision, Sabbath, diet) as entrance requirements. Gentiles come *as Gentiles* (Zech 8:20–23). This makes the Jew/Gentile schism inevitable, contrary to Paul’s intent (Rom 9–11 affirms Israel’s enduring covenant).
- **Eschatology**: Initially imminent (1 Thess 4:15–17); later letters suggest delay (2 Thess, Romans). Resurrection of dead, transformation of living into imperishable bodies. Not static: develops from "soon" to "maybe not in my lifetime" (Wright).

### Women, Household Codes, and Homosexuality
- **Women**: Disputed Pastorals (1 Tim 2:11–15) used to restrict leadership. Authentic letters show women as coworkers: **Phoebe** (deacon/benefactor, Rom 16:1), **Priscilla** (named first in 5/7 mentions, risked life for Paul), **Junia** ("prominent among apostles," Rom 16:7). Gal 3:28 ("no male and female") frames equality in Christ. **Haustafel** (household codes, Eph 5–6, Col 3) likely "Christianizes" patriarchal Roman norms; some scholars (Staggs, Barr) argue Paul subverts them by addressing wives first and centering Christ as ultimate authority.
- **Homosexuality**: Rom 1:26–27 and 1 Cor 6:9–10 traditionally read as condemnation. 1 Tim 1:10 (pseudepigraphical) adds "men who practice homosexuality." Modern debates center on *arsenokoitai* meaning and cultural context (pederasty vs. orientation).

### Influence and Reception
- **Augustine**: Conversion via Rom 13; grace, predestination, original sin from Paul.
- **Luther**: *Sola fide* from Rom 1:17; Romans as "perfect gospel."
- **Calvin**: Romans as key to all Scripture.
- **Modern**: Barth (Romans) – God’s "No" to human religion; Bultmann – existential reading; New Perspective – covenantal nomism vs. legalism.
- **Critics**: Marcion (Paul as *only* true apostle, rejects OT God); Nietzsche, Tolstoy, Jefferson ("first corrupter of Jesus’ doctrines"); Islamic tradition (Ibn Hazm, al-Faruqi) – Paul introduces *shirk* (incarnation, atonement) and Hellenistic corruption.
- **Jewish reclamation**: Boyarin, Fredriksen, Eisenbaum read Paul as Torah-observant Jew ("Paul within Judaism").

### Physical Appearance and Remains
New Testament silent. Apocryphal *Acts of Paul*: "small stature, bald, crooked legs, eyebrows meeting, hooked nose, ruddy face." *Philopatris*: "three cubits" (~4'6"). **Remains**: Tradition places burial on Via Ostiensis. Constantine built first basilica (4th c.); current **Basilica of Saint Paul Outside the Walls** (19th c.). 2002: sarcophagus inscribed "PAULO APOSTOLO MART" found beneath altar. 2009: Benedict XVI announced radiocarbon dating of bone fragments (1st–2nd c.), plus purple/gold linen, incense, blue textiles. Vatican scientists urge caution: dating consistent but not conclusive. Earliest icon (early 4th c.) found in Catacomb of Saint Thekla (2010).

## Terms
- ****Apostle to the Gentiles**** — Paul’s self-designation (Rom 11:13); commissioned to bring the gospel to non-Jews without requiring Torah observance.
- ****Christology**** — Doctrine of Christ’s person; Paul’s is "high" (pre-existent Lord, Son of God) and "binitarian" (Jesus receives divine honors).
- ****Deutero-Pauline**** — Letters possibly written by Paul’s disciples after his death (Colossians, 2 Thessalonians), developing his thought.
- ****Haustafel**** — "Household code" (Eph 5:21–6:9; Col 3:18–4:1); reciprocal duties for wives/husbands, children/parents, slaves/masters.
- ****Justification by faith**** — God declares sinners righteous through trust in Christ, not "works of Torah" (boundary markers); central to Reformation theology.
- ****Merkabah mysticism**** — Jewish visionary tradition (Ezek 1 throne-chariot); some link Paul’s "third heaven" ascent (2 Cor 12) to this.
- ****New Perspective on Paul**** — Scholarly shift (Sanders, Dunn, Wright): Paul opposes Torah as ethnic boundary, not legalistic merit-earning; Judaism was grace-based.
- ****Pseudepigraphical**** — Written under a famous name (Paul) by a later author; applies to Ephesians, Pastorals (1–2 Tim, Titus) per most scholars.
- ****Trito-Pauline**** — Third-generation Pauline school writings (Pastorals), reflecting developed church structure.
- ****τὸν ἔχοντα γνῶσιν**** — "The one having knowledge" (1 Cor 8:10); Gnostics valued Paul’s claim to revelatory *gnosis*.

## Debates and open questions
1. **Acts vs. Letters**: Does Acts accurately reflect Paul’s theology (e.g., justification, Spirit) and chronology (three Jerusalem visits vs. two in Galatians)?
2. **Conversion vs. Call**: Was Paul commissioned to the Gentiles *at* Damascus (Acts) or only later in Antioch (Gal 1:15–2:2)?
3. **Pistis Christou**: "Faith *in* Christ" (objective genitive) or "faithfulness *of* Christ" (subjective genitive)? Alters atonement logic.
4. **Authenticity of Colossians/Ephesians/2 Thessalonians**: Genuine, Deutero-Pauline, or later? Impacts theology of cosmic Christ and church structure.
5. **"In Christ" participation**: Metaphorical union or mystical/ontological reality? (Schweitzer vs. Bultmann vs. Campbell).
6. **Romans 7**: Autobiographical (pre-conversion struggle) or rhetorical *prosopopoeia* (Adam/Israel)?
7. **Paul and Judaism**: "Paul within Judaism" (Boyarin, Eisenbaum) vs. "Paul against Judaism" (traditional Lutheran) vs. "Paul beyond Judaism" (Fredriksen).
8. **Spain journey**: Did Paul reach Spain after Rome (Clement, Muratorian Fragment, Chrysostom) or die in Rome?
9. **Women’s silence (1 Cor 14:34–35)**: Interpolation? Culturally specific? Genuine but limited?
10. **Historical Paul vs. Canonical Paul**: How much of the "real" Paul is recoverable behind the theological portrait in Acts and the disputed letters?

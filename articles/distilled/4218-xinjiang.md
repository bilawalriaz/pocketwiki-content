# Xinjiang

## Overview
Xinjiang, officially the Xinjiang Uygur Autonomous Region (XUAR), is a vast, arid region in northwest China at the crossroads of Central and East Asia. Its strategic location, rich resources, and complex demographic history have made it a focal point of imperial control, cultural exchange, and contemporary geopolitical tension. The region's identity and governance have been shaped by centuries of migration, conflict, and administrative changes, culminating in its current status as an autonomous region within the People's Republic of China (PRC).

## Timeline
- **c. 1800 BC** — Earliest Tarim mummies (Caucasoid type) appear in the region.
- **60 BCE** — Han China establishes the Protectorate of the Western Regions to secure the Silk Road.
- **640 CE** — Tang Dynasty establishes the Anxi Protectorate to control the region.
- **10th–11th c.** — The Kara-Khanid Khanate converts to Islam, beginning the region's Islamization.
- **1759** — Qing dynasty completes conquest of the Dzungar Khanate and Tarim Basin, merging them as "Xinjiang."
- **1884** — Qing dynasty formally establishes Xinjiang as a province.
- **1933** — Short-lived First East Turkestan Republic is declared in Kashgar.
- **1944** — Soviet-backed Second East Turkestan Republic is established in the Ili region.
- **1949** — People's Liberation Army enters Xinjiang; it becomes part of the PRC.
- **1955** — Xinjiang's status changes from a province to the Xinjiang Uygur Autonomous Region.
- **2014** — Chinese government launches the "Strike Hard Campaign Against Violent Terrorism."
- **2017** — Authorities begin operating internment camps for Uyghurs and other Muslims.

## Body

### Names and Identity
The region has been known by many names, including Altishahr ("the six cities"), Chinese Turkestan, and Xiyu ("Western Regions"). The name "Xinjiang" (meaning "new frontier") was officially adopted by the Qing dynasty in 1878 after merging the northern Dzungaria and southern Tarim Basin. The name was contested in 1955 when Uyghur officials objected to its colonial connotations, leading to the compromise name "Xinjiang Uygur Autonomous Region."

### Geography and Demographics
Xinjiang is China's largest province-level division, spanning over 1.6 million km². It is divided by the Tian Shan mountain range into the Dzungarian Basin in the north and the Tarim Basin in the south, which contains the Taklamakan Desert. Only about 9.7% of its land is habitable. The population includes Uyghurs, Han Chinese, Kazakhs, Kyrgyz, and other groups. Historically, Uyghurs were the majority in the southern Tarim Basin, while Han Chinese settlement in the north has increased significantly since the 1950s, altering the demographic balance.

### Historical Control and Conflict
The region was a crossroads for empires, including the Han, Tang, Mongol, and Qing dynasties. The Qing conquest in the 18th century led to the near-eradication of the Dzungar people. After the Qing fell, the region saw instability, including the Dungan Revolt and the rule of Yakub Beg. The Republic of China and later the PRC sought to integrate Xinjiang, facing resistance that included the East Turkestan independence movement. Since 1949, the Chinese government has promoted Han migration and economic development, while suppressing separatism.

### Contemporary Governance and Human Rights
The Chinese government administers Xinjiang through a parallel party-state system, with the Xinjiang Production and Construction Corps (XPCC) playing a key economic and paramilitary role. Since 2014, the "Strike Hard Campaign" has been justified as counter-terrorism but has been widely criticized for mass detention, surveillance, and cultural suppression. International observers and human rights organizations have documented abuses, with some characterizing the policies as genocide against Uyghurs and other minorities. The government maintains these measures are necessary for stability and development.

### Economy
Xinjiang's economy is driven by agriculture (notably cotton), mining (coal, oil, gas), and increasingly, tourism. It is a major producer of polysilicon for solar panels. The XPCC contributes significantly to the regional GDP. Development policies aim to reduce economic disparity with eastern China and have led to major infrastructure projects, including highways and railways connecting Xinjiang to Central Asia and eastern China.

## Terms
- **Autonomous Region**: A provincial-level administrative division in China designated for ethnic minorities, though it does not have the legal right to secede.
- **Xinjiang Production and Construction Corps (XPCC)**: A state-owned economic and paramilitary organization that plays a major role in Xinjiang's economy and governance.
- **East Turkestan**: The name used by some Uyghur nationalists for an independent state they seek to establish in Xinjiang.
- **Strike Hard Campaign**: A Chinese government policy launched in 2014 targeting separatism, terrorism, and extremism in Xinjiang.
- **Tarim Basin**: The large, arid southern basin of Xinjiang, historically home to Uyghur populations and the Taklamakan Desert.
- **Dzungarian Basin**: The northern basin of Xinjiang, historically inhabited by Dzungar Mongols and now with a higher Han Chinese population.
- **Silk Road**: The ancient network of trade routes that passed through Xinjiang, connecting China with Central Asia and the West.
- **Khojas**: A group of Naqshbandi Sufi leaders who ruled parts of the Tarim Basin before the Qing conquest.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Historical Status**: China asserts Xinjiang has been part of China since ancient times, while independence activists argue it was invaded by the CCP in 1949.
- **Human Rights**: The Chinese government describes its policies as counter-terrorism and vocational education, while critics and international bodies have documented mass detention and cultural suppression, with some alleging genocide.
- **Demographic Change**: The shift in ethnic composition due to Han migration is viewed by the government as economic development but by Uyghur activists as a threat to their cultural survival.
- **Economic Development**: The benefits of Xinjiang's resource wealth and infrastructure projects are debated, with concerns over equitable distribution and the role of forced labor in industries like cotton and polysilicon production.

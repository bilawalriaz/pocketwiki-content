# Patriarch Nikon of Moscow

## Overview
Patriarch Nikon (1605–1681) was the seventh Patriarch of Moscow and a dominant figure in 17th-century Russia. He is most significant for his ambitious liturgical reforms, which aimed to align Russian Orthodox practices with contemporary Greek models but instead triggered a permanent schism in the church known as the Raskol (Old Believers). His political power, which at times rivaled that of Tsar Alexei I, ultimately led to his downfall and condemnation.

## Timeline
- **7 May 1605** — Born Nikita Minin in the village of Veldemanovo.
- **1624** — Leaves Makaryev Monastery, returns home, marries, and becomes a parish priest.
- **c. 1635** — After his children's deaths, persuades his wife to become a nun and becomes a monk, taking the name Nikon.
- **1643** — Becomes abbot of Kozheozersky Monastery.
- **1646** — Meets Tsar Alexei I, is appointed archimandrite of Novospassky Monastery.
- **1649** — Becomes Metropolitan of Great Novgorod.
- **1 August 1652** — Elected Patriarch of Moscow after intense persuasion.
- **1654** — Synod decides to revise church service-books to follow Greek models.
- **1658** — Publicly strips off patriarchal vestments and leaves Moscow for New Jerusalem Monastery.
- **December 1667** — Tried and condemned by the Great Moscow Synod; reduced to the rank of simple monk.
- **17 August 1681** — Dies while being transported back to Moscow under a partial pardon.

## Body

### Early Life and Rise to Power
Born to a Mordvin peasant, Nikon experienced a difficult childhood marked by his stepmother's mistreatment. He entered monastic life early, but returned to secular life to marry and serve as a priest. After the death of his children, he saw it as a sign to re-enter monasticism permanently. His eloquence and administrative skill caught the attention of Tsar Alexei I, leading to rapid promotion. He became a leading member of the "Zealots of Piety," a reformist circle advocating for religious renewal.

### Liturgical Reforms and the Raskol
As Patriarch, Nikon launched bold reforms to correct what he believed were errors in Russian Orthodox practice. Consulting Greek and Kievan scholars, he concluded that Muscovite service-books and icons had deviated from ancient Constantinopolitan models. He mandated revisions to align with contemporary Greek usage, which he considered more authentic. This included changes to liturgical rites, the manner of making the sign of the cross, and iconographic styles. These reforms were enforced with severity, including the destruction of non-compliant icons. The changes were deeply unpopular with traditionalists, leading to a lasting schism (Raskol) and the emergence of the Old Believers, who rejected the reforms as heretical.

### Political Power and Downfall
From 1652 to 1658, Nikon wielded immense political power, often acting as co-ruler with Tsar Alexei, especially during the Tsar's military campaigns. He championed the supremacy of the church in spiritual matters and protested the 1649 legal code that subordinated the clergy to the state. This created powerful enemies among the nobility. In 1658, in a dramatic gesture, Nikon left Moscow, hoping to demonstrate his indispensability. The move backfired; the Tsar grew cold, and Nikon's absence led to a prolonged crisis. After years without a patriarch, a pan-Orthodox synod in 1667 condemned Nikon for various offenses, including reviling the Tsar. He was stripped of his sacerdotal functions and exiled to a remote monastery, where he remained until his death.

## Terms
*   **Patriarch of Moscow and All Rus':** The highest-ranking bishop and primate of the Russian Orthodox Church.
*   **Raskol (Schism):** The division in the Russian Orthodox Church caused by Nikon's reforms, separating the official church from the Old Believers.
*   **Old Believers:** The conservative faction that rejected Nikon's liturgical reforms, adhering to the older pre-reform practices.
*   **Zealots of Piety:** A reformist circle in the 17th-century Russian church advocating for religious renewal and stricter observance.
*   **Synod:** A council of church officials convened to discuss and decide on matters of doctrine and discipline.
*   **Sobornoye Ulozheniye:** The Russian Legal Code of 1649, which Nikon opposed for reducing the status of the clergy.
*   **Old Byzantine style:** The architectural style Nikon promoted for new churches, replacing the traditional Russian tent-like style.
*   **Sacerdotal functions:** The duties and powers of a priest or bishop, particularly the authority to perform sacraments.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Authenticity of Reforms:** Scholars debate whether Nikon's reforms truly restored ancient practices or simply imposed newer Greek models onto an older, venerable Russian tradition. Research suggests the unrevised Muscovite books were actually older than the Greek books Nikon used as a model.
*   **Nature of Nikon's Power:** Historians analyze the precise nature of the relationship between Nikon and Tsar Alexei, debating whether Nikon was a true co-ruler or an overreaching subordinate.
*   **Motives for Condemnation:** The synod's reasons for deposing Nikon are complex; while he was accused of personal misconduct, the underlying conflict was a profound struggle over the relationship between church and state authority in Russia.

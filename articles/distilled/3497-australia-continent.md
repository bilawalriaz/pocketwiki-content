# Australia (continent)

## Overview
The continent of Australia, also known as Sahul or Meganesia, is the smallest, flattest, and oldest landmass on Earth. It comprises mainland Australia, Tasmania, and the island of New Guinea, which were connected as a single landmass during the Pleistocene ice age. Its long isolation led to unique ecosystems and the development of distinct indigenous cultures, while modern history is marked by European colonization and the emergence of diverse nations.

## Timeline
- **c. 65,000–50,000 years ago** — Indigenous Australians arrive on the continent.
- **c. 42,000–48,000 years ago** — Papuan habitation begins in New Guinea.
- **18,000–10,000 years ago** — Rising sea levels separate the Sahul landmass into modern Australia, New Guinea, and Tasmania.
- **1606** — Dutch navigator Willem Janszoon makes the first documented European landing on Australia.
- **1770** — British explorer James Cook charts the eastern coastline.
- **1788** — The First Fleet establishes a British colony at Sydney Cove.
- **1901** — The Commonwealth of Australia is proclaimed.
- **1942** — The Bombing of Darwin is the largest foreign attack on Australia.
- **1975** — Papua New Guinea achieves independence.

## Body

### Terminology and Definition
The continent is geographically defined as the landmasses on the Australian Plate: mainland Australia, Tasmania, and New Guinea. It is distinguished from the country of Australia. The name "Sahul" derives from the Sahul Shelf, part of its continental shelf. The term "Oceania" is a broader regional concept used by the United Nations, which includes Australia, New Zealand, and Pacific islands, but excludes Southeast Asian nations like Indonesia and the Philippines. Archaeological terms for the ancient landmass have evolved from "Australasia" to "Greater Australia" and now "Sahul" or "Meganesia."

### Indigenous Peoples and Early History
Indigenous Australians, including Aboriginal Australians and Torres Strait Islanders, are the original inhabitants, having arrived at least 50,000 years ago. Evidence from Lake Mungo indicates early religious ritual. Papuan habitation began between 42,000 and 48,000 years ago. For millennia, legends of a hypothetical southern continent, *Terra Australis Incognita*, existed in European thought.

### European Exploration and Colonization
Dutch explorers were the first Europeans to sight and land on Australia in the 17th century. British explorer James Cook claimed the eastern coast in 1770. The British colony of New South Wales was established in 1788. In the 19th century, British control extended to New Guinea, which was later administered by Australia. The Commonwealth of Australia federated in 1901.

### Modern History and Politics
Papua New Guinea became self-governing in 1973 and independent in 1975. The western half of New Guinea was incorporated into Indonesia, receiving "Special Autonomy" status in 2001. Australia is a federal parliamentary constitutional monarchy. Papua New Guinea is also a Commonwealth realm, with its governor-general elected by the legislature.

### Geography and Climate
The continent is the lowest, flattest, and oldest landmass, with no active volcanism on the mainland. Its climate ranges from tropical in the north and New Guinea to arid and semi-arid in the interior, with temperate conditions in the southeast. New Guinea's highlands experience snowfall.

### Ecology
Long isolation fostered high endemism: 83% of Australian mammals, 89% of reptiles, and 93% of amphibians are unique. Marsupials and monotremes dominate. The flora is adapted to aridity and fire, with sclerophyllous plants like eucalyptus and acacia. New Guinea's flora is a mix of tropical Asian and Australasian species. The continent's infertile soils and stable climate allowed for specialized evolution.

### Economy and Demographics
Australia has a highly developed, service-based economy and is a major global economy. Papua New Guinea's economy is developing and resource-rich. Since 1945, Australia has received over 7 million immigrants, creating a multicultural society. Papua New Guinea is one of the world's most culturally and linguistically diverse nations, with over 820 indigenous languages.

## Terms
- **Sahul**: The name for the Pleistocene landmass comprising Australia, New Guinea, and Tasmania, derived from the Sahul Shelf.
- **Continental Shelf**: The submerged edge of a continent; the Sahul Shelf connects Australia and New Guinea.
- **Endemic**: A species native and restricted to a certain place.
- **Sclerophyllous**: Plants with hard, leathery leaves adapted to reduce water loss, typical of Australian flora.
- **Marsupial**: A mammal, like a kangaroo, that carries its young in a pouch.
- **Monotreme**: An egg-laying mammal, such as the platypus.
- **Commonwealth Realm**: A sovereign state where King Charles III is the monarch and head of state.
- **Tok Pisin**: An English-based creole language and one of the official languages of Papua New Guinea.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The precise timing of the first human arrival in Australia is debated, with evidence pointing to at least 50,000 years ago, and possibly 65,000 years ago.
- The definition and boundaries of "Oceania" versus the "continent of Australia" vary among geographical, political, and statistical organizations.
- The implementation of "Special Autonomy" for Papua province in Indonesia is partial and often criticized.

# Somaliland

## Overview
Somaliland is a self-declared sovereign state in the Horn of Africa, located on the southern coast of the Gulf of Aden. It is internationally recognised as part of Somalia but has functioned as an independent, self-governing entity since 1991, following a war of independence. Its status remains a central issue in regional politics, with its government seeking formal international recognition while maintaining informal ties with several countries.

## Timeline
- **c. 10,000 years ago** — Area inhabited during the Neolithic age; rock art created.
- **14th–15th centuries** — Zeila-based Adal Sultanate established in the region.
- **Mid-18th century** — Isaaq Sultanate established as a successor state to Adal.
- **1884–1886** — Britain signs treaties with clans, establishing the Somaliland Protectorate.
- **26 June 1960** — British Somaliland gains independence as the State of Somaliland.
- **1 July 1960** — State of Somaliland unites with Italian Somaliland to form the Somali Republic.
- **1988–1991** — Somali National Movement (SNM) wages war of independence against Barre regime.
- **May 1991** — SNM-led conference in Burao declares independence, restoring sovereignty.
- **31 May 2001** — Constitutional referendum ratifies independence from Somalia (97.1% vote in favour).
- **26 December 2025** — Israel becomes the first UN member state to formally recognise Somaliland.

## Body

### Early History and Sultanates
The region has a long history of habitation, with significant Neolithic rock art sites like Laas Geel. Ancient trading networks connected it to Egypt and the wider Indian Ocean world. In the early Islamic period, Somali Muslim kingdoms emerged, including the Adal Sultanate. By the 18th century, successor states like the Isaaq Sultanate, governed by the Guled dynasty, flourished. The Isaaq Sultanate is considered the pre-colonial predecessor to modern Somaliland.

### Colonial Era and Independence
British influence began with treaties signed with Somali clans between 1884 and 1886, creating the Somaliland Protectorate. The territory was administered as part of British India until 1898, then by the Foreign and Colonial Offices. Resistance to colonial rule included the 20-year Dervish War (1900–1920) and the 1945 Sheikh Bashir Rebellion. The protectorate gained independence on 26 June 1960 as the State of Somaliland, which was recognised by 35 states during its brief sovereignty.

### Union with Somalia and Civil War
Five days after independence, Somaliland voluntarily united with Italian Somaliland to form the Somali Republic. The union proved problematic, with northerners feeling marginalised. A 1961 referendum on a new constitution was boycotted in the north. After a 1969 military coup, the regime of Siad Barre increasingly targeted the Isaaq clan, culminating in a brutal crackdown on the SNM-led insurgency in 1988. This campaign, described by a UN investigation as genocide, killed tens of thousands and displaced hundreds of thousands. The Barre regime collapsed in 1991.

### Restoration of Sovereignty and Governance
In May 1991, the SNM-led conference in Burao declared the restoration of Somaliland's sovereignty. The territory has since established a democratic system of government with a bicameral parliament, regular elections, and a constitution ratified by a 2001 referendum. The government is a power-sharing coalition of clans, with three political parties allowed nationally. It maintains a military and police force but faces challenges like youth unemployment and limited international aid due to its lack of recognition.

### International Status and Disputes
Somaliland is a member of the Unrepresented Nations and Peoples Organization. It has informal ties with several countries, including Ethiopia, which signed a 2024 memorandum of understanding for port access in exchange for eventual recognition. On 26 December 2025, Israel became the first UN member state to formally recognise Somaliland. The territory disputes control of its eastern regions, particularly Sool and Sanaag, with the neighbouring Somali state of Puntland and the local SSC-Khatumo administration, which does not recognise Somaliland's sovereignty.

## Terms
- **Isaaq Sultanate**: A Somali kingdom that ruled parts of the Horn of Africa in the 18th and 19th centuries, considered the pre-colonial predecessor to modern Somaliland.
- **Dervish War (Somaliland Campaign)**: A series of military expeditions (1900–1920) between the British and the Dervish forces led by Mohammed Abdullah Hassan.
- **SNM (Somali National Movement)**: A rebel group that fought the Barre regime and led the movement for Somaliland's independence in 1991.
- **Shir**: A traditional Somali meeting or conference where elders and leaders discuss and decide on community matters.
- **Clan**: A patrilineal social and political unit central to Somali society, often divided into sub-clans.
- **Guban**: A semi-desert coastal plain parallel to the Gulf of Aden.
- **Haud**: An important grazing area for livestock in the interior plateau.
- **ZAAD**: A mobile money transfer service launched in 2009, a key part of Somaliland's payment system.
- **SSC-Khatumo**: A pro-unionist administration in eastern Somaliland that does not recognise the Somaliland government's sovereignty.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **International Recognition**: The core political debate is whether Somaliland will achieve widespread international recognition as a sovereign state, a status currently held only by Israel among UN members.
- **Border Disputes**: Control over the eastern regions of Sool and Sanaag remains contested between Somaliland, the Somali federal state of Puntland, and the local SSC-Khatumo administration.
- **Governance Model**: Scholars debate the success of Somaliland's model, which integrates traditional clan-based governance with modern democratic institutions, as a potential template for state-building in the region.

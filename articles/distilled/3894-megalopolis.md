# Megalopolis

## Overview
A megalopolis (or megaregion) is a large, integrated network of adjacent metropolitan areas that function as a continuous urban region through shared transportation, economic, and ecological systems. The concept is significant because it introduced a larger-scale framework for understanding and planning urban growth, moving beyond individual cities to consider coordinated policy across vast, interconnected urban landscapes.

## Timeline
- **4th century BC** — The settlement of Megalopolis is founded in Arcadia, Greece, giving the term its original name.
- **1832** — The term "megalopolis" is first defined geographically as "a very large, heavily populated urban complex."
- **Late 1950s–early 1960s** — Jean Gottmann directs "A Study of Megalopolis," applying the term to the U.S. Northeast seaboard from Boston to Washington, D.C.
- **2005** — Robert E. Lang and Dawn Dhavale publish a report exploring U.S. megaregions.
- **2007** — Lang and Nelson publish an article grouping 20 "megapolitan" areas into 10 megaregions.
- **2019** — China's NDRC publishes guidelines distinguishing "city clusters" (城市群) from smaller "metropolitan areas."

## Body

### Definition and Characteristics
A megalopolis consists of two or more roughly adjacent metropolitan areas. Their integration is defined by common systems of transport, economy, resources, and ecology, which blur the boundaries between population centers. While some separation remains, the perception of a continuous urban area is valuable for coordinating policy at an expanded scale. Jean Gottmann, in his foundational studies, defined a megalopolis as having a population of 25 million, while Constantinos Doxiadis defined a "small megalopolis" as having about 10 million people.

### Modern Development and Measurement
Modern megalopolises are often aided by interlinked ground transportation corridors like rail and highways. Traveling through these corridors is informally called "megaloping." The concept has been applied globally, with different terms and legal meanings. In Brazil, "megarregião" has a specific legal definition. In China, the official term is "城市群" (chéngshì qún), meaning "city cluster," which is now a subject of national government planning. To address inconsistent international urban data, an Urban Metric System (UMS) has been proposed. UMS uses vector field calculations based on the attractive and repulsive forces of inhabitants and jobs to define urban area limits consistently.

### Cultural Representations
The megalopolis concept has influenced popular culture, often depicted as a vast, futuristic urban landscape. Examples include Mega-City One in the *Judge Dredd* comics, "the Sprawl" (Boston-Atlanta Metropolitan Axis) in William Gibson's *Sprawl trilogy*, and the titular city in Francis Ford Coppola's 2024 film *Megalopolis*.

## Terms
- **Megalopolis/Megaregion**: A clustered network of large, integrated metropolitan areas.
- **Megaloping**: Informal travel throughout a megalopolis using its commuter passageways.
- **City Cluster (城市群)**: The official Chinese term for a megalopolis, defined as an area where cities are relatively densely distributed.
- **Urban Metric System (UMS)**: A proposed system for computing consistent urban area limits and central points across different countries and time periods.
- **Metropolitan Area**: A large population center and its surrounding communities, which form a building block of a megalopolis.

## Debates and Open Questions
The source highlights a core scholarly debate: the definition of a megalopolis is not universal. Population thresholds vary (e.g., Gottmann's 25 million vs. Doxiadis's 10 million), and terms like "megaregion" and "city cluster" are used interchangeably by different scholars and countries. Furthermore, the application of the concept is complicated by the use of arbitrary, non-comparable national definitions for urban data, which is the problem the proposed Urban Metric System (UMS) aims to solve.

# Tycho Brahe

## Overview
Tycho Brahe (1546–1601) was a Danish nobleman and the last great astronomer before the telescope. His unprecedentedly accurate naked-eye observations of stars, planets, comets, and a supernova provided the essential data that enabled Johannes Kepler to formulate his laws of planetary motion, a cornerstone of the Scientific Revolution. Brahe also proposed his own geo-heliocentric model of the universe, which remained influential for decades.

## Timeline
- **14 Dec 1546** — Born at Knutstorp Castle, Scania, Denmark.
- **1560** — Witnesses a predicted solar eclipse, sparking his interest in precise astronomical observation.
- **1566** — Loses part of his nose in a duel with Manderup Parsberg.
- **11 Nov 1572** — Observes a "new star" (supernova SN 1572) in Cassiopeia, challenging the idea of an unchanging celestial realm.
- **1573** — Publishes *De nova stella*, coining the term "nova."
- **1576** — King Frederick II grants him the island of Hven to build Uraniborg, a major observatory.
- **1577** — Observes the Great Comet of 1577, proving comets are not atmospheric phenomena and lie beyond the Moon.
- **1584** — Completes the underground observatory Stjerneborg for more stable measurements.
- **1588** — King Frederick II dies; Tycho's royal support begins to wane.
- **1597** — Forced to leave Denmark by King Christian IV; enters exile.
- **1599** — Becomes Imperial Court Astronomer for Emperor Rudolf II in Prague.
- **24 Oct 1601** — Dies in Prague; his data is inherited by Johannes Kepler.

## Body

### Early Life and the 1572 Supernova
Born into Danish nobility, Tycho was raised by an uncle and educated in law, but his passion was astronomy. A solar eclipse in 1560 and inaccurate planetary tables he observed in 1563 convinced him that progress required systematic, accurate observations. His discovery of a supernova in 1572 was pivotal. By showing it had no parallax against the fixed stars, he proved it was a distant stellar phenomenon, not a change in Earth's atmosphere. This refuted the Aristotelian axiom of celestial immutability and established his reputation.

### Uraniborg and Observational Methods
With royal patronage, Tycho built Uraniborg ("Castle of the Heavens") on the island of Hven. It was a self-contained research center with observatories, an alchemical laboratory, and a printing press. He designed and built large, precise instruments like quadrants and sextants, and later moved key instruments underground to Stjerneborg to eliminate building vibrations. His observations of stellar and planetary positions were about five times more accurate than any before, with a target precision of one arcminute. He also created the first systematic tables for correcting atmospheric refraction.

### The Tychonic System and Comet Observations
Tycho admired Copernicus's mathematical model but rejected a moving Earth based on Aristotelian physics, the lack of observed stellar parallax, and scripture. He devised a compromise: the Tychonic system, where the Sun and Moon orbit the Earth, but the other planets orbit the Sun. His observations of the Great Comet of 1577 were crucial. By showing its parallax was less than the Moon's, he proved it was a superlunar object moving through space, which shattered the concept of solid, crystalline celestial spheres required by both Ptolemaic and Copernican models.

### Exile, Kepler, and Legacy
After falling out of favor with the new Danish king, Tycho left Hven in 1597. He eventually settled in Prague as Imperial Astronomer, where he hired Johannes Kepler. Though Kepler disagreed with Tycho's planetary model, he deeply valued Tycho's observational data. On his deathbed, Tycho urged Kepler to use his data to complete the *Rudolphine Tables* of planetary motion. Kepler did so, and his resulting laws of planetary motion, derived from Tycho's meticulous records of Mars, provided definitive support for heliocentrism.

## Terms
- **Heliocentrism:** A model of the universe with the Sun at the center, as proposed by Copernicus.
- **Geo-heliocentrism:** A hybrid model (like the Tychonic system) where the Earth is stationary at the center, the Sun orbits the Earth, and the other planets orbit the Sun.
- **Parallax:** The apparent shift in position of a nearby object against a distant background when viewed from different points. The lack of stellar parallax was a major argument against Earth's motion.
- **Stellae novae (novae):** "New stars" that appear suddenly; now known to be exploding stars (supernovae) or other cataclysmic events.
- **Aristotelian physics:** The ancient Greek theory that the heavens are perfect and unchanging, made of a fifth element (aether), while the Earth is composed of four elements and is naturally at rest.
- **Crystalline spheres:** The hypothetical, transparent, rotating spheres that were thought to carry the planets in their orbits in pre-modern astronomy.
- **Prosthaphaeresis:** A set of trigonometric identities used to convert multiplication into addition, a key calculation method before logarithms.
- **Morganatic marriage:** A marriage between people of unequal social rank, which prevents the spouse of lower rank and any children from inheriting the titles, privileges, and property of the spouse of higher rank.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Cause of Death:** While traditionally attributed to a bladder or kidney ailment, modern analysis of his exhumed remains (2010-2012) found no evidence of mercury poisoning or other poisons, ruling out murder. The exact medical cause remains uncertain.
- **Accuracy of Star Catalog:** While Brahe claimed an accuracy of one arcminute, analysis shows the median error in his published star catalog was about 1.5 arcminutes, with systematic errors introduced during data processing.
- **Adoption of His System:** The Tychonic system remained popular, especially in Catholic countries, well into the 18th century as a "safe" alternative to both Ptolemaic and Copernican models, only being definitively excluded by the discovery of stellar aberration in 1729.

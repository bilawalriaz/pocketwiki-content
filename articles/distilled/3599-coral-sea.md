# Coral Sea

## Overview
The Coral Sea is a marginal sea of the South Pacific Ocean, located off the northeast coast of Australia. It is globally significant for containing the Great Barrier Reef, the world's largest reef system and a UNESCO World Heritage Site. The sea's warm, clear waters support immense biodiversity but face threats from climate change, shipping, and pollution, leading to the establishment of major marine protected areas.

## Timeline
- **c. 58–48 million years ago** — The Coral Sea basin forms due to geological uplift and subsidence.
- **May 1942** — The Battle of the Coral Sea, a major WWII naval confrontation, takes place.
- **1975** — Oil exploration projects on the Great Barrier Reef are terminated.
- **1981** — The Great Barrier Reef is declared a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
- **2004** — Approximately one-third of the GBR Marine Park is fully protected from fishing.
- **2007** — A major Mw 8.1 earthquake strikes the Solomon Islands, causing a deadly tsunami.
- **2010** — The Chinese ship *Shen Neng 1* runs aground, causing the largest recorded oil spill damage to the GBR.
- **2013** — The Coral Sea Commonwealth Marine Reserve is proclaimed.
- **2017** — The reserve is renamed the Coral Sea Marine Park.
- **2020** — Advanced sonar mapping reveals deep-sea features and potential new species.

## Body

### Geography and Geology
The Coral Sea is bounded by Australia's Queensland coast to the west, Vanuatu and New Caledonia to the east, and the Solomon Islands to the northeast. It merges with the Tasman Sea to the south and the Pacific Ocean to the east. The sea's basin formed between 58 and 48 million years ago. Its topography includes the continental shelf hosting the Great Barrier Reef, the Coral Sea Plateau, and several deep basins and ridges. Seismic activity is recorded but is less intense than in areas to the east and north.

### Hydrology and Climate
Major currents form a counter-clockwise gyre, including the East Australian Current, which transports warm water southward. The climate is subtropical, with warm, stable temperatures in the north (27–28°C) and seasonal variation in the south (19–24°C). The region is frequently hit by tropical cyclones, especially between January and April. Annual rainfall is high (1,000–3,000 mm), and climate change has made record-breaking sea surface temperatures, which cause coral bleaching, significantly more likely.

### Flora and Fauna
The reefs and islands are rich in biodiversity. The Great Barrier Reef alone contains over 2,000 plant species, 400 coral species, and more than 1,500 fish species. Key fauna include whales, dolphins, sea turtles, and over 200 bird species. The crown-of-thorns starfish is a major coral predator, and outbreaks can cause significant reef loss. The sea also hosts numerous sharks, with the grey reef shark being the most common.

### Human Activities and Protection
Coastal areas were populated at least 40,000 years ago. Modern activities include shipping, fishing, tourism, and limited petroleum exploration (which was banned on the GBR in the 1970s). Tourism contributed A$5.1 billion to the Australian economy in 2006–2007. Major environmental threats include pollution from land runoff, shipping accidents, and climate change. In response, large protected areas have been established, including the Great Barrier Reef Marine Park (1975) and the Coral Sea Marine Park (2017), which is one of the world's largest marine protected areas.

## Terms
- **Marginal sea:** A sea partially enclosed by islands, archipelagos, or peninsulas.
- **Bioregion:** A geographically distinct area defined by its unique assemblage of natural communities and species.
- **Atoll:** A ring-shaped coral reef, island, or series of islets, surrounding a lagoon.
- **Continental shelf:** The extended perimeter of a continent and associated coastal plain, submerged under shallow seas.
- **Seamount:** An underwater mountain formed by volcanic activity.
- **Coral bleaching:** A stress response in corals, often due to high temperatures, where they expel symbiotic algae, turning white and often dying.
- **Crown-of-thorns starfish (*Acanthaster planci*):** A large, multiple-armed starfish that preys upon coral polyps.
- **IUCN category:** A classification system for protected areas defined by the International Union for Conservation of Nature.

## Debates and Open questions
The effectiveness and level of protection within the Coral Sea Marine Park are debated. The 2018 management plan reduced the area with the highest protection (IUCN category II) from 50.78% to 24%, contrary to the recommendations of the government's expert review panel. The long-term impact of the 2010 *Shen Neng 1* oil spill on reef recovery is also a concern, with estimates suggesting a 10 to 20-year recovery period.

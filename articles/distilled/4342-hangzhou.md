# Hangzhou

## Overview
Hangzhou is the capital of Zhejiang province, China, and a major historical, cultural, and economic hub. Situated at the southern end of the Grand Canal and the head of Hangzhou Bay, it is renowned for its West Lake and has been one of China's most important cities for over a millennium. Its modern significance stems from its role as a global technology and e-commerce center, hosting headquarters for companies like Alibaba, while its historical importance includes serving as the capital of the Southern Song dynasty.

## Timeline
- **c. 5000 BC** — Liangzhu culture inhabits the area around present-day Hangzhou.
- **222 BC** — Qiantang county is established by the Qin dynasty.
- **AD 589** — Hangzhou is made the seat of the prefecture of Hang under the Sui dynasty.
- **907–978** — Hangzhou serves as the capital of the Wuyue Kingdom.
- **1132–1276** — Hangzhou (Lin'an) becomes the capital of the Southern Song dynasty.
- **1276** — The city is captured by Mongol armies of Kublai Khan.
- **1992** — Hangzhou's economy begins rapid development after opening up.
- **2016** — Hangzhou hosts the G20 summit.
- **2022** — Hangzhou hosts the Asian Games.

## Body

### Historical Significance
Hangzhou's history extends back to the Neolithic Hemudu and Liangzhu cultures. It was established as a county in 222 BC and became a prefecture in AD 589. Its golden age began when it was chosen as the capital of the Southern Song dynasty in 1132, following the Jurchen conquest of northern China. As Lin'an, it became a global metropolis, described by travelers like Marco Polo and Ibn Battuta as one of the world's greatest cities. The city was a center of trade, culture, and government, with a population estimated to be over one million by 1276. It remained an important commercial center under the subsequent Yuan dynasty.

### Geography and Climate
Located in the Yangtze River Delta, Hangzhou's city center is built around the eastern and northern sides of West Lake. The Qiantang River, known for its massive tidal bore, flows through the city. Hangzhou has a humid subtropical climate with four distinct seasons, featuring long, hot, humid summers and chilly, cloudy winters. It is affected by the Asian monsoon plum rains in June and typhoons in late summer.

### Economy and Industry
Hangzhou's economy has grown rapidly since 1992, evolving from light industry and textiles into a major e-commerce and technology hub. It is the headquarters for Alibaba Group, Ant Group, NetEase, Geely, and DeepSeek. The city's "1 + 6" industrial clusters prioritize the digital economy alongside cultural, financial, and tourism sectors. As of 2018, its GDP had grown to RMB 1.35 trillion. The Hangzhou metropolitan area's economy is larger than those of some major nations.

### Culture and Tourism
Hangzhou is celebrated for its cultural heritage, centered on the UNESCO-listed West Lake Cultural Landscape. The city is the origin of Longjing (Dragon Well) tea, a famous green tea. Local Zhejiang cuisine is known for dishes like West Lake Vinegar Fish and Dongpo Pork. The city has a distinct Wu dialect and is home to major museums, including the China National Silk Museum. Tourism is a vital economic sector, promoted through campaigns like the "Modern Marco Polo" initiative.

### Transportation and Infrastructure
Hangzhou is a major transportation hub. Its metro system, which opened in 2012, had expanded to about 516 km by 2022. Hangzhou East station is one of China's busiest rail hubs, with high-speed service to Shanghai taking 50 minutes. The city is served by Hangzhou Xiaoshan International Airport and has an extensive public bicycle rental system. A pilot AI-based Internet Court was established in 2019 to handle e-commerce disputes.

## Terms
- **Sub-provincial city:** An administrative division in China with a budget and planning status above a prefecture-level city but below a province.
- **Grand Canal:** The world's longest canal or artificial river, stretching from Hangzhou in the south to Beijing in the north.
- **West Lake:** A freshwater lake in Hangzhou and a UNESCO World Heritage Site, central to the city's landscape and culture.
- **Southern Song dynasty:** The period of Chinese history (1127–1279) when the Song court retreated south after losing northern China to the Jurchens.
- **Humid subtropical climate:** A climate type characterized by hot, humid summers and cool to mild winters.
- **Tidal bore:** A strong tide that pushes up a river or narrow bay against the direction of the current, notably occurring on the Qiantang River.
- **Digital economy:** An economic sector based on digital computing technologies, which is a leading growth driver for Hangzhou.
- **Wu dialect:** A group of Sinitic languages spoken in Shanghai, Zhejiang, and southern Jiangsu, including the Hangzhou dialect.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Historical population:** Estimates for Hangzhou's population during the Southern Song dynasty vary. The *Encyclopædia Britannica* cites over 2 million, while historian Jacques Gernet estimates well over one million by 1276. Official 1270 census figures listed 186,330 families but likely undercounted non-residents and soldiers.
- **Marco Polo's account:** The accuracy of Marco Polo's description of Hangzhou is debated. His claims of "12,000 stone bridges" and a city "greater than any in the world" are considered exaggerations, though some scholars argue the measurements could be plausible if different units were used.

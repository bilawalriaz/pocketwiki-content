# Vanuatu

## Overview
Vanuatu is an archipelagic nation in the South Pacific, formed by volcanic islands first settled by Melanesian people around 3,000 years ago. Its modern history is defined by a unique Anglo-French colonial administration (the New Hebrides) that ended with independence in 1980, after which it has navigated political instability, natural disasters, and the economic challenges of a small island developing state.

## Body

### Prehistory and Settlement
The archipelago was settled about 3,000 years ago (c. 1100–700 BCE) by Lapita people, who brought crops like yam and taro and domesticated animals. Archaeological evidence, including the Teouma burial site, shows their presence across the islands. Over time, the Lapita culture fragmented, and their Austronesian languages evolved into the 100+ indigenous languages of Vanuatu today, the highest density per capita in the world. A 16th–17th century chief named Roy Mata is said to have united local clans, an era corroborated by archaeological sites.

### European Contact and Colonial Era
Spanish explorer Queirós arrived in 1606, followed by French and British explorers in the 18th century. The 19th century brought sandalwood traders, "blackbirding" (indentured labour), and missionaries. By the 1880s, both France and Britain claimed parts of the islands. In 1906, they established the Anglo-French Condominium, a joint administration with separate legal and financial systems. This dysfunctional government saw continued land expropriation and labour abuses. World War II brought a massive US military presence that undermined colonial authority and inspired cargo cults like John Frum.

### Path to Independence
Post-war modernization and economic development were slow. In the 1960s–70s, movements for independence emerged, notably the Vanua'aku Pati (VP) and the Nagriamel movement. Political tensions between pro-Anglophone independence groups and pro-French "Moderates" led to violence. A compromise led to the 1979 election won by the VP. Independence was achieved on 30 July 1980, but was immediately followed by a secessionist revolt on Espiritu Santo, quelled with help from Papua New Guinea.

### Post-Independence Politics and Economy
Vanuatu has been a parliamentary democracy since 1980, but its politics have been highly unstable, with frequent changes of prime minister via no-confidence votes. The economy relies on agriculture (copra, kava, beef), tourism, and offshore financial services. The country is a tax haven and sells citizenship for revenue. It is highly vulnerable to natural disasters, ranking first in the 2021 WorldRiskIndex. In recent decades, Chinese investment and aid have grown significantly, raising concerns about debt and influence.

## Terms
*   **Condominium:** A political territory in which sovereignty is jointly exercised by two or more powers. The New Hebrides was governed by Britain and France in this manner from 1906 to 1980.
*   **Kastom:** A Bislama term for traditional culture, law, and custom.
*   **Lapita:** An ancient archaeological culture of Austronesian peoples who were the first settlers of Vanuatu and other Pacific islands.
*   **Ni-Vanuatu:** The term for the indigenous people of Vanuatu.
*   **Bislama:** The national language and lingua franca of Vanuatu, an English-based creole.
*   **Blackbirding:** The coercion of people through trickery or kidnapping to work as labourers, especially in the 19th-century Pacific.
*   **Cargo Cult:** A Melanesian millenarian movement that incorporates a belief in the future arrival of wealth-generating goods ("cargo").
*   **Nakamal:** A traditional village clubhouse, a meeting point for men and a place to drink kava.
*   **Flag of Convenience:** A business practice where a ship's owner registers a vessel in a foreign country to avoid regulations and taxes of their own country.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   The exact prevalence of human cannibalism in pre-contact Vanuatu is debated, with evidence coming mainly from oral tradition and foreign accounts rather than empirical archaeological proof.
*   The long-term geopolitical implications of Vanuatu's growing debt to and investment from China are a subject of international concern, particularly regarding potential strategic leverage.
*   The sustainability of the citizenship-by-investment program is questioned due to ethical concerns and its role in political scandals, such as the 2023 loss of UK visa-free access.

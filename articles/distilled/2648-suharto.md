# Suharto

## Overview
Suharto was an Indonesian general and politician who served as the country's second and longest-serving president from 1967 to 1998. His 32-year "New Order" regime was characterized by a strong, centralized, authoritarian government that oversaw significant economic development and stability but was also marked by widespread corruption, human rights violations, and political repression. His legacy remains highly divisive, balancing economic progress against brutal dictatorship.

## Timeline
- **8 June 1921** — Born in Kemusuk, near Yogyakarta, Dutch East Indies.
- **1 October 1965** — Counter-coup following the abortive 30 September Movement; Suharto seizes army control.
- **March 1967** — Appointed acting president by the MPRS (People's Consultative Assembly).
- **27 March 1968** — Elected president for a full five-year term by the MPRS.
- **7 December 1975** — Authorizes the invasion of East Timor.
- **9 March 1983** — Named "Father of Development" by the MPR.
- **1997** — Asian financial crisis begins, severely impacting Indonesia.
- **21 May 1998** — Resigns from the presidency amid economic collapse and mass protests.
- **27 January 2008** — Dies in Jakarta; receives a state military funeral.
- **10 November 2025** — Posthumously named a National Hero of Indonesia.

## Body

### Rise to Power
Suharto's path to power began with the 30 September 1965 abortive coup, which he countered, leading to a nationwide anti-communist purge that killed at least 500,000 to over 1 million people. This eliminated the Indonesian Communist Party (PKI), one of President Sukarno's key support pillars. Through a complex political struggle involving student agitation and military pressure, Suharto gradually sidelined Sukarno, using the 1966 Supersemar decree to gain authority. He was formally appointed acting president in 1967 and elected president in 1968.

### The "New Order" Regime
Suharto established the "New Order," a centralized, authoritarian state dominated by the military under a "Dual Function" (Dwifungsi) policy. He promoted the state ideology of Pancasila to justify his rule and suppress dissent. Politically, he controlled elections through his vehicle, Golkar, and forced opposition parties to merge, ensuring no effective opposition. His regime relied on the military and intelligence agencies to maintain strict internal security, crushing insurgencies and suppressing student and political activism.

### Economic Policy and Development
Inheriting an economy with 650% inflation, Suharto appointed a team of US-educated economists (the "Berkeley Mafia") who implemented free-market policies, stabilizing the economy by 1969. The New Order opened sectors to foreign investment and secured foreign aid. Through a series of five-year development plans (REPELITA), the government invested in infrastructure, agriculture, and health, leading to sustained GDP growth (averaging around 7% annually), a drop in poverty from 45% (1970) to 11% (1996), and improved education and life expectancy. This progress earned him the title "Father of Development."

### Foreign Policy and Conflicts
Suharto's foreign policy was anti-communist and aligned with the West, securing economic support during the Cold War. He was a founding member of ASEAN (1967). His most significant military action was the 1975 invasion and 23-year occupation of East Timor, which resulted in an estimated minimum of 90,800 conflict-related deaths. He also oversaw the integration of West Irian (Papua) into Indonesia in 1969.

### Corruption, Scandal, and Downfall
Suharto's regime became increasingly corrupt, with his family and cronies amassing vast wealth through monopolies, government contracts, and "donations" to family-run foundations (yayasans). By the 1990s, growing authoritarianism and corruption fueled public discontent. The 1997 Asian financial crisis, which caused the rupiah to collapse and the economy to shrink by 13.7%, was the breaking point. Widespread riots and student protests led to Suharto's resignation on 21 May 1998.

### Post-Presidency and Legacy
After resigning, Suharto faced corruption investigations but was never tried due to poor health. He died in 2008. His legacy is deeply contested: he is praised for economic development and stability but condemned for brutal dictatorship, mass killings, and corruption. In 2024, a corruption accusation against him was formally repealed by the MPR, and in 2025, he was posthumously named a National Hero, a move that sparked protests.

## Terms
- **New Order**: The political regime of Suharto (1967-1998), characterized by a strong, centralized, anti-communist, and authoritarian government.
- **Pancasila**: Indonesia's official philosophical foundation, consisting of five principles. Suharto made adherence to it mandatory for all organizations to justify his rule and suppress dissent.
- **Dwifungsi ("Dual Function")**: A doctrine allowing the Indonesian military to have an active role in all levels of government, economy, and society.
- **Supersemar**: The 1966 presidential decree that gave Suharto authority to take any action necessary to maintain security, which he used to ban the PKI and purge Sukarno loyalists.
- **Golkar ("Functional Groups")**: The political vehicle Suharto used to contest and dominate elections, winning landslide majorities in every election under his rule.
- **MPR (People's Consultative Assembly)**: The supreme legislative body that elected the president and, under Suharto, acted as a rubber stamp for his policies.
- **KKN (Korupsi, Kolusi, dan Nepotisme)**: An acronym for corruption, collusion, and nepotism, widely used to describe the systemic graft of the Suharto era.
- **Reformasi**: The term for the pro-democracy reform movement that emerged in the 1990s, culminating in Suharto's resignation.

## Debates and open questions
- The identity and aims of the organizers of the 30 September 1965 movement remain debated, with the army's version blaming the PKI being contested by other theories.
- The exact death toll of the 1965-66 anti-communist purge is uncertain, with estimates ranging from 500,000 to over 1 million killed.
- The extent of the Suharto family's wealth is disputed, with estimates ranging from $15 billion to over $73 billion.
- Suharto's posthumous designation as a National Hero in 2025 reignited debate over whether his economic achievements should be honored given his record of human rights abuses and corruption.

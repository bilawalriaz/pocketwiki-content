# Shiraz

## Overview
Shiraz is the capital of Fars province in southwestern Iran, a city with a history spanning over four millennia. Known as the "city of poets, literature, and flowers," it has been a major cultural, economic, and administrative center, particularly celebrated for its role in Persian arts and literature during the medieval period. Its strategic location on trade routes and its moderate climate have sustained its importance for over a thousand years.

## Timeline
- **c. 2000 BCE** — Earliest reference as "Tiraziš" on Elamite clay tablets.
- **693 CE** — City founded or restored by the Umayyad Caliphate.
- **894 CE** — First congregational mosque (Atigh Jame' Mosque) founded by the Saffarids.
- **933 CE** — Buyid dynasty takes control, making Shiraz a major cultural and economic center.
- **13th century** — Shiraz becomes a leading center of arts and letters, home to poets Sa'di and Hafez.
- **1504** — Captured by Ismail I, founder of the Safavid Dynasty.
- **1762** — Karim Khan Zand makes Shiraz his capital, leading to a major building boom.
- **1844** — The Báb declares his mission in Shiraz, founding the Bábí faith (precursor to the Baháʼí Faith).
- **1910** — Pogrom occurs in the Jewish quarter.
- **2000** — Shiraz Special Economic Electronic Zone (SEEZ) established.

## Body

### History and Founding
The site of Shiraz was likely settled in the pre-Islamic era, with Achaemenid and Sasanian remains found nearby. The modern city was founded or restored in 693 CE by the Umayyad Caliphate to rival the Sasanian capital of Istakhr. It grew slowly until the 9th century, when it became the capital of the Saffarid dynasty. Under the subsequent Buyid dynasty (10th–11th centuries), Shiraz flourished as a prosperous economic and cultural center. It was spared destruction by the Mongols and Tamerlane through tribute. In the 13th century, it became a renowned "House of Knowledge" (Dar al-'Elm). The city was made a capital again by Karim Khan Zand in 1762, but later declined under the Qajar dynasty. It is the birthplace of the Báb, making it a holy city for Baháʼís.

### Culture and Demographics
Shiraz is famed as the birthplace of the poets Sa'di and Hafez, whose tombs are major landmarks. The city is known for its gardens, such as Eram Garden, and historically for its wine. The population is predominantly Shia Muslim, but Shiraz has historically had significant Jewish, Christian, and Baháʼí communities. The Jewish community, once 20,000 strong, has largely emigrated. The city's crafts include inlaid mosaic work, silverware, and carpet weaving.

### Economy and Industry
Shiraz is the economic center of southern Iran. Its economy is based on provincial agricultural products like grapes and citrus fruits, alongside industries including cement, sugar, textiles, and metalwork. It hosts a major oil refinery and is a primary hub for Iran's electronics industry, with 53% of the country's electronic investment centered there. The Shiraz Special Economic Electronic Zone was established in 2000 to boost manufacturing. The city is also a major shopping destination with numerous malls and bazaars.

### Geography and Climate
Located at 1,500 meters (4,900 ft) above sea level in the Zagros Mountains, Shiraz has a semi-arid climate with hot summers and cool winters. A seasonal "Dry River" flows through the city. The climate is generally moderate, but extreme weather events, such as flash floods, can occur.

## Terms
- **Rudkhaneye Khoshk**: The "Dry River," a seasonal river flowing through northern Shiraz.
- **Dar al-'Elm**: "House of Knowledge," a classical name for Shiraz reflecting its 13th-century status as a center of learning.
- **Atigh Jame' Mosque**: The oldest mosque in Shiraz, founded in 894 CE.
- **Vakil Bazaar**: A historic covered bazaar constructed during the Zand dynasty.
- **SEEZ**: Shiraz Special Economic Electronic Zone, established in 2000 to boost electronics manufacturing.
- **Baháʼí Faith**: A religion founded in the 19th century, for which Shiraz is a holy city as the birthplace of its prophet, the Báb.

## Debates and Open Questions
Scholars debate the significance of ancient Shiraz. Some sources suggest it was merely a way-station, while others, citing its frequent appearance in Persepolis administrative archives, argue it was one of the most important settlements in the area during the Achaemenid era. The exact nature and extent of the pre-Islamic settlement at the site of the modern city remain subjects of archaeological investigation.

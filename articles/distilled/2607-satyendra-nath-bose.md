# Satyendra Nath Bose

## Overview
Satyendra Nath Bose (1894–1974) was an Indian theoretical physicist and mathematician whose work on quantum mechanics in the early 1920s laid the foundation for Bose–Einstein statistics and the theory of the Bose–Einstein condensate. His insights into the behavior of identical particles were pivotal in the development of quantum statistics, and the class of elementary particles he helped describe were named bosons in his honor.

## Timeline
- **1 January 1894** — Born in Calcutta (now Kolkata), India.
- **1913** — Earned a Bachelor of Science in mixed mathematics from Presidency College, Calcutta, standing first.
- **1915** — Earned a Master of Science in mixed mathematics from the University of Calcutta, setting a new record for marks.
- **1921** — Joined the University of Dhaka as a Reader in the Physics Department.
- **1924** — Wrote "Planck's Law and the Hypothesis of Light Quanta," deriving Planck's radiation law using a novel method for counting identical particles.
- **1924** — Albert Einstein translated and published Bose's paper in *Zeitschrift für Physik*.
- **1926** — Returned to Dhaka and was made Head of the Department of Physics.
- **1945** — Left Dhaka and returned to Calcutta upon the imminent partition of India.
- **1954** — Awarded the Padma Vibhushan by the Government of India.
- **1958** — Elected a Fellow of the Royal Society.
- **4 February 1974** — Died in Calcutta.

## Body

### Early Life and Education
Bose was born in Calcutta into a Bengali Kayastha family. He excelled academically, passing his matriculation exam in 1909 fifth in merit. He attended Presidency College and later the Science College of the University of Calcutta, where he earned top marks in both his Bachelor of Science (1913) and Master of Science (1951) degrees in mixed mathematics. After completing his MSc, he began research in the theory of relativity. He married Ushabati Ghosh in 1914, and they had nine children.

### Research Career and Bose–Einstein Statistics
From 1916 to 1921, Bose was a lecturer at Rajabazar Science College. In 1921, he joined the newly founded University of Dhaka, where he established new departments and taught advanced courses. In 1924, while preparing a lecture on the ultraviolet catastrophe, Bose developed a new method for counting states of identical particles. He argued that the Maxwell–Boltzmann distribution was inadequate for microscopic particles and that photons are indistinguishable. He wrote a paper deriving Planck's quantum radiation law using this approach and sent it to Albert Einstein. Einstein recognized its importance, translated it into German, and had it published. This work established Bose–Einstein statistics and the concept of particle indistinguishability, a cornerstone of quantum statistics.

### Bose–Einstein Condensate and Later Career
Einstein extended Bose's statistical method to atoms, predicting a new state of matter now known as the Bose–Einstein condensate—a dense collection of bosons (particles with integer spin, named after Bose by Paul Dirac). This phenomenon was experimentally confirmed in 1995. After returning to Dhaka in 1926, Bose became Head of the Physics Department, despite lacking a doctorate, on Einstein's recommendation. He guided research in X-ray spectroscopy and other fields. Following the partition of India in 1947, he returned to Calcutta, taught at the university, and later served as Vice-Chancellor of Visva-Bharati University. He continued research in nuclear physics and applied science until his death.

### Honours and Legacy
Bose was awarded the Padma Vibhushan in 1954 and was a Fellow of the Royal Society. He held the position of National Professor for 15 years. The S.N. Bose National Centre for Basic Sciences was established in his honor. Although nominated for the Nobel Prize in Physics multiple times, he was not awarded it. His work is considered a top achievement of 20th-century Indian science. Seven Nobel Prizes have been awarded for research related to his concepts.

## Terms
- **Bose–Einstein statistics**: A statistical framework for describing the behavior of identical particles with integer spin (bosons), where the number of particles in a given quantum state can be any non-negative integer.
- **Bosons**: A class of elementary subatomic particles that obey Bose–Einstein statistics and have integer spin. Named after S.N. Bose by Paul Dirac.
- **Bose–Einstein condensate**: A state of matter formed when bosons are cooled to temperatures very close to absolute zero, causing a large fraction of the particles to occupy the lowest quantum state, resulting in macroscopic quantum phenomena.
- **Planck's radiation law**: A law in physics describing the spectral density of electromagnetic radiation emitted by a black body in thermal equilibrium at a given temperature.
- **Ultraviolet catastrophe**: A problem in classical physics where a black-body radiator would emit radiation with infinite power at short wavelengths, a paradox resolved by quantum theory.
- **Indistinguishable particles**: Particles that are identical in all intrinsic properties (mass, charge, spin), such that swapping two particles does not create a new, distinct physical state.
- **Phase space**: A mathematical space in which all possible states of a system are represented, with each state corresponding to a unique point.

## Debates and Open questions
- The reason Bose's initial paper was rejected by the British journal *Philosophical Magazine* before he sent it to Einstein is not known.
- Bose was nominated for the Nobel Prize in Physics in 1956, 1959, and 1962, but the Nobel Committee's expert, Oskar Klein, deemed his work not worthy of the prize. The criteria for this evaluation remain a point of historical discussion.

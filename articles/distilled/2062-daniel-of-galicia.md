# Daniel of Galicia

## Overview
Daniel Romanovich (1201–1266) was a pivotal ruler of the Rurikid dynasty who united the principalities of Galicia and Volhynia, navigated the Mongol invasions, and was crowned the first King of Ruthenia. His long reign restored the region's power and laid the foundations for the Kingdom of Galicia–Volhynia, making him a defining figure in the post-Kievan Rus' period.

## Timeline
- **1199** — Daniel's father, Roman the Great, unites Galicia and Volhynia.
- **1205** — Roman dies; Daniel is exiled at age four.
- **1211** — Daniel is restored to the Galician throne at age nine.
- **1223** — Daniel fights in the Battle of the Kalka River against the Mongols.
- **1238** — Daniel regains control of most of Galicia, including Halych.
- **1240** — Kiev falls to the Mongols; Daniel moves his capital to Kholm.
- **1245** — Daniel defeats a coalition at Yaroslav, securing Galicia.
- **1246** — Daniel is summoned to the Golden Horde and accepts Mongol overlordship.
- **1253** — Daniel is crowned King of Ruthenia by a papal representative.
- **1259** — Daniel complies with a Mongol ultimatum to destroy his fortifications.
- **1266** — Daniel dies and is succeeded by his son Leo.

## Biography

### Early Life and Struggle for Power
Daniel was the son of Roman Mstislavich, who had united Galicia and Volhynia in 1199. After Roman's death in 1205, Daniel and his family were exiled by Galician boyars (nobles). His childhood was spent at the court of his uncle, King Andrew II of Hungary. In 1211, a coalition of Hungarians and Galicians restored the nine-year-old Daniel to the throne of Halych, but he was deposed again in 1213 by a boyar rebellion. He spent the next decades intermittently fighting to control Galicia and Volhynia, often against rival princes, boyars, and the intervention of Polish and Hungarian neighbors. In 1223, he participated in the disastrous Battle of the Kalka River against the Mongols, from which he narrowly escaped.

### Mongol Invasions and the Fall of Kiev
In 1239, as the Mongols advanced, Daniel sent his voivode (military commander) Dmytro to defend Kiev, but the city fell on 6 December 1240. In 1241, the Mongols destroyed Halych, forcing Daniel and his brother Vasylko to flee to Poland and relocate their capital to Kholm. In 1246, Daniel was summoned to the Golden Horde's capital at Sarai and was forced to accept Mongol suzerainty, though he later sought Western alliances to counter their influence.

### Establishment of the Kingdom
After defeating a combined enemy force at Yaroslav in 1245, Daniel finally secured all of Galicia. He implemented domestic reforms to stabilize his realm, inviting German, Polish, Armenian, and Jewish merchants and artisans, founding towns like Lviv (1256), and creating peasant-based infantry units. To counter the Mongols, he sought aid from Pope Innocent IV, offering to place his lands under Rome's ecclesiastical authority. In 1253, the Pope crowned him King of Ruthenia, though Daniel bitterly noted he had expected an army, not just a crown. Despite this, he continued to resist Mongol pressure, repelling assaults in 1254 but ultimately complying with a destructive ultimatum in 1259.

### Later Reign and Legacy
In his final years, Daniel engaged in dynastic marriages to secure alliances with Lithuania and Poland. By his death in 1266, he had reconstructed his father's territories, minimized Mongol tribute, and elevated his principality's economic and social standing. Chroniclers praised him as an exemplary ruler, and his royal title was last held by his grandson, Yuri I, who died in 1308.

## Terms
- **Boyars**: The powerful land-owning nobility in Kievan Rus' and its successor states.
- **Golden Horde**: The Mongol and later Turkicized khanate established in Eastern Europe after the Mongol invasions.
- **Kumiz**: Fermented mare's milk, a traditional drink of the Mongols and steppe nomads.
- **Voivode**: A military commander or governor of a fortress or region.
- **Rurikids**: The ruling dynasty of Kievan Rus' and its successor principalities, descended from the Varangian prince Rurik.
- **Suzerainty**: A relationship in which one state controls the foreign affairs of a tributary state while allowing it internal autonomy.
- **Principality**: A territory ruled by a prince, a common political unit in medieval Rus'.
- **Halych**: The capital city of the Principality of Galicia.

## Debates and Open Questions
The exact nature and extent of Daniel's submission to the Mongols is a point of historical analysis. While he formally accepted overlordship and provided soldiers, his subsequent foreign policy—including seeking a papal crown and Western alliances—demonstrated a clear strategy of resistance. The chroniclers' portrayal of him as a perfect ruler, second only to Solomon, is also a subject of scholarly consideration, reflecting the political and cultural ideals of his time.

# Sylhet

## Overview
Sylhet is a major metropolitan city in northeastern Bangladesh, serving as the administrative center for the Sylhet Division. It is the country's third-largest urban area, known for its tea plantations, natural scenery, and significant economic role, particularly through remittances from the Bangladeshi diaspora. The city's history spans from ancient settlements to its establishment in the 14th century, after which it was ruled by various powers before becoming part of modern Bangladesh.

## Timeline
- **1303** — Sultan Shamsuddin Firoz Shah conquers Sylhet, making it part of the Bengal Sultanate.
- **16th century** — Controlled by Baro-Bhuiyan zamindars; later becomes a sarkar (district) of the Mughal Empire.
- **1778-1790** — Robert Lindsay serves as District Collector, describing Sylhet as a "small bazaar."
- **1867** — Sylhet municipal board is established.
- **1874** — Sylhet becomes part of Colonial Assam.
- **1897** — A devastating earthquake demolishes almost the entire town; a new town is built.
- **1947** — Following a referendum and the partition of British India, Sylhet becomes part of East Pakistan.
- **1995** — Government of Bangladesh declares Sylhet the sixth divisional headquarters.
- **2001** — Municipality is upgraded to the Sylhet City Corporation.
- **2009** — Sylhet is made a metropolitan city.

## Body

### Etymology and History
The name "Sylhet" is an anglicisation of the native name *Śilhôṭ*, derived from the Sanskrit *Śrīhaṭṭa*. This name comes from the deity Śrīhaṭṭanātha, associated with the Nātha dynasty that promoted settlement in the region between the 12th and 13th centuries. The city's recorded history begins with its conquest in 1303 by the Sultan of Lakhnauti. It was later controlled by local zamindars before becoming a significant Mughal outpost in the 17th century. British rule began in the 18th century, and Sylhet became a key source of lascars (sailors) for the British Empire. The city was rebuilt after a major earthquake in 1897. The tea industry's establishment in the early 20th century increased its importance. After the 1947 partition referendum, Sylhet joined East Pakistan, and later Bangladesh.

### Geography and Climate
Sylhet is located in a topographically distinctive region characterized by hills and basins. Its geology is complex, with limestone deposits indicating the area was once under the ocean. The region is seismically active, having experienced three major earthquakes of at least 7.5 magnitude in the last 150 years, the most recent in 1918. The city has a tropical monsoon climate with a hot, humid rainy season (April–October) and a shorter dry season (November–February). Annual average rainfall is 4,200 mm, with nearly 80% falling between May and September.

### Administration and Demographics
Sylhet is administered by the Sylhet City Corporation, which was established in 2001 and expanded in 2021 to cover 79.5 square kilometers and 42 wards. As of the 2022 census, the city had a population of 532,839 with a literacy rate of 84.57%. The majority of the population are Bengali Muslims (90.52%), with significant Bengali Hindu and other minorities. The Sylheti language is widely spoken, while Standard Bengali is the official language.

### Economy and Infrastructure
Sylhet's economy is closely linked to the Bangladeshi diaspora, especially in the UK, with remittances driving growth in real estate and construction. The city is a center for religious and ecotourism. Its hinterland contains Bangladesh's largest natural gas fields, sole crude oil field, and largest tea plantations. Key industries include power, fertilizer, cement, and ready-made garments. Transport infrastructure includes Osmani International Airport (the country's third-busiest), a railway station, and road connections via the N2 highway. The city faces challenges with electricity and water shortages, and some water sources have arsenic contamination.

### Education and Notable Institutions
Sylhet is home to two public universities: Shahjalal University of Science and Technology and Sylhet Agricultural University, along with several private universities and medical colleges. The Bangladesh Army's 17th Infantry Division is based at Jalalabad Cantonment. The city also hosts the Sylhet International Cricket Stadium, home to the Sylhet Titans franchise.

## Terms
- **Sarkar**: A district or administrative unit, particularly under the Mughal Empire.
- **Zamindar**: A landowner or feudal lord in historical South Asia.
- **Lascar**: A sailor or militiaman from South Asia employed on European ships.
- **Beel**: A large, stagnant water body or oxbow lake, often formed by tectonic subsidence.
- **Metropolitan Thana**: A police station jurisdiction within a metropolitan area.
- **Mouza**: A type of administrative district, typically a small geographical area.
- **Remittances**: Money sent home by migrants working abroad, a major part of Sylhet's economy.
- **Sufi**: A mystic or member of a Muslim order devoted to spiritual practices.

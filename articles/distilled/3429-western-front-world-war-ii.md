# Western Front (World War II)

## Overview
The Western Front was a major theatre of World War II, encompassing the conflict between the Allied powers and Nazi Germany in Western Europe. It was marked by two distinct phases: a rapid Axis victory in 1940, followed by a prolonged Allied advance from 1944 to 1945 that culminated in Germany's defeat. The front's outcome was decisive in ending the war in Europe.

## Timeline
- **1 Sep 1939** — Germany invades Poland; Britain and France declare war.
- **Apr 1940** — Germany invades Denmark and Norway (Operation Weserübung).
- **May–Jun 1940** — Germany invades and defeats France, Belgium, the Netherlands, and Luxembourg.
- **Jun 1940** — France signs armistice; Vichy France established.
- **19 Aug 1942** — Allied Dieppe Raid ends in disaster but provides lessons.
- **6 Jun 1944** — Allied invasion of Normandy (Operation Overlord).
- **15 Aug 1944** — Allied invasion of Southern France (Operation Dragoon).
- **25 Aug 1944** — Liberation of Paris.
- **16 Dec 1944** — German Ardennes offensive (Battle of the Bulge) begins.
- **7 Mar 1945** — US forces capture the Ludendorff Bridge at Remagen, crossing the Rhine.
- **8 May 1945** — Germany signs unconditional surrender; V-E Day.

## Body

### Axis Victories (1939–1940)
After the declaration of war, a period of minimal ground combat known as the Phoney War ensued. This ended with Germany's invasion of Denmark and Norway in April 1940. In May, Germany launched the Battle of France, using a "blitzkrieg" strategy to rapidly defeat Allied forces. Luxembourg, the Netherlands, Belgium, and France fell within weeks. The British Expeditionary Force evacuated from Dunkirk, and France signed an armistice on 22 June 1940, leading to the occupation of northern France and the creation of the collaborationist Vichy regime in the south.

### Interlude and Build-Up (1941–1944)
With Britain undefeated after the Battle of Britain, Germany focused on the Eastern Front and built the Atlantic Wall defensive fortifications along the French coast. The Allies conducted a disastrous practice raid on Dieppe in 1942, learning crucial lessons for a future invasion. During this period, the main Allied effort was in the Mediterranean, while a strategic bombing campaign against Germany was waged by the US Eighth Air Force by day and RAF Bomber Command by night. By 1944, German forces in the West were dispersed to defend against a possible Allied landing.

### The Second Front (1944–1945)
The Allied invasion of Normandy on 6 June 1944 opened the long-awaited second front. After a slow breakout, Allied forces rapidly liberated France, with Paris freed on 25 August. A second invasion, Operation Dragoon, liberated southern France. The advance stalled at the German border due to supply shortages and the fortified Siegfried Line. A bold Allied airborne operation, Market Garden, failed to secure a crossing over the Rhine at Arnhem. In December, Germany launched a major counter-offensive in the Ardennes (Battle of the Bulge), which was eventually repulsed.

### Invasion of Germany and End of the War
In early 1945, Allied forces fought through the Rhineland, capturing 280,000 German prisoners. The Rhine was crossed at several points in March, most famously by US troops at Remagen. Allied armies then fanned out across Germany. The US 12th Army Group encircled 300,000 Germans in the Ruhr Pocket. While some leaders like Churchill urged a drive for Berlin, Supreme Commander Eisenhower halted major Western Allied advances at the Elbe and Mulde rivers, leaving Berlin to the Soviets. Germany surrendered unconditionally on 7 May 1945, with operations ceasing on 8 May.

## Terms
- **Blitzkrieg**: A military strategy emphasizing rapid, concentrated attacks with combined arms (tanks, infantry, air support) to disorganize and encircle the enemy.
- **Phoney War**: The period of World War II from September 1939 to May 1940 with minimal military operations on the Western Front.
- **Atlantic Wall**: A system of coastal fortifications built by Nazi Germany along the coast of continental Europe to repel an Allied invasion.
- **Operation Overlord**: The codename for the Allied invasion of Normandy on 6 June 1944.
- **Siegfried Line (Westwall)**: A German defensive system of fortifications along the western border of Germany.
- **V-1/V-2 Vergeltungswaffen**: German "reprisal weapons," the world's first long-range guided ballistic missiles and cruise missiles.
- **Red Ball Express**: A massive, truck-based supply system used by the Allies to transport materiel from the Normandy beaches to the front lines.
- **SHAEF**: Supreme Headquarters, Allied Expeditionary Forces, the headquarters of the Commander of Allied Expeditionary Forces in northwestern Europe during WWII.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Broad-front vs. narrow-front strategy**: A major debate among Allied commanders. Eisenhower favored a broad advance to share the burden, while Montgomery argued for a concentrated "narrow front" thrust, potentially to capture Berlin first.
- **The decision not to take Berlin**: Eisenhower's decision to halt at the Elbe and leave Berlin to the Soviets remains a subject of historical debate, balancing military pragmatism against political considerations for the post-war order.
- **German casualty figures**: Precise German losses on the Western Front are difficult to establish, with sources providing conflicting numbers, particularly for the final months of the war.

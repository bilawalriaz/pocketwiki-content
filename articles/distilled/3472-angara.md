# Angara

## Overview
The Angara is a major Siberian river in Russia, notable as the sole outlet of Lake Baikal and the headwater tributary of the Yenisey River. Its course has been significantly altered by a series of major hydroelectric dams constructed since the 1950s, which created large reservoirs and flooded historical settlements and agricultural lands.

## Timeline
- **c. 1630** — The Angara and Ilim rivers become key water routes for Russian colonization of Siberia.
- **1950s–1980s** — Four major hydroelectric dams are constructed on the Angara.
- **1976** — Valentin Rasputin publishes the novel *Farewell to Matyora*, criticizing dam construction.
- **1991** — Valentin Rasputin publishes *Siberia, Siberia*, further criticizing dam construction.

## Body

### Course and Geography
The Angara flows 1,849 km from Lake Baikal through Irkutsk Oblast and Krasnoyarsk Krai, joining the Yenisey near Strelka. It was historically known as the Lower Angara and, below its junction with the Ilim, as the Upper Tunguska. Its drainage basin covers 1,039,000 km².

### Dams and Reservoirs
Four major dams—Irkutsk, Bratsk, Ust-Ilimsk, and Boguchany—were built from the 1950s onward. Their reservoirs flooded villages, the historic Ilimsk fort, and agricultural areas. This development was criticized by Soviet intellectuals, including writer Valentin Rasputin, for its impact on rural communities.

### Navigation and Historical Importance
The river is navigable only in isolated sections due to the dams, none of which have ship locks. Despite this, the Angara and Ilim were crucial for Russian expansion into Siberia, connecting the Yenisey, Lake Baikal, and the Lena River via portages. Its transportation role diminished after the construction of an overland route and the Trans-Siberian Railway.

## Terms
- **Headwater tributary**: The primary river that feeds into a larger river system.
- **Drainage basin**: The area of land where all water drains into a common outlet.
- **Hydroelectric plant**: A facility that generates electricity using the flow of water.
- **Reservoir**: An artificial lake created by damming a river.
- **Ship lock**: A device for raising and lowering boats between stretches of water of different levels.
- **Portage**: The carrying of a boat or cargo over land between two navigable waters.

## Debates and Open Questions
The construction of dams on the Angara sparked criticism from Soviet intellectuals, notably Valentin Rasputin, who opposed the flooding of villages and agricultural lands. The long-term ecological and social impacts of these reservoirs on the Baikal region and river communities remain subjects of discussion.

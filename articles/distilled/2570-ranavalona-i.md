# Ranavalona I

## Overview
Ranavalona I (1778–1861) was the sovereign of the Kingdom of Madagascar from 1828 to 1861. A pivotal and controversial figure, she pursued aggressive isolationism, military expansion, and cultural preservation to protect Malagasy sovereignty against European influence. Her 33-year reign saw significant population decline due to warfare, forced labor, and harsh justice, leading to her characterization as a tyrant, though modern scholarship often reinterprets her actions as a defense of national independence.

## Body

### Accession to the Throne
Princess Ramavo was betrothed to Prince Radama by his father, King Andrianampoinimerina. Upon Radama's death in 1828 without an heir, the rightful successor by custom was his nephew Rakotobe. However, military officers conspired to support Ramavo's claim, securing the army's backing. She declared herself successor on 11 August 1828 and systematically eliminated rivals, including Rakotobe and his family. Her 1829 coronation made her the first female sovereign of the Kingdom of Imerina since its 1540 founding.

### Government and Domestic Policy
Ranavalona ruled from the royal Rova compound in Antananarivo, where the new palace Manjakamiadana was built between 1839 and 1842. She continued many of Radama I's policies, encouraging foreign technology while restricting foreign influence. She relied on aristocratic advisers, often marrying her most powerful ministers. Her first chief adviser, the progressive Andriamihaja, was executed in 1830. She then married the conservative Field Marshal Rainiharo, who served as Prime Minister until his death in 1852. She communicated policy through letters and strengthened traditional rituals to bolster her authority.

### Preservation and Expansion of Realm
Ranavalona continued military campaigns to subjugate the entire island, using a standing army of 20,000 to 30,000 soldiers raised through *fanompoana* (forced labor as tax payment). Conquered populations faced mass execution or enslavement, with approximately one million slaves brought to Imerina between 1820 and 1853. These campaigns, combined with high soldier mortality from malaria and the widespread use of the *tangena* ordeal—a poisonous trial used to determine guilt—led to a catastrophic population decline, estimated by some to be as much as half during her reign.

### Repression of Christianity
Initially, Ranavalona allowed the London Missionary Society (LMS) to operate schools and a printing press. However, as conversions grew, she saw Christianity as a threat to traditional authority. In 1835, she formally banned its practice for Malagasy subjects, though foreigners were permitted to worship freely. Christians were subjected to fines, imprisonment, the *tangena* ordeal, or execution. Most missionaries departed, and persecution intensified in waves, notably in 1849 when 1,900 people were punished and 18 executed.

### Protection of Sovereignty and Foreign Plots
Ranavalona annulled the Anglo-Merina treaty to end British influence and the slave trade ban, which cut off modern weaponry imports. She countered this by using the expertise of shipwrecked Frenchman Jean Laborde to establish domestic arms factories. France and Britain vied for influence, with France seeking to exploit the island via the Lambert Charter signed by her son, Radama II. A 1857 coup plot involving Lambert and Radama II was discovered and thwarted, leading to the expulsion of most Europeans. In 1834, she politely declined a marriage alliance proposal from the Omani Sultan Said bin Sultan.

### Succession and Death
Ranavalona designated her son, Radama II, as successor, though conservative factions preferred her nephew. Progressive ministers ensured Radama II's accession upon her death on 16 August 1861. Her funeral was marked by a tragic explosion that destroyed several royal residences. Her body was later moved by French colonial authorities in 1897.

## Terms
- **Fanompoana**: Traditional forced labor in lieu of tax payments, used for public works and military conscription.
- **Tangena**: A harsh trial by ordeal using a poisonous nut to determine guilt or innocence; often fatal.
- **Sampy**: Royal idols believed to embody the supernatural power of the Merina kingship.
- **Kabary**: Traditional public oratory used by Merina sovereigns to communicate policy.
- **Hasina**: Ancestral blessings bestowed by the sovereign through traditional rituals.
- **Andevo**: The slave class in Malagasy society.
- **Rova**: The royal palace compound in Antananarivo.
- **Lambert Charter**: An 1855 agreement granting Frenchman Joseph-François Lambert exploitation rights to Madagascar's resources.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Historical Characterization**: Western contemporaries depicted Ranavalona as a "mad tyrant." Modern historians argue her policies were rational measures to safeguard sovereignty against European imperialism.
- **Population Decline**: Estimates of population loss vary, with some suggesting a decline of up to half, attributed to warfare, forced labor, disease, and the *tangena* ordeal.
- **Radama II's Role**: Accounts differ on whether Radama II was a willing participant or an unwitting pawn in the 1857 coup plot.
- **Legacy in Madagascar**: Views remain complex; she is condemned by many for her brutality, but admired by others for her defense of Malagasy independence and traditions.

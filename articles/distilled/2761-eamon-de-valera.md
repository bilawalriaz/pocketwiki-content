# Éamon de Valera

## Overview
Éamon de Valera (1882–1975) was a dominant Irish statesman, serving as head of government for 21 years and head of state for 14. Central to the revolutionary period, the 1937 Constitution, and the evolution of the Free State into a sovereign republic, his career spanned from the 1916 Easter Rising to the early 1970s, making him a defining, though divisive, figure in modern Irish history.

## Timeline
- **14 October 1882** — Born in New York City.
- **24 April 1916** — Commands Irish Volunteers at Boland's Mill during the Easter Rising.
- **10 July 1917** — Elected MP for East Clare in a by-election.
- **21 January 1919** — Dáil Éireann first meets; de Valera is imprisoned.
- **April 1919** — Becomes President of Dáil Éireann after escaping Lincoln Gaol.
- **6 December 1921** — Anglo-Irish Treaty is signed in London.
- **June 1922 – May 1923** — Irish Civil War; de Valera leads the anti-Treaty side.
- **March 1926** — Founds the Fianna Fáil party.
- **9 March 1932** — Becomes President of the Executive Council (Prime Minister).
- **1 July 1937** — Constitution of Ireland is approved by plebiscite.
- **29 December 1937** — Constitution comes into force; de Valera becomes Taoiseach.
- **2 September 1939** — Ireland declares neutrality in World War II ("The Emergency").
- **17 June 1959** — Elected President of Ireland.
- **29 August 1975** — Dies in Dublin.

## Body

### Revolutionary Period and Civil War
Born in New York and raised in Ireland, de Valera was a mathematics teacher who joined the Irish Volunteers in 1913. He commanded a garrison during the 1916 Easter Rising. Sentenced to death, his life was saved by his American citizenship and British concerns over antagonizing the United States. He became a leading Sinn Féin figure and was elected President of Dáil Éireann in 1919. He led a U.S. fundraising mission (1919–1920) but did not travel to London for the 1921 Treaty negotiations. The Anglo-Irish Treaty, creating the Irish Free State as a British dominion, split the republican movement. De Valera led the anti-Treaty minority out of the Dáil, and the ensuing Civil War was won by pro-Treaty forces.

### Founding Fianna Fáil and Constitutional Change
After the Civil War, de Valera concluded that abstentionism was ineffective. In 1926, he founded Fianna Fáil to achieve a republic from within the constitutional system. He became head of government in 1932 and dismantled the 1922 Free State Constitution's links to the UK. The 1937 Constitution, drafted under his direction, renamed the state "Éire," claimed jurisdiction over the whole island, removed the Governor-General, and created a powerful elected President. It also enshrined conservative Catholic social teaching, notably banning divorce and recognizing the Church's "special position."

### World War II and Later Governments
De Valera's government maintained strict neutrality during World War II, termed "The Emergency," despite pressure from both Allied and Axis powers. His post-war governments saw periods in and out of power. He served as Taoiseach again from 1951–1954 and 1957–1959. His final term introduced the First Programme for Economic Expansion and a harsh response to an IRA border campaign. In 1959, he retired as Taoiseach to become President of Ireland, serving two terms until 1973.

### Legacy and Historical Assessment
De Valera's legacy is contested. Biographer Tim Pat Coogan characterizes his rule as one of economic and cultural stagnation, while Diarmaid Ferriter argues the stereotype of him as an austere, backward figure is largely a later construction. A deeply religious man, his beliefs evolved from militant republicanism to strong social and fiscal conservatism. He was criticized for establishing a conservative Catholic state and his wartime neutrality. However, he is credited with guiding the state to full sovereignty through constitutional means and maintaining democratic stability.

## Terms
*   **Abstentionism:** The policy of refusing to take seats in a parliament (e.g., Dáil Éireann or Westminster) on the grounds that the parliament's authority is not recognized.
*   **Anglo-Irish Treaty (1921):** The agreement that ended the Irish War of Independence, creating the Irish Free State as a dominion within the British Commonwealth and partitioning Ireland.
*   **Dáil Éireann:** The lower house of the Irish parliament; the assembly of elected representatives (TDs).
*   **Fianna Fáil:** The republican political party founded by de Valera in 1926, which dominated Irish politics for much of the 20th century.
*   **Taoiseach:** The title of the Irish head of government (Prime Minister), created by the 1937 Constitution.
*   **The Emergency:** The term used for the period of World War II in Ireland, during which the state maintained official neutrality.
*   **Oath of Allegiance:** A constitutional requirement for Irish Free State politicians to swear fidelity to the King and the Free State, which de Valera and his party initially refused to take.
*   **President of the Executive Council:** The title of the Irish head of government before the 1937 Constitution; a less powerful office than Taoiseach.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Role in the Civil War:** Historians debate the extent of de Valera's responsibility for the outbreak of the Civil War and his influence over the anti-Treaty IRA during the conflict.
*   **Easter Rising Conduct:** There are conflicting accounts of his bravery and mental state during the 1916 Rising, with detractors claiming he suffered a nervous breakdown and supporters highlighting his leadership.
*   **Historical Reputation:** A major historiographical debate exists between assessments like Coogan's, which view his legacy negatively, and Ferriter's, which seeks to reassess and contextualize his actions within the constraints of his time.
*   **Constitutional Legacy:** Scholars continue to analyze the motivations behind the 1937 Constitution, particularly its incorporation of Catholic social teaching and its claim to the whole island, and its long-term impact on Irish society.

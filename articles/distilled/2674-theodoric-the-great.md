# Theodoric the Great

## Overview
Theodoric the Great (c. 454–526) was king of the Ostrogoths and ruler of Italy from 493 to 526. He created a vast Gothic empire from the Atlantic to the Adriatic by conquering Italy and later controlling the Visigothic Kingdom. His reign is noted for preserving Roman administration and culture while maintaining a separate Gothic military elite, making his kingdom one of the most stable and prosperous successor states to the Western Roman Empire.

## Timeline
- **c. 454** — Born to an Ostrogothic nobleman, Theodemir, in Pannonia.
- **461** — Taken as a hostage to Constantinople, where he received a Roman education.
- **471** — Recognized as King of the Ostrogoths by 6,000 Goths.
- **483** — Made *magister militum* (master of soldiers) by Emperor Zeno.
- **488** — Ordered by Zeno to invade Italy and overthrow Odoacer.
- **493** — Entered Ravenna after defeating Odoacer, becoming ruler of Italy.
- **500** — Visited Rome, holding games and restoring public largesse.
- **505–511** — Expanded into the Balkans and brought the Visigothic Kingdom under his control.
- **522** — Had the philosopher Boethius executed on charges of treason.
- **526** — Died of dysentery; succeeded by his grandson Athalaric.

## Body

### Early Life and Rise to Power
Theodoric was born c. 454 into the Amal dynasty of the Ostrogoths. In 461, he was sent as a hostage to Constantinople, where he received a Roman education (*paideia*). He returned to Pannonia c. 470 and gained recognition as king in 471. Throughout the 470s, he campaigned for the Eastern Roman Empire against rivals like the Gothic chieftain Theodoric Strabo, while also frequently raiding imperial territory. In 483, Emperor Zeno made him a commander of Eastern forces and consul in 484.

### Conquest and Rule of Italy
In 488, Emperor Zeno ordered Theodoric to overthrow Odoacer, the Germanic king of Italy. After a series of battles, Theodoric besieged Odoacer in Ravenna. A treaty in 493 made them joint rulers, but at a celebratory banquet, Theodoric killed Odoacer and seized sole power. He settled his Gothic warriors in concentrated areas around Pavia, Ravenna, and Picenum, using a modified Roman *hospitalitas* system to grant them land. His kingdom was highly "Roman"; he upheld Roman law for the Roman population while Goths lived under their own customs, a principle of "integration through separation." He promoted a major building program, restoring cities like Rome and his capital, Ravenna, where he built a palace and mausoleum.

### Foreign Policy and Hegemony
Theodoric built a vast network of alliances through marriage, positioning himself as the senior Western monarch. He married Audofleda, sister of the Frankish king Clovis, and married his daughters to the kings of the Visigoths and Burgundians. In 505–511, he expanded into the Balkans and, after the Visigothic king Alaric II was defeated by the Franks in 507, Theodoric became regent for his grandson Amalaric, bringing the Visigothic Kingdom under his direct control by 511. This created a Gothic superstate stretching from the Atlantic to the Danube.

### Final Years and Legacy
Theodoric's later reign was marked by paranoia and religious tension. In 522, he had the philosopher Boethius and his father-in-law Symmachus executed for alleged treason. An Arian Christian, his relations with the Catholic Roman aristocracy and the Orthodox Eastern Empire soured. He died in 526, succeeded by his young grandson Athalaric, with his daughter Amalasuintha as regent. His kingdom fragmented after his death, leading to the Gothic War with the Eastern Empire. He lived on in medieval Germanic legend as Dietrich von Bern.

## Terms
- **Paideia**: The classical Greek and Roman system of education and cultural training.
- **Foederati**: Barbarian groups (like the Goths) who served as allied troops under treaty with the Roman Empire.
- **Hospitalitas**: A Roman system for billeting soldiers, adapted by Theodoric to settle Gothic warriors on land.
- **Arianism**: A Christian theological position (deemed heretical by the Catholic Church) that held Christ was created by and subordinate to God the Father.
- **Princeps**: An archaic Roman title meaning "first citizen," traditionally used by emperors.
- **Magister militum**: A high-ranking military command in the late Roman Empire, meaning "master of soldiers."
- **Res Publica**: Latin for "public matter," the term for the Roman state, which Theodoric used for his domain.
- **Amal dynasty**: The royal lineage of the Ostrogoths, from which Theodoric claimed descent.

## Debates and Open Questions
A central debate concerns the nature of Theodoric's power. Some historians, like Jonathan J. Arnold, argue he was effectively a Western Roman emperor, citing his use of the title *princeps*, imperial ceremonies, and the view of contemporary Italian nobles. Others, like Hans-Ulrich Wiemer, reject this, contending that Theodoric maintained a clear separation between Goths and Romans, that the Eastern Empire never formally recognized him as emperor, and that his title was always "king" (*rex*). This debate hinges on whether his rule represented a restoration of Roman imperial authority in the West or a new, distinct barbarian kingdom that merely adopted Roman forms.

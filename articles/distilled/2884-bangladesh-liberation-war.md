# Bangladesh Liberation War

## Overview
The Bangladesh Liberation War (1971) was an armed conflict that led to the independence of Bangladesh from Pakistan. Sparked by a brutal military crackdown in East Pakistan, the war involved a Bengali guerrilla resistance, a massive refugee crisis, and a decisive Indian military intervention. It fundamentally altered the geopolitical landscape of South Asia and resulted in widespread atrocities, including what many scholars and international observers have termed a genocide.

## Timeline
- **25 March 1971** — Pakistan Army launches Operation Searchlight in East Pakistan, initiating the Bangladesh genocide.
- **26 March 1971** — Sheikh Mujibur Rahman declares the independence of Bangladesh.
- **17 April 1971** — A provisional government for Bangladesh is formed in Meherpur.
- **3 December 1971** — India enters the war following Pakistani preemptive air strikes.
- **16 December 1971** — Pakistani forces in Dhaka surrender to the Allied Forces of India and the Mukti Bahini.
- **25 January 1972** — The Soviet Union recognizes Bangladesh.
- **8 April 1972** — The United States recognizes Bangladesh.

## Body

### Background and Causes
The Dominion of Pakistan, created in 1947, consisted of two geographically and culturally separate wings: West Pakistan and East Pakistan (modern-day Bangladesh). Despite East Pakistan having a larger population, political and economic power was concentrated in the West. Grievances included economic disparity, political marginalization, and cultural suppression, notably the 1948 imposition of Urdu as the sole national language, which sparked the Bengali Language Movement. The 1970 Bhola cyclone, which killed an estimated 300,000 people, further highlighted the government's neglect of the east.

### The 1970 Election and Escalation
In the 1970 national elections, the Awami League, led by Sheikh Mujibur Rahman, won a landslide majority, securing a constitutional right to form a government. The West Pakistani establishment, led by Zulfikar Ali Bhutto and President Yahya Khan, refused to transfer power. Negotiations failed, and on 25 March 1971, the military launched Operation Searchlight to crush Bengali nationalism. The operation targeted students, intellectuals, and minorities, leading to mass killings. In response, Rahman declared independence, and the Mukti Bahini (freedom fighters) formed to wage a guerrilla war.

### The War and Indian Intervention
The Mukti Bahini, aided by India, conducted guerrilla operations, liberating territory. An estimated 10 million refugees fled to India, creating a humanitarian crisis. On 3 December 1971, after Pakistan launched preemptive air strikes on India, India formally entered the war. The Indian Air Force quickly achieved air superiority in the east, and Indian and Mukti Bahini forces advanced rapidly on Dhaka. The Pakistani army, unable to defend the capital, surrendered on 16 December 1971, marking the largest surrender of military personnel since World War II.

### Atrocities and International Reactions
The Pakistani military and allied militias (Razakars, Al-Badr, Al-Shams) engaged in a systematic campaign of mass murder, rape, and displacement. Casualty estimates range from 300,000 to 3 million, with 200,000 to 400,000 women raped. The conflict was a major Cold War episode: the United States supported Pakistan, while the Soviet Union backed India and Bangladesh. China, a Pakistani ally, vetoed Bangladesh's UN membership in 1972. Most UN member states recognized Bangladesh as sovereign in 1972.

## Terms
- **Mukti Bahini**: The guerrilla resistance movement formed by Bengali military, paramilitary, and civilians to fight for Bangladesh's independence.
- **Operation Searchlight**: The codename for the planned military crackdown by the Pakistan Army that began on 25 March 1971.
- **Razakars, Al-Badr, Al-Shams**: Radical religious militias created by the Pakistan Army to assist in operations against the local populace.
- **Instrument of Surrender**: The document signed on 16 December 1971 by Lt. Gen. A.A.K. Niazi, formalizing the surrender of Pakistani forces in East Pakistan.
- **One Unit scheme**: A political arrangement in Pakistan that merged all provinces of West Pakistan into a single unit to counterbalance East Pakistan's population majority.
- **Provisional Government of Bangladesh**: The government-in-exile formed in April 1971 to lead the independence movement and seek international recognition.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Casualty Figures**: The exact number of people killed during the war is highly debated, with estimates ranging from 300,000 to 3 million. Independent researchers have proposed figures between 125,000 and 505,000.
- **International Recognition**: The war's recognition as a genocide is contested. While Bangladeshi media and many international publications use the term, Pakistani authorities dispute the accusations against their forces.
- **Role of the United Nations**: The UN condemned human rights violations but failed to intervene politically before the war began. Its Security Council resolutions for a ceasefire were vetoed by the Soviet Union.

# Gregory of Nazianzus

## Overview

Gregory of Nazianzus (c. 329 – 25 January 390) was a Greek-speaking Christian theologian, prelate, and rhetorician who served as Archbishop of Constantinople from 380 to 381. Known as "Theologian" and one of the Cappadocian Fathers, he profoundly shaped Trinitarian doctrine, especially pneumatology (theology of the Holy Spirit), and set the literary and theological standard for Byzantine Christianity.

## Timeline

- **c. 329** — Born in Arianzus, Cappadocia, to a Christian family
- **361** — Ordained presbyter in Nazianzus by his father
- **362–363** — Composed *Invectives Against Julian*
- **370** — Friend Basil becomes Bishop of Caesarea
- **372** — Ordained Bishop of Sasima by Basil
- **374** — Parents die; withdraws to monastery at Seleukia
- **379** — Goes to Constantinople; establishes Anastasia church
- **380** — Entroned as Bishop of Constantinople
- **381** — Leads First Council of Constantinople; resigns during the council
- **383** — Retires to Arianzus; begins *De Vita Sua*
- **390** — Dies at Arianzus on 25 January

## Body

### Early Life and Education

Born into a wealthy Christian family in southwest Cappadocia, Gregory received classical training in rhetoric and philosophy in Athens, where he studied under Himerius and Proaeresius. There, he formed a lifelong friendship with Basil of Caesarea and encountered the future Emperor Julian. A storm at sea led him to vow lifelong service to Christ, marking the beginning of his spiritual journey.

### Priesthood and Early Ministry

Returning to Nazianzus in 361, Gregory reluctantly accepted ordination as presbyter under pressure from his father. He later joined Basil in ascetic life before returning to assist his father amid local theological conflicts. His *Invectives Against Julian* (362–363) defended Christianity against the emperor’s anti-Christian policies, emphasizing love, patience, and theosis (deification). After Julian’s death, he worked to counter Arianism alongside Basil, achieving public rhetorical victories.

### Episcopate in Sasima and Nazianzus

In 372, Basil ordained Gregory Bishop of Sasima, a remote and impoverished see, to strengthen his own position against Anthimus of Tyana. Gregory resented the appointment, calling it a “dreadful, pokey little hole,” and made little effort to govern. After his parents’ death in 374, he administered Nazianzus but refused formal episcopal consecration, donating his inheritance to the poor. He withdrew to a monastery at Seleukia until Basil’s death in 379.

### At Constantinople

Following Emperor Theodosius I’s accession, Gregory was invited to Constantinople in 379 to lead the Nicene cause. He transformed a villa into the church of Anastasia and delivered five theological orations defending Trinitarian orthodoxy, particularly the divinity of the Holy Spirit. Despite popular support, he faced opposition from Arian mobs and political intrigue, including betrayal by Maximus I. Theodosius’s arrival in 380 secured Gregory’s enthronement as Bishop of Constantinople.

### Council and Retirement

At the First Council of Constantinople (381), Gregory presided after Meletius’s death but resigned dramatically during the council due to exhaustion and canonical disputes over his transfer from Sasima. Theodosius accepted his resignation. Returning to Cappadocia, Gregory continued combating Apollinarianism and composed *De Vita Sua*. He retired to Arianzus in 383, living as a hermit until his death in 390.

## Terms

- **Theologian**: Title granted only to Gregory, John the Apostle, and Symeon the New Theologian in Orthodox tradition.
- **Cappadocian Fathers**: Basil of Caesarea, Gregory of Nyssa, and Gregory of Nazianzus; key developers of Trinitarian doctrine.
- **Pneumatology**: Theological study of the Holy Spirit’s nature and role.
- **Theosis (Deification)**: Process by which believers attain union with God through grace.
- **Homoousia**: Nicene term affirming the Son’s consubstantiality with the Father.
- **Anomoios/Homoiousios**: Arian and Semi-Arian terms describing the Son as “unlike” or “like” the Father.
- **Asceticism**: Practice of spiritual discipline through self-denial and withdrawal.
- **Patristic Age**: Period of early Christian theological writing, ending around 800 AD.
- **Nicene Orthodoxy**: Post-381 doctrinal consensus affirming full divinity of Father, Son, and Holy Spirit.
- **Oration**: Rhetorical discourse; Gregory’s theological works are called "Five Theological Orations."

## Debates and Open Questions

- **Canonical legitimacy of Gregory’s transfer**: His opponents at the Council of Constantinople questioned the legality of moving him from Sasima to Constantinople, contributing to his resignation.
- **Extent of Gregory’s development of “procession”**: While he introduced the term to distinguish the Spirit’s relationship to the Father from the Son’s generation, he did not fully elaborate the concept, leaving later theologians to expand it.
- **Motivations behind his resignation**: Whether driven by genuine humility, political pressure, or health concerns remains debated among scholars.
- **Role of rhetoric in theology**: Gregory’s classical education raised questions about the appropriateness of pagan rhetorical forms in Christian doctrine—a tension that influenced later Byzantine thought.

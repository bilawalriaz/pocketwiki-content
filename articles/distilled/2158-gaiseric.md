# Gaiseric

## Overview
Gaiseric (c. 389–477 AD) was the king of the Vandals and Alans who transformed a migrating Germanic tribe into a major Mediterranean power. His conquest of Roman North Africa and the sack of Rome in 455 AD were pivotal events in the decline of the Western Roman Empire, as he secured a wealthy kingdom that challenged imperial control over the western Mediterranean.

## Timeline
- **428** — Gaiseric becomes king of the Vandals after his half-brother Gunderic's death.
- **428/429** — Leads the Vandal migration from Hispania to North Africa.
- **435** — Signs a treaty with Rome, recognized as king of conquered lands in exchange for tribute.
- **439** — Captures the city of Carthage, seizing the Roman fleet and a major revenue source.
- **442** — Signs a new treaty with Rome, recognized as independent ruler of Byzacena and part of Numidia.
- **455** — Sacks Rome for two weeks following the murder of Emperor Valentinian III.
- **460** — Preemptively destroys a Roman invasion fleet being assembled by Emperor Majorian.
- **468** — Inflicts a catastrophic defeat on a combined Roman-Eastern fleet led by Basiliscus.
- **474** — Makes peace with the Eastern Roman Empire.
- **477** — Dies in Carthage; succeeded by his son Huneric.

## Body

### Path to Kingship and Migration
After his father's death, Gaiseric became the second most powerful Vandal noble under his half-brother King Gunderic. Following Gunderic's death in 428, Gaiseric became king. Facing pressure from the more numerous Visigoths in Hispania, he decided to migrate his people. In 428/429, he led the Vandals, along with Alans and Goths, across the Strait of Gibraltar to North Africa, possibly invited by the Roman governor Bonifacius. After defeating Roman forces, including at the Battle of Mérida (428), the migration began.

### Conquest of North Africa
In Africa, Gaiseric's forces quickly overran Roman territories. They defeated Bonifacius at the Battle of Calama and besieged Hippo Regius for 14 months. A 435 treaty with Rome recognized Gaiseric as king of his conquests in exchange for tribute and his son Huneric as a hostage. In a surprise attack in 439, Gaiseric captured Carthage, a devastating blow to the Empire. This gave him control of a wealthy province, a large Roman fleet, and the ability to tax the grain supply to Italy. He made Carthage his capital and built a powerful navy, challenging Roman dominance of the Mediterranean.

### Sack of Rome and Later Exploits
In 455, after Emperor Valentinian III was murdered and his daughter Eudocia—betrothed to Gaiseric's son—was married to the usurper's son, Gaiseric invaded Italy. He sacked Rome systematically for two weeks, plundering immense wealth and capturing Empress Eudoxia and her daughters, but avoided total destruction after negotiations with Pope Leo I. The sack paralyzed the Western imperial government.

Gaiseric later repulsed two major Roman attempts to reclaim Africa. In 460, he destroyed Emperor Majorian's invasion fleet at Carthago Nova. In 468, he used fireships to annihilate a massive combined Roman-Eastern fleet led by Basiliscus, a defeat that cost the Empire enormous sums of gold and silver. These victories secured his kingdom. He made peace with the Eastern Empire in 474 and died in 477, leaving a powerful Vandal state that lasted until Justinian's reconquest in the 6th century.

## Terms
- **Vandals**: A Germanic people who migrated under Gaiseric from Hispania to establish a kingdom in North Africa.
- **Alans**: An Iranian nomadic people who allied with the Vandals during their migration.
- **Arianism**: A Christian doctrine considered heretical by the Roman Catholic Church; Gaiseric enforced it at his court.
- **Foederati**: Barbarian groups allied with the Roman Empire under a special treaty, as the Vandals were initially recognized.
- **Carthage**: A major Roman city and port in North Africa; its capture by Gaiseric in 439 was a turning point.
- **Sack of Rome (455)**: The systematic plundering of the city of Rome by the Vandals, lasting two weeks.
- **Fireships**: Ships set on fire and sent into enemy fleets, a tactic Gaiseric used to destroy the Roman armada in 468.
- **Nicene Christianity**: The orthodox form of Christianity adhered to by the Roman Empire, which the Vandals persecuted.

## Debates and Open questions
- **Scale of Migration**: The number of people Gaiseric led to Africa is debated; ancient sources suggest 80,000, but scholars argue it was likely closer to 20,000.
- **Religious Persecution**: The timing and severity of Vandal persecution of Nicene Christians is debated. While the historian Victor of Vita claimed it began immediately after the capture of Carthage, scholar Peter Heather argues systematic policies were likely implemented only after 442, and were not uniformly applied across all Vandal provinces.
- **Motives for Migration**: The invitation by Roman governor Bonifacius is presented as a possibility, not a certainty, in the source.

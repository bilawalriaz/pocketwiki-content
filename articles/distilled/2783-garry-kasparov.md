# Garry Kasparov

## Overview
Garry Kasparov is a Russian chess grandmaster, writer, and political activist who was the World Chess Champion from 1985 to 2000. He is widely considered one of the greatest chess players of all time, holding the record for the longest period as the world's No. 1 ranked player. His career is defined by his intense rivalry with Anatoly Karpov, his break from the World Chess Federation (FIDE), and his historic matches against computers, most notably IBM's Deep Blue.

## Timeline

- **1993** — Breaks from FIDE to form the Professional Chess Association (PCA), splitting the world title.
- **1997** — Loses a match to IBM's Deep Blue, the first defeat of a reigning world champion by a computer under standard controls.

- **2009–2010** — Coaches Magnus Carlsen, who rises to world No. 1.
- **2013** — Leaves Russia; later obtains Croatian citizenship.
- **2014** — Loses FIDE presidential election to Kirsan Ilyumzhinov.
- **2022** — Designated a "foreign agent" by Russia.

## Body

### Early Life and Rise to the Top
Born to a Jewish father and Armenian mother in Baku, Kasparov adopted his mother's surname at age 12 after his father's death. He began serious chess study at age seven and trained at Mikhail Botvinnik's chess school. He won the Soviet Junior Championship in 1976 and 1977. Kasparov became the youngest-ever player to qualify for the USSR Chess Championship in 1978. He won the World Junior Championship in 1980 and earned the Grandmaster title that year. He rose rapidly through the world rankings, becoming the youngest-ever world No. 1 in January 1984.

### World Championship Rivalry with Karpov
Kasparov's first World Championship match against Karpov (1984–85) was abandoned without a result by FIDE President Florencio Campomanes after 48 games, with Karpov leading 5–3. In the rematch later in 1985, Kasparov won 13–11 to become the youngest undisputed champion. He defended the title against Karpov in 1986, 1987, and 1990, winning all three matches. Their five matches comprised 144 games, with Kasparov scoring 21 wins, 19 losses, and 104 draws.

### Break with FIDE and the Title Split
In 1993, Kasparov and his challenger Nigel Short rejected FIDE's organization and formed the PCA, creating a rival world championship. This split the title for 13 years. Kasparov defended his PCA title against Viswanathan Anand in 1995. He lost the "Classical" title to Vladimir Kramnik in 2000 but remained the world's highest-rated player until his retirement in 2005, citing a lack of personal goals and frustration with the title's disunity.

### Chess and Computers
Kasparov was a pioneer in using computers for preparation. His most famous computer matches were against IBM's Deep Blue. He won their first match in 1996 but lost the 1997 rematch, becoming the first reigning champion to lose a standard match to a computer. He later suggested human intervention may have occurred, though IBM denied this. He also played landmark games against Deep Junior (2003) and X3D Fritz (2003).

### Post-Retirement and Politics
After retiring, Kasparov focused on writing and political activism. He authored the acclaimed *My Great Predecessors* series and other chess books. Politically, he became a vocal critic of Vladimir Putin, forming opposition movements like the United Civil Front. He left Russia in 2013, citing fear of persecution, and later obtained Croatian citizenship. He served as chairman of the Human Rights Foundation from 2011 to 2024 and founded the Renew Democracy Initiative in 2017.

## Terms
- **Grandmaster (GM):** The highest title a chess player can attain, awarded by FIDE.
- **FIDE:** The Fédération Internationale des Échecs, the international governing body of chess.
- **Elo rating:** A system for calculating the relative skill levels of players in zero-sum games like chess.
- **Interzonal:** A tournament that was part of the World Championship cycle, where players qualified for the Candidates Tournament.
- **Candidates Tournament:** A tournament to determine the challenger for the World Chess Championship.
- **PCA (Professional Chess Association):** A rival chess organization formed by Kasparov and Short in 1993 after breaking from FIDE.
- **Classical World Championship:** The line of the world title descended from the original FIDE championship, which Kasparov held until 2000.
- **Berlin Defence:** A solid, drawish opening system for Black in the Ruy Lopez, famously used by Kramnik to neutralize Kasparov in their 2000 match.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The termination of the 1984–85 World Championship match remains controversial. Some, including grandmasters and historians, have alleged FIDE President Campomanes was a KGB agent acting to prevent Karpov's defeat, a claim others dismiss.
- Kasparov's 1997 loss to Deep Blue sparked debate about cheating and the role of human intervention in computer chess. Kasparov suggested IBM's team may have intervened between games, while IBM stated the only human input was allowed under the match rules.
- Kasparov's support for Anatoly Fomenko's "New Chronology" theory, which posits mainstream ancient history is based on misdating, was a notable and controversial intellectual stance, though he later renounced his support for the theory while maintaining skepticism of mainstream history.

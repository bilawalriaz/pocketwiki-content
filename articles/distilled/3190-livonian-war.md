# Livonian War

## Overview
The Livonian War (1558–1583) was a protracted conflict over the control of Old Livonia (modern Estonia and Latvia). It pitted the expansionist Tsardom of Russia against a shifting coalition of the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth, Sweden, and Denmark-Norway. The war ended with Russia's defeat, leading to the dissolution of the Livonian Confederation and the partition of its territories among the victorious powers, fundamentally reshaping the political and religious map of the Baltic region.

## Timeline
- **1558** — Russia invades Livonia, capturing Dorpat and Narva.
- **1561** — Livonian Order dissolved; its lands secularized and assigned to Lithuania. Sweden establishes the Duchy of Estonia.
- **1569** — Union of Lublin creates the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth.
- **1570** — Magnus of Holstein declared King of Livonia as a Russian vassal.
- **1577** — Russian forces reach their peak, raiding deep into Livonia.
- **1578** — Stephen Báthory becomes King of Poland and Grand Duke of Lithuania.
- **1578** — Joint Swedish–Polish–Lithuanian victory at the Battle of Wenden.
- **1579** — Polish–Lithuanian forces capture Polotsk.
- **1581** — Siege of Pskov begins; Sweden captures Narva.
- **1582** — Truce of Jam Zapolski ends war between Russia and Poland–Lithuania.
- **1583** — Truce of Plussa ends war between Russia and Sweden.

## Body

### Prelude and Causes
By the mid-16th century, the Livonian Confederation was a decentralized, religiously divided, and politically weak entity. It was coveted by its expansionist neighbors: Russia sought a Baltic trade corridor; Sweden desired ports to challenge Danish dominance; and Poland–Lithuania aimed to prevent Russian expansion and secure its trade routes. A mutual defensive alliance signed by Livonian estates at the Treaty of Pozvol (1557) provided Tsar Ivan IV with a casus belli, as it violated a prior Livonian pledge not to ally with Poland–Lithuania.

### Russian Dominance and Foreign Intervention (1558–1577)
Russia's initial invasion was highly successful, leading to the collapse of the Livonian Order. This prompted interventions: Poland–Lithuania absorbed the Order's secularized lands in 1561, Sweden established the Duchy of Estonia, and Denmark-Norway acquired the Bishopric of Ösel–Wiek. The conflict became a multi-front struggle, intertwined with the Northern Seven Years' War (1563–1570) among the western Baltic powers. Russia achieved its greatest territorial gains in the 1570s, creating the vassal Kingdom of Livonia under Duke Magnus and raiding deep into Livonia, but failed to capture major coastal cities.

### The Turn of the Tide (1576–1583)
The election of the capable Stephen Báthory as King of Poland and Grand Duke of Lithuania in 1576 reversed Russia's fortunes. Báthory reformed the Polish–Lithuanian army and allied with Sweden. Their joint forces won the pivotal Battle of Wenden (1578). Báthory then launched a series of successful campaigns into Russian territory, capturing Polotsk (1579) and Velikie Luki (1580), and besieging Pskov. Simultaneously, Sweden recaptured Narva (1581). Exhausted and facing a two-front war, Ivan IV agreed to separate truces.

### Aftermath and Consequences
The Truce of Jam Zapolski (1582) forced Russia to surrender all its Livonian holdings and Polotsk to Poland–Lithuania. The Truce of Plussa (1583) granted Sweden most of Ingria and northern Livonia. The war ended the Livonian Confederation, partitioned its lands, and established Poland–Lithuania and Sweden as the dominant Baltic powers. Russia was excluded from the Baltic coast for nearly a century. The conflict also entrenched the Counter-Reformation in Polish–Lithuanian Livonia and set the stage for future Polish–Swedish wars over the region.

## Terms
- **Livonian Confederation:** A decentralized political entity in modern Estonia and Latvia, comprising the Teutonic Order, prince-bishoprics, and free cities.
- **Duchy of Livonia:** Created in 1561 from the secularized lands of the Livonian Order, initially under Lithuanian sovereignty.
- **Dominium maris baltici:** The contemporary term for the struggle for control over the Baltic Sea and its trade routes.
- **Oprichnina:** A period of political repression and state terror in Russia under Ivan IV, which weakened the state.
- **Hussars:** Light cavalry troops reformed and expanded by Stephen Báthory, crucial to Polish–Lithuanian military success.
- **Truce of Jam Zapolski (1582):** Agreement ending the war between Russia and Poland–Lithuania, resulting in significant Russian territorial losses.
- **Truce of Plussa (1583):** Agreement ending the war between Russia and Sweden, granting Sweden control over Ingria and northern Livonia.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The extent to which the internal weaknesses of the Livonian Confederation, rather than external aggression, caused the war.
- Whether Ivan IV's primary goal was a permanent Baltic trade corridor or merely a temporary outlet for trade and military pressure.
- The impact of the oprichnina on Russia's military capacity and political will during the prolonged conflict.
- The degree to which the Northern Seven Years' War (1563–1570) among the western powers enabled Russia's initial dominance in Livonia.

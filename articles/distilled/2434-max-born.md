# Max Born

## Overview
Max Born (1882–1970) was a German-British theoretical physicist instrumental in developing quantum mechanics. He is best known for formulating the statistical interpretation of the wavefunction, which describes the probability of finding a particle in a given state. His work provided the mathematical foundation for quantum theory and influenced a generation of physicists, earning him the 1954 Nobel Prize in Physics.

## Timeline
- **11 December 1882** — Born in Breslau, Germany (now Wrocław, Poland).
- **1904** — Enters the University of Göttingen, studying under mathematicians Hilbert, Klein, and Minkowski.
- **1906** — Wins the Philosophy Faculty Prize and earns his Ph.D. in Mathematics.
- **1918** — Discusses ionic compound formation with Fritz Haber, leading to the Born–Haber cycle.
- **1921** — Returns to Göttingen as a professor, establishing it as a leading physics center.
- **1925** — With Werner Heisenberg, formulates matrix mechanics, a foundational representation of quantum mechanics.
- **July 1926** — Publishes the standard interpretation of the probability density function (ψ*ψ) for the Schrödinger equation.
- **January 1933** — Suspended from his professorship by the Nazi regime due to his Jewish heritage.
- **1936** — Appointed Tait Professor of Natural Philosophy at the University of Edinburgh.
- **31 August 1939** — Becomes a naturalized British citizen, one day before World War II begins.
- **1954** — Awarded the Nobel Prize in Physics for his statistical interpretation of the wavefunction.
- **5 January 1970** — Dies in Göttingen, Germany.

## Body

### Early Life and Education
Born studied at several German universities before joining the University of Göttingen in 1904, where he formed close ties with mathematicians David Hilbert and Hermann Minkowski. He earned his Ph.D. in Mathematics in 1906 for work on the stability of elastic wires. After brief military service and study in Cambridge, he returned to Göttingen to complete his habilitation on the Thomson model of the atom. His early research included work on special relativity with Minkowski, introducing the concept of Born rigidity.

### Career in Germany and Quantum Mechanics
Born held academic positions in Berlin and Frankfurt before returning to Göttingen in 1921. There, he collaborated with experimentalist James Franck and mentored a remarkable group of students and assistants, including Werner Heisenberg, Pascual Jordan, and Enrico Fermi. In 1925, Heisenberg presented Born with a paper on quantum theory. Recognizing the mathematical structure as matrix algebra, Born, with Jordan, transcribed and extended it into the matrix mechanics formulation of quantum mechanics. This work established the non-commutative relationship between position and momentum. In 1926, Born provided the crucial statistical interpretation of Erwin Schrödinger's wavefunction, stating that its square modulus (ψ*ψ) represents the probability density for finding a particle.

### Exile and Later Life
Following the Nazi rise to power in 1933, Born was suspended from his position and emigrated to the United Kingdom. He worked at Cambridge and then became a professor at the University of Edinburgh in 1936, where he continued his research. He became a British citizen in 1939. After retiring in 1952, he was awarded the 1954 Nobel Prize in Physics, shared with Walther Bothe. In his Nobel lecture, he reflected on the philosophical implications of probability in science. He died in Göttingen in 1970.

## Terms
- **Matrix Mechanics**: An early formulation of quantum mechanics developed by Heisenberg, Born, and Jordan, using matrices to describe physical observables like position and momentum.
- **Wavefunction (ψ)**: A mathematical function in quantum mechanics that describes the quantum state of a system.
- **Probability Density Function (ψ*ψ)**: The square of the wavefunction's magnitude, which gives the probability density for finding a particle at a specific point in space.
- **Born–Haber Cycle**: A thermodynamic cycle used to calculate the lattice energy of an ionic compound.
- **Born Rigidity**: A concept in special relativity describing a deformation-free motion of a body.
- **Habilitation**: A post-doctoral qualification in German-speaking academic systems required to become a university professor.
- **Privatdozent**: A senior academic position at a German university, often an unpaid lecturer qualified to supervise doctoral theses.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The statistical interpretation of the wavefunction was a point of contention with Albert Einstein, who famously stated, "God does not play dice," expressing his discomfort with the inherent randomness of quantum mechanics.
- The relative credit for the development of matrix mechanics among Heisenberg, Born, and Jordan has been a historical discussion, with Heisenberg receiving the 1932 Nobel Prize alone for the creation of quantum mechanics.

# Flower

## Overview
Flowers are the reproductive structures of angiosperms (flowering plants), evolving 150–190 million years ago in the Jurassic. Their key innovation—enclosing ovules in carpels and utilizing animal pollinators—granted superior reproductive efficiency, allowing angiosperms to dominate ~90% of living land plant species and replace gymnosperms in most ecosystems. A typical flower comprises four whorls (sepals, petals, stamens, carpels) on a receptacle; after pollination and double fertilization, the ovule becomes a seed and the ovary a fruit for dispersal. Humans have cultivated flowers for ~10,000 years for food (e.g., broccoli, rice), medicine, spices, perfumes, and deep cultural symbolism.

## Timeline
- **150–190 Mya** — Flowers originate in the Jurassic (molecular estimate).
- **125–130 Mya** — Earliest definitive angiosperm fossils appear (Early Cretaceous).
- **~10,000 years ago** — Humans begin cultivating plants for flowers (gardens).
- **1753** — Linnaeus publishes *Species Plantarum*, first taxonomy to emphasize floral morphology (stamen number/union).
- **1963** — Sokal & Sneath introduce numerical taxonomy based on tabulated floral traits.
- **Modern** — Genetic sequencing supplements morphology as taxonomic bedrock; climate change threatens pollinator-flower coevolution.

## Body

### Structure and Morphology
A complete flower has four whorls on a receptacle (tip of the pedicel/stalk): **calyx** (sepals—modified leaves, often green/waxy, protective), **corolla** (petals—often coloured, scented, UV-patterned to attract pollinators), **androecium** (stamens—filament + anther with four pollen sacs/thecae; produce microspores → pollen), and **gynoecium** (one or more carpels—stigma, style, ovary containing ovules on placenta via funiculi). The perianth = calyx + corolla. Flowers are **perfect** (bisexual/hermaphrodite) if they have both sexes, **imperfect** (unisexual) if not; species with both sexes on one plant are **monoecious**, with separate male/female plants **dioecious**. Symmetry: **radial** (regular, multiple planes), **bilateral** (irregular, one plane—often coevolved with specific pollinators), or asymmetric. **Inflorescence** = cluster of flowers on an axis; **pseudanthia** (e.g., daisy, sunflower) mimic a single flower via many florets. **Floral formulae/diagrams** encode organ numbers, fusion, symmetry.

### Development and Genetics
Floral transition: shoot apical meristem (SAM) → floral meristem (growth-limited). Timing cues: hormones (gibberellins), temperature, day length (photoperiod), often cold requirement (vernalization). **Florigen** (mobile signal from leaves) triggers SAM switch. **ABC model**: A alone → sepals (whorl 1); A+B → petals (whorl 2); B+C → stamens (whorl 3); C alone → carpels (center). Extended **ABCDE model** adds D/E for ovule identity. All controlled by MADS-box gene network. Circadian rhythm governs daily opening/closing and solar tracking.

### Pollination
Pollen transfer from anther to stigma. **Self-pollination** (same flower/plant) vs. **cross-pollination** (different plants—favored for genetic diversity). **Biotic vectors** (~80% of angiosperms): insects, birds, bats, etc. Flowers offer rewards (nectar, pollen, oils, shelter, pseudocopulation) and signals (colour, UV nectar guides, scent, shape). **Coevolution** drives specialization (e.g., night-opening honeysuckle for moths), increasing efficiency but raising extinction risk if partner lost. **Abiotic vectors**: wind (small, light, smooth, non-sticky pollen; no showy parts/nectar/scent) or water (rare).

### Fertilization, Seed and Fruit Development
Pollen on stigma → pollen tube grows down style → enters ovule via micropyle → releases two sperm. **Double fertilization**: (1) sperm + egg → diploid zygote (embryo); (2) sperm + two polar nuclei → triploid endosperm (nutrient tissue). Zygote → embryo (root, shoot, cotyledons) + suspensor; vascular tissue forms. Ovary → fruit (pericarp = fruit wall: exocarp, mesocarp, endocarp); other floral parts senesce (floral senescence). Fruit protects seed and aids dispersal.

### Seed Dispersal
Vectors: **external** (biotic: birds/bats eating fruit; abiotic: wind/water) or **internal** (ballistic: fruit explodes, e.g., dwarf mistletoe). Dispersal reduces mother-daughter competition and enables colonization.

### Evolution
Angiosperms diverged from gymnosperms (cones) and pteridophytes (spores). Key innovations: carpels enclosing ovules, stamens presenting pollen, perianth protection, double fertilization (delayed endosperm investment), reduced gametophytes. Result: greater reproductive security/efficiency → ecological dominance. **Coevolution** with pollinators drove diversification (speciation via specialization). Climate change now disrupts pollinator populations, causing plant extinctions.

### Taxonomy
Floral morphology used since classical Greece. Linnaeus (1753): 24 classes based on stamina. 18th–19th c.: natural systems using whole plant. 1963: numerical taxonomy (Sokal & Sneath) — objective but ahistorical. Modern: DNA sequencing, palynology, cytology supplement (not replace) floral/inflorescence morphology as taxonomic bedrock.

### Human Uses
**Food**: edible flowers (salads, teas); inflorescences as vegetables (broccoli, cauliflower, artichoke); staple crops (rice, wheat, maize = ~50% cropland). **Spices**: saffron (*Crocus*), cloves (*Syzygium aromaticum*). **Medicine/drugs**: phytochemicals, antimicrobials (herbal teas, essential oils); sources of cannabis, bush lily, Madagascar periwinkle. **Perfumes/essential oils**. **Floristry**: cut-flower production/arrangement. **Culture**: ~10,000 years garden cultivation; symbolism in art, literature, flags (national flowers, e.g., *Hibiscus × rosa-sinensis* for Malaysia), rituals (burial, wreaths), festivals; bred for longevity, colour, appeal.

## Terms
- **Angiosperm** — Flowering plant; seeds enclosed in an ovary (fruit).
- **Perianth** — Non-reproductive whorls collectively: calyx (sepals) + corolla (petals).
- **Androecium** — Male whorl: stamens (filament + anther) producing pollen.
- **Gynoecium** — Female whorl: one or more carpels (stigma, style, ovary with ovules).
- **Perfect flower** — Bisexual/hermaphrodite: has both functional stamens and carpels.
- **Monoecious / Dioecious** — Species with separate male/female flowers on same plant / on separate plants.
- **Inflorescence** — Complete flower cluster on an axis; includes stems, bracts, flowers.
- **Pseudanthium** — Inflorescence of many florets resembling a single flower (e.g., sunflower).
- **Double fertilization** — Two sperm fuse: one with egg (zygote), one with two polar nuclei (triploid endosperm).
- **Florigen** — Mobile flowering signal produced in leaves under favorable conditions; acts on shoot tips.

## Debates and open questions
- **Origin timing**: Molecular clocks (150–190 Mya) vs. earliest fossils (125–130 Mya); exact divergence from other seed plants unresolved.
- **Evolutionary mode**: Gradual transformation of sporophylls vs. sudden homeotic mutations; which floral organ originated first.
- **Coevolution stability**: Specialization increases pollination efficiency but creates extinction vortices; climate change impact on mutualisms.
- **Taxonomic weight**: Balancing genomic data against traditional floral morphology in classification.

# Hubei

## Overview
Hubei is a major province in Central China, historically significant as the heartland of the ancient State of Chu and a key region in Chinese unification. Its modern economy is the seventh-largest among Chinese provinces, driven by agriculture, industry, and hydroelectric power from the Yangtze River, with its capital Wuhan serving as a crucial political, cultural, and transportation hub.

## Body

### History
The Hubei region was home to Neolithic cultures and later became the core of the State of Chu, a powerful entity that blended northern and southern Chinese cultures. Chu was eventually conquered by the State of Qin, which unified China in 221 BC. The region was a key battleground during the Three Kingdoms period (208–221 AD) and remained under southern Chinese rule during periods of northern division. Under the Qing dynasty, Huguang province was split into Hubei and Hunan. The 1911 Wuchang Uprising in Wuhan initiated the revolution that ended imperial rule. In the 20th century, Hubei experienced civil war, Japanese occupation during World War II, and internal strife during the Cultural Revolution, including the 1967 Wuhan Incident. The late 20th and early 21st centuries were defined by massive hydroelectric projects, notably the Three Gorges Dam, which required the resettlement of millions. The province was the initial epicenter of the COVID-19 pandemic in late 2019 and early 2020.

### Geography
Hubei's landscape is dominated by the Jianghan Plain in the center and south, surrounded by mountain ranges in the west and peripheries. The Yangtze and Han rivers are its major waterways, meeting at Wuhan. The province is dotted with thousands of lakes, earning it the name "Province of Lakes." It has a humid subtropical climate with hot, humid summers and cool winters. The Three Gorges Dam, located at Yichang in western Hubei, is a defining geographical and economic feature.

### Economy
Hubei is a major agricultural producer ("Land of Fish and Rice") and industrial center, with strengths in automobiles, metallurgy, and high-tech commodities. Its economy is the seventh-largest in China and the richest among inland provinces. The Three Gorges Dam provides significant hydroelectric power. The province hosts several national-level economic and technological development zones, particularly in Wuhan, focusing on optical electronics, telecommunications, and automotive manufacturing.

### Demographics and Culture
The population is predominantly Han Chinese, with significant Miao and Tujia minorities in the southwest. The predominant religions are Chinese folk religions, Taoism, and Buddhism. People speak Southwestern Mandarin dialects. Hubei cuisine is noted for dishes like steamed Wuchang bream. Traditional operas include Hanju and Chuju. The province is a major center for higher education, with Wuhan hosting the world's largest college student population.

### Transportation
Historically reliant on the Yangtze and Han rivers, Hubei's modern transport network includes extensive railways, with Wuhan as a key junction for north-south and east-west high-speed lines. The first Yangtze River bridge in Hubei was completed in Wuhan in 1957. The province has multiple airports, with Wuhan Tianhe International Airport as the main hub.

## Terms
- **State of Chu:** A powerful state during the Eastern Zhou dynasty (Spring and Autumn and Warring States periods) that controlled much of the Yangtze River region and had a unique culture blending northern and southern Chinese elements.
- **Jingzhou:** A historical province (zhou) established by the Han dynasty, covering much of modern Hubei and Hunan.
- **Huguang:** A province established under Mongol rule that later, under the Qing dynasty, was split into the modern provinces of Hubei and Hunan.
- **Wuchang Uprising:** The 1911 armed revolt in Wuhan that triggered the Xinhai Revolution, leading to the fall of the Qing dynasty and the establishment of the Republic of China.
- **Three Gorges Dam:** A massive hydroelectric gravity dam on the Yangtze River at Yichang, Hubei, completed in 2012, which is the world's largest power station.
- **Jianghan Plain:** The fertile alluvial plain in central and southern Hubei formed by the Yangtze and Han rivers, known for agriculture and numerous lakes.
- **Southwestern Mandarin:** A group of Mandarin Chinese dialects spoken in southwestern China, including Hubei.
- **Project 131:** An underground military command headquarters built in Xianning, Hubei, during the late 1960s due to fears of nuclear war.

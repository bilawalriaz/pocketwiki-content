# David Bowie

## Overview

David Bowie (1947–2016) was an English singer, songwriter, and actor whose constant reinvention of persona, sound, and visual style made him one of the most influential musicians of the 20th century. Dubbed the "chameleon of rock," he shaped glam rock, art rock, electronica, and pop, while his stagecraft and fashion left an indelible mark on popular culture.

## Timeline

- **1962** — Forms first band, the Konrads
- **1969** — Releases "Space Oddity," first UK top-five hit
- **1972** — Debuts Ziggy Stardust persona; glam rock breakthrough
- **1973** — Retires Ziggy Stardust at Hammersmith Odeon
- **1975** — Releases "plastic soul" album *Young Americans*; first US #1 with "Fame"
- **1976** — *Station to Station* introduces Thin White Duke; stars in *The Man Who Fell to Earth*
- **1977–1979** — Berlin Trilogy: *Low*, *“Heroes”*, *Lodger*
- **1983** — *Let’s Dance* becomes peak commercial success
- **1989–1991** — Fronts hard rock band Tin Machine
- **2004** — Retires from touring after heart attack
- **2013** — Returns with *The Next Day* after decade-long hiatus
- **2016** — Dies Jan 10; final album *Blackstar* released two days prior

## Body

### Early Life and Influences

Born David Robert Jones in Brixton, London, Bowie was raised in a musically rich household. His half-brother Terry Burns, who suffered from schizophrenia, introduced him to modern jazz, Buddhism, and Beat poetry—formative influences that shaped his artistic sensibility. A childhood eye injury left him with anisocoria, becoming a signature feature. He studied art, music, and design, absorbing influences from Little Richard, Elvis Presley, and Anthony Newley.

### Rise to Fame and Personas

Bowie began performing in local bands in 1962, adopting the name "Bowie" to avoid confusion with Davy Jones of the Monkees. His 1969 hit "Space Oddity" marked his first major success. In 1972, he unveiled Ziggy Stardust—a gender-bending alien rock star—for *The Rise and Fall of Ziggy Stardust and the Spiders from Mars*, defining glam rock. He retired the character in 1973, citing its psychological toll.

### Musical Evolution

Throughout the 1970s, Bowie shifted styles rapidly: from soul (*Young Americans*) to art rock and electronica (*Station to Station*, the Berlin Trilogy with Brian Eno). The Berlin Trilogy—*Low*, *“Heroes”*, and *Lodger*—pushed boundaries with ambient textures and experimental structures. In 1983, *Let’s Dance*, co-produced by Nile Rodgers, marked his greatest commercial success. Later decades saw him exploring industrial, jungle, and drum-and-bass with Tin Machine and solo work.

### Acting and Other Ventures

Bowie acted in over 30 films and stage productions, favoring cameos and supporting roles. Notable performances include *The Man Who Fell to Earth* (1976), *Merry Christmas, Mr. Lawrence* (1983), and *The Prestige* (2006). He was also a painter, writer, and early adopter of internet culture, launching BowieNet in 1998. In 1997, he issued "Bowie Bonds"—asset-backed securities based on future royalties—pioneering celebrity finance.

### Personal Life and Legacy

Bowie married Angela Barnett in 1970; they had a son, Duncan. He later married model Iman in 1992. His sexuality sparked debate: he declared himself gay in 1972 and bisexual in 1976, later calling the latter "the biggest mistake." Diagnosed with liver cancer in 2014, he kept it private. *Blackstar*, released on his 69th birthday, was hailed as a farewell masterpiece. He died on January 10, 2016.

## Terms

- **Ziggy Stardust**: Bowie’s androgynous alien rock star persona (1972–1973)
- **Thin White Duke**: A cold, aristocratic persona from *Station to Station* (1976)
- **Berlin Trilogy**: Three experimental albums (*Low*, *“Heroes”*, *Lodger*) made with Brian Eno (1977–1979)
- **Plastic Soul**: Term Bowie used to describe his soul-influenced sound on *Young Americans* (1975)
- **Halloween Jack**: Persona from *Diamond Dogs* (1974)
- **Bowie Bonds**: Asset-backed securities issued against future royalties (1997)
- **Anisocoria**: Condition causing unequal pupil sizes; Bowie’s left pupil was permanently dilated
- **Major Tom**: Recurring fictional astronaut character in “Space Oddity” and later songs
- **Krautrock**: German experimental rock genre influencing the Berlin Trilogy
- **Tin Machine**: Hard rock band Bowie fronted with Reeves Gabrels (1989–1991)

## Debates and Open Questions

- **Sexuality**: Bowie’s public declarations of being gay (1972) and bisexual (1976) remain debated. He later called the bisexuality claim “the biggest mistake I ever made,” though Angie Bowie supported it.
- **Fascist Comments (1976)**: Bowie’s pro-fascist remarks as the Thin White Duke sparked outrage. He blamed cocaine addiction and the persona, later apologizing and criticizing racism throughout the 1980s and 1990s.
- **Artistic Intent of *Blackstar***: While widely interpreted as a deliberate farewell, some question whether its themes were premeditated or retroactively framed after his death.
- **Legacy of Tin Machine**: Critics initially dismissed the band as “pompous,” but some later reappraised its influence on alternative rock.
- **Posthumous Releases**: The ongoing release of archival material raises questions about curation and the integrity of Bowie’s intended legacy.

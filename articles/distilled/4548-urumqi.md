# Ürümqi

## Overview
Ürümqi is the capital and largest city of the Xinjiang Uyghur Autonomous Region in Northwestern China. It serves as a major regional transport, cultural, political, and commercial center, and has experienced significant economic development since the 1990s. Its history reflects the broader political and demographic shifts in Xinjiang, from nomadic settlements to Qing conquest, warlord rule, and integration into the People's Republic of China.

## Timeline
- **648 CE** — Tang government founds the city as Luntai, part of the Protectorate General to Pacify the West.
- **1755** — Qing dynasty takes Ürümqi during its conquest of the Dzungar Khanate.
- **1763–1767** — Qing settlers expand the town into a walled city, renaming it Dihua ("to enlighten and civilize").
- **1884** — Xinjiang becomes a province, with Dihua as its capital.
- **1911–1949** — After the Qing collapse, Ürümqi is ruled by a succession of warlords.
- **1 February 1954** — The Chinese Communist Party restores the name "Ürümqi," deeming "Dihua" ethnically chauvinist.
- **1962** — Following protests in Yining, Ürümqi becomes the de facto political and economic capital of Xinjiang.
- **July 2009** — Major ethnic riots occur between Han Chinese and Uyghurs, resulting in significant casualties.
- **25 October 2018** — Ürümqi Metro Line 1 opens.

## Body

### Etymology and Name
The name "Ürümqi" likely derives from Oirat words meaning "beautiful pasture." An alternative theory proposes an origin from the Old Uyghur word *yürüng* ("white") combined with the Chinese *chin* ("fortified post"), referencing a Tang-dynasty garrison. The Qing renamed the city Dihua in 1767, but the Communist Party restored the name Ürümqi in 1954.

### Early History and Qing Rule
The area was first inhabited by nomadic Jushi people. The Tang dynasty established a garrison town named Luntai in 648 CE. After the Tang retreat, the region saw little permanent settlement until the Qing conquest of the Dzungar Khanate in 1755. The Qing built a walled city (Dihua) and established garrisons, attracting Han Chinese and Hui Muslim migrants. By the late 18th century, Dihua was a commercial and cultural center. It became the capital of the new Xinjiang province in 1884.

### Republican and People's Republic Eras
After the Qing fell, Ürümqi was ruled by warlords until 1949. The city's name was changed back to Ürümqi in 1954. In 1962, following unrest in Yining, the city became Xinjiang's primary administrative and economic hub, and a railway was built to connect it to eastern China. Economic development accelerated in the late 1970s under Deng Xiaoping's reforms, particularly in the oil and gas industry. Ethnic tensions have periodically erupted, notably in the 1997 bombings and the large-scale 2009 riots.

### Geography and Climate
Ürümqi is the most remote major city from any sea, located about 2,500 km from the nearest coastline. It sits in a basin watered by rivers from the Tian Shan mountains. The city has a continental climate with extreme temperature variations between summer and winter. It is known for severe winter air pollution, primarily from coal heating and industrial emissions.

### Economy and Infrastructure
Ürümqi is a major industrial and consumer center, accounting for a significant portion of Xinjiang's industrial output. It is a key node in the China Western Development project. The city has a modern metro system, a major international airport, and is a hub for the Lanzhou–Xinjiang High Speed Railway. The annual China-Eurasia Expo promotes trade.

## Terms
- **Dzungar Khanate:** An Oirat-speaking Mongol state that controlled the region before the Qing conquest.
- **Silk Road:** Ancient trade route network connecting China to the West, along which Ürümqi was a key stop.
- **Bannermen:** Elite military-social groups (Manchu, Mongol, Han) under the Qing dynasty.
- **Hui:** A Chinese-speaking Muslim ethnic group.
- **Yi–Ta incident:** 1962 protests in Yining and Tacheng leading to a mass exodus to the Soviet Union and the administrative shift to Ürümqi.
- **China Western Development:** A national strategy to boost economic growth in western China.
- **PM2.5:** Fine particulate matter in air pollution, a key metric for air quality.
- **Köppen climate classification:** A system for classifying climates based on temperature and precipitation.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The exact etymology of "Ürümqi" is debated, with competing Oirat and Old Uyghur-Chinese origin theories.
- The demographic impact and legacy of the Qing-era Dzungar genocide remain a point of historical study.
- The causes and casualty figures of the 2009 riots are disputed between official Chinese accounts and Uyghur sources.

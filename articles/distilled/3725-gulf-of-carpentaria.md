# Gulf of Carpentaria

## Overview
The Gulf of Carpentaria is a large, shallow sea off the northern coast of Australia, enclosed on three sides by land. It is significant for its unique geography, Aboriginal language regions, early European exploration, and modern industries like mining and fishing. The Gulf's environment and history are shaped by its tropical climate and its geological past as a dry lake during the last ice age.

## Body

### Geography and Climate
The Gulf is a vast, shallow body of water, about 300,000 km² in area, with a general depth of 55–66 metres. Its tidal range is 2–3 metres. The surrounding land is generally flat and low-lying, including Arnhem Land to the west, Cape York Peninsula to the east, and the Gulf Country to the south. The region supports the world's largest intact savanna woodlands, dominated by Eucalyptus and Melaleuca trees. The climate is hot and humid with distinct wet (December–March) and dry (April–November) seasons. The Gulf is prone to tropical cyclones, which average three per year and are thought to transport sediments clockwise along the coast. A unique meteorological phenomenon, the Morning Glory cloud, appears in the southern Gulf in September and October.

### History and Exploration
The Gulf is the traditional land of Aboriginal peoples, with distinct language groups such as the Yulluna and Kayardild. The first European to visit was Dutch explorer Willem Janszoon in 1605–06. His countryman Jan Carstenszoon named the gulf in 1623. Later explorers included Abel Tasman (1644) and Matthew Flinders (1802–03). The first overland expedition to reach the Gulf was the ill-fated Burke and Wills expedition in 1861.

### Coral Reefs and Marine Environment
While no near-surface patch or barrier reefs exist today, a submerged coral reef province was discovered in the southern Gulf in 2003 and 2005. These patch reefs, with upper surfaces at a mean depth of 28.6 metres, indicate a past phase of reef growth under cooler, lower sea-level conditions. The Gulf's extensive seagrass beds support commercial shrimp operations.

### Industry and Economy
Key industries include mining (zinc, lead, silver from the McArthur River and Century Zinc mines), cattle, and commercial fishing. The port at Karumba exports minerals and is proposed for major expansion. Illegal fishing by Indonesian vessels was a significant issue in 2005 but declined by 2011 due to enforcement.

## Terms
- **Sahul Shelf**: The continental shelf that was exposed as dry land during the last ice age, connecting Australia and New Guinea.
- **Savanna woodland**: A grassy ecosystem with scattered trees, here dominated by Eucalyptus and Melaleuca species.
- **Fringing reefs**: Coral reefs that grow directly from the shore or a shallow platform.
- **Multibeam swath sonar**: A survey technology that maps the seafloor in detail using multiple sound beams.
- **Quaternary**: The current geological period, spanning the last 2.6 million years, including ice ages.
- **Tidal range**: The vertical difference in water level between high and low tide.
- **Tropical cyclone**: A rotating storm system with high winds and rain, forming over warm tropical waters.
- **Morning Glory cloud**: A rare, long, tube-shaped cloud formation that appears in the southern Gulf in spring.

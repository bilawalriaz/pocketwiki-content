# Middle Paleolithic

## Overview
The Middle Paleolithic (or Middle Stone Age in Africa) is the second subdivision of the Old Stone Age, spanning roughly 300,000 to 50,000 years ago. It is a critical period in human evolution, marked by the development of advanced tool technologies, the emergence of early behavioral modernity, and the migration of anatomically modern humans out of Africa, leading to their eventual replacement of other hominin species like Neanderthals.

## Timeline
- **c. 300,000 – 50,000 years ago** — Broad span of the Middle Paleolithic period.
- **c. 200,000 BP** — Development of the Levallois (prepared-core) stone tool technique.
- **c. 125,000 years ago** — Anatomically modern humans begin migrating out of Africa.
- **c. 120,000 years ago** — Evidence of long-distance trade for commodities like ochre.
- **c. 110,000 years ago** — Evidence of shellfish cooking in Neanderthal sites in Italy.
- **c. 100,000 years ago** — Burials at Qafzeh and Es Skhul caves in Israel.
- **c. 90,000 years ago** — Hunting of large catfish with specialized tools in central Africa.
- **c. 250,000 years ago** — Widespread use of fire and cooking of food begins.
- **c. 60,000 – 35,000 years ago** — Late Middle Paleolithic in Great Britain.
- **50,000 – 40,000 years ago** — The period is succeeded by the Upper Paleolithic.

## Body

### Behavioral Modernity and Social Structure
The earliest evidence of behavioral modernity—complex thought and culture—appears during this period, though it becomes common only in the subsequent Upper Paleolithic. Burials at sites like Krapina (c. 130,000 BP) and Qafzeh (c. 100,000 BP) suggest the possible development of religious ideology, such as belief in an afterlife, though secular explanations are also proposed. Artistic expression is evidenced by objects like bracelets and ochre from Blombos Cave. Societies were small, egalitarian bands, similar to some modern hunter-gatherers, where care for the elderly was practiced. The traditional model of a strict sexual division of labor (men hunting, women gathering) is suggested by some scholars to have evolved only after 45,000 years ago.

### Nutrition and Diet
While hunting and gathering remained primary, diets diversified. Groups began supplementing food with seafood, as seen in shellfish cooking in Italy (c. 110,000 BP) and specialized catfish hunting in Africa (c. 90,000 BP). Meat preservation techniques like smoking and drying emerged. Evidence from butchered human bones suggests cannibalism may have been common, possibly due to food shortages or for religious reasons, though alternative explanations like excarnation or carnivore damage are also considered.

### Technology and Tool Use
A major technological advance was the Levallois technique, a prepared-core method that allowed for the efficient production of controlled, consistent stone flakes. This enabled the creation of the first composite tools: stone-tipped spears hafted onto wooden shafts. However, hunting practices varied; Neanderthals primarily used thrusting spears for ambush attacks rather than projectile weapons. The use of fire became widespread, and cooking began around 250,000 years ago, possibly to defrost meat. A controversial hypothesis suggests dogs may have been domesticated during this period.

## Terms
- **Middle Paleolithic / Middle Stone Age**: The second subdivision of the Old Stone Age (c. 300,000–50,000 years ago), equivalent terms used for Europe/Asia and Africa respectively.
- **Levallois technique**: A prepared-core stone tool manufacturing method that allowed for more controlled and efficient flake production.
- **Composite tools**: Tools made from multiple components, such as stone flakes hafted onto wooden shafts to create spears.
- **Behavioral modernity**: A set of cognitive and cultural traits, including symbolic thought, art, and complex social organization.
- **Egalitarian band society**: A small-scale social group with relatively equal status among members and shared resources.
- **Excarnation**: The practice of exposing the dead to the elements or animals to deflesh the bones, possibly for ritual purposes.
- **Hafting**: The process of attaching a stone tool head to a handle or shaft.
- **Chibanian**: The geological age of the Middle Pleistocene, during which much of the Middle Paleolithic occurred.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Nature of burials**: Were Middle Paleolithic burials evidence of religious belief (afterlife concepts) or for secular reasons?
- **Tool morphology**: Were Middle Paleolithic tools a series of functionally specific, preconceived forms, or a simple continuum reflecting edge maintenance?
- **Cannibalism**: Was the practice common, and if so, was it for nutritional survival during shortages or for ritual/religious reasons?
- **Dog domestication**: The controversial claim that dogs were domesticated during the Middle Paleolithic (c. 100,000 BCE or earlier) based on canine DNA analysis.
- **Sexual division of labor**: Did a strict division of labor by sex exist in the Middle Paleolithic, or did it evolve later in the Upper Paleolithic?

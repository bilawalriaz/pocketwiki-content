# Houston

## Overview
Houston is the most populous city in Texas and the Southern United States, and the fourth-most populous in the nation. Founded in 1836, it grew from a regional trading center into a global city with a diversified economy anchored by energy, healthcare, and aeronautics. Its strategic location near the Gulf of Mexico, lack of formal zoning, and waves of immigration have shaped its rapid growth, economic power, and status as one of America's most ethnically diverse major cities.

## Timeline
- **August 30, 1836** — Houston is founded by Augustus and John Allen at the confluence of Buffalo and White Oak Bayous.
- **June 5, 1837** — Houston is incorporated as a city.
- **1839** — The capital of the Republic of Texas is relocated from Houston to Austin.
- **1900** — A devastating hurricane destroys Galveston, accelerating efforts to make Houston a deep-water port.
- **1901** — Oil is discovered at Spindletop near Beaumont, launching the Texas oil boom.
- **1914** — President Woodrow Wilson opens the deep-water Port of Houston.
- **1945** — The M.D. Anderson Foundation forms the Texas Medical Center.
- **1961** — NASA's Manned Space Center (now Johnson Space Center) is established in Houston.
- **2001** — Tropical Storm Allison causes catastrophic flooding.
- **2017** — Hurricane Harvey stalls over the city, causing record rainfall and catastrophic flooding.

## Body

### Founding and Early Growth
Houston was founded on August 30, 1836, by Augustus and John Allen, who named it for Sam Houston, hero of the Battle of San Jacinto. It briefly served as the capital of the Republic of Texas before the capital moved to Austin in 1839. The city developed as a commercial center, symbiotic with the port of Galveston, and became a railroad hub by 1890. The 1900 Galveston hurricane and the 1901 Spindletop oil discovery shifted economic focus to Houston, leading to the creation of the Houston Ship Channel and a population boom.

### 20th Century: Energy, Medicine, and Aerospace
World War II spurred industrial growth along the ship channel. Post-war, the economy diversified with the founding of the Texas Medical Center (1945) and the establishment of NASA's Manned Space Center (1961). The availability of air conditioning in the 1950s attracted companies and residents, fueling an economic boom centered on the energy sector. By 1930, Houston had become Texas's most populous city.

### Economy and Port
Houston's economy has a broad base in energy, manufacturing, aeronautics, and transportation. The Port of Houston ranks first in the U.S. for international waterborne tonnage. The city is a leading center for building oilfield equipment and is home to numerous Fortune 500 headquarters. Since the 2020s, technology startups have become a fast-growing sector. The Houston metropolitan area's GDP in 2022 was $633 billion.

### Geography and Climate
The city is built on flat, marshy Gulf coastal plain land, making it prone to flooding. Its climate is humid subtropical, with hot, humid summers and mild winters. Houston is highly vulnerable to hurricanes and tropical storms, as evidenced by major floods from Tropical Storm Allison (2001) and Hurricane Harvey (2017). The city has over 150 active faults, and land subsidence from groundwater pumping has been an issue.

### Demographics and Diversity
Houston is a majority-minority city and is described as the most racially and ethnically diverse major city in the U.S. In 2019, its population was 45.8% Hispanic or Latino, 23.3% non-Hispanic white, 22.4% Black or African American, and 6.5% Asian. The city has a large and growing international community, with an estimated 600,000 undocumented immigrants in the metro area in 2017.

### Government and Politics
Houston has a strong-mayor form of government. It is a home-rule city with nonpartisan elections. The city is politically divided, often swaying between Republicans and Democrats. In 2009, Houston became the first U.S. city with over 1 million people to elect an openly gay mayor, Annise Parker. Texas has banned sanctuary cities, but Houston has stated it will not assist ICE agents with immigration raids.

### Culture and Sports
Houston is a global city with strengths in culture, medicine, and research. It is home to the Houston Museum District and Theater District. The city hosts major annual events like the Houston Livestock Show and Rodeo. Houston has professional sports teams in all major leagues except the NHL, including the Astros (MLB), Rockets (NBA), Texans (NFL), and Dynamo (MLS). The Astrodome, opened in 1965, was the world's first indoor domed sports stadium.

### Infrastructure and Transportation
Houston is an automobile-dependent city with an extensive freeway system. Public transportation is provided by METRO, which operates buses and a light rail system. The city is served by two major airports: George Bush Intercontinental (IAH) and William P. Hobby (HOU). The Texas Medical Center is the largest medical center in the world, employing over 73,600 people.

## Terms
- **Home rule city**: A city that has a charter and exercises powers of local self-government not denied by the state constitution or legislature.
- **Humid subtropical climate (Cfa)**: A climate characterized by hot, humid summers and generally mild winters, with year-round precipitation.
- **Port of Houston**: A complex of facilities including the Houston Ship Channel, which is the leading U.S. port for international waterborne tonnage.
- **Texas Medical Center**: The world's largest concentration of healthcare and research institutions, located in Houston.
- **Strong-mayor government**: A municipal government structure where the mayor serves as the chief executive officer with significant administrative authority.
- **Zoning**: A system of land-use regulation that designates permitted uses of land in specific areas. Houston is the largest U.S. city without formal zoning.
- **Salt dome**: A geologic structure formed by the upward movement of a thick layer of salt, which can trap oil and gas deposits.
- **Fault creep**: The slow, steady movement along a geological fault, which reduces the risk of sudden, earthquake-causing slippage.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Impact of No Zoning**: Scholars and residents debate whether Houston's lack of formal zoning has contributed to urban sprawl and flooding, or has instead provided affordable housing and economic flexibility.
- **Flooding and Development**: There is ongoing debate over the effectiveness of post-Harvey regulations requiring homes in floodplains to be elevated, and the tension between development pressures and flood risk management.
- **Political Identity**: Houston is described as a politically divided city, with its balance of power often swaying between Republican and Democratic influences in a generally conservative state.

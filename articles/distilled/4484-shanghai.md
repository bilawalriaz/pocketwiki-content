# Shanghai

## Overview
Shanghai is a provincial-level direct-administered municipality in China and the country's most populous city, with a population of 30,050,000 in its urban area as of 2026. It is a global center for finance, business, and innovation, and its history as a treaty port and its modern economic reforms have made it a symbol of China's rapid development and integration into the world economy.

## Timeline
- **1074** — Shanghai's status upgraded from a village to a market town.
- **1554** — First city wall built to protect against Japanese pirate raids.
- **1684** — Kangxi Emperor reverses the ban on oceangoing vessels, boosting trade.
- **1842** — Treaty of Nanking opens Shanghai as one of five treaty ports.
- **1863** — British and American settlements merge to form the Shanghai International Settlement.
- **1927** — Shanghai is separated from Jiangsu province and becomes a municipality.
- **1937** — Battle of Shanghai leads to Japanese occupation of Chinese-administered areas.
- **1949** — People's Liberation Army takes control of Shanghai.
- **1990** — Deng Xiaoping's reforms lead to the redevelopment of Pudong New Area.
- **2013** — Shanghai Free-Trade Zone, mainland China's first, is launched.
- **2022** — A major COVID-19 outbreak leads to a city-wide lockdown from April 5 to June 1.

## Body

### Etymology and Early History
The name "Shanghai" (上海) means "On the Sea," first recorded in the 11th-century Song dynasty. The city's official abbreviation, "Hu" (沪), derives from a 4th- or 5th-century name for the mouth of Suzhou Creek. The area was inhabited 6,000 years ago and was part of various ancient kingdoms. During the Tang and Song dynasties, the nearby Qinglong Town was a major port. Shanghai's status grew after 1684 when the ban on oceangoing trade was reversed, and by 1735 it was the major trade port for the lower Yangtze region.

### Treaty Port Era and Golden Age
The First Opium War led to the 1842 Treaty of Nanking, which opened Shanghai to international trade. The Shanghai International Settlement and French Concession were established, governed separately from the Chinese-held Old City. Despite internal rebellions and attacks, the city flourished. By the 1930s, it was a primary commercial and financial hub of Asia, attracting immigrants from around the world, including White Russians and Jewish refugees. The city was elevated to a municipality in 1927.

### War, Revolution, and Reform
Shanghai suffered during the Second Sino-Japanese War, with the Battle of Shanghai in 1937 leading to occupation. After World War II, the Chinese Communist Party took control in 1949. During the Cold War, trade was limited to socialist countries, and the city's global influence declined. The Cultural Revolution (1966–1976) caused severe social disruption. A major turning point came in 1990 when Deng Xiaoping's reforms spurred the redevelopment of Pudong, reintroducing foreign investment and re-establishing Shanghai as a global financial center.

### Economy and Infrastructure
Shanghai is a global financial center, home to the Shanghai Stock Exchange and the world's busiest container port, the Port of Shanghai. Its economy is driven by finance, retail, IT, real estate, and manufacturing. The Shanghai Free-Trade Zone, launched in 2013, is the first in mainland China. The city has an extensive public transport network, including the Shanghai Metro, the world's largest by route length, and two major airports. Private car ownership is managed through a monthly license plate auction system.

### Demographics and Culture
As of 2023, Shanghai had a population of 24,874,500, with a significant number of domestic migrants and a foreign community. The local vernacular is Shanghainese, a Wu Chinese dialect. The city's culture is a blend of local Wuyue traditions and Western influences, known as "Haipai" culture. This is reflected in its architecture, cuisine (such as Benbang and Haipai styles), and arts. Shanghai is a major center for education and research, hosting numerous universities and scientific institutions.

## Terms
- **Direct-administered municipality**: A Chinese city with provincial-level administrative status, under the direct control of the central government.
- **Treaty port**: A port opened to foreign trade by treaty, often under extraterritorial concessions, following military defeat.
- **Shanghai International Settlement**: The area of Shanghai governed by foreign residents from 1863 until 1943.
- **Pudong New Area**: A district east of the Huangpu River, transformed from farmland into a major financial and commercial hub since the 1990s.
- **Shanghai Free-Trade Zone**: Mainland China's first free-trade zone, established in 2013 to incentivize foreign investment.
- **Shanghainese**: The local Wu Chinese dialect spoken in Shanghai.
- **Haipai culture**: A cultural style blending local Wuyue traditions with Western influences, emerging in early 20th-century Shanghai.
- **Benbang cuisine**: A local Shanghai cooking style emphasizing condiments and original flavors, with sugar as a key ingredient.
- **Shanghai Clique**: A powerful political faction in the Chinese central government composed of officials with ties to Shanghai's administration.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The exact origin and interpretation of the name "Shanghai" are disputed, with some historical records suggesting a much earlier reference than the 11th century.
- The city's population control policies, implemented since 2017, aim to manage growth but have resulted in population declines, raising questions about long-term demographic and economic impacts.

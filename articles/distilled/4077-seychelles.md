# Seychelles

## Overview
Seychelles is an archipelagic country of 115 islands in the Indian Ocean, the smallest and least populated sovereign nation in Africa. It transitioned from a plantation colony to a tourism-based economy after independence in 1976, now boasting Africa's highest per capita GDP and Human Development Index. Its history is marked by colonial competition, post-independence political shifts, and a modern focus on environmental conservation and a "blue economy."

## Timeline
- **15 March 1503** — First recorded European sighting by Vasco da Gama's expedition.
- **1756** — France formally claims the islands, naming them after Jean Moreau de Séchelles.
- **1770** — First permanent settlement by French colonists and enslaved Africans.
- **16 May 1794** — British HMS *Orpheus* arrives; islands remain under French control as neutral territory.
- **1814** — Treaty of Paris formalizes British control after the Napoleonic Wars.
- **1903** — Seychelles becomes a separate British crown colony from Mauritius.
- **29 June 1976** — Independence from the United Kingdom.
- **5 June 1977** — Coup d'état by France-Albert René ousts President James Mancham.
- **1993** — New constitution approved, ending the one-party socialist state.
- **25 October 2020** — Wavel Ramkalawan wins the first opposition presidential election.
- **26 October 2025** — Patrick Herminie wins presidential runoff, returning United Seychelles to power.

## Body

### History and Politics
The islands were uninhabited until French colonization in the 18th century, which brought enslaved Africans for plantation labor. Britain gained control in 1814, abolished slavery in 1835, and later introduced indentured laborers from India. Seychelles gained independence in 1976. A 1977 coup installed France-Albert René, who ruled a socialist one-party state until 1993. The country transitioned to a multi-party democracy, culminating in the historic 2020 election where the opposition won for the first time. The political landscape is dominated by the former ruling United Seychelles party and the Seychelles National Party.

### Geography and Environment
The Seychelles sits on a submerged microcontinent, with two main island groups: the granitic Inner Islands and the coral Outer Islands. The climate is tropical and humid. The nation is a global leader in conservation, protecting 42% of its territory. It is renowned for unique flora and fauna, including the coco de mer palm and the Aldabra giant tortoise. A major environmental initiative is its marine spatial plan, creating one of the world's largest ocean protected areas to support a "blue economy."

### Economy and Society
The economy shifted from plantations (cinnamon, vanilla, copra) to tourism after the airport opened in 1971. Tourism is now the primary industry. The government has encouraged foreign investment and developed an offshore financial sector. Seychelles has the highest literacy rate in sub-Saharan Africa (95.9%). Society is a multicultural creole blend of French, British, Indian, African, and Chinese influences, with Seychellois Creole as the national language. The country has a high incarceration rate, though it has dropped significantly since 2014.

## Terms
- **Archipelagic country:** A nation consisting of a chain or group of islands.
- **Creole culture:** A culture that has developed over time from a mixture of different ethnic and social origins.
- **Indentured laborers:** Workers under contract to work for an employer for a specific number of years, often used as a substitute for slave labor after abolition.
- **Blue economy:** An economic concept relating to the sustainable use of ocean resources for economic growth and improved livelihoods.
- **Marine spatial plan:** A public process of analyzing and allocating the spatial and temporal distribution of human activities in marine areas.
- **Microcontinent:** A fragment of continental crust that has broken off from a main continental landmass.
- **Coco de mer:** A palm species endemic to the Seychelles, producing the world's largest seed.
- **Aldabra giant tortoise:** A large species of tortoise native to the Aldabra Atoll in the Seychelles.
- **Moutya:** A traditional dance and music form with roots in the era of slavery, added to UNESCO's Intangible Cultural Heritage list in 2021.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The source notes that while Arab and Swahili sailors likely knew of the islands earlier, there is no evidence of permanent pre-European settlement, though tombs on Silhouette Island have been conjectured to belong to later Maldivian and Arab traders.
- The economic impact of piracy is quantified (costing 4% of GDP in 2008-2009), but the long-term effectiveness of international anti-piracy efforts is not detailed.
- The government's strategy to reduce dependence on tourism by promoting other sectors like fishing and offshore finance is ongoing, with its ultimate success yet to be determined.

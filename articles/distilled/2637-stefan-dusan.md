# Stefan Dušan

## Overview
Stefan Dušan (c. 1308–1355), known as "the Mighty," was the medieval Serbian king and emperor who transformed Serbia into the most powerful state in Southeast Europe. His reign marked the territorial, political, and cultural peak of the Serbian Empire, but his sudden death created a power vacuum that led to the empire's fragmentation and enabled Ottoman expansion into the Balkans.

## Timeline
- **c. 1308** — Born to King Stefan Dečanski and Theodora Smilets
- **1321** — Crowned "Young King" after his father's victory in civil war
- **1331** — Usurped throne from his father; crowned King of Serbia
- **1334** — Conquered Byzantine territories in Macedonia; peace treaty with Byzantium
- **1346** — Crowned Emperor of Serbs, Greeks, Bulgarians, and Albanians at Skopje
- **1347** — Conquered Epirus, Aetolia, and Acarnania
- **1348** — Conquered Thessaly
- **1350** — Invaded Bosnia; excommunicated by Constantinople Patriarch
- **1354** — Repelled Hungarian invasion; began diplomatic correspondence with Pope
- **1355** — Died suddenly on 20 December while planning crusade against Ottomans

## Body

### Rise to Power
Dušan was the eldest son of King Stefan Dečanski. After his father's victory in a civil war following King Milutin's death in 1321, Dušan was crowned "Young King" in 1322. He gained military experience defeating Bosnian forces in 1329 and Bulgarian Emperor Michael III Shishman at the 1330 Battle of Velbužd. Tensions with his father escalated in 1331, possibly due to noble pressure after Dečanski declined to attack Byzantium following Velbužd. Dušan besieged his father, captured him at Petrich on 21 August 1331, and had him imprisoned. He was crowned king in early September 1331.

### Imperial Expansion
Dušan systematically conquered Byzantine territories in the western Balkans, beginning major campaigns in 1333. He exploited the Byzantine civil war between John V Palaiologos and John Kantakouzenos, conquering Macedonia, Epirus, and Thessaly by 1348. His empire stretched from the Danube to the Gulf of Corinth. He negotiated with Venice for fleet access to attack Constantinople, but Venice declined a military alliance. In 1350, he invaded Bosnia to reclaim the Hum region but failed to achieve decisive victory. His premature death in 1355 prevented his planned capture of Constantinople and crusade against the Ottomans.

### Imperial Coronation and Church Policy
On 16 April 1346, Dušan convened a grand assembly at Skopje that elevated the Serbian Archbishopric to a Patriarchate, with Joanikije II as first Patriarch. Dušan was crowned "Emperor and autocrat of Serbs and Romans." He placed Mount Athos under Serbian jurisdiction and granted extensive privileges to Athonite monasteries. The Ecumenical Patriarchate excommunicated him in 1350 for these actions. In 1354, Dušan offered to recognize papal supremacy in exchange for support against the Ottomans, but his death prevented any union.

### Lawmaker and Administration
Dušan enacted his famous Code (Dušan's Code) in 1349, with 155 clauses proclaimed at Skopje, supplemented by 66 additional clauses at Serres in 1353–1354. The Code addressed church organization, noble privileges, and criminal law, drawing from Byzantine legal traditions while incorporating Serbian customary law. It applied only to Serbian territories, not the Greek provinces where existing Byzantine laws continued. Dušan maintained a multinational administration, appointing Serbs to key positions while allowing Greeks to retain local administrative roles under Serbian governors.

### Military Organization
The Serbian army employed wedge-shaped heavy cavalry attacks with horse archers on flanks. It included Serbian feudal forces, Albanian light cavalry (15,000 spearmen), Greek troops, and Western mercenaries—German knights and Catalan halberdiers. Dušan maintained a personal "Alemannic Guard" of German knights led by Palman. Serbian expansion relied primarily on siege warfare rather than major field battles.

## Terms
- **Tsar**: Slavic equivalent of Emperor; Dušan adopted this title in 1345
- **Autocephaly**: Independence of a church from external ecclesiastical authority
- **Despot**: High Byzantine court title granted to nobility
- **Caesar**: Imperial title for military commanders
- **Sebastocrator**: Byzantine title for relatives of the emperor
- **Nomokanon**: Church canon law code covering civil and family law
- **Prostagma**: Byzantine imperial decree adopted by Dušan
- **Appanage**: Territory granted to a member of the royal family

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Birth year**: Sources conflict between c. 1308 and 1312; recent scholarship suggests 1312 based on genealogical evidence
- **Ultimate ambitions**: Historians debate whether Dušan planned to conquer Constantinople and establish a joint Serbian-Greek empire replacing Byzantium
- **Relations with Ottomans**: The nature of Dušan's dealings with Ottoman Sultan Orhan remains unclear, including whether his daughter Theodora's marriage was intended to secure peace
- **Cause of death**: His sudden death at Prizren in 1355 remains unexplained in sources
- **Legacy interpretation**: Serbian folk tradition often portrayed Dušan negatively under church influence, contrasting with his historical achievements

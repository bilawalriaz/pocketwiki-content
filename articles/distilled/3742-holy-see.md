# Holy See

## Overview
The Holy See is the central governing body of the Catholic Church and Vatican City, encompassing the office of the Pope as Bishop of Rome. It is the spiritual and administrative authority for the worldwide Catholic Church and holds the status of a sovereign juridical entity under international law, distinct from but headquartered in Vatican City.

## Timeline
- **1st century** — Tradition holds the apostolic see of Rome was founded by Saints Peter and Paul.
- **313** — The Edict of Milan recognizes the legal status of the Catholic Church.
- **380** — The Edict of Thessalonica makes Christianity the state church of the Roman Empire.
- **756** — The Donation of Pepin grants the Holy See sovereignty over territory, forming the Papal States.
- **1075** — The *Dictatus papae* asserts peak papal temporal power, including deposing emperors.
- **1309–1376** — The Avignon Papacy relocates the papacy to France, straining its authority.
- **1870** — The Kingdom of Italy captures Rome, ending the Papal States and making the Pope a "prisoner in the Vatican."
- **1929** — The Lateran Treaty establishes Vatican City as an independent state under the Holy See's sovereignty.

## Body

### History and Sovereignty
According to Catholic tradition, the Holy See was founded in the 1st century. Its temporal power grew through medieval grants like the Donation of Pepin (756), which created the Papal States. This sovereignty peaked with events like the *Dictatus papae* (1075) but faced challenges, including the Avignon Papacy (1309–1376) and the loss of territory to Italy in 1870. Despite 59 years without territory, the Holy See maintained diplomatic relations, proving its international legal personality. The 1929 Lateran Treaty resolved the "Roman Question" by creating Vatican City State, which the Holy See governs with "full ownership, exclusive dominion, and sovereign authority."

### Organization and Governance
The Holy See is an absolute monarchy governed by the Pope through the Roman Curia, its central administration. The Curia includes dicasteries (ministries) like the Dicastery for the Doctrine of the Faith and the Secretariat of State, which directs foreign policy. The Cardinal Secretary of State acts as its chief administrator. During a papal vacancy (*sede vacante*), the College of Cardinals governs, with the Camerlengo managing temporal affairs. The Holy See's finances are overseen by bodies like the Administration of the Patrimony of the Apostolic See.

### International Legal Status and Diplomacy
The Holy See is a subject of international law, maintaining diplomatic relations with 180 sovereign states and the European Union. It is a member or observer in organizations like the United Nations and the International Atomic Energy Agency. Its diplomats, called nuncios, represent the Holy See, not Vatican City. This status is based on its continuous diplomatic practice, even during periods without territory. The Holy See is distinct from Vatican City, which is the territorial state that ensures the Pope's independence.

### Relationship with Vatican City
Vatican City is a "minuscule support-state" created by the Lateran Treaty to guarantee the Holy See's spiritual freedom and sovereignty. The Holy See governs Vatican City, but they are separate legal entities. Foreign embassies are accredited to the Holy See, and it is the Holy See that signs treaties. The Holy See also holds extraterritorial rights over various sites in Rome.

## Terms
- **Holy See (Sancta Sedes):** The central governing body of the Catholic Church, synonymous with the papacy and the Diocese of Rome.
- **Vatican City State:** The independent city-state established in 1929, over which the Holy See holds sovereignty.
- **Roman Curia:** The administrative apparatus through which the Pope governs the Catholic Church.
- **Papal Nuncio:** A diplomatic representative of the Holy See to a foreign state or international organization.
- **Lateran Treaty:** The 1929 agreement between the Holy See and Italy that established Vatican City's independence.
- **Sede Vacante:** The period when the papal see is vacant, between the death/resignation of a pope and the election of a successor.
- **College of Cardinals:** The body of senior bishops who advise the Pope and elect a new pope during a vacancy.
- **Extraterritoriality:** The legal principle granting the Holy See authority over certain properties in Rome and abroad, similar to embassy privileges.

## Debates and Open Questions
The source notes that after Italy seized Rome in 1870, there was "some uncertainty among jurists" about whether the Holy See could continue as an independent personality in international law without territory. This was resolved by its sustained diplomatic practice. The distinction between the Holy See and Vatican City State is a key point of clarification in international law and diplomacy.

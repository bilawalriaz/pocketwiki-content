# Florence

## Overview
Florence (Firenze) is the capital of Tuscany, Italy, and a global center for art, architecture, and culture. It was a major medieval financial hub and is widely considered the birthplace of the Renaissance, a period of profound artistic and intellectual rebirth that began in the 14th century. The city's historic center is a UNESCO World Heritage Site, and its legacy in art, language, and politics has had a lasting impact on Western civilization.

## Timeline
- **59 BC** — Founded as a Roman garrison colony named Florentia.
- **c. 1000 AD** — Margrave Hugo chooses Florence as his residency.
- **1100** — Florence is a self-governing commune (city-state).
- **1348** — The Black Death reduces the population by over half.
- **1378** — Revolt of the Ciompi, a brief uprising by wool workers.
- **1434** — Cosimo de' Medici begins the family's de facto rule.
- **1494** — Medici are expelled; a republican government is restored.
- **1512** — Medici regain control with Spanish and Papal support.
- **1531** — Florence officially becomes a monarchy under Alessandro de' Medici.
- **1865** — Florence becomes the capital of the Kingdom of Italy.
- **1871** — The capital is moved to Rome.
- **1982** — The Historic Centre of Florence is declared a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

## Body

### Roman Origins and Medieval Rise
Florence was established by the Romans in 59 BC as a colony for veterans. It grew into an important commercial center due to its location on the Via Cassia and the Arno river. After periods of Ostrogoth and Byzantine rule, it prospered under Lombard and later Carolingian control. By the 11th century, it was a powerful commune. Its merchant banking community introduced key financial innovations like bills of exchange and double-entry bookkeeping, financing European monarchs and the papacy. The city's primary industry was textiles, using the Arno for power and trade.

### The Renaissance and Medici Rule
Florence is considered the birthplace of the Renaissance, a cultural movement emphasizing humanism, realism, and classical values. The powerful Medici family, bankers to the popes, became de facto rulers in the 15th century. Lorenzo de' Medici ("the Magnificent") was a great patron of arts, commissioning works by Michelangelo, Leonardo da Vinci, and Botticelli. The Medici were expelled in 1494, leading to a period of republican rule under the influence of the friar Girolamo Savonarola. The Medici returned in 1512, and in 1531, the city became a hereditary monarchy. The dynasty produced two popes (Leo X and Clement VII) and queens of France, ruling as Grand Dukes of Tuscany until 1737.

### Language, Literature, and Music
The Florentine dialect, used by literary giants Dante Alighieri, Petrarch, and Boccaccio in the 14th century, became the basis for standard Italian. Dante's *Divine Comedy* is a foundational work of Italian literature. In music, Florence was a key center during the Renaissance and Baroque periods. The Florentine Camerata, a group of intellectuals, experimented with setting Greek myths to music in the late 16th century, leading to the invention of opera. The piano was also invented in Florence in 1709.

### Art and Architecture
Florence was the epicenter of High Renaissance art, which focused on naturalism, emotion, and individualism. Artists like Donatello, Masaccio, Botticelli, Leonardo da Vinci, and Michelangelo created masterpieces that are now housed in the city's numerous museums, such as the Uffizi Gallery and the Accademia. The city's skyline is dominated by the Cathedral of Santa Maria del Fiore (the Duomo), whose massive brick dome, engineered by Filippo Brunelleschi, was a feat of Renaissance architecture. Other key landmarks include the Palazzo Vecchio, the Ponte Vecchio, and the Basilica of Santa Croce.

### Modern Florence
Florence served as Italy's capital from 1865 to 1871. During World War II, it suffered a German occupation and the destruction of all its Arno bridges except the Ponte Vecchio. It was liberated in 1944. A devastating flood of the Arno in 1966 damaged many art treasures. Today, tourism is the dominant industry, with the city attracting millions of visitors annually to its cultural sites. Florence is also a major center for fashion, with brands like Gucci and Ferragamo headquartered there, and remains a significant economic and educational hub.

## Terms
- **Renaissance**: A cultural, artistic, political, and economic revival of classical Greek and Roman ideas that began in Italy in the 14th century.
- **Commune**: A medieval city-state governed as a republic by its leading citizens.
- **Medici**: A powerful banking family that ruled Florence and later Tuscany, becoming major patrons of the arts and producing two popes.
- **Humanism**: An intellectual movement of the Renaissance that emphasized human potential and the study of classical texts.
- **Florin**: A gold coin first minted in Florence in 1252, which became a standard currency for international trade.
- **ZTL (Zona Traffico Limitato)**: A restricted traffic zone in the city center where access is limited to authorized vehicles.
- **Stendhal syndrome**: A psychosomatic disorder causing rapid heartbeat, dizziness, and confusion when exposed to objects of great personal significance, such as art.

## Debates and open questions
- The exact origin of the name "Firenze" is debated among linguists. One view holds it derives from the locative form of *Florentiae*, while another disputes this.
- The precise population of Florence at its height around 1325 is uncertain, with estimates for the urban population ranging up to 120,000.
- The role and legacy of Girolamo Savonarola remain complex; he is seen by some as a puritanical reformer and by others as a fanatical tyrant.

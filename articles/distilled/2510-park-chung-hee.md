# Park Chung Hee

## Overview
Park Chung Hee (1917–1979) was a South Korean army officer and politician who ruled from 1961 until his assassination. He seized power via a military coup and established an authoritarian presidential system. His regime is defined by a stark duality: it oversaw the "Miracle on the Han River," a period of rapid industrialization that transformed South Korea into a major economy, while implementing a repressive dictatorship that curtailed civil liberties and human rights.

- **Oct 26, 1979** — Assassinated by KCIA Director Kim Jae-gyu.

## Body

### Early Life and Military Career
Born into extreme poverty, Park's childhood was marked by hunger. He attended teacher-training school but was drawn to the military, influenced by Japanese militarism. In 1939, he wrote a blood oath to gain admission to the Manchukuo Army Military Academy, adopting the Japanese name Takagi Masao. After serving in the Manchukuo Imperial Army, he returned to Korea after WWII, joined the South Korean army, and rose rapidly during the Korean War, becoming a brigadier general by 1953.

### Rise to Power
Following the ousting of President Syngman Rhee in 1960, South Korea's Second Republic was plagued by instability. Park formed the Military Revolutionary Committee and led the May 16, 1961 coup, initially tolerated by the U.S. He established the Korean Central Intelligence Agency (KCIA) to consolidate power. After serving as junta chairman, he was elected president in 1963.

### Presidency: Foreign Policy and Economic Development
Park's foreign policy was transactional and anti-communist. He normalized relations with Japan in 1965, securing reparations and loans. He sent over 300,000 troops to the Vietnam War at the U.S. request, receiving substantial aid in return. Relations with the U.S. were complex; while reliant on its security guarantee, Park clashed with Washington over his authoritarian rule and secretly pursued a nuclear weapons program in the 1970s until forced by the U.S. to abandon it.

Domestically, Park launched Five-Year Plans to drive export-oriented industrialization. The state partnered with and nurtured family-owned conglomerates known as *chaebols* (e.g., Hyundai, Samsung, LG), rewarding them with loans and subsidies for meeting export targets. This strategy led to the "Miracle on the Han River," drastically reducing poverty and building a modern industrial base. However, this growth came at the cost of labor rights and was accompanied by state control over the economy.

### Authoritarian Rule and the Yushin System
After a close 1971 election, Park grew anxious about his hold on power, especially with the U.S. Nixon Doctrine signaling reduced American commitment. In 1972, he dissolved the legislature in a self-coup and implemented the Yushin ("rejuvenation") Constitution. This document created a legal dictatorship, granting him sweeping powers to rule by decree, suspending constitutional freedoms, and allowing indefinite presidential terms. His regime used the KCIA to arrest, torture, and execute political opponents. Public discontent grew, culminating in the nationwide Busan–Masan Uprising in October 1979.

### Assassination and Legacy
On October 26, 1979, Park was assassinated by KCIA Director Kim Jae-gyu during a private banquet. Whether the act was spontaneous or a premeditated coup remains unclear. Park's legacy is deeply polarized in South Korea. He is credited by many for laying the foundation for the country's economic prosperity. Critics condemn his brutal authoritarianism, human rights abuses, and prioritization of economic growth over democratic freedoms. His daughter, Park Geun-hye, later served as South Korea's president from 2013 until her impeachment in 2017.

## Terms
*   **Chaebol:** Large, family-controlled South Korean conglomerates supported by the state to drive export-led industrialization.
*   **Miracle on the Han River:** The period of rapid economic growth and industrialization in South Korea from the 1960s to the 1990s, which began under Park's rule.
*   **Yushin Constitution:** The 1972 constitution that established Park's authoritarian "Fourth Republic," granting him dictatorial powers.
*   **Korean Central Intelligence Agency (KCIA):** The powerful intelligence and secret police agency created by Park in 1961 to suppress domestic opposition.
*   **Saemaul Undong (New Village Movement):** A state-led campaign launched in 1970 to modernize rural South Korea through infrastructure projects.
*   **Busan–Masan Uprising:** The major anti-government protests in October 1979 that challenged Park's Yushin system shortly before his assassination.
*   **May 16 coup:** The 1961 military coup d'état led by Park Chung Hee that overthrew the democratically elected Second Republic.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Economic Growth vs. Human Rights:** Scholars debate whether the economic development achieved under Park justifies or is separate from his severe political repression.
*   **Nature of the Assassination:** It remains unclear whether Kim Jae-gyu's act was spontaneous to prevent bloodshed or part of a pre-arranged coup.
*   **Park's Personal Corruption:** While his regime fostered systemic corruption through its ties to *chaebols*, Park himself was often depicted as not personally corrupt, though a half-million dollars was found in his safe after his death.
*   **Historical Collaboration:** Park's early career in the Japanese-sponsored Manchukuo army and his admiration for Japanese militarism lead critics to label him a pro-Japanese collaborator, a contentious issue in Korean historical memory.

# Xi'an

## Overview
Xi'an, historically known as Chang'an, is one of China's oldest and most important cities, serving as the capital for multiple major dynasties. It is a major cultural, industrial, and educational hub in Northwest China, renowned for its historical sites like the Terracotta Army and its role as a terminal point of the ancient Silk Road.

## Body

### Historical Significance and Names
The area has been inhabited for at least 500,000 years. It became a political center with the Zhou dynasty (11th century BC) and was unified as the Qin capital. The Han dynasty formally founded Chang'an in 202 BC, building the massive Weiyang Palace. The city was devastated at the end of the Tang dynasty. Under the Ming dynasty, the current city wall was built (1370), and the name "Xi'an" (Western Peace) was adopted in 1369. The city has held many names, including Fenghao, Xianyang, Chang'an, Daxing, and Anxi.

### Modern Era and Conflicts
During the Qing dynasty, a segregated Manchu banner quarter was established. Relations between Han civilians and Manchu bannermen were poor. In 1911, revolutionaries stormed the Manchu fort, killing most of its 20,000 inhabitants. The 1936 Xi'an Incident was a pivotal moment in the Chinese Civil War, leading to a temporary alliance between the Kuomintang and Communists against Japan. The city was captured by Communist forces in 1949. In 2022, it experienced a major COVID-19 outbreak and lockdown.

### Geography and Climate
Xi'an lies on the Guanzhong Plain, bordered by the Qin Mountains to the south and the Wei River to the north. It has a temperate, monsoon-influenced climate with hot, humid summers and cold, dry winters. The National Time Service Center, which generates China's standard time, is located nearby.

### Economy and Industry
Since the 1980s, Xi'an has developed into a major economic hub under China's Western Development policy. Key industries include equipment manufacturing, tourism, and service outsourcing. It is a major center for software development and aerospace. The Xi'an International Trade and Logistics Park is a key dry port for China-Europe freight trains.

### Education and Research
Xi'an is a major educational center with over 60 universities, including Xi'an Jiaotong University and Northwestern Polytechnical University. It ranks among the world's top cities for scientific research output.

### Culture and Religion
The city is home to diverse religious sites, including the Great Mosque (Tang dynasty origin), the Nestorian Stele (781 AD), and numerous Buddhist pagodas like the Giant Wild Goose Pagoda. Local cuisine includes dishes like *yangrou paomo* (lamb soup with bread) and *roujiamo* (meat bun).

## Terms
- **Chang'an**: The historical name for Xi'an, meaning "Perpetual Peace," used during the Han and Tang dynasties.
- **Guanzhong Plain**: The fertile plain where Xi'an is located, historically known as the "Nation of Heaven."
- **Hukou**: China's household registration system, used for demographic tracking.
- **Nestorian Stele**: A 781 AD limestone stele documenting early Christianity in China.
- **Qinqiang**: The oldest form of Chinese opera, originating in the region.
- **Silk Road**: Ancient trade route for which Xi'an was a major eastern terminus.
- **Terracotta Army**: Collection of clay soldiers buried with Qin Shi Huang, near Xi'an.
- **Third Front Construction**: Mao-era policy to develop industry in China's interior.

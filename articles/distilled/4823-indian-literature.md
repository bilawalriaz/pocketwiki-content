# Indian literature

## Overview
Indian literature encompasses the vast literary traditions of the Indian subcontinent, produced in numerous languages from ancient oral epics to modern works. It is a cornerstone of Indian cultural identity, reflecting its religious, philosophical, and social evolution over millennia. The field is defined by its multilingualism, with 22 officially recognized languages in the Indian Constitution and 24 recognized by the Sahitya Akademi, India's national literary academy.

## Timeline
- **c. 1500–1200 BCE** — The Rig Veda, the earliest Sanskrit literature, is composed.
- **End of 2nd millennium BCE** — The Sanskrit epics Ramayana and Mahabharata are codified.
- **c. 300 BCE – 300 CE** — The Sangam period of ancient Tamil literature flourishes.
- **1st century CE** — Ancient Meitei literature appears with works like the Ougri and Numit Kappa.
- **7th–12th centuries CE** — The Bhakti movement originates in Tamilakam, spreading devotional poetry.
- **9th–10th centuries CE** — Literature in Kannada and Telugu emerges.
- **13th century CE** — Medieval Odia literature begins with Sarala Das's translation of the Mahabharata.
- **14th–18th centuries CE** — The Bhakti movement spreads north, producing poets like Kabir and Tulsidas.
- **1913** — Rabindranath Tagore becomes India's first Nobel laureate in literature.

## Body

### Ancient and Classical Literature
The earliest Indian literature was oral. Sanskrit literature begins with the Vedic period (c. 1500–1200 BCE), including the Rig Veda and Upanishads. This was followed by the great epics, the Ramayana and Mahabharata. Classical Sanskrit literature saw a rapid development, with playwrights like Kālidāsa (author of *Recognition of Shakuntala*) and texts standardizing grammar like Pāṇini's *Ashtadhyayi*. Parallel traditions emerged in other languages, including the Pāli Canon of Buddhist literature and the ancient Tamil Sangam literature (c. 300 BCE–300 CE), which dealt with human emotions and relationships. Ancient Meitei literature also appeared in the 1st century CE.

### Medieval and Bhakti Literature
From the 9th century onward, literature in modern Indian languages like Kannada, Telugu, Marathi, and Bengali began to develop. A transformative period was the Bhakti movement (6th century CE onwards), which emphasized personal devotion. Originating in the south, it spread north between the 14th and 18th centuries, producing devotional poets like Kabir, Tulsidas, and Guru Nanak. This movement led to literature in vernacular dialects, significantly differing from classical Sanskrit traditions.

### Modern and Regional Literatures
Modern Indian literature is characterized by the flourishing of numerous regional languages. Bengali literature gained international fame with Rabindranath Tagore's 1913 Nobel Prize. Hindi literature evolved from medieval religious poetry to modern forms. Other languages, including Gujarati, Assamese, Odia, and Punjabi, developed rich literary heritages. The 20th century also saw the rise of Indian English literature, a unique dialect born from colonial history. Contemporary literature includes significant movements like Dalit literature, which advocates for caste equality, and LGBTQ literature.

## Terms
*   **Sahitya Akademi:** India's national academy of letters, which recognizes 24 literary languages.
*   **Sangam literature:** The ancient Tamil literature corpus from the Sangam period (c. 300 BCE–300 CE).
*   **Bhakti movement:** A medieval Hindu religious movement emphasizing personal devotion, which greatly influenced regional literatures.
*   **Prakrit:** A group of Middle Indo-Aryan languages used in ancient and medieval Indian literature alongside Sanskrit.
*   **Pāli Canon:** The standard collection of scriptures in the Theravada Buddhist tradition.
*   **Charyapadas:** Buddhist songs composed from the 8th to 12th centuries, considered early examples of Assamese and Bengali literature.
*   **Dalit literature:** Literature written by Dalits about their experiences, advocating for social justice and equality.
*   **Jnanpith Award:** One of India's highest literary honors, awarded annually for outstanding contribution to Indian literature.

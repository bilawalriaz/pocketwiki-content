# Madurai

## Overview
Madurai is a major city in Tamil Nadu, India, with a documented history of over 2,500 years. It is the cultural capital of Tamil Nadu, historically the seat of the Tamil Sangams (ancient scholarly assemblies), and a major pilgrimage center due to its iconic Meenakshi Temple. Its continuous habitation and evolving political control—from ancient Pandyan kings to British colonial rule—make it a key case study in the urban and cultural history of South India.

## Timeline
- **c. 300 BCE** — Earliest recorded mentions by Greek ambassador Megasthenes and in Kautilya's *Arthashastra*.
- **3rd century BCE – 3rd century CE** — The third Tamil Sangam is traditionally held in Madurai.
- **c. 590 CE** — Pandyas under Kadunkon oust the Kalabhra dynasty and re-establish control.
- **Early 9th century** — Chola dynasty captures Madurai from the Pandyas.
- **Early 13th century** — Second Pandyan empire established with Madurai as capital.
- **1378 CE** — Vijayanagara Empire annexes the Madurai Sultanate.
- **1559 CE** — Madurai becomes independent under the Nayak dynasty.
- **1801** — Madurai comes under direct British East India Company control.
- **1866** — Madurai is constituted as a municipality.
- **1921** — Mahatma Gandhi first adopts the loin cloth in Madurai.
- **1971** — Municipality upgraded to a Municipal Corporation.
- **2004** — Second bench of the Madras High Court begins functioning in Madurai.

## Body

### Historical Evolution
Madurai's recorded history begins in the 3rd century BCE. It was the capital of the Pandyan dynasty, which was later displaced by the Kalabhra and then the Chola empires. The city changed hands between Cholas and Pandyas until the second Pandyan empire was established. Post-Pandyan rule saw a succession of powers: the Delhi Sultanate (forming the independent Madurai Sultanate), the Vijayanagara Empire, and the Nayak dynasty. British rule began in 1801, leading to administrative modernization, including the demolition of temple fortifications and the creation of a municipality in 1866. The city was a site of early 20th-century nationalist activity, including Gandhi's adoption of the loin cloth and the 1939 temple entry movement.

### Urban Form and Architecture
The city's layout is concentric, built around the Meenakshi and Koodal Azhagar temples. This design was formalized by Nayak king Viswanatha Nayak in the 16th century, following *Shilpa Shastras* (traditional architectural rules). Streets are named after Tamil months and correspond to festival calendars. The temple precincts (*prakarams*) and streets accommodate elaborate processions, with chariot sizes increasing with distance from the center. British urbanization in the 19th century added industrial and administrative layers.

### Demographics and Administration
As of the 2011 census, the city had a population of 1,017,865, with a high literacy rate of 81.95%. Tamil is the primary language, with Sourashtra as the largest minority. The city is administered by the Madurai Municipal Corporation (established 1971), with 100 wards. It is represented in the state assembly by six constituencies and in the national parliament by one Lok Sabha constituency. The Madras High Court bench, established in 2004, is a significant judicial seat.

### Economy and Infrastructure
Madurai is an industrial and educational hub. Key industries include automobile, rubber, chemical, and granite manufacturing. The economy has a strong agricultural base, notably for paddy and jasmine ("Madurai Malli"). The city is a transport node with national highways, a major railway junction, and an international airport. It is home to numerous educational institutions, including Madurai Kamaraj University and several medical and engineering colleges, with an AIIMS under construction.

### Religious and Cultural Significance
Madurai is famed for its temples, most notably the Meenakshi Amman Temple, a major Hindu pilgrimage site with 14 towering gateways (*gopurams*). The Koodal Azhagar Temple is a revered Vishnu shrine. The city also has significant Islamic and Christian sites. It is known as "Thoongaa Nagaram" (the city that never sleeps) for its active nightlife and hosts major festivals like the 10-day Meenakshi Tirukkalyanam, attracting a million visitors. Traditional sports like *jallikattu* (bull-taming) remain culturally important.

## Terms
- **Sangam**: A legendary assembly of Tamil scholars and poets; the third is traditionally associated with Madurai.
- **Pandyan Dynasty**: An ancient Tamil dynasty that ruled parts of South India, with Madurai as a primary capital.
- **Gopuram**: A monumental, ornate entrance tower, typically of a Hindu temple in the Dravidian style.
- **Prakaram**: An outer precinct or pathway surrounding a temple shrine.
- **Shilpa Shastras**: Ancient Indian texts outlining rules for architecture and sculpture.
- **Divya Desam**: One of the 108 Vishnu temples revered in the works of the Alvar saints.
- **Jallikattu**: A traditional bull-taming sport played in Tamil Nadu as part of Pongal celebrations.
- **Municipal Corporation**: A local government body for large urban areas in India, with elected representatives.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The identification of "Methora" in Megasthenes' accounts is contested; some scholars believe it refers to Mathura in North India, not Madurai.
- The exact dates and historicity of the three Tamil Sangams are debated, with the third Sangam's traditional dating (3rd century BCE – 3rd century CE) being a matter of scholarly discussion.
- The city's ranking as the "dirtiest city" in the 2025 Swachh Survekshan report highlights an ongoing challenge in balancing its cultural heritage with modern urban sanitation and waste management.

# Indira Gandhi

## Overview
Indira Gandhi (1917–1984) was India's first and only female Prime Minister, serving four terms between 1966 and 1984. A central figure in Indian politics, she was known for her uncompromising leadership, centralization of power, and role in major events like the 1971 war with Pakistan and the 1975–77 Emergency. Her legacy is marked by both significant achievements in foreign policy and economic development, and controversy over authoritarian rule.

## Body

### Early Life and Political Rise
Indira Gandhi was the daughter of India's first Prime Minister, Jawaharlal Nehru. She was educated at home and at various schools in India, Switzerland, and England, including Somerville College, Oxford. She married Feroze Gandhi in 1942, and they had two sons, Rajiv and Sanjay. After her father's death in 1964, she was appointed to the Rajya Sabha (upper house of Parliament) and served as Minister of Information and Broadcasting under Prime Minister Lal Bahadur Shastri. Following Shastri's death in 1966, she won the Congress party leadership election and became Prime Minister, initially perceived as a puppet by party bosses.

### First Three Terms (1966–1977)
Gandhi quickly consolidated power, splitting the Congress party in 1969 after conflicts with senior leaders over issues like bank nationalization. Her second term saw a military conflict with China in 1967. Her third term (1971–1977) was marked by the decisive 1971 war with Pakistan, which led to the creation of Bangladesh and established India as the dominant regional power. She launched the "Garibi Hatao" (Remove Poverty) campaign and authorized India's first nuclear test in 1974. In 1975, the Allahabad High Court voided her 1971 election for electoral malpractice. In response, she declared a State of Emergency, suspending civil liberties and ruling by decree until 1977.

### Opposition and Return (1977–1984)
The Emergency led to a massive anti-incumbency wave, and Gandhi lost the 1977 election. She was arrested in 1978 but returned to power in a 1980 landslide. Her fourth term was dominated by the Sikh separatist movement in Punjab. In June 1984, she ordered Operation Blue Star, a military assault on the Golden Temple in Amritsar to remove militants, which caused significant damage and casualties. On 31 October 1984, she was assassinated by two of her Sikh bodyguards in retaliation for the operation.

### Foreign Policy
Gandhi pursued a non-aligned but pro-Soviet foreign policy, signing a friendship treaty with the USSR in 1971. She strengthened India's position in South Asia, annexing Sikkim in 1975 and establishing the South Asian Association for Regional Cooperation (SAARC) in 1983. She was a staunch supporter of Palestinian causes and critical of U.S. policy, though relations improved under President Reagan. She chaired the Non-Aligned Movement from 1983 to 1984.

### Economic and Domestic Policy
Gandhi's economic policies shifted from liberalization to state socialism. She nationalized banks, coal, steel, and oil industries. The Green Revolution under her leadership made India largely self-sufficient in food. During the Emergency, she implemented land reforms and anti-poverty programs. In her final term, she initiated Operation Forward, a cautious move toward economic deregulation. She also resolved the language policy dispute by guaranteeing the use of both Hindi and English as official languages.

## Terms
*   **Rajya Sabha:** The upper house of India's Parliament.
*   **Lok Sabha:** The lower house of India's Parliament.
*   **Non-Aligned Movement (NAM):** An international organization of states not formally aligned with any major power bloc during the Cold War.
*   **Operation Blue Star:** The 1984 Indian military operation to remove Sikh militants from the Golden Temple in Amritsar.
*   **Garibi Hatao:** A Hindi slogan meaning "Remove Poverty," central to Gandhi's political platform.
*   **Green Revolution:** A period of increased agricultural production in India due to new technologies and policies.
*   **State of Emergency:** A period from 1975–1977 when civil liberties were suspended and Gandhi ruled by decree.
*   **Five-Year Plan:** A centralized economic planning model used in India to set growth targets and resource allocation.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Ideology:** Scholars debate whether Gandhi was a genuine socialist or used populist, socialist rhetoric for political expediency. Her policies showed contradictions, such as nationalizing industries while also courting business interests.
*   **The Emergency:** Her declaration of the Emergency in 1975 remains highly controversial. Supporters argue it was necessary to restore order, while critics view it as a move to suppress dissent and consolidate authoritarian power.
*   **Operation Blue Star:** The decision to storm the Golden Temple is debated. Some see it as a necessary action against militancy, while others criticize it as a disproportionate military assault on a sacred site that inflamed communal tensions.
*   **Legacy of Authoritarianism:** Critics accuse her of damaging internal party democracy, weakening state chief ministers, and fostering a culture of nepotism in Indian politics.

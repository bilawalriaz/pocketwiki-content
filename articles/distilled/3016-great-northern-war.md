# Great Northern War

## Overview
The Great Northern War (1700–1721) was a major European conflict in which a coalition led by Russia successfully challenged and ultimately dismantled the Swedish Empire's dominance in Northern, Central, and Eastern Europe. The war marked the decline of Sweden as a great power and the rise of Russia as a dominant force in the Baltic region and European politics.

## Timeline
- **1700** — Coalition of Denmark-Norway, Saxony-Poland-Lithuania, and Russia attacks Sweden. Sweden defeats Denmark at Travendal and Russia at Narva.
- **1701–1706** — Charles XII campaigns in Poland-Lithuania, dethroning Augustus II and forcing him to sign the Treaty of Altranstädt (1706).
- **1703** — Russia founds Saint Petersburg, securing access to the Baltic Sea.
- **1709** — Decisive Russian victory at the Battle of Poltava destroys the main Swedish army; Charles XII flees to the Ottoman Empire.
- **1710** — Coalition captures Riga and Tallinn, evicting Sweden from the Baltic provinces.
- **1713–1714** — Russia occupies Finland. Charles XII returns from exile.
- **1715** — Hanover and Brandenburg-Prussia join the anti-Swedish coalition.
- **1716–1718** — Charles XII's campaigns in Norway end with his death at Fredriksten (1718).
- **1719–1721** — Treaties of Stockholm (1719/1720), Frederiksborg (1720), and Nystad (1721) formally end the war.

## Body

### Background and Outbreak
By the mid-17th century, Sweden had built a Baltic empire through a professional army and efficient administration. However, its state could not sustain prolonged warfare, and its resources were drained. In the late 1690s, the adventurer Johann Patkul allied Russia, Denmark-Norway, and Saxony-Poland-Lithuania against Sweden via the secret Treaty of Preobrazhenskoye. They attacked in 1700, sensing an opportunity with the young Charles XII on the Swedish throne.

### Early Swedish Successes (1700–1709)
Charles XII first defeated Denmark, forcing it out of the war in 1700. He then turned east, defeating Russia at Narva. From 1701 to 1706, he campaigned in Poland-Lithuania, defeating Saxon-Polish forces, dethroning Augustus II, and installing Stanisław Leszczyński as king. The Treaty of Altranstädt (1706) forced Augustus to renounce the Polish crown and ally with Sweden. Meanwhile, Russia recovered from Narva, captured Ingria, and founded Saint Petersburg (1703). Charles XII then invaded Russia, but his campaign ended in the catastrophic defeat at Poltava (1709), after which he fled to the Ottoman Empire.

### The War After Poltava (1709–1714)
The defeat at Poltava revived the anti-Swedish coalition. Denmark-Norway and Saxony re-entered the war. Russia captured Riga and Tallinn (1710) and occupied Finland (1713–14). Charles XII's exile in the Ottoman Empire led to the Pruth River Campaign (1711), where Russia was defeated but negotiated a retreat. Charles eventually returned to Sweden in 1714.

### Final Campaigns and Peace (1715–1721)
After Charles XII's return, Hanover and Prussia joined the coalition. Sweden lost its German possessions. Charles launched two failed campaigns in Norway and was killed in 1718. The war concluded with a series of treaties: the Treaties of Stockholm (1719/1720) with Hanover and Prussia, the Treaty of Frederiksborg (1720) with Denmark, and the Treaty of Nystad (1721) with Russia. Sweden ceded its Baltic provinces and southern Pomerania, lost its exemption from the Sound Dues, and its absolute monarchy ended. Russia secured its Baltic conquests and emerged as a great power.

## Terms
- **Sound Dues**: Transit taxes on cargo moving between the North Sea and the Baltic Sea, historically collected by Denmark.
- **Treaty of Altranstädt (1706)**: Forced Augustus II to renounce the Polish crown and ally with Sweden.
- **Battle of Poltava (1709)**: Decisive Russian victory that destroyed the main Swedish army and turned the tide of the war.
- **Pruth River Campaign (1711)**: A failed Russian invasion of the Ottoman Empire, resulting in a treaty that allowed Charles XII's return.
- **Treaty of Nystad (1721)**: Final peace treaty where Sweden ceded its Baltic provinces to Russia.
- **Age of Liberty**: Period in Swedish history (1718–1772) following the death of Charles XII, characterized by a shift from absolute monarchy to parliamentary rule.

# Qara Qoyunlu

## Overview
The Qara Qoyunlu ("Black Sheep Turkomans") were a Turkoman, culturally Persianate, Muslim dynasty that ruled parts of present-day Azerbaijan, Armenia, Iran, Turkey, and Iraq from c. 1374 to 1468. They rose from vassalage to the Jalayirid Sultanate to become a major regional power, competing with the Timurids and their rivals, the Aq Qoyunlu ("White Sheep Turkomans"). Their rule is noted for the spread of Shia Islam, patronage of Persianate culture, and architectural achievements, particularly under Sultan Jahan Shah.

## Timeline
- **c. 1374** — Qara Qoyunlu become vassals of the Jalayirid Sultanate.
- **1400** — Defeated by Timur; leader Qara Yusuf flees to Mamluk Egypt.
- **1406-1408** — Qara Yusuf returns, defeats Timurids at Nakhchivan and Sardrud, reclaims Azerbaijan.
- **1410** — Capture Baghdad and Tabriz, effectively ending the Jalayirid Sultanate.
- **1410** — Armenia falls under Qara Qoyunlu control.
- **1420** — Death of Qara Yusuf sparks succession wars with Timurid intervention.
- **1436-1438** — Jahan Shah, with Timurid support, defeats his brother Iskander and seizes the throne.
- **1447** — Upon Timurid Shah Rukh's death, Jahan Shah becomes independent ruler.
- **1452-1453** — Jahan Shah expands south, taking Isfahan, Shiraz, and Yazd.
- **1465** — Blue Mosque in Tabriz completed.
- **1467** — Jahan Shah is killed by the Aq Qoyunlu at the Battle of Chapakchur.
- **1468** — Aq Qoyunlu under Uzun Hasan conquer Qara Qoyunlu territories.

## Body

### Origins and Rise
The Qara Qoyunlu were a confederation of Turkoman tribes, likely named for the black sheep on their flags. Their ruling family descended from the Baharlu tribe of the Oghuz Turks. Initially vassals of the Jalayirid Sultanate, they gained independence after their leader Qara Yusuf returned from exile in Mamluk Egypt. He decisively defeated the Timurids, reoccupied Tabriz in 1409, and captured Baghdad in 1410, ending Jalayirid rule.

### Rule and Expansion
The dynasty's power peaked under Jahan Shah (r. 1436-1467). After securing his throne with Timurid backing, he expanded significantly following the Timurid Empire's decline after 1447, annexing parts of Iraq and western Iran. His rule was marked by internal strife with rebellious sons. The Qara Qoyunlu state was organized on Persianate models, using Persian for administration and diplomacy. Provinces were governed by military beys, and cities by darugha officials.

### Religion and Culture
The Qara Qoyunlu are considered forerunners of the Safavid dynasty in promoting Shia Islam as a unifying ideology, though the extent of their rulers' personal Shia commitment is debated. Culturally, they were patrons of Persianate arts. Jahan Shah was a poet in Azerbaijani and Persian. Architecturally, they are famed for the Blue Mosque in Tabriz (completed 1465), with its innovative tilework that influenced Mamluk and Ottoman designs. Jahan Shah's son, Pir Budaq, was a major patron of refined manuscript production.

### Decline and Legacy
Internal conflicts and a catastrophic defeat by the Aq Qoyunlu in 1467 led to the dynasty's collapse. By 1468, the Aq Qoyunlu had conquered their territories. A branch of the Qara Qoyunlu later migrated to India, where Sultan Quli Qutb Shah founded the Qutb Shahi dynasty of Golconda.

## Terms
- **Qara Qoyunlu**: "Black Sheep Turkomans"; the name of the Turkoman dynasty.
- **Aq Qoyunlu**: "White Sheep Turkomans"; the rival Turkoman dynasty that ultimately defeated the Qara Qoyunlu.
- **Jalayirid Sultanate**: The Baghdad-based dynasty the Qara Qoyunlu initially served and later overthrew.
- **Timurid Empire**: The empire of Timur (Tamerlane) and his successors, a major rival and occasional overlord of the Qara Qoyunlu.
- **Persianate**: A culture influenced by Persian language, literature, and administrative traditions, adopted by many Turkic dynasties.
- **Shia Islam**: The branch of Islam that the Qara Qoyunlu are credited with promoting as a state ideology.
- **Darugha**: A city official responsible for financial, administrative, and political affairs.
- **Bey**: A military governor of a province, a position often hereditary.
- **Blue Mosque (Tabriz)**: A major architectural monument of the Qara Qoyunlu, completed in 1465, renowned for its tilework.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Religious Affiliation**: Scholars debate the sincerity of the Qara Qoyunlu rulers' Shia Islam. While they promoted it, some rulers minted coins with the names of the four caliphs (rejected by Shia), and contemporary rival sources do not note Shia inclinations.
- **Ethnic and Linguistic Impact**: The dynasty's rule accelerated the Turkicization of Azerbaijan and parts of Fars, but the exact nature and pace of this process is a subject of historical study.
- **Cultural Patronage**: The relative artistic contributions of Jahan Shah versus his son Pir Budaq are distinguished, with Pir Budaq being noted as a more active patron of refined manuscripts.

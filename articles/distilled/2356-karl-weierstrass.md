# Karl Weierstrass

## Overview
Karl Weierstrass (1815–1897) was a German mathematician, often called the "father of modern analysis," who rigorously formalized the foundations of calculus. His work provided the precise definitions and proofs necessary to place analysis on a solid logical footing, influencing all subsequent mathematics.

- **c. 1834–1838** — Attended University of Bonn without completing a degree, focusing on private mathematics study.

- **1843–1850** — Worked as a schoolteacher in Deutsch Krone and Braunsberg, teaching mathematics, physics, botany, and gymnastics.

- **1870** — Began tutoring Sofia Kovalevskaya, whom he mentored for four years.
- **19 February 1897** — Died in Berlin from pneumonia.

## Body

### Biography and Career
Weierstrass was born into a Roman Catholic family. His early interest in mathematics conflicted with his father's plan for him to study law and finance at the University of Bonn, leading him to leave without a degree. He later trained as a teacher in Münster, where he studied under Christoph Gudermann. After teaching at secondary schools, his mathematical publications brought him recognition. He received an honorary doctorate in 1854 and subsequently held academic positions in Berlin, becoming a full professor in 1864. He is noted for his mentorship of mathematician Sofia Kovalevskaya.

### Soundness of Calculus
Weierstrass's primary contribution was establishing rigorous foundations for calculus. At the time, definitions of limits and continuity were vague. While Bernard Bolzano had defined limits earlier, his work was unknown. Augustin-Louis Cauchy had advanced ideas but did not clearly distinguish between continuity and uniform continuity. Weierstrass formalized the concept of uniform convergence, first noted by Gudermann, and used it to correct errors in analysis. He formulated the modern epsilon-delta definition of continuity, which states that a function *f(x)* is continuous at *x₀* if for every ε > 0, there exists a δ > 0 such that |x - x₀| < δ implies |f(x) - f(x₀)| < ε. Using this, he proved the Intermediate Value Theorem and the Bolzano–Weierstrass theorem, applying the latter to study continuous functions on closed, bounded intervals.

### Calculus of Variations
Weierstrass also revolutionized the calculus of variations, the field concerned with finding functions that optimize certain integrals. He provided a complete reformulation of the theory, establishing necessary conditions for strong extrema. He co-devised the Weierstrass–Erdmann condition, which gives sufficient conditions for an extremal curve to have a corner, aiding in finding minimizing curves.

## Terms
- **Epsilon-delta definition**: The formal, rigorous definition of a limit or continuity, using the Greek letters ε (epsilon) and δ (delta) to denote arbitrary small positive quantities.
- **Uniform convergence**: A type of convergence of a sequence of functions where the rate of convergence is independent of the point in the domain; crucial for preserving properties like continuity in limits.
- **Intermediate Value Theorem**: A theorem stating that a continuous function on a closed interval takes on every value between its values at the endpoints.
- **Bolzano–Weierstrass theorem**: A theorem stating that every bounded sequence in Euclidean space has a convergent subsequence.
- **Calculus of variations**: A field of mathematical analysis that deals with optimizing functionals, often expressed as integrals.
- **Weierstrass–Erdmann condition**: Conditions specifying that certain quantities must be continuous at a corner point of an extremal curve in a variational problem.

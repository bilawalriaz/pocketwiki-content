# Kamehameha I

## Overview
Kamehameha I, also known as Kamehameha the Great, was the conqueror and first ruler of the Kingdom of Hawaii. He unified the Hawaiian Islands through a combination of military prowess, strategic alliances, and the adoption of Western technology, establishing a single kingdom that endured after his death.

## Timeline
- **c. 1736–1761** — Birth of Kamehameha (Paiʻea) in Kohala, Hawaii Island.
- **1782** — Death of his uncle, King Kalaniʻōpuʻu; Kamehameha receives the war god Kūkaʻilimoku and Waipiʻo Valley.
- **1782** — Battle of Mokuʻōhai; Kamehameha defeats his cousin Kīwalaʻō, gaining control of Kona, Kohala, and Hāmākua districts.
- **1790** — Invasion of Maui; victory at the Battle of Kepaniwai.
- **1790** — Acquisition of Western advisors Isaac Davis and John Young after the capture of the ship *Fair American*.
- **1791** — Death of rival chief Keōua Kūʻahuʻula; Kamehameha becomes sole King of Hawaii Island.
- **1795** — Conquest of Maui, Molokaʻi, and Oʻahu, culminating in the decisive Battle of Nuʻuanu.
- **1796** — Failed invasion of Kauaʻi due to storms; suppression of a rebellion in Hilo.
- **1804** — A devastating epidemic (*maʻi ʻokuʻu*) halts preparations for a second invasion of Kauaʻi.
- **1810** — Peaceful unification of the islands with the submission of Kauaʻi's King Kaumualiʻi.
- **May 8 or 14, 1819** — Death of Kamehameha I; his burial site is kept secret.

## Body

### Birth and Early Life
Kamehameha was born to Kekuʻiapoiwa II and Keōua Kalanikupuapa'ikalaninui. His birth date is debated, with estimates ranging from 1736 to 1761; historian Ralph Simpson Kuykendall believed it was between 1748 and 1761. A common early date of 1758, linked to Halley's Comet, is considered inconsistent with life events. The 1736 date, supported by Samuel Kamakau and Abraham Fornander, is widely accepted. After his father's early death, Kamehameha was raised in the court of his uncle, King Kalaniʻōpuʻu.

### Conquest and Unification
Following Kalaniʻōpuʻu's death in 1782, Kamehameha was given the war god Kūkaʻilimoku but not the kingship, which went to his cousin Kīwalaʻō. Backed by five powerful Kona chiefs, Kamehameha defeated Kīwalaʻō in 1782. He then spent years consolidating power on Hawaii Island, aided by Western weapons and advisors like John Young and Isaac Davis, who came into his service after the 1790 incidents involving the ships *Eleanora* and *Fair American*.

Kamehameha launched a campaign to unite all the islands. He invaded Maui in 1790, defeating Kalanikūpule at the Battle of Kepaniwai. After securing Hawaii Island by eliminating his last rival, Keōua Kūʻahuʻula, in 1791, he conquered Maui, Molokaʻi, and Oʻahu in 1795. The campaign culminated in the Battle of Nuʻuanu, where over 700 of Kalanikūpule's warriors were driven over the Pali cliff. A planned invasion of Kauaʻi was thwarted by storms and a major epidemic. In 1810, Kauaʻi's King Kaumualiʻi peacefully submitted, completing the unification.

### Rule and Legacy
As king, Kamehameha unified the legal system and used tax products to promote trade with Europe and the United States. He is credited with the Law of the Splintered Paddle, a decree protecting non-combatants. He maintained the traditional Hawaiian religion and the *kapu* system during his reign. He died in 1819, and his body was hidden in the secret *hūnākele* custom to protect his *mana* (spiritual power). His final resting place remains unknown. He had many wives and children; only those through his highest-ranking wife, Keōpūolani, succeeded him. His legacy includes the public holiday King Kamehameha I Day, first proclaimed in 1871.

## Terms
*   **Mana**: The spiritual power or life force believed to be inherent in people, objects, or places.
*   **Kapu**: The ancient Hawaiian system of laws and taboos that governed social and religious life.
*   **Aliʻi**: The Hawaiian noble class or chiefs.
*   **Heiau**: A traditional Hawaiian temple.
*   **Hānai**: A traditional, informal adoption practice.
*   **Kūkaʻilimoku**: The Hawaiian god of war, to whom Kamehameha was the guardian.
*   **Peleleu**: A large, double-hulled war canoe.
*   **Hūnākele**: The custom of secretly hiding the remains of a high chief to protect their mana.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Birth Date**: The exact year of Kamehameha's birth is debated, with scholarly estimates ranging from 1736 to 1761.
*   **Paternity**: While officially the son of Keōua, oral traditions and accounts from figures like Kameʻeiamoku suggest he may have been the son of Maui chief Kahekili II, a claim dismissed by later King Kalākaua as scandalous.
*   **Number of Wives and Children**: The exact count is uncertain due to destroyed records, with estimates for wives ranging from 21 to 30.

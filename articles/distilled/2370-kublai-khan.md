# Kublai Khan

## Overview
Kublai Khan (1215–1294) was the founder and first emperor of the Yuan dynasty, the Mongol-led dynasty that ruled China. As the grandson of Genghis Khan, he completed the conquest of the Song dynasty, becoming the first non-Han emperor to rule all of China proper. His reign was marked by the establishment of a sophisticated administrative system, significant cultural and economic developments, and costly, largely unsuccessful military campaigns against Japan, Vietnam, and other neighbors.

## Timeline
- **1215** — Kublai Khan is born.
- **1260** — Kublai is proclaimed Great Khan (Khagan) at a kurultai, beginning a civil war with his brother Ariq Böke.
- **1264** — Ariq Böke surrenders, ending the Toluid Civil War and solidifying Kublai's rule.
- **1271** — Kublai formally establishes the Yuan dynasty, proclaiming the dynastic name "Great Yuan."
- **1274** — First failed invasion of Japan.
- **1276** — The Song dynasty capital, Hangzhou, falls to the Yuan.
- **1279** — Final defeat of Song loyalists at the Battle of Yamen, unifying China under Yuan rule.
- **1281** — Second, larger failed invasion of Japan.
- **1287** — Nayan's rebellion in Manchuria is crushed by Kublai.
- **1294** — Kublai Khan dies; succeeded by his grandson Temür Khan.

## Body

### Early Life and Rise to Power
Kublai was the grandson of Genghis Khan and second son of Tolui. He showed an early attraction to Han Chinese culture, surrounding himself with Chinese advisors like Liu Bingzhong and Buddhist monks. As viceroy of northern China under his brother Möngke Khan, he administered territory, boosted agriculture, and conquered the Dali Kingdom in Yunnan (1253). After Möngke's death in 1259, Kublai was proclaimed Great Khan in 1260, but faced a civil war with his brother Ariq Böke, who had been elected by a rival kurultai. Kublai's victory in 1264 marked the effective division of the Mongol Empire, with his real power limited to the Yuan realm.

### Establishment of the Yuan Dynasty
In 1271, Kublai formally established the Yuan dynasty, claiming orthodox succession from prior Chinese dynasties. He moved his capital to Khanbaliq (Dadu, modern Beijing) and adopted Chinese political models, creating a provincial administrative system. His government relied on a mix of Mongol, Chinese, and other advisors, including the Tibetan lama Drogön Chögyal Phagpa, who was made Imperial Preceptor. Kublai promoted economic growth by rebuilding the Grand Canal and extending highways. A key innovation was the state-issued paper currency, *jiaochao*, which became the world's first fiat money, though later issues led to inflation.

### Military Campaigns and Foreign Relations
Kublai completed the conquest of the Song dynasty in 1279, unifying China. He then launched a series of costly invasions to expand Yuan influence. He twice attempted to invade Japan (1274, 1281), both times thwarted by storms and flawed ship design. He also invaded Vietnam (Đại Việt) multiple times, Burma, and Java, achieving only tributary relationships at best. These campaigns strained the treasury. Internationally, Kublai maintained relations with the western Mongol khanates (Ilkhanate, Golden Horde) and attracted foreign visitors like Marco Polo. He also faced internal threats, notably from his rival Kaidu and the rebellion of the Mongol prince Nayan in 1287.

### Administration and Culture
As emperor, Kublai patronized arts and sciences, employing Muslim astronomers, mathematicians, and physicians who introduced new knowledge to China. He was religiously tolerant, though he favored Buddhism and suppressed Taoism early in his reign. His court was multicultural, with officials from across Eurasia. However, his later years were marked by personal tragedy, including the deaths of his beloved wife Chabi (1281) and his heir Zhenjin (1286), as well as the failure of his military campaigns. He became depressed and physically ill, dying in 1294.

## Terms
- **Khagan**: The supreme ruler of the Mongol Empire, a title meaning "Khan of Khans."
- **Kurultai**: A traditional Mongol great council of nobles to elect a new leader or decide major policies.
- **Yuan dynasty**: The Mongol-led dynasty of China founded by Kublai Khan in 1271.
- **Appanage**: An estate or territory granted to a member of the ruling family.
- **Jiaochao**: The first unified paper currency issued by Kublai Khan in 1260.
- **Imperial Preceptor**: A high-ranking Buddhist lama (like Drogön Chögyal Phagpa) appointed to oversee religious affairs.
- **Branch Secretariat**: The administrative unit governing a province in the Yuan dynasty.
- **Kheshig**: The Mongol imperial guard.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The legitimacy of Kublai's election as Great Khan in 1260 is debated, as it was contested by his brother Ariq Böke and not supported by all Mongol factions.
- Scholars debate the extent to which Kublai's adoption of Chinese administrative and cultural models was a pragmatic necessity versus a genuine sinicization.
- The economic impact of his paper currency policies is analyzed as both an innovative fiscal tool and a cause of destabilizing inflation.
- The reasons for the repeated failures of his invasions of Japan are attributed to factors like poor ship design, rushed preparation, and weather, but their relative importance is still discussed.

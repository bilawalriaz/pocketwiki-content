# Hans Christian Ørsted

## Overview
Hans Christian Ørsted (1777–1851) was a Danish physicist and chemist whose 1820 discovery that electric currents create magnetic fields founded the field of electromagnetism. This work, known as Oersted's law, was a pivotal step toward unifying the concepts of electricity, magnetism, and energy, and it directly enabled the development of the electric telegraph. He also made significant contributions to chemistry, including the first isolation of metallic aluminium.

## Body

### Early Life and Scientific Formation
Ørsted developed an early interest in science through his father's pharmacy. He and his brother Anders excelled at the University of Copenhagen, where Hans earned his doctorate in 1799. A travel scholarship (1801–1804) exposed him to European science, including the work of Johann Wilhelm Ritter, who believed in a connection between electricity and magnetism. This idea resonated with Ørsted's Kantian belief in the unity of nature and directed his research toward physics. He became a professor in 1806, developing the university's science programs and laboratories.

### Discovery of Electromagnetism
In 1820, Ørsted published his landmark finding: a magnetic compass needle was deflected by an electric current. Contrary to popular myth, this was not an accident but the result of deliberate investigation he had begun around 1818. He initially thought magnetic effects radiated from the wire like light, but further work showed the current produces a circular magnetic field. This discovery, for which he received major awards, proved a direct link between electricity and magnetism. It immediately inspired André-Marie Ampère's mathematical work and suggested the possibility of the electric telegraph, which became a commercial reality nearly two decades later.

### Chemistry and Later Work
In 1825, Ørsted successfully isolated metallic aluminium by reacting aluminium chloride with potassium amalgam, though the resulting metal was impure. He considered the discovery of limited importance, and it was later refined by Friedrich Wöhler. In his later career, Ørsted founded key scientific institutions, including the Society for the Dissemination of Natural Sciences (1824) and the College of Advanced Technology (1829), which became the Technical University of Denmark. He was elected to numerous foreign scientific societies.

## Terms
- **Oersted's Law**: The principle that an electric current creates a magnetic field around it.
- **Voltaic pile**: The first true electric battery, invented by Alessandro Volta in 1800.
- **Kantian thought**: The philosophical framework of Immanuel Kant, emphasizing the unity of nature, which influenced Ørsted's scientific approach.
- **Gedankenexperiment**: A thought experiment, a term Ørsted was the first to explicitly describe and name.
- **Piperine**: A chemical compound extracted from black pepper, which Ørsted first isolated and named in 1819.
- **Piezometer**: An instrument for measuring the compressibility of liquids, which Ørsted designed in 1822.
- **Copley Medal**: A prestigious scientific award from the Royal Society of London, awarded to Ørsted in 1820.
- **Amalgam**: An alloy of mercury with another metal, used by Ørsted in his aluminium isolation process.

## Debates and Open Questions
The source notes that the popular story of Ørsted discovering electromagnetism accidentally during a lecture is a myth; his work was the result of a deliberate, multi-year research program. His initial interpretation of the magnetic effect was incorrect, and his discovery of aluminium was initially overlooked due to its limited publication and his own ambivalence toward its importance.

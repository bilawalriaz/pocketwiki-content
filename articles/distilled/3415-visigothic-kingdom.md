# Visigothic Kingdom

## Overview
The Visigothic Kingdom was a Germanic successor state to the Western Roman Empire, controlling southwestern Gaul and the Iberian Peninsula from the 5th to the early 8th century. Evolving from a Roman-allied (*foederati*) settlement into an independent kingdom centered first in Toulouse and later in Toledo, it preserved Roman law and classical culture, unified Hispania, and its collapse led to the Muslim conquest and the start of the *Reconquista*.

## Timeline
- **418** — Visigoths under King Wallia are settled in Aquitania as Roman *foederati*.
- **466–484** — King Euric expands the kingdom, achieving formal independence by 475.
- **507** — Franks defeat and kill King Alaric II at Vouillé, seizing most of Gaul.
- **549–554** — Civil war invites Byzantine intervention, creating the province of Spania.
- **585** — King Liuvigild conquers the Suebic Kingdom, uniting most of Hispania.
- **589** — King Reccared I converts to Nicene Christianity at the Third Council of Toledo.
- **624** — King Swinthila expels the last Byzantine garrisons from Hispania.
- **654** — King Recceswinth completes the *Liber Iudiciorum* (Visigothic Code).
- **711** — Muslim Berber forces invade; King Roderic is killed at the Battle of Guadalete.
- **718** — Visigothic nobleman Pelagius begins resistance in Asturias, starting the *Reconquista*.

## Body

### Origins and the Federate Kingdom
The Visigoths, a Romanized Germanic people, became allies (*foederati*) of the Western Roman Empire. After sacking Rome in 410, they were granted land in Aquitania in 418 to help restore order against invaders like the Vandals. This settlement, likely under the *hospitalitas* system, began their kingdom, which soon expanded into Roman territory with its capital at Toulouse.

### The Kingdom of Toulouse and Loss of Gaul
Under kings like Theodoric I and Euric, the Visigoths expanded in Gaul and Hispania. Euric's victory at Arles (c. 471) and a 475 treaty established independence. However, conflict with the Franks led to a decisive defeat at Vouillé in 507. King Alaric II was killed, and the Visigoths lost most Gallic lands, retaining only Septimania. This shifted the kingdom's center to Hispania.

### The Arian Kingdom in Hispania
After losing Gaul, internal strife and a 540s civil war invited Byzantine intervention, establishing the province of Spania. King Liuvigild (569–586) consolidated power, reconquered territory, and conquered the Suebic Kingdom in 585, uniting nearly all Hispania. The Visigoths and Hispano-Romans remained distinct largely due to the Visigoths' adherence to Arian Christianity, conflicting with the local Nicene (Catholic) majority.

### The Catholic Kingdom of Toledo and Legal Unification
Liuvigild's son, Reccared I, converted to Nicene Christianity in 589, unifying the ruling elite with the Hispano-Roman population and the Church. The Church's influence grew through the Councils of Toledo, key political institutions. This era saw the compilation of the *Liber Iudiciorum* (Visigothic Code) under Kings Chindaswinth and Recceswinth (completed 654). This code abolished separate laws for Goths and Romans, applying a single legal system based on Roman law to all subjects.

### Decline and Muslim Conquest
The 7th century saw frequent civil wars and regicide. Despite successes like expelling the Byzantines in 624, internal divisions weakened the kingdom. In 711, a Muslim Berber force under Tariq ibn Ziyad invaded. King Roderic was betrayed and killed at Guadalete. The invaders rapidly conquered the peninsula. Visigothic nobles and other Christians retreated to the northern mountains, where Pelagius established the Kingdom of Asturias and began the *Reconquista*.

### Culture and Legacy
The Visigoths preserved classical Roman culture, maintaining urban infrastructure like aqueducts. Their capital, Toledo, and the founded city of Reccopolis showed Byzantine influence. Intellectual life flourished, producing scholars like Isidore of Seville, whose *Etymologies* was a foundational medieval encyclopedia. The Visigothic Code became the basis for Spanish law throughout the Middle Ages.

## Terms
*   ***Foederati***: Barbarian groups granted land within the Roman Empire for military service under a treaty (*foedus*).
*   ***Hospitalitas***: A late Roman system for billeting soldiers or settlers, likely the basis for the Visigoths' initial land grant.
*   **Arianism**: A heretical Christian teaching that Christ was created by and subordinate to God the Father, followed by the Visigoths until 589.
*   **Nicene Christianity**: The orthodox Catholic faith defined by the Councils of Nicaea and Constantinople, which became the Visigothic state religion after 589.
*   ***Liber Iudiciorum***: The "Book of Judgements," the comprehensive Visigothic legal code completed in 654, applying equally to all subjects.
*   **Councils of Toledo**: Ecclesiastical councils that became the primary political and legislative assemblies of the Visigothic Kingdom after its conversion to Catholicism.
*   ***Reconquista***: The centuries-long process by which Christian kingdoms in northern Iberia gradually reconquered the peninsula from Muslim rule, beginning after 718.
*   ***gens Gothorum***: The "Gothic people," reflecting the late Visigothic ethnic fusion of Visigoths and Hispano-Romans into a single political identity.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   The exact nature of the initial 418 Visigothic settlement in Aquitania is debated: whether they received land directly or primarily tax revenues.
*   The role of Count Julian in facilitating the 711 Muslim invasion is considered possibly mythical by some historians.
*   The causes of the rapid Visigothic collapse in 711 are debated, with factors including internal civil wars, aristocratic betrayal, and possible social discontent.

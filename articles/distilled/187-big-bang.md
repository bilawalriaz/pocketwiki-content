# Big Bang

## Overview
The Big Bang is the prevailing cosmological model describing the universe's expansion from an extremely hot, dense state approximately 13.787±0.02 billion years ago. It explains the observed abundance of light elements, the cosmic microwave background (CMB) radiation, galactic redshifts (Hubble's law), and the large-scale structure of the universe. The model incorporates a brief period of cosmic inflation—accelerated expansion in the earliest moments—to resolve the horizon and flatness problems. Current observations indicate the universe's mass-energy density consists of roughly 5% ordinary matter, 27% dark matter, and 68% dark energy, the latter driving an accelerating expansion. While strongly supported by evidence, the model leaves open questions regarding baryon asymmetry, the nature of dark matter and dark energy, and the physics preceding inflation.

## Timeline
- **c. 13.787±0.02 Ga** — Age of the universe; end of inflation / start of hot Big Bang (reheating).
- **< 10⁻³² s** — **Inflation**: Universe doubles in size >80 times; quantum fluctuations seed large-scale structure.
- **~10⁻¹² s** — Electroweak symmetry breaking: electromagnetic and weak forces separate.
- **~10⁻⁶ s** — Quarks and gluons combine into baryons (protons, neutrons).
- **~1 s** — Electron-positron annihilation; neutrinos decouple.
- **~3–20 min** — **Big Bang Nucleosynthesis (BBN)**: Formation of light nuclei (H, He, Li).
- **~50,000 yr** — Matter-radiation equality: matter density overtakes radiation density.
- **~380,000 yr** — **Recombination**: Electrons and nuclei form neutral atoms; universe becomes transparent; CMB photons released.
- **~1 Gyr** — Formation of first stars, galaxies, and quasars.
- **~9 Gyr** — Dark energy begins to dominate; expansion starts accelerating.
- **13.787 Ga** — Present day.

## Body

### Theoretical Foundations and Assumptions
Big Bang models rest on three assumptions: the universality of physical laws, the cosmological principle, and the modeling of matter as a perfect fluid (zero viscosity, pressure proportional to density). The cosmological principle—homogeneity and isotropy on large scales—is confirmed to 10⁻⁵ by CMB temperature uniformity. Combined with general relativity, these assumptions yield the Friedmann–Lemaître–Robertson–Walker (FLRW) metric and Friedmann equations, where the universe's geometry and expansion rate are determined solely by its mass-energy density.

### Mass-Energy Content and Horizons
The current density budget is ~5% luminous matter, ~27% dark matter, and ~68% dark energy. Because the universe has a finite age and light has finite speed, a **particle horizon** limits the most distant observable events. Conversely, accelerating expansion creates a **future horizon**: light emitted today may never reach sufficiently distant objects. Thermalization in the early universe depended on the ratio of particle interaction rates to the Hubble expansion rate; processes faster than expansion reached equilibrium.

### Inflation and the Hot Big Bang
Inflation (c. 10⁻³² s duration) expanded the universe exponentially, solving the horizon, flatness, and magnetic monopole problems. It supercooled the universe, diluting pre-existing particles. **Reheating** then converted the inflaton field's energy into a hot, dense plasma of Standard Model particles (temperature 10¹¹–10²⁹ K), initiating the hot Big Bang. **Baryogenesis** (an unknown reaction violating baryon number conservation) produced a matter-antimatter asymmetry of ~1 part in 30 million.

### Particle Epoch and Nucleosynthesis
As the universe cooled:
*   **10⁻¹² s**: Electroweak symmetry breaking.
*   **10⁻⁶ s**: Quarks confine into protons and neutrons.
*   **1 s**: Electron-positron annihilation leaves photons dominating energy density.
*   **3–20 min (BBN)**: At ~10⁹ K, protons and neutrons fuse into deuterium, helium-4, helium-3, and lithium-7. Predicted ratios (by mass: ⁴He/H ≈ 0.25, ²H/H ≈ 10⁻³, ⁷Li/H ≈ 10⁻⁹) match observations well, except for a factor-of-two discrepancy in lithium-7 (the "cosmological lithium problem").

### Recombination, Structure Formation, and Acceleration
At ~380,000 years (~3000 K), electrons combined with nuclei (**recombination**), releasing the CMB. Slight density fluctuations—seeded by quantum fluctuations during inflation—grew via gravitational collapse, aided by cold dark matter (CDM), forming stars, galaxies, and clusters. The **Lambda-CDM (ΛCDM)** model fits observations with ~23% CDM and ~4.6% baryonic matter. Observations of Type Ia supernovae (1998) and CMB data reveal the expansion is accelerating, attributed to dark energy (cosmological constant or quintessence), which now constitutes ~73% of energy density.

### Concept History
*   **1912**: Vesto Slipher measures galactic redshifts.
*   **1922**: Alexander Friedmann derives expanding-universe solutions to Einstein's equations.
*   **1927**: Georges Lemaître independently derives Friedmann equations; proposes expansion causes redshifts.
*   **1929**: Edwin Hubble publishes distance-redshift relation (Hubble's law).
*   **1931**: Lemaître proposes the "primeval atom" (Big Bang origin).
*   **1948–49**: George Gamow, Ralph Alpher, and Robert Herman develop BBN and predict CMB; Fred Hoyle coins "Big Bang" (1949 BBC broadcast) contrasting it with his steady-state model.
*   **1964**: Arno Penzias and Robert Wilson discover CMB, decisively favoring Big Bang over steady-state.
*   **1981**: Alan Guth proposes inflation to solve horizon/flatness problems.
*   **1990s–present**: COBE, WMAP, Planck, and supernova surveys precision-measure parameters and confirm acceleration.

### Observational Evidence ("Four Pillars")
1.  **Hubble's Law**: Recessional velocity *v* = *H₀D* (*H₀* ≈ 70.4 km/s/Mpc per WMAP). Uniform expansion predicted by Friedmann/Lemaître before observation. Current **Hubble tension**: CMB-inferred *H₀* is lower than distance-ladder measurements.
2.  **CMB**: Discovered 1964; near-perfect blackbody (2.7255 K). COBE (1990) confirmed spectrum to 1 part in 10⁴; COBE (1992) and WMAP/Planck detected anisotropies (~1 part in 10⁵) matching inflationary predictions and constraining geometry (spatially flat).
3.  **Primordial Abundances**: BBN predictions for light elements match observations from metal-poor gas clouds; 2011 discovery of pristine gas clouds with no heavy elements supports BBN origin.
4.  **Structure Formation**: Galaxy/quasar distributions and evolution match ΛCDM simulations; distant galaxies appear younger and structurally distinct, contradicting steady-state.

### Problems and Open Issues
*   **Baryon Asymmetry**: Observed matter dominance requires baryogenesis satisfying Sakharov conditions (baryon number violation, C/CP violation, departure from equilibrium). Standard Model effects are insufficient.
*   **Dark Energy**: ~68% of density; negative pressure drives acceleration. Nature unknown; cosmological constant faces a 120-order-of-magnitude discrepancy with quantum field theory predictions ("worst prediction in physics").
*   **Dark Matter**: ~27% of density; inferred from galaxy rotation curves, cluster dynamics, CMB anisotropies, and lensing. No lab detection yet. CDM faces **cuspy halo** (density profiles) and **dwarf galaxy** (missing satellites) problems.
*   **Horizon/Flatness/Monopole Problems**: Resolved by inflation, but inflation's mechanism, duration, and initial conditions remain unconstrained; some founders argue it is untestable or disfavored.
*   **Pre-Inflation / Singularity**: Classical GR breaks down at Planck scale (10⁻⁴³ s, 10³² K). Quantum gravity required; proposals include Hartle-Hawking no-boundary, string theory branes, eternal inflation, and emergent time.

### Misconceptions
1.  **Origin of the universe**: The model describes evolution *from* a hot, dense state, not the absolute origin; inflation precedes the hot Big Bang.
2.  **Explosion into space**: Expansion is intrinsic metric expansion (FLRW), not motion through a pre-existing void; "Everywhere Stretch" is a better analogy.
3.  **Superluminal recession**: Galaxies beyond the Hubble sphere recede faster than *c*; this is permitted in GR as it is not a local velocity.

### Implications
**Ultimate Fate**: ΛCDM with cosmological constant predicts eternal acceleration: gravitationally bound systems (galaxies) remain intact but suffer heat death (entropy maximum). Phantom dark energy models allow a **Big Rip** tearing all structures apart. **Pre-inflationary cosmology** is speculative; proposals include quantum fluctuations, emergent universe, cyclic/brane models, and multiverse scenarios. The Big Bang singularity likely marks the limit of classical GR, not necessarily the beginning of time.

## Terms
- ****Cosmological Principle**** — The assumption that on sufficiently large scales, the universe is homogeneous (same everywhere) and isotropic (same in all directions).
- ****FLRW Metric**** — The exact solution of Einstein's field equations describing a homogeneous, isotropic expanding (or contracting) universe.
- ****Reheating**** — The process after inflation where the energy of the inflaton field decays into a hot, thermal plasma of Standard Model particles, initiating the hot Big Bang.
- ****Baryogenesis**** — A hypothetical physical process in the early universe that generated an asymmetry between baryons and antibaryons, explaining the dominance of matter.
- ****Big Bang Nucleosynthesis (BBN)**** — The epoch (~3–20 minutes post-Big Bang) when protons and neutrons fused to form light nuclei (H, He, Li); a key pillar of evidence.
- ****Recombination**** — The epoch (~380,000 years) when electrons combined with nuclei to form neutral atoms, making the universe transparent and releasing the CMB.
- ****Lambda-CDM (ΛCDM)**** — The standard cosmological model: a flat universe with cold dark matter (CDM) and a cosmological constant (Λ, dark energy).
- ****Dark Energy**** — A component with large negative pressure (~68% of density) causing the accelerated expansion of the universe; nature unknown.
- ****Particle Horizon**** — The maximum distance from which light could have reached us since the beginning of the universe; defines the observable universe.
- ****Hubble Tension**** — A statistically significant discrepancy between the Hubble constant (*H₀*) measured from the CMB (early universe) and from the cosmic distance ladder (late universe).

## Debates and open questions
*   **Nature of Dark Energy**: Is it a cosmological constant (vacuum energy), a dynamic scalar field (quintessence), or a modification of gravity? The 120-order magnitude discrepancy between theory and observation remains unresolved.
*   **Identity of Dark Matter**: Is it a new particle (WIMP, axion, sterile neutrino) or do observations require modified gravity (MOND)? Direct detection experiments have not yet succeeded.
*   **Baryon Asymmetry Mechanism**: What specific beyond-Standard-Model physics satisfies the Sakharov conditions to produce the observed matter-antimatter imbalance?
*   **Inflationary Mechanism**: What is the inflaton field? Is inflation eternal? Can it be falsified? The "multiverse" prediction is debated as unscientific.
*   **Hubble Tension**: Is the discrepancy due to systematic errors in distance-ladder or CMB analyses, or does it require new physics (e.g., early dark energy, neutrino properties)?
*   **Cosmological Lithium Problem**: Why does observed ⁷Li abundance fall a factor of ~2 below BBN predictions? Nuclear physics, stellar depletion, or new physics?
*   **Quantum Gravity / Initial Singularity**: Does a theory of quantum gravity (string theory, loop quantum gravity) resolve the singularity? Did time begin at the Big Bang, or is it emergent?
*   **Pre-Inflationary Cosmogony**: What, if anything, preceded inflation? Are multiverse, cyclic, or no-boundary proposals testable?

# Montreal

## Overview
Montreal is the largest city in Quebec and the second-largest in Canada, founded in 1642 as a French colonial mission. It is a major global hub for culture, commerce, technology, and education, distinguished as the largest primarily French-speaking city in the Americas. Its history reflects a blend of Indigenous, French, and British influences, evolving from a fur-trading post to a modern metropolis.

## Timeline
- **c. 14th century** — Saint Lawrence Iroquoians establish the village of Hochelaga at the foot of Mount Royal.
- **1642** — French colonists found Ville-Marie (Montreal) as a Roman Catholic mission.
- **1760** — Montreal falls to the British during the Seven Years' War.
- **1832** — Montreal is incorporated as a city.
- **1844–1849** — Montreal serves as the capital of the Province of Canada.
- **1967** — Hosts the International and Universal Exposition (Expo 67).
- **1976** — Hosts the Summer Olympic Games, the only Canadian city to do so.
- **2002** — Montreal merges with 27 surrounding municipalities on the Island of Montreal.
- **2006** — Named a UNESCO City of Design.
- **2023** — First branch of the Réseau express métropolitain (REM) automated transit network opens.

## Body

### Etymology and History
The land is known as Mooniyaang in Ojibwe and Tiohtià:ke in Mohawk. French settlers named their 1642 settlement Ville-Marie. The name "Montréal" is generally derived from Mount Royal, the triple-peaked hill at the city's center. Archaeological evidence shows Indigenous occupation of the island for at least 4,000 years. The French colony grew as a fur-trade center but faced Iroquois raids. It was ceded to Britain in 1760. Montreal was briefly occupied by American forces during the Revolution (1775–1776) and served as Canada's capital from 1844 to 1849. Its economic dominance peaked in the 19th century, but Toronto surpassed it in the 1970s. The 1976 Olympics brought prestige but also significant debt. A major municipal merger in 2002 was partially reversed in 2006.

### Geography and Climate
Montreal is centered on the Island of Montreal at the confluence of the Saint Lawrence and Ottawa Rivers. The city is named after Mount Royal (232 m / 761 ft). It has a humid continental climate with hot, humid summers and cold, snowy winters. The lowest recorded temperature was −37.8 °C (−36 °F) in 1957, and the highest was 37.6 °C (99.7 °F) in 1975.

### Demographics
As of the 2021 census, the city's population was 1,762,949, with a metropolitan population of 4,291,732. French is the official language; 85.7% of city residents were fluent in French. The city is highly bilingual, with 58.5% able to speak both French and English. Visible minorities comprised 38.8% of the population. The largest immigrant-origin groups are from Haiti, Algeria, and France. The population is predominantly Christian (49.5%), with Islam (12.7%) as the largest non-Christian religion.

### Economy
Montreal has the second-largest economy in Canada by GDP. Key industries include aerospace, technology, pharmaceuticals, finance, and culture. It is a global hub for artificial intelligence research and video game development. The Port of Montreal is one of the world's largest inland ports. The city hosts the headquarters of numerous international organizations, including the International Civil Aviation Organization (ICAO).

### Culture and Education
Montreal is a major cultural center, hosting over 100 annual festivals, including the world's largest jazz and comedy festivals. It is renowned for its churches, with an estimated 650 on the island. The city has the highest concentration of post-secondary students in North America. Major universities include McGill University (English) and Université de Montréal (French).

### Transportation
Public transit is operated by the Société de transport de Montréal (STM), which runs the Metro subway system (inaugurated 1966) and an extensive bus network. The city is served by Pierre Elliott Trudeau International Airport. Major rail lines connect Montreal to other Canadian cities and the U.S. The Réseau express métropolitain (REM), a new automated light rail system, began opening in 2023. Montreal is also known for its successful BIXI bike-share program.

## Terms
- **CEGEP**: A publicly funded college in Quebec providing pre-university and technical programs between high school and university.
- **Expo 67**: The 1967 International and Universal Exposition, a major world's fair held in Montreal.
- **Golden Square Mile**: A historic neighbourhood where Montreal's business elite built their homes in the late 19th century.
- **Hochelaga**: A pre-contact Saint Lawrence Iroquoian village located at the foot of Mount Royal.
- **Mount Royal**: The triple-peaked hill in the center of the island, for which the city is named.
- **RÉSO**: The official name for Montreal's extensive underground pedestrian network connecting buildings and transit.
- **Seigneurial title**: A form of land ownership under the French regime in New France.
- **Ville-Marie**: The original name of the settlement founded in 1642, now a borough of Montreal.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The exact origin of the name "Montréal" is debated. While generally derived from "Mount Royal," one theory suggests an Italian translation via a 1556 map, a claim disputed by the Commission de toponymie du Québec.
- The reasons for the disappearance of the Saint Lawrence Iroquoians from the St. Lawrence valley before 1603 are not definitively known, with theories including outmigration, epidemics, or intertribal wars.
- The 2002 municipal merger was highly controversial, particularly in English-speaking suburbs, leading to partial demergers in 2006.

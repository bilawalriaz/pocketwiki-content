# Peru

## Overview
Peru is a megadiverse country in western South America with one of the longest histories of civilization in the Americas, tracing its heritage back to the Caral–Supe civilization (c. 3000 BCE). It was home to the Inca Empire, the largest state in the pre-Columbian Americas, before being conquered by the Spanish in the 16th century. After independence in 1821, Peru experienced political instability, economic booms and busts, and internal conflict, shaping its modern identity as a representative democratic republic with a mixed economy and rich cultural heritage.

## Timeline
- **c. 3000–1800 BCE** — Caral–Supe civilization flourishes, the earliest known civilization in the Americas.
- **15th century** — The Inca Empire expands rapidly under Pachacuti, becoming the largest pre-Columbian state.
- **1532** — Spanish conquistador Francisco Pizarro captures the Inca emperor Atahualpa at Cajamarca.
- **1571** — The Spanish suppress the last Inca resistance at Vilcabamba.
- **1821** — José de San Martín declares Peruvian independence in Lima.
- **1824** — The Battle of Ayacucho secures Peru's independence from Spain.
- **1879–1884** — The War of the Pacific results in Peru losing territory to Chile.
- **1968** — General Juan Velasco Alvarado leads a military coup, initiating radical reforms.
- **1990** — Alberto Fujimori is elected president, implementing neoliberal economic policies.
- **2000** — Fujimori resigns amid corruption scandals and flees the country.
- **2022** — President Pedro Castillo is removed from office by Congress after attempting to dissolve it.
- **2026** — Keiko Fujimori is sworn in as president after a closely contested election.

## Body

### Pre-Columbian and Colonial History
Peruvian territory shows evidence of human presence from c. 12,500 BCE. Complex societies developed, including the Caral–Supe, Chavín, Nazca, Moche, and Chimú cultures. The Inca Empire, originating from Cusco, expanded from 1438 to 1533 to control a vast Andean region. The Spanish conquest began in 1532 with the capture of Atahualpa. The Viceroyalty of Peru was established with its capital in Lima, becoming a major source of silver and gold for the Spanish Crown. Indigenous populations collapsed due to disease and exploitation, while African slaves were brought in as labor. The Bourbon Reforms in the 18th century increased taxes and partitioned the viceroyalty, provoking major indigenous rebellions like that of Túpac Amaru II (1780–1783).

### Independence and 19th Century
Independence was proclaimed in 1821 by José de San Martín and achieved militarily by Simón Bolívar, culminating in the decisive Battle of Ayacucho in 1824. The early republic suffered political instability. A period of stability from the 1840s to 1860s was fueled by guano exports. The War of the Pacific (1879–1884) with Chile, triggered by a dispute over nitrate-rich territories, ended in defeat and the loss of the Tarapacá region.

### 20th Century to Present
The 20th century was marked by political turmoil, including the authoritarian regime of Augusto B. Leguía and the rise of the APRA party. A military government under Juan Velasco Alvarado (1968–1975) enacted major agrarian and economic reforms. The 1980s and 1990s saw severe economic crisis and an internal conflict between the state and guerrilla groups like Sendero Luminoso (Shining Path). Alberto Fujimori's presidency (1990–2000) implemented neoliberal reforms but was also marked by human rights abuses and corruption. The 21st century has been characterized by economic growth, persistent corruption scandals leading to the removal of multiple presidents, and social unrest, including major protests in 2022–2023 following the ousting of President Pedro Castillo.

### Government and Economy
Peru is a unitary semi-presidential republic with a bicameral Congress. Its 1993 constitution grants significant power to the president, though Congress can impeach without cause, leading to frequent political crises. The economy is the 47th largest globally, driven by mining (especially copper, gold, and silver), agriculture, and fishing. Despite growth, inequality and poverty persist, with a large informal sector. Peru is a member of trade blocs like the Pacific Alliance and APEC.

### Society and Culture
Peru's population is multiethnic, with Mestizos (60.2%) and Quechua (22.3%) as the largest groups. Spanish is the main language, with Quechua and Aymara also official in regions where they predominate. Roman Catholicism is the dominant religion, often syncretized with indigenous traditions. Peruvian culture blends indigenous, Spanish, African, and Asian influences, evident in its cuisine, music, and arts. Lima is a global culinary capital, and the country's gastronomy is internationally renowned.

## Terms
- **Mestizo**: A person of combined European and Indigenous American ancestry.
- **Quechua**: An Indigenous language family spoken by millions in the Andes, once the official language of the Inca Empire.
- **Viceroyalty**: A territory administered by a viceroy, a representative of the monarch, in the Spanish Empire.
- **Guano**: Accumulated excrement of seabirds or bats, used as a high-nitrate fertilizer; a major 19th-century Peruvian export.
- **Sendero Luminoso (Shining Path)**: A Maoist guerrilla group that fought the Peruvian state from the 1980s to the early 2000s.
- **Fujimorism**: The political ideology and legacy of Alberto Fujimori, characterized by neoliberal economics and authoritarian governance.
- **Indigenismo**: An early 20th-century intellectual and artistic movement that emphasized Indigenous cultures and themes.
- **Syncretism**: The amalgamation of different religions, cultures, or schools of thought.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The extent to which the Spanish conquest, rather than other factors, caused the catastrophic decline of the indigenous population.
- The long-term impact of Fujimori's neoliberal reforms and authoritarian methods on Peru's democratic institutions and social fabric.
- The causes of Peru's persistent political instability, including the role of the 1993 constitution in enabling frequent presidential removals.
- The effectiveness of Peru's economic model in reducing poverty and inequality despite periods of strong growth.

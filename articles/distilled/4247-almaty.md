# Almaty

## Overview
Almaty is Kazakhstan's largest and most populous city, a major cultural and financial center in Central Asia. Historically the nation's capital from 1929 to 1997, it remains a vital economic hub, generating about 20% of the country's GDP. Its location in the foothills of the Trans-Ili Alatau mountains defines its geography, climate, and seismic risk.

## Timeline
- **c. 1000–900 BC** — First farmers and cattle-breeders establish settlements in the Bronze Age.
- **700 BC – early AD** — Saka and later Wusun tribes inhabit the region; it becomes an early education center.
- **1218** — Region invaded by the Mongol Empire.
- **1854** — Russia builds Fort Zailiyskoe (renamed Fort Vernoe/Verny).
- **1887** — A major earthquake nearly destroys Verny.
- **1921** — Verny is renamed Alma-Ata.
- **1927** — Capital of the Kazakh ASSR transferred from Kyzyl-Orda to Alma-Ata.
- **1930** — Turkestan–Siberia Railway completed, boosting the city's growth.
- **1991** — Kazakhstan declares independence; Alma-Ata is the capital.
- **1993** — City renamed from Alma-Ata to Almaty.
- **1997** — Capital status transferred to Akmola (now Astana).
- **2011** — Almaty Metro opens; city co-hosts the Asian Winter Games.

## Body

### Status and Etymology
Almaty was the capital of the Kazakh SSR and independent Kazakhstan until 1997. It is now a city of republican significance, often called the "southern capital." The name derives from the medieval settlement *Almatau* ("Apple Mountain"). A disputed theory links it to the Kazakh word for "apple" (*алма*), translating as "full of apples." The region is considered a possible ancestral home of the modern domestic apple (*Malus sieversii*).

### History
The area has been inhabited since the Bronze Age. It was ruled by various entities, including the Qara Khitai, Mongols, and Kazakh Khanate, before being absorbed by the Russian Empire in the 1850s. Russia founded Fort Verny in 1854, which was renamed Alma-Ata in 1921 after becoming the capital of the Kazakh ASSR in 1927. The city grew rapidly due to the railway and administrative functions. During WWII, it received evacuated industries and people from European USSR. After independence in 1991, the capital moved to Astana in 1997, but Almaty remains the economic and cultural heart.

### Geography and Climate
Located in southeastern Kazakhstan, Almaty sits at 700–900 meters elevation in the foothills of the Trans-Ili Alatau mountains. It has a humid continental climate (Köppen Dfa) with hot summers and cold winters. Annual precipitation is 650–700 mm, with spring being the wettest season. The city is in a seismically active zone, having suffered destructive earthquakes in 1887, 1889, and 1911.

### Economy and Infrastructure
Almaty generates ~20% of Kazakhstan's GDP and is a regional financial center, hosting the Kazakhstan Stock Exchange and major banks. The Almaty Metro opened in 2011. The city is served by Almaty International Airport and is a hub on the Western Europe–Western China Highway. Air pollution is a significant problem, with cars identified as a major source.

### Culture and Landmarks
Almaty is considered Kazakhstan's cultural capital, with a European character. Key landmarks include the earthquake-resistant Ascension Cathedral (1907), the Green Bazaar, and the pedestrian Arbat area. The Medeu skating rink (1,691 m altitude) and Shymbulak ski resort are major sports venues. The city hosted the 1978 Primary Health Care conference (Alma Ata Declaration), the 2011 Asian Winter Games, and the 2017 Winter Universiade.

## Terms
- **Kazakh ASSR/SSR**: The Kazakh Autonomous Soviet Socialist Republic and later Kazakh Soviet Socialist Republic, administrative divisions of the USSR.
- **Turkestan–Siberia Railway**: A crucial railway completed in 1930 that spurred Alma-Ata's growth.
- **Alma Ata Declaration**: A 1978 WHO/UNICEF declaration from a conference in the city, marking a paradigm shift in global public health.
- **Malus sieversii**: A wild apple species from the Almaty region, a candidate for the ancestor of the modern domestic apple.
- **Saka**: An ancient Iranian nomadic people who inhabited the region from c. 700 BC.
- **Dzungars**: A group of Oirat Mongols who invaded and dominated the Kazakh people in the 18th century.
- **Jeltoqsan riot**: A 1986 protest in Alma-Ata against the dismissal of a Kazakh leader by Soviet General Secretary Gorbachev.
- **Gamma+ global city**: A city classification indicating significant regional influence, as assigned by the Globalization and World Cities Research Network.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The etymology of "Almaty" is disputed, with theories linking it to "apple" or a Turkic word for "father."
- The region west of the Tian Shan mountains is thought to be the apple's ancestral home, with *Malus sieversii* as a candidate ancestor, but this is presented as a scientific hypothesis.
- The city's severe air pollution is a major ongoing issue, with morbidity for certain diseases noted as high despite a lack of major industrial installations.

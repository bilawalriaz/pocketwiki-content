# Edward Jenner

## Overview
Edward Jenner (1749–1823) was an English physician and scientist who pioneered the concept of vaccination by developing the world's first vaccine against smallpox. His work laid the foundation for modern immunology and is credited with saving more lives than any other medical intervention, ultimately leading to the global eradication of smallpox.

## Timeline
- **17 May 1749** — Born in Berkeley, Gloucestershire, England.
- **1770** — Began apprenticeship under surgeon John Hunter in London.
- **1788** — Elected Fellow of the Royal Society for his study of cuckoo brood parasitism.
- **1792** — Obtained MD from the University of St Andrews.
- **14 May 1796** — Conducted his first vaccination experiment, inoculating James Phipps with cowpox.
- **1798** — Published *An Inquiry into the Causes and Effects of the Variolæ Vaccinæ*, describing the smallpox vaccine.
- **1802** — Granted £10,000 by Parliament for his work on vaccination.
- **1803** — The Spanish Balmis Expedition used his vaccine globally.
- **1808** — The National Vaccine Establishment was founded with government aid.
- **1821** — Appointed physician to King George IV and mayor of Berkeley.
- **26 January 1823** — Died in Berkeley, Gloucestershire.
- **1980** — WHO declared smallpox eradicated, a result of global vaccination efforts.

## Body

### Early Life and Education
Jenner received a basic education before being apprenticed at age 13 to a surgeon. In 1770, he began a formal apprenticeship in surgery and anatomy under John Hunter in London, who influenced him with the advice "Don't think; try." After returning to Berkeley, he became a successful country doctor and surgeon. He earned his MD in 1792 and was a member of local medical societies.

### Zoological Work
Before his vaccine work, Jenner was a respected naturalist. In 1788, he was elected to the Royal Society for his study of the cuckoo. He demonstrated that the newly hatched cuckoo, not the adult, pushes the host's eggs and chicks from the nest, using a temporary depression in its back. This work showed his skill in observation and experiment, which later informed his medical research.

### Development of the Smallpox Vaccine
In Jenner's time, smallpox was a major killer, and the risky practice of variolation (inoculation with smallpox material) was used for immunity. By 1768, John Fewster had noted that cowpox infection seemed to protect against smallpox. Others, including Benjamin Jesty in 1774, had tested cowpox inoculation. Jenner's unique contribution was to rigorously prove immunity through subsequent challenges and show that protective material could be passed from person to person. On 14 May 1796, he inoculated James Phipps with pus from a milkmaid's cowpox blister. Phipps later proved immune to smallpox. Jenner published his findings in 1798, coining the terms "vaccine" and "vaccination" from *Variolae vaccinae* (cowpox). Despite initial skepticism, vaccination was accepted, and variolation was banned in Britain in 1840.

### Later Life and Legacy
Jenner's work brought him international fame and financial support from Parliament. He became president of the Jennerian Society (1803) to promote vaccination and was appointed physician to King George IV in 1821. He continued to observe that vaccination reduced smallpox severity. His vaccine enabled the global eradication of smallpox, declared by the WHO in 1980. He is hailed as "the father of immunology."

## Terms
- **Variolation**: The older, riskier practice of inoculating a person with material from a smallpox blister to induce immunity.
- **Vaccination**: The process of inoculating with a vaccine to produce immunity against a disease. The term was coined by Jenner from *Variolae vaccinae* (cowpox).
- **Cowpox**: A mild disease in cattle caused by a virus similar to the smallpox virus. Infection with cowpox provided cross-immunity to smallpox.
- **Smallpox**: A severe, often fatal infectious disease caused by the variola virus, eradicated globally by 1980.
- **Immunology**: The branch of medicine and biology concerned with immunity. Jenner is considered its pioneer.
- **Brood parasitism**: A reproductive strategy where an animal (like the cuckoo) relies on other species to raise its young.
- **Variolous material**: Infectious matter taken from a smallpox pustule, used in variolation.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The "milkmaid story"—that Jenner's idea came solely from observing unblemished milkmaid complexions—is widely repeated but appears to be a myth; his work built on prior observations by others like Fewster and Jesty.
- While Jenner's conclusions were partly correct and partly erroneous, the core principle of vaccination was sound and became widely accepted despite initial deliberation by the medical establishment.

# Veracruz (city)

## Overview
Veracruz is Mexico's oldest, largest, and historically most significant port city on the Gulf of Mexico. Founded by Hernán Cortés in 1519, it served as the primary gateway for colonial trade and immigration, shaping the nation's economic and cultural landscape. Its strategic importance made it a frequent target for pirates and foreign invasions, earning it the title "Heroic City" multiple times.

## Body

### Colonial Foundation and Wealth
The Spanish captain Juan de Grijalva first arrived at the island of San Juan de Ulúa in 1518. Hernán Cortés founded the mainland settlement in 1519, naming it Villa Rica de la Vera Cruz ("Rich Village of the True Cross") for its gold and the day of his landing. The city became the most important port in New Spain, with a wealthy merchant class that was at times more prosperous than that of Mexico City. Its wealth attracted pirate raids, leading to the construction of Fort San Juan de Ulúa. The port was the central hub for the Spanish treasure fleet, exporting gold and silver while importing goods and, via the Portuguese, enslaved Africans.

### Conflicts and the "Heroic City" Title
Veracruz's strategic value led to repeated foreign attacks. It successfully defended against a French attack in the 1837 Pastry War, earning its second "Heroic City" title. It was invaded by the United States in 1847 and occupied by French forces in 1864 to support Emperor Maximilian I. Following the 1914 Tampico Affair, U.S. troops occupied the port for seven months, prompting the city's fourth "Heroic City" designation.

### Modern Port and Economy
After a period of decline, the port was modernized in the early 20th century. By the late 20th century, petroleum production was the state's primary economic driver, but the port has since re-emerged as the main economic engine. Federal intervention in 1991 led to automation and the creation of a new port management company, Empresa de Servicios Portuarios de Veracruz, by 1991. Today, it is Mexico's principal port for imports and exports, especially for the automotive industry, handling 700,000 to 800,000 vehicles per year.

### Culture and Society
Veracruz is a cultural melting pot of indigenous, Spanish, and Afro-Caribbean influences, visible in its food and music. The annual Veracruz Carnaval, celebrated since 1866, is the largest in Mexico. Traditional music, Son Jarocho, features African and Caribbean rhythms and includes the song "La Bamba." Cuisine blends indigenous staples like corn and chili peppers with Spanish ingredients like olive oil and capers, exemplified by the signature dish Huachinango a la Veracruzana (red snapper Veracruz-style).

## Terms
*   **Son Jarocho:** Traditional Veracruzian music with African and Caribbean influences.
*   **Huachinango a la Veracruzana:** A signature dish of red snapper prepared with tomatoes, chili peppers, olives, and capers.
*   **Malecón:** A boardwalk stretching along the Gulf of Mexico, popular for evening strolls.
*   **Zócalo:** The main public square, officially Plaza de Armas, a center of social activity.
*   **Danzón:** A social dance of Cuban origin, popular in Veracruz since the 1870s.
*   **Spanish Treasure Fleet:** The convoy system used by Spain to transport precious metals and goods across the Atlantic.
*   **Pastry War:** An 1838 conflict between France and Mexico, partly triggered by claims of French citizens' property damage.
*   **Tampico Affair:** A 1914 incident between U.S. and Mexican forces that precipitated the U.S. occupation of Veracruz.

## Debates and Open Questions
The article notes a scholarly debate regarding the origin of the name for the island of San Juan de Ulúa, stating it is derived from the local name for the Aztecs, "coluha or acolhua," which evolved into "Ulúa." The fate of the Isla de Sacrificios has also been a subject of ongoing controversy among authorities, tour operators, and fishermen since its closure to the public following a 1983 cleanup project.

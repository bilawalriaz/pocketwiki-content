# Theodor Mommsen

## Overview

Theodor Mommsen (1817–1903) was a German classical scholar, historian, jurist, journalist, politician, and archaeologist, widely regarded as one of the greatest classicists of the 19th century. He received the 1902 Nobel Prize in Literature for his historical writings, including *The History of Rome*, and his works on Roman law significantly impacted the German civil code. Beyond scholarship, he was a prominent liberal politician and a fierce opponent of antisemitism, while also holding militant German nationalist views toward Slavic peoples.

## Body

### Early Life and Education

Mommsen was born to German parents in Garding in the Duchy of Schleswig in 1817, then ruled by the king of Denmark, and grew up in Bad Oldesloe in Holstein, where his father was a Lutheran minister. His parents were originally from Friesland. He studied mostly at home, though he attended the Gymnasium Christianeum in Altona for four years, receiving his diploma in 1837. Unable to afford study at Göttingen, he enrolled at the University of Kiel, where he studied jurisprudence from 1838 to 1843. During this time he roomed with Theodor Storm, later a renowned poet; together with Mommsen's brother Tycho, the three published a poetry collection (*Liederbuch dreier Freunde*).

Based on his research into Roman law, Mommsen devoted himself to Ancient History, then just emerging as a separate discipline. A royal Danish grant enabled him to visit France and Italy to study preserved classical Roman inscriptions. During the 1848 revolution he worked as a war correspondent in then-Danish Rendsburg, supporting German annexation of Schleswig-Holstein and constitutional reform. Forced to leave by the Danes, he became a professor of law at Leipzig that same year, but had to resign in 1851 after protesting the new Saxon constitution. He then held professorships in Zurich and Breslau before becoming a research professor at the Berlin Academy of Sciences in 1857.

### Academic Career and Recognition

In 1858 Mommsen was appointed to the Academy of Sciences in Berlin and became professor of Roman History at the University of Berlin in 1861, lecturing until 1887. He received numerous honors: foreign membership in the Royal Netherlands Academy of Arts and Sciences (1859), the Prussian medal Pour le Mérite (1868), honorary citizenship of Rome, election to the American Antiquarian Society (1870) and the American Philosophical Society (1873), and the 1902 Nobel Prize in Literature—one of the very few non-fiction writers to receive it.

In 1880, a fire broke out in his workroom-library; after being burned while saving papers, he was restrained from re-entering. Several old manuscripts were destroyed, including one on loan from Trinity College, Cambridge, an important Jordanes manuscript from Heidelberg, and two others from Brussels and Halle. Mommsen had sixteen children with his wife Marie, daughter of publisher Karl Reimer. Their daughter Maria married the great Classics scholar Ulrich von Wilamowitz-Moellendorff; grandson Theodor Ernst Mommsen became a medieval history professor in the United States, and great-grandsons Hans and Wolfgang Mommsen were German historians.

### Editor and Organizer

As secretary of the Historical-Philological Class at the Berlin Academy (1874–1895), Mommsen organized countless scientific projects, mostly editions of original sources. His most significant undertaking was the *Corpus Inscriptionum Latinarum*, a collection of all known ancient Latin inscriptions. Inspired early in his career when publishing inscriptions of the Neapolitan Kingdom (1852), and trained by Bartolomeo Borghesi of San Marino, the complete corpus comprised seventeen volumes, the latest published in 1986. Fifteen appeared in Mommsen's lifetime, five prepared by himself. Its basic principle was the method of autopsy: all modern transcriptions were checked against the original inscriptions, contrary to previous collections.

He also published fundamental collections in Roman law, including the *Corpus Iuris Civilis* and the *Codex Theodosianus*, and played an important role in the *Monumenta Germaniae Historica*, editions of Church Fathers' texts, and *limes romanus* (Roman frontiers) research.

### Political Career

Mommsen served as a delegate to the Prussian House of Representatives (1863–1866, 1873–1879) and the Reichstag (1881–1884), moving from the liberal German Progress Party to the National Liberal Party and finally the Secessionists. Though he supported German Unification, he was disappointed with the German Empire's politics and pessimistic about its future. He strongly disagreed with Otto von Bismarck's social policies in 1881, advising collaboration between Liberals and Social Democrats and narrowly avoiding prosecution for his strong language.

As a liberal nationalist, Mommsen favored assimilation of ethnic minorities into German society. In 1879, when Heinrich von Treitschke began a political campaign against Jews (the Berliner Antisemitismusstreit), Mommsen strongly opposed antisemitism, writing a harsh pamphlet denouncing Treitschke's views. He suggested Jews could follow the example of peoples of Schleswig-Holstein and Hanover, who gave up special customs when integrating into Prussia. In 1890 he co-founded the Association for Combating Antisemitism (Abwehrverein). However, he was also a vehement German nationalist with militant attitudes toward Slavic nations, calling Czechs "apostles of barbarism" in an 1897 letter and writing that "the Czech skull is impervious to reason, but it is susceptible to blows."

### Influence and Legacy

Mommsen published over 1,500 works and established a new framework for systematic study of Roman history, pioneering epigraphy (the study of inscriptions on material artifacts). His unfinished *History of Rome* (three volumes, 1854–1856) has long been considered his main work, though the *Corpus Inscriptionum Latinarum* may be most relevant today. His *Roman Constitutional Law* (1871–1888) was important for ancient history research. *The Provinces of the Roman Empire from Caesar to Diocletian* (1885) appeared as Volume 5 of his history; the missing fourth volume on imperial Rome was reconstructed from student lecture notes and published in German (1992) and English (1996).

Fellow Nobel Laureate Bernard Shaw cited Mommsen's interpretation of Julius Caesar as an inspiration for his play *Caesar and Cleopatra*. Naval historian Alfred Thayer Mahan formulated the thesis for *The Influence of Sea Power Upon History* while reading Mommsen's *History of Rome*. A Gymnasium (academic high school) in Bad Oldesloe bears his name, and his birthplace Garding styles itself "Mommsen-Stadt Garding."

## Terms

- **Epigraphy**: The study of inscriptions on material artifacts, which Mommsen pioneered.
- **Corpus Inscriptionum Latinarum**: A comprehensive collection of ancient Latin inscriptions, Mommsen's major editorial project.
- **Method of autopsy**: The editorial principle requiring all transcriptions of inscriptions to be checked against the originals.
- **Corpus Iuris Civilis**: A fundamental collection of Roman law edited by Mommsen.
- **Codex Theodosianus**: A collection of Roman law compiled under Emperor Theodosius, edited by Mommsen.
- **Limes Romanus**: The Roman frontier fortifications, subject of research Mommsen organized.
- **Berliner Antisemitismusstreit**: The 1879 political campaign against Jews by Heinrich von Treitschke, which Mommsen opposed.
- **Abwehrverein**: The Association for Combating Antisemitism, co-founded by Mommsen in 1890.
- **Pour le Mérite**: A Prussian medal awarded to Mommsen in 1868.

## Debates and Open Questions

The source notes that Mommsen never wrote the intended fourth volume of his *History of Rome*, meant to be a general account of imperial Rome; a makeshift reconstruction from student lecture notes was published posthumously. His militant German nationalism and advocacy of violence against Slavic peoples, particularly his 1897 remarks about Czechs, stand in tension with his opposition to antisemitism and remain a subject of scholarly consideration regarding the complexity of his political legacy.

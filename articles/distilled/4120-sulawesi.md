# Sulawesi

## Overview
Sulawesi, also known as Celebes, is the world's 11th-largest island and a major part of Indonesia. Its unique, four-peninsula shape and complex geological history have created a distinct biogeographical region with a high level of endemic species, making it a critical area for studying evolution and biodiversity.

## Timeline
- **c. 1.04–1.48 million years ago** — Oldest evidence of archaic humans (stone tools) found at Calio site.
- **c. 45,000 years ago** — Discovery of the world's oldest known figurative cave art (a warty pig) in Leang Tedongnge cave.
- **c. 3000 BC – AD 1300** — Period of megalith construction in Central Sulawesi.
- **13th century** — Access to trade goods and iron begins altering cultural patterns, allowing larger political units.
- **1605** — Kingdom of Luwu becomes the first on Sulawesi to embrace Islam.
- **1523–1525** — First European visitors (Portuguese) arrive.
- **1669** — Treaty of Bongaya forces Sultan Hasanuddin of Gowa to cede trade control to the Dutch East India Company.
- **1905** — Entire island becomes part of the Dutch East Indies colony.
- **1949** — Sulawesi becomes part of the independent United States of Indonesia.
- **1960** — Island administratively divided into two provinces.
- **2000–2004** — Creation of Gorontalo and West Sulawesi provinces, bringing the total to six.

## Body

### Geology and Geography
Sulawesi is a composite island formed by the collision of tectonic plates from Asia and Australia. This complex origin has created a rugged, mountainous interior that has historically isolated its four peninsulas, making sea travel more practical than overland routes. The island is prone to earthquakes due to its location in an active collision zone. It is not truly oceanic but a mosaic of continental fragments.

### Prehistory and Early Art
The island shows evidence of human presence for over a million years. A major discovery is its ancient cave art, with paintings in Maros dated to at least 40,000 years old. In 2021, a warty pig painting was dated to 45,500 years, making it the oldest known figurative art. In 2026, hand stencils were dated to 67,800 years, the earliest known rock art globally. These finds establish Sulawesi as a key site for understanding early human symbolic behavior.

### History and Society
From the 13th century, trade networks connected Sulawesi to the Majapahit Empire. European contact began with the Portuguese in the 1520s, followed by the Dutch, who gained control after a war with the Gowa kingdom in 1669. The island was fully incorporated into the Dutch East Indies by 1905 and became part of independent Indonesia in 1949. The population is predominantly Muslim, with a significant Christian minority, especially in the north and among the Toraja people.

### Flora and Fauna
Sulawesi is part of Wallacea, a biogeographical transition zone between Asian and Australian species. Its fauna is highly endemic, with 62% of its 127 native mammal species found nowhere else. Notable endemics include the anoa (dwarf buffalo), babirusa, and various tarsiers and macaques. The island's birds are less isolated, with 31% endemism. Its freshwater lakes and rivers host unique species of fish, shrimp, and snails.

### Environment and Conservation
The primary environmental threat is deforestation, with 80% of lowland forests lost or degraded by 2007. This loss, driven by logging, agriculture, and mining, endangers endemic species. The island has six national parks and numerous nature reserves, but many are threatened by human activities. Conservation efforts are critical to protect its unique biodiversity.

## Terms
- **Wallacea**: A biogeographical region between the Asian and Australian continental shelves, characterized by deep-water barriers that have limited species migration, leading to high endemism.
- **Endemic**: A species that is native and restricted to a certain place, such as an island.
- **Terrane**: A fragment of crustal material formed on, or broken off from, one tectonic plate and accreted to the crust lying on another plate.
- **Megalith**: A large stone that has been used to construct a structure or monument, either alone or together with other stones.
- **Austronesian**: A large language family spread across Southeast Asia, Oceania, and Madagascar, whose speakers are believed to have migrated through the region.
- **Swidden agriculture**: A traditional farming method that involves clearing a piece of land by burning vegetation, cultivating it for a few years, then leaving it fallow.
- **Vicariant process**: A geographical event that splits a population, leading to the evolution of separate species.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The exact origin and meaning of the name "Celebes" is unclear.
- The traditional view of Sulawesi's formation via multiple micro-continental collisions has been challenged by a newer hypothesis involving a single Miocene collision and subsequent fragmentation.
- The original purpose of the hundreds of granite megaliths in Central Sulawesi remains unknown.
- The precise timing and routes of early human migrations into and through Sulawesi are still being refined by new archaeological and genetic evidence.

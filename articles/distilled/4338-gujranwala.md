# Gujranwala

## Overview
Gujranwala is the fifth-largest city in Pakistan, located in Punjab province. It is a major industrial hub known as the "City of Wrestlers," famous for its cuisine and historical significance as the birthplace of Sikh ruler Ranjit Singh. Its modern economy is a key part of Pakistan's industrial "Golden Triangle."

## Body

### History and Founding
Gujranwala is a relatively modern city, founded in the 18th century by the Gurjar tribe. Its name means "abode of the Gujjars." The city rose to prominence under Sikh rule. After Mughal power weakened, the Sukerchakia Misl, led by Charat Singh, established a fort near Gujranwala between 1756 and 1758 and made it their capital in 1763. The city was the birthplace of Ranjit Singh in 1780, who initially used it as his capital until capturing Lahore in 1799. His commander, Hari Singh Nalwa, later established the city's grid street plan and built a mud wall around it.

### British Rule and Partition
The British captured the area in 1848, leading to rapid development, including the establishment of a municipality (1867) and railway connection (1881). The city saw violent riots in 1919 following the Jallianwala Bagh massacre. At the time of the 1941 census, non-Muslims (Hindus and Sikhs) owned two-thirds of the city's properties. Following the 1947 Partition of India, Gujranwala experienced severe rioting and ethnic violence, resulting in the displacement of its Hindu and Sikh populations and a massive influx of Muslim refugees from Indian Punjab.

### Modern Economy
Gujranwala is Pakistan's third-largest industrial center after Karachi and Faisalabad, contributing 5% to 9% of the national GDP. It forms part of the "Golden Triangle" of industrial cities with Sialkot and Gujrat. Key industries include the manufacture of sanitary fittings, auto parts, electric fans, and iron and steel. The city is also a major center for hosiery and textile production. The surrounding region is highly productive for wheat and rice cultivation.

### Geography and Urban Form
The city is located in the Rachna Doab, a plain between the Chenab and Ravi rivers. Its old city, centered on the Shahi Bazaar, was laid out in a grid plan by Hari Singh Nalwa. Growth under British rule occurred outside the old walls, including the Civil Lines neighborhood for Europeans. Post-1947, suburban areas like Satellite Town (1950) were developed to house refugees. Much of the city's later growth has been unplanned.

## Terms
*   **Misl:** An independent Sikh chieftainship, usually consisting of the chief's kinsmen.
*   **Rachna Doab:** A strip of land between the Chenab and Ravi rivers in Punjab.
*   **Golden Triangle:** A term for the industrial region comprising Gujranwala, Sialkot, and Gujrat.
*   **Jagir:** A grant of land revenue given in lieu of a salary, often to a military commander.
*   **Civil Lines:** A neighborhood built by the British to house European residents.
*   **Partition:** The 1947 division of British India into the independent nations of India and Pakistan.
*   **Lohar:** A clan of blacksmiths, historically associated with metalworking in the city.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   The exact origins and founding date of Gujranwala are unclear. While traditionally believed to be named after a Gujjar named Khanpur Sansi, recent scholarship suggests it may have been the village of Serai Gujran.
*   The severity of the 1919 riots is noted as among the most violent in British India, with historical records burned in attacked offices.

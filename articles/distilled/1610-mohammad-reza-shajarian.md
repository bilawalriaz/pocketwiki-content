# Mohammad-Reza Shajarian

## Overview
Mohammad-Reza Shajarian (1940–2020) was Iran’s preeminent master (*Ostad*) of Persian traditional music, a vocalist whose career spanned six decades. He synthesized the classical *Radif* repertoire with the vocal styles of early 20th-century masters and the instrumental phrasing of tar virtuoso Jalil Shahnaz, forging a distinct, highly ornamented style. Shajarian was one of the few artists permitted to perform publicly after the 1979 Revolution, but his later support for the 2009 Green Movement led to a state ban on his work, transforming him into a symbol of cultural resistance. He was also a noted calligrapher, instrument inventor, and teacher to a generation of leading singers.

## Body

### Early Life and Musical Formation
Born into a family of Quran reciters in Mashhad, Shajarian began vocal training at age five through the disciplined *tajwid* (Quranic recitation) tradition. At 12, he secretly began studying the *Radif*—the canonical collection of melodic modes (*gusheh*) organizing Persian classical music—defying his father’s religious objections to music. This early conflict prompted his brief use of the pseudonym "Siavash Bidakani" at his 1959 Radio Khorasan debut. He soon reverted to his birth name, studying under four pillars of the tradition: setar player Ahmad Ebadi, vocalist Esmaeil Mehrtash, *Radif* authority Nour-Ali Boroumand, and master of early repertoire Abdollah Davami. He also absorbed the styles of pre-radio era singers like Reza Gholi Mirza Zelli and Qamar-ol-Moluk Vaziri. Crucially, he modeled his phrasing on the tar (long-necked lute) playing of Jalil Shahnaz, translating instrumental *tez* (fast) passages and ornamentation into vocal lines.

### Collaborative Peak and the *Dastgah* System
Shajarian’s artistry matured through sustained partnerships with leading composers and instrumentalists of the post-1970s generation: Parviz Meshkatian (santour), Mohammad Reza Lotfi (tar/setar), Hossein Alizadeh (tar/setar), and Faramarz Payvar (santour). These collaborations produced landmark albums structured around the *Dastgah* system—the modal framework governing Persian art music—such as *Raast-Panjgaah* (1976), *Bidaad* (1985, in *Homayoun*), *Nava* (1986), and *Dastan* (1987, in *Chahargah*). He also explored regional traditions, recording Mazandarani, Azeri, Kurdish, and Luri folk repertoires. His ensemble work included the "Masters of Persian Music" (with son Homayoun, Kayhan Kalhor, and Alizadeh) and the Ava and Shahnaz Ensembles, the latter named for his idol Jalil Shahnaz.

### Pedagogy and Instrument Innovation
Shajarian taught at Tehran University’s Department of Fine Arts and privately mentored a roster of singers who now define the contemporary scene, including Shahram Nazeri, Mohammad Esfahani, Homayoun Shajarian, Mojgan Shajarian, and Sina Sarlak. He studied santour (hammered dulcimer) under Jalal Akhbari and later Payvar not to perform, but to internalize the *Radif*’s intervallic logic. In later life, he designed and crafted novel instruments—exhibited in 2011 and 2013—seeking timbres that could realize his musical imagination beyond the standard ensemble.

### Political Engagement and State Censorship
The 1985 album *Bidaad* marked a turning point; its lyrics lamenting a "wonderful place reduced to shambles" were widely read as allegory for post-revolutionary Iran. In 2009, after President Ahmadinejad dismissed protesters as "dust and trash," Shajarian declared himself "the voice of dust and trash" and demanded state broadcaster IRIB cease airing his works—including *Rabbana*, the Ramadan fast-breaking anthem broadcast annually since 1979. The ban held through the 2010s despite public outcry and appeals from presidents Rouhani (2013, 2017). His 2009 song "Language of Fire" ("Lay down your gun... hear") addressed security forces directly. In a 2016 interview, Shajarian clarified his critique targeted Ahmadinejad personally, not the Islamic Republic system, and recounted walking off stage in Stockholm when audience chants turned anti-regime, refusing to politicize his art further.

### Final Years and Legacy
Diagnosed with kidney cancer in 2001 (publicly revealed 2016), Shajarian continued performing until health failed. A leaked clip from the documentary *The Voice of Dust and Ash* (filmed in California and Iran) went viral in 2016 and 2020: "My voice is part of Iran's ancient culture, to remind the people of the world that we have had a culture of love, peace and friendship." He died 8 October 2020 in Tehran; a private ceremony preceded burial at the Tomb of Ferdowsi, the 10th-century epic poet, symbolically linking Iran’s greatest modern vocalist to its foundational literary voice. Honors include two Grammy nominations (2004, 2006), France’s Legion of Honour (2014), the Aga Khan Music Award (2019), and NPR’s "50 Great Voices" (2010).

## Terms
- ****Radif**** — The canonical repertoire of Persian classical music: a structured collection of melodic fragments (*gusheh*) grouped into modal systems (*Dastgah*), transmitted orally.
- ****Dastgah**** — A modal framework in Persian art music comprising a specific scale, characteristic motifs, and a prescribed sequence of *gusheh*; analogous to a mode-plus-form.
- ****Ostad**** — Honorific title meaning "master," conferred on musicians who have fully internalized the *Radif* and gained peer recognition as teachers and exemplars.
- ****Santour**** — Trapezoidal hammered dulcimer with 72–96 strings, central to Persian classical ensembles; Shajarian studied it to understand *Radif* intervallic structure.
- ****Tar**** — Long-necked, waisted lute with six strings and movable frets; the primary melodic instrument in classical ensembles. Jalil Shahnaz’s tar style shaped Shajarian’s vocal phrasing.
- ****Tajwid**** — Rules governing Quranic recitation; Shajarian’s earliest vocal training, providing breath control, ornamentation, and modal sensibility.
- ****Gusheh**** — Individual melodic unit within the *Radif*; each has a distinct interval pattern, mood, and typical ornamentation.
- ****IRIB**** — Islamic Republic of Iran Broadcasting, the state media monopoly that banned Shajarian’s work (including the Ramadan staple *Rabbana*) after his 2009 political stance.
- ****Green Movement**** — 2009 Iranian protest movement disputing Mahmoud Ahmadinejad’s re-election; Shajarian’s public support triggered his censorship.
- ****Rabbana**** — Quranic prayer ("Our Lord...") set to music by Shajarian; broadcast daily at sunset during Ramadan on Iranian state media from 1979 until his 2009 ban.

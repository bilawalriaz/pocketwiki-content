# Daegu

## Overview
Daegu is a major metropolitan city in southeastern South Korea, the nation's third or fourth largest urban center. Historically a provincial capital and economic hub for textiles, it is now a political stronghold for conservatism and is focusing on fashion and high-tech industries. Its strategic location and basin geography have shaped its role as a transportation, commercial, and cultural center for the Yeongnam region.

## Timeline
- **c. 1500–300 BC** — Mumun pottery period settlements and burials are found in the Greater Daegu area.
- **Proto–Three Kingdoms** — Daegu is the site of the chiefdom known as Dalgubeol, later absorbed into Silla.
- **757** — The city is given its current name, Daegu.
- **927** — Battle of Gong Mountain between Goryeo and Later Baekje forces occurs in northern Daegu.
- **1601** — Daegu becomes the administrative capital of Gyeongsang Province.
- **1895** — One of Korea's first modern post offices is established in Daegu as part of the Kabo reforms.
- **1946** — The Daegu October Incident, a major social disorder, occurs.
- **1950** — The Battle of Taegu takes place during the Korean War within the Pusan Perimeter.
- **1980s** — Daegu separates from Gyeongsangbuk-do to become a directly governed city.
- **1995** — The Daegu gas explosions kill 101 people.
- **2003** — The Daegu subway fire kills 192 people; the city hosts the Summer Universiade.
- **2008** — The Daegu-Gyeongbuk Free Economic Zone is designated.
- **2011** — Daegu hosts the World Championships in Athletics.
- **2020** — Daegu is the epicenter of South Korea's initial COVID-19 outbreak.
- **2023** — Gunwi County is integrated into Daegu.

## Body

### History and Geography
Daegu is located in a basin surrounded by mountains, with the Geumho and Nakdong Rivers flowing nearby. Archaeological evidence shows prehistoric Mumun settlements. In the Proto–Three Kingdoms period, it was the chiefdom of Dalgubeol, which was absorbed into the Silla Kingdom. In 757, it received its current name. During the Later Three Kingdoms period, it was the site of the 927 Battle of Gong Mountain. In the Joseon dynasty, Daegu became the capital of Gyeongsang Province in 1601, a status it held for nearly 300 years, and was a key transportation hub on the Great Yeongnam Road. The city was a center of independence movements against Japanese imperialism in the early 20th century. After 1945, it experienced social unrest, guerrilla activity, and major battles during the Korean War, remaining within the Pusan Perimeter. Post-war, it saw explosive population growth and became a political base for conservative leaders like Park Chung Hee.

### Economy and Industry
Daegu was an economic motor for South Korea from the 1960s to 1980s, with textiles as its core industry, earning it the nickname "Textile City." Its humid subtropical climate also makes it ideal for apple production, leading to the nickname "Apple City." The city's economy has faced challenges due to the decline of the textile industry. To foster revival, the Daegu-Gyeongbuk Free Economic Zone was established in 2008, focusing on fashion and high-tech industries. The city is promoting itself as a fashion hub, with developments like the Esiapolis district. Major companies like Samsung and Kolon were founded in Daegu, and it is the economic core of the Daegu-Gyeongbuk region.

### Culture and Society
Daegu is known as a conservative city with strong Buddhist and Confucian traditions, alongside a significant Christian presence. Key cultural sites include the Gatbawi stone Buddha on Palgongsan Mountain, historic temples like Donghwasa, and traditional markets such as the 350-year-old Yangnyeongsi herbal medicine market. The downtown area of Dongseongno is a major commercial center. The city hosts numerous festivals, including the Daegu International Opera Festival and the Chimac Festival. It is also a medical hub for southeastern Korea, with major university hospitals. Demographically, the population is largely homogeneous, with a small community of immigrants and U.S. military personnel.

### Transportation and Administration
Daegu is a major inland transportation hub. Dongdaegu Station is a key stop for the KTX high-speed rail on the Gyeongbu Line. The city has a metro system with two heavy rail lines and an elevated monorail line. Daegu International Airport serves regional destinations. Administratively, Daegu is a metropolitan city divided into seven districts and two counties. It is a stronghold for the conservative People Power Party and has produced four South Korean presidents.

## Terms
- **Mumun pottery period**: A prehistoric era in Korea (c. 1500–300 BC) characterized by plain, undecorated pottery.
- **Proto–Three Kingdoms period**: An era before the Three Kingdoms of Korea, featuring chiefdoms like Mahan, Jinhan, and Byeonhan.
- **Silla**: One of the Three Kingdoms of Korea, which unified the peninsula in the late 7th century.
- **Gyeongsang Province**: One of the traditional eight provinces of the Joseon dynasty, for which Daegu served as the capital.
- **Pusan Perimeter**: A defensive line held by UN and South Korean forces during the Korean War, which included Daegu.
- **Daegu-Gyeongbuk Free Economic Zone**: A special economic zone designated in 2008 to promote investment and industry in the region.
- **Dongseongno**: The main downtown and commercial street in central Daegu.
- **Yangnyeongsi**: A historic market in Daegu specializing in Korean medicinal herbs for over 350 years.

## Debates and open questions
- The historical record notes that in 689, Silla's King Sinmun considered moving the capital from Gyeongju to Daegu but was unable to do so. The single source for this event suggests it indicates an attempt to reinforce royal authority and entrenched resistance from Gyeongju elites as the cause of failure.

# Ronald Reagan

## Overview
Ronald Reagan (1911–2004) was the 40th U.S. President (1981–1989) and a transformative figure in American conservatism. His presidency, known as the Reagan era, was defined by economic policies called "Reaganomics," a confrontational Cold War stance that evolved into diplomacy with the Soviet Union, and a realignment of American politics toward conservative principles.

## Timeline
- **Feb 6, 1911** — Born in Tampico, Illinois.
- **1932** — Graduated from Eureka College; began radio broadcasting career.
- **1937** — Moved to California; began film acting career.
- **1947–1952, 1959–1960** — Served two terms as president of the Screen Actors Guild (SAG).
- **Nov 1964** — Gave "A Time for Choosing" speech, launching his national political profile.
- **1966–1975** — Served as Governor of California.
- **Nov 4, 1980** — Won the presidential election in a landslide over Jimmy Carter.
- **Mar 30, 1981** — Survived an assassination attempt.
- **Aug 5, 1981** — Fired ~12,000 striking air traffic controllers (PATCO).
- **Nov 6, 1984** — Won reelection in a historic landslide over Walter Mondale.
- **Dec 8, 1987** — Signed the Intermediate-Range Nuclear Forces (INF) Treaty with the Soviet Union.
- **Nov 5, 1994** — Announced his diagnosis with Alzheimer's disease.
- **Jun 5, 2004** — Died in Los Angeles, California.

## Body

### Early Life and Career
Born to a Catholic father and a Disciples of Christ mother, Reagan was influenced by his mother's faith. He graduated from Eureka College in 1932 and worked as a sports broadcaster before moving to Hollywood in 1937. He appeared in 53 films, with *Knute Rockne, All American* (1940) and *Kings Row* (1942) being notable. During World War II, he served in the Army Air Forces, producing training films. As SAG president, he fought for actors' residuals and testified before the House Un-American Activities Committee about communist influence in Hollywood. He married actress Nancy Davis in 1952.

### Political Rise
Reagan began as a Democrat and New Deal supporter but shifted rightward in the 1950s, influenced by his work as a motivational speaker for General Electric. His 1964 "A Time for Choosing" speech for Barry Goldwater made him a conservative star. Elected Governor of California in 1966, he raised taxes to balance the budget and cracked down on university protests. He narrowly lost the 1976 Republican primary to Gerald Ford but won the presidency in 1980, campaigning on tax cuts, reduced government, and a strong national defense.

### Domestic Policy: "Reaganomics"
Reagan's economic program combined tax cuts, deregulation, and reduced domestic spending. The Economic Recovery Tax Act of 1981 slashed income tax rates. While the economy recovered from a severe recession (1981–82) and inflation fell, the national debt nearly tripled due to tax cuts and increased military spending. His administration deregulated industries, notably savings and loans, contributing to a later crisis. He broke the PATCO strike in 1981, signaling a tougher stance on organized labor. His domestic agenda also included an intensified "War on Drugs" and a slow, criticized response to the AIDS epidemic.

### Foreign Policy and the Cold War
Reagan initially escalated the Cold War with a major military buildup, the Strategic Defense Initiative (SDI), and rhetoric calling the Soviet Union an "evil empire." He ordered the 1983 invasion of Grenada and supported anti-communist movements worldwide via the Reagan Doctrine. His second term saw a shift toward diplomacy with Soviet leader Mikhail Gorbachev. Their summits led to the 1987 INF Treaty, eliminating an entire class of nuclear missiles. The Iran-Contra affair, where his administration illegally sold arms to Iran to fund Nicaraguan Contras, was a major scandal that damaged his credibility.

### Legacy and Post-Presidency
Reagan left office with high approval ratings after overseeing a long economic expansion and a thaw in Cold War tensions. His presidency solidified a conservative realignment in U.S. politics. He was diagnosed with Alzheimer's disease in 1994 and died in 2004. Historical rankings have placed him in the upper tier of presidents, though debates continue over the economic inequality of his era and the fiscal impact of his policies.

## Terms
*   **Reaganomics:** The economic policies of the Reagan administration, emphasizing tax cuts, deregulation, and reduced government spending on domestic programs.
*   **Supply-side economics:** An economic theory arguing that economic growth can be most effectively created by lowering taxes and decreasing regulation, which would theoretically benefit all levels of society.
*   **Stagflation:** A period of slow economic growth and relatively high unemployment accompanied by rising prices (inflation).
*   **Détente:** The easing of strained relations, especially in a political situation; the policy of relaxing tensions between the U.S. and Soviet Union during the Cold War, which Reagan rejected.
*   **Reagan Doctrine:** A strategy implemented by the Reagan administration to oppose the global influence of the Soviet Union by supporting and funding anti-communist guerrilla movements.
*   **Iran-Contra affair:** A political scandal in the U.S. during the Reagan administration where senior officials secretly facilitated the sale of arms to Iran, which was subject to an arms embargo, and used the proceeds to fund the Contras in Nicaragua.
*   **Strategic Defense Initiative (SDI):** A proposed missile defense system intended to protect the U.S. from attack by ballistic strategic nuclear weapons, nicknamed "Star Wars."

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Economic Impact:** Scholars debate whether Reagan's policies caused the "Great Divergence" in income inequality or if they were responsible for the robust economic growth of the 1980s and 1990s.
*   **Role in Ending the Cold War:** Historians argue over the extent to which Reagan's military pressure and rhetoric accelerated the collapse of the Soviet Union versus the roles of internal Soviet weaknesses and the policies of Mikhail Gorbachev.
*   **Fiscal Responsibility:** Critics point to the tripling of the national debt as a failure of fiscal conservatism, while supporters argue the tax cuts spurred growth that ultimately increased revenue.
*   **AIDS Response:** The Reagan administration's delayed and inadequate response to the AIDS epidemic remains a significant point of criticism from historians and public health advocates.

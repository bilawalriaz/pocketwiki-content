# Douala

## Overview
Douala is the largest city and economic capital of Cameroon, serving as the commercial hub for the entire Central African Economic and Monetary Community (CEMAC) region. Its strategic location on the Wouri River estuary has made it a major port and gateway for the country's exports, shaping its history from a pre-colonial trading town to a modern metropolis facing challenges of rapid growth and poverty.

## Timeline
- **c. 1472** — Portuguese explorers visit the area; the Wouri estuary is named *Rio dos Camarões* (Shrimp River).
- **By 1650** — A town forms from immigrants speaking the Duala language.
- **18th Century** — The town becomes a center of the transatlantic slave trade.
- **1884-1895** — Douala is a German protectorate, renamed Kamerunstadt.
- **1902** — The capital of German Kamerun is moved from Douala to Buéa.
- **1919** — After World War I, Douala comes under French mandate.
- **1940-1946** — Douala serves as the capital of Cameroon.
- **1960** — Cameroon gains independence; Douala becomes the major economic city.
- **1991** — Douala is the center of the "ghost town" civil disobedience campaign for multi-party democracy.

## Body

### History and Name
The area was first visited by the Portuguese around 1472. By 1650, a town of Duala-speaking immigrants was established. It was a key center for the transatlantic slave trade in the 18th century. In 1884, it became the capital of the German protectorate of Kamerun, renamed Kamerunstadt. The capital moved to Buéa in 1902, and the city was renamed Douala in 1907. After World War I, it became part of French Cameroon. Douala was the capital of Cameroon from 1940 to 1946. Following independence in 1960, it solidified its role as the economic capital, while Yaoundé became the political capital. In 1991, it was the epicenter of major pro-democracy protests.

### Geography and Climate
Douala is located on the Atlantic coast, approximately 210 km west of Yaoundé, on the banks of the Wouri River. It has a tropical monsoon climate (Köppen *Am*) with warm, humid conditions year-round. The average annual temperature is 27.0 °C (80.6 °F) with 83% humidity. It receives heavy rainfall, averaging 3,600 mm (140 in) annually, with August being the wettest month.

### Urban Structure and Architecture
The city is divided into seven districts, including the business district of Akwa and the administrative district of Bonanjo. Neighborhoods like New Bell and Bépanda are very densely populated. German colonial urban planning began in 1896, followed by French construction in the 1920s-1950s. Key historical structures include the Villa Mandessi Bell (1904) and the Cathedral of Saints Peter and Paul (1925-1930). Post-independence architecture includes the monumental sculpture *La Nouvelle Liberté* (2007).

### Economy and Population
As of 2025, Douala's population is estimated at 4.3 million. It is the economic engine of Cameroon and the CEMAC region, handling major exports like oil, cocoa, coffee, and timber. Despite this, about 30% of its population lives in poverty. The city has a modest oil resource but strong agricultural conditions. Its main economic challenges include a heavy civil service and vulnerability to extreme weather events like floods and storms.

### Transportation and Infrastructure
Douala is served by the Douala International Airport (DLA), the busiest in the CEMAC region. The Port of Douala, historically Central Africa's largest, is being supplemented by the new deep-water port at Kribi. The city is linked by rail to other major Cameroonian cities. Road infrastructure is developed but suffers from decay and corruption. A Bus Rapid Transit (BRT) network is planned.

## Terms
- **CEMAC**: The Central African Economic and Monetary Community, a regional economic union of six countries including Cameroon.
- **Duala**: The indigenous language of the city's namesake people.
- **Köppen climate classification *Am***: A climate type characterized by a short dry season in a tropical wet climate.
- **Lingua franca**: A common language used for communication between people who do not share a native language.
- **Unitary state**: A system of political organization where the central government holds supreme authority.
- **Ghost town operation (*ville morte*)**: A form of civil disobedience involving a general strike and shutdown of economic activities.

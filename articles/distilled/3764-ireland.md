# Ireland

## Overview
Ireland is an island in the North Atlantic, divided between the independent Republic of Ireland and Northern Ireland, which is part of the United Kingdom. Its history is marked by waves of migration, cultural synthesis, and political conflict, particularly over sovereignty and religious identity. The island's geography, mild climate, and complex past have shaped a distinct culture with global influence, especially in literature and music.

## Timeline
- **c. 33,000 BC** — Earliest evidence of human activity (reindeer bone in Castlepook Cave).
- **c. 4,350 BC** — Earliest evidence of farming in Ireland (Ferriter's Cove).
- **5th century** — Christianisation begins with missions of Palladius and Saint Patrick.
- **23 April 1014** — Battle of Clontarf ends major Viking power in Ireland.
- **1 May 1169** — Norman invasion begins at Bannow Strand.
- **1542** — Henry VIII creates the Kingdom of Ireland.
- **1801** — Acts of Union merge Ireland with Great Britain into the United Kingdom.
- **1845–1851** — The Great Famine causes over one million deaths and mass emigration.
- **1916** — Easter Rising in Dublin.
- **1921** — Anglo-Irish Treaty leads to the partition of Ireland.
- **1949** — Republic of Ireland Act declares the state a republic.
- **1998** — Good Friday Agreement restores power-sharing in Northern Ireland.

## Body

### Geography and Climate
Ireland is the second-largest island of the British Isles, separated from Great Britain by the North Channel and Irish Sea. Its landscape features low-lying mountains around a central plain, with the River Shannon as its longest river. The climate is mild and oceanic, influenced by the Atlantic, resulting in lush vegetation and the nickname "Emerald Isle." Woodland cover is now about 10%, mostly non-native plantations, down from extensive forests in the Middle Ages.

### Prehistory and Early History
Human presence dates back over 30,000 years. Mesolithic communities were established by 8,000 BC, followed by Neolithic farmers who built monuments like the Céide Fields. The Bronze Age began c. 2500 BC. The arrival of Celtic language and culture is debated; genetic evidence points to a major migration of Bell Beaker people around the mid-3rd millennium BC, who largely replaced earlier farmer lineages. By the 1st century AD, Gaelic Ireland was established, divided among petty kings and provincial kingdoms.

### Christianisation and Viking Age
Christianisation began in the 5th century, leading to a monastic culture that preserved Latin learning during the Early Middle Ages. From the 9th century, Vikings raided and settled, founding major coastal cities like Dublin. Their power in Ireland ended after the Battle of Clontarf in 1014.

### Norman Invasion and English Rule
A Norman invasion in 1169, invited by an Irish king, led to English claims of sovereignty. English rule expanded during the Tudor conquest in the 16th–17th centuries, followed by British colonisation through plantations. A system of Protestant Ascendancy was established, legally disadvantaging the Catholic majority. Ireland became part of the United Kingdom in 1801.

### Famine, Nationalism, and Partition
The Great Famine (1845–1851) caused a population collapse through death and emigration. The 19th and early 20th centuries saw the rise of Irish nationalism. After the 1916 Easter Rising and a war of independence, the Anglo-Irish Treaty (1921) partitioned the island. The Irish Free State evolved into the sovereign Republic of Ireland (declared 1949), while Northern Ireland remained in the UK.

### The Troubles and Peace Process
Northern Ireland experienced decades of sectarian conflict known as "the Troubles" from the late 1960s. The 1998 Good Friday Agreement established a power-sharing government and cross-border institutions, largely ending the violence. Both parts of Ireland joined the EEC in 1973; the UK (including Northern Ireland) left the EU in 2020, with Northern Ireland granted special single-market access for goods.

### Culture and Society
Irish culture, expressed through the Irish language, Gaelic games, music, and literature, has had a significant global impact. The island has produced four Nobel laureates in Literature. English is the dominant language, though Irish is an official language in the Republic and taught in schools. Society has become more secular and multicultural since the late 20th century.

## Terms
- **Gaelic Ireland**: The early medieval Irish society organised into kingdoms and ruled by kings under a system of Brehon Law.
- **Brehon Law**: The early Irish legal system administered by jurists called brehons.
- **Plantations**: State-sponsored colonisation of Irish land with settlers from Britain, primarily in the 16th and 17th centuries.
- **Protestant Ascendancy**: The ruling class of Anglican Protestants in Ireland who held political and economic power after the 17th century.
- **Home Rule**: The campaign for Irish self-government within the United Kingdom in the late 19th and early 20th centuries.
- **The Troubles**: The ethno-nationalist conflict in Northern Ireland from the late 1960s to the 1990s.
- **Good Friday Agreement (1998)**: The peace treaty that established power-sharing in Northern Ireland and cross-border cooperation.
- **Celtic Tiger**: The period of rapid economic growth in the Republic of Ireland from the mid-1990s to 2007.
- **Gaeltacht**: Irish-speaking regions in the Republic of Ireland.
- **GAA (Gaelic Athletic Association)**: The governing body for Gaelic sports like football and hurling.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The origin and migration of Celtic peoples to Ireland is heavily debated, with theories ranging from large-scale invasion to cultural diffusion and recent genetic studies suggesting a major Bell Beaker migration.
- The authenticity of the 1155 Papal Bull *Laudabiliter*, which allegedly authorised English intervention in Ireland, is disputed.
- The extent of the High King of Ireland's actual political power before the Viking Age is questioned by historians.

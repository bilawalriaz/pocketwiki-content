# Hainan

## Overview
Hainan is China's southernmost province, consisting of Hainan Island and over 200 smaller islands in the South China Sea. Historically part of Guangdong, it became a separate province in 1988 and was designated a Special Economic Zone. Its strategic location and tropical climate have made it a focus for tourism, agriculture, and, most recently, a major national project to develop the world's largest free-trade port by 2035.

## Timeline
- **110 BC** — Western Han dynasty establishes a military garrison, marking recorded Chinese administration.
- **1939** — Japanese occupation begins during the Second Sino-Japanese War.
- **1 May 1950** — People's Republic of China (PRC) gains control of the island.
- **1 October 1984** — Hainan becomes an Administrative Region with its own People's Government.
- **1988** — Hainan is separated from Guangdong to become a province and a Special Economic Zone.
- **2010** — Government announces plans to develop Hainan into an "international tourist destination" by 2020.
- **13 April 2018** — Plan announced to transform Hainan into a pilot free-trade zone by 2020 and a free-trade port by 2025.
- **18 December 2018** — "Customs closure" implemented, making Hainan a separate customs territory.
- **2021** — Hainan Free Trade Port Law is adopted by the National People's Congress.
- **18 December 2025** — Hainan Free Trade Port launches island-wide independent customs operations.

## Body

### History and Administration
Hainan was first recorded by Chinese officials in 110 BC. For centuries, it was governed under Guangdong province and served as a place of exile for political dissidents. The indigenous Li (Hlai) people were displaced by later Han Chinese migration. The Japanese occupied the island from 1939 until the end of World War II. The PRC took control in 1950, initially administering it as a branch of Guangdong. In 1988, Hainan was elevated to a full province and designated a Special Economic Zone to attract investment. In 2018, a master plan was launched to develop the entire island into the Hainan Free Trade Port, with a goal of becoming the world's largest by 2035. This involves low tariffs, reduced taxes, and relaxed visa rules to draw foreign business and tourism.

### Geography and Environment
Hainan Island, separated from the mainland by the 20 km wide Qiongzhou Strait, is the 42nd largest island in the world. Its terrain features an ancient volcanic field in the north and mountains in the interior, with Wuzhi Mountain as the highest peak at 1,840 m. The climate is tropical, with average temperatures between 23–28 °C. The province administers the disputed Paracel (Xisha) and Spratly (Nansha) Islands in the South China Sea, though sovereignty is contested by several nations. Hainan has significantly better air quality than mainland China and hosts diverse tropical forests, though some species are threatened by human activity and invasive species.

### Demographics and Culture
The population is predominantly Han Chinese (82%), who speak various Chinese languages including Hainanese Min and Cantonese. The largest indigenous group is the Li (15%), a Kra–Dai-speaking people recognized as one of China's 56 ethnic groups. Other minorities include the Miao and Zhuang. The Utsuls, descendants of Cham refugees, are classified as Hui (Muslim). The cuisine is known for its seafood and dishes like Wenchang Chicken. The island has a history as a place of exile, which contributed to a distinct cultural atmosphere.

### Economy
Hainan's economy is driven by agriculture, tourism, and its special economic policies. It is a major producer of rubber, tropical fruits, and areca nuts. Tourism is a key sector, with millions of visitors annually drawn to its beaches and resorts. The government has actively promoted medical tourism and a duty-free shopping program. The Hainan Free Trade Port initiative aims to shift the economy toward high-end services, finance, and international trade. The province has also developed a high-speed rail ring and the Wenchang Space Launch Site.

## Terms
- **Special Economic Zone (SEZ):** A designated area where business and investment laws differ from the rest of the country to encourage economic growth.
- **Sinicization:** The process of Chinese cultural and political influence spreading to non-Han areas.
- **Kra–Dai:** A language family spoken across Southeast Asia and southern China, including the Li languages.
- **Customs Closure (Feng Guan):** The implementation of a separate customs territory, where goods entering from Hainan to mainland China are treated as imports.
- **Hainanese Min:** A variety of Min Chinese spoken by the majority Han population on Hainan.
- **Li (Hlai):** The largest indigenous ethnic group on Hainan Island.
- **Utsul:** A community of Cham descent near Sanya, classified as Hui (Muslim) by the Chinese government.
- **Qiongzhou Strait:** The body of water separating Hainan Island from the Leizhou Peninsula of Guangdong.

## Debates and open questions
- **Sovereignty of South China Sea Islands:** China's administration of the Paracel and Spratly Islands through Hainan's Sansha city is disputed by Vietnam, the Philippines, Malaysia, Brunei, and Taiwan.
- **Free Trade Port Viability:** Experts have raised concerns about whether the Hainan Free Trade Port can comply with global trading practices and whether it is intended to replace Hong Kong's role as a trading entrepôt.
- **Environmental Impact:** While Hainan has strong air quality, its biodiversity faces threats from tourism, deforestation, and invasive species, with some plants already declared extinct.

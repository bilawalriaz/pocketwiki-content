# Gottlob Frege

## Overview
Gottlob Frege (1848–1925) was a German philosopher, logician, and mathematician widely regarded as the father of analytic philosophy and one of the greatest logicians since Aristotle. His work in the late 19th century revolutionized logic and laid the foundations for the philosophy of language and the modern foundations of mathematics, though he was largely unrecognized during his lifetime.

## Timeline
- **8 Nov 1848** — Born in Wismar, Mecklenburg-Schwerin.
- **1869** — Matriculated at the University of Jena.
- **1871** — Continued studies at the University of Göttingen.
- **1873** — Attained doctorate in mathematics (geometry) at Göttingen.
- **1879** — Published *Begriffsschrift*, marking the birth of modern predicate logic.
- **1884** — Published *The Foundations of Arithmetic*, formulating the logicist project.
- **1892** — Published "On Sense and Reference," introducing a key distinction in philosophy of language.
- **1893** — Published Volume I of *Basic Laws of Arithmetic*.
- **1903** — Published Volume II of *Basic Laws of Arithmetic*; Russell's paradox was revealed.
- **1918** — Retired from the University of Jena.
- **26 Jul 1925** — Died in Bad Kleinen.

## Body

### Logic and the Foundations of Mathematics
Frege's primary goal was to demonstrate that arithmetic is a branch of logic, a project known as **logicism**. To achieve this, he developed a new formal system in his 1879 book *Begriffsschrift* ("Concept-Script"). This system introduced **quantified variables**, solving the problem of multiple generality (e.g., distinguishing "every boy loves some girl" from "some girl is loved by every boy") and forming the basis of modern **predicate logic**. His magnum opus, *Basic Laws of Arithmetic* (1893, 1903), attempted to derive all arithmetic laws from logical axioms, the most crucial being **Basic Law V**, which defined the extension (or set) of a concept.

In 1903, Bertrand Russell discovered that Basic Law V led to a contradiction, now known as **Russell's paradox** (the set of all sets that do not contain themselves). This undermined Frege's system. However, later work showed that much of his logicist program could be salvaged. For instance, **Frege's theorem** demonstrates that arithmetic can be derived from **Hume's principle** (the number of Fs equals the number of Gs if and only if the Fs and Gs can be put into one-to-one correspondence) within a weaker logical framework.

### Philosophy of Language
Frege's philosophical work, particularly his 1892 paper "On Sense and Reference," was foundational for analytic philosophy. He argued that expressions have two aspects of meaning: **reference** (the object in the world they denote) and **sense** (the "mode of presentation" or cognitive content). For example, "the morning star" and "the evening star" have the same reference (the planet Venus) but different senses. This distinction explained how informative statements can be made and addressed puzzles about identity. His work also emphasized the **context principle** (a word's meaning is given in the context of a sentence) and the **principle of compositionality** (the meaning of a sentence is determined by the meanings of its parts and their syntax).

### Later Life and Legacy
Frege was a professor at the University of Jena for most of his career. He was an introverted lecturer who rarely engaged in dialogue. His work was initially overlooked but was later championed by figures like Bertrand Russell, Ludwig Wittgenstein, and Rudolf Carnap, profoundly influencing 20th-century philosophy and logic. A diary published posthumously revealed extreme right-wing and anti-Semitic views from the end of his life, contrasting with his reputation as a purely rational thinker.

## Terms
- **Logicism**: The philosophical thesis that mathematics is reducible to logic.
- **Predicate Logic**: A formal system using quantifiers (∀, ∃) and variables to express propositions about objects, invented by Frege.
- **Basic Law V**: Frege's axiom stating that two concepts have the same extension if and only if they apply to the same objects; its inconsistency was revealed by Russell's paradox.
- **Russell's Paradox**: A contradiction arising from considering the set of all sets that do not contain themselves, which showed Frege's Basic Law V was inconsistent.
- **Frege's Theorem**: The result that the axioms of arithmetic can be derived from Hume's principle within second-order logic.
- **Hume's Principle**: The principle that the number of Fs is the same as the number of Gs if and only if the Fs and Gs can be put into one-to-one correspondence.
- **Sense (Sinn)**: The cognitive content or "mode of presentation" of an expression, determining its reference.
- **Reference (Bedeutung)**: The object in the world that an expression denotes or picks out.
- **Context Principle**: The principle that a word only has meaning in the context of a sentence.
- **Psychologism**: The view that logical and mathematical truths are laws of human psychology, which Frege opposed.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **The Salvage of Logicism**: Scholars debate how much of Frege's original logicist program can be recovered. Key proposals include replacing Basic Law V with Hume's principle (Neo-Fregeanism) or using weakened logical systems.
- **Interpretation of Sense and Reference**: The precise nature of Frege's distinction between sense and reference, and its implications for theories of meaning and truth, remain actively discussed.
- **Influence and Originality**: There is scholarly debate about the extent of influence on Frege from philosophers like Hermann Lotze and the originality of his logical notation compared to earlier systems.

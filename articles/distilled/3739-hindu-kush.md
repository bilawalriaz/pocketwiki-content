# Hindu Kush

## Overview
The Hindu Kush is an 800-kilometre mountain range in Central and South Asia, forming the western section of the Hindu Kush Himalayan Region. It stretches from central Afghanistan into northwestern Pakistan and southeastern Tajikistan, acting as a major geographical and cultural barrier between the Amu Darya and Indus River valleys. Historically, it has been a critical passageway for invasions, trade, and the spread of religions like Buddhism and Islam, and remains a strategically significant region in contemporary conflicts.

- **1st century CE** — Buddhism becomes widespread in the Hindu Kush region.

- **1398** — Timur's army crosses the Hindu Kush to invade northern India.
- **1526** — Babur, based in Kabul, wins the Battle of Panipat, founding the Mughal Empire.

- **1896** — Amir Abdur Rahman Khan invades and converts Kafiristan to Islam, renaming it Nuristan.
- **1980s** — Soviet forces and Afghan mujahideen fight in the Hindu Kush during the Soviet-Afghan War.
- **2001** — Taliban destroy the Bamiyan Buddhas; US-led campaign begins in the Hindu Kush after 9/11.
- **2019** — Hindu Kush Himalaya Assessment reports on climate change impacts and future scenarios.

## Body

### Etymology and Names
The name "Hindu Kush" is relatively recent, first appearing in the 14th-century works of Ibn Battuta. Popular translations include "Killer of Hindus," based on Ibn Battuta's account of Indian slaves dying in the mountains. Scholars also propose it derives from "Hindu Koh" ("mountains of India") or ancient Avestan for "water mountain." Historically, the range was known in Vedic Sanskrit as *upariśaina*, by Greeks as *Caucasus Indicus*, and during the Mughal period as *Hindu Koh*.

### Geography and Geology
The range divides the Amu Darya watershed to the north from the Indus watershed to the south. Its highest point is Tirich Mir (7,708 m) in Pakistan. Geologically, it formed from the collision of the Indian and Eurasian plates and consists of metamorphic and intrusive rocks. The range is seismically active and is divided into western, central, and eastern sections, with the "High Hindu Kush" in the east containing peaks over 7,000 m. Key passes include the Salang Pass in Afghanistan and the Broghil and Lowari passes in Pakistan.

### Historical Significance
The Hindu Kush was a major center of Buddhism, home to sites like the Bamiyan Buddhas. It served as a conduit for invasions into the Indian subcontinent by figures like Alexander the Great, Mahmud of Ghazni, Timur, and Babur. The region saw the spread of Islam, with Kabul falling in 808 CE and the Hindu Shahi dynasty being conquered in the 10th century. The mountain passes were also integral to the slave trade between Central and South Asia for centuries. In the modern era, it was a barrier to the British Empire, a Cold War theatre, and remains a conflict zone involving the Taliban and al-Qaeda.

### Climate Change
The 2019 Hindu Kush Himalaya Assessment found the region has warmed by 0.1°C per decade since 1901, accelerating to 0.2°C per decade over the last 50 years. Glaciers have retreated since the 1970s, except in the Karakoram and eastern Pamir. Future projections under various warming scenarios indicate significant glacier loss (one-third to over two-thirds by 2100), increased monsoon precipitation, and a peak in glacier meltwater runoff around 2060 followed by decline. Adaptation efforts are underway but require urgent strengthening.

## Terms
- **Hindu Kush Himalayan Region (HKH):** The greater mountain system of which the Hindu Kush is the western section.
- **Amu Darya:** The river north of the Hindu Kush, historically known as the Oxus.
- **Indus River:** The major river system south of the Hindu Kush.
- **Kotal:** A term for a high mountain pass in the region.
- **Salang Pass:** The most important mountain pass in Afghanistan, linking its north and south.
- **Bamiyan Buddhas:** Giant rock-carved Buddhist statues in Afghanistan, destroyed in 2001.
- **Hindu Shahi:** A dynasty that controlled Kabul and the Hindu Kush region before the Islamic conquest.
- **Kafiristan:** A region in the Hindu Kush that retained polytheistic beliefs until its conversion to Islam in 1896.
- **Permafrost:** Ground that remains frozen for two or more consecutive years, vulnerable to climate change.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The exact origin and meaning of the name "Hindu Kush" is debated, with multiple scholarly theories.
- The precise impact of future climate change on regional water resources and glacier meltwater timing is uncertain due to complex topography and model limitations.
- The effectiveness and coordination of current adaptation efforts against climate change in the HKH region are questioned, with a need for urgent strengthening.

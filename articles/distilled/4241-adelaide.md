# Adelaide

## Overview
Adelaide is the capital and most populous city of South Australia, and the fifth-most populous city in Australia. Founded in 1836 as a planned colony for free settlers, it is renowned for its distinctive grid layout and surrounding parklands, cultural festivals, and high liveability rankings. The city is the traditional land of the Kaurna people.

## Body

### Foundation and Early History
Adelaide was founded in 1836 as the planned capital for a freely settled British colony, based on Edward Gibbon Wakefield's ideas. Unlike other Australian cities, it has no convict settlement history. Colonel William Light, the first surveyor-general, designed the city centre in a grid layout with wide boulevards and a ring of parklands, a design now listed as national heritage. The early colony faced economic uncertainty and leadership clashes, but by the 1840s was exporting wool, wheat, and wine. South Australia became a self-governing colony in 1856.

### 20th Century Development
Adelaide was Australia's third-largest city for most of the 20th century. World War II brought industrial stimulus, with the Playford Government promoting manufacturing. Post-war, international manufacturers like Holden and Chrysler established factories, transforming the city into a motor city. The Dunstan Governments of the 1970s oversaw a cultural revival and progressive social reforms, including the decriminalisation of homosexuality in 1975. The city's population was surpassed by Brisbane's in the early 1960s.

### 21st Century and Modern Adelaide
The 21st century has seen significant infrastructure investment, including upgrades to the Adelaide Oval and the construction of a new Royal Adelaide Hospital. Major events like the Adelaide Festival, Fringe, and WOMADelaide are now annual. The city hosts numerous sporting events, including the Tour Down Under cycling race and the Adelaide 500 motor race. Adelaide is consistently ranked among the world's most liveable cities and was named the world's second National Park City in 2021.

### Geography and Climate
Adelaide is situated on the Adelaide Plains between the Gulf St Vincent and the Mount Lofty Ranges. The metropolitan area covers 870 km². The city has a hot-summer Mediterranean climate (Csa) with hot, dry summers and cool, moderately wet winters. It is one of Australia's most seismically active regions, with its largest recorded earthquake (magnitude 5.6) occurring in 1954.

### Governance and Demography
Adelaide is the seat of the South Australian Government. The metropolitan area is divided among nineteen local government areas, with the City of Adelaide being the oldest municipal authority in Australia (est. 1840). In 2024, the metropolitan population was 1,469,163. The population is ageing more rapidly than other Australian capitals, with 27.5% aged 55 or older. About 31.3% of residents are overseas-born, with the largest groups from England, India, and China.

### Economy and Industry
Health care and social assistance is South Australia's largest employment sector. The city is a hub for defence technology, with major facilities near Edinburgh and Osborne. Adelaide was once the heart of Australia's automotive industry, though local manufacturing has ceased. The Adelaide Hills wine region is economically significant. The city is home to major companies like Santos and Coopers, and the Australian Space Agency.

### Culture and Education
Adelaide is known as the "City of Churches" and hosts a major annual Christmas pageant. Its arts scene is anchored by institutions on North Terrace, including the Art Gallery of South Australia and the South Australian Museum. The city is a UNESCO "City of Music" and has more live music venues per capita than any other southern hemisphere capital. Education is a key sector, with the University of Adelaide (founded 1874) and Flinders University being major institutions. Adelaide is the birthplace of three Nobel laureates.

### Infrastructure
Adelaide is a strategic transport hub with a metropolitan public transport system (Adelaide Metro) comprising buses, trains, and trams. The city has one freeway and four expressways. Adelaide Airport serves nearly 9 million passengers annually. Water is supplied from seven reservoirs and supplemented by the River Murray and a desalination plant. The energy market was privatised in 1999, and the state now has significant wind and solar power capacity.

## Terms
*   **Kaurna**: The traditional Aboriginal owners of the Adelaide region.
*   **Tarndanya**: The Kaurna name for the area of the city centre and surrounding Park Lands, meaning 'male red kangaroo rock'.
*   **Light's Vision**: The grid layout and parkland design for Adelaide created by Colonel William Light.
*   **Adelaide Parklands**: The ring of parks surrounding Adelaide's city centre, part of Light's original plan.
*   **SANFL**: The South Australian National Football League, the oldest surviving football league of any code in Australia.
*   **O-Bahn Busway**: A unique guided busway system in Adelaide's north-eastern suburbs.
*   **SACE**: The South Australian Certificate of Education, the qualification for Years 11 and 12 students.
*   **SAHMRI**: The South Australian Health and Medical Research Institute, a major medical research facility.
*   **Adelaide Metro**: The public transport system serving the Adelaide metropolitan area.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   The location chosen by Colonel Light for the city was initially unpopular with settlers and the first governor due to its distance from the harbour and lack of fresh water.
*   The privatisation of the state's electricity assets in 1999 was highly controversial and has been deemed a failure by some, leading to South Australia having the highest retail electricity prices in the country.
*   The proposed merger of the University of Adelaide and the University of South Australia failed in 2018 over issues of naming and leadership, though a successful merger was later announced in 2022.

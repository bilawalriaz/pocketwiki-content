# Sean Connery

## Overview
Sir Sean Connery (1930–2020) was a Scottish actor and producer whose portrayal of James Bond in seven films (1962–1983) defined the cinematic spy genre and launched a global franchise. Beyond Bond, he forged a respected character-actor career, winning an Academy Award for *The Untouchables* (1987) and earning acclaim for collaborations with directors Alfred Hitchcock, Sidney Lumet, John Huston, and Steven Spielberg. His trajectory—from Edinburgh milkman and Royal Navy seaman to international star and knighthood—exemplifies mid-century social mobility through popular culture. Connery’s Scottish nationalism shaped his politics and philanthropy, while his later tax residency and controversial statements on domestic violence complicated his public legacy.

## Timeline
- **25 Aug 1930** — Born in Edinburgh, Scotland, to a Catholic factory-worker father and Protestant cleaning-woman mother.
- **1946–1949** — Served in Royal Navy; discharged at 19 with duodenal ulcer.
- **1951–1953** — Worked as artist’s model; placed third in Mr. Universe bodybuilding contest (sources differ on year/class).
- **1953–1957** — Toured in *South Pacific*; studied Ibsen, Shaw, Shakespeare under mentor Robert Henderson.
- **1957** — First leading screen role: Mountain McLintock in BBC’s *Requiem for a Heavyweight*.
- **1962** — Cast as James Bond in *Dr. No*; line “Bond … James Bond” becomes cultural catchphrase.
- **1964–1965** — Starred in Hitchcock’s *Marnie* and Lumet’s *The Hill* (Cannes Best Screenplay).
- **1971** — Returned for *Diamonds Are Forever* after one-film absence (George Lazenby).
- **1983** — Final Bond appearance in non-Eon *Never Say Never Again*; wrist broken by fight choreographer Steven Seagal.
- **1986** — Won BAFTA Best Actor for *The Name of the Rose*; played mentor Ramírez in *Highlander*.
- **1987** — Won Oscar and Golden Globe for Best Supporting Actor (*The Untouchables*).
- **1989** — Played Henry Jones Sr. in *Indiana Jones and the Last Crusade*; script input praised by Harrison Ford.
- **5 Jul 2000** — Knighted by Queen Elizabeth II at Holyrood Palace after 1997/98 nominations reportedly vetoed.
- **8 Jun 2006** — Confirmed retirement at AFI Lifetime Achievement Award ceremony.
- **31 Oct 2020** — Died aged 90 in Nassau, Bahamas; cause: pneumonia and respiratory failure; dementia in final years.

## Body

### Early Life and Formative Jobs
Connery grew up in a Fountainbridge tenement (since demolished) of mixed Irish-Scottish heritage; his paternal great-grandfather was an Irish Traveller. After leaving school at 13, he delivered milk for St. Cuthbert’s Co-operative, a job he credited with his lifelong mental map of Edinburgh. Navy service (1946–49) gave him two tattoos—“Mum and Dad” and “Scotland Forever”—and a duodenal ulcer that ended his naval career. Post-discharge, he worked as lorry driver, lifeguard, coffin polisher, and artist’s model (15 shillings/hour at Edinburgh College of Art), where painter Richard Demarco called him “a virtual Adonis.” Bodybuilding (third in 1950/53 Mr. Universe) and a Manchester United trial (£25/week, declined at age 23) preceded his turn to acting.

### Stage Apprenticeship and Early Screen Work
A 1951 King’s Theatre backstage job led to *South Pacific* chorus work (1953), then featured roles and understudying. Mentor Robert Henderson, an American actor, lent him Ibsen, Proust, Tolstoy, and Shakespeare, and urged elocution lessons. Connery’s “hard man” reputation solidified after a 1954 confrontation with Edinburgh’s Valdor gang. Early TV included *Dixon of Dock Green* (1956) and BBC’s *Requiem for a Heavyweight* (1957), his first lead. Film debuts: *No Road Back* (1957), *Hell Drivers* (1957), *Action of the Tiger* (1957), *Time Lock* (1957). In *Another Time, Another Place* (1958), he disarmed Lana Turner’s gangster boyfriend Johnny Stompanato on set, prompting threats from Mickey Cohen’s associates. Disney’s *Darby O’Gill and the Little People* (1959) and BBC Shakespeare (*Hotspur* in *An Age of Kings*, Macbeth in 1961 CBC TV) rounded his pre-Bond résumé.

### James Bond: Casting, Stylization, and Stardom
Producer Albert “Cubby” Broccoli’s wife Dana championed Connery over Ian Fleming’s objections (“overgrown stunt-man”). Fleming relented after *Dr. No*’s success, retroactively giving Bond Scottish parentage in *You Only Live Twice* (1964). Director Terence Young tutored Connery in movement, diction, and dress—“how to walk, how to talk, even how to eat”—creating the “icily disdainful style” critic Peter Bradshaw highlights. The self-introduction “Bond … James Bond” became, per Bradshaw, “the most famous self-introduction … a kind of challenge, or seduction.” Five Eon films followed (1962–67), then *Diamonds Are Forever* (1971) and the non-Eon *Never Say Never Again* (1983). All seven were commercial hits; AFI later ranked Bond the third-greatest cinema hero. Connery grew to resent the role (“fed up to here … I’d like to kill him”), feeling typecast and unable to walk streets without being addressed as Bond.

### Non-Bond Range: 1960s–1980s
Connery leveraged Bond clout for auteur projects. *Marnie* (1964): he insisted on reading Hitchcock’s script (“I’m not Cary Grant”), earning the director’s respect. *The Hill* (1965, Lumet): a brutal military-prison drama winning Cannes Best Screenplay; Lumet called Connery’s charm-based performance “the equivalent of high comedy.” *The Bowler and the Bunnet* (1967): Connery directed/presented this documentary on the Fairfield Experiment, a Glasgow shipyard industrial-relations reform backed by George Brown (Wilson cabinet). *The Man Who Would Be King* (1975, Huston): co-starring Michael Caine, both actors’ favorite film. *Robin and Marian* (1976): Roger Ebert praised Connery–Hepburn chemistry (“They glow”). Ensemble work: *Murder on the Orient Express* (1974), *A Bridge Too Far* (1977). *Zardoz* (1974, Boorman): panned initially, later cult; Boorman noted Connery writing poetry on set. *Time Bandits* (1981, Gilliam): cast from a script joke (“Sean Connery—or someone of equal but cheaper stature”). *Outland* (1981): sci-fi marshal role.

### Late-Career Renaissance and Retirement
*The Name of the Rose* (1986, Annaud) won BAFTA Best Actor and revived commercial appeal. *The Untouchables* (1987, De Palma): Irish-American cop Jim Malone earned Oscar/Golden Globe; Ebert cited the “human element” Connery brought. *Indiana Jones and the Last Crusade* (1989, Spielberg): Henry Jones Sr. benefited from Connery’s writing-stage input (per Ford). Blockbusters followed: *The Hunt for Red October* (1990), *The Rock* (1996), *Entrapment* (1999). Voice role as Draco in *Dragonheart* (1996); cameo as Richard Lionheart in *Robin Hood: Prince of Thieves* (1991). Disappointments: *First Knight*, *Just Cause* (1995), *The Avengers* (1998), *The League of Extraordinary Gentlemen* (2003)—the last prompting retirement (“idiots now making films in Hollywood”). *Finding Forrester* (2000) drew strong reviews. Turned down Gandalf (*Lord of the Rings*—reported $30M + 15% gross), Dumbledore (*Harry Potter*), and the Architect (*Matrix*). Final screen work: voice in *From Russia with Love* video game (2005) and *Sir Billi* (2012, Scottish animation).

### Personal Life, Politics, and Tax Controversies
Married Diane Cilento (1962–73); son Jason Connery (actor). Cilento’s 2006 autobiography alleged mental/physical abuse; Connery’s 1965 *Playboy* and 1993 *Vanity Fair* quotes on hitting women were disavowed in 2006 (“no level of abuse … ever justified”). Married Micheline Roquebrune (1975–2020); affair with Lynsey de Paul (late 1980s). Residences: Domaine de Terre Blanche (France, 1979–99), Lyford Cay (Bahamas, 1990s–), Kranidi villa (Greece, neighbor King Willem-Alexander). Knighted 2000 after reported 1997/98 vetoes by Donald Dewar over SNP support. SNP member from 1967 (inspired by Fairfield shipyard filming); funded party until 2001 UK ban on overseas political donations. Tax-exile accusations: released docs showing £3.7M UK tax paid (1997–98, 2002–03); critics noted lower effective rate. Spanish authorities investigated 1999 Marbella villa sale (£5.5M alleged fraud); Connery cleared, wife and 16 others charged. Golf enthusiast (lessons from Peter Alliss for *Goldfinger*; played with Jack Nicklaus, Jackie Stewart). Football: Celtic F.C. youth, later Rangers F.C. (friendship with chairman David Murray).

### Death and Legacy
Died 31 Oct 2020, Nassau; cremated, ashes scattered in Scotland (2022). Tributes from across film industry: Bond successors (Lazenby, Dalton, Brosnan, Craig), co-stars (Ford, Costner, De Niro, Hedren), producers (Wilson, Broccoli: “revolutionized the world … largely responsible for the success of the film series”). Honors: “Greatest Living Scot” (2004 *Sunday Herald*), “Scotland’s Greatest Living National Treasure” (2011 EuroMillions), *People* “Sexiest Man Alive” (1989) and “Sexiest Man of the Century” (1999). Tied with Roger Moore at seven Bond portrayals (counting *Never Say Never Again*). 2024: Edinburgh International Film Festival launched £50,000 Sean Connery Prize for Feature Filmmaking Excellence (audience-voted from world-premiere shorts).

## Terms
- **Eon Productions**: The primary production company for the official James Bond film series, founded by Albert “Cubby” Broccoli and Harry Saltzman.
- **Duodenal ulcer**: A sore in the lining of the duodenum (first part of small intestine); hereditary in Connery’s family, caused his Navy discharge.
- **Typecasting**: The process by which an actor becomes strongly identified with a specific role, limiting perceived range; Connery fought this throughout his Bond years.
- **Fairfield Experiment**: A 1960s worker-participation industrial-relations program at Fairfield Shipbuilding, Glasgow, documented in Connery’s *The Bowler and the Bunnet*.
- **Non-Eon Bond film**: *Never Say Never Again* (1983), produced by Jack Schwartzman’s Taliafilm via Kevin McClory’s Thunderball rights, outside the official Eon canon.
- **Tax exile**: A person who relocates to a low-tax jurisdiction to reduce liability; Connery’s Bahamas residency triggered UK political and Spanish legal scrutiny.
- **Scottish National Party (SNP)**: Centre-left party advocating Scottish independence; Connery joined c. 1967, funded until 2001 legislative ban on overseas donations.
- **BAFTA Fellowship**: Lifetime-achievement award from the British Academy of Film and Television Arts (Connery, 1998).
- **Commander of the Order of Arts and Letters**: French state honor for contributions to arts/literature (Connery, 1987).
- **Kennedy Center Honors**: U.S. lifetime artistic-achievement award (Connery, 1999).

## Debates and open questions
- **Domestic-violence allegations**: Cilento’s memoir and Connery’s own 1965/1993 quotes vs. his 2006 disavowal; biographers and commentators remain divided on interpretation.
- **Tax-exile status**: Whether Connery’s UK tax payments (£3.7M over two discrete periods) constituted fair contribution given his earnings; Spanish investigation outcome (cleared personally, wife charged) leaves ambiguity.
- **Knighthood delay**: 1997/98 nominations reportedly blocked by Donald Dewar over SNP funding; no official confirmation, but widely reported in UK press.
- **Mr. Universe placement**: Official site claims 1950 third place; most sources cite 1953 (Junior class third or Tall Man unplaced); primary records unavailable.
- **Gandalf refusal**: Connery cited script incomprehension; reported $450M potential earnings (salary + gross points) makes this a notable “what if” in film history.
- **Bond portrayal count**: Whether *Never Say Never Again* “counts” for record-tying seven with Roger Moore; AFI and Guinness typically note the distinction (Eon vs. non-Eon).

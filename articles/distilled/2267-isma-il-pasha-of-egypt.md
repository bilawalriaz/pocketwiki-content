# Isma'il Pasha of Egypt

## Overview
Isma'il Pasha (1830–1895), known as Ismail the Magnificent, was the Khedive (Great Master/Sovereign Prince) of Egypt and Sudan from 1863 to 1879. He pursued ambitious modernization and territorial expansion, aiming to align Egypt with Europe, but his massive borrowing led to severe debt and foreign intervention, culminating in his deposition by the Ottoman Sultan under British and French pressure.

## Timeline
- **25 Nov or 31 Dec 1830** — Born in Cairo, grandson of Muhammad Ali Pasha.
- **19 Jan 1863** — Proclaimed Khedive after the death of his uncle Sa'id.
- **1867** — Secures Ottoman firman recognizing his title of Khedive and direct succession.
- **1869** — Suez Canal opens; lavish festival held.
- **1874** — Annexes Darfur.
- **16 Nov 1875** — Egyptian army defeated by Ethiopia at Battle of Gundet.
- **Mar 1876** — Second defeat by Ethiopia at Battle of Gura.
- **1875** — Sells Egypt's Suez Canal shares to Britain for £3,976,582.
- **1876** — Anglo-French control over Egyptian finances established.
- **26 Jun 1879** — Deposed by Ottoman Sultan Abdülhamid II; exiled.
- **2 Mar 1895** — Dies in Constantinople.

## Body

### Reforms and Modernization
Isma'il greatly modernized Egypt and Sudan, investing heavily in infrastructure and institutions. He built over 900 miles of railroads, 5,000 miles of telegraph lines, 8,000 miles of irrigation canals, and 4,500 schools. He remodeled the customs system, created a sugar industry, and expanded the cotton industry. A new quarter of Cairo was built on a Parisian model. Education budgets increased tenfold, and a national library was founded in 1871. In 1866, he established an advisory assembly of delegates, which gained influence over governmental affairs. He also replaced Ottoman Turkish with Egyptian Arabic as the language of administration in 1866.

### Territorial Expansion and Conflict with Ethiopia
Isma'il expanded Egypt's boundaries in Africa, annexing Darfur in 1874. His ambition to control the Nile basin led to conflict with Ethiopia under Emperor Yohannes IV. After the Ottoman cession of the Habesh Eyalet in 1865, Egyptian forces under Governor Werner Munzinger Pasha annexed Bogos in 1872. Attempts to occupy the Ethiopian highlands resulted in defeats at Gundet (1875) and Gura (1876), where Isma'il's son was captured. A "cold war" ensued until the 1884 Hewett Treaty returned Bogos to Ethiopia. The Red Sea Province he created was later taken by Italy, forming the basis for Eritrea. His jurisdiction also included the northern Somali coast until 1884.

### The Suez Canal and Financial Crisis
Isma'il's reign is closely tied to the Suez Canal. He renegotiated the original concession, leading to arbitration by Napoleon III. He oversaw the Egyptian portion of its construction and hosted a grand opening festival in 1869. However, his lavish spending, costly wars, and borrowing caused the national debt to soar from £3 million to over £100 million. In 1875, he sold Egypt's Suez Canal shares to Britain. This led to the establishment of the Caisse de la Dette and joint Anglo-French control over state finances. By 1878, he was forced to accept a constitutional role with European ministers in his government.

### Deposition and Exile
European financial control fueled the Urabi Revolt, a nationalist movement led by Colonel Ahmed Urabi. Isma'il, hoping to use the revolt to regain autonomy, did little to oppose it. When Britain and France demanded the reinstatement of their ministers in 1879, Isma'il refused. The Ottoman Sultan Abdülhamid II, pressured by Britain and France, deposed him on 26 June 1879. His son Tewfik Pasha succeeded him. Isma'il was exiled, first to Italy and then to Constantinople, where he died in 1895.

## Terms
- **Khedive**: A title (meaning "Great Master/Sovereign Prince") for the hereditary ruler of Egypt and Sudan, recognized by the Ottoman Empire in 1867.
- **Firman**: An official decree or order issued by the Ottoman Sultan.
- **Wāli**: The title of "Governor" used by Isma'il's predecessors, which he sought to elevate to Khedive.
- **Caisse de la Dette**: The "Debt Commission," an international body established to manage Egypt's public debt.
- **Urabi Revolt**: A nationalist uprising (1879–1882) led by Colonel Ahmed Urabi against monarchical rule and European financial control.
- **Hewett Treaty (1884)**: An agreement between Britain, Egypt, and Ethiopia that returned the Bogos region to Ethiopia.
- **Mixed Courts**: A judicial system where European residents were tried by judges from their own countries, not Egyptian courts.

## Debates and Open questions
- The exact date of Isma'il's birth is debated: 25 November or 31 December 1830.
- The primary cause of his deposition is framed as a result of British and French pressure due to financial insolvency and the Urabi Revolt, but the interplay between his personal ambition, nationalist sentiment, and European imperialism remains a central historical analysis point.

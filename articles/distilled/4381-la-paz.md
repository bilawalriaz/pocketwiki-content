# La Paz

## Overview
La Paz is the seat of government of Bolivia and the world's highest administrative capital, situated in a canyon at roughly 3,650 meters (11,975 ft) above sea level. Founded by the Spanish in 1548, it is a major political, economic, and cultural center, renowned for its dramatic topography, colonial history, and vibrant indigenous traditions. Its unique geography, with a population spread across a deep bowl-like depression, has shaped its social structure, climate, and transportation challenges.

## Timeline
- **20 October 1548** — Founded by Spanish conquistador Alonso de Mendoza at the Inca settlement of Laja.
- **1781** — Indigenous leader Túpac Katari lays siege to the city for six months.
- **16 July 1809** — Pedro Domingo Murillo leads a revolution, marking the start of South American independence wars.
- **1825** — City renamed "La Paz de Ayacucho" after a key republican victory.
- **1898** — La Paz becomes the de facto seat of national government, though Sucre remains the constitutional capital.
- **30 May 2014** — The first line of the Mi Teleférico cable car system opens.
- **2019** — The Gran Poder festival is declared an Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity by UNESCO.

## Body

### Geography and Climate
La Paz is set in a canyon created by the Choqueyapu River, surrounded by the high mountains of the Altiplano, with the snow-capped Illimani overlooking the city. Its extreme altitude creates a subtropical highland climate with rainy summers and dry winters. The city's topography directly influences its social geography: more affluent residents live in the lower, central areas, while lower-income residents live in the surrounding hills and the neighboring city of El Alto on the Altiplano.

### History
The area was an Inca trading route before the Spanish founded La Paz in 1548 to commemorate the end of civil wars in Peru. The city was a frequent site of indigenous revolts against colonial rule, most notably the 1781 siege by Túpac Katari. The 1809 revolution led by Pedro Domingo Murillo was a pivotal event in the Spanish American Wars of Independence. After Bolivian independence, La Paz's economic importance grew, leading it to become the de facto government seat in 1898, a shift reflecting the move of economic power from Potosí's silver mines to tin resources near Oruro.

### Government and Economy
As the seat of government, La Paz houses the presidential palace (Palacio Quemado) and the Plurinational Legislative Assembly, while Sucre retains judicial power. It is Bolivia's economic engine, generating 24% of the nation's GDP and hosting the headquarters of major companies and industries. The city's economy includes a large informal sector, evidenced by its numerous markets, and it is a principal center for manufacturing, particularly food, tobacco, and consumer goods.

### Culture and Tourism
La Paz is a cultural hub where Andean, colonial, and modern influences converge. It is renowned for its colonial architecture, such as the San Francisco Church and Metropolitan Cathedral, and its museums. The city is famous for its vibrant markets, including the Witches' Market, and festivals like the Gran Poder procession. Its gastronomy features traditional dishes like salteña and plato paceño, and its textile art, characterized by geometric designs and the use of the poncho and aguayo, reflects ancient Andean traditions.

### Transportation
Public transportation is vital, with 81.9% of residents using it. The system includes minibuses (micros), taxis, and the state-owned PumaKatari bus service. A major innovation is Mi Teleférico, the world's largest urban cable car network, which connects the canyon city of La Paz with the plateau city of El Alto, significantly reducing travel times. The city is served by El Alto International Airport, one of the highest commercial airports in the world.

## Terms
- **Altiplano**: A high plateau in the Andes Mountains where La Paz is located.
- **Choqueyapu River**: The river that created the canyon in which La Paz is built.
- **Illimani**: The triple-peaked, snow-covered mountain that overlooks La Paz.
- **Palacio Quemado**: The historic presidential palace of Bolivia in La Paz, meaning "Burnt Palace."
- **Plurinational Legislative Assembly**: The bicameral legislature of Bolivia, seated in La Paz.
- **Cholita**: A term for women of Andean indigenous background; they faced segregation in La Paz until the 1980s.
- **Mi Teleférico**: The state-owned company operating La Paz's extensive urban cable car system.
- **Gran Poder**: An annual religious and folkloric procession in La Paz, declared a UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage.
- **Ekeko**: A pre-Columbian god of abundance, central to the Alasitas fair in La Paz.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The city's name is a subject of cultural debate. In 2017, former President Evo Morales suggested renaming it to its Aymara name, "Chuquiago Marka."
- The future of La Paz's colonial architecture is uncertain due to a lack of funds for restoration, leading to the demolition or decay of historic buildings.
- The city's water supply is threatened by climate change-induced glacier melt, with studies indicating the El Alto water system is particularly vulnerable.

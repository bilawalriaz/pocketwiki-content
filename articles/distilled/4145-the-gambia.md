# The Gambia

## Overview
The Gambia is a small West African nation whose territory is defined by the Gambia River, making it the smallest country on the African mainland. Its history is marked by early trans-Saharan trade, European colonization, and a post-independence political journey that includes a long period of democracy, a military coup, and a recent return to civilian rule following a constitutional crisis.

## Timeline
- **9th–10th centuries** — Arab traders introduce Islam and establish trans-Saharan trade routes.
- **1455** — Portuguese become the first Europeans to enter the region.
- **1765** — British Empire establishes a colony.
- **18 February 1965** — The Gambia gains independence from the UK.
- **24 April 1970** — The Gambia becomes a republic within the Commonwealth.
- **22 July 1994** — Yahya Jammeh overthrows President Dawda Jawara in a bloodless coup.
- **1 December 2016** — Adama Barrow wins the presidential election, defeating Jammeh.
- **January 2017** — ECOWAS military intervention leads to Jammeh's removal; Barrow is sworn in.
- **8 February 2018** — The Gambia officially rejoins the Commonwealth of Nations.
- **4 December 2021** — Adama Barrow wins re-election.

## Body

### Etymology and Geography
The name "Gambia" derives from the Mandinka term for the river. The country's elongated shape mirrors the river's lower reaches, with borders defined in 1889 by an Anglo-French agreement. It is surrounded by Senegal on three sides and the Atlantic Ocean to the west, with a total area of 11,300 square kilometres. The climate is tropical savannah, with a rainy season from June to September.

### History: Trade, Colonization, and Independence
Arab Muslim merchants established trans-Saharan trade networks in the region by the 9th century, introducing Islam. The Portuguese arrived by sea in the mid-15th century, but the British later established control, founding the colony of Bathurst (now Banjul) in 1816. Formal boundaries with French-controlled territories were set in 1889. The Gambia gained independence in 1965 under Prime Minister Dawda Jawara and became a republic in 1970. Jawara's government was overthrown in a 1994 coup led by Yahya Jammeh, who ruled for 22 years.

### Contemporary Politics and the 2016 Crisis
Jammeh's rule was characterized by authoritarianism and human rights abuses. In the 2016 presidential election, he was defeated by a coalition-backed candidate, Adama Barrow. Jammeh initially accepted the results but then refused to leave office, triggering a constitutional crisis. The Economic Community of West African States (ECOWAS) conducted a military intervention, and Jammeh was removed in January 2017. Barrow's government has since worked to restore democratic institutions, and The Gambia rejoined the Commonwealth in 2018.

### Economy and Society
The economy is based on agriculture (especially groundnuts), fishing, and tourism. In 2022, 17.2% of the population lived in extreme poverty. English is the sole official language, but Mandinka, Wolof, and Fula are widely spoken. The population is predominantly Sunni Muslim (96%), with a Christian minority (4%). The Gambia is a founding member of ECOWAS and an active contributor to UN peacekeeping missions.

## Terms
- **Trans-Saharan trade:** Historic trade networks crossing the Sahara Desert, linking North Africa with West Africa.
- **Protectorate:** A territory under the control of a foreign power that maintains local rulers for administration.
- **Commonwealth of Nations:** A voluntary association of 56 independent countries, mostly former territories of the British Empire.
- **ECOWAS:** The Economic Community of West African States, a regional political and economic union.
- **Senegambia Confederation:** A 1982 political union between The Gambia and Senegal, dissolved by The Gambia in 1989.
- **AFPRC:** The Armed Forces Provisional Ruling Council, the military junta that ruled The Gambia from 1994 to 1996.
- **Groundnut:** A legume crop (peanut) that is a primary agricultural export for The Gambia.

## Debates and open questions
- The precise origin of the name "Gambia" is debated, with possible derivations from Mandinka or Serer languages.
- The 1996 constitutional reform process, which enabled Yahya Jammeh's long rule, is noted as having been manipulated to benefit him.
- The long-term success of The Gambia's democratic transition under President Barrow remains uncertain, with challenges including ethnic tensions, restoring public trust, and the need for constitutional reforms like term limits.

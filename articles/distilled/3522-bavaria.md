# Bavaria

## Overview
Bavaria is Germany's largest state by area and second-most populous, known for its distinct cultural identity, economic strength, and conservative politics. Its history spans from Celtic settlements to a powerful duchy and kingdom within the Holy Roman Empire, before becoming a modern federal state. Bavaria's unique traditions, Catholic heritage, and economic prowess, particularly in automotive and technology industries, make it a significant region within Germany and Europe.

## Timeline
- **c. 450 BC** — Celtic Iron Age settlement founded at Feldmoching-Hasenbergl.
- **8–5 BC** — Roman military camp built, later becoming Augusta Vindelicorum (Augsburg).
- **c. 555 AD** — Duchy of Bavaria created within Francia.
- **1180** — Wittelsbach family begins ruling Bavaria after Henry the Lion's deposition.
- **1623** — Bavaria gains Elector status in the Holy Roman Empire during the Thirty Years' War.
- **1806** — Bavaria becomes a kingdom under Napoleon.
- **1871** — Bavaria joins the German Empire, retaining some special rights.
- **1918** — Bavarian monarchy falls; the Free State of Bavaria is proclaimed.
- **1919** — Bamberg Constitution establishes Bavaria within the Weimar Republic.
- **1945** — Modern Bavarian state is established after World War II.
- **1949** — Bavaria becomes part of West Germany (Federal Republic of Germany).
- **2023** — State election sees CSU maintain power in coalition with Free Voters.

## Body

### History
Bavaria's history begins with Celtic tribes in the Iron Age, followed by Roman rule as part of the provinces Raetia and Noricum. After the Roman collapse, the Duchy of Bavaria was established in Francia around 555 AD. The Agilolfing dynasty ruled until 788, when Charlemagne deposed Tassilo III. The Wittelsbach family took control in 1180 and ruled for over 700 years. Bavaria gained Elector status in 1623 and became a kingdom in 1806. It joined the German Empire in 1871 but retained autonomy in some areas. The monarchy ended in 1918, leading to a turbulent period that included the short-lived Bavarian Soviet Republic. The modern Free State was founded in 1945 and joined West Germany in 1949, despite initial parliamentary objections to the Basic Law.

### Geography and Climate
Bavaria is landlocked, bordering Austria, the Czech Republic, Switzerland, and several German states. Major rivers include the Danube and Main. The climate varies from temperate at lower elevations to alpine at higher altitudes. Recent decades have seen warming trends, with hotter summers, more rain in winter, and the near-disappearance of most Alpine glaciers.

### Politics and Government
Bavaria is a parliamentary democracy with a unicameral Landtag (State Parliament). It is dominated by the conservative Christian Social Union (CSU), which has governed almost continuously since 1945. The state has a tradition of direct democracy at the local level. The government is led by a Minister-President and consists of several ministries. Bavaria uniquely calls itself a "Free State," a term with no legal distinction from "State" under German law.

### Economy
Bavaria has one of Europe's largest economies, with a high GDP and low unemployment. Key industries include automotive manufacturing (home to BMW and Audi), technology (Siemens, Allianz), and tourism. It is Germany's most visited state, with Oktoberfest being a major attraction. The state has strong economic ties with neighboring countries.

### Culture and Society
Bavarian culture is heavily influenced by its Catholic heritage, especially in the Altbayern region. Traditions include folk music, festivals, and traditional dress (Lederhosen and Dirndl). The cuisine features distinctive items like Weißwurst and beer brewed under the historic Reinheitsgebot purity law. Three main German dialects are spoken: Austro-Bavarian, Swabian, and East Franconian. The state is home to numerous museums, with collections originating from the Wittelsbach dynasty.

## Terms
- **Landtag**: The unicameral state parliament of Bavaria.
- **Regierungsbezirk**: One of Bavaria's seven administrative regions, each with a district government.
- **CSU (Christian Social Union)**: The conservative political party that has dominated Bavarian politics since 1945.
- **Reinheitsgebot**: The historic German beer purity law, established in Bavaria in 1516.
- **Tracht**: Traditional Bavarian costumes, including Lederhosen for men and Dirndl for women.
- **Altbayern**: The historical regions forming the core of Bavaria before 1806, distinct from Franconia and Swabia.
- **Freistaat**: The German term "Free State," used by Bavaria, Saxony, and Thuringia with no legal difference from "State."
- **Bezirk**: A self-governing regional corporation in Bavaria, territorially identical to a Regierungsbezirk.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Bavarian Identity**: There is an ongoing discussion about the strength of a separate Bavarian identity versus a German one, with some emphasizing "Bavarians first, Germans second."
- **Cultural Uniformity**: Bavaria is not culturally uniform; the regions of Altbayern, Franconia, and Swabia have distinct dialects and traditions.
- **Political Dominance**: The long-standing dominance of the CSU is a subject of political analysis, as is its relationship with the national CDU.
- **Human Rights Legislation**: A 2017 law allowing preventive detention has been criticized by some as violating human rights conventions.

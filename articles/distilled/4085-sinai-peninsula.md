# Sinai Peninsula

## Overview
The Sinai Peninsula is a triangular land bridge between Africa and Asia, forming the only part of Egypt located in West Asia. It has been integral to Egypt since the First Dynasty (c. 3100 BC) and has served as a strategic corridor for trade, mining, and military campaigns. Its modern significance stems from its biblical associations, its role in 20th-century Arab-Israeli conflicts, and its current status as a tourist destination.

## Timeline
- **c. 3100 BC** — Part of Egypt from the First Dynasty.
- **c. 5th–4th millennium BCE** — Chalcolithic cave paintings discovered north of Mount Catherine.
- **106 AD** — Conquered by Rome, becoming part of the province of Arabia Petraea.
- **527–565 AD** — Saint Catherine's Monastery constructed by Emperor Justinian.
- **1517** — Incorporated into the Ottoman Empire.
- **1882** — Falls under British control following the Anglo-Egyptian War.
- **1906** — Ottoman administration formally transferred to British-controlled Egypt.
- **Oct 1956** — Invaded by Israel, UK, and France during the Suez Crisis.
- **May 1967** — Egypt orders UNEF out; Israel occupies the entire peninsula in the Six-Day War.
- **Oct 1973** — Egypt launches the Yom Kippur War to retake Sinai.
- **1979** — Egypt-Israel peace treaty signed.
- **1982** — Israel completes withdrawal from Sinai, except Taba (returned 1989).

## Body

### Geography and Administration
Sinai is a triangular peninsula bordered by the Mediterranean Sea, the Gulf of Suez, and the Gulf of Aqaba. It is linked to Africa by the Isthmus of Suez (125 km wide) and to Asia by an isthmus about 200 km wide. The peninsula is arid, mountainous, and sparsely populated inland, with a cold climate due to high altitudes. Administratively, the majority is divided into the North and South Sinai Governorates. Three other governorates (Suez, Ismailia, Port Said) span the Suez Canal into African Egypt. The largest city is Arish.

### Ancient History and Name
The peninsula was a key source of turquoise for ancient Egyptians, who called it "Mining Country." The modern name "Sinai" may derive from the Mesopotamian moon-god Sin or the Hebrew word for "burning bush." It was part of the Persian province of Abar-Nahara after the conquest by Cambyses. Under Roman rule, it became part of Arabia Petraea. The fortress Tjaru in western Sinai was a place of banishment, connected to Canaan by the Way of Horus.

### Modern Conflicts and Peace
Sinai's strategic location made it a focal point in Arab-Israeli wars. After the 1956 Suez Crisis, UNEF was stationed there until 1967. Israel occupied the entire peninsula in the Six-Day War, leading to the War of Attrition (1967–70). Egypt's 1973 Yom Kippur War offensive regained territory east of the Suez Canal. The 1979 peace treaty led to a full Israeli withdrawal by 1982, with the peninsula demilitarized into zones monitored by the Multinational Force and Observers (MFO).

### Contemporary Issues and Economy
Since the early 2000s, Sinai has faced security challenges, including terrorist attacks against tourists motivated by Bedouin poverty and the Sinai insurgency post-2011. Kidnapping of refugees, particularly Eritreans, has also been reported. The economy relies heavily on tourism, centered on sites like Mount Sinai, Saint Catherine's Monastery, and Red Sea resorts. Agriculture includes cactus cultivation, and dromedary herding is important.

## Terms
- **Land bridge**: A narrow connection of land between two larger landmasses, here linking Africa and Asia.
- **Isthmus**: A narrow strip of land connecting two larger areas, like the Isthmus of Suez.
- **Chalcolithic**: The Copper Age, a period of prehistory between the Neolithic and Bronze Age.
- **Arabia Petraea**: The Roman province encompassing the Sinai Peninsula and surrounding regions.
- **UNEF (United Nations Emergency Force)**: A peacekeeping force deployed in Sinai after the 1956 war.
- **Bar Lev Line**: Israel's defensive line along the Suez Canal, breached by Egypt in 1973.
- **MFO (Multinational Force and Observers)**: An international peacekeeping force monitoring the Egypt-Israel peace treaty.
- **Bedouin**: Semi-nomadic Arab people traditionally inhabiting desert regions of North Africa and the Middle East.

## Debates and Open questions
The origin of the name "Sinai" is a source of contention, with theories linking it to the Mesopotamian moon-god Sin or the Hebrew word for "burning bush." The exact location of the Biblical Mount Sinai is also debated, with the traditional site near Saint Catherine's Monastery being one candidate.

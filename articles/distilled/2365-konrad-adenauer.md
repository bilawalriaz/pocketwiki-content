# Konrad Adenauer

## Overview
Konrad Adenauer (1876–1967) was the first Chancellor of West Germany (1949–1963). As leader of the Christian Democratic Union (CDU), he established the Federal Republic as a stable, democratic, and prosperous state integrated with the West. His leadership shaped post-war foreign policy, reconciliation with former enemies, and early European integration.

## Timeline
- **5 January 1876** — Born in Cologne, Prussia.
- **1917–1933** — Serves as Mayor of Cologne.
- **April 1933** — Dismissed as mayor by the Nazi Party.
- **December 1945** — Dismissed as mayor again by British authorities.
- **January 1946** — Becomes informal leader of the new CDU.
- **15 September 1949** — Elected first Chancellor of West Germany.
- **18 April 1951** — Signs Treaty of Paris, establishing the ECSC.
- **27 March 1952** — Survives an assassination attempt; a police officer is killed.
- **May 1955** — West Germany joins NATO; Bundeswehr founded in November.
- **January 1963** — Signs Élysée Treaty with France.
- **16 October 1963** — Resigns as Chancellor; succeeded by Ludwig Erhard.
- **19 April 1967** — Dies in Rhöndorf, West Germany.

## Body

### Early Life and Pre-War Career
Born into a devout Catholic family in Cologne, Adenauer's youth was shaped by the *Kulturkampf*, fostering a lifelong resentment of Prussianism. He studied law and joined the Centre Party in 1906. As Mayor of Cologne (1917–1933), he oversaw major infrastructure projects and maintained order during the post-WWI revolution. He advocated for an autonomous Rhenish state to prevent French annexation, a plan rejected by Berlin. During the Weimar Republic, he served as president of the Prussian State Council.

### Under Nazi Rule and World War II
Adenauer was a staunch opponent of the Nazi Party. He was dismissed as mayor in April 1933, had his assets frozen, and lived in hiding and poverty. He was briefly imprisoned after the Night of the Long Knives (1934) and again after the July 1944 plot against Hitler. He survived the war with the help of friends and a former communist municipal worker who saved him from a deportation list.

### Founding the CDU and Becoming Chancellor
After WWII, Adenauer was reinstalled as Cologne's mayor by the Americans but was dismissed by the British in December 1945. This freed him to build a new, interdenominational party—the CDU—which he saw as essential to prevent the return of anti-democratic forces. He became the CDU's leader and chairman of the Parliamentary Council that drafted West Germany's Basic Law (constitution). In 1949, at age 73, he was elected Chancellor by a single-vote margin.

### Domestic Policy and the "Economic Miracle"
Adenauer's government shifted focus from denazification to economic recovery and integration. He pushed through amnesty laws for former Nazis to foster social cohesion, while also initiating reparations (*Wiedergutmachung*) for victims of Nazi persecution, including a landmark agreement with Israel. Under his leadership, and with economics minister Ludwig Erhard, West Germany experienced the *Wirtschaftswunder* ("economic miracle"), a period of rapid growth, rising living standards, and the establishment of a "social market economy" with a robust welfare state.

### Foreign Policy and European Integration
Adenauer's foreign policy was defined by a firm Atlanticist orientation and close ties with the West, particularly France, the UK, and the US. He was a driving force behind European integration, signing the Treaty of Rome in 1957 to found the European Economic Community. He refused to recognize East Germany or the Oder–Neisse line as Poland's border, prioritizing Western alignment over immediate reunification. He successfully negotiated West German rearmament and NATO membership in 1955, and later signed the Élysée Treaty with France in 1963.

### Final Years and Legacy
Adenauer's later years were marked by the Berlin Wall crisis of 1961 and the *Spiegel* affair, a press freedom scandal that damaged his government. He fulfilled a promise to resign in 1963, handing power to Erhard. He remained CDU chairman until 1966. Adenauer is remembered as the architect of West Germany's democratic stability, economic recovery, and reintegration into the international community. He was voted the "greatest German of all time" in a 2003 television poll.

## Terms
*   **Christian Democratic Union (CDU):** The interdenominational, Christian democratic party founded after WWII, which Adenauer led and which dominated West German politics for decades.
*   **Basic Law:** The constitution of West Germany, promulgated in 1949, which served as the legal foundation for the Federal Republic.
*   **Wirtschaftswunder:** The "economic miracle" of rapid growth and rising prosperity in West Germany during the 1950s and early 1960s.
*   **Social Market Economy:** An economic model combining a free-market capitalist system with social welfare policies and state regulation.
*   **European Coal and Steel Community (ECSC):** The first international organization based on supranational principles, established by the Treaty of Paris in 1951, a precursor to the EU.
*   **Bundeswehr:** The unified military of West Germany, established in 1955 after its integration into NATO.
*   **Oder–Neisse line:** The post-WWII border between Germany and Poland, which Adenauer's government refused to formally recognize.
*   **Wiedergutmachung:** The German term for the process of restitution and compensation for victims of Nazi persecution.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Reunification vs. Western Integration:** A central debate concerns whether Adenauer's firm policy of integrating West Germany with the West (*Westbindung*) permanently cemented Germany's division, or whether it was the only realistic strategy given Soviet power. Critics argue he missed opportunities for reunification, such as with the 1952 Stalin Note, while supporters contend his "magnet theory"—that a prosperous, democratic West would eventually attract the East—was the correct long-term strategy.
*   **Denazification and Amnesty:** Adenauer's policy of ending denazification and granting amnesties to former Nazis to achieve social stability and enable rearmament remains controversial. It is debated whether this was a pragmatic necessity for building a functioning state or a moral compromise that allowed many perpetrators to escape justice.
*   **Relationship with the United States:** Scholars analyze the complex and sometimes strained relationship between Adenauer and U.S. presidents, particularly John F. Kennedy. Adenauer's deep distrust of Kennedy's commitment to Berlin and his perceived naiveté hampered effective Western coordination during the Berlin crisis of 1961.

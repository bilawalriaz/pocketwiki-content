# B. F. Skinner

## Overview  
Burrhus Frederic Skinner (1904–1990) was an American psychologist, behaviorist, and inventor who developed radical behaviorism and the experimental analysis of behavior. His work on operant conditioning, reinforcement schedules, and verbal behavior fundamentally shaped psychology, education, and social theory.

## Body  

### Behaviorism and Operant Conditioning  
Skinner developed **radical behaviorism**, which holds that behavior is shaped by environmental consequences rather than internal mental states. He distinguished **respondent behaviors** (elicited by stimuli, modified via classical conditioning) from **operant behaviors** (emitted voluntarily, strengthened via operant conditioning). Operant responses increase in frequency when followed by reinforcement. Skinner emphasized measuring response rate as the most reliable indicator of behavioral strength.

### Foundations of Behavior Theory  
In *The Behavior of Organisms* (1938), Skinner addressed three core questions:  
1. **Origin of operant behavior**: Behavior varies naturally; variations reinforced become part of the repertoire—a process he called **shaping**.  
2. **Control of behavior**: A **discriminative stimulus** signals that a response will be reinforced, forming a three-term contingency (stimulus-response-reinforcer).  
3. **Complex behavior**: Explained through **chaining** (linking simple responses via conditioned reinforcers) and **rule-governed behavior** (verbal stimuli evoking complex responses).

### Reinforcement and Schedules  
Reinforcement strengthens behavior. **Positive reinforcement** adds a stimulus (e.g., praise); **negative reinforcement** removes an aversive one (e.g., stopping rain by opening an umbrella). Punishment suppresses behavior but has lasting negative effects. Skinner and Ferster analyzed **schedules of reinforcement**: continuous, fixed/variable interval, and fixed/variable ratio—each producing distinct response patterns. These principles were applied in **token economies** in institutions.

### Verbal Behavior  
Skinner extended behaviorist analysis to language in *Verbal Behavior* (1957), arguing that speech is governed by the same principles of operant conditioning. He categorized verbal behavior by function (e.g., mands, tacts). Noam Chomsky’s 1959 critique challenged this view, sparking the cognitive revolution. Skinner did not formally respond.

### Inventions and Applications  
- **Operant conditioning chamber (Skinner box)**: Controlled environment for studying animal learning.  
- **Cumulative recorder**: Device measuring response rates graphically.  
- **Air crib**: Temperature-controlled infant bed; controversial due to public misunderstanding.  
- **Teaching machine**: Mechanical device for programmed instruction, promoting active learning.  
- **Pigeon-guided missile (Project Pigeon/ORCON)**: WWII attempt to use operant-conditioned pigeons for missile guidance; ultimately abandoned.

### Social Theory and Politics  
In *Walden Two* (1948) and *Beyond Freedom and Dignity* (1971), Skinner envisioned applying behavioral science to create a more humane society. He advocated for positive reinforcement over punishment in governance and education, opposing corporal punishment and rote learning. His ideas influenced educational practices like the Personalized System of Instruction.

## Terms  
- **Radical behaviorism**: Approach focusing solely on observable behavior and environmental influences, rejecting internal mental states as causes.  
- **Operant conditioning**: Learning process where behavior is modified by its consequences (reinforcement or punishment).  
- **Discriminative stimulus**: Environmental cue signaling that a response will lead to reinforcement.  
- **Shaping**: Gradual modification of behavior by reinforcing successive approximations toward a target behavior.  
- **Rule-governed behavior**: Behavior controlled by verbal rules or instructions rather than direct reinforcement.  
- **Cumulative recorder**: Instrument that records response frequency as a continuously rising line on paper.  
- **Token economy**: System where desired behaviors are reinforced with tokens exchangeable for rewards.  
- **Three-term contingency**: Relationship between discriminative stimulus, response, and consequence.  
- **Extinction**: Weakening of behavior when reinforcement is withheld.  
- **Superstitious behavior**: Behavior maintained by coincidental reinforcement, not causal relationship.

## Debates and Open Questions  
- **Chomsky vs. Skinner on language**: Chomsky argued that human language cannot be explained by conditioning alone, citing creativity and recursion. Behavior analysts counter that Skinner’s model was misrepresented and remains empirically viable.  
- **Project Pigeon legacy**: Though technically successful, the pigeon-guided missile was dismissed due to skepticism and preference for conventional technology.  
- **Air crib controversy**: Public backlash stemmed from conflation with laboratory animal research; Skinner denied using his daughter in experiments.  
- **Ethical concerns**: Critics question whether applying operant principles to humans risks manipulation, especially in education and social policy.  
- **Superstition experiment reinterpretation**: Later studies suggest pigeons’ behaviors may reflect classical conditioning rather than adventitious reinforcement, complicating Skinner’s original interpretation.

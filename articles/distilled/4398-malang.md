# Malang

## Overview
Malang is a major city in East Java, Indonesia, with a history stretching back to the ancient Hindu-Buddhist kingdoms of Kanjuruhan and Singhasari. Known for its mild climate, it became a popular colonial retreat and is now a significant educational and cultural center, recognized for its preserved historical heritage and diverse population.

## Timeline
- **760 AD** — The Dinoyo inscription marks the recorded beginning of Malang's government under the Hindu kingdom of Kanjuruhan.
- **1222 AD** — Ken Arok wins the Battle of Ganter, establishing the Kingdom of Tumapel (later Singhasari) with its capital in the Malang region.
- **1292 AD** — The Singhasari kingdom falls to Jayakatwang of Kediri after King Kertanegara is killed.
- **1359 AD** — Majapahit King Hayam Wuruk visits Malang, touring parks and pilgrimage sites like Singhasari Temple.
- **1527 AD** — The Sengguruh Kingdom, a Majapahit successor state, is established in Malang under Arya Terung.
- **1879 AD** — Malang is connected to Java's railroad network, spurring modern development.
- **1914 AD** — Malang is officially designated a *gemeente* (city) under Dutch colonial administration.
- **1942 AD** — The Imperial Japanese Army occupies Malang.
- **1945 AD** — Malang becomes part of the newly independent Republic of Indonesia.
- **2001 AD** — Malang's government is restructured into the current Malang City Government.

## Body

### Early History and Hindu Kingdoms
The Malang basin was initially a volcanic landscape that formed ancient lakes. Human settlement began on surrounding mountain slopes during the Paleolithic era, moving to the dried basin in the Holocene period. The first recorded kingdom was Kanjuruhan, documented by the 760 AD Dinoyo inscription. This area later became part of the Mataram Kingdom and then the Kahuripan Kingdom under King Airlangga. After Airlangga divided his realm, Malang fell within the territory of Jenggala and later Panjalu (Kediri).

### The Singhasari and Majapahit Empires
The collapse of Panjalu led to the rise of the Singhasari Kingdom in Malang. Founded by Ken Arok after his victory at the Battle of Ganter in 1222 AD, its capital was initially Tumapel (Kutaraja). The capital later moved to Singosari in 1254 AD. Singhasari fell in 1292 AD to a rebellion by Jayakatwang of Kediri. The subsequent Majapahit Empire made the Malang region (Tumapel) a provincial territory. King Hayam Wuruk's 1359 visit highlights its continued importance for recreation and ancestral pilgrimage.

### Later Kingdoms and Colonial Era
After Majapahit's decline, the Hindu Sengguruh Kingdom emerged in Malang, led by descendants of Majapahit royalty. Its ruler, Arya Terung, eventually adopted Islam and aligned with the Demak Sultanate. The Dutch colonized the area, modernizing it and exploiting its cool climate for European settlements. The city was connected to the railroad in 1879 and formally established as a city in 1914. During the Japanese occupation (1942-1945), colonial buildings were repurposed.

### Modern City and Demographics
Post-independence, Malang grew into an educational hub, hosting major universities like Brawijaya and Malang State University. Its population is predominantly Javanese (Arekan culture), with Madurese and Chinese minorities. The city is a center for religious education, with numerous Islamic and Christian institutions. Economically, it is East Java's third-largest city, with trade, services, and tourism as key sectors. It faces challenges like urban congestion and informal settlements.

## Terms
- **Kecamatan**: An administrative district within an Indonesian city or regency.
- **Kelurahan**: An administrative village or urban sub-district, the lowest level of urban governance.
- **Gemeente**: A Dutch colonial term for a municipality or city, used in the East Indies.
- **Sima**: A term from ancient Javanese inscriptions for a tax-exempt civil village or estate.
- **Watak**: A historical administrative region or district, one level below a kingdom's central authority.
- **Arekan**: The cultural and dialectal identity of the Javanese people in the Malang and Surabaya regions, noted for its egalitarian and straightforward character.
- **Boso Walikan**: A local language game in Malang involving the reversal of word pronunciations (e.g., "Malang" becomes "Ngalam").
- **Pesantren**: An Islamic boarding school, common in Indonesia.
- **Adipura**: An Indonesian award given to cities for clean environmental management and urban parks.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The exact location of the legendary temple **Malangkuçeçwara**, from which one theory derives the city's name, is debated between the Mount Buring area and the Tumpang area.
- The origin of the name "Malang" is uncertain, with competing theories linking it to the Malangkuçeçwara temple or to a Javanese word meaning "to block" from a 1614 Mataram conquest story.
- There is scholarly debate over whether **Singhasari Temple** was built during the Singhasari or the subsequent Majapahit kingdom, as it was intended to honor the last Singhasari king.

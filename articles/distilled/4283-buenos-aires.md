# Buenos Aires

## Overview
Buenos Aires is the capital and largest city of Argentina, located on the southwest of the Río de la Plata. It is a major global city and cultural hub, known for its European-influenced architecture, rich cultural life, and history as a destination for millions of immigrants, making it a diverse "melting pot." As an autonomous federal district, it serves as the country's financial, industrial, and commercial center.

## Timeline
- **1536** — First settlement founded by Pedro de Mendoza; abandoned in 1541.
- **1580** — Permanent settlement established by Juan de Garay.
- **1880** — Buenos Aires federalized and split from Buenos Aires Province after the Argentine Civil War.
- **1913** — Buenos Aires Underground (Subte) opens, the oldest in the Southern Hemisphere.
- **1945** — Peronism emerges as a political force with a demonstration at Plaza de Mayo.
- **1976-1983** — Military dictatorship; "Dirty War" results in thousands of *desaparecidos*.
- **1994** — Constitutional amendment grants the city autonomy.
- **1996** — First elected Chief of Government (mayor) takes office.
- **2010** — Argentina legalizes same-sex marriage, a first in Latin America.
- **2018** — Hosts the Summer Youth Olympics.

## Body

### Etymology and Foundation
The name "Buenos Aires" derives from "Santa María de los Buenos Aires" (Holy Mary of the Good Winds), a devotion brought by Spanish sailors. The first settlement, founded in 1536, was abandoned due to indigenous attacks. The permanent city was established in 1580 by Juan de Garay.

### Political History
Buenos Aires has been central to Argentina's political conflicts. Its liberal, trade-oriented views often clashed with the more nationalistic provinces. The city was federalized in 1880, with its mayor appointed by the president. A 1994 constitutional amendment granted it autonomy, leading to the first mayoral election in 1996. The city endured blockades in the 19th century and was the site of key events like the May Revolution (1810), the rise of Peronism (1945), and the "Dirty War" (1976-1983).

### Demographics and Society
Buenos Aires is a multicultural city shaped by massive European immigration, primarily from Spain and Italy, in the late 19th and early 20th centuries. This created a diverse population with a distinct dialect, Rioplatense Spanish, influenced by Italian prosody. The city also has significant Jewish, Chinese, and Korean communities. Its population has remained around 3 million since 1947, while the surrounding metropolitan area has grown to over 16 million.

### Economy and Infrastructure
The city is Argentina's economic powerhouse, with a high Human Development Index. Its economy is dominated by the services sector, particularly finance and real estate. Manufacturing remains significant in the southern districts. Buenos Aires has an extensive transportation network, including the historic Subte, a vast commuter rail system, and the port of Buenos Aires, one of South America's busiest.

### Culture and Arts
Known as the "Paris of South America," Buenos Aires boasts a vibrant cultural scene. It is the birthplace of tango, a UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage. The city has more bookstores per capita than any other major city and hosts the largest annual book fair in the Spanish-speaking world. Its architecture is an eclectic mix of European styles, and it has a thriving theater district, with more theaters than any other city globally.

## Terms
- **Autonomous City:** A federal district with its own constitution and elected government, separate from the surrounding province.
- **Criollos:** People of Spanish descent born in the Americas, as opposed to *peninsulares* (those born in Spain).
- **Desaparecidos:** "Disappeared people"—victims of forced disappearance by state actors, particularly during Argentina's last military dictatorship.
- **Lunfardo:** An argot or slang that originated in the late 19th century, heavily influenced by Italian dialects, and commonly used in tango lyrics.
- **Peronism:** A political movement based on the ideas and legacy of Juan Perón, emphasizing social justice, economic independence, and political sovereignty.
- **Porteños:** The demonym for residents of Buenos Aires, meaning "people of the port."
- **Rioplatense Spanish:** The dialect of Spanish spoken in the Río de la Plata region, characterized by *voseo* (use of *vos* instead of *tú*) and Italian-influenced intonation.
- **Villa miseria:** A type of slum or shantytown found on the outskirts of Argentine cities.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The source notes a historical tension between Buenos Aires' liberal, cosmopolitan identity and the more nationalistic, Catholic views of Argentina's provinces, a conflict that has shaped the country's internal politics since the 19th century.
- The city's autonomy remains limited compared to the provinces, with federal authorities retaining control over key institutions like the Federal Police and the port, a point of ongoing political negotiation.

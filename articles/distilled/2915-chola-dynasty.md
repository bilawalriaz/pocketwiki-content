# Chola dynasty

## Overview
The Chola dynasty was a Tamil imperial power from Southern India that, at its peak between the 9th and 13th centuries CE, created a vast maritime empire influencing South and Southeast Asia. They are renowned for their military conquests, sophisticated administration, and significant cultural and architectural achievements, particularly in temple construction and Tamil literature.

## Timeline
- **c. 3rd century BCE** — Earliest datable references to the Cholas in Ashokan Edicts.
- **c. 300 CE** — End of the Sangam period; beginning of an interregnum where Chola power declined.
- **c. 848 CE** — Vijayalaya Chola founds the Imperial Chola dynasty.
- **Early 10th century** — Aditya I defeats the Pallavas and Pandyas, expanding Chola territory.
- **11th century** — Peak of Chola power under Rajaraja I and Rajendra I.
- **1025 CE** — Rajendra I launches naval raids on the Srivijaya Empire in Southeast Asia.
- **1070 CE** — Kulothunga Chola I ascends the throne, beginning the Later Chola dynasty.
- **1215–1216 CE** — Kulothunga Chola III is defeated by the Pandya dynasty, marking the start of Chola decline.
- **1279 CE** — The Chola dynasty ceases to exist, having been absorbed by the Pandyan Empire.

## Body

### Origins and Early History
The earliest references to the Cholas are from the 3rd century BCE in Ashokan inscriptions. Their early history is primarily known through Sangam literature (c. 600 BCE), which records kings like Karikala and Kocengannan, though their chronology is uncertain. The Chola heartland was the fertile Kaveri River valley, with early capitals at Uraiyur and Kaveripattinam. After the Sangam period, the dynasty entered a long interregnum (c. 300–848 CE), during which they were displaced by the Kalabhras and later overshadowed by the Pallavas and Pandyas. A branch known as the Telugu-Cholas ruled in the Rayalaseema region during this time.

### The Imperial Cholas
The Chola Empire was re-founded in 848 CE by Vijayalaya. His successors, notably Aditya I and Parantaka I, expanded their territory by defeating the Pallavas and Pandyas. The empire reached its zenith under Rajaraja I and Rajendra I in the 11th century. Rajendra I conquered regions up to the Ganges in the north, built the new capital Gangaikonda Cholapuram, and launched naval expeditions against the Srivijaya Empire in Southeast Asia and the Maldives. This period saw the Cholas become a dominant military and economic power in the Indian Ocean region.

### Later Cholas and Decline
The Later Chola dynasty began with Kulothunga Chola I in 1070 CE, who was of mixed Chola-Chalukya lineage. While capable rulers like Kulothunga III continued to hold territory, the dynasty faced increasing pressure from the resurgent Pandya dynasty and the Hoysalas. After Kulothunga III's defeat by the Pandyas in 1215–1216, Chola power rapidly declined. They lost control of Sri Lanka and their traditional territories, eventually being absorbed into the Pandyan Empire by 1279.

### Administration, Culture, and Religion
The Chola administration was highly organized, with their territory centered on the Kaveri delta. They were great patrons of Hinduism, particularly Shaivism, and constructed monumental temples like the Brihadeeswarar Temple. Their conquests facilitated the spread of Hindu cultural influence across Southeast Asia. The Imperial Chola era was a golden age for Tamil literature, producing works like the *Periapuranam* and grammatical texts like *Nannul*. They also patronized Telugu literature through their feudatories.

## Terms
- **Sangam period:** The era of ancient Tamil literature (c. 600 BCE–300 CE) that provides early, though often legendary, accounts of the Cholas.
- **Interregnum:** A period of decline and obscurity for the Cholas between the end of the Sangam age and their imperial revival in the 9th century.
- **Feudatory:** A lesser ruler or state that owes allegiance and service to a more powerful sovereign, in this case, the Chola emperor.
- **Srivijaya:** A maritime empire based in Southeast Asia (modern Indonesia/Malaysia) that was a major trade rival and target of Chola naval raids.
- **Kalabhras:** An obscure dynasty that invaded and ruled parts of Tamil country during the Chola interregnum.
- **Brihadeeswarar Temple:** A monumental Hindu temple built by Rajaraja I in Thanjavur, a masterpiece of Chola architecture.
- **Gangaikonda Cholapuram:** The new capital city built by Rajendra I to celebrate his northern military campaigns.

## Debates and open questions
- The internal chronology and succession of the Early Chola kings from Sangam literature is "still far from settled."
- The exact nature and duration of the Chola interregnum are not well-documented, with little known about the dynasty's fate between c. 300 and 848 CE.
- There is a modern popular culture debate, noted in the source, where some Tamil film personalities have asserted that the Cholas were not Hindu, a claim that contrasts with the historical record of their Hindu patronage and temple construction.

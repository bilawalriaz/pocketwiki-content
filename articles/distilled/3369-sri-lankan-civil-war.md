# Sri Lankan civil war

## Overview
The Sri Lankan civil war (1983–2009) was a 26-year conflict between the Sri Lankan government and the Liberation Tigers of Tamil Eelam (LTTE), who fought to create an independent Tamil state, Tamil Eelam, in response to decades of discrimination and violence against Sri Lankan Tamils. The war ended with the military defeat of the LTTE but left over 100,000 dead, massive displacement, and ongoing allegations of war crimes by both sides.

## Timeline
- **1948** — Sri Lanka gains independence; Ceylon Citizenship Act discriminates against Indian Tamils.
- **1956** — "Sinhala Only Act" makes Sinhala the sole official language, sparking anti-Tamil pogroms.
- **1972** — Velupillai Prabhakaran forms the Tamil New Tigers (TNT), precursor to the LTTE.
- **1976** — LTTE formed; Tamil United Liberation Front (TULF) passes Vaddukkodei resolution for Tamil Eelam.
- **23 July 1983** — LTTE ambush kills 13 soldiers, triggering the "Black July" anti-Tamil pogrom and the start of the civil war.
- **1987** — Indo-Sri Lanka Accord signed; Indian Peace Keeping Force (IPKF) deployed.
- **21 May 1991** — LTTE assassinates former Indian Prime Minister Rajiv Gandhi.
- **1995–2002** — Eelam War III; government recaptures Jaffna in 1995.
- **2002** — Ceasefire agreement signed with Norwegian mediation.
- **2006** — LTTE closes Mavil Aru sluice gates, sparking Eelam War IV.
- **19 May 2009** — Sri Lankan government declares victory; LTTE admits defeat.

## Body

### Origins and Ethnic Tensions
The conflict's roots lie in the colonial era. British rule (1815–1948) brought Tamil laborers from India and created a civil service where Tamils were overrepresented due to educational advantages. After independence, the Sinhalese-majority government enacted discriminatory policies: the 1948 Citizenship Act made many Indian Tamils stateless, and the 1956 "Sinhala Only Act" marginalized Tamil speakers. These policies, alongside state-sponsored colonization of Tamil areas and anti-Tamil pogroms in 1956, 1958, 1977, 1981, and 1983, fueled Tamil militancy.

### Rise of the LTTE and Outbreak of War
The LTTE, formed in 1976 from the TNT, began assassinating politicians and police. The 1981 burning of the Jaffna Public Library and the 1983 "Black July" pogrom, where 3,500–4,000 Tamils were killed, radicalized the Tamil population and swelled LTTE ranks. The LTTE systematically eliminated rival Tamil groups and pioneered suicide bombings, carrying out over 378 such attacks.

### Indian Intervention and Eelam Wars I–III
India initially supported Tamil militants through its intelligence agency RAW. The 1987 Indo-Sri Lanka Accord led to the deployment of the Indian Peace Keeping Force (IPKF), which fought the LTTE until withdrawing in 1990. The war continued in phases: Eelam War II (1990–1995) saw brutal fighting and massacres of civilians by both sides; Eelam War III (1995–2002) began after a failed ceasefire, with the government recapturing Jaffna in 1995 and the LTTE retaliating with major offensives and terrorist attacks in the south.

### 2002 Peace Process and Collapse
A 2002 ceasefire, mediated by Norway, led to peace talks where the LTTE dropped its demand for a separate state in favor of federalism. However, the process collapsed due to mutual distrust, LTTE violence, and political instability in the south. The LTTE's 2004 split (the Karuna faction) and the 2004 tsunami, which complicated aid distribution, further destabilized the situation.

### Eelam War IV and Final Offensive (2006–2009)
Fighting resumed in 2006 after the LTTE closed the Mavil Aru sluice gates. The government launched offensives in the east (2006–2007) and north (2008–2009), systematically capturing LTTE territory. The final stages involved intense fighting in a "no-fire zone," with the UN estimating 6,500–40,000 civilian deaths. The LTTE was militarily defeated in May 2009; its leader Prabhakaran was killed on 18 May.

### Aftermath and Accountability
The war left over 100,000 dead, 300,000 internally displaced, and vast areas contaminated by landmines. The government established the Lessons Learnt and Reconciliation Commission (LLRC), but it was criticized for lack of independence. International bodies, including a UN Panel of Experts, found "credible allegations" of war crimes by both the military and LTTE, including attacks on civilians, executions, and the use of human shields. The Sri Lankan government has resisted independent international investigations.

## Terms
- **LTTE (Liberation Tigers of Tamil Eelam):** Tamil militant group founded by Velupillai Prabhakaran, fighting for an independent Tamil state.
- **Tamil Eelam:** The proposed independent state for Sri Lankan Tamils in the north and east.
- **Sinhala Only Act (1956):** Law making Sinhala the sole official language, marginalizing Tamil speakers.
- **Black July (1983):** Anti-Tamil pogrom in Colombo that marked the start of the civil war.
- **IPKF (Indian Peace Keeping Force):** Indian military force deployed in Sri Lanka from 1987 to 1990 under the Indo-Sri Lanka Accord.
- **Suicide bombings:** A signature LTTE tactic, with over 378 attacks carried out during the war.
- **No-fire zone:** Areas declared by the government during the final offensive, where heavy fighting and civilian casualties occurred.
- **LLRC (Lessons Learnt and Reconciliation Commission):** Sri Lankan commission appointed to review the war, criticized for limited independence.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Civilian casualties in the final phase:** Estimates range from 15,000 to 70,000 deaths. The UN cited "credible sources" for up to 40,000, while the Sri Lankan government disputes high figures and blames the LTTE for using human shields.
- **War crimes accountability:** Allegations exist against both the Sri Lankan military (bombing civilians, shelling hospitals) and the LTTE (using civilians as shields, recruiting child soldiers). The government has resisted independent international investigations, while human rights groups and some UN bodies demand them.
- **Genocide allegations:** Some groups and tribunals have accused the Sri Lankan state of genocide against Tamils, a charge the government denies. This remains a highly contested and unresolved issue.
- **Role of international actors:** Debates continue about the responsibility of India, the US, UK, and others in the conflict's escalation and the failure of the peace process.

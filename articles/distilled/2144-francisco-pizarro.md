# Francisco Pizarro

## Overview
Francisco Pizarro was a Spanish conquistador who led the expedition that conquered the Inca Empire in the early 16th century. His actions, beginning with exploratory voyages and culminating in the capture and execution of the Inca emperor Atahualpa, facilitated the Spanish colonization of Peru and the destruction of the Inca state, profoundly altering the region's political, religious, and cultural landscape.

## Timeline
- **c. 1478** — Born in Trujillo, Spain, to a poor family.
- **1509** — Sails to the New World with Alonso de Ojeda.
- **1513** — Accompanies Vasco Núñez de Balboa across the Isthmus of Panama, becoming one of the first Europeans to see the Pacific from the Americas.
- **1524** — Leads first failed expedition to Peru from Panama.
- **1526** — Begins second expedition; his pilot discovers Inca wealth off the coast.
- **1528** — Returns to Spain; secures royal authorization for conquest.
- **1530** — Departs Panama on his third and final expedition to Peru.
- **1532** — Founds San Miguel de Piura; captures Inca emperor Atahualpa at Cajamarca.
- **1533** — Executes Atahualpa; Spanish forces capture the Inca capital, Cuzco.
- **1535** — Founds the city of Lima.
- **1538** — Defeats and executes his former partner, Diego de Almagro.
- **1541** — Assassinated in Lima by supporters of Almagro's son.

## Body

### Early Life and Career
Pizarro was born c. 1478 in Trujillo, Spain, the illegitimate son of a poor family. He arrived in the New World in 1509 and gained early experience in the colonies. He participated in Vasco Núñez de Balboa's crossing of the Isthmus of Panama in 1513, which led to the first European sighting of the Pacific Ocean from the Americas. Pizarro later served as mayor of Panama City.

### Expeditions to Peru
Pizarro's interest in Peru was sparked by reports of a wealthy land called "Pirú." In 1524, he formed a partnership with Diego de Almagro and Hernando de Luque to explore and conquer the south. His first two expeditions (1524, 1526) were largely failures, plagued by bad weather, hunger, and hostile natives. However, during the second expedition, his pilot captured an Inca raft carrying gold and silver, providing proof of the region's wealth. After famously drawing a line in the sand to rally his men ("The Famous Thirteen"), Pizarro reached the Inca settlement of Tumbes in 1528, confirming reports of a powerful empire. He returned to Panama to prepare for a final conquest.

### Royal Authorization and Conquest
Unable to secure permission for a third expedition from the governor of Panama, Pizarro sailed to Spain. In 1529, Queen Isabella signed the Capitulación de Toledo, authorizing the conquest and naming Pizarro governor of "New Castile." He returned to Peru in 1530 with his brothers and a larger force. In 1532, Pizarro moved inland and founded San Miguel de Piura, the first Spanish settlement in Peru. He then marched to Cajamarca, where he captured the Inca emperor Atahualpa on November 16, 1532, after a swift attack on his army. Despite receiving a massive ransom of gold and silver, Pizarro had Atahualpa executed in 1533. Spanish forces then captured the Inca capital, Cuzco, completing the conquest.

### Aftermath and Death
Pizarro founded Lima in 1535 as the new capital. The conquest led to the destruction of the Inca state, the imposition of Christianity, and the subjugation of the native population under the encomienda system. Pizarro's partnership with Diego de Almagro collapsed over jurisdictional disputes, leading to Almagro's defeat and execution in 1538. Pizarro was assassinated in Lima on June 26, 1541, by a group of supporters of Almagro's son.

## Terms
- **Conquistador:** A Spanish soldier and explorer involved in the conquest of the Americas in the 16th century.
- **Capitulación de Toledo:** The 1529 royal contract that authorized Pizarro's conquest of Peru and named him governor.
- **Encomienda:** A Spanish labor system where colonists were granted the right to the labor of a specific number of native people.
- **Atahualpa:** The last sovereign emperor of the Inca Empire, captured and executed by Pizarro.
- **Cuzco:** The historic capital and heart of the Inca Empire.
- **Lima:** The city founded by Pizarro in 1535, which became the capital of colonial Peru.
- **Garrote:** An execution method by strangulation or crushing of the spinal cord, used to kill Atahualpa.
- **Adelantado:** A Spanish colonial governor and military commander, a title granted to Pizarro.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The exact date of Pizarro's birth is uncertain, with sources placing it in the 1470s, probably 1475.
- The decision to execute Atahualpa after he had paid the ransom remains controversial. Pizarro and Hernando de Soto reportedly opposed it, but Pizarro consented due to pressure from his soldiers, particularly Almagro. King Charles I later expressed displeasure with the execution.
- The limits of the governorates awarded to Pizarro and Almagro were ambiguously defined, leading directly to their fatal conflict.

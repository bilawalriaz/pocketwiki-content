# Nasir al-Din al-Tusi

## Overview
Nasir al-Din al-Tusi (1201–1274) was a Persian polymath whose work in astronomy, mathematics, and philosophy made him one of the most influential scientists of the medieval Islamic world. He established trigonometry as an independent mathematical discipline and created astronomical models that may have influenced the Copernican revolution.

## Timeline
- **1201** — Born in Tus, Khorasan (modern Iran)
- **c. 1235** — Writes *Nasirean Ethics* while employed by the Nizari Ismaili state
- **1256** — Captured after the fall of Maymun-Diz to Mongol forces under Hulagu Khan
- **1258** — Present during the Mongol siege of Baghdad; helps administer religious endowments afterward
- **1259** — Begins construction of the Maragheh observatory in Azerbaijan
- **1272** — Completes the *Zij-i Ilkhani* (Ilkhanic Tables), his major astronomical work
- **1274** — Dies in Baghdad

## Biography
Born into a Shia family in Tus, al-Tusi began studying the Quran, logic, philosophy, mathematics, medicine, and astronomy at an early age. After his father's death, he traveled widely to study under renowned scholars in Nishapur, Mosul, and elsewhere. He initially worked for the Nizari Ismaili state, writing philosophical and ethical works. Following the Mongol conquest, he entered the service of Hulagu Khan, who respected his scientific expertise. Al-Tusi convinced Hulagu to build the Maragheh observatory, where he and his team produced highly accurate astronomical tables. He later administered religious endowments across Sunni and Shia institutions, helping preserve scholarly infrastructure under Mongol rule.

## Achievements

### Astronomy
Al-Tusi's most significant astronomical work was the *Zij-i Ilkhani*, based on observations at the Maragheh observatory. He criticized Ptolemy's use of the equant—a mathematical device that violated the principle of uniform circular motion—and developed a geometric solution called the Tusi couple, which generates linear motion from two circular motions. This technique was later used by Ibn al-Shatir and possibly by Nicolaus Copernicus. Al-Tusi also correctly described the Milky Way as composed of many tightly clustered stars, three centuries before Galileo's telescopic confirmation.

### Mathematics
Al-Tusi established trigonometry as a discipline independent of astronomy. His *Treatise on the Quadrilateral* provided the first systematic exposition of spherical trigonometry, listing six distinct cases of right spherical triangles. He stated the sine law for both plane and spherical triangles and discovered the law of tangents for spherical triangles, providing proofs for these results.

### Logic and Philosophy
A supporter of Avicennian logic, al-Tusi wrote commentaries on Avicenna's theory of propositions. His most famous philosophical work, *Akhlaq-i Nasiri* (*Nasirean Ethics*), compares Islamic teachings with Aristotelian and Platonic ethics and became widely influential in the Muslim world. He also contributed significantly to Shia theology through works like *Tajrid al-I'tiqad*.

### Biology and Chemistry
In *Akhlaq-i Nasiri*, al-Tusi defended a version of Aristotle's scala naturae (great chain of being), placing humans above animals, plants, and minerals. He described different types of learning, recognizing observational learning as the most advanced form in animals. In chemistry, he articulated an early version of the law of conservation of mass, stating that total mass remains constant during chemical transformations.

### Color Theory
Building on Aristotle's and Ibn Sina's work, al-Tusi proposed that color space is two-dimensional, identifying five distinct paths from black to white through yellow, red, green, blue, and grey.

## Terms
- **Tusi couple**: A geometric device that generates linear motion from the sum of two circular motions, used to replace Ptolemy's equant.
- **Equant**: A point in Ptolemaic astronomy from which a planet appears to move uniformly, though it does not move uniformly around the center of its orbit.
- **Zij-i Ilkhani**: Al-Tusi's major astronomical tables, completed in 1272, used for calculating planetary positions.
- **Scala naturae**: Aristotle's hierarchical classification of living things, from minerals through plants and animals to humans.
- **Sine law**: A mathematical relationship stating that the ratio of a side of a triangle to the sine of its opposite angle is constant.
- **Avicennian logic**: The logical system developed by the philosopher Avicenna (Ibn Sina), which al-Tusi defended and expanded.
- **Nizari Ismailis**: A Shia Muslim community that maintained a state with fortified strongholds in Persia before the Mongol conquest.

## Debates and Open Questions
Scholars debate whether Copernicus was influenced by al-Tusi's work. The Tusi couple appears in Copernicus's heliocentric model with identical geometric construction and lettering, but no direct transmission of al-Tusi's texts to Copernicus has been proven. Possible intermediaries include Jewish scholars like Abner of Burgos or transmission through the Maragheh observatory to Muslim Spain and then to Europe. Some scholars argue Copernicus could have independently derived the Tusi couple using Euclid's methods, given his known dissatisfaction with Ptolemaic equants. There is also debate about whether al-Tusi's biological writings suggest an evolutionary theory, though he never explicitly stated that species change over time.

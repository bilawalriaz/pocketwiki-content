# Kingdom of Kush

## Overview
The Kingdom of Kush was an ancient Nubian civilization centered along the Nile in modern Sudan and southern Egypt. It was a major regional power for over a millennium, at times rivaling and even ruling Egypt. Kush developed a distinct culture, language, and technology, and its history is crucial for understanding the complex interactions between ancient African civilizations.

## Timeline
- **c. 2500–1500 BC** — Kerma culture flourishes as a dominant Nubian power.
- **c. 1504–1070 BC** — Nubia is absorbed into the Egyptian New Kingdom.
- **c. 1070 BC** — Kush becomes independent with its capital at Napata after Egypt's collapse.
- **c. 754–656 BC** — Kushite 25th Dynasty rules Egypt, expelling Assyrians.
- **671–663 BC** — Assyrians conquer Egypt, sacking Thebes and ending Kushite rule there.
- **c. 591 BC** — Capital moves from Napata to Meroë after a sack by Egypt's Psamtik II.
- **25–22 BC** — War with the Roman Empire ends in a peace treaty.
- **c. 4th century AD** — Kingdom declines and fragments due to internal strife and invasions.

## Body

### Origins and Egyptian Rule
The region of Nubia was an early cradle of civilization. The city-state of Kerma emerged as a dominant force between 2450 and 1450 BC. Following the New Kingdom period, when Nubia was under Egyptian rule (c. 1550–1070 BC), the Kushites reestablished an independent kingdom centered at Napata. Though culturally influenced by Egypt, Kushite culture, language, and ethnicity remained distinct.

### The Kushite Empire and Rule Over Egypt
In the 8th century BC, Kushite kings extended their control northward. King Kashta peacefully became king of Upper Egypt, and his successor Piye conquered a fragmented Lower Egypt, establishing the 25th Dynasty. Kushite pharaohs ruled Egypt for over a century, reviving Egyptian culture and architecture. Their empire was eventually expelled by the Assyrian kings Esarhaddon and Ashurbanipal in the mid-7th century BC.

### The Napatan and Meroitic Periods
After losing Egypt, Kush continued as a major power. The capital was later moved to Meroë, which became a center for trade, ironworking, and agriculture. Kush engaged in conflict and diplomacy with Ptolemaic and Roman Egypt. A peace treaty with Rome in 22 BC secured peace for centuries. The kingdom began to fade in the 1st–2nd centuries AD due to war with Rome and internal decline.

### Decline and Fall
Kush weakened and disintegrated in the 4th century AD due to worsening climate, internal rebellions, and invasions by the Noba and Blemmyes peoples. The Aksumite Empire also captured Meroë. The kingdom dissolved into three successor states: Nobatia, Makuria, and Alodia.

### Culture and Technology
The Kushites developed their own language and script, the Meroitic alphabet. They were master archers and pioneered technologies like the saqiyah (a water wheel for irrigation) and hafirs (reservoirs). Archaeological evidence shows they used tetracycline as an early antibiotic and had a sophisticated understanding of mathematics and geometry. Their architecture included distinctive steep-sided pyramids and rock-cut temples.

## Terms
- **Kush**: The native name for the kingdom and its people, recorded in Egyptian as *kꜣš*.
- **Kerma**: The dominant Nubian city-state and culture before Egyptian conquest (c. 2500–1500 BC).
- **Napata**: The first capital of the independent Kingdom of Kush, located near Jebel Barkal.
- **Meroë**: The later capital of Kush, known for iron production and as a center of trade.
- **Meroitic alphabet**: The script developed by the Kushites, used in hieroglyphic and cursive forms.
- **Saqiyah**: A water wheel or scoop wheel developed by the Kushites for irrigation.
- **Hafir**: A type of reservoir developed to store rainwater.
- **Twenty-fifth Dynasty**: The Kushite dynasty that ruled Egypt from c. 747 to 656 BC.
- **Noba**: A people who invaded Kushite territory in the 4th century AD, giving their name to Nubia.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The extent of cultural and political continuity between the earlier Kerma culture and the later Kingdom of Kush is difficult to determine.
- Scholars debate why the Kushites chose to conquer Egypt in the 8th century BC, with one hypothesis linking it to a claim of religious legitimacy from the god Amun.
- The classification of the Meroitic language is uncertain; it may belong to the Afro-Asiatic or Nilo-Saharan language families.
- The study of Kush has been overshadowed by Egyptology, and scholars argue it should be recognized as a distinct state with its own complex development.

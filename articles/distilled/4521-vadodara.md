# Vadodara

## Overview
Vadodara, also known as Baroda, is a major city in Gujarat, India, located on the Vishwamitri River. It is historically significant as the former capital of the Gaekwad dynasty's Baroda State and is a prominent cultural and industrial hub, known for its landmarks, educational institutions, and petrochemical industries.

## Timeline
- **c. 1000 BCE** — Prehistoric habitation evidenced at Akota (now a Vadodara suburb).
- **6th century** — Inhabitants of Akota shift to a new site near modern Vadodara.
- **812** — First recorded mention of Vadodara (as Vaḍapadraka) in a Rashtrakuta copper-plate grant.
- **11th century** — Vadodara gains importance under the Chaulukya dynasty, becoming a city (pura).
- **1344-45** — Afghan amirs revolt from the Delhi Sultanate at Vadodara; Sultan Muhammad bin Tughluq suppresses it.
- **1411** — Firuz Khan, governor of Vadodara, rebels for the Gujarat Sultanate throne after his father's death.
- **1451** — Vadodara is sacked by troops of the Malwa Sultanate.
- **Late 15th century** — English travelers refer to the town as "Baroda."
- **1573** — After Akbar conquers Gujarat, Vadodara is given to Nawab Aurang Khan.
- **1705** — Vadodara is looted for two days by a Koli raid.
- **1963** — Construction begins on Gujarat Refinery outside Vadodara.
- **1974** — The city's official name is changed from Baroda to Vadodara.

## Body

### Etymology and History
The city's name evolved from Chandanavati (after Raja Chandan) to Vadpatraka/Vadodará, likely derived from the Sanskrit *vatodara* ("in the belly of the banyan tree"). The name "Baroda" derives from 15th-century English usage. Archaeological evidence at Akota shows continuous habitation from prehistory through the early centuries CE, with trade links and a flourishing Jain community under the Maitraka dynasty. Vadodara itself emerged as a significant settlement by the 11th century under the Chaulukya dynasty. It was later ruled by the Delhi Sultanate, Gujarat Sultanate, and Mughals. A major re-foundation under Sultan Mahmud Begada created the city of Daulatabad, which became the basis for modern Vadodara. The city was the capital of the Maratha Gaekwad dynasty's Baroda State until Indian independence.

### Geography and Climate
Vadodara is located at an elevation of 128 ft (39 m) on the fertile plain between the Mahi and Narmada rivers. It has a borderline tropical savanna climate (Köppen Aw) with three seasons: hot summer (March-July), monsoon (mid-June to mid-September), and dry winter (November-February). Average annual rainfall is about 950 mm (37 in), but torrential rains can cause catastrophic flooding of the Vishwamitri River, as in 2005 and 2008. The city is in seismic zone-III.

### Demographics and Economy
The 2011 census recorded a municipal population of 1,670,806, with a literacy rate of 90.48%. Hinduism (85.39%) is the main religion, followed by Islam (11.20%). Gujarati is the primary language (71.37%). Vadodara's economy is driven by large-scale industries, particularly petrochemicals, with the Gujarat Refinery (established 1963) and complexes operated by Indian Petrochemicals Corporation Limited (now Reliance Industries). It is a major hub for power equipment, rail coach, and defense aircraft manufacturing, and has a growing pharmaceutical and IT sector.

### Government and Civic Administration
The city is governed by the Vadodara Municipal Corporation (VMC), established in 1951. The VMC is divided into 19 wards with 76 councilor seats, 50% reserved for women. Executive power lies with the Municipal Commissioner, an IAS officer. The city elects one member to the Lok Sabha and five to the Gujarat Vidhan Sabha. The Vadodara Urban Development Authority (VUDA) handles broader regional planning.

### Culture and Transport
Vadodara is known as the "Cultural City" and houses museums like the Baroda Museum and Picture Gallery. It is a major transport junction on the Mumbai-Delhi and Mumbai-Ahmedabad rail and road corridors. Vadodara Airport is Gujarat's first "Green Airport." The city is home to the Maharaja Sayajirao University of Baroda and other higher education institutions.

## Terms
- **Pura**: A Sanskrit term for a city, used to denote Vadodara's elevated status by the 11th century.
- **Jagir**: A grant of land revenue given to a noble or official in lieu of a salary.
- **Vishaya**: An administrative province or district in early medieval India.
- **Rathayatra**: A chariot procession, often of religious significance.
- **Tirthankara**: A savior and spiritual teacher in Jainism.
- **Kachcha**: Temporary or makeshift structures, often used to describe slum housing.
- **EWS**: Economically Weaker Section, a category for housing and welfare schemes.
- **VMC**: Vadodara Municipal Corporation, the local governing body.
- **VUDA**: Vadodara Urban Development Authority, responsible for regional planning.

## Debates and Open Questions
The exact timing of the various name changes for the city (from Chandanavati to Vadodara) is difficult to ascertain historically. The shift of the main Jain center from Akota to Vadodara around the 11th century is noted, but the precise causes and timeline are part of the broader historical narrative rather than a subject of active scholarly debate presented in the source.

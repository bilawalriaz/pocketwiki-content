# Southern Cone

## Overview
The Southern Cone is a geographical and cultural subregion in the southernmost part of South America, primarily comprising Argentina, Chile, and Uruguay. It is distinguished by its temperate climate, high standard of living, and significant European immigration, which has shaped its modern societies. The region is the second most prosperous in the Americas after Northern America.

## Timeline
- **19th–20th centuries** — Massive European and Middle Eastern immigration reshapes the demographics and culture of Argentina, Chile, and Uruguay.
- **1880–1920** — Peak period of Italian emigration to the Southern Cone.
- **1970s–1980s** — Right-wing military dictatorships rule Argentina, Chile, and Uruguay; collaborate in Operation Condor against leftist opposition.
- **Early 1980s** — Argentina and Uruguay restore democratic governance.
- **1990** — Chile transitions to democracy.
- **2013** — Pope Francis, born in Buenos Aires, Argentina, is elected—the first pope from the Western Hemisphere.

## Body

### Geography and Extent
The core Southern Cone countries are Argentina, Chile, and Uruguay, bounded by the Pacific and Atlantic Oceans. The definition sometimes extends to include Paraguay and Brazil's four southernmost states (Paraná, Rio Grande do Sul, Santa Catarina, and São Paulo), which share similar climate, high industrialization, and European ancestry. Paraguay is occasionally included geographically but is often excluded due to its subtropical climate, lower Human Development Index (HDI), and distinct Guaraní cultural identity. The region's climate is predominantly temperate, ranging from Mediterranean to sub-Antarctic, with the Andes forming a major north-south divide. The Atacama Desert is noted as the driest place on Earth.

### Culture and Language
Culture is heavily influenced by 19th and 20th-century immigration. Football is a dominant passion, with Argentina and Uruguay being multiple FIFA World Cup winners. Iconic figures include the gaucho (Argentina/Uruguay) and huaso (Chile). The asado (barbecue) and mate (infusion) are shared culinary traditions. Spanish is the primary language, with two main dialects: Rioplatense Spanish (Argentina/Uruguay), influenced by Italian intonation, and Chilean Spanish, characterized by the omission of the letter "s". Portuguese is spoken in southern Brazil. Native American languages like Mapudungun, Quechua, and Guarani persist, and numerous European immigrant languages (Italian, German, Polish) are also present.

### Demography and Ethnicity
The population is strongly shaped by European immigration. People of direct European descent make up 88% of Uruguay, 85% of Argentina, and 65% of Chile. The Southern Cone hosts the world's largest Italian diaspora, with 62.5% of Argentines claiming Italian ancestry. A large German diaspora is also present, particularly in southern Brazil and Chile. Genetic studies show predominant European ancestry (e.g., ~79% in Argentina), with significant indigenous and smaller African components. Arab ancestry is also notable, comprising up to 11% in Argentina.

### Education, Standards of Living, and Politics
The Southern Cone is Latin America's most prosperous region, with high HDIs (Chile 0.862, Argentina 0.878, Uruguay 0.865), life expectancy, and literacy. Economically, the countries are noted for pro-free market policies. Politically, after enduring military dictatorships in the 1970s-80s, Argentina, Uruguay, and Chile have experienced democratic stability since their respective transitions in the 1980s and 1990.

## Terms
- **Human Development Index (HDI):** A composite statistic of life expectancy, education, and per capita income indicators, used to rank countries by development.
- **Gauchos/Huasos:** Skilled horsemen and cowboys considered icons of national identity in Argentina/Uruguay and Chile, respectively.
- **Asado:** A traditional barbecue cooking method and social event typical of the region.
- **Mate:** A traditional caffeinated infusion from the holly plant, widely consumed as a social drink.
- **Rioplatense Spanish:** A dialect of Spanish spoken in the Río de la Plata basin (Argentina and Uruguay), heavily influenced by Italian immigration.
- **Operation Condor:** A campaign of political repression and state terror in the 1970s-80s involving intelligence operations and assassination of opponents, implemented by the right-wing dictatorships of several South American countries.
- **Mestizo:** A person of combined European and Indigenous American descent.
- **Pampas:** The vast, fertile plains of central Argentina, Uruguay, and southern Brazil.

## Debates and Open questions
- The precise geographical and cultural boundaries of the Southern Cone are debated, particularly regarding the inclusion of Paraguay and southern Brazilian states.
- The extent and impact of historical European immigration versus indigenous heritage on national identity and genetic composition remain subjects of study and discussion.

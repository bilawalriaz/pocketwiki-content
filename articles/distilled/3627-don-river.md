# Don (river)

## Overview
The Don River is the fifth-longest river in Europe, flowing 1,870 km from Central Russia to the Sea of Azov. It has been a vital geographical and historical artery, serving as a border, trade route, and cradle for various cultures from antiquity to the modern era.

## Timeline
- **c. 4000 BC** — The Volga-Don region is proposed as the Proto-Indo-European homeland.
- **Antiquity** — Known to Greeks as Tanaïs; considered a Europe-Asia border by some geographers.
- **Early Middle Ages** — Lower Don controlled by the Khazar Khaganate, which built the fortress Sarkel.
- **14th century** — The Don estuary area (Azak) is speculated to be a source of the Black Death.
- **Late 14th century** — Fort of Donkov founded by princes of Ryazan.
- **16th-17th centuries** — The fertile valley is settled by the Don Cossacks.
- **1568** — Donkov is destroyed by Crimean Tatars and later relocated.
- **1914-19** — Kochetovsky Ship Lock is built on the lower Don.
- **2004-08** — Kochetovsky Lock is doubled.

## Body

### Geography and Etymology
The Don rises near Novomoskovsk, southeast of Tula, and flows 1,870 km to the Sea of Azov. Its main city is Rostov-on-Don, and its primary tributary is the Seversky Donets. The river's name derives from the Avestan and Rigveda word *dānu*. Its basin is bordered by the Dnieper, Volga, and Oka basins.

### Ancient and Medieval History
In antiquity, the river was called Tanaïs (Τάναϊς) by Greeks and was a major trade route. Some ancient geographers, like those cited in the Book of Jubilees, viewed it as a border between Europe and Asia. The area was influenced by the Byzantine Empire due to trade. In the Early Middle Ages, the Khazar Khaganate controlled the lower Don and built the fortress Sarkel. After the Mongol conquests, the basin became part of the Golden Horde, which established the trade outpost Azak at the estuary.

### Early Modern and Modern Era
The middle and upper Don were not accurately mapped until the Tsardom of Russia conquered the area in the 16th century. The Don Cossacks settled the valley in the 16th and 17th centuries. The region is famously depicted in Mikhail Sholokhov's Nobel Prize-winning novel *And Quiet Flows the Don*.

### Dams and Canals
The 101-km Volga–Don Canal connects the Don to the Volga River near their closest point. The Tsimlyansk Dam raises the Don's level at this junction. Navigation on the lower Don is maintained by a series of three dam-and-lock complexes, with the Kochetovsky Lock being the most significant. Plans exist for further dams to improve shipping.

## Terms
- **Proto-Indo-Europeans**: A hypothetical prehistoric people considered the common ancestor of Indo-European language speakers.
- **Khazar Khaganate**: A major medieval empire of Turkic origin in Eastern Europe and the North Caucasus.
- **Sarkel**: A large limestone fortress built by the Khazars on the left bank of the Don.
- **Don Cossacks**: A group of Cossacks who settled the Don valley and were named after the river.
- **Volga–Don Canal**: A 101-km canal linking the Don and Volga rivers, enabling navigation between the Sea of Azov and the Caspian Sea.
- **Ship Lock**: A device for raising and lowering boats between stretches of water of different levels on a waterway.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The exact homeland of the Proto-Indo-Europeans is debated; the Kurgan hypothesis places it in the Volga-Don region.
- The Don estuary area (Azak) has been speculated, but not definitively proven, to be a source of the Black Death in the mid-14th century.

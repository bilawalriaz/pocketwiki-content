# Sultan Agung of Mataram

## Overview
Sultan Agung (r. 1613–1645) was the third Sultan of the Mataram Sultanate in Central Java. He is a pivotal figure in Indonesian history, renowned for expanding Mataram to its greatest territorial extent through military conquest, implementing key administrative reforms, and leading resistance against the Dutch East India Company (VOC). His reign represents the peak of Mataram's power and is central to the cultural and political history of Java.

## Timeline
- **1613** — Rangsang (Sultan Agung) ascends the throne of Mataram.
- **1614** — Attacks on Surabaya and Malang fail; new Karta Palace is built.
- **1615** — Conquers Wirasaba.
- **1616** — Defeats Surabaya's retaliatory attack; conquers Lasem.
- **1617** — Conquers Pasuruan; Pajang rebels.
- **1619** — Conquers the major coastal city of Tuban.
- **1622** — Captures Sukadana, an ally of Surabaya.
- **1624** — Captures Madura after a fierce battle.
- **1625** — Completes the conquest of Surabaya after a five-year war.
- **1628–1629** — Leads two failed sieges against the Dutch at Batavia (Jakarta).
- **1632** — Begins construction of his royal burial complex at Imogiri.
- **1645** — Dies, succeeded by his son Amangkurat I.

## Body

### Territorial Expansion and Conflict with the Dutch
Sultan Agung aggressively expanded Mataram's territory. After early failures against Surabaya, he systematically weakened it by capturing its allies, Sukadana (1622) and Madura (1624), before conquering Surabaya itself in 1625. This made Mataram the dominant power across most of Java. His primary external foe was the Dutch VOC, based in Batavia. Despite his powerful land army, his contempt for trade meant he neglected naval power. This proved decisive when his sieges of Batavia in 1628 and 1629 failed, as the Dutch used their naval superiority to resupply. Following this, he waged a "holy war" against the Hindu Balinese, capturing Balambangan in East Java but failing to conquer Bali itself.

### Administration and Reforms
Sultan Agung's major legacy lies in his administrative reforms. To govern his expanded realm, he created a rational structure by establishing *kadipaten* (duchies) headed by appointed *adipati* (dukes), particularly in western Java. He reformed the tax code and aligned the judicial system with Quranic precepts. He also commissioned major public works, including the Karta Palace and the royal graveyard at Imogiri. This *kabupaten* (regency) system proved so durable that it was retained by the Dutch colonial administration and forms the basis of modern Indonesian regional government.

### Culture and Legacy
Sultan Agung is credited with significant cultural contributions, including the creation of the indigenous Javanese calendar and the mystical manuscript *Sastra Gending*. He established a standard court language, Bagongan, to unify officials. While he is associated with developments in sacred dance (*bedhaya*), gamelan, and *wayang* (shadow puppetry), historical evidence for high artistic achievement in his court is scarce. He is revered as a National Hero of Indonesia for unifying Java and resisting colonialism. His tomb at Imogiri remains a major pilgrimage site, reflecting his enduring near-mythical status in Javanese culture.

## Terms
- **Mataram Sultanate:** A powerful Islamic kingdom that dominated Central and East Java in the 16th and 17th centuries.
- **VOC (Dutch East India Company):** The Dutch chartered company that established colonial outposts in the East Indies, including Batavia (Jakarta).
- **Patih:** The viceregent or chief minister of a Javanese kingdom.
- **Kadipaten/Kabupaten:** A duchy or regency, a territorial administrative unit headed by an *adipati* (duke/regent).
- **Adipati/Bupati:** The title for the ruler of a *kadipaten*; later adapted by the Dutch as *regent*.
- **Bagongan:** A standardized Javanese court language established by Sultan Agung to promote unity.
- **Imogiri:** The royal burial complex built by Sultan Agung, which remains the resting place for Javanese royalty.
- **Walisanga:** The nine saints credited with spreading Islam in Java; Giri Kedaton was associated with one of them.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Artistic Achievement:** The source notes a lack of historical evidence for claims of high artistic development in Sultan Agung's court, with information being scarce and difficult to interpret.
- **Parentage:** A minority, unverified account suggests Sultan Agung was not the biological son of Sultan Anyakrawati but was switched at birth with the child of Prince Purbaya.
- **Cause of Expansion Halt:** While rebellions are cited, the failure to conquer Batavia is presented as a key factor that weakened Mataram's momentum and encouraged vassal revolts.

# Évariste Galois

## Overview
Évariste Galois (1811–1832) was a French mathematician and political activist whose work, completed before his death at age 20, laid the foundations for modern abstract algebra. He solved a 350-year-old problem by determining the condition for a polynomial equation to be solvable by radicals, creating what is now called Galois theory. His life was marked by brilliant mathematical insight, passionate Republican politics, and a tragic, early death in a duel.

## Timeline
- **25 Oct 1811** — Born in Bourg-la-Reine, France.
- **1828** — Fails entrance exam for École Polytechnique; publishes first paper on continued fractions.
- **28 Jul 1829** — His father dies by suicide.
- **1830** — Publishes three papers, including one laying foundations for Galois theory and another introducing finite fields.
- **14 Jul 1831** — Arrested for protesting in uniform; imprisoned until October.
- **29 Apr 1832** — Released from prison after serving a six-month sentence.
- **29 May 1832** — Writes a final letter outlining his mathematical ideas.
- **30 May 1832** — Mortally wounded in a duel.
- **31 May 1832** — Dies in a Paris hospital.
- **1846** — Joseph Liouville publishes Galois's collected manuscripts.

## Body

### Early Life and Mathematical Promise
Galois showed exceptional mathematical talent as a teenager, mastering advanced texts like Legendre's geometry and Lagrange's works on equations. He failed the entrance exam for the prestigious École Polytechnique twice, possibly due to his unconventional thinking and the recent death of his father. He published his first paper on continued fractions in 1829 and began submitting groundbreaking work on polynomial equations to the Academy of Sciences. These submissions were repeatedly lost or rejected, notably by referees Augustin-Louis Cauchy and Joseph Fourier.

### Political Activism and Imprisonment
Living during the turbulent July Revolution of 1830, Galois was a staunch Republican. He was expelled from the École Normale for a critical letter and joined a Republican artillery unit. His political fervor led to multiple arrests: first for a perceived threat against King Louis Philippe at a banquet, and again for protesting while armed on Bastille Day 1831. He served six months in prison, during which he continued his mathematical work.

### Final Days and Legacy
After his release in April 1832, Galois was drawn into a duel under obscure circumstances, likely involving a romantic dispute. Convinced of his death, he spent the night before the duel writing letters and summarizing his mathematical ideas. He died from his wounds on May 31, 1832. His manuscripts were finally published by Joseph Liouville in 1846, revealing his profound contributions.

### Mathematical Contributions
Galois's core achievement was **Galois theory**, which links the solvability of a polynomial equation to the structure of an associated permutation group (the **Galois group**). He proved an equation is solvable by radicals if and only if its Galois group is a **solvable group**. This provided a definitive answer to the ancient problem of solving polynomial equations. He also pioneered **group theory**, using the term "group" in a technical sense, and introduced the concept of a **finite field** (now called a **Galois field**). His work on continued fractions proved key theorems about their periodicity for quadratic surds.

## Terms
- **Galois theory**: A branch of algebra that connects field theory and group theory to study polynomial equations and their symmetries.
- **Galois group**: The group of permutations of the roots of a polynomial that preserves all algebraic relations between them.
- **Solvable group**: A group that can be built from abelian groups through a series of extensions; determines if a polynomial is solvable by radicals.
- **Finite field (Galois field)**: A field with a finite number of elements, fundamental to modern algebra and coding theory.
- **Radicals**: Expressions involving roots (e.g., square roots, cube roots) of numbers.
- **Quadratic surd**: An irrational number that is a root of a quadratic equation with rational coefficients.
- **Normal subgroup**: A subgroup that is invariant under conjugation by all elements of the group, crucial for constructing quotient groups.
- **Abelian group**: A group where the operation is commutative (a *b* = *b* *a*).

## Debates and Open Questions
- The exact reasons for Galois's repeated failures at the École Polytechnique entrance exam are unclear, with accounts differing on whether it was due to his behavior, the examiners, or both.
- The true motive and identity of Galois's duel opponent remain obscure and debated, with conflicting historical accounts.
- The decade-long delay (1832–1842) between Galois's death and Liouville's study of his papers is unexplained; it may have been influenced by the political climate following the June Rebellion of 1832.

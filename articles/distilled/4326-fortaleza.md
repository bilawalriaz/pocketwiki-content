# Fortaleza

## Overview
Fortaleza is the capital of Ceará state in Northeastern Brazil and the country's fourth-largest city by population. It is the economic and cultural hub of the Northeast region, with a major port, a diversified economy, and a vibrant cultural identity known for humor, music, and cuisine.

## Timeline
- **1603** — Portuguese colonization begins with the construction of Fort of São Tiago.
- **1649** — Dutch West Indies Company builds Fort Schoonenborch, giving the city its name.
- **1726** — Fortaleza is officially founded as a village.
- **1799** — Fortaleza becomes the capital of Ceará state.
- **1812** — Customs building constructed, marking growth as a regional export center.
- **1954** — Universidade Federal do Ceará (UFC) is founded.
- **1980s** — Fortaleza surpasses Recife to become the second most populous city in Northeastern Brazil.
- **2012** — Fortaleza Metro begins operations.
- **2014** — City hosts matches for the FIFA World Cup.
- **2022** — Census records population of 2,428,708, making it Brazil's fourth-largest city.

## Body

### History and Etymology
The city's name means "Fortress" in Portuguese, referencing the Dutch Fort Schoonenborch built in 1649. After the Dutch ceded the fort to the Portuguese in 1654, it was renamed Fortaleza da Nossa Senhora de Assunção. The settlement was officially founded as a village in 1726 and became the state capital in 1799. It consolidated as an urban center in the 19th century, supported by the cotton industry.

### Geography and Climate
Fortaleza lies on the Atlantic coast. Its vegetation is coastal, with remaining mangrove areas in parks like Cocó Park. The city has seven environmental conservation units. The climate is tropical wet and dry (Köppen: Aw/As), with high temperatures year-round. The rainy season is from January to July, and the dry season from August to December. Annual rainfall averages 1,584 mm but is highly variable, with severe droughts recorded in 1877 and 1958.

### Demographics and Society
The 2022 census recorded a population of 2,428,708, with 60% identifying as Pardo (multiracial), 32.7% as White, and 7% as Black. The city experienced significant European and Syrian-Lebanese immigration in the early 20th century. Income inequality remains significant, with a Gini coefficient of 0.61 in 2010. About 22.9% of the population lives in favelas (slums). Catholicism is the dominant religion (67.88% in 2010), followed by Protestantism (21.35%).

### Economy
Fortaleza has the largest GDP in Northeast Brazil, reaching 73.4 billion Reais in 2021. The economy is centered on the tertiary (services) and secondary (industry) sectors. The city is a major commercial and industrial center, with a vital trade port. The Port of Pecém, in its metropolitan region, has strategic transit times to Europe and the United States.

### Culture
Fortaleza is recognized as a UNESCO "Design City." Its cultural life includes major theaters like Theatro José de Alencar and the Dragão do Mar Center of Art and Culture. The city is known as the "humor capital of Brazil," with a strong tradition of comedy. Forró is the most popular music genre. Its cuisine features Northeastern dishes, with a strong emphasis on seafood, especially crab, shrimp, and lobster. The traditional "caranguejada" (crab feast) is a weekly event.

### Transportation
The city is served by Pinto Martins International Airport. The Fortaleza Metro, opened in 2012, consists of one metro line and two light rail lines. Public bus transport is provided by the Integrated Transport System (SIT-FOR). The Port of Mucuripe and the Port of Pecém are key freight hubs. The BR-116 highway starts in Fortaleza.

### Sports
Football is the most popular sport. The main teams are Fortaleza Esporte Clube and Ceará Sporting Club. The city hosted 2013 Confederations Cup and 2014 World Cup matches at the Arena Castelão stadium. It will host matches for the 2027 FIFA Women's World Cup. Fortaleza is also home to the Brazilian Futsal Confederation.

## Terms
- **Pardo**: A Brazilian census category for multiracial people, especially those of mixed European, African, and Indigenous ancestry.
- **Gini coefficient**: A measure of income inequality, where 0 represents perfect equality and 1 represents perfect inequality.
- **Favela**: An informal settlement or slum, typically in an urban area.
- **Forró**: A genre of music and dance originating in Northeastern Brazil.
- **Jangada**: A traditional Brazilian fishing raft or sailboat.
- **Tertiary sector**: The part of the economy providing services rather than goods.
- **Mercosur**: A South American trade bloc.
- **Köppen climate classification**: A system for classifying the world's climates.

# Franco-Prussian War

## Overview
The Franco-Prussian War (1870–1871) was a conflict between France and the North German Confederation, led by Prussia. It was a decisive Prussian victory that led to the unification of Germany, the collapse of Napoleon III's Second French Empire, and a major shift in the European balance of power, with Germany replacing France as the continent's dominant land power.

## Timeline
- **19 July 1870** — War begins; France declares war on Prussia.
- **2 August 1870** — French forces invade German territory at Saarbrücken.
- **4 August 1870** — Battle of Wissembourg; first major engagement, French defeat.
- **18 August 1870** — Battle of Gravelotte; largest battle of the war, French retreat to Metz.
- **1 September 1870** — Battle of Sedan; decisive French defeat, Napoleon III captured.
- **4 September 1870** — Second Empire falls; Government of National Defence proclaimed in Paris.
- **19 September 1870** — Siege of Paris begins.
- **28 January 1871** — Paris falls; armistice signed.
- **18 January 1871** — German Empire proclaimed at Versailles.
- **10 May 1871** — Treaty of Frankfurt signed; France cedes Alsace-Lorraine and pays indemnity.
- **18 March – 28 May 1871** — Paris Commune uprising and its bloody suppression.

## Body

### Causes and Outbreak
The war's roots lie in French determination to reassert its dominant position in Europe after Prussia's victory over Austria in 1866. Prussian Chancellor Otto von Bismarck sought to use a war with France to unite the independent southern German states (Baden, Württemberg, Bavaria, Hesse-Darmstadt) with the North German Confederation. The immediate trigger was the candidacy of Prince Leopold of Hohenzollern for the Spanish throne, which France opposed. After Leopold withdrew, French demands that Prussia renounce future claims were rejected by King Wilhelm I. Bismarck then published a deliberately provocative summary of the "Ems Dispatch," inflaming French public opinion. A bellicose French government, pressured by public sentiment, ordered mobilization on 15 July, leading Prussia to mobilize in response. France declared war on 19 July, having failed to secure any allies.

### Military Campaign and French Defeat
The German coalition mobilized more effectively and invaded northeastern France on 4 August. German forces were superior in numbers, training, leadership, and use of modern technology like railways and artillery. A series of rapid Prussian victories in eastern France culminated in the Siege of Metz and the decisive Battle of Sedan (1 September), where Emperor Napoleon III and his army were captured. A new Government of National Defence in Paris continued the war. German forces besieged Paris for over four months. Despite the formation of new French armies in the provinces under leaders like Léon Gambetta, they were repeatedly defeated in the Loire, northern, and eastern campaigns. Paris fell on 28 January 1871, leading to an armistice.

### Aftermath and Consequences
The war ended with the Treaty of Frankfurt (10 May 1871). France was forced to pay a five billion franc indemnity and cede most of Alsace and parts of Lorraine, which became the Imperial Territory of Alsace-Lorraine. The German states were proclaimed a unified German Empire under Prussian leadership. In France, the defeat ended imperial rule and began the Third Republic. The humiliation and political turmoil triggered the Paris Commune, a revolutionary government that seized power for two months before being violently suppressed. The war's outcome created lasting French revanchism (a desire for revenge) and fundamentally altered European geopolitics, establishing Germany as the new continental power.

## Terms
*   **Bismarck's Realpolitik:** A pragmatic, power-focused foreign policy pursued by Prussian Chancellor Otto von Bismarck, aimed at unifying Germany through calculated conflicts.
*   **Chassepot rifle:** The superior French breech-loading rifle with a longer range (1,500 m) than the Prussian Dreyse needle gun.
*   **Dreyse needle gun:** The Prussian breech-loading rifle, older and with shorter range (600 m) than the Chassepot, but used with effective tactics.
*   **Government of National Defence:** The provisional republican government formed in Paris on 4 September 1870 after the fall of Napoleon III.
*   **Krupp guns:** Superior Prussian steel breech-loading artillery with longer range and faster rate of fire than French muzzle-loading guns.
*   **Mitrailleuse:** An early French volley gun, a precursor to the machine gun, but used ineffectively as artillery.
*   **Revanchism:** A French political attitude after the war, characterized by a desire to reclaim lost territories (Alsace-Lorraine) and avenge the defeat.
*   **Siege of Metz:** The encirclement and eventual surrender of the French Army of the Rhine (173,000 men) on 26 October 1870.
*   **Treaty of Frankfurt:** The 1871 peace treaty that ended the war, imposing territorial losses and a heavy indemnity on France.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   Historians debate whether Bismarck deliberately provoked the war to unite Germany or simply exploited the circumstances as they unfolded. All agree he recognized the potential for new alliances.
*   The political motivations and military passivity of Marshal Bazaine during the Siege of Metz, including his alleged negotiations with Bismarck, remain a subject of historical criticism and debate.
*   The extent of war crimes committed by Prussian forces, including summary executions of prisoners and violence against civilians, is documented but the precise scale is part of the historical record.

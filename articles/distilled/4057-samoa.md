# Samoa

## Overview
Samoa is a Polynesian island country in the South Pacific, consisting of two main islands and several smaller ones. Its history is marked by early Lapita settlement, centuries of indigenous rule, and a colonial period under Germany and New Zealand before gaining independence in 1962. The nation is a parliamentary democracy that blends Christian principles with the traditional *faʻa Sāmoa* (Samoan way) of life, and it faces modern challenges in governance, health, and economic development.

## Timeline
- **c. 3500 years ago** — Lapita people discover and settle the Samoan Islands.
- **1722** — Dutch explorer Jacob Roggeveen becomes the first known non-Polynesian to sight the islands.
- **1830** — Christian missionary work begins with the arrival of John Williams.
- **1889** — A massive storm in Apia harbour ends a military standoff between colonial powers.
- **1899** — The Tripartite Convention divides the islands; western Samoa becomes German Samoa.
- **1914** — New Zealand troops occupy German Samoa at the start of World War I.
- **1918-1919** — A devastating influenza epidemic, introduced by a ship from New Zealand, kills about one-fifth of the population.
- **1929** — "Black Saturday": New Zealand police kill Mau movement leader Tupua Tamasese Lealofi III and others during a peaceful demonstration.
- **1962** — Western Samoa gains independence from New Zealand, becoming the first independent Polynesian nation.
- **1997** — The country officially changes its name from "Western Samoa" to "Samoa."
- **2009** — Samoa switches from driving on the right to driving on the left.
- **2021** — Fiamē Naomi Mataʻafa becomes Samoa's first female prime minister after a constitutional crisis.

## Body

### Early History and Colonial Era
The Samoan Islands were settled by the Lapita people around 3,500 years ago, who developed a distinct language and culture. Pre-colonial society was organized under paramount chiefly titles and lineages, with the four highest titles (*tama a ʻāiga*) forming the apex of the *matai* (chiefly) system. European contact began in the 18th century, but significant interaction started in the 1830s with missionaries, whalers, and traders. The late 19th century saw intense rivalry between the United States, Germany, and Britain for influence, leading to civil wars. The Tripartite Convention of 1899 formally divided the archipelago, with Germany taking the western islands and the U.S. the eastern islands (American Samoa).

### German and New Zealand Rule
German administration (1900–1914) suppressed traditional governance and faced the non-violent Mau a Pule resistance. After a bloodless New Zealand occupation in 1914, the territory was administered first as a League of Nations mandate and later as a UN Trust Territory. New Zealand's rule was marred by the 1918 influenza pandemic, which killed approximately 20% of the population due to a breach of quarantine. This tragedy fueled the Mau independence movement, which gained momentum through non-violent protest despite violent suppression, including the 1929 Black Saturday killings. The movement succeeded in securing political concessions and ultimately independence.

### Independence and Modern Samoa
Independence was achieved on 1 January 1962. The 1960 constitution established a parliamentary democracy that integrates the traditional *matai* system. The head of state is a paramount chief, and the unicameral legislature (*Fono*) is mostly composed of elected *matai*. In 1997, the country changed its official name from "Western Samoa" to "Samoa." The economy relies on agriculture, tourism, and remittances. Samoa has faced significant health crises, including a major measles outbreak in 2019. In 2017, the constitution was amended to declare Samoa a Christian nation. The 2021 election ended decades of rule by one party, though it precipitated a constitutional crisis resolved by the courts.

## Terms
- **Faʻa Sāmoa**: The traditional Samoan way of life, encompassing social, political, and cultural customs.
- **Matai**: A Samoan chief or person of rank; only *matai* can stand for election to the Samoan seats in the *Fono*.
- **Fono**: The unicameral legislative assembly of Samoa.
- **Mau**: A non-violent pro-independence movement that resisted colonial rule in the early 20th century.
- **Lapita**: A prehistoric Austronesian people who were the ancestors of modern Polynesians and settled the Pacific islands.
- **Itūmālō**: A political district; Samoa is divided into eleven of these traditional districts.
- **Tama a ʻāiga**: The four highest-ranking royal lineages in Samoa: Malietoa, Tupua Tamasese, Mataʻafa, and Tuimalealiʻifano.
- **O le Ao o le Malo**: The head of state of Samoa, a position held by a paramount chief.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The 2017 constitutional amendment making Christianity the state religion has been debated for its potential impact on the traditional separation of church and state and on minority faiths.
- The 2021 election and subsequent constitutional crisis highlighted tensions between the modern parliamentary system and the traditional *matai* authority, raising questions about democratic consolidation.
- The long-term economic impact of the 2021 time zone change (to UTC+13) to better align with Australia and New Zealand is still unfolding.
- The effectiveness of the 2025 Samoa Marine Spatial Plan in balancing conservation with economic needs is a subject of ongoing stakeholder consultation.

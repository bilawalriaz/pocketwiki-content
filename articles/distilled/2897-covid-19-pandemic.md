# COVID-19 pandemic

## Overview
The COVID-19 pandemic, caused by the SARS-CoV-2 virus, was a global health crisis that began in late 2019. It led to millions of deaths, severe economic disruption, and profound social changes, marking a defining event of the early 21st century. The pandemic prompted unprecedented public health responses, including rapid vaccine development, and highlighted global inequalities in health and resources.

## Timeline
- **Dec 2019** — Outbreak of pneumonia of unknown cause identified in Wuhan, China.
- **30 Jan 2020** — WHO declares a Public Health Emergency of International Concern (PHEIC).
- **11 Mar 2020** — WHO declares COVID-19 a pandemic.
- **9 Nov 2020** — Pfizer announces positive initial results for its vaccine candidate.
- **14 Dec 2020** — UK reports discovery of the Alpha variant.
- **24 Nov 2021** — Omicron variant detected in South Africa.
- **5 May 2023** — WHO downgrades COVID-19 from a global health emergency.
- **2025** — Experts generally consider the pandemic to have transitioned to an endemic phase.

## Body

### Origins and Naming
The outbreak was first identified in Wuhan, China, in December 2019, with early cases linked to a seafood market. The virus, SARS-CoV-2, is a coronavirus closely related to bat viruses. The WHO named the disease COVID-19 and the virus SARS-CoV-2 in February 2020. The scientific consensus points to a zoonotic origin, though other hypotheses have been proposed.

### Epidemiology and Disease
COVID-19 symptoms range from asymptomatic to severe respiratory illness. Transmission occurs primarily through airborne particles. The virus's basic reproduction number (R0) was initially estimated between 1.4 and 2.5, with later analysis suggesting it could be as high as 5.7. Official case counts are considered underestimates due to limited testing, especially early on. Mortality is measured by the infection fatality ratio (IFR), which varies significantly by age, from 0.002% at age 10 to 15% at age 85. Official death tolls are also undercounts; estimates of true deaths from COVID-19 range from 18.5 to 35.2 million by early 2024.

### Variants and Treatment
The virus has mutated into numerous variants, with the WHO designating key ones as Variants of Concern (VoC) or Interest (VoI), initially using Greek letters (e.g., Alpha, Delta, Omicron). For the first two years, treatment was primarily supportive. In 2021, the antiviral Paxlovid was approved. Vaccines were developed rapidly and deployed from December 2020, with over 5.6 billion people receiving at least one dose by August 2024. They are credited with preventing millions of deaths.

### Global Response and Impact
National responses varied from strict lockdowns and elimination strategies ("zero-COVID") to mitigation ("flattening the curve"). Measures included travel restrictions, mask mandates, and contact tracing. The pandemic caused the largest global recession since the Great Depression, widespread supply shortages, and a shift to remote work for many. It also led to misinformation, political tensions, and increased discrimination, particularly against people of Asian descent. Educational institutions worldwide were disrupted, and the environment saw a temporary reduction in pollution due to decreased human activity.

### Transition to Endemicity
The WHO ended the public health emergency in May 2023, though it continued to call COVID-19 a pandemic. By 2025, experts generally agreed the virus had become endemic, meaning it is a persistent, manageable part of global health. There is no universal agreement on when a pandemic officially ends, as it is both a biological and social phenomenon.

## Terms
*   **SARS-CoV-2:** Severe acute respiratory syndrome coronavirus 2; the virus that causes COVID-19.
*   **PHEIC:** Public Health Emergency of International Concern; a formal declaration by the WHO of an extraordinary event requiring a coordinated international response.
*   **R0 (Basic Reproduction Number):** The average number of people one infected person will spread the disease to in a susceptible population.
*   **IFR (Infection Fatality Ratio):** The proportion of deaths among all infected individuals, including asymptomatic and undiagnosed cases.
*   **CFR (Case Fatality Ratio):** The proportion of deaths among confirmed diagnosed cases.
*   **Variant of Concern (VoC):** A variant with evidence of increased transmissibility, more severe disease, or reduced effectiveness of vaccines and treatments.
*   **Endemic:** A disease that is constantly present in a population or region, as opposed to a pandemic, which is widespread across multiple countries or continents.
*   **Non-Pharmaceutical Interventions (NPIs):** Public health measures not involving drugs or vaccines, such as masking, social distancing, and lockdowns, used to slow disease spread.
*   **Long COVID:** A condition characterized by long-term symptoms persisting after the initial infection, such as fatigue and cognitive dysfunction.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Pandemic End Point:** There is no universally agreed-upon definition for when a pandemic ends. The WHO's 2023 decision to end the PHEIC was not a declaration that the pandemic was over, and experts continue to debate whether COVID-19 should still be described as a pandemic.
*   **Origin of the Virus:** While the scientific consensus favors a zoonotic origin, other hypotheses, including a potential laboratory incident, have been proposed and remain subjects of investigation and debate.
*   **True Mortality Toll:** Significant uncertainty remains around the exact global death toll, with estimates of excess mortality far exceeding official confirmed counts. Distinguishing deaths directly caused by COVID-19 from those caused by indirect consequences (e.g., overwhelmed healthcare systems) is an ongoing challenge.

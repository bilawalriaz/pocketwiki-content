# History of Peru

## Overview
Peru's history spans over 15,000 years, from early hunter-gatherers to the present. It features the rise of a major cradle of civilization, the vast Inca Empire, a transformative Spanish conquest, and a turbulent republican era marked by political instability, economic cycles, and internal conflict. Understanding this history is key to grasping modern South America's complex dynamics.

## Timeline
- **c. 3000 BC** — Norte Chico (Caral) civilization emerges, one of the world's cradles of civilization.
- **1438–1532** — The Inca Empire (Tahuantinsuyo) expands to become the largest pre-Columbian American state.
- **1532** — Francisco Pizarro arrives; the Spanish capture Inca ruler Atahualpa at Cajamarca.
- **1535** — The Spanish found the city of Lima.
- **1542** — The Spanish Crown establishes the Viceroyalty of Peru.
- **1821** — José de San Martín proclaims Peruvian independence in Lima.
- **1824** — The Battle of Ayacucho secures final independence from Spain.
- **1879–1883** — The War of the Pacific results in territorial losses to Chile.
- **1968** — General Juan Velasco Alvarado overthrows the government, initiating radical military reform.
- **1980–2000** — Internal conflict with Shining Path and MRTA insurgents; Alberto Fujimori's authoritarian presidency.
- **2021** — Pedro Castillo is elected president.
- **2022** — Castillo is impeached and removed; Dina Boluarte becomes the first female president.

## Body

### Pre-Hispanic Era
Human presence in Peru dates back over 11,000 years. The transition to settled agriculture began around 6000 BC with the domestication of plants like corn and animals like the llama. This led to complex societies, culminating in the Norte Chico civilization at Caral (c. 2500 BC), one of the world's earliest cradles of civilization. Subsequent cultures like the Moche, Nazca, and Wari developed advanced metallurgy, pottery, and social organization. These were precursors to the Inca Empire, which originated from a tribe in Cusco. Under Pachacuti, the Inca expanded into the Tahuantinsuyo, a vast empire with a sophisticated administrative system, road network, and Quechua as its official language.

### Spanish Conquest and Viceroyalty
The Spanish conquest began with Francisco Pizarro's arrival in 1531. A smallpox epidemic had already destabilized the Inca Empire, causing a civil war. Pizarro exploited this, capturing and executing Atahualpa in 1532. Spanish control consolidated with the founding of Lima (1535) and the establishment of the Viceroyalty of Peru (1542). The Spanish implemented the *encomienda* system, extracting tribute and labor from the indigenous population, whose numbers plummeted due to epidemic diseases. The Viceroyalty became the center of Spanish wealth, especially from Potosí's silver mines, but faced persistent indigenous rebellions, such as that of Túpac Amaru II in 1780.

### Independence and the Early Republic
Independence movements were inspired by the Napoleonic invasion of Spain. José de San Martín proclaimed independence in 1821, but final victory came only after Simón Bolívar's forces, led by Antonio José de Sucre, won the Battle of Ayacucho in 1824. The early republic was marked by political instability, military anarchy, and conflicts with neighbors. A brief Peru-Bolivian Confederation (1836–1839) was dissolved after a war. The mid-19th century saw prosperity from guano exports, ending with the devastating War of the Pacific (1879–1883), where Peru lost Tarapacá and Arica to Chile.

### The 20th Century: Instability and Conflict
The 20th century saw alternation between military and civilian rule. The "Aristocratic Republic" (1895–1919) gave way to Augusto Leguía's authoritarian *Oncenio*. The mid-century saw the rise of mass politics with parties like APRA. A military government under Juan Velasco Alvarado (1968–1975) enacted radical reforms, including agrarian reform. The 1980s and 1990s were dominated by the internal conflict with the Shining Path and MRTA, which claimed tens of thousands of lives. Alberto Fujimori's presidency (1990–2000) ended the insurgency but was marked by authoritarianism, human rights violations, and corruption, culminating in his resignation and exile.

### Contemporary Peru
The 21st century has focused on restoring democracy and addressing past violence. The Truth and Reconciliation Commission documented the internal conflict. Politically, the period has seen both stability and severe crisis, with multiple presidents since 2016. The COVID-19 pandemic exacerbated poverty and inequality. In 2022, President Pedro Castillo was impeached and removed after attempting a self-coup, leading to widespread protests under his successor, Dina Boluarte. Political instability continued with the ousting of interim presidents in 2025 and 2026, culminating in the election of Keiko Fujimori in 2026.

## Terms
- **Norte Chico (Caral):** One of the six cradles of civilization, located in the Supe Valley, dating to approximately 2500 BC.
- **Tahuantinsuyo:** The Quechua name for the Inca Empire, meaning "The Four United Regions."
- **Encomienda:** A Spanish colonial system granting colonists the right to demand tribute and forced labor from indigenous people in exchange for "protection" and religious instruction.
- **Viceroyalty:** A territory administered by a viceroy, a direct representative of the Spanish monarch.
- **Mita:** An Inca system of mandatory public service, adapted by the Spanish for forced labor in mines.
- **Caudillo:** A military or political leader, often a dictator, common in 19th-century Latin America.
- **APRA (American Popular Revolutionary Alliance):** A major Peruvian political party founded in 1924 by Víctor Raúl Haya de la Torre, with nationalist and social-democratic ideals.
- **Shining Path (Sendero Luminoso):** A Maoist insurgent group that waged a violent internal conflict in Peru from the 1980s until the early 2000s.
- **Fujimorato:** The period of Alberto Fujimori's presidency (1990–2000), characterized by economic shock therapy, authoritarianism, and the defeat of insurgent groups.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Origins of Civilization:** The discovery of complex sites like Caral challenges traditional timelines for the emergence of civilization in the Americas.
- **Impact of Conquest:** Historians debate the relative roles of disease, military technology, and internal divisions in the Spanish conquest of the Inca Empire.
- **Legacy of Internal Conflict:** The findings of the Truth and Reconciliation Commission regarding the causes and responsibility for the violence of 1980–2000 remain politically sensitive.
- **Political Instability:** The causes of Peru's chronic political instability since 2016, including the role of Congress, political parties, and social movements, are subjects of ongoing analysis.

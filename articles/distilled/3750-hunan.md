# Hunan

## Overview
Hunan is a major inland province in Central China, historically significant as a center of agriculture, scholarship, and revolution. It is the birthplace of Mao Zedong and numerous other influential Chinese leaders, and its people are credited with shaping much of modern Chinese history. Today, it is a populous province with a large economy and a rich cultural heritage.

## Timeline
- **c. 350 BC** — The area enters written Chinese history as part of the Zhou dynasty.
- **278 BC** — The Qin state conquers the region from Chu.
- **1664** — Hunan province is created from the former Huguang province.
- **1795–1806** — The Miao Rebellion occurs due to ethnic tensions.
- **1850–1864** — The Taiping Rebellion spreads into Hunan; the local Xiang Army helps suppress it.
- **1920** — A famine kills an estimated 2 million people.
- **1927** — Mao Zedong leads the Autumn Harvest Uprising, establishing a short-lived Hunan Soviet.
- **1934** — Communist guerrillas begin the Long March from the Hunan-Jiangxi border.
- **1944** — Changsha falls to Japanese forces during Operation Ichi-Go.
- **1949** — Communist forces return to Hunan as the Nationalists retreat south.
- **1966–1976** — Hunan actively supports the Cultural Revolution.
- **1992** — Wulingyuan is inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

## Body
### History and Settlement
Hunan was first inhabited by ancestors of modern Miao, Tujia, Dong, and Yao peoples. Chinese rule began around 350 BC. For centuries, it attracted Han Chinese migrants from the north, who displaced and assimilated indigenous groups and developed rice agriculture. This migration intensified during periods of northern disorder, such as the Eastern Jin dynasty and the Yuan dynasty invasions. The province was known for its scholarly activity, centered on the Yuelu Academy, and its agricultural surplus. It was also considered conservative and hostile to outsiders. Hunan was a key battleground during the Taiping Rebellion and later became a revolutionary base for Mao Zedong. As Mao's home province, it strongly supported the Cultural Revolution but was slower to adopt post-1976 economic reforms.

### Geography and Climate
Hunan is located on the south bank of the Yangtze River. Its terrain is over 80% mountains and hills, with plains in the center and north. The Xiang, Zi, Yuan, and Lishui rivers flow into Lake Dongting, China's second-largest freshwater lake, in the north. The climate is humid subtropical with hot, humid summers and cool, damp winters. The province's highest point is Lingfeng at 2,115.2 meters.

### Economy
Hunan is China's 10th-largest provincial economy. As of 2025, its nominal GDP was US$790 billion. Historically an agricultural exporter of goods like rhubarb and tea, it has become an important center for steel, machinery, electronics, and construction equipment manufacturing. The Lengshuijiang area is a major center for antimony extraction. The city of Liuyang is the world's top center for fireworks manufacturing.

### Demographics and Culture
As of the 2000 census, Hunan's population was 64.4 million, with 89.79% Han Chinese and 10.21% from minority groups, primarily Tujia and Miao. The province has complex dialects, including Xiang, Southwestern Mandarin, and Gan. Hunanese cuisine is known for its spicy, dry flavor from chili peppers. Huaguxi is a popular local form of Chinese opera. The province's cultural industries contributed 5.27% to its GDP in 2022.

### Education and Research
Hunan hosts 137 institutions of higher education. It is home to five Double First-Class universities: Hunan University, Central South University, Hunan Normal University, the National University of Defense Technology, and Xiangtan University. As of 2024, Changsha and Xiangtan ranked among the world's top 200 cities for scientific research output.

## Terms
- **Xiang River**: The major river flowing through Hunan, part of its largest drainage system.
- **Dongting Lake**: The largest lake in Hunan and second-largest freshwater lake in China.
- **Yuelu Academy**: One of China's four major ancient academies, now part of Hunan University.
- **Xiang Army**: A Hunanese army raised by Zeng Guofan that helped suppress the Taiping Rebellion.
- **Autumn Harvest Uprising**: A 1927 communist uprising led by Mao Zedong in Hunan.
- **Double First-Class University**: A Chinese university designation for institutions in world-class discipline construction.
- **Xiang**: The abbreviation for Hunan on vehicle license plates, from the Xiang River.
- **Wulingyuan**: A UNESCO World Heritage Site in Hunan noted for quartzite sandstone pillars.

## Debates and Open Questions
The source notes that early travelers regarded Hunan as "the most conservative and virulently hostile to outsiders," and the Jesuits considered it the "most impenetrable part" of China, suggesting a historical reputation for insularity and resistance to outside influence.

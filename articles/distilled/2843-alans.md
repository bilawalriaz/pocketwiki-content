# Alans

## Overview
The Alans were an ancient Iranic nomadic people, part of the Sarmatian group, who migrated from Central Asia to the North Caucasus and later into Europe and North Africa. They were renowned for their elite cavalry and played a significant role in the Migration Period, influencing the military tactics of the Romans and Germanic tribes. The Alans are considered the ancestors of the modern Ossetians.

## Timeline
- **1st century CE** — Alans dominate the Sarmatians on the Pontic-Caspian steppe, raiding Parthia and Roman provinces.
- **215–250 CE** — Goths break Alan dominance on the Pontic Steppe, assimilating many Alans.
- **375 CE** — Hunnic defeat of the Goths triggers a westward migration of many Alans and Germanic tribes.
- **406 CE** — Alans cross the Rhine into Gaul with Vandals and Suebi.
- **409 CE** — Alans cross the Pyrenees into the Iberian Peninsula.
- **418 CE** — Visigoths defeat the Iberian Alans, who then submit to the Vandal king.
- **428 CE** — Vandals and Alans cross into North Africa, founding a kingdom.
- **9th century** — Alans in the Caucasus establish the Christian Kingdom of Alania.
- **1239–1277** — Most Caucasian Alans submit to the Mongol Empire.
- **1395** — Tamerlane's invasion destroys the Kingdom of Alania, forcing Alans into the Caucasus mountains.

## Body

### Origins and Early History
The Alans formed from the merger of the Massagetae and other Iranian tribal groups. They were pushed west by the Kangju people from the Syr Darya basin. By the 1st century CE, they had migrated to the Pontic-Caspian steppe, becoming the dominant Sarmatian group. Roman sources first mention them in this period, noting raids into the Parthian Empire and Roman provinces like Armenia and Media. Archaeological evidence supports their entry into the Sarmatian area between the 1st and 2nd centuries CE.

### Migration and Settlement in Europe
After the Hunnic invasion of the steppe around 375 CE, many Alans migrated west. Some joined the Goths in the Battle of Adrianople (378 CE). A major group crossed the Rhine into Gaul in 406 CE with Vandals and Suebi. In Gaul, Alans split: one group under Goar settled in the Orleanais and allied with Rome, while another under Respendial continued with the Vandals into Iberia in 409 CE. In Iberia, the Alans were defeated by the Visigoths in 418 CE and merged with the Vandal kingdom. In 428 CE, they crossed into North Africa with the Vandals, where their rulers later styled themselves "King of the Vandals and Alans."

### Medieval Alania and Legacy
Alans who remained in the Caucasus eventually formed the Kingdom of Alania, a regional power that converted to Byzantine Orthodoxy in the 10th century. They were pressured by Bulgars, Khazars, and later the Mongols, who invaded in the 13th century. Many Alans were recruited into Mongol armies, serving as elite guards. The kingdom was finally destroyed by Tamerlane in 1395, forcing the Alans into the mountains. These Caucasian Alans are the direct ancestors of the modern Ossetians. Genetic studies on Alanic burials show haplogroups like G2 and R1a, supporting this lineage.

### Warfare and Culture
The Alans were famed for their highly mobile cavalry. They used tactics like the feigned retreat to draw enemies into vulnerable positions. As mercenaries, they demonstrated sophisticated combined-arms tactics, using missile cavalry to pin enemies before a shock charge. Their military influence impacted Roman and Germanic armies. They spoke an Eastern Iranian language, Alanic, which evolved into modern Ossetian. The name "Alan" derives from the Old Iranian term for "Aryan."

## Terms
- **Sarmatians**: A group of ancient Iranic nomadic peoples from whom the Alans emerged.
- **Pontic-Caspian steppe**: The vast grassland region north of the Black Sea and Caspian Sea, a heartland for nomadic groups like the Alans.
- **Feigned retreat**: A cavalry tactic where warriors pretend to flee to lure enemies into a vulnerable position before counterattacking.
- **Kingdom of Alania**: A medieval Christian state in the North Caucasus, established by Alans in the 9th century.
- **Ossetians**: An ethnic group in the Caucasus considered the direct descendants of the Alans.
- **Aryan**: A self-designation of Indo-Iranian peoples, from which the name "Alan" is derived.
- **Haplogroup G2**: A Y-DNA genetic lineage found in Alanic burials and common among modern Ossetians.
- **Assianism**: A modern revival of the traditional Ossetian polytheistic religion.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The exact relationship between the Alans, Aorsi, and Yancai mentioned in Chinese sources is debated, though many scholars connect them.
- The extent of Alanic influence on Gothic culture and military tactics after the 3rd century is a subject of study.
- The primary cause of the Alans' apostasy from Christianity in 932, as reported by al-Mas‘udi, is unclear.
- The location and historical reality of the 12-year Mongol siege of an Alan mountain fortress, reported by Giovanni da Pian del Carpine, is uncertain.

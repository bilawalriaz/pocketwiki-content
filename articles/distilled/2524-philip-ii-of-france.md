# Philip II of France

## Overview
Philip II Augustus (r. 1180–1223) was the first French king to style himself "King of France," transforming the realm from a fragmented feudal territory into the most powerful and prosperous kingdom in Europe. His reign culminated in the decisive Battle of Bouvines (1214), which destroyed the Angevin Empire of England and established the unchallenged authority of the French crown.

## Timeline
- **21 August 1165** — Born in Gonesse, son of Louis VII and Adela of Champagne.
- **1 November 1179** — Crowned and anointed king at Reims.
- **28 April 1180** — Married Isabella of Hainault, bringing the County of Artois as dowry.
- **1185** — Acquired the County of Amiens, beginning major expansion of the royal demesne.
- **1190–1192** — Participated in the Third Crusade, returning early after the Siege of Acre.
- **1194–1199** — Fought a war with Richard I of England over Normandy and other territories.
- **May 1200** — Signed the Treaty of Le Goulet with King John of England.
- **1202–1204** — Conquered Normandy and most of the Angevin lands in France from John.
- **1208–1229** — Allowed vassals to participate in the Albigensian Crusade against Cathars in the south.
- **27 July 1214** — Decisive victory over a coalition of England, Flanders, and the Holy Roman Empire at the Battle of Bouvines.
- **14 July 1223** — Died at Mantes-la-Jolie, succeeded by his son Louis VIII.

## Body

### Consolidation of Royal Power
Philip systematically expanded the royal demesne (the crown's direct lands) through purchase, inheritance, and conquest, acquiring territories like Amiens, Clermont, and Alençon. He built a professional army funded by demesne revenues, which by his reign's end could muster thousands of knights, sergeants, and urban militiamen. He also established the first active French navy. Domestically, he curbed noble power, granted privileges to towns and the emerging bourgeoisie, and built the Wall of Philip II Augustus around Paris, enhancing its security and status.

### Wars with the Plantagenets
Philip's primary foreign policy goal was the destruction of the Angevin Empire held by the Plantagenet kings of England. He exploited dynastic disputes, befriending and then turning Henry II's sons against him. After Henry II's death, he fought Richard I, gaining and losing territories in Normandy. Following Richard's death, Philip skillfully maneuvered against King John, using feudal law to declare John's lands forfeit for failing to appear at court. By 1204, Philip had conquered Normandy, Anjou, and much of Aquitaine, reducing English holdings in France to a small coastal strip.

### The Battle of Bouvines and Its Aftermath
In 1214, Philip faced a grand coalition: King John invaded from the west, while Holy Roman Emperor Otto IV and the Count of Flanders attacked from the north. Philip's victory at Bouvines was total. The battle shattered the coalition, led to Otto's overthrow, and ended English hopes of recovering lost territories. The victory cemented French royal authority and had profound consequences in England, where the weakened John was forced by his barons to sign the Magna Carta in 1215.

### Domestic Policy and Legacy
Philip transformed Paris into a European capital, paving its main streets, building the Louvre fortress, and founding the University of Paris in 1200. He reversed his father's tolerance of Jews, expelling them from the demesne in 1182 (though allowing their return in 1198). He did not directly lead the Albigensian Crusade but permitted his vassals to participate, which ultimately facilitated the French crown's expansion into the south of France—a process completed by his son and grandson.

## Terms
- **Demesne**: The land directly owned and administered by the king, as opposed to lands held by vassals.
- **Angevin Empire**: The collection of territories in France and the British Isles ruled by the Plantagenet dynasty of England.
- **Liege Lord**: The superior feudal lord to whom a vassal owes primary allegiance and service.
- **Suzerainty**: The right of a feudal lord to exercise authority over a vassal's territory.
- **Interdict**: A papal sanction that suspends religious services in a territory, used as a political tool.
- **Bourgeoisie**: The emerging class of town-dwelling merchants and professionals, distinct from the nobility and peasantry.
- **Capetian**: The royal dynasty of France to which Philip II belonged, founded by Hugh Capet.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The exact reasons for Philip's immediate hostility and annulment attempt after his marriage to Ingeborg of Denmark are unclear, with claims of consanguinity or personal aversion debated.
- The extent to which Philip's victory at Bouvines directly caused the Magna Carta in England is a matter of historical interpretation, as internal baronial pressures were also key.
- The role of Philip's personal piety versus political opportunism in his complex relationship with the papacy (e.g., over the Albigensian Crusade and his marital issues) is analyzed by scholars.

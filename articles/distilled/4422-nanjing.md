# Nanjing

## Overview
Nanjing is a major city in eastern China and the capital of Jiangsu province. It is one of the Four Great Ancient Capitals of China, having served as the seat of government for numerous dynasties and regimes from the 3rd century to 1949. Its strategic location on the Yangtze River has made it a perennial center of culture, education, politics, and commerce, though its history is also marked by periods of devastating conflict.

## Timeline
- **c. 480 BCE** — Earliest named urban foundations, Yuecheng and Yecheng, are attributed to this period.
- **229–280** — Serves as the capital of Eastern Wu during the Three Kingdoms period.
- **317–589** — Functions as the capital for the Eastern Jin and Southern dynasties (Jiankang).
- **589** — Sui dynasty conquers and demolishes Jiankang.
- **937–975** — Capital of the Southern Tang kingdom.
- **1368–1421** — Capital of the Ming dynasty under the Hongwu Emperor.
- **1421** — Capital of the Ming dynasty is moved to Beijing; Nanjing becomes a secondary capital.
- **1842** — Treaty of Nanking is signed, ending the First Opium War.
- **1853–1864** — Capital of the Taiping Heavenly Kingdom (as Tianjing).
- **1912** — Briefly serves as the capital of the Republic of China.
- **1927–1937** — Capital of the Republic of China during the "Nanjing Decade."
- **1937–1938** — Site of the Nanjing Massacre during the Second Sino-Japanese War.
- **1949** — Captured by Communist forces; becomes the capital of Jiangsu province in 1952.

## Body

### Historical Significance and Names
Nanjing's prominence stems from its repeated role as a national capital. The name "Nanjing" (Southern Capital) was formally established in 1368. Earlier names include Jinling (Ridge with the Gold), used from the 4th century BCE, and Jiankang, the name during the Six Dynasties period (3rd–6th centuries). The city was a major cultural and Buddhist center during the Southern Dynasties, with a population possibly exceeding 300,000 by the early 6th century. After its demolition by the Sui dynasty in 589, it was rebuilt as the capital of the Southern Tang (937–975) and later as the primary capital of the Ming dynasty (1368–1421), when its walls and layout were established.

### Ming, Qing, and Modern Eras
After the Ming capital moved to Beijing in 1421, Nanjing remained a secondary capital and a thriving commercial hub. It was the site of the Treaty of Nanking (1842) and later became the capital of the Taiping Heavenly Kingdom (1853–1864). In the late Qing, it was a center for modernization and education. The Republic of China made it the national capital in 1927, launching the ambitious "Capital Plan" for urban modernization. The city suffered the Nanjing Massacre (1937–38) after its fall to Japanese forces. It was recaptured by the Communists in 1949 and became the provincial capital of Jiangsu in 1952.

### Geography and Climate
Nanjing is situated in the Yangtze River Delta, with the Yangtze River flowing past its west and north sides. It has a humid subtropical climate with four distinct seasons, hot summers, and cold winters. It is traditionally counted among the "Three Furnaces" of the Yangtze for its summer heat. The city is rich in mineral resources, including strontium, and has abundant water resources from the Yangtze and groundwater.

### Economy and Education
Nanjing's economy is dominated by the service sector, particularly finance, culture, and tourism. It is a major industrial and R&D hub, especially in electronics, IT, and pharmaceuticals. As of 2021, the city had 68 institutions of higher learning, including 13 double-first-class universities. Nanjing University is ranked among the world's top 10 by the Nature Index. The city has the fifth-largest scientific research output globally.

### Culture and Transport
As an ancient capital, Nanjing is a major cultural center with numerous heritage sites, museums, and libraries. It is a transport hub with a major inland port, an international airport, and an extensive metro system. The city hosted the 2014 Summer Youth Olympics.

## Terms
- **Jiankang**: The name of Nanjing during the Six Dynasties period (317–589), when it was the capital of successive southern regimes.
- **Taiping Heavenly Kingdom**: A rebel state that controlled large parts of southern China from 1851 to 1864, with Nanjing (renamed Tianjing) as its capital.
- **Nanjing Massacre**: The mass murder and rape of civilians by Japanese troops after the fall of Nanjing in December 1937, with death toll estimates between 300,000 and 350,000.
- **Capital Plan**: An ambitious urban modernization scheme for Nanjing launched by the Nationalist government in 1927, designed with American assistance.
- **Double First-Class**: A Chinese government initiative to develop world-class universities and disciplines.
- **Nature Index**: A database that tracks institutional output in high-quality natural science journals, used to rank research performance.
- **Sub-provincial city**: A Chinese administrative division with a status slightly below a province, enjoying greater autonomy than a prefecture-level city.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The exact locations and chronologies of the earliest named urban foundations, Yuecheng and Yecheng, attributed to the 5th century BCE, remain subjects of academic debate.
- The total death toll of the Nanjing Massacre is a subject of historical and political contention, with estimates ranging from 300,000 to 350,000 according to post-war tribunals.
- During the early Ming, the court was divided over whether Nanjing should remain the permanent capital, with proposals to relocate to other cities abandoned in 1375 and 1392.

# Vancouver

## Overview
Vancouver is a major city in Western Canada, located in the Lower Mainland region of British Columbia. It is the most populous city in the province and the third-largest metropolitan area in Canada. The city is renowned for its high population density, ethnic diversity, and consistent ranking as one of the world's most liveable cities, though it also faces significant challenges with housing affordability.

## Timeline
- **8,000-10,000 years ago** — Archaeological records indicate Aboriginal people were living in the Vancouver area.
- **1791** — Spanish explorer José María Narváez explores the coast of present-day Point Grey and Burrard Inlet.
- **1867** — A makeshift tavern, the nucleus of the future city, is built by "Gassy Jack" on the edge of the Hastings Mill property.
- **1870** — The colonial government surveys the settlement and renames it "Granville".
- **1884** — The site is selected as the terminus for the Canadian Pacific Railway.
- **1886** — The City of Vancouver is incorporated and renamed; the Great Vancouver Fire razes the entire city.
- **1887** — The Canadian Pacific transcontinental railway is extended to the city.
- **1929** — Amalgamation with Point Grey and South Vancouver gives the city its final boundaries.
- **1986** — Expo 86 World Exposition is held in Vancouver.
- **2010** — Vancouver co-hosts the Winter Olympics and Paralympics with Whistler.

## Body

### History and Development
Indigenous settlement in the area began over 10,000 years ago, with the Squamish, Musqueam, and Tsleil-Waututh peoples establishing villages. European exploration began in the late 18th century, but the first permanent settlement, originally called Gastown, started around a tavern in 1867. The settlement was renamed Granville and later Vancouver in 1886, the same year it was incorporated and devastated by a fire. The city's growth was fueled by its selection as the western terminus of the Canadian Pacific Railway, which arrived in 1887, establishing it as a vital trade and transportation hub. The economy was initially dominated by logging and resource exports, later expanding into manufacturing and commerce.

### Demographics and Diversity
Vancouver is one of Canada's most ethnically and linguistically diverse cities. As of the 2021 census, 49.3% of residents are not native English speakers, and 54.5% belong to visible minority groups. The largest visible ethnic group is Chinese, with significant communities also from the Philippines, India, and other Asian countries. The city has a large immigrant population, with 42.2% of residents born outside Canada. It also has a significant Indigenous community and a large LGBT community, with a recognized gay enclave in the West End.

### Economy and Industry
Vancouver's economy is diverse, with its large natural seaport being a key asset. The Port of Vancouver is Canada's busiest and most diversified port. While forestry remains the largest industry, tourism is the second-largest, attracting over 10 million visitors in 2017. The city is also a major film and television production centre, earning the nickname "Hollywood North," and hosts headquarters for various lifestyle and technology companies. However, Vancouver faces severe challenges with housing affordability, consistently ranking as one of the most expensive cities in the world.

### Urban Planning and Geography
The city is characterized by the urban planning philosophy of "Vancouverism," which emphasizes high-density, mixed-use development in urban centres to avoid sprawl. This approach, which began in the late 1950s, has contributed to its high livability rankings. Geographically, Vancouver is located on the Burrard Peninsula, surrounded by water and mountains, including the North Shore Mountains. It has a mild, oceanic climate and is known for its urban parks, such as Stanley Park, and its integration with the surrounding natural environment.

### Government and Politics
Vancouver is governed under a unique charter and has a political landscape that has shifted over time. Historically dominated by the centre-right Non-Partisan Association (NPA), the city has seen significant victories for left-leaning parties like Vision Vancouver and the Coalition of Progressive Electors (COPE). A political consensus has emerged around issues like protecting urban parks, developing rapid transit, and harm-reduction drug policies. The city is part of the Metro Vancouver regional government, which oversees shared services.

## Terms
- **Vancouverism**: An urban planning design philosophy characterized by high-rise residential and mixed-use development in urban centres, with strict requirements for setbacks and open space to preserve livability.
- **Metro Vancouver**: The regional government body for the Greater Vancouver area, responsible for services like drinking water, sewage, and regional planning.
- **Lower Mainland**: A geographic and cultural region in southwestern British Columbia that includes Vancouver and the Fraser Valley.
- **Hollywood North**: A nickname for Vancouver (and Toronto) due to its large film and television production industry.
- **Gastown**: The original settlement that became the nucleus of Vancouver, founded around a tavern in 1867.
- **Canadian Pacific Railway (CPR)**: The transcontinental railway whose western terminus was established in Vancouver in the 1880s, driving the city's early growth.
- **Coast Salish**: The group of Indigenous peoples, including the Squamish, Musqueam, and Tsleil-Waututh, who have lived in the Vancouver area for millennia.
- **EcoDensity**: A 2006 City of Vancouver planning initiative exploring how density, design, and land use can contribute to environmental sustainability and affordability.

## Debates and open questions
- **Housing Affordability**: Vancouver is consistently ranked as one of the most expensive cities in the world, with ongoing debates about causes (e.g., foreign investment, "snow washing") and solutions (e.g., increased density, cooperative housing).
- **Media Ownership**: There is concern about the concentration of media ownership, with major newspapers owned by Postmedia Network, which has spurred the growth of independent online media alternatives.
- **Economic Future**: Since the COVID-19 pandemic, some economists have warned of a potential long-term "economic doom loop" for Vancouver, drawing comparisons to the decline of San Francisco.

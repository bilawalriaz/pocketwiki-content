# Region

## Overview
Geographic regions are broad portions of Earth's surface defined by physical, human, or environmental characteristics. They are fundamental concepts in geography for organizing and understanding spatial patterns, though their boundaries are often imprecise and conceptual rather than legally fixed. The study of these regions, called regional geography, helps analyze everything from ecosystems to cultural identities and political structures.

## Timeline
- **c. 3000 BC** — Early terrestrial cartography begins to distinguish global land and water regions.
- **1492–1800** — Historical geographer D.W. Meinig defines "source regions" of European colonization in early America.
- **1975–1996** — Scotland uses local government regions as administrative units.
- **2007** — Denmark implements a new regional administrative structure.
- **2011** — Regions of England cease to be administrative units, becoming statistical only.

## Body

### Definition and Scope
A region is a portion of Earth's surface broadly divided by physical features (physical geography), human impact (human geography), or their interaction (environmental geography). Unlike more confined "locations" or "places," regions typically have imprecise, sometimes transitory boundaries. Their areal extent is often measured in square kilometers or hectares. The concept is used across geography's branches, with specific terms like ecoregion, cultural region, and bioregion.

### Global and Continental Regions
The largest global regions are the continents (land) and oceans (water). Continental regions are often conceptual constructs based on human history and broad experiences, lacking distinct physical boundaries. They are mental constructs created to manage and study large areas, influenced by academic study, media, and personal exploration. Some are defined by major features like the Amazon basin or Sahara.

### Regional Geography
Regional geography is the branch that studies regions of all sizes, focusing on defining their unique character through natural and human elements. It involves regionalization, the technique of delimiting space into regions. This approach is considered a distinct method within geographical sciences.

### Human Geography Regions
Human geography examines patterns of human interaction with environments, leading to various region types:
*   **Historical Regions:** Defined by past human activity and settlement patterns, such as Meinig's "Northwest European Atlantic Protestant Region" or "Greater New England" in America, and Siberia in Russia.
*   **Tourism Regions:** Designated by governments or bureaus based on common characteristics to attract visitors, like Tuscany or the Lake District.
*   **Natural Resource Regions:** Areas defined by resource occurrence, such as the Rumaila oil field or the Pennsylvania Coal Region, which also become economic and cultural regions.
*   **Religious Regions:** Areas associated with a religion, like Christendom or the Muslim world, or formally defined ecclesiastical areas like dioceses.
*   **Political Regions:** Based on political units, from sovereign states to subnational units like provinces and multinational groupings like the EU.
*   **Administrative Regions:** Formal subnational entities used by many countries (e.g., Chile's *región*, France's *région*, Russia's *oblast*). The term derives from Latin *regio* ("to rule").
*   **Functional Regions:** Areas organized by internal flows and interactions (e.g., travel-to-work areas), defined by the principle of self-containment.
*   **Military Regions:** Shorthand for an Army Region, a large formation between an Army Group and a Theater, used rarely due to its size.

### Other Region Types
*   **Traditional/Informal Regions:** Common territorial divisions that define local identity but are not formal administrative units, as in parts of England, Japan, and the United States.
*   **Media Geography:** Studies spatial attributes through media outputs, analyzing how images and representations shape understanding of places.

## Terms
*   **Region:** A portion of Earth's surface defined by broad physical, human, or environmental characteristics, with often imprecise boundaries.
*   **Regional Geography:** The branch of geography that studies regions, focusing on their unique character and the techniques of delimiting them.
*   **Human Geography:** The study of patterns and processes shaping human interaction with environments, encompassing social, cultural, and economic aspects.
*   **Physical Geography:** The study of natural features and processes of the Earth's surface, such as landforms and ecosystems.
*   **Environmental Geography:** The study of the interaction between humanity and the environment.
*   **Ecoregion:** A term used in environmental geography for a region defined by ecosystems.
*   **Cultural Region:** A term used in cultural geography for a region defined by shared cultural traits.
*   **Functional Region:** An area organized by internal flows and interactions, defined by the principle of self-containment.
*   **Administrative Region:** A formal subnational entity used for governance in many countries.

## Debates and Open Questions
The source text notes that continental regions are "conceptual constructs, usually lacking distinct boundaries" and are "mental constructs" derived from various sources of knowledge. This implies an ongoing scholarly discussion about how these large-scale regions are defined and perceived.

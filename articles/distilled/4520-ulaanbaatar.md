# Ulaanbaatar

## Overview
Ulaanbaatar is the capital and largest city of Mongolia, home to nearly half the country's population. It is the world's coldest capital city by average yearly temperature and serves as Mongolia's cultural, industrial, and financial heart. The city's history as a nomadic Buddhist monastic center that became a permanent settlement reflects Mongolia's own journey from a theocratic state to a modern democracy.

## Timeline
- **1639** — Founded as a nomadic Buddhist monastic center named Örgöö.
- **1727** — Treaty of Kyakhta regulates Qing-Russian trade, establishing a caravan route through the city.
- **1778** — The city settles permanently at its modern location.
- **1911** — Following Qing collapse, the city becomes the capital of the independent Bogd Khanate.
- **1921** — Communist Soviet-Mongolian forces capture the city, ending the Bogd Khanate.
- **1924** — Renamed Ulaanbaatar and declared capital of the new Mongolian People's Republic.
- **1950s** — Modern urban planning begins, replacing ger districts with Soviet-style flats.
- **1990** — Large demonstrations in the city lead to Mongolia's transition to democracy and a market economy.
- **2008** — Deadly riots occur following disputed parliamentary elections.
- **2021** — Chinggis Khaan International Airport opens south of the city.

## Body

### Names and History
The city was founded in 1639 as a mobile yurt monastery called Örgöö ("Palace"), later known as Urga in the West. It moved 29 times before settling in 1778 at its current location on the Beijing-Kyakhta caravan route. It was the seat of the Jebtsundamba Khutuktu, the spiritual head of Tibetan Buddhism in Mongolia. After the 1911 independence, it was renamed Niislel Khüree ("Capital Khüree"). In 1924, it was officially renamed Ulaanbaatar ("Red Hero"), possibly in honor of Damdin Sükhbaatar, following pressure from a Soviet Comintern representative.

### Modern Development and Challenges
Modern urban planning began in the 1950s, with Soviet-style construction. Since the 1990 democratic transition, rapid migration from rural areas has caused explosive population growth, from 535,000 in 1990 to over 1.5 million by 2021. Much of this growth is in "ger districts" (areas of traditional yurts) lacking basic infrastructure. This, combined with excessive coal use for heating, makes Ulaanbaatar one of the world's most polluted cities in winter, causing severe respiratory illnesses.

### Geography and Climate
Located at about 1,350 meters elevation in a valley on the Tuul River, Ulaanbaatar has a cold semi-arid climate. It is the coldest capital city globally, with an average annual temperature of 0.2 °C. It lies in a zone of discontinuous permafrost. The city is surrounded by protected mountains, including the sacred Bogd Khan Mountain.

### Governance and Economy
Ulaanbaatar is an independent municipality governed by a city council. It is Mongolia's primate city, responsible for about two-thirds of the national GDP. The economy is dominated by service industries (43% of GDP) and mining (25%). Major corporations are headquartered here.

### Culture and Infrastructure
The city is a cultural hub with monasteries like Gandantegchinlen, museums, and theaters. It is the country's primary transport hub, served by the Trans-Mongolian Railway and Chinggis Khaan International Airport. Severe traffic congestion is a major problem, with average peak-hour speeds of 8.9 km/h in 2021.

## Terms
- **Ger district**: An area of Ulaanbaatar where residents live in traditional yurts (gers), often lacking modern infrastructure like plumbing and central heating.
- **Jebtsundamba Khutuktu**: The spiritual head of the Gelug lineage of Tibetan Buddhism in Mongolia.
- **Primate city**: A city that is disproportionately larger and more influential than any other city in the country.
- **Permafrost**: Ground that remains frozen for two or more consecutive years.
- **Semi-arid climate**: A climate with low precipitation, lying between arid and humid climates.
- **Trans-Mongolian Railway**: The rail line connecting Ulaanbaatar with Moscow and Beijing.
- **Naadam**: A traditional Mongolian festival featuring wrestling, horse racing, and archery.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The exact reason for the 1924 renaming to Ulaanbaatar is debated; it was possibly in honor of Damdin Sükhbaatar, but the name was pushed by a Soviet official.
- The effectiveness of the government's 2019 ban on raw coal burning in favor of coke briquettes to reduce pollution is inconclusive, as smoke remains a major concern.
- The demolition of historic buildings since 2013, despite public outcry, raises ongoing questions about balancing development with cultural preservation.

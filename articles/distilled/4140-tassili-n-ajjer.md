# Tassili n'Ajjer

## Overview
Tassili n'Ajjer is a vast sandstone plateau in southeastern Algeria, covering over 72,000 km². It is globally significant for containing one of the most important collections of prehistoric rock art, documenting the transition from a habitable savanna to the modern Sahara Desert. The site is a UNESCO World Heritage Site and Biosphere Reserve, valued for its unique geology, ecology, and archaeological record.

## Timeline
- **c. 750–550 million years ago** — Formation of the Tuareg Shield, the geological foundation for the region.
- **541–485 million years ago** — First sediment deposits in the area during the Cambrian period.
- **458–443 million years ago** — Sahara region located near the South Pole; a major ice sheet forms.
- **443 million years ago onward** — Ice melts, leaving meltwater channels; formation of the Inner Tassili Group sandstone.
- **Silurian–Devonian periods** — Transgression (sea advance) deposits layers of sediment, forming the Intra and External Tassili Groups.
- **Miocene (23–5.3 million years ago)** — Magmatic uplift arches the Tuareg Shield, tilting Tassili's layers and causing deep river erosion.
- **Pliocene (5.3–2.58 million years ago)** — Volcanism covers the southwestern Adrar region with craters and lava flows.
- **c. 10,000–7500 BP** — Hunter-gatherer occupation during the early Holocene.
- **c. 7500–4000 BCE** — Transition to pastoralism; first domesticated cattle appear in the archaeological record.
- **1910** — Prehistoric rock art at Tassili first reported.
- **1930s** — French legionnaires, notably Lt. Charles Brenans, bring the site to wider Western attention.
- **1956–1970** — Archaeologist Henri Lhote conducts major (and later controversial) expeditions to document the art.
- **1982** — Tassili n'Ajjer designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

## Body

### Geology and Geography
The plateau is composed primarily of sandstone, stained by desert varnish (a thin layer of metallic oxides). Erosion has created nearly 300 natural rock arches, deep gorges, and permanent water pools. Its geological history spans from the Neoproterozoic formation of the Tuareg Shield to Pliocene volcanism. The region's uplift and erosion, particularly during the Miocene, carved the dramatic "rock forests" and river systems that define its landscape today.

### Ecology
Despite its desert location, the plateau's higher elevation and water-retentive sandstone support a more verdant habitat. It features open woodland with endangered endemic trees like the Saharan cypress and myrtle. The area is an Important Bird Area and historically hosted isolated populations of species like the West African crocodile. The aoudad (Barbary sheep) is the only extant animal depicted in the ancient rock art.

### Archaeological Context and Pastoralism
The site was primarily occupied during the Neolithic by transhumant pastoralists. Archaeological evidence shows a shift from hunter-gatherer groups (10,000–7500 BP) to pastoral societies after 7500 BP, coinciding with an unpredictable climate. The rock art, studied alongside other Saharan sites, documents the introduction and spread of animal husbandry: cattle herding (3000–2000 BCE), followed by horses (before 1000 BCE), and finally camels, which enabled long-distance trans-Saharan trade.

### Prehistoric Rock Art
The art, dating from approximately 12,000 years ago, is divided into five stylistic traditions:
1.  **Archaic (10,000–7500 BCE):** Etchings of large wild animals and hunting scenes by hunter-gatherers.
2.  **Round Head (7550–5050 BCE):** Paintings of large, floating humanoid figures with featureless heads, often in ritual contexts.
3.  **Bovidian/Pastoral (4500–4000 BCE):** Depicts domesticated cattle, sheep, goats, and diverse herding communities, reflecting societal change.
4.  **Horse (from 2000 BCE):** Shows horses, chariots, and riders, associated with hunting. Includes indecipherable Libyan-Berber script.
5.  **Camel (from 1000 BCE):** Features camels as the primary transport, alongside men with weapons, reflecting the rise of trade caravans.

A famous example is the "Running Horned Woman" from the Round Head period, interpreted as an early depiction of the "African Sacred Feminine."

### Fungoid Art Theory
A controversial theory, proposed by Giorgio Samorini and popularized by Terence McKenna, suggests some fungoid-like paintings depict the ritual use of psychoactive mushrooms. Proponents cite figures holding mushroom-like objects or with mushrooms growing from their bodies as evidence. Critics, including archaeologist Henri Lhote and writer Brian Akers, dispute this interpretation, arguing the images are not realistic representations of known psychoactive fungi.

## Terms
*   **Desert varnish:** A thin, dark coating of manganese and iron oxides deposited on rock surfaces in arid environments.
*   **Transhumant pastoralism:** A form of nomadic herding involving seasonal movement of livestock between fixed grazing areas.
*   **Parietal art:** Rock art made on the walls or ceilings of caves and rock shelters.
*   **Neolithic:** The final stage of the Stone Age, characterized by the development of agriculture and pottery.
*   **Holocene:** The current geological epoch, beginning approximately 11,700 years ago after the last glacial period.
*   **Bovidian:** Relating to or depicting cattle (bovids).
*   **Terrane:** A fragment of crustal material formed on, or broken off from, one tectonic plate and accreted to the crust of another.
*   **Accretion:** The gradual growth of a landmass by the addition of material to its margins.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Dating and Chronology:** The exact dating of the rock art periods is flexible and subject to ongoing reconstruction as new archaeological technology and interpretation develop.
*   **Fungoid Art Interpretation:** The theory that certain paintings depict the use of psychedelic mushrooms remains highly debated, with critics arguing the imagery is not a clear representation of fungi.
*   **Lhote's Methods:** The work of archaeologist Henri Lhote is criticized for potential damage to original paintings during tracing and photography, and accusations of faking some images.

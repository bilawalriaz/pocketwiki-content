# Yellowstone National Park

## Overview
Yellowstone National Park, established in 1872, is the world's first national park, renowned for its geothermal features and wildlife. It sits atop a massive supervolcano, the Yellowstone Caldera, whose ongoing volcanic activity fuels the park's famous geysers and hot springs. As the centerpiece of the Greater Yellowstone Ecosystem, it represents one of the largest nearly intact temperate ecosystems on Earth.

## Timeline
- **c. 11,000 years ago** — Earliest evidence of Native American presence in the region (Clovis point).
- **1807–1808** — John Colter, a Lewis and Clark expedition member, becomes the first known American to enter the area.
- **1870** — The Washburn–Langford–Doane Expedition explores the region and names Old Faithful geyser.
- **1871** — The Hayden Geological Survey provides a comprehensive report that convinces Congress to protect the area.
- **March 1, 1872** — President Ulysses S. Grant signs the Yellowstone National Park Protection Act, establishing the park.
- **1886–1916** — The U.S. Army is commissioned to manage and protect the park.
- **1917** — Administration is transferred to the newly created National Park Service.
- **1959** — The 7.2-magnitude Hebgen Lake earthquake, the region's most powerful in recorded history, damages park infrastructure.
- **1978** — Yellowstone is designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
- **1988** — Massive forest fires burn over one-third of the park, leading to a major revision of fire management policies.
- **1995–2003** — Yellowstone is placed on the List of World Heritage in Danger due to tourism impacts and invasive species.
- **June 2022** — Record rainfall and flooding cause widespread damage, forcing a multi-day park closure.

## Body

### Human History and Establishment
Native Americans have used the Yellowstone region for at least 11,000 years, mining obsidian and managing the ecosystem with low-intensity fires. Early non-native accounts of its geothermal wonders were often dismissed as rumors. The first detailed expeditions in 1869 and 1870 generated media attention, and the 1871 Hayden Survey, with its photographs and paintings, provided the crucial evidence for Congress. The park was established in 1872, but early management was underfunded. The U.S. Army took over protection in 1886, building Fort Yellowstone and implementing policies that formed the basis for the National Park Service, which assumed control in 1917.

### Geology and Volcanism
The park's unique landscape is created by the Yellowstone hotspot, a mantle plume beneath the North American tectonic plate. This system has produced three cataclysmic eruptions in the last 2.1 million years, the most recent 640,000 years ago, which formed the 45-by-28-mile Yellowstone Caldera. This "supervolcano" has a magma chamber estimated to be 37 miles long and 3 to 7 miles deep. Its ongoing heat fuels over 10,000 hydrothermal features, including two-thirds of the world's geysers. The area experiences thousands of small earthquakes annually, with six magnitude 6+ events in recorded history.

### Ecology and Wildlife
Yellowstone is the core of the 20-million-acre Greater Yellowstone Ecosystem, the largest intact temperate ecosystem in the world. It is a premier megafauna habitat, home to grizzly bears, wolves, and the largest public bison herd in the U.S., which grew from fewer than 50 in 1902 to 6,000 in 2023. Wolves were eradicated by 1926 but were successfully reintroduced starting in the 1990s. The park's ecology is a dynamic balance; for example, the recovery of aspen forests is linked to wolves altering elk grazing patterns. Threats include invasive species like lake trout, which prey on native cutthroat trout, and diseases like brucellosis in bison and elk.

### Fire Management and Climate
Wildfire is a natural ecological process in Yellowstone. Lodgepole pines, the dominant tree, depend on fire to open their cones and clear ground for growth. A catastrophic drought in 1988 led to fires that burned 793,000 acres, or over one-third of the park. This event, while causing minimal wildlife mortality, fundamentally changed fire policy to allow natural fires to burn unless they threaten people or property. The park's climate varies greatly with altitude, from warm summers to severe winters, and it is vulnerable to extreme weather, including a powerful F4 tornado in 1987 and record flooding in 2022.

## Terms
*   **Caldera:** A large cauldron-like hollow that forms shortly after the emptying of a magma chamber in a volcanic eruption. The Yellowstone Caldera was formed by a supereruption 640,000 years ago.
*   **Supervolcano:** A volcano capable of producing an eruption with an ejecta volume greater than 1,000 cubic kilometers. Yellowstone is considered a supervolcano.
*   **Geothermal:** Relating to heat produced within the Earth. Yellowstone's geysers, hot springs, and fumaroles are fueled by the heat from the Yellowstone hotspot.
*   **Megafauna:** Large or giant animals. Yellowstone is the most famous megafauna location in the contiguous United States.
*   **Greater Yellowstone Ecosystem:** The 20-million-acre region centered on Yellowstone National Park, considered the largest nearly intact ecosystem in the Earth's northern temperate zone.
*   **Brucellosis:** A bacterial disease that can cause miscarriage in cattle. It is present in Yellowstone's bison and elk herds, creating conflict with ranchers.
*   **Parkitecture:** A rustic architectural style used by the National Park Service, exemplified by the Old Faithful Inn, designed to harmonize with the natural environment.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Bison Management:** There is ongoing conflict between conservationists and ranchers over the management of bison that leave the park, primarily due to fears of brucellosis transmission to livestock.
*   **Grizzly Bear Protections:** The legal status of the grizzly bear has been contested, with its listing under the Endangered Species Act being removed, reinstated, and removed again through court battles.
*   **Future Volcanic Activity:** While the time between Yellowstone's last three supereruptions was 600,000 to 800,000 years, scientists caution that this small sample size cannot be used to accurately predict future events.
*   **Wolf Reintroduction:** The reintroduction of wolves in the 1990s was controversial but is now seen as a key factor in restoring ecological balance, such as influencing elk behavior and aiding aspen recovery.

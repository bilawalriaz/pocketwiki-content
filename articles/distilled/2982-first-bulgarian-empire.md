# First Bulgarian Empire

## Overview
The First Bulgarian Empire was a medieval state in Southeastern Europe (681–1018 AD). Founded by Bulgar tribes under Asparuh, it evolved from a pagan khanate into a major Christian empire and primary Byzantine rival. Its significance lies in its military power, adoption of Christianity and the Slavic script, and its emergence as the foremost cultural and spiritual center of Slavic Europe during its Golden Age.

## Timeline
- **680–681** — Asparuh's Bulgars defeat Byzantine Emperor Constantine IV; Byzantium recognizes Bulgaria as an independent state.
- **705** — Khan Tervel assists Byzantine Emperor Justinian II, gaining territory and the title "Caesar."
- **717–718** — Bulgarian army helps break the Arab siege of Constantinople.
- **811** — Byzantine Emperor Nicephorus I is killed at the Battle of Varbitsa Pass; his skull is used as a drinking cup by Khan Krum.
- **864** — Khan Boris I converts to Christianity, adopting the name Michael.
- **893** — Council of Preslav establishes Old Bulgarian as the state language and moves the capital to Preslav.
- **895–896** — Simeon I defeats the Magyars, forcing their migration to Pannonia, and defeats the Byzantines at Boulgarophygon.
- **917** — Simeon I's crushing victory at the Battle of Anchialus establishes Bulgarian military supremacy in the Balkans.
- **927** — Byzantine Empire recognizes the Bulgarian Patriarchate and Simeon I's title of Emperor (Tsar).
- **968–971** — Kievan Rus' under Sviatoslav I invades, capturing Preslav and making Bulgaria a vassal.
- **1014** — Byzantine Emperor Basil II defeats the Bulgarians at the Battle of Kleidion and blinds 15,000 prisoners.
- **1018** — Last Bulgarian strongholds surrender; the First Bulgarian Empire is annexed by the Byzantine Empire.

## Body

### Establishment and Early Conflicts
The state was founded in 681 when Bulgar tribes, led by Asparuh, settled south of the Danube and defeated Byzantine forces, securing recognition and annual tribute. The early period involved consolidation and conflict with Byzantium. Under Khan Tervel, Bulgaria aided Byzantium against the Arabs in 717–718. Internal instability followed the end of the Dulo clan in 753, which Byzantine Emperor Constantine V exploited with nine campaigns. Stability was restored after Khan Kardam's victory at Marcellae in 792.

### Expansion and the Golden Age
Under Khan Krum (r. 803–814), Bulgaria doubled in size, eliminating the Avar Khaganate and expanding into Thrace and Macedonia. His victory over Byzantine Emperor Nicephorus I in 811 was a major triumph. After a period of peace under Omurtag, expansion continued under Boris I, who converted to Christianity in 864. This decision, influenced by political and internal factors, aligned Bulgaria with the Christian world and strengthened the monarch's authority. The Council of Preslav in 893 cemented this shift by adopting Old Bulgarian as the liturgical language.

The empire reached its zenith under Simeon I (r. 893–927), who sought to create a joint Bulgarian-Byzantine state. His military victories, including the decisive Battle of Anchialus in 917, led to Byzantine recognition of his imperial title and the Bulgarian Patriarchate in 927. His successor, Peter I, presided over a 40-year period of peace and cultural flourishing.

### Decline and Fall
The empire's strategic position grew difficult, surrounded by Magyars, Pechenegs, Kievan Rus', and Byzantium. In 968, the Byzantines incited a Rus' invasion, leading to the capture of Preslav and the abdication of Emperor Boris II in 971. Resistance continued in the west under the Cometopuli brothers, with Samuel eventually becoming emperor. He fought a 40-year war against Byzantium, initially winning a major victory at the Battle of the Gates of Trajan in 986.

The tide turned under Byzantine Emperor Basil II, who waged a war of attrition. The decisive Battle of Kleidion in 1014, followed by the blinding of 15,000 prisoners, broke Bulgarian resistance. Samuel died shortly after, and by 1018, the empire was fully annexed by Byzantium.

### Society, Government, and Economy
The state was a hereditary monarchy. The ruler's title evolved from Khan to Knyaz (Prince) and finally Tsar (Emperor). The government included high officials like the *kavhan* (First Minister) and *ichirgu-boila*. Society was stratified into the nobility (*boila*), a second aristocratic class (*bagains*), and the common people. The economy was primarily agricultural, supplemented by animal husbandry, mining, and extensive trade with Byzantium, the Rus', and Central Europe. Bulgaria was highly urbanized for its time, with capitals like Pliska and Preslav being major centers.

### Religion and Culture
Before 864, the state was pagan, with the Bulgars worshipping the sky god Tangra and the Slavs worshipping deities like Perun. Christianity was adopted in 864, leading to the creation of an autonomous Bulgarian Church, which became a Patriarchate in 927. This fostered a cultural golden age, with the creation of the Cyrillic script in the Preslav Literary School and a flourishing of Old Church Slavonic literature. A dualist heresy, Bogomilism, also emerged during this period. The state's cultural output, known as the Pliska-Preslav culture, included distinctive architecture, ceramics, and art like the Madara Rider relief.

## Terms
*   **Bulgars:** Turkic semi-nomadic warrior tribes who founded the state and formed its initial ruling elite.
*   **Khan/Knyaz/Tsar:** Titles for the Bulgarian monarch, evolving from a tribal leader (Khan) to a prince (Knyaz) and finally an emperor (Tsar).
*   **Boila:** The highest-ranking noble class in early Bulgarian society, later transformed into the *bolyar* class.
*   **Old Church Slavonic:** The first Slavic literary language, based on the dialect of Thessaloniki, used for liturgy and literature after Christianization.
*   **Glagolitic Alphabet:** The first Slavic alphabet, created by Saints Cyril and Methodius, later largely replaced by the Cyrillic script.
*   **Cyrillic Alphabet:** A script developed in the Preslav Literary School, based on the Greek alphabet with added letters for Slavic sounds.
*   **Patriarchate:** The highest level of an autocephalous (independent) Orthodox Church, granted to the Bulgarian Church in 927.
*   **Bogomilism:** A dualist heretical sect that originated in Bulgaria during the 10th century, rejecting the material world as evil.
*   **Zadruga:** A rural community of peasants with collective responsibility, forming the base of the agricultural economy.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   The exact nature of the relationship between the initial Bulgar settlers and the local Slavic tribes is debated. While some sources suggest a treaty, most historians agree the Slavs were politically subjugated but allowed to retain customs in exchange for tribute and military service.
*   The precise reasons behind Khan Boris I's decision to convert to Christianity in 864 are not explicitly stated in sources, though historians infer political, diplomatic, and internal social factors.
*   The ethnic composition and size of Asparuh's initial Bulgar force are uncertain, with estimates ranging from around 10,000 to a considerably larger tribe.

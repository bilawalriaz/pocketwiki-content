# Muhammad al-Bukhari

## Overview
Al-Bukhari (810–870 CE) was a 9th-century Muslim scholar of hadith (sayings and actions of the Prophet Muhammad) from Bukhara. He is widely regarded as the most important hadith scholar in Sunni Islam, primarily for compiling *Sahih al-Bukhari*, the most authentic collection of hadith after the Quran. His life and work were central to the canonization of Sunni orthodoxy and the science of hadith criticism.

## Timeline
- **21 July 810 (13 Shawwal 194 AH)** — Born in Bukhara, present-day Uzbekistan.
- **c. 821 CE** — Began studying hadith.
- **826 CE (age 16)** — Performed the Hajj pilgrimage with his mother and brother.
- **c. 828 CE** — Wrote *Qadhāyas-Sahābah wa at-Tābi'īn* and began *Al-Tārīkh al-Kabīr* in Medina.
- **846 CE** — Compiled the first version of *Sahih al-Bukhari* in Firabrī.
- **Later years** — Was exiled from Nishapur, possibly due to theological disputes or scholarly jealousy.
- **1 September 870 (1 Shawwal 262 AH)** — Died in Khartank, near Samarkand.

## Body

### Early Life and Education
Al-Bukhari was born into a Persian family of hadith scholars; his father died when he was an infant. He memorized vast numbers of hadith narrations from a young age. He traveled extensively across the Abbasid Caliphate, studying under prominent scholars like Ahmad ibn Hanbal and Ishaq ibn Rahwayh, and is said to have memorized over 600,000 hadith narrations.

### Major Works
His magnum opus is *Sahih al-Bukhari*, a collection of approximately 7,563 hadith narrations considered by Sunnis to be the most authentic book after the Quran. It is one of the *Kutub al-Sittah* (six major hadith collections) and, with *Sahih Muslim*, forms the *Sahihayn* (the two authentic collections). He also authored *Al-Tārīkh al-Kabīr* (a biographical dictionary of hadith narrators), *Al-Adab al-Mufrad* (a collection on ethics), and theological treatises like *Khalq Af'āl al-'Ibād*.

### Theological Controversy and Later Life
Al-Bukhari was involved in the *mihna* (inquisition) over the createdness of the Quran. He held that the human act of reciting the Quran was created, a position that led to his exile from Nishapur. He spent his final years teaching and died in Khartank. His tomb is now part of the Imam Bukhari Mausoleum in Uzbekistan.

### School of Law and Theology
Scholars debate his legal affiliation. Some, like Jonathan Brown, identify him with the Ahl al-Hadith (traditionalists) following Ahmad ibn Hanbal. Others, like Scott Lucas, suggest his legal principles were similar to the Zahiri and Hanbali schools. Many scholars, including Al-Dhahabi, consider him an independent *mujtahid* (independent legal scholar). Theologically, some scholars, such as Christopher Melchert, link him to the Kullabi school, which held that the Quran is uncreated but interpreted certain attributes of God metaphorically.

## Terms
*   **Hadith**: A record of the traditions or sayings of the Prophet Muhammad, revered and considered sacred by Muslims.
*   **Muhaddith**: A scholar of hadith, specializing in its collection, authentication, and interpretation.
*   **Sahih**: An authentic or sound hadith collection, meeting strict criteria of chain of transmission and content.
*   **Kutub al-Sittah**: The six major collections of hadith in Sunni Islam.
*   **Mihna**: An inquisition or doctrinal test imposed by Abbasid authorities, notably over the createdness of the Quran.
*   **Mujtahid**: A Muslim jurist qualified to exercise independent reasoning (ijtihad) in Islamic law.
*   **Ahl al-Hadith**: A traditionalist school of thought emphasizing the primacy of hadith over analogical reasoning (qiyas) in law.
*   **Kullabi**: An early theological school that held the Quran is uncreated but interpreted God's attributes metaphorically.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Reason for Exile**: Was al-Bukhari exiled from Nishapur due to his theological stance on the createdness of human recitation of the Quran, or because of the jealousy of other scholars, as asserted by Al-Dhahabi and Taj al-Din al-Subki?
*   **Legal Affiliation**: Was al-Bukhari primarily a Hanbali traditionalist, a Zahiri, a Shafi'i, or an independent mujtahid who did not adhere to any of the four major madhhabs (schools of law)?
*   **Theological School**: To what extent was al-Bukhari a follower of the Kullabi school of theology, and how did this influence his hadith selections and interpretations?

# Lyon

## Overview
Lyon is France's third-largest city, located at the confluence of the Rhône and Saône rivers. It is a major economic, cultural, and historical hub, renowned for its gastronomy, silk production history, and role in the invention of cinema. Its historic districts are a UNESCO World Heritage Site, and it will host the 2030 Winter Olympics.

## Timeline
- **43 BC** — Roman settlement founded on Fourvière hill as Colonia Copia Felix Munatia, later called Lugdunum.
- **461** — Lugdunum becomes capital of the Kingdom of the Burgundians.
- **14th century** — Lyon comes under French control.
- **Late 15th century** — Italian merchants introduce fairs, making Lyon France's economic counting house.
- **1572** — St. Bartholomew's Day Massacre violence against Huguenots occurs in Lyon.
- **1793** — City besieged and renamed "Commune-Affranchie" during the French Revolution.
- **1831 & 1834** — Major uprisings by silk workers (canuts) for better conditions.
- **1895** — Auguste and Louis Lumière invent the cinematograph in Lyon.
- **1998** — Historic site of Lyon inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
- **2015** — Metropolis of Lyon, a new metropolitan authority, is formed.
- **2030** — Lyon will be the centre of the Winter Olympics.

## Body

### History and Identity
Lyon's history begins with its Roman foundation in 43 BC as Lugdunum, a strategic communications hub that became the capital of Gallia Lugdunensis. The city's name likely derives from the Celtic god Lug and the word for "hill." After periods under Burgundian and Holy Roman rule, Lyon entered the Kingdom of France in the 14th century. The Renaissance brought economic prosperity through banking and the silk trade, strengthening ties with Italy. The city suffered severe violence during the 1572 St. Bartholomew's Day Massacre and was brutally suppressed during the French Revolution in 1793. The 19th century saw industrial growth and social unrest, notably the canuts' uprisings. In World War II, Lyon was both a centre of Nazi occupation and a stronghold of the French Resistance, using its traboules (secret passageways) for escape.

### Geography and Administration
Lyon is defined by the confluence of the Rhône and Saône rivers, creating the Presqu'île peninsula. Key geographical features include the western hill of Fourvière ("the hill that prays") and the northern hill of La Croix-Rousse ("the hill that works"). The commune of Lyon is divided into nine arrondissements. Since 2015, it has been the core of the Metropolis of Lyon, a directly elected authority governing 58 suburban municipalities. This unique territorial collectivity is the only one of its kind in France.

### Economy and Institutions
Lyon has a diversified economy with major sectors in banking, chemicals, pharmaceuticals, biotechnology, and software, including video games. It hosts the international headquarters of Interpol and the International Agency for Research on Cancer (IARC). The city is the second-largest student centre in France, with nearly 200,000 students. Key business districts include La Part-Dieu and the Cité Internationale. Tourism is significant, driven by events like the Fête des lumières (Festival of Lights) and the city's gastronomic reputation.

### Culture and Landmarks
Lyon's historic centre, encompassing Roman ruins, Renaissance Vieux Lyon, the silk-weaving slopes of Croix-Rousse, and the Presqu'île, is a UNESCO World Heritage Site. The city is famed for its gastronomy, with traditional bouchon restaurants and dishes like quenelle and salade lyonnaise. The Lumière brothers pioneered cinema here in 1895. Cultural institutions include the Musée des Beaux-Arts and the Musée des Confluences. The annual Fête des lumières celebrates the city's title as "Capital of Lights."

### Transport
Lyon is a major transport hub. Lyon–Saint-Exupéry Airport serves international flights, connected to the city by the Rhônexpress tram. The city has two main railway stations, Lyon-Part-Dieu and Lyon-Perrache, and was the first French city linked to Paris by TGV in 1981. Its public transit system (TCL) includes four metro lines, eight tram lines, and an extensive bus network. The Vélo'v bicycle-sharing system, introduced in 2005, was the first of its kind in France.

## Terms
- **Traboules**: Narrow passageways through buildings and courtyards, historically used by silk workers and later by the French Resistance.
- **Canuts**: The silk workers of Lyon, known for their 19th-century uprisings.
- **Presqu'île**: The peninsula formed by the confluence of the Rhône and Saône rivers, forming the city's core.
- **Metropolis of Lyon**: A directly elected metropolitan authority formed in 2015, governing Lyon and 58 suburban communes.
- **Bouchon**: A traditional Lyonnais restaurant serving local fare.
- **Fête des lumières**: An annual four-day festival of lights held in December.
- **Gallo-Roman**: Relating to the culture of Gaul under Roman rule.
- **Franco-Provençal**: A Romance language historically spoken in the region, distinct from both French and Provençal.

## Debates and open questions
The etymology of "Lyon" (Lugdunum) is debated, with theories linking it to the Celtic god Lug ("hill of Lug"), a word for "crow" ("hill of crows"), or a term for "light" ("shining hill"). The formation of the Metropolis of Lyon's electoral wards, which group suburban communes and divide the city of Lyon into six wards, has been criticized by suburban mayors for diluting their representation, though proponents argue it ensures fairer population-based representation.

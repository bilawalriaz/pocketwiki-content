# Alhambra

## Overview
The Alhambra is a palace and fortress complex in Granada, Spain, begun in 1238 CE. It is the only well-preserved palace from the medieval Islamic world and a masterpiece of Moorish architecture. Its significance lies in its continuous history as a royal residence, first for the Nasrid dynasty (the last Muslim rulers of al-Andalus) and later for Spanish monarchs, making it a layered monument of Islamic and Renaissance art and a symbol of cultural transition.

## Timeline
- **c. 9th century** — First reference to a fortress, Qal‘at al-Ḥamra, on the Sabika hill.
- **1238 CE** — Muhammad I Ibn al-Ahmar begins construction of the Alhambra as a new citadel and residence.
- **1302–1309** — Muhammad III builds the Partal Palace, the oldest surviving Nasrid palace.
- **1314–1325** — Isma'il I begins the "classical" Nasrid architectural period, starting the Comares Palace complex.
- **1333–1354** — Yusuf I constructs the Hall of Ambassadors and the Puerta de la Justicia.
- **1354–1391** — Muhammad V builds the Palace of the Lions, marking the apogee of Nasrid architecture.
- **January 1492** — The last Nasrid sultan, Muhammad XI, surrenders Granada to the Catholic Monarchs.
- **1527–1537** — Charles V builds a Renaissance-style palace within the complex.
- **1812** — French troops under Napoleon damage the Alhambra, attempting to dynamite it upon retreat.
- **1832** — Washington Irving publishes *Tales of the Alhambra*, spurring international interest.
- **1923–1936** — Leopoldo Torres Balbás begins scientific, systematic restoration.
- **1984** — The Alhambra and Generalife are designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

## Body

### Origins and Nasrid Construction
The Alhambra was built on the Sabika hill, a site with earlier fortresses. Construction was initiated in 1238 by Muhammad I, founder of the Nasrid dynasty, the last Muslim state in al-Andalus. The complex was transformed into a self-contained palatine city with a sophisticated water supply system. The most significant architectural campaigns occurred in the 14th century under Yusuf I and Muhammad V, who created the defining royal palaces: the Comares Palace and the Palace of the Lions.

### Architecture and Decoration
Nasrid architecture is characterized by courtyards with central water features, intricate interior decoration, and a harmonious proportional system. Decoration focused on interiors, using tile mosaics (zilīj) on lower walls and carved stucco (yesería) on upper walls, featuring geometric patterns, vegetal motifs, and Arabic inscriptions. A key feature is muqarnas, a "stalactite"-like sculpting used for vaulted ceilings. The complex includes the Alcazaba fortress, the Mexuar (administrative area), the Comares Palace (state palace), and the Palace of the Lions (private royal residence).

### Christian and Modern History
After the 1492 conquest, the Alhambra became a royal court for Ferdinand and Isabella. Charles V later built a Renaissance palace within the complex. The site fell into disrepair, suffering damage from Napoleon's troops in 1812. Its modern revival began with 19th-century Romantic travelers, notably Washington Irving. Systematic restoration started in the 20th century under Leopoldo Torres Balbás, who reversed earlier inaccurate additions. It is now a major tourist attraction and UNESCO site.

### Key Structures and Features
The Alcazaba is the oldest part, a fortress with the Torre de la Vela watchtower. The Nasrid Palaces include the Court of the Myrtles with its reflective pool, and the Hall of Ambassadors, the largest room. The Palace of the Lions is famed for its courtyard with the Fountain of the Lions and halls with elaborate muqarnas domes. The Generalife, outside the walls, was the Nasrid summer palace and estate. The complex's water system, the Acequia del Sultan, drew from the Darro River.

## Terms
- **Moorish architecture**: The style of Islamic architecture developed in al-Andalus (Islamic Iberia).
- **Nasrid dynasty**: The last Muslim dynasty to rule in the Iberian Peninsula, based in Granada (1238–1492).
- **Alcazaba**: The fortified citadel or military quarter of the complex.
- **Muqarnas**: A form of ornamented vaulting in Islamic architecture, resembling honeycombs or stalactites.
- **Zilīj (Alicatado)**: Mosaic tilework made from individually cut pieces, used for wall decoration.
- **Yesería**: Carved stucco decoration, often with intricate geometric and vegetal patterns.
- **Mirador**: A small room projecting from a building, designed to provide scenic views.
- **Rawda**: The royal mausoleum of the Nasrid dynasty.
- **Acequia**: An irrigation canal or water channel.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The original Arabic names of most Nasrid-era buildings are unknown; many current names (e.g., Palace of the Lions) are later inventions.
- The exact chronology of construction is difficult to determine due to the use of fragile materials and continuous modifications by later rulers.
- The function of the large "Alhambra vases" is unclear; they may have been purely decorative.

# Mogadishu

## Overview
Mogadishu is the capital and most populous city of Somalia, located on the Indian Ocean coast. It has been a major port and commercial hub for millennia, connecting Indian Ocean trade networks. Its history includes periods of great prosperity as a medieval sultanate, followed by colonial rule, independence, civil war, and recent reconstruction.

## Timeline
- **1st century AD** — Ancient port of Sarapion, a predecessor to Mogadishu, is mentioned in the *Periplus of the Erythraean Sea*.
- **9th–13th century** — Mogadishu Sultanate rises as a pre-eminent power, controlling the Indian Ocean gold trade.
- **13th century** — The city comes under the Ajuran Sultanate, an important player in medieval Silk Road maritime trade.
- **14th–15th centuries** — Mogadishu enjoys its height of prosperity and is described as a wealthy, populous city by travelers like Ibn Battuta.
- **17th century** — The Ajuran Sultanate collapses; the Hiraab Imamate takes control of Mogadishu and parts of southern Somalia.
- **Late 19th century** — The Sultanate of the Geledi becomes the dominant power on the Benadir Coast, with influence over Mogadishu.
- **1894** — A Somali chief signs a treaty with the Italian Commercial Company of Benadir, beginning Italian influence.
- **1905** — Italy establishes Mogadishu as the capital of Italian Somaliland.
- **1 July 1960** — Mogadishu becomes the capital of the newly independent Somali Republic.
- **21 October 1969** — A military coup led by Siad Barre establishes the Somali Democratic Republic.
- **1991** — The Somali Civil War begins, devastating the city.
- **August 2011** — Al-Shabaab militants withdraw from Mogadishu, allowing a period of major reconstruction to begin.

## Body

### Ancient and Medieval Foundations
Mogadishu's predecessor, the ancient port of Sarapion, was part of a network of Somali city-states engaged in trade with the Roman Empire and other Indian Ocean partners. The city's founding ethnicity is debated; modern scholarship rejects older Arab/Persian founding myths, emphasizing pre-existing indigenous Somali communities. By the 13th century, it was a leading Islamic and commercial center. The Mogadishu Sultanate, which later became part of the Ajuran Empire, dominated the regional gold trade, minted currency, and was described by 14th-century traveler Ibn Battuta as an exceedingly large, wealthy city ruled by a Somali sultan. The city's textile industry, producing *toob benadir* cloth, was renowned.

### Early Modern and Colonial Periods
After the Ajuran Sultanate's collapse in the 17th century, the Hiraab Imamate ruled Mogadishu for about two centuries. By the late 19th century, the Geledi Sultanate became the dominant power on the coast. Italian influence began with treaties in the 1880s, leading to direct colonial rule after 1906. Italy made Mogadishu the capital of Italian Somaliland in 1905, developing infrastructure like a railway and roads. After World War II, the city became the capital of the UN Trust Territory of Somaliland, administered by Italy until independence in 1960.

### Civil War and Reconstruction
Following independence, Mogadishu was the capital of the Somali Republic until a 1969 coup brought Siad Barre to power. His regime's collapse in 1991 triggered a devastating civil war, with factional fighting and international intervention, including the 1993 Battle of Mogadishu. The city was largely controlled by militant groups until 2006, when the Islamic Courts Union took over. After Ethiopian and African Union intervention, the Transitional Federal Government re-established control in 2007. Al-Shabaab insurgents continued violence until their withdrawal from Mogadishu in 2011. Since then, a period of reconstruction has been led by the municipal government, the diaspora, and international partners like Turkey, involving infrastructure projects, new businesses, and a construction boom.

## Terms
- **Sultanate**: A state ruled by a sultan, a sovereign monarch.
- **Ajuran Sultanate**: A medieval Somali empire that controlled much of the Horn of Africa and Indian Ocean trade.
- **Hiraab Imamate**: A powerful 17th-20th century kingdom in southern and central Somalia that succeeded the Ajuran.
- **Geledi Sultanate**: A Somali kingdom that dominated the Benadir Coast in the 18th and 19th centuries.
- **Italian Somaliland**: A colony of the Kingdom of Italy from 1908 to 1960.
- **Transitional Federal Government (TFG)**: The internationally recognized central government of Somalia from 2004 to 2012.
- **Al-Shabaab**: A militant Islamist group that fought against the Somali government and international forces.
- **Benadir**: A historical coastal region of Somalia; the modern Banaadir region is coextensive with Mogadishu.
- **Toob benadir**: A type of specialized cotton cloth historically produced in Mogadishu for export.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The founding ethnicity of Mogadishu and its early sultanate is debated. Older theories suggested Arab or Persian founders, but modern scholarship, based on archaeology and oral sources, indicates pre-existing indigenous Somali communities with whom immigrants integrated.
- The origin of the name "Mogadishu" is uncertain. Theories include derivation from Somali words meaning "sight killer" or "place where sheep are slaughtered," or a link to the Hebrew word for "holy place."

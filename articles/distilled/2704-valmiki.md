# Valmiki

## Overview
Valmiki is a legendary Hindu sage and the traditional author of the *Ramayana*, one of Hinduism's two great epics (*Itihasas*). Revered as *Ādi Kavi* ("the first poet"), he is credited with composing the first Sanskrit *shloka* (verse) and the foundational epic that has shaped Indian culture for millennia.

## Timeline
- **c. 8th–4th centuries BCE** — Scholars' estimates for the earliest stage of the *Ramayana* text.
- **c. 3rd century CE** — Later stages of the *Ramayana* text extend to this period.
- **Treta Yuga (mythical)** — According to the *Vishnudharmottara Purana*, Valmiki is born as a form of Brahma to compose the *Ramayana*.
- **Traditional** — Valmiki is born as the tenth son of the god Varuna after his seed falls into a termite hill (*valmīka*).
- **Traditional** — As a young Brahmin named Lohajangha, he becomes a thief, is reformed by the Saptarishi sages, and meditates until an anthill forms around him, earning his new name.
- **Traditional** — While bathing, he witnesses a hunter kill a mating crane, prompting his spontaneous utterance of the first *shloka* in grief and rage.
- **Traditional** — He composes the entire *Ramayana* in the same meter.
- **Traditional** — He shelters the exiled Sita, who gives birth to his disciples, the twins Lava and Kusha, to whom he teaches the *Ramayana*.
- **Post-Mahabharata war** — He is alive during the Mahabharata era and advises Yudhisthira to worship Shiva.
- **Later tradition** — He is believed to be reincarnated as the poet Tulsidas, who composed the *Ramcharitamanas*.
- **Ashvin full moon day** — Celebrated as Valmiki Jayanti (Pargat Diwas), his birth anniversary.

## Body

### Authorship and the *Ramayana*
Valmiki is celebrated as the author of the *Ramayana*, a *smriti* epic of 24,000 *shlokas* across seven *kaṇḍas* (cantos). The text itself attributes the work to him. The epic narrates the story of Prince Rama of Ayodhya, whose wife Sita is abducted by the demon-king Ravana of Lanka. Scholars estimate the text's composition spanned from the 8th to 4th centuries BCE for its earliest layers, with later stages extending to the 3rd century CE, though its original date is unknown due to a history of interpolations and redactions.

### Origin as the First Poet
Legends describe Valmiki's transformation into a sage. One account states he was born Varuna's son; another says he was a Brahmin thief named Lohajangha who was reformed by the Saptarishi sages and meditated until an anthill formed around him. His status as *Ādi Kavi* stems from the moment he uttered the first *shloka*. While going to bathe, he saw a hunter kill a male crane, causing its mate to die of grief. From his sorrow, a spontaneous couplet emerged, which he later used as the meter for the entire *Ramayana*.

### Role in the Epics
Within the *Ramayana*, Valmiki is central to the *Uttarakāṇḍa* (last canto), where he shelters the exiled Sita and teaches the epic to her twin sons, Lava and Kusha. Scholars note this canto may not be original to Valmiki, as its narrative is often indirect. He also appears in the *Mahabharata* era, advising King Yudhisthira on worship after the great war. Later texts, like the *Vishnudharmottara Purana*, describe him as a divine incarnation of Brahma and a precursor to the poet Tulsidas.

## Terms
- **Itihasa**: A class of Hindu epic literature, literally meaning "thus indeed it was," referring to historical or legendary narratives.
- **Smriti**: "That which is remembered," a category of Hindu texts considered secondary to *shruti* (revealed scripture), including epics and law codes.
- **Shloka**: A Sanskrit verse, typically a couplet in a specific meter, considered the first poetic form in Sanskrit literature.
- **Ādi Kavi**: "The first poet," a title given to Valmiki.
- **Kavya**: A term for poem or poetic composition.
- **Kaṇḍa**: A canto or book of an epic; the *Ramayana* has seven.
- **Saptarishi**: The seven great sages of Hindu tradition.
- **Mantra**: A sacred utterance, sound, or phrase used for meditation or ritual.
- **Siddhi**: Spiritual power or enlightenment achieved through practice.
- **Ashram**: A spiritual hermitage or monastery.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The historicity of Valmiki is uncertain; he is a legendary figure, and the *Ramayana* is a product of centuries of oral and written tradition.
- Scholars debate the originality of the *Uttarakāṇḍa*, with some like Robert and Sally Goldman suggesting it may be a later addition not composed by Valmiki.
- The precise dating of the *Ramayana*'s composition remains unknown due to its long process of redaction and interpolation.

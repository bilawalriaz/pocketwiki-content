# Saint Lucia

## Overview
Saint Lucia is a volcanic island nation in the eastern Caribbean, part of the Windward Islands. Its strategic location made it a frequent battleground between European colonial powers, shaping its history and culture. Today, it is an independent Commonwealth realm with a mixed economy heavily reliant on tourism.

## Timeline
- **200–400 AD** — Arawak people are the first proven inhabitants.
- **c. 800 AD** — Kalinago (Island Caribs) seize control from the Arawaks.
- **1660** — French sign a treaty with the native Caribs.
- **1663** — English take control of the island.
- **1814** — British gain complete control after the Napoleonic Wars.
- **1924** — Representative government introduced.
- **1951** — Universal suffrage established.
- **1979** — Saint Lucia becomes an independent state.
- **2021** — Saint Lucia Labour Party wins election; Philip J. Pierre becomes Prime Minister.

## Body

### Early Inhabitants and Colonial Struggle
The Arawak people settled Saint Lucia around 200–400 AD, followed by the Kalinago around 800 AD. European contact began in the 16th century, with Spanish and French explorers. The French were the first to establish a colony, signing a treaty with the Caribs in 1660. Control then shifted repeatedly between the French and English (later British) over the next 150 years, with the island changing hands or being declared neutral territory numerous times. This frequent conflict earned it the nickname "Helen of the West." The British secured permanent control in 1814.

### Path to Independence
Representative government was introduced in 1924, with universal suffrage following in 1951. Saint Lucia was a member of the West Indies Federation from 1958 to 1962. It gained internal self-government in 1967 and achieved full independence on 22 February 1979 under Prime Minister Sir John Compton, while remaining a Commonwealth realm with the British monarch as head of state.

### Post-Independence Politics and Economy
Politics since independence have seen power alternate between the United Workers Party (UWP) and the Saint Lucia Labour Party (SLP). The economy has shifted from a historical reliance on banana exports to a dominant tourism sector. Key challenges have included natural disasters like Hurricane Allen (1980) and Hurricane Tomas (2010), and economic slowdowns from global events like the 2001 September 11 attacks and the COVID-19 pandemic.

### Geography and Climate
Saint Lucia is a volcanic island of 617 km², dominated by mountains. Its highest point is Mount Gimie (950 m). Famous landmarks include the Pitons, two volcanic plugs, and the Sulphur Springs, the world's only drive-in volcano. The climate is tropical, with a dry season from December to May and a wet season from June to November.

### Government and Society
Saint Lucia is a parliamentary democracy and constitutional monarchy. Its legal system is a mix of civil law and English common law. The population is predominantly of African and mixed descent. English is the official language, but Saint Lucian French Creole (Kwéyòl) is widely spoken. Christianity is the main religion, with Roman Catholicism being the largest denomination.

## Terms
- **Commonwealth realm:** A sovereign state that has the British monarch as its head of state.
- **Kalinago:** The indigenous people of the island, also known as Island Caribs.
- **Kwéyòl:** Saint Lucian French Creole, a language derived from French and West African languages.
- **Pitons:** Two mountainous volcanic plugs that form Saint Lucia's most famous landmark.
- **Sulphur Springs:** The world's only drive-in volcano, located in the Qualibou depression.
- **Small Island Developing State (SIDS):** A designation for island nations with specific development challenges.
- **Eastern Caribbean Dollar (EC$):** The regional currency used by Saint Lucia and other Eastern Caribbean states.

## Debates and open questions
- The exact date and nature of initial European contact are debated; Christopher Columbus may have sighted the island in 1502, but it was noted on Spanish maps earlier.
- The origin of the island's name is debated: legend attributes it to French sailors shipwrecked on St. Lucy's feast day, but a 1520 Vatican globe shows it as "Sancta Lucia," suggesting earlier Spanish naming.

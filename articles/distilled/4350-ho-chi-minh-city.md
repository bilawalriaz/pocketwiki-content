# Ho Chi Minh City

## Overview
Ho Chi Minh City (HCMC), also known as Saigon, is Vietnam's most populous city and its primary financial and economic hub. Its history as a major settlement, from a Cham and Khmer village to a Vietnamese, French colonial, and finally unified Vietnamese metropolis, reflects the broader political and economic transformations of Southeast Asia. The city's geography, defined by rivers and canals, has made it a vital transport and trade center, contributing roughly a quarter of Vietnam's GDP.

- **2025** — Provinces of Bình Dương and Bà Rịa–Vũng Tàu are merged into the city; "Saigon" becomes the official name of the city's administrative centre.

## Body

### Historical Development
The area was initially part of Cambodian polities. Vietnamese control was formalized in 1698 with the establishment of the Gia Định settlement. It served as a capital for the Nguyễn lords before the Nguyễn dynasty's formal establishment in 1802. French colonization began with the 1859 capture of the citadel; the city was ceded in 1862 and developed as the "Pearl of the Orient." After French recognition of Vietnamese independence, Saigon became the capital of the State of Vietnam (1949–1955) and then the Republic of Vietnam (South Vietnam) until its capture in 1975. Following reunification in 1976, it was renamed Ho Chi Minh City.

### Geography and Climate
Located in Southeast Vietnam on the Mekong Delta, the city is defined by the Saigon River and a network of canals. It has a tropical savanna climate with distinct rainy (May–November) and dry (December–April) seasons. The average annual temperature is 28°C. Flooding is a seasonal issue, with a once-in-100-year flood projected to inundate 23% of the city.

### Demographics and Administration
As of 2025, the city's population exceeds 14 million. The majority are ethnic Vietnamese (Kinh), with the largest minority being the Chinese (Hoa) community, concentrated in the Chợ Lớn district. The city is a municipality at the provincial level. In 2025, its administrative area was expanded to include the former provinces of Bình Dương and Bà Rịa–Vũng Tàu, adding industrial and coastal zones.

### Economy and Transport
HCMC is Vietnam's major financial center, with the largest gross regional domestic product. Its economy is driven by industry, services, and foreign direct investment. The city is the country's busiest international transport hub. Tân Sơn Nhất International Airport handles nearly half of Vietnam's international arrivals. The Port of Saigon is among Southeast Asia's busiest container ports. A new metro system (Line 1 opened in 2024) and expressways are being developed to manage heavy traffic, which is dominated by motorcycles.

### Culture and Tourism
The city's architecture blends French colonial, Chinese, and modern styles. Key landmarks include the Independence Palace, Notre-Dame Cathedral, and Bến Thành Market. It is a major tourist destination, known for its street food, nightlife in areas like Bùi Viện Street, and museums such as the War Remnants Museum. In the first half of 2025, it welcomed over 22 million visitors.

## Terms
- **Nam tiến**: The historical southward expansion of Vietnamese territory.
- **Cochinchina**: The southern region of Vietnam under French colonial rule.
- **Đô thành**: Vietnamese term for a metropolitan city.
- **Hoa**: The ethnic Chinese minority in Vietnam.
- **Chợ Lớn**: The historic Chinatown district of Ho Chi Minh City.
- **Tet Offensive**: A major 1968 military campaign during the Vietnam War.
- **Reunification Express**: The train line connecting Ho Chi Minh City and Hanoi.
- **TEU**: Twenty-foot Equivalent Unit, a standard measure for container port capacity.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The exact etymology of "Sài Gòn" is debated; it may derive from Khmer "Prey Nokôr" or a Chinese term for "embankment."
- The city's actual population is debated, with official census figures sometimes considered underestimates due to unregistered migrants.
- The long-term impact of the 2025 provincial mergers on the city's governance, economy, and infrastructure is a subject of ongoing planning and observation.

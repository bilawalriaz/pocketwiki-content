# Ahmedabad

## Overview
Ahmedabad is the most populous city in the Indian state of Gujarat and a major economic, industrial, and cultural hub. Historically a center for the textile industry, it has evolved into a diverse metropolis known for its unique architecture, vibrant festivals, and significant role in India's independence movement. The city's historic core was declared a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2017.

## Timeline
- **11th century** — Area known as Ashaval; Chaulukya ruler Karna I establishes Karnavati.
- **1411** — Sultan Ahmed Shah I founds Ahmedabad as the new capital of the Gujarat Sultanate.
- **1487** — Sultan Mahmud Begada fortifies the city with a large outer wall.
- **1573** — Mughal emperor Akbar conquers Gujarat; Ahmedabad becomes a thriving trade center.
- **1818** — East India Company takes control of Ahmedabad during the Third Anglo-Maratha War.
- **1864** — Railway link established between Ahmedabad and Mumbai.
- **1915-17** — Mahatma Gandhi establishes ashrams in the city, making it a center for nationalist activities.
- **1960** — Ahmedabad becomes the capital of the newly formed state of Gujarat.
- **2002** — Communal violence includes the Gulbarg Society massacre.
- **2017** — The historic walled city is declared a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

## Body

### History and Foundation
The area was inhabited from the 11th century, known as Ashaval and later Karnavati. In 1411, Sultan Ahmed Shah I of the Gujarat Sultanate selected a forested area along the Sabarmati River for his new capital, naming it Ahmedabad. The city was fortified in 1487. It flourished under the Mughals as a textile trade center before passing to the Marathas and then the British East India Company in 1818. Under British rule, it became a major city in the Bombay Presidency, with its textile industry earning it the nickname "Manchester of India."

### Modern History and Post-Independence
Ahmedabad was a key site in the Indian independence movement, hosting Gandhi's ashrams and major protests. After independence in 1947, it experienced communal violence and an influx of Hindu migrants. It became Gujarat's capital in 1960, a period of growth in education and industry. The capital shifted to Gandhinagar in the late 1970s. The city faced economic decline, social unrest, the devastating 2001 Gujarat earthquake, and the 2002 Gujarat violence. It has since seen redevelopment and was selected as a "smart city."

### Demographics and Society
As of the 2011 census, Ahmedabad's population was 5,577,940, making it India's fifth-most populous city. Hindus (81.56%) and Muslims (13.51%) are the largest religious communities. Gujarati is the primary language. The city has a significant population living in informal settlements or slums, though efforts like the Slum Networking Project (SNP) have improved basic services and living conditions for many.

### Geography and Climate
Ahmedabad is located on the banks of the Sabarmati River in a semi-arid region. The river historically dried up in summer but is now kept flowing via the Narmada River. The city is divided into the historic eastern bank and the modern western bank. It has a hot semi-arid climate with three seasons: hot summers, a monsoon, and mild winters. The Ahmedabad Heat Action Plan, developed after a deadly 2010 heatwave, is a pioneering public health initiative.

### Economy and Infrastructure
The economy was historically based on textiles but has diversified into pharmaceuticals, chemicals, automobiles, and IT. Major companies like Zydus Lifesciences and Adani Group are headquartered here. Infrastructure includes the Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel International Airport, a major railway junction, the Ahmedabad Metro, and the Janmarg BRTS. The city is also a major center for education, hosting institutions like IIM Ahmedabad and CEPT University.

### Culture and Sports
Ahmedabad is known for its Indo-Saracenic architecture, the pol housing system, and festivals like Uttarayan (kite-flying) and Navratri (Garba dance). Its cuisine is predominantly vegetarian. Cricket is immensely popular, with the Narendra Modi Stadium being the world's largest cricket stadium. The city will host the 2030 Commonwealth Games and has been proposed as a host for the 2036 Summer Olympics.

## Terms
- **Pol**: A traditional housing cluster in the old city, comprising families linked by caste, profession, or religion.
- **Indo-Saracenic**: An architectural style fusing Hindu craftsmanship with Persian elements, prominent in Ahmedabad's historic mosques.
- **Chawl**: A tenement building for industrial workers, common in older parts of the city.
- **Swadeshi movement**: A nationalist campaign promoting Indian-made goods, which boosted Ahmedabad's textile industry.
- **Slum Networking Project (SNP)**: A participatory initiative by the Ahmedabad Municipal Corporation to provide infrastructure and tenure security to slum residents.
- **Smart Cities Mission**: A Government of India flagship program to develop urban areas with modern infrastructure and technology.
- **BRTS (Janmarg)**: A bus rapid transit system providing public transportation in the city.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The exact historical location of the pre-Ahmedabad city of Karnavati is not definitively known.
- Census data on slum populations is contested, with local entities suggesting official figures underestimate informal settlements.
- There is an ongoing challenge to balance inclusive, pro-poor development with initiatives aimed at creating a global, investment-focused city.
- The capacity of communities to maintain upgraded infrastructure from projects like the SNP remains a concern.

# Baghdad

## Overview
Baghdad is the capital and largest city of Iraq, located on the Tigris River. Founded in 762 AD, it became the intellectual and cultural heart of the Islamic Golden Age before suffering repeated destruction and decline. Today, it remains Iraq's primary political, economic, and cultural center, generating 40% of the nation's GDP.

## Timeline
- **762 AD** — Founded by Abbasid Caliph al-Mansur as the "Round City."
- **8th–9th C.** — Flourishes as a global center of learning, home to the House of Wisdom.
- **1258** — Captured and sacked by the Mongol Empire, ending the Abbasid Caliphate.
- **1534** — Captured by the Ottoman Empire, becoming the capital of Ottoman Iraq.
- **1917** — Captured by British forces during World War I.
- **1921** — Becomes capital of the British Mandate of Mesopotamia.
- **1932** — Capital of the independent Kingdom of Iraq.
- **1958** — Monarchy overthrown in a coup; Baghdad becomes capital of the Iraqi Republic.
- **2003** — U.S.-led coalition invades Iraq; Saddam Hussein's statue toppled in Baghdad.
- **2013–2017** — City experiences high levels of violence during the war against ISIS.
- **2017** — Territorial defeat of ISIS in Iraq; attacks decline.

## Body

### Foundation and Golden Age
Baghdad was founded on a site with ancient settlement, including Neo-Babylonian remains. Caliph al-Mansur commissioned its construction in 762 AD as a new capital for the Abbasid Caliphate. Designed as a circular city with advanced infrastructure, it quickly grew into a major commercial and intellectual hub. During the Islamic Golden Age (8th–9th centuries), Baghdad was likely the world's largest city, with a population exceeding one million. It became renowned as the "Center of Learning," housing the House of Wisdom and fostering scholarship in science, medicine, and philosophy among a multi-ethnic, multi-religious population.

### Decline and Invasions
Baghdad's prominence waned due to internal strife within the Caliphate, including the relocation of the capital to Samarra. The city was ruled by successive dynasties, including the Buwayhids and Seljuk Turks. Its catastrophic decline began with the Mongol sack of 1258, which destroyed much of the city, massacred its inhabitants, and ended the Abbasid Caliphate. This event is often cited as a blow from which Islamic civilization never fully recovered. Subsequent centuries saw Baghdad ruled by various powers, including Timur (Tamerlane), the Safavids, and finally the Ottomans, who made it the administrative capital of Ottoman Iraq in 1535.

### Modern Era
Baghdad became the capital of British Mandate Iraq in 1921 and of the independent kingdom in 1932. Under the Ba'ath Party, the city experienced growth and prosperity fueled by oil wealth in the 1970s. This was followed by decades of conflict: the Iran-Iraq War (1980s), the Gulf War (1991), and severe economic sanctions. The 2003 U.S. invasion led to the fall of Saddam Hussein's regime but triggered a violent insurgency and civil war. The city suffered severe infrastructural damage and a loss of cultural heritage. Following the territorial defeat of ISIS in 2017, reconstruction efforts have been underway, though challenges remain.

### Demographics and Economy
Baghdad's population is predominantly Arab, with significant Kurdish, Turkmen, Assyrian, and other minority communities. The city is religiously diverse, historically with a Sunni majority, but now with a Shi'a majority (about 52%). It was once home to a large Jewish community, which has dwindled to a few hundred. Economically, Baghdad is Iraq's financial hub, hosting the Central Bank, major corporations, and the Iraq Stock Exchange. Its economy is dominated by the public sector and oil-related industries.

## Terms
- **Abbasid Caliphate:** The third caliphate to succeed the Islamic Prophet Muhammad, ruling from 750 to 1258 AD.
- **House of Wisdom (Bayt al-Hikmah):** A major intellectual center and library in Baghdad during the Islamic Golden Age, where scholars translated and studied Greek, Persian, and Indian texts.
- **Islamic Golden Age:** A period of cultural, economic, and scientific flourishing in the history of Islam, traditionally dated from the 8th to the 14th century.
- **Round City:** The original circular core of Baghdad, designed by al-Mansur, featuring concentric walls and a central mosque-palace complex.
- **Mongol sack of Baghdad (1258):** The catastrophic siege and destruction of the city by the Mongol Empire, marking the end of the Abbasid Caliphate.
- **Ottoman Iraq:** The period when the region of Iraq was part of the Ottoman Empire, with Baghdad as its administrative capital (1534–1917).
- **Ba'ath Party:** A secular Arab nationalist party that ruled Iraq from 1968 to 2003 under Saddam Hussein.
- **ISIS (Islamic State):** A militant Islamist group that controlled large parts of Iraq and Syria, including areas near Baghdad, before its territorial defeat in 2017.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The etymology of the name "Baghdad" is disputed, with theories pointing to Middle Persian, Old Babylonian, or even Akkadian origins.
- Scholars debate the exact population of Baghdad at its peak during the Golden Age, with estimates ranging from over one million to two million.
- The long-term impact of the 1258 Mongol sack on the decline of Islamic civilization is a subject of historical debate.

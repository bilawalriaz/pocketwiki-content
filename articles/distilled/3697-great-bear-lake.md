# Great Bear Lake

## Overview
Great Bear Lake is the largest lake entirely within Canada and the eighth-largest in the world. Located in the Northwest Territories on the Arctic Circle, it is a remote, ecologically significant body of water central to the identity and culture of the local Délı̨nę people. Its immense size, clarity, and geological history make it a key feature of the Canadian boreal forest.

## Timeline
- **Pleistocene** — Lake forms from glacial Lake McConnell in pre-glacial valleys reshaped by ice erosion.
- **1828** — Explorer John Franklin notes the lake's exceptional water clarity.
- **1930** — Gilbert LaBine discovers uranium deposits in the region.
- **1965–1981** — Echo Bay Mines Limited operates at the former Port Radium site, recovering silver and copper.
- **1995** — A record-breaking 32.65 kg (72.0 lb) lake trout is caught by angling.
- **5 March 2016** — A tank truck carrying heating fuel falls through the ice road near Délı̨nę.

## Body

### Geography and Formation
Great Bear Lake has a surface area of 31,153 km² and a maximum depth of 446 m. It is covered with ice from late November to July. The lake lies between the Kazan Uplands of the Canadian Shield and the Interior Plains. It originated as part of glacial Lake McConnell and has been altered by post-glacial rebound since the ice melted. Its eastern margin is formed by Precambrian sedimentary, metamorphic, and igneous rocks.

### Human Use and Economy
The community of Délı̨nę is located on the Keith Arm. An ice road connects it to the winter road network, though this infrastructure is vulnerable, as evidenced by a 2016 fuel spill incident. The lake is a destination for fishing and hunting lodges. Historically, the region was significant for mining; uranium was discovered in 1930 at Port Radium, and later silver and copper were extracted until 1981.

### Cultural Significance
The lake is paramount to the identity, laws, and culture of the Délı̨nę people, a Dene group for whom conservation is critical. A prophecy by the Dene Elder ɂehtsǝ́o Erǝ́ya states that as the world dries up, life will end on the banks of Great Bear Lake, which is seen as a physical beating heart to humanity. These beliefs drive local self-governance and environmental sustainability efforts.

## Terms
- **Boreal forest:** A biome characterized by coniferous forests, found in high northern latitudes.
- **Canadian Shield:** A large area of exposed Precambrian rock forming the ancient geological core of North America.
- **Délı̨nę:** A community of the Sahtu Dene people located on Great Bear Lake.
- **Post-glacial rebound:** The rise of land masses that were depressed by the weight of ice sheets during the last glacial period.
- **Precambrian:** The earliest geological eon, forming the oldest rocks on Earth.
- **Sahtu Dene:** A Dene people whose name is derived from the lake (Sahtú).
- **Pitchblende:** A uranium-rich mineral ore.

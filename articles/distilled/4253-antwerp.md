# Antwerp

## Overview
Antwerp is a major port city in northern Belgium, serving as the country's most populous municipality and a global hub for the diamond trade. Its strategic location on the Scheldt river has made it a center of international commerce for centuries, experiencing a dramatic golden age in the 16th century before facing decline and later resurgence. The city's history is marked by its role in the Dutch Revolt, its fortification, and its modern reinvention as a fashion and cultural capital.

## Timeline
- **c. 567 CE** — Earliest recorded Germanic name for the settlement, *Andhunerbo*, appears.
- **980** — Antwerp becomes a margraviate of the Holy Roman Empire.
- **1531** — The world's first purpose-built commodity exchange opens.
- **1576** — The Spanish Fury: Spanish troops sack the city, killing 8,000 citizens.
- **1585** — The city is captured by Spanish forces after a siege; Protestant citizens are expelled.
- **1648** — Treaty of Münster closes the Scheldt to navigation, crippling Antwerp's trade.
- **1830** — Antwerp is captured by Belgian insurgents during the Belgian Revolution.
- **1920** — The city hosts the Summer Olympics.
- **1944** — Antwerp is liberated by British forces; later targeted by V-2 missiles.
- **1956–1965** — A Ten-Year Plan expands and modernizes the Port of Antwerp.

## Body

### Historical Rise and Golden Age
Antwerp's origins trace to a Gallo-Roman settlement, but it rose to prominence in the 16th century after the silting of Bruges' harbor. It became the commercial capital of Europe, handling 40% of world trade by the early 1500s. The city's economy was fueled by the spice trade, banking, and a massive influx of silver from the Americas. Its Stock Exchange, opened in 1531, served merchants of all nations. This era, however, ended with the religious and political turmoil of the Reformation and the Dutch Revolt.

### Decline and Resurgence
The Spanish capture of Antwerp in 1585 and the subsequent closure of the Scheldt river by the Treaty of Münster (1648) destroyed its trading economy, leading to a long decline. The city's population fell below 40,000 by 1800. Napoleon recognized its strategic value and began harbor improvements. After Belgian independence in 1830, Antwerp was fortified and its port was systematically expanded in the 20th century, culminating in the post-WWII Ten-Year Plan that restored its status as a major global port.

### Economy and Demographics
The Port of Antwerp is one of the world's largest, handling high volumes of general cargo and hosting a massive petrochemical industry. The city is also the world's diamond trading hub, with 85% of the world's rough diamonds passing through its district annually. Antwerp is highly diverse, with about 180 nationalities and over 50% of its population having a parent who was not a Belgian citizen at birth. It is home to a significant Haredi Jewish community and a notable Jain community involved in the diamond trade.

### Culture and Urban Landscape
Antwerp's architecture blends historic Gothic and Baroque buildings with contemporary designs like the Port House by Zaha Hadid. The city is a global fashion center, home to the influential "Antwerp Six" designers and the Royal Academy of Fine Arts. Its cultural institutions include the Royal Museum of Fine Arts, the Rubenshuis, and the Plantin-Moretus Museum (a UNESCO site). The city's diverse neighborhoods range from the historic inner city to the Art Nouveau Zurenborg district.

## Terms
- **Margraviate:** A border province of the Holy Roman Empire, ruled by a margrave.
- **Scheldt:** A major river flowing through Antwerp, providing access to the North Sea.
- **Spanish Fury (1576):** A violent sack of Antwerp by mutinous Spanish troops during the Dutch Revolt.
- **Dutch Revolt:** The 16th-century uprising of the northern Low Countries against Spanish rule.
- **Haredi:** A stream of Orthodox Judaism characterized by strict adherence to traditional religious law.
- **Diamond Bourse:** An official exchange for trading diamonds.
- **Brialmont Fortresses:** A 19th-century ring of fortifications built around Antwerp for national defense.
- **Premetro:** The underground section of Antwerp's tram network.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The etymology of "Antwerp" is debated, with theories ranging from a Germanic meaning "land thrown up at the riverbank" to a Celtic meaning "those who live on both banks." The popular folklore of the giant Druon Antigoon is considered unlikely.
- The long-term impact of the 2009 public rejection of the Lange Wapper viaduct project and the subsequent "Toekomstverbond" agreement to cover the Ring road remains a subject of urban planning discussion.
- The future of Antwerp's diamond industry is uncertain, facing competition from cheaper labor markets and the geopolitical impact of sanctions on Russian diamonds.

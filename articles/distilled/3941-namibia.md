# Namibia

## Overview
Namibia is a sparsely populated country in Southern Africa, known for its arid landscapes and history of colonial rule. It gained independence in 1990 after a long struggle, transitioning from German and South African control to a parliamentary democracy. The nation faces challenges like poverty and inequality despite economic growth from mining and tourism, and its history includes the early 20th-century genocide of the Herero and Nama peoples.

## Timeline
- **1884** — German Empire establishes the colony of German South West Africa.
- **1904–1908** — Herero and Nama genocide occurs under German rule.
- **1915** — South African forces defeat Germany, taking control of the territory.
- **1920** — League of Nations mandates South Africa to administer the territory.
- **1966** — UN assumes direct responsibility; SWAPO launches an armed insurgency.
- **1988** — Tripartite Accord leads to ceasefire and peace process.
- **1990** — Namibia gains independence from South Africa on 21 March.
- **1994** — South Africa cedes Walvis Bay to Namibia.
- **2021** — Germany and Namibia agree on a "reconciliation agreement" for colonial-era atrocities.
- **2024** — President Hage Geingob dies; succeeded by Nangolo Mbumba.
- **2025** — Netumbo Nandi-Ndaitwah is sworn in as Namibia's first female president.

## Body

### Pre-colonial and Colonial Era
Namibia has been inhabited since prehistoric times by Khoisan peoples, with Bantu groups arriving around the 14th century. European exploration began in the late 15th century, but formal colonization started in 1884 when Germany established German South West Africa. A 1897 rinderpest epidemic led to the creation of a veterinary fence, the "Red Line." From 1904 to 1908, German forces carried out a punitive campaign against the Herero and Nama, resulting in the first genocide of the 20th century, which killed about 80% of the Herero and half the Nama population. This history has shaped modern Namibian identity and relations with Germany.

### South African Rule and Independence Struggle
After World War I, South Africa administered the territory under a League of Nations mandate, later applying apartheid policies. In 1966, the UN declared South Africa's rule illegal and renamed the territory Namibia. The South West Africa People's Organisation (SWAPO) led an armed struggle, which became part of the Cold War-era South African Border War. A 1988 peace accord led to a UN-supervised transition. Namibia achieved independence in March 1990, with SWAPO winning the first elections. Walvis Bay was reintegrated in 1994.

### Post-Independence Politics and Society
Namibia is a parliamentary democracy where SWAPO has won every election since 1990. The constitution guarantees human rights, though issues like corruption and LGBT rights remain contested. The country is ethnically diverse, with English as the sole official language, though Oshiwambo is the most widely spoken home language. Christianity is the predominant religion. Education is free, and literacy rates are high for the region.

### Economy and Geography
Namibia's economy relies on mining (especially diamonds and uranium), tourism, and agriculture. It is classified as an upper-middle-income country but has high income inequality and unemployment. Geographically, it is the driest country in sub-Saharan Africa, featuring the Namib and Kalahari deserts. Water scarcity is a major challenge, with dependence on groundwater. The country has a well-developed infrastructure and is a regional transport hub.

## Terms
- **Bantu expansion**: The historical migration of Bantu-speaking peoples from central Africa into southern Africa, beginning around the 14th century in Namibia.
- **German South West Africa**: The name of the German colony in present-day Namibia from 1884 to 1915.
- **Herero and Nama genocide**: The systematic killing of the Herero and Nama peoples by German colonial forces between 1904 and 1908.
- **SWAPO**: The South West Africa People's Organisation, the liberation movement that led the independence struggle and has been the ruling party since 1990.
- **Apartheid**: The system of institutionalized racial segregation and discrimination enforced in South Africa and its administered territories, including Namibia, from the late 1940s.
- **Tripartite Accord**: A 1988 agreement between South Africa, Angola, and Cuba, mediated by the US and USSR, that paved the way for Namibian independence and Cuban withdrawal from Angola.
- **Gini coefficient**: A measure of income inequality, where 0 represents perfect equality and 100 represents perfect inequality; Namibia's was 59.1 in 2015.
- **Communal wildlife conservancies**: Community-managed areas for sustainable natural resource use, established after independence to promote conservation and local livelihoods.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The historical significance and legacy of the Herero and Nama genocide, including its potential influence on later genocides and ongoing reparations discussions with Germany.
- The effectiveness of Namibia's post-independence policies in addressing deep-rooted poverty and inequality despite economic growth.
- The impact of the push for English as the sole official language on education and social cohesion in a multilingual society.
- The future economic transformation promised by potential offshore oil discoveries in the Orange Basin.

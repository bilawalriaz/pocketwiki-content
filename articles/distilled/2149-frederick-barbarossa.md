# Frederick Barbarossa

## Overview
Frederick I, known as Barbarossa ("Red Beard"), was Holy Roman Emperor from 1155 to 1190. He is considered one of the Empire's greatest medieval emperors, remembered for his ambitious attempts to restore imperial authority in Germany and Italy, his conflicts with the papacy, and his death during the Third Crusade. His reign marked a significant struggle between secular and ecclesiastical power and saw the revival of Roman law as a tool of governance.

## Body
### Rise to Power and Imperial Policy
Frederick, from the Hohenstaufen dynasty, was elected king in 1152, succeeding his uncle Conrad III. His dual Welf and Hohenstaufen lineage made him an acceptable compromise candidate. He inherited a fragmented Germany of over 1,600 states where royal power had been weak for decades. Frederick sought to restore imperial authority, modeled on Charlemagne, by using pragmatic concessions to nobles and reviving the *Corpus Juris Civilis* (Justinian Code). This Roman legal framework provided a rational basis for his rule and countered papal claims of divine authority.

### Italian Campaigns and Papal Conflict
Frederick undertook six Italian expeditions to assert imperial dominance over wealthy northern cities and the papacy. His first campaign (1154-55) secured his imperial coronation but saw a revolt in Rome. Subsequent campaigns (1158-74) involved crushing rebellions in Milan, establishing imperial officials, and supporting antipopes against Pope Alexander III. This conflict culminated in his defeat at Legnano (1176) and the Peace of Venice (1177), where he recognized Alexander III's papacy. Later, the Peace of Constance (1183) granted Italian cities significant self-governance, marking a turning point in the balance of power.

### Domestic Rule and the Third Crusade
In Germany, Frederick maintained order through vigorous but conciliatory measures, such as granting the *Privilegium Minus* to Austria. He later crushed the power of his rival, Henry the Lion, in 1180-81. In 1189, he joined the Third Crusade, leading a large, meticulously organized army overland. After victories in Anatolia, he drowned in the Saleph River on 10 June 1190. His death caused most of his army to disband, significantly weakening the Crusader effort.

## Terms
- **Holy Roman Emperor**: The ruler of the Holy Roman Empire, a multi-ethnic complex of territories in Western and Central Europe during the Middle Ages.
- **Hohenstaufen**: The German royal dynasty to which Frederick belonged, often called Ghibellines.
- **Welf (Guelf)**: A rival German noble family, often allied with the papacy, from which Frederick's mother descended.
- **Corpus Juris Civilis**: The "Body of Civil Law," a compilation of Roman law under Emperor Justinian I, revived and used by Frederick to legitimize imperial authority.
- **Lombard League**: An alliance of northern Italian cities formed to resist imperial authority and taxation.
- **Investiture Controversy**: A conflict between secular rulers and the papacy over the appointment of church officials, which had weakened imperial power before Frederick's reign.
- **Diet**: A formal assembly of nobles and clergy in the Holy Roman Empire to discuss matters of state.
- **Peace of Venice (1177)**: The treaty reconciling Emperor Frederick I and Pope Alexander III, ending their schism.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The exact circumstances of Frederick's death are debated, with accounts ranging from drowning while swimming to being thrown from his horse.
- Historians debate the extent to which Frederick's policies were a pragmatic adaptation versus a romantic, reactionary attempt to revive a classical Roman empire.
- The true size of his Third Crusade army is uncertain, with medieval sources claiming 100,000 men, while modern scholars estimate 12,000–15,000.
- The long-term impact of his use of Roman law versus his political concessions in Italy remains a subject of scholarly analysis regarding his legacy.

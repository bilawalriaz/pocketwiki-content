# Nanyue

## Overview
Nanyue (204–111 BC) was an ancient kingdom in southern China and northern Vietnam founded by a former Qin general. It bridged the Han Chinese heartland and the southern Yue peoples, promoting ethnic integration. Its conquest by the Han dynasty shaped the early history and demographics of the Lingnan region.

## Timeline
- **c. 218 BC** — Qin Shi Huang dispatches armies to conquer the Hundred Yue tribes in Lingnan.
- **214 BC** — Qin completes conquest, establishing Nanhai, Guilin, and Xiang Commanderies.
- **204 BC** — Zhao Tuo, a Qin official, founds the Kingdom of Nanyue after the Qin collapse.
- **196 BC** — Han Emperor Gaozu sends an envoy; Zhao Tuo submits as a Han vassal.
- **183 BC** — Zhao Tuo declares himself emperor and attacks the Han vassal state of Changsha.
- **179 BC** — Han Emperor Wen sends a conciliatory envoy; Zhao Tuo reverts to the title "king."
- **137 BC** — Zhao Tuo dies; his grandson Zhao Mo becomes king.
- **135 BC** — Minyue attacks Nanyue; Zhao Mo seeks military aid from Han Emperor Wu.
- **113 BC** — Prime Minister Lü Jia opposes King Zhao Xing's plan to fully submit to Han.
- **112 BC** — Lü Jia stages a coup, killing King Zhao Xing and Han emissaries.
- **111 BC** — Han Emperor Wu sends a massive army; Nanyue is conquered and dissolved.

## Body

### Founding and Early Relations with Han
Founded in 204 BC by Zhao Tuo, a Qin general, Nanyue emerged after the Qin collapse. He fortified southern commanderies and established an independent kingdom with Panyu (modern Guangzhou) as capital. The nascent Han dynasty, seeking to secure its border, sent envoy Lu Jia in 196 BC. Zhao Tuo accepted a Han royal seal, acknowledging Han suzerainty while retaining substantial autonomy.

### Zenith under Zhao Tuo and Cultural Integration
Zhao Tuo ruled for over a century, navigating Han relations. He declared himself emperor during the aggressive reign of Empress Dowager Lü, repelling a Han expedition. After her death, he resumed nominal vassalage under Emperor Wen. His policy of "Harmonizing and Gathering the Hundred Yue tribes" encouraged Han immigration and cultural assimilation. The kingdom adopted Qin-Han bureaucracy, Chinese writing, and weights and measures, while allowing Yue chieftains regional autonomy, fostering a blended Han-Yue culture.

### Decline and Fall
Successors were less capable. King Zhao Mo (r. 137–122 BC) sought Han protection against Minyue, increasing Han influence. His grandson, Zhao Xing, became king in 113 BC. Under pressure from his Han-born mother, Zhao Xing sought full incorporation into the Han Empire. Prime Minister Lü Jia, a Yue nobleman, vehemently opposed this. In 112 BC, Lü Jia executed a coup, killing the king, queen dowager, and Han emissaries. Emperor Wu responded with a massive five-pronged invasion in 111 BC. Han forces captured Panyu; the last king, Zhao Jiande, and Lü Jia were killed. Nanyue was dissolved into nine Han commanderies.

### Legacy and Historiography
Nanyue's 93-year existence allowed Lingnan relatively peaceful development during the Qin-Han transition. Its rulers, though ethnically Han, promoted integration with native Yue peoples. In Vietnam, the kingdom is known as the Triệu dynasty. Traditional Vietnamese historiography, such as the 13th-century *Đại Việt sử ký*, considered it the legitimate start of Vietnamese history. From the 18th century, a counter-view emerged, and after 1945, Vietnam's official stance became that Nanyue was a foreign regime. The name "Vietnam" derives from "Nam Việt" (Nanyue).

## Terms
- **Hundred Yue (Baiyue):** Collective term for non-Chinese ethnic groups in southern China and northern Vietnam.
- **Commandery-County System:** Administrative system from the Qin, dividing the kingdom into commanderies (jùn) and counties (xiàn).
- **Suzerainty:** Relationship where a weaker state acknowledges nominal authority of a stronger one while retaining autonomy.
- **Lingnan:** Region "south of the mountain ranges," modern Guangdong, Guangxi, and northern Vietnam.
- **Triệu dynasty:** Vietnamese name for Nanyue's ruling house, from the Chinese surname Zhao (趙).
- **Harmonizing and Gathering the Hundred Yue tribes (Heji Baiyue):** Assimilation policy promoting integration of Han settlers with native Yue populations.
- **Outer Vassal State (Wai Shu Zhuhou Guo):** Vassal state with significant autonomy, as Nanyue initially was to Han.
- **Inner Vassal State (Nei Shu Zhuhou Guo):** Vassal state more fully integrated into imperial administration, which Zhao Xing sought to become.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Legitimacy in Vietnamese History:** Debate over whether Nanyue is legitimate Vietnamese history. Traditional views included it; modern Vietnamese orthodoxy classifies it as foreign occupation.
- **Extent of Qin Conquest:** Scholarship questions if Qin conquered territory in modern Vietnam, suggesting Chinese control there may have begun with Nanyue.
- **Ethnic Composition and Language:** The precise ethnic makeup and languages of the Yue peoples are debated, with links proposed to ancestors of modern Zhuang or Austroasiatic speakers.

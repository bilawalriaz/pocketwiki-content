# Port Harcourt

## Overview
Port Harcourt is a major port city and the capital of Rivers State, Nigeria. Founded by the British colonial administration in 1912 to export coal, its economy and growth were transformed by the discovery of crude oil in the region, making it the center of Nigeria's petroleum industry. It is now one of Nigeria's most populous cities, facing significant challenges from rapid urbanization and environmental pollution.

## Body

### Founding and Colonial Purpose
Port Harcourt was created in 1912 by the colonial administration under Governor Frederick Lugard. Its primary purpose was to serve as a coal-exporting port for the collieries discovered in Enugu in 1909, connected by the newly built Eastern Line railway. The port was named in August 1913 after Lewis Harcourt, then the British Secretary of State for the Colonies. The city's land was ceded from local communities, including the Diobu people.

### Economic Transformation and Growth
The city's economy shifted dramatically from coal to petroleum after crude oil was discovered in Oloibiri in 1956. The first export shipment occurred in 1958, and Port Harcourt became the hub of Nigeria's oil industry, attracting multinational firms like Shell and Chevron. This association with petroleum spurred modernization and rapid, often uncontrolled, urbanization. The city also grew as the commercial center of the former Eastern Region and the Niger Delta. It was captured by Nigerian forces during the Civil War in 1968.

### Geography and Climate
Port Harcourt is located along the Bonny River in the oil-rich Niger Delta. The city features a tropical monsoon climate with heavy rainy seasons and a short dry season from December to February. Average temperatures are relatively constant, between 25 and 28 °C. The city is highly congested, leading to a 2009 law to spread development to surrounding communities. The Greater Port Harcourt region spans eight local government areas.

### Environmental Concerns
Since late 2016, Port Harcourt has suffered from severe particle (soot) pollution, with PM2.5 levels recorded at 11 times WHO specifications. The pollution, attributed to illegal refineries, gas flaring, and industrial emissions, covers surfaces in black soot, impairs visibility, and has caused residents to relocate. The state government has taken action by shutting down companies found to be high-volume emitters.

### Culture and Education
Port Harcourt was named UNESCO World Book Capital for 2014, the first city in Black Africa to receive the honor, hosting the annual Port Harcourt Book Festival. The city is the birthplace or home of several prominent Nigerian musicians, including Burna Boy and Duncan Mighty. It hosts numerous tertiary institutions, including the University of Port Harcourt, which was ranked first in Nigeria by Times Higher Education in 2015.

### Infrastructure and Services
The city is served by Port Harcourt International Airport and a Nigerian Air Force base used for domestic flights. It has two main oil refineries at Eleme. Healthcare is led by the University of Port Harcourt Teaching Hospital. Water supply is managed by the Port Harcourt Water Corporation, established in 2014 to provide potable water and wastewater services.

## Terms
- **Niger Delta**: A vast river delta in Nigeria, extremely rich in petroleum resources.
- **Collieries**: Coal mines and the buildings associated with them.
- **Harmattan**: A dry, dusty wind that blows from the Sahara over West Africa.
- **PM2.5**: Fine particulate matter in the air with a diameter of 2.5 micrometers or less, a key indicator of air pollution.
- **World Book Capital**: A designation by UNESCO to recognize a city's commitment to promoting books and reading.
- **Greater Port Harcourt**: A planned new city development project aimed at decongesting the main Port Harcourt metropolis.
- **Ikwerre**: The primary ethnic group indigenous to the Port Harcourt area.
- **Gas flaring**: The burning of natural gas associated with oil extraction, a major source of pollution and greenhouse gases.

## Debates and Open Questions
The source highlights ongoing debates and concerns regarding the causes and solutions to Port Harcourt's severe air pollution, with suspected sources ranging from industrial operations to illegal refineries. The effectiveness of government interventions, such as shutting down specific companies, in resolving the crisis remains a critical open question. Furthermore, the success of the Greater Port Harcourt development plan in managing the city's congestion and growth is an ongoing project.

# Persian Gulf

## Overview
The Persian Gulf is a shallow, mediterranean sea in West Asia, connecting to the Indian Ocean via the Strait of Hormuz. It is a critical global energy hub, holding vast oil and gas reserves, and has been a crossroads of trade, conflict, and cultural exchange for millennia. Its ecology is unique but threatened by industrialization and pollution.

## Timeline
- **c. 550 BC** — Achaemenid Empire established; Greek sources begin using "Persian Gulf."
- **224–651 AD** — Sasanian Empire rules the region; Gulf called "Pūdīg."
- **Early 16th C.** — Portuguese capture Hormuz, beginning 250 years of influence.
- **1602** — Safavid Persia expels the Portuguese from Bahrain.
- **1622** — Safavid Persia, with British help, takes Hormuz from the Portuguese.
- **1820** — General Maritime Treaty signed with British, ending major piracy.
- **1971** — British withdrawal from "East of Suez," leading to modern Gulf states' independence.
- **1980–1988** — Iran–Iraq War, with attacks on Gulf shipping.
- **1991** — Gulf War follows Iraq's invasion of Kuwait.
- **2002** — Gulf nations produce ~25% of world oil and hold ~2/3 of crude reserves.

## Body

### Geography and Oceanography
The Persian Gulf is an extension of the Arabian Sea, covering about 251,000 km². It is very shallow, with an average depth of 50 meters. Its waters are hypersaline due to high evaporation and limited freshwater input, creating a deficit filled by inflow from the Strait of Hormuz. Eight countries border it: Iran, Iraq, Kuwait, Saudi Arabia, Qatar, the UAE, Oman, and Bahrain.

### History and Naming
The region has been inhabited since the Paleolithic. The modern marine Gulf formed as sea levels rose between 12,000 and 6,000 years ago. Historically, it was a trade route for civilizations like Sumer and Dilmun. The name "Persian Gulf" derives from the Achaemenid Empire (550–330 BC). A modern naming dispute exists, with Arab states often using "Arabian Gulf," though "Persian Gulf" is the historical and international standard.

### Colonial Era and Modern Conflicts
Portuguese dominance from the 16th century was challenged by Safavid Persia and European rivals like the Dutch and British. The British Empire established political control over several Gulf states from the 18th to 20th centuries. The region's strategic importance grew with oil, leading to major conflicts like the Iran-Iraq War and the 1991 Gulf War. The U.S. and U.K. maintain significant military presences.

### Economy and Resources
The Persian Gulf is the world's largest source of petroleum. In 2002, its nations held nearly two-thirds of global crude oil reserves and about 35% of natural gas reserves. The region is also a major hub for fertilizer exports, accounting for 30–35% of global urea exports in the 2020s. The Strait of Hormuz is a critical chokepoint for global energy trade.

### Ecology and Wildlife
The Gulf's ecosystem is unique but endangered. It hosts over 700 fish species, extensive rocky reefs, and important habitats for dugongs and dolphins. However, industrialization, oil spills, and coastal development (including artificial islands) have damaged coral reefs, mangroves, and wildlife. Pollution from ships and land is a primary threat.

## Terms
- **Mediterranean sea**: A sea partially enclosed by land, like the Persian Gulf.
- **Strait of Hormuz**: The narrow passage connecting the Persian Gulf to the Gulf of Oman and the Indian Ocean.
- **Shatt al-Arab**: The river delta forming the northwestern shore, formed by the Tigris and Euphrates.
- **Exclusive economic zone (EEZ)**: A maritime area where a state has special rights over resource exploration and use.
- **Pan-Arabism**: A political ideology advocating unity among Arab peoples, which influenced the naming dispute.
- **Liquefied natural gas (LNG)**: Natural gas cooled to a liquid state for easier transport.
- **Hypersaline**: Having a very high concentration of salt, as in the Persian Gulf's waters.
- **Dugong**: A large marine mammal (sea cow) found in Gulf waters, threatened by habitat loss.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Naming Dispute**: The primary debate is over the name "Persian Gulf" versus "Arabian Gulf," reflecting historical claims and modern political rivalries.
- **Ecological Future**: The long-term impact of climate change, coastal development, and pollution on the Gulf's unique but fragile ecosystems remains a critical concern.
- **Geopolitical Stability**: The region's role as a global energy supplier makes its stability a constant subject of international strategic analysis.

# Goths

## Overview
The Goths were an East Germanic people whose migrations and conflicts were pivotal in the decline of the Western Roman Empire and the formation of medieval Europe. First recorded in the 3rd century AD near the Black Sea, they split into the Visigoths and Ostrogoths, establishing kingdoms in Iberia and Italy before their eventual assimilation.

## Timeline
- **c. 1st century AD** — Goths (as Gutones) first attested near the Vistula River in the Wielbark culture region.
- **Mid-2nd century** — Gothic migration from the Baltic region towards the Black Sea begins.
- **238 AD** — First recorded Gothic raid on the Roman Empire, sacking Histria.
- **250–251 AD** — Gothic king Cniva defeats and kills Roman Emperor Decius at the Battle of Abrittus.
- **269 AD** — Emperor Claudius II defeats a major Gothic invasion force at the Battle of Naissus.
- **c. 375 AD** — Huns invade Gothic lands, forcing many Goths to seek refuge in the Roman Empire.
- **378 AD** — Goths under Fritigern defeat and kill Emperor Valens at the Battle of Adrianople.
- **382 AD** — Treaty makes the Thervingi (Visigoths) *foederati* (allied troops) of the Eastern Roman Empire.
- **410 AD** — Visigoths under Alaric I sack the city of Rome.
- **493 AD** — Ostrogoths under Theodoric the Great conquer Italy, establishing the Ostrogothic Kingdom.
- **507 AD** — Visigoths lose most of Gaul to the Franks at the Battle of Vouillé, moving their capital to Toledo.
- **553 AD** — Ostrogothic Kingdom in Italy destroyed by the Byzantine Empire.
- **711 AD** — Visigothic Kingdom in Hispania falls to the Umayyad Caliphate.

## Body

### Origins and Early History
The Goths' origins are debated. The 6th-century historian Jordanes claimed they migrated from Scandinavia (Scandza) to the Vistula delta, a tradition partially supported by archaeological evidence of the Wielbark culture and linguistic similarities. By the 1st century AD, they were known as Gutones. In the mid-2nd century, they began migrating southeast, forming the Chernyakhov culture by the mid-3rd century near the Black Sea. Here, they adopted nomadic customs from Sarmatians and became a significant military power.

### Raids and Conflict with Rome (3rd–4th centuries)
From the 230s, Goths launched major raids into the Roman Empire, both by land and sea, culminating in the devastating defeat of Emperor Decius in 251. After a period of coexistence and heavy recruitment into the Roman army, the Hunnic invasion of the 370s triggered a crisis. Gothic refugees, led by Fritigern, were admitted into the Empire but rebelled after mistreatment, leading to the Gothic War. The decisive Battle of Adrianople (378) shattered Roman military power in the East and forced the Empire to settle Goths as *foederati* within its borders.

### Division into Visigoths and Ostrogoths
Following the Hunnic onslaught, two main Gothic groups emerged. The **Visigoths** ("western Goths"), led by Alaric I, sacked Rome in 410 and later settled in Gaul and Hispania, establishing a kingdom with its capital at Toledo. They converted to Catholicism in 589, assimilating with the Hispano-Roman population before their kingdom fell to the Umayyad Caliphate in 711. The **Ostrogoths** ("eastern Goths") initially lived under Hunnic rule. After the Huns' defeat, they established a powerful kingdom in Italy under Theodoric the Great (493–526), which was later destroyed by the Byzantine Empire in the Gothic War (535–554). A remnant, the Crimean Goths, survived in Crimea until the late Middle Ages.

### Culture and Legacy
The Goths spoke the Gothic language, the earliest attested Germanic language, preserved in Bishop Ulfilas's 4th-century Bible translation. They were initially pagans but converted to Arian Christianity in the 4th century; the Visigoths later became Catholic. Their art featured polychrome metalwork, as seen in the Visigothic Treasure of Guarrazar. Though their kingdoms fell, the Goths left a lasting legacy. Their name was later applied to medieval architectural and literary styles (Gothic architecture, Gothic literature) and the modern Goth subculture, though these have no direct connection to the ancient people. In Spain and Sweden, claims of Gothic ancestry became important for national identity.

## Terms
- **Foederati**: Barbarian groups (like the Goths) who were granted land within the Roman Empire in exchange for military service under their own leaders.
- **Arianism**: A Christian doctrine (deemed heretical by the mainstream Church) that held Christ was created by and subordinate to God the Father. Most Goths converted to this form of Christianity.
- **Chernyakhov culture**: An archaeological culture (3rd–4th centuries AD) in the Black Sea region, associated with Gothic and other Germanic peoples.
- **Wielbark culture**: An archaeological culture (1st century AD onward) in the Vistula region, linked to early Goths.
- **Visigoths**: The "western Goths," who established a kingdom in Hispania after the 5th century.
- **Ostrogoths**: The "eastern Goths," who established a kingdom in Italy in the 5th century.
- **Crimean Goths**: A remnant Gothic population in Crimea that maintained a distinct identity into the Middle Ages.
- **Spatha**: A long, straight, double-edged sword used by Gothic warriors, adopted from Roman military equipment.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Scandinavian Origins**: The historicity of Jordanes' account of a migration from Scandinavia is debated. While archaeological and genetic evidence supports a link to the Baltic region, the scale and nature of the migration (elite clans vs. mass movement) are uncertain.
- **Ethnic Composition**: The Goths were likely a diverse confederation. Genetic studies show that Gothic-associated populations (like the Wielbark culture) had Scandinavian ancestry but also mixed with local and steppe peoples, supporting the view of ethnically diverse "Gothic" groups.
- **Extent of Ermanaric's Kingdom**: The 4th-century Gothic king Ermanaric's realm is described by Jordanes as vast, but historians debate whether this was a large, unified state or a more limited sphere of influence.

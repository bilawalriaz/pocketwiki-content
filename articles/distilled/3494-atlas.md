# Atlas

## Overview
An atlas is a collection of maps, traditionally bound as a book, that presents geographical, political, and often statistical information about Earth, its regions, or other celestial bodies. The term originated in the 16th century and has evolved from customizable, hand-colored volumes to standardized, mass-produced publications and digital formats, serving as essential tools for education, navigation, and reference.

## Timeline
- **Early 16th century** — Italian cartographer Pietro Coppo prepares the first work with systematically arranged maps of uniform size, but it is not published.
- **1570** — Abraham Ortelius publishes *Theatrum Orbis Terrarum*, conventionally considered the first modern atlas.
- **1595** — Gerardus Mercator publishes *Atlas Sive Cosmographicae Meditationes...*, coining the term "atlas" in a geographical context.
- **1635–1667** — Joan Blaeu publishes his *Atlas Novus* and later the monumental *Atlas Maior* in the Netherlands.
- **1675** — John Ogilby's *Britannia* is the first atlas printed at a specific scale (one inch to one mile).
- **19th century** — States begin producing national atlases.
- **1881** — Publication of *Andrees Allgemeiner Handatlas* in Germany and the *Rand McNally Atlas* in the United States.
- **1938** — The *Geographers' A–Z Street Atlas* is first published in the United Kingdom.
- **1963** — The *National Geographic Atlas of the World* is first published in the United States.
- **21st century** — Digital and interactive atlases, such as the *North American Environmental Atlas*, become prominent.

## Body

### Etymology and Definition
The word "atlas" in a geographical sense was coined by Gerardus Mercator in 1595. He used it as a mark of respect for the Titan Atlas, whom he considered the first great geographer. Mercator's original title defined an atlas as a description of the creation of the entire universe, not merely a collection of maps. By the mid-17th century, the term had come to mean specifically a bound collection of maps.

### Historical Development
The first modern atlas is conventionally Abraham Ortelius's *Theatrum Orbis Terrarum* (1570), though Pietro Coppo had created a similar systematic work earlier without publishing it. Early printed atlases (16th–19th centuries) were often not pre-bound; clients could select maps to be colored and bound to their preference, meaning copies with the same title page could differ in content. The 19th century saw the rise of national atlases produced by states.

### Types and Formats
Atlases are categorized by use. A **travel atlas** (or road map) is designed for easy use during travel, often with spiral bindings. A **desk atlas** resembles a reference book. Specialized atlases include **star atlases** for the celestial sphere and **anatomical atlases** for the human body. Modern atlases are typically standardized publications, unlike their customizable predecessors, and many are now available in multimedia or digital formats.

### Selected Important Atlases
Significant historical atlases include Blaeu's *Atlas Maior* (1662–1667) and the *Klencke Atlas* (1660), one of the world's largest books. The 19th and 20th centuries produced enduring works like *Stielers Handatlas* (Germany, 1817–1944), the *Times Atlas of the World* (UK, 1895–present), and the *National Geographic Atlas of the World* (US, 1963–present). Contemporary examples include the *North American Environmental Atlas*.

## Terms
- **Atlas**: A collection of maps, typically bound as a book, presenting geographical or other spatial information.
- **Cartographer**: A person who draws or produces maps.
- **Scale**: The ratio of a distance on a map to the corresponding distance on the ground (e.g., 1:250,000).
- **Celestial sphere**: An imaginary sphere of arbitrarily large radius, concentric with Earth, onto which celestial objects are projected.
- **Asterism**: A pattern of stars, smaller than a constellation, recognized in popular culture.
- **National atlas**: An atlas produced by a state to represent the geography and statistics of its territory.
- **Multimedia format**: An atlas presented using digital or electronic media, not just printed paper.

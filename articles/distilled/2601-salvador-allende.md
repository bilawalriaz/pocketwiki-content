# Salvador Allende

## Overview
Salvador Allende was a Chilean socialist politician who served as president from 1970 to 1973. He is historically significant as the first Marxist to be elected president in a liberal democracy in Latin America. His presidency, which pursued a democratic transition to socialism, ended in a military coup d'état backed by the United States, leading to his death and the start of a 17-year dictatorship.

## Timeline
- **26 June 1908** — Salvador Allende is born in Santiago, Chile.
- **1933** — Co-founds a section of the Socialist Party of Chile in Valparaíso.
- **1938** — Becomes Minister of Health in the Popular Front government.
- **1970 (4 Sept)** — Wins a plurality in the presidential election as the Popular Unity candidate.
- **1970 (24 Oct)** — Congress confirms him as president after a failed kidnapping of General Schneider.
- **1970 (3 Nov)** — Assumes the presidency, signing a Statute of Constitutional Guarantees.
- **1972** — Receives the Lenin Peace Prize from the Soviet Union.
- **1973 (29 June)** — A failed coup attempt, the "Tanquetazo," occurs.
- **1973 (22 Aug)** — The Chamber of Deputies passes a resolution accusing the government of unconstitutional acts.
- **1973 (11 Sept)** — Military coup led by General Augusto Pinochet; Allende dies by suicide in La Moneda Palace.

## Body

### Early Life and Political Rise
Allende was born into a progressive, upper-middle-class family. He studied medicine at the University of Chile, where he was influenced by the social determinants of disease. He co-founded the Socialist Party of Chile in 1933 and held various political offices, including deputy, senator, and Minister of Health. He unsuccessfully ran for president in 1952, 1958, and 1964, with CIA intervention noted in the 1964 election.

### The 1970 Election and Path to Power
In 1970, Allende won the presidency as the candidate of the Popular Unity coalition with a 36.6% plurality. Under the constitution, Congress had to confirm the winner. The election was marked by the kidnapping and death of Army Commander-in-Chief General René Schneider, a constitutionalist opposed to military intervention. His death ended military opposition to Allende's confirmation. Allende took office after agreeing to a Statute of Constitutional Guarantees.

### "The Chilean Path to Socialism"
As president, Allende pursued "The Chilean Path to Socialism," a democratic transition to a socialist society. His government nationalized major industries, including copper mining and banking, expanded education and healthcare, and implemented land reform and redistribution. He also initiated Project Cybersyn, an innovative computer network for economic planning. His policies aimed to improve living standards for the working class and poor.

### Economic Crisis and Political Conflict
Allende's economic policies initially saw growth but soon led to high inflation, shortages, and a fiscal deficit. This was exacerbated by a U.S.-backed campaign of economic destabilization, including funding strikes. He faced intense opposition from a Congress dominated by the Christian Democrats and the right-wing National Party, as well as from the judiciary. A constitutional crisis peaked in August 1973 when the Chamber of Deputies accused the government of unconstitutional acts and called on the military to enforce order.

### The 1973 Coup and Aftermath
On 11 September 1973, the military, led by General Augusto Pinochet, staged a coup. Allende refused to resign and died by suicide in the presidential palace during the attack. The coup ended Chile's democratic period and installed a military dictatorship that dissolved Congress, suspended the constitution, and carried out widespread persecution, resulting in thousands of disappearances and deaths. The dictatorship lasted until 1990.

## Terms
*   **Popular Unity (Unidad Popular):** The left-wing coalition that nominated Allende for president in 1970.
*   **Statute of Constitutional Guarantees:** A document Allende signed, promising to respect the constitution, which secured Christian Democratic support for his confirmation by Congress.
*   **Project Cybersyn:** An experimental computer network designed for real-time economic management and decision-making during Allende's presidency.
*   **Tanquetazo:** A failed coup attempt on 29 June 1973, led by a tank regiment.
*   **Constitutionalism:** The doctrine, held by figures like General Schneider, that the military's role is strictly professional and apolitical.
*   **Track I and Track II:** Covert CIA plans to prevent Allende from taking power or to unseat him after his election.
*   **Government Junta:** The military governing body that ruled Chile after the 1973 coup.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Circumstances of Death:** While a 2011 autopsy confirmed suicide, the exact circumstances were disputed for decades, with some alleging assassination.
*   **U.S. Role:** The degree of direct U.S. involvement in the 1973 coup itself remains debated, though declassified documents confirm extensive efforts to destabilize Allende's government.
*   **Economic Causes:** The primary cause of Chile's economic crisis under Allende is debated, with factors including his policies, U.S. intervention, and international market forces like falling copper prices.
*   **Soviet Support:** The extent and nature of Soviet economic and political support for Allende's government is detailed in various sources but remains a subject of historical analysis.

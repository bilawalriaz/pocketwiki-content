# Nuremberg

## Overview
Nuremberg (German: Nürnberg) is the second-largest city in Bavaria and the largest in Franconia. First documented in 1050, it grew around an imperial castle into a leading city of the Holy Roman Empire, a center of Renaissance art and science, and later the site of infamous Nazi rallies and post-war war crimes trials. Today, it is a major transport, industrial, and cultural hub known for its medieval heritage and Christmas market.

## Timeline
- **1050** — First documentary mention as the location of an imperial castle.
- **1219** — Emperor Frederick II grants the "Great Charter of Freedom," granting town rights and imperial immediacy.
- **1356** — Charles IV's Golden Bull names Nuremberg as the city for newly elected kings to hold their first Imperial Diet.
- **1423** — Emperor Sigismund grants permanent custody of the Imperial Regalia to the city.
- **1471** — Johannes Mueller (Regiomontanus) builds an astronomical observatory in Nuremberg.
- **1525** — Nuremberg accepts the Protestant Reformation.
- **1632** — City is occupied by Swedish forces during the Thirty Years' War.
- **1806** — Nuremberg's independence ends; it is incorporated into Bavaria.
- **1835** — The first German railway line opens between Nuremberg and Fürth.
- **1935** — The Nuremberg Laws, revoking citizenship for Jews, are passed at a Nazi rally.
- **1945** — The medieval city centre is largely destroyed by Allied bombing; the city is captured by U.S. forces.
- **1945-46** — The Nuremberg trials for Nazi war criminals are held in the city's Palace of Justice.

## Body

### Medieval and Early Modern Rise
Nuremberg's importance grew from its strategic location on trade routes. It became a Free Imperial City in the 13th century, hosting frequent Imperial Diets and storing the Imperial Regalia from 1424 until 1796. The city was a major center for trade, art, and publishing during the Renaissance, associated with figures like Albrecht Dürer. It accepted the Reformation in 1525 but suffered severe decline during the Thirty Years' War (1618–1648) due to military occupation and trade disruption. Its independence ended in 1806 when it was incorporated into Bavaria.

### Nazi Era and World War II
The Nazi Party chose Nuremberg for its annual rallies due to the city's imperial history. The 1935 rally saw the passage of the antisemitic Nuremberg Laws. The city was also a center for Nazi propaganda and the headquarters of Julius Streicher. During Kristallnacht in 1938, its synagogues were destroyed. As a major military production site, Nuremberg was heavily bombed in 1945, destroying about 80% of its medieval centre. It was captured after fierce fighting in April 1945.

### Post-War and Modern City
The Nuremberg trials (1945–46) were held in the city's largely undamaged Palace of Justice to symbolically judge Nazism where it was glorified. The city was meticulously rebuilt after the war, restoring much of its historic appearance. Today, Nuremberg is a prosperous industrial and commercial center, known for its engineering, electrical equipment, and toy manufacturing. It is a major transport hub with an airport, high-speed rail connections, and a position on the Rhine–Main–Danube Canal.

## Terms
- **Free Imperial City**: A city in the Holy Roman Empire subject only to the emperor, not to a local prince or bishop.
- **Imperial Diet (Reichstag)**: The deliberative body of the Holy Roman Empire, where princes and cities met.
- **Imperial Regalia**: The ceremonial objects of the Holy Roman Emperor, including crown, scepter, and orb.
- **Immediacy (Reichsfreiheit)**: The status of being directly subject to the Emperor, granting self-governance.
- **Burgrave**: A noble appointed to administer a castle and its surrounding territory for the emperor.
- **Kristallnacht**: The state-sponsored pogrom against Jews throughout Nazi Germany on November 9–10, 1938.
- **Nuremberg Laws**: Antisemitic laws passed in 1935 that stripped German Jews of citizenship.
- **Conurbation**: A large metropolitan area formed by the merging of several cities and towns.

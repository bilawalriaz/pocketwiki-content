# Rabat

## Overview
Rabat is the capital city of Morocco, located on the Atlantic coast at the mouth of the Bou Regreg river. It is one of Morocco's four imperial cities, and its medina is a UNESCO World Heritage Site. The city's history spans from ancient Roman ruins to its modern role as the nation's administrative center, established during the French Protectorate and confirmed at independence in 1956.

## Timeline
- **1st century BC** — Romans build the city of Sala Colonia at the site of modern Chellah.
- **1150/1151** — Almohad caliph Abd al-Mu'min builds a kasbah (citadel) on the site of the current Kasbah of the Udayas.
- **c. 1195** — Almohad caliph Abu Yusuf Ya'qub al-Mansur begins construction of a new fortified capital, Ribat al-Fath, including the Hassan Tower.
- **1627** — Rabat and Salé unite to form the autonomous Republic of Bou Regreg, a haven for corsairs.
- **1666** — 'Alawi dynasty founder al-Rashid conquers the area, ending the republic's independence.
- **1912** — French establish a protectorate; Rabat is made the administrative center.
- **1956** — Morocco achieves independence; Rabat is confirmed as the capital.
- **2011** — The Rabat–Salé tramway, Morocco's first, opens.
- **2015** — Rabat becomes part of the Rabat-Salé-Kénitra administrative region.

## Body

### Ancient and Medieval Origins
The area's earliest known settlement is the Roman city of Sala Colonia, built on a site with earlier Phoenician and Mauretanian activity. Sala was a Roman naval outpost and commercial way station. It was largely abandoned by the 5th century. In the medieval Islamic period, the site saw successive fortifications. The Almohads established a major kasbah in the 12th century. In the late 12th century, Caliph Ya'qub al-Mansur launched an ambitious project to build a new imperial capital, Ribat al-Fath, with vast walls and a monumental mosque (the Hassan Tower). This project was left unfinished after his death in 1199, and the site was largely abandoned during the subsequent Marinid period.

### Corsair Republic and 'Alawi Rule
In the early 17th century, Morisco refugees from Spain settled in the area, uniting Rabat and Salé into the autonomous Republic of Bou Regreg in 1627. This republic became a base for corsairs (pirates) known as the "Salé Rovers." The 'Alawi dynasty conquered the area in 1666 but allowed piracy to continue until the early 19th century. 'Alawi sultans, particularly Moulay Isma'il and Sidi Muhammad ibn 'Abdallah, developed the city as a royal residence, expanding the kasbah and building the Dar al-Makhzen palace. By the end of the 19th century, the city had 20,000–25,000 inhabitants.

### French Protectorate and Modern Capital
France established a protectorate over Morocco in 1912. After riots in Fez, the French administrator Hubert Lyautey relocated the capital to Rabat, citing its coastal location and proximity to the future economic hub of Casablanca. The French designed a modern administrative quarter, the Ville Nouvelle. Rabat's population grew from an estimated 35,000–40,000 in 1912 to approximately 156,000 by 1952. After Moroccan independence in 1956, King Mohammed V kept the capital in Rabat. The post-independence period saw the departure of most foreign residents and continued urban growth.

### Geography and Climate
Rabat is located on the Atlantic coast at the mouth of the Bou Regreg river, opposite the city of Salé. Its neighborhoods range from the historic Medina and Kasbah of the Udayas to modern districts like Agdal and the affluent Souissi. The city has a hot-summer Mediterranean climate (Csa) with mild, damp winters and warm, dry summers. Average annual precipitation is 560 mm.

### Culture and Landmarks
Rabat's cultural institutions include the Mohammed V Theatre and the Grand Theatre of Rabat, designed by Zaha Hadid. The city hosts the Mawazine music festival. Key historic monuments are the Kasbah of the Udayas, the unfinished Hassan Tower, the Mausoleum of Mohammed V, and the archaeological site of Chellah, which contains both Marinid ruins and the Roman city of Sala Colonia. The city's medina is a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

### Education and Transport
Major educational institutions include Mohammed V University (founded 1957). Rabat is served by Rabat–Salé Airport and a national rail network (ONCF) connecting it to other major cities. The Rabat–Salé tramway, opened in 2011, connects the two cities.

## Terms
- **Kasbah**: A citadel or fortified quarter within a city.
- **Medina**: The old, walled part of a North African city.
- **Ribat**: An Islamic fortified monastery or outpost.
- **Corsair**: A pirate, especially one operating from the Barbary Coast.
- **Moriscos**: People of Muslim or Moorish descent who were expelled from Spain in 1609.
- **'Alawi dynasty**: The current ruling dynasty of Morocco, founded in the 17th century.
- **Ville Nouvelle**: The modern, planned quarter of a city, often built during French colonial rule.
- **Mellah**: A Jewish quarter in a North African city.

## Debates and open questions
The existence of a Phoenician settlement in the area of Rabat has been debated by archaeologists. The exact location of the 10th-century ribat founded by the Umayyads of Cordoba or their allies has not been confirmed by historians.

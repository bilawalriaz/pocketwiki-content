# Ulysses S. Grant

## Overview
Ulysses S. Grant was the 18th U.S. president (1869–1877) and the Commanding General of the Union Army who led it to victory in the American Civil War. His presidency was defined by efforts to secure civil rights for African Americans during Reconstruction, stabilize the post-war economy, and manage foreign policy, though it was later marred by scandals and economic depression. His military reputation, once diminished by the "Lost Cause" narrative, has been significantly rehabilitated by modern historians.

## Body

### Early Life and Military Career
Born in Ohio, Grant graduated from West Point in 1843. He served with distinction in the Mexican–American War, where he learned logistics and strategy from generals Zachary Taylor and Winfield Scott. After the war, he struggled financially in civilian life and resigned from the Army in 1854, citing alcohol problems. He freed his only slave, William Jones, in 1859.

### Civil War Leadership
Grant rejoined the Union Army in 1861, rising to prominence with victories at Forts Henry and Donelson. Despite controversy after the bloody Battle of Shiloh, President Lincoln defended him. His Vicksburg campaign (1863) was a strategic masterpiece that split the Confederacy. After the victory at Chattanooga, Lincoln promoted him to command all Union armies. Grant devised a coordinated strategy to defeat Robert E. Lee in the Overland Campaign, enduring heavy casualties at battles like Cold Harbor before besieging Petersburg. Lee surrendered at Appomattox in April 1865.

### Presidency and Reconstruction
Elected in 1868, Grant was a strong advocate for civil rights. He signed the act creating the Department of Justice and used the Enforcement Acts to crush the Ku Klux Klan. His administration supported the Fifteenth Amendment, which guaranteed voting rights for Black men. However, Northern support for Reconstruction waned during his second term, and "Redeemer" Democrats used violence to regain control of Southern states. Grant's response to the Panic of 1873 was ineffective, and his second term was plagued by scandals like the Whiskey Ring.

### Foreign Policy and Native American Policy
Grant's foreign policy, led by Secretary of State Hamilton Fish, successfully settled the Alabama Claims with Britain through arbitration. His attempt to annex Santo Domingo was defeated in the Senate. His "Peace Policy" for Native Americans aimed to assimilate them into Anglo-American culture, replacing tribal treaties with individual wards of the state. The policy was undermined by conflicts like the Great Sioux War and the Battle of the Little Bighorn.

### Post-Presidency and Legacy
After a world tour, Grant lost a bid for a third Republican nomination in 1880. He lost his fortune in a financial scandal but wrote his *Personal Memoirs* while dying of cancer, providing for his family. He died in 1885. His historical reputation, once damaged by the "Lost Cause" myth and scandals, has been rehabilitated by modern scholars who emphasize his military skill and civil rights achievements.

## Terms
*   **Reconstruction:** The period (1865–1877) after the Civil War during which the federal government attempted to reintegrate Southern states and define the legal status of African Americans.
*   **Fifteenth Amendment:** Ratified in 1870, it prohibited federal and state governments from denying a citizen the right to vote based on "race, color, or previous condition of servitude."
*   **Enforcement Acts:** A series of laws (1870–1871) passed to protect African Americans' voting rights and combat the Ku Klux Klan by authorizing federal prosecution of civil rights violations.
*   **Alabama Claims:** A series of demands for damages by the U.S. against Great Britain for British-built Confederate warships (like the CSS *Alabama*) that destroyed Union merchant ships during the Civil War.
*   **Panic of 1873:** A severe economic depression triggered by the failure of Jay Cooke & Company, leading to bank failures, railroad bankruptcies, and widespread unemployment.
*   **Whiskey Ring:** A scandal (1875) in which distillers and government officials conspired to evade federal taxes on spirits, defrauding the government of millions.
*   **"Peace Policy":** Grant's approach to Native American affairs, which aimed to replace corrupt Indian agents with religious appointees and move tribes onto reservations for assimilation and Christianization.
*   **Lost Cause:** A pseudohistorical ideology that romanticizes the Confederacy and its cause, often downplaying slavery and portraying Confederate leaders as noble while diminishing Union leaders like Grant.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Effectiveness of Reconstruction:** Historians debate whether Grant did enough to protect African Americans in the South, especially as Northern political will faded during his second term.
*   **Cause of the Panic of 1873:** Scholars discuss whether the depression was primarily caused by the Coinage Act of 1873 (the "Crime of '73") or by broader structural issues in the post-war economy.
*   **Grant's Personal Struggles:** The extent and impact of Grant's alcoholism during his military and political career remain a subject of biographical analysis and debate.
*   **Historical Reputation:** The dramatic fall and subsequent 21st-century rehabilitation of Grant's reputation among historians is a key topic in Civil War and presidential historiography.

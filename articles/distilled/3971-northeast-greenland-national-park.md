# Northeast Greenland National Park

## Overview
Northeast Greenland National Park is the world's largest national park, protecting a vast, remote Arctic wilderness. Established in 1974 and expanded in 1988, it is significant for its immense size, its status as the first national park in the Kingdom of Denmark, and its role in conserving a unique polar ecosystem with globally important wildlife populations.

## Timeline
- **22 May 1974** — Park established from the northern part of Ittoqqortoormiit Municipality.
- **January 1977** — Designated an international biosphere reserve.
- **1988** — Park expanded by 272,000 km² to its present size.

## Body

### Geography and Establishment
The park covers 972,000 km² of Greenland's interior and northeastern coast, an area larger than Tanzania. Its borders are largely straight lines, adjoining the Sermersooq and Avannaata municipalities. The interior is part of the Greenland Ice Sheet, but large ice-free areas exist along the coast and on Peary Land in the north. It includes the geographical areas of King Frederick VIII Land and King Christian X Land.

### Population and Administration
The park has no permanent human population. In 1986, 40 people at Mestersvig were the last recorded residents, involved in mine cleanup. Since then, censuses have recorded zero permanent inhabitants. Occasional use occurs at about 40 sites in summer. In winter 2008, only 31 people and about 110 dogs were present at coastal stations like Daneborg (headquarters of the Sirius Patrol), Danmarkshavn, Station Nord, and Mestersvig, plus the inland Summit Camp. The Zackenberg research station is summer-only. The park is overseen by the Greenland Department of Environment and Nature.

### Wildlife
The park's coastal regions host an estimated 5,000 to 15,000 musk oxen, representing about 40% of the world population as of 1993. Other mammals include polar bears, walrus, Arctic fox, stoat, collared lemming, Arctic hare, and a small population of Greenland wolf. Marine mammals include ringed, bearded, harp, and hooded seals, as well as narwhal and beluga whale. Breeding birds include the great northern diver, barnacle goose, pink-footed goose, common and king eider, gyrfalcon, snowy owl, sanderling, ptarmigan, and raven.

## Terms
*   **National Park:** A protected area of land owned or managed by a government, set aside for conservation and recreation.
*   **Biosphere Reserve:** An international designation for areas of terrestrial and coastal ecosystems promoting conservation and sustainable use.
*   **Greenland Ice Sheet:** The vast body of ice covering most of Greenland, the second-largest ice body in the world.
*   **Sirius Patrol:** The Danish military unit responsible for patrolling and policing the remote Northeast Greenland National Park.
*   **Musk Ox:** A large Arctic mammal with long, shaggy fur and curved horns, adapted to cold climates.
*   **Peary Land:** A peninsula in far northern Greenland, one of the most northerly land areas in the world.
*   **Zackenberg Ecological Research Operations (ZERO):** A year-round research station in the park focused on high-Arctic ecology and climate.

# Mombasa

## Overview
Mombasa is Kenya's oldest and second-largest city, a historic Indian Ocean trading hub whose strategic location led to control by multiple powers. Its rich Swahili culture, major port, and tourism industry make it a vital economic and cultural center in East Africa.

## Timeline
- **c. 900 AD** — Traditional founding date of Mombasa.
- **1151** — Arab geographer al-Idrisi mentions Mombasa as a prosperous trading town.
- **c. 1300** — Oldest stone mosque, Mnara, is built.
- **1498** — Portuguese explorer Vasco da Gama becomes the first known European to visit.
- **1502** — Mombasa becomes an independent sultanate, renamed Mvita.
- **1589** — Portuguese recapture Mombasa after an Ottoman expedition and Zimba raids.
- **1593** — Portuguese begin building Fort Jesus.
- **1698** — Capture of Fort Jesus brings Mombasa under the influence of the Imamate of Oman.
- **1824-1826** — British protectorate over Mombasa.
- **1887** — Mombasa is relinquished to the British East Africa Association.
- **1895** — Comes under formal British administration; becomes capital of the British East Africa Protectorate.
- **1907** — Capital of the protectorate is moved to Nairobi.

## Body

### Ancient and Medieval Foundations
Mombasa's founding is linked to legendary rulers Mwana Mkisi and Shehe Mvita, whose names have linguistic ties to Central Africa, acknowledging Bantu-speaking origins. The city was a prosperous trading town by the 12th century, exporting ivory, millet, sesame, and coconuts. It was part of the Kilwa Sultanate from the early 14th century until 1513. Its architecture, like the ogee arch in the 1570 Mandhry Mosque, suggests indigenous Swahili development.

### Colonial and Pre-Colonial Rule
After Portuguese encounters beginning in 1498, Mombasa was sacked, rebuilt, and contested for centuries. The Portuguese built Fort Jesus in 1593. Omani control began with the capture of Fort Jesus in 1698, followed by periods of local sultanates. A brief British protectorate existed from 1824-1826. Britain formally took control in 1887, making Mombasa the capital of the East Africa Protectorate and the terminus of the Uganda Railway. The capital was moved to Nairobi around 1907 due to health concerns about Mombasa's swampy ground.

### Modern City and Economy
Today, Mombasa is a tourism-based economy and home to Kenya's most important seaport, Kilindini Harbour, which serves regional trade. Key industries include an oil refinery, cement production, and a growing call centre business supported by undersea telecom cables. The city faces challenges like high youth unemployment (44% in 2016) and coastal erosion due to climate change. A 2018 directive requires buildings in the Old Town and CBD to be painted white with blue trim to boost tourism.

### Geography and Transport
The city is centered on Mombasa Island, separated from the mainland by creeks and connected by bridges, a causeway, and ferries. Moi International Airport is the second-largest in Kenya. The Mombasa–Nairobi Standard Gauge Railway, completed in 2017, replaced the old Uganda Railway. The port is a major hub, and the Dongo Kundu Bypass Highway is under construction to improve connectivity to the south coast.

## Terms
- **Swahili**: A Bantu language and culture developed along the East African coast through centuries of trade and interaction.
- **Kilwa Sultanate**: A medieval sultanate centered on the island of Kilwa Kisiwani that controlled much of the Swahili Coast trade.
- **Fort Jesus**: A Portuguese fort built in Mombasa in 1593 to secure their control over the region.
- **Imamate of Oman**: An Islamic state based in Oman that exerted control over parts of the Swahili Coast, including Mombasa, from the late 17th century.
- **Kilindini Harbour**: The deep-water natural port of Mombasa, the largest in East Africa.
- **Uganda Railway**: A colonial railway built by the British (1896-1901) connecting Mombasa to Uganda, which spurred the city's growth.
- **Matatu**: A privately owned minibus used for public transport in Kenya.
- **Taarab**: A music genre popular in the Swahili Coast, with influences from Arab, Indian, and African traditions.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The exact founding date of Mombasa is unknown; Kenyan school books place it at 900 AD, but this is a traditional rather than definitively proven date.
- The origins of Swahili architecture are debated; features like the ogee arch in the Mandhry Mosque suggest indigenous African development rather than solely adoption from non-African Muslims.
- The city's identity and governance have been shaped by successive foreign powers (Portuguese, Omani, British), leading to ongoing discussions about cultural heritage and autonomy.

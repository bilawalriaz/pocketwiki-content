# Baja California peninsula

## Overview
The Baja California peninsula is a long, narrow landmass in northwestern Mexico that separates the Gulf of California from the Pacific Ocean. It is a region of significant geological, ecological, and historical interest, known for its desert landscapes, unique biodiversity, and the centuries-old misconception that it was an island.

## Timeline
- **1510** — The name "California" appears in the romance novel *Las Sergas de Esplandián*.
- **1532** — Hernán Cortés sends ships to find the "Island of California"; they disappear.
- **1539** — Francisco de Ulloa explores the coasts, proving Baja California is a peninsula.
- **1622** — A map by Henry Briggs popularizes the erroneous idea of California as an island.
- **1690s–1800s** — Spanish Jesuit missionaries establish the first missions in Baja California.
- **1701** — Eusebio Kino's map provides definitive proof of the peninsula's connection to the mainland.
- **1804** — The province of Las Californias is divided into Alta (Upper) and Baja (Lower) California.
- **1821** — Following Mexican independence, the Baja California Territory is established.
- **1931** — The territory is divided into Northern and Southern territories.
- **1952** — The northern territory becomes the state of Baja California.
- **1974** — The southern territory becomes the state of Baja California Sur.

## Body

### Historical Exploration and Naming
The peninsula's name derives from a mythical "Island of California" described in a 1510 Spanish novel. Early explorers, including Hernán Cortés, believed it was an island. In 1539, Francisco de Ulloa's expedition confirmed it was a peninsula, but the island myth persisted in European cartography for nearly two centuries. The myth was finally debunked by maps from explorer Eusebio Kino in the early 1700s. The Spanish divided the region into "Alta California" (now the U.S. state) and "Baja California" in 1804.

### Geology and Geography
The peninsula was once part of the North American Plate. About 12 to 15 million years ago, tectonic activity along the East Pacific Rise began separating it, and it is now part of the Pacific Plate, moving northwest. The Gulf of California Rift Zone defines its eastern edge. The Peninsular Ranges, an eroded Jurassic-Cretaceous batholith, form its mountainous backbone. The climate is predominantly hot desert, with Mediterranean conditions in the north.

### Ecoregions and Environment
The peninsula hosts diverse ecoregions, including the California chaparral in the northwest, pine-oak forests in the northern and southern mountains, and extensive deserts like the Vizcaíno and Sonoran. The Gulf of California xeric scrub lines the eastern coast. Unique ecosystems include desert mangroves, which have accumulated thick peat layers over millennia, recording sea-level rise.

### Political Divisions and Modern Era
After Mexican independence, the peninsula was a single territory. It was divided into northern and southern territories in 1931. The north became the state of Baja California in 1952, and the south became Baja California Sur in 1974. The 1973 completion of the Trans-Peninsular Highway greatly improved connectivity and boosted tourism, which is now a major industry centered on ecotourism, sports fishing, and resorts.

### Indigenous Peoples and Culture
At the time of Spanish contact, the peninsula was home to several indigenous groups, including the Yuman-speaking Cucapá, Kiliwa, Pai Pai, Cochimí, and Kumiai. Each had distinct clothing traditions using natural materials like animal hides, plant fibers, and pigments. The "Flor de Cucapá" is a modern traditional costume created in 1954 to symbolize the state's cultural identity.

## Terms
- **Peninsula:** A piece of land almost surrounded by water or projecting out into a body of water.
- **Tectonic Plate:** A massive, irregularly shaped slab of solid rock, part of the Earth's lithosphere.
- **Batholith:** A large mass of intrusive igneous rock that forms from cooled magma deep in the Earth's crust.
- **Xeric Shrubland:** A plant community characterized by drought-adapted shrubs and sparse vegetation.
- **Ecoregion:** An area of similar climate, geology, and native plant and animal communities.
- **Mediterranean Climate:** Characterized by hot, dry summers and mild, wet winters.
- **Peat:** A brown, soil-like material consisting of partially decayed vegetation, formed in waterlogged conditions.
- **Trans-Peninsular Highway:** Mexican Federal Highway 1, the main paved road running the length of the peninsula.

## Debates and Open Questions
The source notes that the persistence of the "California as an island" myth in cartography is debated. One theory suggests it originated from a 1620 map by friar Antonio Ascension based on misconceptions. A 2023 opinion by Garcia and Jorge proposes the error may have been sustained because Spain kept its cartography secret from other European powers.

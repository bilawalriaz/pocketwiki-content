# Kapila

## Overview
Kapila is a legendary Vedic sage credited as the founder of the Samkhya school of Hindu philosophy, a dualistic system distinguishing matter (prakriti) from spirit (purusa). His influence spans Hindu, Jain, and Buddhist traditions, where he is variously regarded as a philosopher, an incarnation of Vishnu, or a previous life of the Buddha. The historical Kapila likely lived in the 7th-6th century BCE, though his name is applied to multiple figures across Indian texts.

## Timeline
- **c. 7th-6th century BCE** — Traditional period for the sage Kapila, founder of Samkhya philosophy.
- **c. 1200-1000 BCE** — The Kapila Śākhā, a school of the Yajurveda, is mentioned in texts from this period.
- **c. 500 BCE** — The Shvetashvatara Upanishad mentions both "Samkhya" and "Kapila."
- **c. 5th century CE** — Gaudapada lists Kapila among seven great sages.
- **c. 12th century CE** — Hemacandra's Jain epic poem mentions Kapila as a Brahmin who converted to Jainism.

## Body

### Philosophical Founder
Kapila is traditionally considered the first master of the Samkhya school, with his student Āsuri. He is credited with authoring the *Samkhya-sutra* (also called *Kapila-sutra*), which aphoristically presents Samkhya's dualistic philosophy. This system was later elaborated in texts like the *Samkhyakarika*. In dialogues such as the *Mahabharata*, Kapila defends principles like *ahimsa* (non-violence).

### Depictions in Hindu Texts
Hindu Puranas present multiple, sometimes conflicting, genealogies for Kapila. The *Bhagavata Purana* describes him as the son of sage Kardama and Devahūti, to whom he teaches Samkhya philosophy. Other texts identify him as an incarnation of Vishnu, a son of Kashyapa, or a son of Prahlada. The *Brahmanda Purana* explicitly calls him an incarnation of Vishnu. His imagery in temple art often depicts him as a bearded ascetic in meditation.

### Role in Other Traditions
In Jainism, Kapila appears in texts like the *Uttaradhyayana-sutra* and is described as a contemporary of Krishna. Buddhist literature, such as the *Jataka tales*, states the Buddha was Kapila in a previous life. Scholars have long studied the relationship between Kapila's and Buddha's teachings, noting shared concepts like the goal of ending suffering, though their methods differ significantly. The city of Kapilavastu, where the Buddha was born, is named in Kapila's honor.

### Attributed Works
Numerous texts are attributed to Kapila, though many are lost or of uncertain authorship. These include the core *Samkhya-sutra*, various *Samhitas*, *Smritis*, and works on Ayurveda. The *Kapila-Devahuti Samvada* in the *Bhagavata Purana* is a foundational dialogue presenting his theological Samkhya.

## Terms
- **Samkhya**: A dualistic school of Hindu philosophy founded by Kapila, distinguishing between consciousness (purusa) and matter (prakriti).
- **Prakriti**: In Samkhya, the primordial material cause of the universe, encompassing all physical and psychological phenomena.
- **Purusa**: In Samkhya, pure consciousness or the spirit, which is distinct from and unaffected by matter.
- **Ahimsa**: The principle of non-violence or non-harm to living beings.
- **Vedic sage**: A wise person or seer from the Vedic period (c. 1500-500 BCE) of Indian history.
- **Avatar**: The incarnation or descent of a deity, particularly Vishnu, in earthly form.
- **Dhyana**: Meditation, a key practice in Hindu and Buddhist traditions.
- **Padmāsana**: The lotus position, a seated posture for meditation.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Historicity**: The exact century Kapila lived in is unclear, with suggestions ranging from the 7th to 6th century BCE. The name "Kapila" may refer to different individuals across texts.
- **Relationship to Buddhism**: Scholars debate the extent of Kapila's influence on the Buddha. While some concepts overlap, the teachings on achieving liberation are considered very different.
- **Identity in Puranas**: Puranic accounts conflict on Kapila's parentage and whether he is a human sage, an incarnation of Vishnu, or a son of other figures like Kashyapa or Prahlada.
- **Authorship**: The authorship of the *Samkhya-sutra* and other attributed works is traditionally given to Kapila but is subject to scholarly scrutiny.

# Arctic Archipelago

## Overview
The Arctic Archipelago is a vast group of over 36,000 islands north of mainland Canada, forming much of the country's northern territory. It is a region of immense geographic scale and strategic importance, characterized by its Arctic climate, sparse Inuit population, and ongoing sovereignty disputes over its waterways.

## Timeline
- **c. 2500 BCE** — Paleo-Eskimos arrive from the Canadian mainland, the first human inhabitants.
- **1000–1500 CE** — Thule people, ancestors of the Inuit, replace the Paleo-Eskimos.
- **1570s** — Martin Frobisher's explorations form the basis for British claims.
- **1870–80** — Canadian sovereignty initially limited to islands draining into Foxe Basin, Hudson Bay, and Hudson Strait.
- **1880** — Britain transfers the islands to Canada, establishing full Canadian sovereignty.
- **1895** — The District of Franklin is established, comprising almost all of the archipelago.
- **1999** — The District of Franklin is dissolved with the creation of Nunavut.
- **21st Century** — Climate change causes melting, with estimates projecting a 3.5 cm contribution to global sea level rise by 2100.

## Body

### History and Sovereignty
The first humans, Paleo-Eskimos, arrived around 2500 BCE, later being replaced by the Thule people (ancestors of the Inuit) between 1000 and 1500 CE. British claims, based on 1570s explorations, were transferred to Canada in 1880, establishing its sovereignty. A key ongoing dispute concerns the Northwest Passage; Canada claims its waterways as internal waters, while most nations view them as international. This disagreement involves concerns over environmental enforcement, security, and sovereignty. Another resolved dispute was over Hans Island in the Nares Strait, ownership of which is now shared with Denmark.

### Geography and Environment
The archipelago covers about 1,424,500 km² and consists of 36,563 islands, separated by the waterways of the Northwest Passage. It extends 2,400 km longitudinally and 1,900 km from the mainland to Cape Columbia on Ellesmere Island. After Greenland, it is the world's largest high-Arctic land area. The climate is Arctic, with tundra terrain except in mountains. The region is showing effects of climate change, with computer models estimating melting will contribute 3.5 cm to global sea level rise by 2100.

### Population and Settlement
Human settlement is extremely thin and scattered, consisting mainly of coastal Inuit communities on the southern islands. As of 2021, the total population was 23,073. Of the more than 36,000 islands, only 11 are populated. Baffin Island is the largest and most populous, with 13,309 people, accounting for over half the archipelago's total population.

## Terms
- **Arctic Archipelago**: The group of 36,563 islands north of the Canadian mainland, also called the Canadian Arctic Archipelago.
- **Northwest Passage**: The network of waterways separating the islands of the archipelago and the mainland; its legal status is disputed.
- **Thule people**: The ancestors of today's Inuit, who replaced earlier Paleo-Eskimo inhabitants between 1000 and 1500 CE.
- **Nunavut**: The Canadian territory created in 1999, which includes most of the archipelago.
- **District of Franklin**: A former administrative district (established 1895) that comprised almost all of the archipelago before Nunavut's creation.
- **Queen Elizabeth Islands**: The northernmost cluster of islands, including Ellesmere Island; formerly called the Parry Islands.
- **Hans Island**: A small island in the Nares Strait whose sovereignty was disputed for decades between Canada and Denmark.

## Debates and open questions
- **Northwest Passage Status**: Canada claims the waterways are internal waters, while most maritime nations consider them international. This affects environmental enforcement, security, and sovereignty.
- **Climate Change Impact**: While melting is occurring, the precise contribution to global sea level rise (estimated at 3.5 cm by 2100) and its broader effects are subjects of ongoing study.

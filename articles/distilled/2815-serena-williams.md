# Serena Williams

## Overview
Serena Williams is an American tennis player widely regarded as one of the greatest athletes of all time. She dominated the sport for over two decades, winning a record 23 Grand Slam singles titles in the Open Era and redefining women's tennis with her powerful, athletic style. Her career, marked by historic achievements, comebacks, and cultural impact, significantly increased diversity and commercial appeal in the sport.

## Body

### Early Life and Professional Debut
Born to Oracene Price and Richard Williams, Serena began playing tennis at age four. Her father, Richard, coached her and sister Venus, moving the family to Compton, California, to toughen them, and later to Florida for advanced training. Williams turned professional in 1995 at age 14. After a brief hiatus, she burst onto the scene in 1997, upsetting top-10 players Mary Pierce and Monica Seles. She won her first Grand Slam singles title at the 1999 US Open, becoming the second African-American woman to do so.

### First Dominance and the "Serena Slam" (2002–2003)
Williams's first peak came from 2002 to 2003. She won four consecutive Grand Slam singles titles—the 2002 French Open, Wimbledon, and US Open, followed by the 2003 Australian Open—each time defeating her sister Venus in the final. This non-calendar year Grand Slam was dubbed the "Serena Slam." She also achieved a Career Grand Slam and held all four major titles simultaneously.

### Injuries, Comebacks, and Return to No. 1 (2004–2010)
Following her initial dominance, Williams's career was interrupted by injuries and a decline in form. She staged a major comeback by winning the 2007 Australian Open unseeded. She gradually reclaimed her top ranking, culminating in a return to dominance starting with the 2012 Wimbledon Championships. In 2012, she won Olympic gold in singles, completing a Career Golden Slam, and began a period where she won eight of thirteen majors.

### Second "Serena Slam" and Record-Breaking (2014–2017)
From 2014 to 2015, Williams achieved her second "Serena Slam," winning four straight majors again. In 2015, she also completed the Surface Slam (winning all four majors across different surfaces in one year). At the 2017 Australian Open, she won her 23rd Grand Slam singles title, surpassing Steffi Graf's Open Era record. She was pregnant during this victory.

### Later Career, Retirement, and 2026 Return
After giving birth in 2017, Williams returned to reach four more Grand Slam finals but did not win another major. In August 2022, she announced her impending "evolution" away from professional tennis, playing her final match at the US Open. In June 2026, she made a surprise return to competition, accepting wildcards to play doubles at the Queen's Club Championships and Wimbledon, and singles at Wimbledon.

### Doubles and Olympic Success with Venus
With her sister Venus, Serena formed one of the most successful doubles teams in history. They won 14 Grand Slam doubles titles, remaining undefeated in major finals (14–0). They achieved a non-calendar year Grand Slam in doubles (2009–2010) and won four Olympic gold medals together, an all-time record in tennis.

### Playing Style and Legacy
Williams was an aggressive baseliner with a powerful serve, considered the greatest in women's tennis history. Her game combined speed, power, and mental fortitude, allowing for extraordinary comebacks. She and Venus are credited with ushering in a new era of power and athleticism in women's tennis. She is the highest-earning female athlete of all time and has been named the greatest female tennis player by numerous outlets.

## Terms
- **Grand Slam**: The four major tennis tournaments: Australian Open, French Open, Wimbledon, and US Open.
- **Open Era**: The period in professional tennis beginning in 1968, when Grand Slam tournaments were open to both amateur and professional players.
- **Career Golden Slam**: Winning all four Grand Slam singles titles and an Olympic gold medal in singles over the course of a career.
- **Serena Slam**: A non-calendar year Grand Slam, where a player holds all four major titles consecutively but not within a single calendar year.
- **Surface Slam**: Winning all four Grand Slam titles on three different surfaces (hard, clay, grass) within a single calendar year.
- **WTA**: Women's Tennis Association, the principal organizing body of women's professional tennis.
- **Aggressive Baseliner**: A playing style focused on powerful groundstrokes from the back of the court to dictate points.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Greatest of All Time (GOAT) Debate**: While widely considered the greatest female tennis player, her status relative to other legends like Steffi Graf and Margaret Court is a subject of ongoing discussion.
- **Impact of Coaching and Family**: The role of her father Richard Williams's unconventional coaching methods and the dynamic of competing against her sister Venus have been analyzed for their influence on her career.
- **2018 US Open Final Controversy**: The match against Naomi Osaka, marred by code violations and a game penalty against Williams, sparked debates about sexism in officiating and sportsmanship.

# Léon Foucault

## Overview
Jean Bernard Léon Foucault (1819–1868) was a French physicist whose experiments provided direct, visual proof of Earth's rotation and significantly advanced the measurement of the speed of light. His work bridged theoretical physics and practical instrumentation, with inventions and tests that remain fundamental in optics and astronomy.

## Timeline
- **18 September 1819** — Born in Paris.
- **c. 1840s** — Abandoned medical studies for physics; assisted Alfred Donné and collaborated with Hippolyte Fizeau on light research.
- **1849** — Demonstrated that absorption and emission lines at the same wavelength originate from the same material.
- **1850** — Used a rotating mirror to measure the speed of light, showing it travels slower in water than air.
- **1851** — Publicly demonstrated Earth's rotation using a pendulum at the Panthéon, Paris.
- **1852** — Used and named the gyroscope as a simpler proof of Earth's rotation.
- **1855** — Received the Copley Medal; discovered eddy currents ("Foucault currents") in a rotating copper disc.
- **1857** — Invented the Foucault polarizer.
- **1858** — Devised the Foucault knife-edge test for shaping telescope mirrors.
- **1862** — Measured the speed of light as 298,000 km/s; made a member of the Bureau des Longitudes.
- **11 February 1868** — Died in Paris, probably from multiple sclerosis.

## Body

### Early Career and Light Research
Foucault began his scientific career improving photographic processes and assisting in lectures on microscopic anatomy. His early collaborations with Hippolyte Fizeau involved studying solar light intensity and the interference of infrared radiation. A key 1849 experiment established that the spectral lines of absorption and emission for a given material are identical, differing only due to the light source's temperature.

### Proving Earth's Rotation
Foucault's most famous achievement was the 1851 pendulum experiment at the Panthéon. By suspending a long, heavy pendulum and allowing it to swing freely, he showed that its plane of oscillation slowly rotated, providing a direct, observable effect of Earth's diurnal (daily) rotation on its axis. This experiment caused a worldwide sensation. The following year, he introduced the gyroscope as a conceptually simpler device to demonstrate the same principle.

### Measurement of Light and Optical Innovations
Foucault made two major contributions to measuring the speed of light. His 1850 rotating mirror experiment, which showed light travels slower in water than in air, was critical evidence against Newton's corpuscular theory of light. In 1862, using a refined method with Charles Wheatstone’s revolving mirror, he measured the speed of light as 298,000 km/s, a value only 0.6% less than the modern accepted value.

His optical innovations included the Foucault polarizer (1857) and the Foucault knife-edge test (1858). This test revolutionized telescope making by allowing opticians to precisely determine the shape of a mirror by measuring the focal lengths of its different zones, replacing previous trial-and-error methods.

### Later Work and Honours
In his later years, Foucault worked on regulating electric lights and modifying James Watt's centrifugal governor. He devised a method to safely view the sun by depositing a thin silver film on a telescope's object glass. He received numerous honours, including the Copley Medal (1855), membership in the Royal Society of London (1864), and having his name inscribed on the Eiffel Tower.

## Terms
- **Foucault pendulum**: A device consisting of a long, heavy pendulum that swings freely, whose plane of oscillation appears to rotate due to Earth's rotation, thus demonstrating that rotation.
- **Gyroscope**: A spinning wheel or disc whose axis of rotation is free to change in any direction; Foucault used it as a simpler proof of Earth's rotation.
- **Eddy currents (Foucault currents)**: Loops of electrical current induced within conductors by a changing magnetic field, causing energy loss as heat.
- **Foucault knife-edge test**: A method for testing the shape of a telescope mirror by focusing light onto its center of curvature and using a knife edge to reveal deviations from the desired form.
- **Foucault polarizer**: An optical device invented by Foucault to produce polarized light.
- **Diurnal motion**: The daily rotation of the Earth on its axis.
- **Corpuscular theory of light**: The theory, proposed by Newton, that light consists of a stream of particles. Foucault's experiment showing light slows in water helped disprove it.
- **Copley Medal**: A prestigious scientific award given by the Royal Society of London.

# Weak interaction

## Overview
The weak interaction is one of the four fundamental forces of nature, responsible for certain types of radioactive decay such as beta decay, and plays a crucial role in nuclear fission, fusion, and stellar nucleosynthesis. Unlike the other forces, it uniquely violates parity symmetry and can change the flavor of quarks and leptons. Its behavior is best described by electroweak theory, which unifies it with electromagnetism.

## Timeline
- **1933**: Enrico Fermi proposes the first theory of the weak interaction (Fermi's interaction), modeling beta decay as a four-fermion contact interaction.
- **Mid-1950s**: Chen-Ning Yang and Tsung-Dao Lee suggest that the weak interaction may violate parity symmetry.
- **1957**: Chien Shiung Wu confirms parity violation in the weak interaction via the Wu experiment.
- **1957**: Robert Marshak and George Sudarshan, and somewhat later, Richard Feynman and Murray Gell-Mann propose the V−A (vector minus axial vector or left-handed) theory of weak interactions.
- **1960s**: Sheldon Glashow, Abdus Salam, and Steven Weinberg unify electromagnetism and the weak interaction into electroweak theory.
- **1964**: James Cronin and Val Fitch observe CP violation in kaon decays, winning them the 1980 Nobel Prize in Physics.
- **1973**: Makoto Kobayashi and Toshihide Maskawa show that CP violation in the weak interaction required more than two generations of particles, effectively predicting the existence of a then unknown third generation.
- **1979**: Glashow, Salam, and Weinberg receive the Nobel Prize in Physics for electroweak theory.
- **1983**: Direct confirmation of W and Z bosons at CERN.
- **2008**: Kobayashi and Maskawa receive half of the Nobel Prize in Physics for their work on CP violation and three generations.
- **2012**: CMS and ATLAS at the LHC announce discovery of a Higgs-like boson (~125 GeV/c²).
- **2013**: Higgs boson tentatively confirmed.

## Body

### Properties and Mechanisms
The weak interaction operates only at subatomic distances—less than the diameter of a proton—and is mediated by massive W+, W−, and Z bosons. These bosons have masses around 90 GeV/c², making the weak force short-range and relatively weak in strength, with a coupling constant between 10⁻⁷ and 10⁻⁶. The interaction affects all fermions of the Standard Model and the Higgs boson, but does not produce bound states. It is the only fundamental interaction that violates parity (P) and, more rarely, charge-parity (CP) symmetry.

### Flavor Change and Decay Processes
A defining feature of the weak interaction is its ability to change the flavor of quarks and leptons. In beta-minus decay, a down quark in a neutron emits a virtual W− boson and becomes an up quark, converting the neutron into a proton and emitting an electron and an electron antineutrino. Similarly, electron capture converts a proton into a neutron via absorption of an electron and emission of a neutrino. All mesons are unstable due to weak decay, and processes governed by the weak interaction typically occur much more slowly than those mediated by the strong or electromagnetic forces. Such decay makes radiocarbon dating possible, as carbon-14 decays through the weak interaction to nitrogen-14.

### Weak Isospin and Hypercharge
Particles possess a quantum number called weak isospin (T₃), which governs how they interact with W± bosons. Left-handed fermions have T₃ = ±½, while right-handed fermions have T₃ = 0. Weak isospin is conserved in interactions. Weak hypercharge (Y_W = 2(Q − T₃)) was introduced to support electroweak unification and serves as the generator of the U(1) component of the electroweak gauge group.

### Charged-Current and Neutral-Current Interactions
There are two types of weak interactions: charged-current (involving W± bosons) and neutral-current (involving Z⁰ bosons). Charged-current interactions involve flavor change and are strictly governed by chirality and weak isospin. Neutral-current interactions allow any fermion to interact via Z⁰ exchange without changing flavor, and are described using the weak charge (Q_w = 2T₃ − 4Q sin²θ_w), where θ_w ≈ 29° is the weak mixing angle.

### Electroweak Theory
Electromagnetic and weak interactions are unified in the Standard Model as aspects of a single electroweak force. This theory was developed around 1968 by Sheldon Glashow, Abdus Salam, and Steven Weinberg, and they were awarded the 1979 Nobel Prize in Physics for their work. At high energies, four massless gauge bosons exist; at low energies, spontaneous symmetry breaking via the Higgs mechanism gives mass to three of them (W±, Z⁰), leaving the photon massless. The electroweak force is believed to have separated into the electromagnetic and weak forces during the quark epoch of the early universe. This framework successfully predicted the masses of the W and Z bosons before their 1983 discovery.

### Symmetry Violation
The weak interaction uniquely violates parity symmetry, as demonstrated by the Wu experiment. Yang and Lee earned the 1957 Nobel Prize in Physics for their suggestion of parity violation. The V−A theory explains this maximal violation by showing that the weak force acts only on left-handed particles and right-handed antiparticles. Later, CP violation was observed in kaon decays, leading to predictions of a third particle generation. CP violation is thought to contribute to the matter-antimatter asymmetry in the universe, satisfying one of Sakharov's conditions for baryogenesis.

## Terms
- **Beta decay**: A radioactive process where a neutron converts into a proton, emitting an electron and antineutrino via the weak interaction.
- **Boson**: An integer-spin force-carrying particle; W± and Z⁰ mediate the weak force.
- **Chirality**: A property describing whether a particle’s spin aligns with its momentum (left-handed vs. right-handed).
- **Flavor**: A classification of quarks (up, down, charm, strange, top, bottom) and leptons, changeable via the weak interaction.
- **Higgs mechanism**: The process by which gauge bosons acquire mass through interaction with the Higgs field.
- **Parity (P)**: Symmetry under mirror reflection; violated by the weak interaction.
- **CP violation**: Violation of combined charge conjugation and parity symmetry; observed in weak decays.
- **Weak isospin (T₃)**: A quantum number determining how particles couple to W± bosons.
- **Weak hypercharge (Y_W)**: A quantum number related to electric charge and weak isospin, used in electroweak unification.
- **CKM matrix**: A unitary matrix describing probabilities of quark flavor transitions in weak interactions.

## Debates and Open Questions
- Whether the weak interaction remains confining if the electroweak symmetry breaking scale were lowered, as suggested by alternative SU(2) models.
- The exact nature of CP violation and its sufficiency to explain the observed matter-antimatter imbalance in the universe.
- The degree to which the 2012 Higgs-like boson matches the Standard Model Higgs, requiring further data for definitive identification.

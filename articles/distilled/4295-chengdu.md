# Chengdu

## Overview
Chengdu is the capital of Sichuan province and the traditional hub of Western China. It is a major economic, cultural, and transportation center with a history spanning over 2,000 years, making it one of the few Chinese cities to have maintained its name and location continuously since its founding. The city is globally recognized as a "City of Gastronomy" and is closely associated with the giant panda.

## Timeline
- **c. 18th–10th century BC** — The Sanxingdui and Jinsha cultures inhabit the Chengdu Plain.
- **316 BC** — The Qin state conquers the ancient Shu Kingdom.
- **311 BC** — Qin general Zhang Yi builds the city walls of Chengdu.
- **256 BC** — Li Bing constructs the Dujiangyan Irrigation System.
- **220–280 AD** — Chengdu serves as the capital of Liu Bei's Shu Han state during the Three Kingdoms period.
- **1023 AD** — The world's first paper currency, "Jiaozi," is issued in Chengdu during the Song dynasty.
- **1937–1945** — During World War II, Chengdu becomes a major rear base for the Kuomintang government.
- **1949** — The People's Liberation Army takes Chengdu, marking the last major KMT holdout on the mainland.
- **2010** — The Chengdu Metro system officially opens.
- **2021** — Chengdu Tianfu International Airport opens, making Chengdu the third Chinese city with two international airports.
- **2023** — Chengdu hosts the Summer World University Games.

## Body

### History and Identity
Founded by the Kingdom of Shu in the 4th century BC, Chengdu was re-founded by the Qin general Zhang Yi in 311 BC. The city's name, meaning "become a metropolis," has remained unchanged for over two millennia. It served as the capital for several regional kingdoms, most notably the Shu Han during the Three Kingdoms period. The Dujiangyan Irrigation System, built in 256 BC, transformed the Chengdu Plain into the fertile "Land of Abundance." During World War II, the city was a critical base for the Nationalist government and the site of early Allied air operations against Japan. After 1949, its role as a link between Eastern and Western China expanded with new railways and defense industries.

### Economy and Industry
Chengdu is a leading economic, financial, and commercial center in China. Its economy is diverse, with major industries in machinery, automobiles, IT, and food processing. The city is a significant financial hub, ranking 35th globally in 2021, and hosts over 315 Fortune 500 company branches. It is a national center for electronics and IT, with major facilities for Intel, IBM, and others. The automobile industry is comprehensive, with manufacturers like Volvo and Volkswagen. Chengdu is also a key logistics and transportation hub, with two international airports and a major railway network, including the first China-Europe express cargo train service.

### Culture and Society
Chengdu's culture is deeply rooted in Sichuanese traditions. It is UNESCO-designated as a "City of Gastronomy," famous for its spicy cuisine, hot pot, and vibrant teahouse culture. The city is the historic home of literary giants like Du Fu and Li Bai. Sichuan opera, known for its face-changing performances, is a hallmark. Mahjong is a popular social pastime. The city is also a center for scientific research and higher education, home to over 58 universities, including Sichuan University. Chengdu is closely associated with the giant panda, hosting the Chengdu Research Base of Giant Panda Breeding.

### Geography and Climate
Chengdu is located on the Chengdu Plain in central Sichuan, with an elevation ranging from 450 to 720 meters. It is bordered by mountains, including the Longmen and Qionglai ranges. The Jin River flows through the city. The climate is a monsoon-influenced humid subtropical (Köppen Cwa), with four distinct seasons, warm temperatures, and high humidity. It has one of the lowest annual sunshine totals in China.

## Terms
- **Shu Han**: The state in southwestern China during the Three Kingdoms period (220–280), with Chengdu as its capital.
- **Dujiangyan**: An ancient irrigation system built in 256 BC that made the Chengdu Plain fertile.
- **Land of Abundance**: A historical nickname for the Chengdu Plain, referring to its agricultural fertility.
- **City of Gastronomy**: A UNESCO designation recognizing Chengdu's culinary culture.
- **Sichuanese**: The local dialect and broader linguistic group of Sichuan province.
- **Nong Jia Le**: A rural tourism model originating in Chengdu, where rural homes are converted into restaurants and hotels.
- **Golden Sun Bird**: An ancient gold artifact from the Jinsha Site, now a symbol of Chinese cultural heritage.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The etymology of the name "Chengdu" is unclear. The most widely known explanation, from a 10th-century text, links it to a Zhou dynasty saying, but this is not generally accepted by modern scholars.
- The historical population of Chengdu during the Mongol conquest is debated; sources suggest the city proper had fewer than 30,000 residents at the time, despite a call for a larger massacre.

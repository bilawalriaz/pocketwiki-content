# Louis Philippe I

## Overview
Louis Philippe I (1773–1850) was the last King of the French, ruling from 1830 to 1848. His reign, known as the July Monarchy, represented a constitutional monarchy supported by the wealthy bourgeoisie. He was the only French monarch from the Orléans branch of the Bourbon family and his rule ended with the French Revolution of 1848, which established the Second Republic.

## Timeline
- **6 Oct 1773** — Born in Paris to the Duke of Chartres (future Duke of Orléans).
- **1792–1793** — Serves with distinction in the French Revolutionary Wars; promoted to lieutenant general.
- **4 Apr 1793** — Flees France with General Dumouriez after a failed plot, beginning 21 years of exile.
- **1808–1814** — Serves as a British agent in Sicily during the Napoleonic Wars.
- **15 Apr 1809** — Marries Princess Maria Amalia of Naples and Sicily.
- **9 Aug 1830** — Proclaimed King of the French after the July Revolution forces Charles X to abdicate.
- **28 Jul 1835** — Survives an assassination attempt by Giuseppe Fieschi using a "machine infernale."
- **24 Feb 1848** — Abdicates during the February Revolution and flees to England.
- **26 Aug 1850** — Dies in exile at Claremont, Surrey, England.

## Body

### Early Life and Revolutionary Service
Louis Philippe was a Prince of the Blood from the cadet Orléans branch of the House of Bourbon. Educated by the Countess of Genlis, he absorbed liberal Enlightenment ideas. During the French Revolution, he initially supported the cause, joining the Jacobin Club. He served as a colonel and then a general in the Revolutionary Wars, fighting at battles like Valmy and Jemappes. His military career ended when he was implicated in General Dumouriez's 1793 plot to restore the monarchy, forcing him to flee France.

### Exile and Return
Louis Philippe spent 21 years in exile, living under assumed names and working as a teacher in Switzerland and England. He traveled extensively, including to the United States from c. 1796 to 1798, an experience he later said influenced his political beliefs. He served as a British agent in Sicily during the Napoleonic Wars. After Napoleon's defeat, he returned to France during the Bourbon Restoration (1815). Though reconciled with the restored monarchy, he openly sided with the liberal opposition, creating friction with his cousin King Charles X.

### The July Monarchy (1830–1848)
In 1830, the July Revolution overthrew Charles X. Louis Philippe was proclaimed "King of the the French," a title linking the monarchy to the people rather than a territory. His regime, dominated by wealthy industrialists and bankers, was initially popular but grew conservative, especially under statesman François Guizot. His government promoted friendship with the United Kingdom and colonial expansion, notably the conquest of Algeria. Despite an unpretentious style, his rule faced opposition from Legitimists, Republicans, and the working class, whose conditions deteriorated. He survived seven assassination attempts, including a major one in 1835.

### Abdication and Legacy
Economic depression in 1847 fueled discontent, leading to the February Revolution of 1848. Louis Philippe abdicated in favor of his grandson and fled to England, where he died in 1850. His supporters were known as Orléanists. His reign is remembered for its bourgeois character and for being the last French monarchy before the establishment of the Second Republic.

## Terms
- **Citizen King**: Nickname for Louis Philippe, emphasizing his connection to the people and liberal ideals.
- **July Monarchy**: The period of Louis Philippe's reign (1830–1848), established by the July Revolution.
- **Orléanists**: Supporters of Louis Philippe and the Orléans branch of the Bourbon family.
- **Legitimists**: Supporters of the senior, main line of the House of Bourbon, which was displaced by Louis Philippe.
- **Prince of the Blood**: A title for a prince of royal blood, entitled to the style "Serene Highness."
- **Machine infernale**: A multi-barreled volley gun used in the 1835 assassination attempt against Louis Philippe.
- **King of the French**: Title adopted by Louis Philippe, signifying a monarch whose legitimacy derives from the people, not divine right.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The extent to which Louis Philippe's early liberal, revolutionary experiences genuinely shaped his later conservative rule versus the influence of his bourgeois supporters.
- The degree to which his personal popularity and "Citizen King" image masked the regime's increasing repression and class-based policies.
- The role of the 1846 economic depression versus long-term political discontent in causing the 1848 Revolution that ended his reign.

# Egyptian Museum

## Overview
The Museum of Egyptian Antiquities in Cairo is the world's largest collection of Egyptian artifacts, housing over 170,000 items. Established in 1902, it is a foundational institution for Egyptology and a major cultural landmark, though its collections are now being partially transferred to newer museums.

## Timeline
- **1835** — Egyptian government establishes first museum near Ezbekieh Garden.
- **1858** — New museum founded at Boulaq under Auguste Mariette.
- **1889** — Collection moves to a palace in Giza.
- **1902** — Current museum building on Tahrir Square officially opens.
- **1954** — Museum gardens seized for Cairo Municipality Building.
- **1983** — Museum building registered as a heritage site.
- **2004** — Wafaa El Saddik becomes first female director general.
- **2011** — Museum broken into during Egyptian Revolution; artifacts damaged and stolen.
- **2016** — Major renovation project completed.
- **2021** — 22 royal mummies transferred to National Museum of Egyptian Civilization.

## Body

### History and Construction
The museum's origins trace to an 1835 government collection, which was later donated to Austria in 1855. A more permanent museum was established in 1858 at Boulaq by Egyptologist Auguste Mariette. After overcrowding and flood damage, the collection moved to a Giza palace in 1889. The current neoclassical building on Tahrir Square was designed by French architect Marcel Dourgnon, with construction completed by an Italian firm. It officially opened on 15 November 1902, with artifacts arranged chronologically. The museum has undergone several renovations, most recently a major project from 2006 to 2016 that restored its facade and updated displays.

### Collections and Layout
The museum's two main floors are organized thematically and chronologically. The ground floor displays large stone statues, reliefs, and architectural elements from the pre-dynastic to Greco-Roman periods in 42 rooms. The first floor houses smaller items like papyri, coins, textiles, and wooden sarcophagi, including major collections from the New Kingdom (1550–1069 BC). Key holdings include the treasures of Tutankhamun, the Narmer Palette, and the Royal Mummy Collection (transferred in 2021). The museum also contains a library with over 50,000 volumes, including rare archaeological texts.

### Modern Challenges and Developments
The museum has faced security issues, including a 2011 break-in during the Egyptian Revolution where 54 objects were stolen and others damaged. A 2025 theft involved a 3,000-year-old gold bracelet. To address overcrowding, major collections, including all of Tutankhamun's artifacts, are being transferred to the Grand Egyptian Museum in Giza. The museum is overseen by the Ministry of Tourism and Antiquities and has had numerous directors, including prominent Egyptologists like Gaston Maspero.

## Terms
- **Egyptology**: The study of ancient Egyptian history, language, literature, religion, architecture, and art from the 5th millennium BC until the end of its native religious practices in the 4th century AD.
- **Antiquities**: Objects surviving from ancient times, especially those of historical or archaeological interest.
- **Papyrus**: A material prepared from ancient plant material used in writing, painting, or making objects.
- **Sarcophagus**: A stone coffin, especially one adorned with a sculpture or inscription and associated with the ancient civilizations of Egypt, Rome, and Greece.
- **Shabti (Ushabti)**: A small figurine placed in tombs in ancient Egypt to serve as a substitute for the deceased if called upon to perform manual labor in the afterlife.
- **Stele**: An upright stone slab or pillar typically bearing a commemorative inscription or relief design.
- **Narmer Palette**: A significant Egyptian archaeological find, dating from about the 31st century BC, containing some of the earliest hieroglyphic inscriptions ever found.
- **Royal Mummy Parade**: The 2021 ceremonial transfer of 22 royal mummies from the Egyptian Museum to the National Museum of Egyptian Civilization.

## Debates and Open Questions
The source notes that the museum's original chronological display arrangement allotted "no rooms to intermediate periods, which were considered historically insignificant," reflecting a past scholarly bias. The ongoing transfer of collections to the Grand Egyptian Museum raises questions about the future role and identity of the historic Tahrir Square building.

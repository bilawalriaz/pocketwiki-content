# Vientiane

## Overview
Vientiane is the capital and largest city of Laos, situated on the Mekong River near the Thai border. As the nation's administrative, cultural, and economic center, it has been the capital since the 16th century and is home to major Buddhist monuments like Pha That Luang. The city's history reflects a transition from an early Mon city-state to a royal capital, a French colonial administrative center, and finally the seat of the modern Lao state.

## Timeline
- **8th century CE** — The Mon city-state of Chanthaburi (Vientiane) is established.
- **1171 CE** — Vientiane is captured by the Chueang brothers following a failed invasion of the Ngoenyang Kingdom.
- **1354** — Vientiane becomes an administrative city in the newly founded Lan Xang kingdom.
- **1563** — King Setthathirath officially establishes Vientiane as the capital of Lan Xang.
- **1566** — Pha That Luang, the national stupa, is originally built.
- **1943** — During French rule, 53% of Vientiane's population is Vietnamese.
- **1946** — French paratroopers reoccupy the city after Japanese occupation in WWII.
- **1960** — During the Laotian Civil War, Kong Le seizes the capital.
- **1975** — Pathet Lao forces symbolically liberate Vientiane; the monarchy is abolished.
- **2021** — The Boten–Vientiane (China–Laos) railway officially opens.
- **2026** — The city's Bus Rapid Transit (BRT) system begins free trial operations.

## Body

### Early History and Etymology
The name "Vientiane" derives from the Lao *Viangchan*. *Viang* means 'walled city', while *chan* has two possible Sanskrit-derived meanings: *candana* ('sandalwood') or *chandra* ('moon'), leading to interpretations like 'walled city of sandalwood' or 'walled city of the moon'. By the 8th century, Mon peoples had established city-states in the region, including Chanthaburi (Vientiane). These states were culturally similar, introduced Theravada Buddhism, and were loosely bound politically. A 12th-century Vietnamese inscription references Vientiane as a Khmer vassal state.

### Royal Capital and Colonial Era
Vientiane became part of the Lan Xang kingdom in 1354 and was made its official capital in 1563 by King Setthathirath to evade Burmese threats. During French colonial rule, the city served as the administrative capital. The French encouraged Vietnamese migration, which by 1943 resulted in Vietnamese people comprising 53% of the city's population. A French plan to settle more Vietnamese in key Lao regions was interrupted by World War II, during which Vientiane was occupied by Japan before being reoccupied by France in 1946.

### Modern History and Independence
The Laotian Civil War (1959–1975) saw Vientiane change hands between factions. In 1960, paratrooper Kong Le seized the city to demand a neutralist government. The war concluded in 1975 when Pathet Lao troops entered the city, leading to the abolition of the monarchy. Today, Vientiane is the center of Laos' national media, hosting Lao National Television and Lao National Radio.

### Economy and Infrastructure
Vientiane's economy has grown through foreign investment, with the stock exchange opening in 2011. By 2025, the service sector's share of the economy had increased. Transportation infrastructure includes Wattay International Airport, a metre-gauge railway link to Thailand (2009), and the standard-gauge Boten–Vientiane railway to China (2021). A new BRT system opened for trial in 2026, with plans for expansion to the airport and railway station to promote electric vehicle use.

### Tourism and Culture
The city's main attraction is Pha That Luang, a 45-metre golden stupa built in 1566 and restored in 1953, believed to contain a Buddha relic. Other key sites include Wat Si Muang (1563), built on Khmer ruins; the Patuxai memorial monument (1957-1968); and Buddha Park (1958), a sculpture garden 28 km south of the city. The National University of Laos is located in Vientiane.

## Terms
- **Stupa**: A dome-shaped Buddhist monument, often containing relics.
- **Theravada Buddhism**: The oldest surviving branch of Buddhism, predominant in Laos.
- **Lan Xang**: The historical kingdom of Laos, founded in 1354.
- **Pathet Lao**: The communist political movement and army that came to power in 1975.
- **Metre gauge**: A railway track width of 1,000 mm, common in Southeast Asia.
- **Standard gauge**: The international railway track width of 1,435 mm.
- **Bus Rapid Transit (BRT)**: A high-capacity bus-based public transport system.

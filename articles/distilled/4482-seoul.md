# Seoul

## Overview
Seoul is the capital and largest city of South Korea, serving as the nation's political, economic, and cultural hub. Its history spans over two millennia, from its founding in 18 BC to its current status as a global megacity and the core of the world's sixth-largest metropolitan economy. The city's development reflects Korea's broader historical trajectory, including periods of dynastic rule, foreign occupation, devastating war, and rapid modernization.

## Timeline
- **18 BC** — Founded as Wiryeseong, capital of Baekje.
- **1394** — Designated as the planned capital, Hanyang, of the Joseon dynasty.
- **1910** — Annexed by Japan and renamed Gyeongseong (Keijō).
- **1945** — Liberated from Japanese rule; U.S. military government establishes Seoul as a municipal corporation.
- **1950-51** — Changes hands four times during the Korean War, leaving it in ruins.
- **1963** — Major territorial expansions more than double the city's size.
- **1988** — Hosts the Summer Olympic Games.
- **2004** — Constitutional Court rules Seoul must remain the national capital.
- **2012** — Sejong City opens as the de facto administrative capital.

## Body

### History and Name
Seoul's history begins with human habitation from 30,000-40,000 years ago. It was founded in 18 BC as Wiryeseong, the capital of the Baekje kingdom. The city's name, *Seoul*, is a native Korean word meaning "capital city," believed to derive from *Seorabeol*. It has had many historical names, including Hanyang and Hanseong. During the Joseon dynasty (1392-1897), it was planned as the capital, Hanyang, and enclosed by a Fortress Wall. Japan renamed it Gyeongseong (Keijō) during its 1910-1945 annexation. After liberation, "Seoul" became the official name. The city was devastated during the Korean War but was rebuilt and expanded significantly in the 1960s. While Sejong City has become the de facto administrative capital since 2012, a 2004 Constitutional Court ruling mandates Seoul remain the legal capital.

### Geography and Climate
Seoul is located in northwest South Korea, bisected by the Han River. The city proper covers 605.25 km² and is surrounded by mountains, including Bukhansan to the north. It has a humid continental or subtropical climate with hot, humid summers influenced by monsoons and cold, dry winters. Air pollution is a significant environmental challenge, with particulate matter (PM2.5 and PM10) levels often exceeding WHO guidelines, though government policies have led to improvements.

### Government and Demographics
The Seoul Metropolitan Government administers the city, which is divided into 25 districts (*gu*). Seoul is extremely densely populated, though its population has been declining since 2014 due to high costs, aging, and an extremely low fertility rate (0.55 in 2023). Major religions are Christianity and Buddhism, with over half the population irreligious. The city is home to South Korea's most prestigious universities, including Seoul National, Korea, and Yonsei (the "SKY" universities).

### Economy and Technology
Seoul is South Korea's business and financial hub, generating 23% of the national GDP in 2012. It hosts headquarters for global conglomerates like Samsung, LG, and Hyundai. The city is a major technology center, described as the world's "most wired city," with the highest fiber-optic broadband penetration and leading internet speeds. It has three main business districts: Downtown Seoul, Gangnam, and Yeouido.

### Culture and Transportation
Seoul's culture blends historical and modern elements. It has five UNESCO World Heritage Sites, including Joseon dynasty palaces and royal tombs, alongside modern landmarks like the Lotte World Tower. The city is a global hub for K-pop and the Korean Wave. Its transportation network is extensive, featuring one of the world's busiest subway systems, high-speed KTX rail, and two international airports: Incheon (a major global hub) and Gimpo.

## Terms
- **Seoul Special Metropolitan City:** The official administrative designation for Seoul, granting it a status separate from provinces.
- **Hanseong:** The name for Seoul during the Joseon dynasty, meaning "Han Fortress."
- **Gyeongseong (Keijō):** The name given to Seoul during Japanese colonial rule (1910-1945).
- **Fortress Wall of Seoul:** A wall built in the early Joseon dynasty to surround and protect the capital, Hanyang.
- **Han River:** The major river bisecting Seoul, historically significant for settlement and defense.
- **Gu (구):** The administrative districts into which Seoul is divided.
- **KTX:** South Korea's high-speed rail system connecting major cities.
- **Incheon International Airport:** Seoul's primary international airport, one of the busiest in the world.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Capital Status:** While the Constitutional Court ruled Seoul must remain the legal capital, the gradual relocation of government ministries to Sejong City has created a de facto administrative capital, raising questions about the long-term political and economic implications for Seoul.
- **Air Pollution Sources:** The relative contributions to Seoul's air pollution from domestic sources (traffic, industry) versus transboundary pollution from China and Asian dust storms are a subject of ongoing analysis and policy focus.
- **Population Decline:** The causes and consequences of Seoul's shrinking population and ultra-low fertility rate are critical demographic challenges for the city's future.

# Pierre-Simon Laplace

## Overview
Pierre-Simon Laplace (1749–1827) was a French polymath who fundamentally shaped celestial mechanics, probability theory, and mathematical physics. He summarized and extended Newton's gravitational theory, demonstrating the Solar System's long-term stability without divine intervention, and developed the Bayesian interpretation of probability. His analytical methods and concepts, such as potential theory and the Laplace transform, remain foundational in science and mathematics.

- **1814** — Publishes *Essai philosophique sur les probabilités*, articulating Laplace's demon.
- **5 March 1827** — Dies in Paris.

## Body

### Early Life and Career
Born to a comfortable family in Normandy, Laplace initially studied theology at the University of Caen before his mathematical talent was recognized. He moved to Paris in 1771, where d'Alembert helped him secure a position at the École Militaire. This stable income allowed him to focus on research, leading to his election to the Academy of Sciences in 1773. He later collaborated with chemist Antoine Lavoisier on heat experiments (1780–1784).

### Celestial Mechanics and Solar System Stability
Laplace's major work aimed to prove the Solar System's mechanical stability, countering Newton's suggestion that divine intervention was necessary. He tackled the "great inequality" of Jupiter and Saturn, where their orbits appeared to exchange energy. By analyzing higher-order gravitational perturbations, he showed this was a periodic effect due to the near-commensurability of their orbital periods (two of Jupiter's orbits nearly equal five of Saturn's). His *Mécanique céleste* translated Newton's geometric mechanics into calculus, providing a comprehensive analytical framework. While his methods were not precise enough to prove absolute stability (now known to be chaotic on fine scales), they represented a monumental advance.

### Mathematical Physics and Potential Theory
Laplace pioneered the use of scalar potential in gravitational theory, showing it satisfies Laplace's equation (∇²V = 0). He extended Legendre's associated Legendre functions into three dimensions, creating spherical harmonics (or Laplace coefficients), essential for solving Laplace's equation in spherical coordinates. He also formulated Laplace's tidal equations, which describe ocean tides as a barotropic fluid flow, incorporating Earth's rotation and gravity. These equations form the basis of modern dynamic tidal theory.

### Probability and Statistics
In his *Théorie analytique des probabilités* (1812), Laplace systematized probability theory. He developed the Bayesian interpretation, where probability represents degrees of belief updated by evidence. His "rule of succession" estimates the probability of a future event based on past successes. He also proved an early version of the central limit theorem and provided a Bayesian justification for the method of least squares, showing it minimizes expected error for large samples.

### Other Scientific Contributions
Laplace refined Newton's calculation of the speed of sound by accounting for adiabatic compression. He supported the corpuscular theory of light and, using it, suggested that sufficiently massive stars could have gravity so strong that light could not escape—an early concept of a black hole. He also contributed to the theory of capillary action (Young–Laplace equation) and pure mathematics, including work on determinants and integral approximation methods.

### Politics and Personal Life
Laplace was briefly Napoleon's Minister of the Interior in 1799 but was replaced after six weeks. He later aligned with the Bourbon Restoration, receiving the title of marquis. His political philosophy favored gradual, evolutionary change over revolution, drawing analogies from physical stability. He married in 1788 and had two children.

## Terms
- **Celestial Mechanics**: The study of the motions of celestial bodies using physics and mathematics.
- **Nebular Hypothesis**: The theory that the Solar System formed from the gravitational collapse of a rotating cloud of gas and dust.
- **Potential Theory**: A branch of mathematics dealing with scalar potentials (like gravitational potential) that satisfy Laplace's or Poisson's equation.
- **Spherical Harmonics**: A set of functions used to solve partial differential equations on the surface of a sphere, crucial in gravitational and electromagnetic theory.
- **Laplace Transform**: An integral transform that converts functions of time into functions of a complex variable, widely used in solving differential equations.
- **Bayesian Probability**: An interpretation of probability as a measure of belief or confidence, updated as new evidence is obtained.
- **Rule of Succession**: A formula for estimating the probability of a future event based on the number of past successes and trials.
- **Central Limit Theorem**: A statistical theorem stating that the distribution of sample means approximates a normal distribution, regardless of the original distribution, for large sample sizes.
- **Method of Least Squares**: A standard approach in regression analysis to approximate the solution of overdetermined systems by minimizing the sum of the squares of the residuals.
- **Laplace's Demon**: A thought experiment illustrating causal determinism: an intellect that knows all forces and positions could predict the entire future of the universe.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Stability of the Solar System**: Laplace claimed to prove its long-term stability, but modern chaos theory shows it is stable only on coarse scales, with fine-scale motions being chaotic.
- **"I had no need of that hypothesis"**: The famous retort to Napoleon is debated. Some historians, like Hervé Faye, argue Laplace referred not to God's existence but to Newton's invocation of divine intervention to maintain orbital stability.
- **Laplace's Religious Views**: Contemporaries and scholars disagree on whether Laplace was an atheist, deist, or agnostic. His private writings show deistic leanings, but he publicly rejected supernatural explanations in science.
- **Attribution of Ideas**: Laplace has been criticized for insufficiently acknowledging predecessors, such as Legendre in spherical harmonics and Lagrange in potential theory. His *Mécanique céleste* is described as synthesizing a century of work, sometimes presented as his own discoveries.

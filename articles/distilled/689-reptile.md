# Reptile

## Overview
Reptiles are ectothermic, amniotic tetrapod vertebrates traditionally classified into four orders: Testudines (turtles), Crocodilia (crocodilians), Squamata (lizards and snakes), and Rhynchocephalia (tuatara), comprising roughly 12,000 extant species. Classical taxonomy defines Reptilia as a class excluding birds and mammals, but modern cladistics demonstrates this group is paraphyletic because birds (Aves) are nested within Archosauria, making them more closely related to crocodilians than to other living reptiles. Consequently, many phylogenetic systems redefine Reptilia as a monophyletic clade (Sauropsida) that includes birds. Reptiles originated from advanced reptiliomorphs in the late Carboniferous (~310–320 Ma), diverging into the two major lineages Archosauromorpha (crocodilians, birds, kin) and Lepidosauromorpha (lizards, kin) during the Permian. They dominated Mesozoic ecosystems ("Age of Reptiles") until the Cretaceous–Paleogene extinction eliminated non-avian dinosaurs, pterosaurs, and many marine reptiles, leaving squamates, crocodilians, turtles, and birds as the primary survivors.

## Timeline
- **~315 Ma** — Oldest reptile-like footprints (Nova Scotia); *Hylonomus* appears as early candidate for oldest known reptile.
- **~310–320 Ma** — Origin of reptiles from advanced reptiliomorphs in late Carboniferous swamps.
- **Carboniferous Rainforest Collapse** — Amniotes (stem-reptiles) survive better than amphibians due to shelled eggs; acquire herbivory/carnivory; become dominant.
- **Permian Period** — Divergence of Archosauromorpha and Lepidosauromorpha; rise of synapsid megafauna (pelycosaurs, therapsids) and stem-reptiles (pareiasaurs, *Mesosaurus*); first true diapsids (Neodiapsida) and gliding Weigeltisauridae appear.
- **252 Ma (Permian–Triassic Extinction)** — Largest mass extinction; parareptile/synapsid megafauna replaced by archosauromorphs.
- **Triassic Period** — Archosaurs become dominant; evolve into dinosaurs, pterosaurs, and crocodilian ancestors.
- **Jurassic–Cretaceous** — Dinosaurs dominate; feathered theropods give rise to first true birds (Cretaceous); mosasaurs (lepidosauromorphs) dominate seas.
- **66 Ma (Cretaceous–Paleogene Extinction)** — Non-avian dinosaurs, pterosaurs, plesiosaurs, mosasaurs wiped out; only birds, crocodilians, turtles, and choristoderes survive among large reptiles.
- **Cenozoic** — Mammal/bird diversification; squamates recover after 10 Myr and radiate (>95% of living non-avian reptiles); giant tortoises remain megafauna.

## Body

### Classification History and Modern Phylogenetics
Classical taxonomy grouped reptiles and amphibians together (Linnaeus, 18th century) until P.A. Latreille (1825) separated Batracia (amphibians). T.H. Huxley and Richard Owen expanded Reptilia to include fossil "antediluvian monsters" (dinosaurs, synapsids). Huxley later proposed **Sauropsida** (lizards, birds, kin) and **Ichthyopsida** (fishes, amphibians) based on vertebrate groupings. E.S. Goodrich (1916) used Sauropsida/Theropsida distinguished by heart/vessel structure and forebrain. D.M.S. Watson (1956) redefined Sauropsida to exclude birds, including parareptiles, turtles, lepidosaurs, archosaurs, and marine reptiles. Henry Fairfield Osborn and Alfred Romer popularized a four-subclass system based on **temporal fenestrae** (skull openings behind eyes): Anapsida (none), Synapsida (one low), Euryapsida (one high), Diapsida (two). This system is now largely abandoned because the anapsid condition appears variably across unrelated groups.

Modern phylogenetic taxonomy requires monophyletic groups. Traditional Reptilia is paraphyletic because it excludes birds (descended from dinosaurs) and mammals (descended from therapsids). Jacques Gauthier (1988) defined Reptilia as a crown clade including turtles, lepidosaurs, crocodilians, birds, their common ancestor, and all descendants. Modesto and Anderson (2004) proposed a stem-based definition: all amniotes closer to *Lacerta agilis* and *Crocodylus niloticus* than to *Homo sapiens*, effectively synonymizing Reptilia with Sauropsida and including birds. Current consensus (Simões et al. 2022; Jenkins et al. 2026) places turtles within Diapsida, typically as sister to Archosauria (Archelosauria) or nested within Archosauromorpha, contradicting the classical anapsid placement.

### Evolutionary History
**Origin (~310–320 Ma):** First reptiles evolved from advanced reptiliomorphs in Carboniferous swamps. *Casineria* (possible amniote), *Hylonomus* (small, insectivorous, 20–30 cm), *Westlothiana* (reptiliomorph), and *Paleothyris* are early forms. Unambiguous Carboniferous reptiles include *Erpetonyx* and *Carbonodraco*.
**Carboniferous Rainforest Collapse:** Primitive tetrapods (amphibian-like) devastated by drying climate; amniotes, with shelled eggs allowing terrestrial reproduction, radiated rapidly into herbivory and carnivory, establishing reptile dominance ("First Age of Reptiles").
**Skull Evolution:** Ancestral amniote skull condition (anapsid vs. synapsid) is uncertain due to fenestrae in Parareptilia. Synapsida (mammal line) split early with one temporal opening. Diapsida (two openings) evolved later, lightening skulls and strengthening bites. *Araeoscelidia* (late Carboniferous) are morphologically diapsid but may fall outside Neodiapsida.
**Permian:** Synapsids (pelycosaurs *Edaphosaurus*, *Dimetrodon*) became first terrestrial megafauna; replaced by therapsids in mid-Permian. Stem-reptiles flourished: herbivorous pareiasaurs (first large reptiles, >1,000 kg), aquatic *Mesosaurus* (Early Permian, Gondwana), first Neodiapsida (*Youngina*), gliding Weigeltisauridae (Late Permian), and archosauromorph *Protorosaurus* (Late Permian).
**Mesozoic (Age of Reptiles):** Permian–Triassic extinction cleared way for archosauromorphs (erect posture). Archosaurs dominated Triassic; diversified into dinosaurs, pterosaurs, crocodilian ancestors. Lepidosauromorphs produced marine mosasaurs (Cretaceous). Placement of ichthyosaurs and sauropterygians (plesiosaurs) remains controversial (lepidosauromorph vs. archosauromorph vs. basal diapsid). Cretaceous theropods yielded first birds.
**Cenozoic:** K–Pg extinction eliminated non-avian dinosaurs, pterosaurs, marine reptiles. Survivors: birds, crocodilians, turtles, choristoderes (extinct Miocene). Mammals/birds filled megafauna niches; squamates recovered after ~10 Myr and radiated to >95% of ~10,000 extant non-avian reptile species. Giant tortoises persisted as reptilian megafauna.

### Morphology and Physiology
**Circulation:** Lepidosaurs and turtles have a three-chambered heart (two atria, one variably partitioned ventricle, two aortas); shunting of oxygenated/deoxygenated blood varies by species/state, hypothesized to aid thermoregulation/diving. Some squamates (pythons, monitors) achieve functional four-chambered hearts via a muscular ridge subdividing the ventricle during systole, generating avian/mammalian pressure differentials. Crocodilians have an anatomical four-chambered heart with two systemic aortas, enabling pulmonary bypass (hepatic piston diaphragm). Turtle ventricles are imperfectly divided, allowing mixing.
**Metabolism:** Non-avian reptiles are ectothermic/poikilothermic/bradymetabolic; rely on external heat. Enzymes function over wide temperature ranges. Optimal range typically 24–35 °C (lizards); desert iguana *Dipsosaurus dorsalis* 35–40 °C. Low basal metabolism drops temperature rapidly during inactivity. **Gigantothermy** (low surface-to-volume ratio retaining metabolic heat) occurs in leatherback turtles and likely large dinosaurs. Low resting metabolism reduces food needs (crocodile needs 1/10–1/5 food of same-weight lion; can fast half a year), allowing dominance in low-calorie environments. Sustained high-energy output (chasing/flying) generally limited; sit-and-wait predation common.
**Respiration:** All use lungs. **Squamates:** Axial musculature ventilates lungs; constrains breathing during locomotion (breath-holding). Varanids use buccal pumping to supplement; tegus have proto-diaphragm. **Crocodilians:** Muscular "hepatic piston" diaphragm pulls movable pubis/liver down to expand lungs; unidirectional airflow (like birds). **Turtles:** Rigid shell prevents costal ventilation. Solutions vary: *Lissemys* uses muscle sheet enveloping lungs; limb retraction/protraction changes pressure; special muscles (diaphragm analogs) move viscera. Breathing during locomotion differs: green sea turtles hold breath; box turtles breathe continuously via abdominal muscles; red-eared sliders take smaller breaths during movement.
**Skin and Shedding:** Horny cornified epidermis (alpha/beta-keratin) watertight, thin vs. mammals. Scales vary: overlapping (snakes, skinks) to minimal (crocodilians, geckos). Large plate-like scales = **scutes** (turtle shells, crocodilian bodies). Digits end in cornified claws. **Shedding (ecdysis):** Lepidosaurs shed large pieces/entire skin (days/weeks); crocodilians/turtles shed small flakes continuously. Frequency increases with temperature/youth. New layer forms underneath; proteolytic enzymes/lymphatic fluid separate layers. Snakes shed head-to-tail; lizards patchy. **Dysecdysis** (failure) caused by low humidity/temperature, nutritional deficits (low enzymes), dehydration (low lymph), trauma (scars).
**Excretion:** Two small kidneys. Diapsids excrete **uric acid** (nitrogenous waste); turtles excrete **urea** (like mammals). Reptile kidneys lack **loop of Henle**, cannot produce urine more concentrated than body fluid; colon/bladder reabsorb water. Nasal/lingual salt glands excrete excess salts. Urinogenital ducts and rectum empty into **cloaca**. Urinary bladder present in turtles, most lizards; absent in snakes, crocodilians, monitor/legless lizards. Desert species (Galapagos tortoise bladder holds 20% body weight) store water long-term. Turtles have bilobed bladder + accessory bladders.
**Digestion:** Mostly carnivorous/insectivorous; short, simple tracts. Digestion slower than mammals (low metabolism, no mastication). Large reptiles survive months on single meal. Herbivory evolved in Paleozoic (pareiasaurs) and Mesozoic (dinosaurs); today only turtles are predominantly herbivorous, some agamas/iguanas partly. **Gastroliths** (swallowed rocks) grind plant matter in stomach (herbivores); used as ballast in crocodiles/plesiosaurs.
**Nerves:** Brain structure similar to amphibians but larger cerebrum/cerebellum. Encephalization quotient ~1/10 mammals. 12 cranial nerve pairs. Short cochlea uses electrical tuning for frequency range.
**Vision:** Mostly diurnal. Color vision, advanced depth perception. Birds/crocodiles/turtles: rods, single/double cones (UV vision). Lepidosaurs: lost duplex retina; single cone/rod-like class. Burrowers (blind snakes) reduced vision. **Parietal eye** (third eye) in many lepidosaurs: rudimentary retina/lens, detects light/dark/movement, not images. **Infrared pits** in pit vipers, boas, pythons detect body heat for nocturnal hunting. **Nictitating membrane** (translucent third eyelid) in most reptiles/birds; crocodilians use underwater. Geckos/snakes lack eyelids; have transparent **brille** (spectacle) shed with skin.
**Sound/Hearing:** Generally less vocal; hissing (air forced through glottis) common. True vocalization (vibrating laryngeal/glottal folds) in crocodilians, some lizards/turtles. Geckos/turtles have true vocal cords (elastin-rich). **Snakes:** Lack outer/middle ear/tympanum; inner ear cochleae connected to jawbone. Mechanoreceptors transmit ground vibrations via spinal nerves to brain; directional sensitivity to substrate-borne sound; airborne sensitivity unclear.
**Reproduction:** Sexual (some asexual). Cloacal copulation. Males: turtles/crocodilians = single median penis; squamates = paired **hemipenes** (one used per session); tuatara = no copulatory organs (cloacal apposition). Mostly **oviparous** (amniotic eggs: shell, chorion gas exchange, albumin water/protein, allantois waste, amnion osmoregulation, yolk sac nutrients, air space hatching oxygen). No larval stage. **Viviparity/ovoviviparity** in squamates (all boas, most vipers) and extinct marine clades (mesosaurs, mosasaurs, ichthyosaurs, sauropterygians). Degree varies: egg retention to placental analogs. **Parthenogenesis** (asexual diploid clones) in 6 lizard families + 1 snake (geckos, teiids *Aspidocelis*, lacertids *Lacerta*); captive Komodo dragons. **Temperature-dependent sex determination (TDSD)** in turtles, crocodiles, lizards, tuatara; unconfirmed in snakes.
**Longevity:** Giant tortoises >100 years. Genomic analysis (*Chelonoidis abingdonii*, *Aldabrachelys gigantea*) reveals DNA repair gene variants linked to lifespan.

### Cognition and Defense
**Cognition:** Historically underestimated due to small brain size/poor methods. Monitors show cooperation, foraging optimization. Crocodiles: complex social structure. Komodo dragons/turtles: play. Wood turtles outperformed rats in maze learning. Giant tortoises: operant conditioning, visual discrimination, long-term memory. Sea turtles: flipper use analogous to marine mammal foraging. Evidence for sentience (anxiety, pleasure).
**Defense:** Avoidance primary (fleeing to cover/water). **Camouflage:** Cryptic grays/greens/browns; motionless persistence. **Warning/Startle:** Blue-tongued skinks (tongue), frill-necked lizards (frill), hissing (crocodiles, turtles, snakes), rattlesnake tail vibration. **Aposematism:** *Heloderma* (Gila/beaded lizard), coral snakes (venomous); Batesian mimics (non-venomous snakes). **Snake tactics:** Hooding (cobras/hognose), venom (modified saliva via fangs), thanatosis (playing dead), foul musk. **Crocodilians:** Gaping, hissing, body inflation, head-smashing, chasing, high-force bite. **Caudal Autotomy:** Geckos, skinks shed tail (thrashes 20 min in leopard geckos) to distract; regenerates as cartilaginous, shorter, discolored rod. Some tails mimic head (shingleback).

### Relations with Humans
**Culture/Religion:** Dinosaurs iconic since Owen (1842); Crystal Palace (1854); Dickens *Bleak House* (1852). Snakes: Egyptian cobra (pharaoh, Cleopatra), Greek chthonic symbols (Hydra, Medusa, Gaia's children), Hindu worship (Shiva/Vishnu, Nag Panchami), Mesoamerican deities (Quetzalcoatl), Judeo-Christian temptation. Turtles: World Turtle cosmology, steadfastness/longevity.
**Medicine:** Snakebite: ~tens of thousands deaths/year (India); antivenom from hyperimmunized horse serum. Venom cytotoxins researched for cancer. Gila monster saliva: exenatide (Byetta, GLP-1 agonist for diabetes); toxin studied for Alzheimer's. Geckos/turtles: folk medicine (China), no proven efficacy; demand threatens wild turtles.
**Commercial Farming:** Crocodiles farmed for leather (hides), meat; saltwater/Nile species common. Farming incentivizes habitat conservation (Australia). Snakes farmed (East/Southeast Asia) for meat, skin, antivenom; high energy efficiency (ectothermy); fed poultry/pig waste. Risks: overexploitation of wild stock/prey. Turtle farming (food, medicine, pets, conservation) controversial: may increase wild demand/capture; disease introduction risk from releases.
**Captivity:** Herpetariums exhibit reptiles/amphibians. Pets: ball pythons, corn snakes, bearded dragons, iguanas, geckos (leopard, crested), turtles/tortoises. Challenges: UV light, temperature, diet, longevity (outliving owners), *Salmonella* risk (hygiene/hand-washing).

## Terms
- ****Amniote**** — Vertebrate whose embryo develops within a set of protective membranes (amnion, chorion, allantois) and typically a shell, enabling reproduction on dry land.
- ****Sauropsida**** — Clade comprising all amniotes more closely related to modern reptiles (and birds) than to mammals; often used synonymously with a monophyletic Reptilia.
- ****Temporal fenestrae**** — Openings in the skull roof behind the eye sockets; their number/position (none, one low, one high, two) historically classified reptile subclasses (Anapsida, Synapsida, Euryapsida, Diapsida).
- ****Diapsida**** — Major reptile clade characterized by two temporal fenestrae; includes lepidosaurs, archosaurs (crocodilians, birds, dinosaurs), and turtles (secondarily).
- ****Archosauromorpha**** — Clade of diapsids including crocodilians, birds, dinosaurs, pterosaurs, and their extinct relatives; sister group to Lepidosauromorpha.
- ****Lepidosauromorpha**** — Clade of diapsids including lizards, snakes, tuatara, and their extinct relatives (e.g., mosasaurs); sister group to Archosauromorpha.
- ****Ectothermy / Poikilothermy**** — Physiological reliance on external environmental heat sources to regulate body temperature; body temperature fluctuates with surroundings.
- ****Gigantothermy**** — Form of homeothermy in large-bodied ectotherms where low surface-area-to-volume ratio retains metabolic heat, stabilizing body temperature.
- ****Cloaca**** — Single posterior opening serving as the common exit for intestinal, urinary, and reproductive tracts.
- ****Viviparity / Ovoviviparity**** — Live birth: embryos develop inside mother (viviparity: placental nourishment; ovoviviparity: yolk-dependent eggs retained until hatching).
- ****Parthenogenesis**** — Asexual reproduction where an unfertilized egg develops into a viable offspring, producing a diploid clone of the mother.
- ****Temperature-dependent sex determination (TDSD)**** — Mechanism where incubation temperature of the egg determines the sex of the hatchling (common in turtles, crocodilians).
- ****Gastroliths**** — Stones swallowed and retained in the stomach to mechanically grind food (herbivores) or serve as ballast (aquatic reptiles).
- ****Autotomy**** — Voluntary shedding of a body part (typically the tail) as a defense mechanism; the detached part often distracts the predator.
- ****Brille (Spectacle)**** — Transparent, fused eyelid scale covering the eye in snakes and some geckos; shed along with the skin.
- ****Parietal eye**** — Photosensory organ on the skull roof of many lepidosaurs ("third eye"); detects light/dark changes and movement but cannot form images.

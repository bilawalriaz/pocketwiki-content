# Plácido Domingo

## Overview
Plácido Domingo (born 21 January 1941) is a Spanish tenor, conductor, and arts administrator widely considered one of the most versatile and prolific opera singers in history. Raised in his parents' zarzuela (Spanish light opera) company in Mexico, he transitioned from baritone to tenor in his teens, eventually mastering 151 roles across six languages. He dominated the lirico-spinto repertoire—most famously as Cavaradossi, Don José, and the definitive Otello of his generation—before shifting to Wagnerian heldentenor roles in the 1990s and, remarkably, transitioning to Verdi baritone parts (Simon Boccanegra, Rigoletto, Nabucco) in the 2010s. Beyond the stage, he achieved global celebrity through crossover hits ("Perhaps Love" with John Denver), The Three Tenors concerts (the best-selling classical album ever), and opera films with Franco Zeffirelli. He directed the Washington National Opera (1996–2011) and Los Angeles Opera (2003–2019), founded the Operalia competition for young singers, and led extensive humanitarian work. His career was marred in 2019 by credible sexual harassment allegations from multiple colleagues, leading to his resignation from LA Opera, though he denied wrongdoing and no charges were filed.

## Body

### Early Life and Zarzuela Roots
Born in Madrid’s Retiro district, Domingo moved to Mexico at age eight when his parents, established zarzuela stars, formed their own company after a Latin American tour. Immersed in theater from childhood—he could hum complex zarzuela scores at five—he studied piano at the National Conservatory of Music from age fourteen, taking conducting classes with Igor Markevitch and voice with Carlo Morelli (brother of baritone/tenor Renato Zanelli). This conservatory training constituted his only formal vocal instruction; he never had a private singing teacher. By sixteen he was working professionally: accompanying his mother, singing baritone roles in zarzuelas, and substituting as a tenor when needed. His early versatility included assistant conducting *My Fair Lady* (185 performances), playing piano for ballet and TV, arranging for a rock band, and acting in spoken theater.

### Operatic Career: Tenor Prime (1960s–1980s)
Domingo’s leading-role debut came in 1961 as Alfredo (*La traviata*, Monterrey). A pivotal Dallas Civic Opera debut followed opposite Joan Sutherland (*Lucia di Lammermoor*). After a formative 280-performance stint at the Israel National Opera (1962–65), he joined New York City Opera, debuting in *Madama Butterfly* (last-minute) and earning acclaim in the US premiere of Ginastera’s *Don Rodrigo* (1966, opening Lincoln Center). His 1968 Met debut—substituting for Franco Corelli in *Adriana Lecouvreur*—launched a 21-time season-opening record (surpassing Caruso). He quickly conquered Vienna, La Scala, San Francisco, Verona, Covent Garden, and Salzburg (1975 *Don Carlo* with Karajan).

Cavaradossi (*Tosca*) became his most-performed role; Otello, first sung in Hamburg (1975), became his signature (200+ performances, three studio recordings, four films). Laurence Olivier declared: "Domingo plays Othello as well as I do and he has that voice." He began conducting in 1973 (*La traviata*, NYCO) and increasingly led major houses. The 1980s brought mainstream fame: "Perhaps Love" (1981) with John Denver sold nearly four million copies; Zeffirelli’s opera films (*La Traviata*, *Carmen*, *Otello*) reached cinemas; *Newsweek* crowned him "King Of The Opera" (1982). A legendary 1983 rescue of San Francisco’s *Otello* opening night—flying from New York rehearsals for *Les Troyens* to sing at 10:30 PM—cemented his superstar status.

### Repertoire Evolution and Three Tenors (1990s–2010s)
The 1990s marked a deliberate repertoire shift. Domingo dropped early staples (Cavaradossi, Don Carlo, Don José, Hoffmann) and expanded into Wagner (Parsifal 1991, Siegmund 1992, Bayreuth), rare Verdi (*Stiffelio*, *La battaglia di Legnano*), French grand opera (*Hérodiade*, *Le prophète*), Russian (*The Queen of Spades*), Mozart (*Idomeneo* 1994), Baroque/Classical (*Iphigénie en Tauride*, *Tamerlano*), and contemporary premieres (Tan Dun’s *The First Emperor* 2006, Catán’s *Il Postino* 2010, *The Enchanted Island* 2011). He created eight world-premiere roles.

The Three Tenors phenomenon began 7 July 1990 in Rome (World Cup eve), conceived for José Carreras’s leukemia foundation. The recording became the best-selling classical album ever (3M+ US, multi-platinum worldwide); the 1994 Dodger Stadium concert drew 1.3 billion TV viewers. Subsequent World Cup concerts followed (1998 Paris, 2002 Yokohama). Domingo also sang at Olympics (Barcelona 1992, Beijing 2008) and Real Madrid centenary events.

### Baritone Transition and Administrative Leadership
In 2007 Domingo announced his 2009 baritone debut as Simon Boccanegra (Berlin), followed by 29 performances that season including Met and Covent Garden. He systematically added Verdi baritone roles: Rigoletto (2010 Beijing, 2011 Mantua TV film), Athanaël (*Thaïs* 2012), Francesco Foscari (*I due Foscari* 2012–14), Giorgio Germont (*La Traviata* 2013 Met), Conte di Luna (*Il trovatore* 2014 Berlin/Salzburg), Nabucco (2013 Covent Garden, then St. Petersburg, Beijing, Verona, Vienna), Macbeth (2015 Berlin), Don Carlo (*Ernani* 2015 NY), Gianni Schicchi (2015 LA). By 2020 he had sung 151 roles.

Simultaneously, he led two major US companies: Washington National Opera (Artistic Director 1996, General Director 2003–11) and Los Angeles Opera (Artistic Director 2000, General Director 2003–19). At WNO he elevated international casting and championed American operas (*Sophie’s Choice*, *A View From the Bridge*, *A Streetcar Named Desire*). His motto: "When I rest, I rust." He resigned from LA Opera in October 2019 amid harassment allegations.

### Sexual Harassment Allegations
In August 2019, multiple female colleagues accused Domingo of sexual harassment spanning three decades. No criminal charges were filed. LA Opera’s investigation (Gibson Dunn) deemed allegations "credible" based on pattern similarity, found some denials "less credible or lacking in awareness," but found no quid pro quo or retaliation in hiring. Domingo resigned "with a heavy heart." In February 2020 he apologized to "any colleagues who felt uncomfortable or hurt," insisting he never behaved aggressively or harmed careers. In a 2020 *La Repubblica* interview post-COVID, he called charges false and vowed to clear his name.

### Recordings, Film, and Crossover
Domingo made over 200 recordings (nearly all leading tenor roles, many multiple times). RCA Red Seal was his primary 1970s label (frequent Price/Milnes collaborations). He recorded all Puccini tenor roles (1970 *Il tabarro*–2006 *Edgar*), a complete Verdi tenor aria box set, and Wagner (*Tristan und Isolde* 2005 with Nina Stemme, *Die Walküre* 2005). Crossover albums began with *Perhaps Love* (1981, platinum); Latin albums include *Adoro* (1982), *100 años de Mariachi* (1999, platinum US/gold Mexico, his most meaningful Grammy). Film highlights: Zeffirelli’s *La Traviata* and *Otello* (Oscar nominations, Grammy-winning soundtracks), Rosi’s *Carmen* (Grammy), Ponnelle’s *Madama Butterfly*, *Rigoletto a Mantova* (2012). TV: *Live from the Met* broadcasts, *Cosby Show* cameo, *Sesame Street* puppet namesake (Placido Flamingo), 1993 Oscars performance, *Simpsons* voice cameo (2007).

### Humanitarian Work and Young Artists
Domingo’s benefit concerts span decades: 1985 Mexico earthquake (album proceeds, 2007 statue "Grandes valores"), 1997 Hurricane Pauline (Acapulco, funded 150 houses), 2003 Cancún (Ciudad de la Alegria), 2007 Athens (Darfur, Médecins Sans Frontières), 2006 New Orleans post-Katrina ($700K raised, stage named for him), 2010 Haiti, 2016 LA youth orchestra. He founded Operalia (1993), launching careers of Netrebko, Villazón, DiDonato, Stemme, Garifullina, Yoncheva, Calleja, Cura, Martínez, Bizic, Filianoti, Blue, Schrott. He established young artist programs at WNO (Vilar-Domingo), LA Opera, and Palau de les Arts Valencia.

## Terms
- **Zarzuela**: Spanish lyric-dramatic genre alternating spoken dialogue, operatic arias, and popular song; Domingo’s parents were stars, and he championed it lifelong.
- **Lirico-spinto tenor**: Voice type combining lyric warmth with "pushed" (spinto) dramatic power for roles like Cavaradossi, Don José, Canio; Domingo’s primary fach for decades.
- **Heldentenor**: "Heroic tenor" for Wagner’s heaviest roles (Siegmund, Parsifal, Tristan); Domingo added these in his 50s.
- **Comprimario**: Small supporting role (e.g., Borsa, Cassio, Normanno); Domingo’s entry point at Mexico National Opera.
- **Tessitura**: The general pitch range where a role lies; Domingo cited high tessitura concerns in *Les Troyens* (Enée) and *Lohengrin*.
- **Operalia**: Annual international opera competition founded by Domingo (1993); winners gain contracts at major houses.
- **Three Tenors**: Domingo, Pavarotti, Carreras; their 1990 Rome concert recording is the best-selling classical album ever.
- **Crossover**: Classical artists recording popular/Latin repertoire; Domingo’s *Perhaps Love* (1981) pioneered modern opera crossover.
- **General Director**: Top administrative/artistic leadership of an opera company; Domingo held this at WNO (2003–11) and LA Opera (2003–19).
- **Baritone transition**: Unprecedented late-career shift (age 68+) from tenor to Verdi baritone roles (Boccanegra, Rigoletto, Nabucco, Macbeth, etc.).

## Debates and open questions
- **Harassment allegations vs. artistic legacy**: The 2019 accusations (deemed credible by LA Opera investigation) forced resignation and canceled engagements, but Domingo maintains innocence, cites lack of charges/convictions, and continues performing in Europe. The tension between institutional accountability and presumption of innocence remains unresolved publicly.
- **Vocal longevity and fach changes**: Critics debate whether Domingo’s baritone transition represented genuine vocal re-categorization or strategic adaptation to aging tenor top; his ability to sing 151 roles across fachs is historically unprecedented.
- **Administrative dual-role conflicts**: Leading two major companies simultaneously while maintaining a global singing schedule raised questions about divided attention; his 2011 WNO departure was framed as contract non-renewal but followed criticism of overextension.
- **Crossover impact on opera**: Purists questioned whether pop/Latin recordings and Three Tenors spectacles diluted opera’s artistic standards; Domingo argued they expanded the audience funnel.

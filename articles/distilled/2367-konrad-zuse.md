# Konrad Zuse

## Overview
Konrad Zuse (1910–1995) was a German civil engineer and computer pioneer who, working largely in isolation during the 1930s and 1940s, built the world's first programmable, Turing-complete computer (the Z3) and designed the first high-level programming language (Plankalkül). His work, though initially obscured by World War II, laid foundational concepts for modern computing and digital physics.

## Timeline
- **22 June 1910** — Born in Berlin.
- **1935** — Graduated in civil engineering from Technische Hochschule Berlin.
- **1936–1938** — Built the Z1, a mechanical, programmable calculator in his parents' apartment.
- **1940** — Presented the Z2, a relay-based revision of the Z1, to German aviation experts.
- **12 May 1941** — Presented the Z3, the first fully operational, programmable, electromechanical computer.
- **1941** — Founded Zuse Apparatebau to manufacture his machines.
- **1943–1945** — Designed Plankalkül, the first high-level programming language.
- **30 January 1944** — Z1 and Z2 destroyed in a British air raid.
- **Late 1943** — Z3 destroyed in an Allied air raid.
- **1945** — Fled Berlin to Allgäu; work halted by war's end.
- **1946** — IBM took an option on his patents.
- **July 1950** — Completed and delivered the Z4, the world's first commercial computer, to ETH Zurich.
- **1969** — Published *Rechnender Raum* (*Calculating Space*), proposing a computational universe.
- **18 December 1995** — Died in Hünfeld, Hesse.

## Body

### Early Work and the Z1
After graduating in civil engineering, Zuse worked as a design engineer, where tedious manual calculations inspired him to build a computing machine. Beginning in 1935 in his parents' Berlin apartment, he constructed the Z1 (completed 1938), a mechanical, binary floating-point calculator with limited programmability via perforated film. It contained about 30,000 metal parts but was unreliable due to mechanical imprecision. The Z1 and its blueprints were destroyed in a 1944 air raid.

### The Z3 and Wartime Computing
With resources from the German government after 1939, Zuse built the Z2 and then the Z3. The Z3, presented in May 1941, was a binary, 22-bit, electromechanical computer using telephone relays. It featured programmability with loops (though no conditional jumps) and was Turing-complete, a property demonstrated only in 1998. Zuse was unaware of Alan Turing's work. The Z3 was partially funded by the German aviation research institute (DVL) for automating calculations. It was destroyed in a 1943 air raid. Zuse also built special-purpose computers (S1, S2) for calculating aerodynamic corrections for guided missiles; the S2 is considered the first process-controlled computer.

### Post-War: The Z4 and Commercialization
After fleeing Berlin in 1945, Zuse resumed work on the Z4, which had survived the bombing. He founded one of the earliest computer companies, Zuse KG. The Z4 was delivered to ETH Zurich in 1950, becoming the only working digital computer in Central Europe and the second computer in the world to be sold or loaned. Zuse's company later built other computers, including the Z11 and the Z22, the first with magnetic storage memory.

### Plankalkül and Later Ideas
While working on the Z4, Zuse realized machine code was too complex and designed Plankalkül ("Plan Calculus"), the first high-level programming language, including an example of a chess engine. He submitted it as a PhD thesis, but it was rejected for a missing fee. The work was published only in excerpts in 1948 and 1959, and in full in 1972. It slightly influenced ALGOL 58 but was implemented only in 1975. In 1969, Zuse published *Rechnender Raum*, suggesting the universe itself might be a computational structure (digital physics). In his later years, he designed plotters and conceptualized mechanical structures like the "helix tower."

## Terms
*   **Programmable computer:** A machine that can execute a sequence of stored instructions to perform different tasks.
*   **Turing-complete:** A system of computation that can simulate any other computer algorithm, given enough time and memory.
*   **Electromechanical:** Using a combination of electrical and mechanical components, such as relays.
*   **Telephone relay:** An electrically operated switch, used as the fundamental logic element in Zuse's early computers.
*   **High-level programming language:** A programming language with syntax closer to human language, abstracting away hardware details.
*   **Plankalkül:** The first high-level programming language, designed by Zuse in 1943–1945.
*   **Digital physics:** A theoretical perspective suggesting the universe is fundamentally computational.
*   **Process control computer:** A computer used to control an industrial or physical process.
*   **Binary floating-point:** A number representation system using base-2 (binary) and a floating decimal point, used in Zuse's calculators.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Primacy in invention:** Zuse is regarded by some as the inventor of the modern computer, but his work was isolated and unknown in the US/UK during the war. The extent of his influence versus contemporaries like Alan Turing, John von Neumann, and John Atanasoff is a point of historical discussion.
*   **Impact of Plankalkül:** Had Zuse's PhD thesis been published in the 1940s, it might have significantly accelerated the development of programming languages. This counterfactual is noted by computer scientist Donald Knuth.
*   **Turing-completeness of the Z3:** The Z3 was proven Turing-complete in 1998, long after its creation. Zuse himself was unaware of this property and designed it for practical engineering calculations.

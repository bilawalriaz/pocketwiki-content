# Botswana

## Overview
Botswana is a landlocked, upper-middle-income country in Southern Africa, known for its stable democracy and diamond-driven economy. It transformed from one of the world's poorest nations at independence in 1966 to a relatively prosperous state, though it faces challenges like high unemployment, inequality, and a severe HIV/AIDS epidemic. Its governance is a blend of Westminster-style parliamentary democracy and traditional Tswana tribal structures.

## Timeline
- **c. 200,000 years ago** — Botswana region proposed as birthplace of all modern humans.
- **c. AD 600** — Bantu-speaking peoples, ancestors of the Kalanga, migrate into the area.
- **1823–1843** — Mfecane period: invasions from South Africa weaken local chiefdoms.
- **1885** — Britain annexes the territory, declaring the Bechuanaland Protectorate.
- **30 September 1966** — Bechuanaland gains independence as the Republic of Botswana.
- **1977** — Botswana Defence Force (BDF) is formed.
- **1999** — International Court of Justice rules Kasikili Island belongs to Botswana.
- **2002** — Botswana becomes the first country to offer free antiretroviral drugs (ARVs).
- **2014** — Okavango Delta inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
- **2019** — High Court decriminalizes homosexual acts.
- **2021** — Botswana Harvard HIV Reference Laboratory discovers the Omicron COVID-19 variant.
- **2024** — Umbrella for Democratic Change wins election, ending BDP's 58-year rule.

## Body

### History and Politics
The Batswana people are descended from Bantu-speaking migrants. Before colonialism, the region saw the rise and fall of states like Mapungubwe and the Toutswemogala Hill settlement, followed by the disruptive Mfecane period. In 1885, Britain created the Bechuanaland Protectorate, largely to secure a route north. Independence came peacefully in 1966 under Seretse Khama. Botswana is Africa's longest uninterrupted democracy, governed by a parliamentary republic. The Botswana Democratic Party (BDP) dominated politics from 1966 until 2024, when the Umbrella for Democratic Change coalition won a historic election. The government structure blends the Westminster system with traditional tribal authorities.

### Economy
At independence, Botswana had a GDP per capita of about $70. It has since experienced one of the world's fastest growth rates, driven by diamond mining and tourism. The government, which owns 50% of the major mining company Debswana, has maintained sound fiscal policies and high foreign reserves. Despite this, the economy faces high unemployment (25.4% in 2022) and severe income inequality. Efforts are underway to diversify away from diamonds.

### Geography and Environment
Botswana is predominantly flat, with the Kalahari Desert covering about 70% of its land. Key features include the Limpopo River Basin and the Okavango Delta, the world's largest inland delta. The country has rich biodiversity, including large populations of elephants and endangered African wild dogs. Major environmental challenges are drought and desertification, exacerbated by livestock grazing. The government and international partners are implementing conservation and land management projects.

### Demographics and Society
The Tswana are the majority ethnic group (79%), followed by Kalanga (11%) and San (3%). English is the official language, while Setswana is widely spoken. About 77% of the population identifies as Christian. Botswana has been severely affected by the HIV/AIDS epidemic, which peaked with a life expectancy of 49 in 2002. A national ARV program has since dramatically improved treatment access and reduced mother-to-child transmission, with life expectancy rising to 66.4 years by 2024.

### Culture and Sports
Traditional music is vocal and uses string instruments. Basket weaving is a noted craft in the north. The national dish is seswaa (pounded meat). Football is the most popular sport. Botswana has produced notable athletes, including sprinter Letsile Tebogo, who won the country's first Olympic gold medal in the 200m at the 2024 Paris Olympics.

## Terms
- **Batswana**: The plural term for citizens of Botswana; also refers specifically to the Tswana ethnic group.
- **Mfecane**: A period of widespread chaos and warfare in southern Africa during the 1820s-1840s.
- **Bechuanaland Protectorate**: The British colonial name for Botswana from 1885 to 1966.
- **Kalahari Desert**: A large semi-arid sandy savanna covering about 70% of Botswana.
- **Okavango Delta**: A vast inland delta in northern Botswana, a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
- **Debswana**: A joint venture between the Botswana government and De Beers, the country's primary diamond mining company.
- **Setswana**: A Bantu language and the most widely spoken language in Botswana.
- **Seswaa**: The national dish of Botswana, made from pounded meat.

## Debates and Open questions
- The government's relocation of San people from the Central Kalahari Game Reserve is contested. Officials cite wildlife conservation, while critics and UN reports link it to the area's diamond wealth and human rights concerns.
- While Botswana has a long history of stable democracy, recent indices (like V-Dem) noted a period of "democratic backsliding" before the 2024 election, which was seen as a corrective.
- The country's heavy reliance on diamond revenue poses a long-term economic challenge, prompting ongoing debates about the pace and success of economic diversification.

# Nguyễn dynasty

## Overview
The Nguyễn dynasty was the last imperial dynasty of Vietnam, ruling a unified state from 1802 until the French protectorate was established in 1883. It is significant for consolidating modern Vietnam's territory, implementing a Confucian bureaucratic state, and presiding over the country's transition from independence to French colonial rule. The dynasty's emperors were members of the House of Nguyễn Phúc.

## Timeline
- **1558** — Nguyễn Hoàng establishes the Nguyễn lords' domain in the south, laying the foundation for the dynasty.
- **1771–1802** — Tây Sơn rebellion overthrows the Nguyễn lords; the last lord, Nguyễn Ánh, eventually defeats them.
- **1802** — Nguyễn Ánh unifies Vietnam and ascends the throne as Emperor Gia Long, founding the Nguyễn dynasty.
- **1820–1841** — Reign of Emperor Minh Mạng, marked by centralization, expansion, and persecution of minorities.
- **1858** — France and Spain invade Đà Nẵng, beginning the French conquest of Vietnam.
- **1862** — Treaty of Saigon cedes three southern provinces to France, forming the colony of Cochinchina.
- **1883** — Treaties of Huế establish French protectorates over Annam (central) and Tonkin (north).
- **1887** — Cochinchina, Annam, Tonkin, and Cambodia are grouped into French Indochina.
- **1945** — Japan overthrows French administration; Emperor Bảo Đại proclaims the Empire of Vietnam.
- **25 August 1945** — Bảo Đại abdicates following the August Revolution, ending the dynasty's rule.

## Body

### Origins and Establishment
The Nguyễn clan originated in Thanh Hóa Province and held political influence for centuries. After the Lê dynasty was overthrown in 1527, the Nguyễn and Trịnh clans led a loyalist rebellion. Following internal conflict, Nguyễn Hoàng was appointed lord of the southern frontier in 1558, establishing an autonomous domain. His successors expanded southward, clashing with the Trịnh lords in the north and the remnants of the Champa kingdom. The Tây Sơn rebellion (1771–1802) overthrew the Nguyễn lords, but the last heir, Nguyễn Ánh, with French and Siamese assistance, eventually defeated the Tây Sơn and unified the country.

### Imperial Rule and Expansion (1802–1883)
Emperor Gia Long (r. 1802–1819) established a Confucian-bureaucratic state, modeled on the Chinese Qing dynasty, and prioritized defense and infrastructure. His successor, Minh Mạng (r. 1820–1841), centralized power, expanded into Laos and Cambodia, and implemented policies of cultural assimilation and religious persecution, particularly against Catholics and Cham Muslims. This isolationist and conservative approach continued under later emperors, leaving the dynasty ill-prepared for Western industrial powers.

### French Conquest and Protectorate
France invaded in 1858, capturing Saigon by 1859. A series of unequal treaties followed: the 1862 Treaty of Saigon ceded the south (Cochinchina), and the 1883 Treaties of Huế established French protectorates over Annam and Tonkin. After 1883, Nguyễn emperors ruled only nominally under French supervision. The dynasty was fully incorporated into French Indochina in 1887. During World War II, Japan briefly installed Emperor Bảo Đại as the head of the puppet Empire of Vietnam in 1945. He abdicated in August 1945, ending the dynasty's 143-year rule.

### Administration and Society
The Nguyễn state was an absolute monarchy with a hierarchical bureaucracy. The emperor held supreme authority, justified by the Confucian Mandate of Heaven. The administrative system was reorganized by Minh Mạng into 31 provinces. Society was multiethnic, with policies promoting the assimilation of minorities like the Khmer and Cham into Vietnamese culture. Confucianism was the state ideology, while Buddhism was suppressed. The population grew from about 6.5 million in 1802 to an estimated 12–18 million by 1880.

## Terms
- **Mandate of Heaven**: A Confucian concept granting a ruler the divine right to govern, contingent on just and effective rule.
- **Nam tiến**: The centuries-long southward expansion of Vietnamese territory into lands formerly held by Champa and the Khmer.
- **Tây Sơn dynasty**: A peasant rebellion (1771–1802) that overthrew both the Nguyễn and Trịnh lords before being defeated by Nguyễn Ánh.
- **Cochinchina**: The southern region of Vietnam that became a direct French colony after 1862.
- **Annam**: The French protectorate covering central Vietnam, where the Nguyễn emperor nominally ruled from Huế.
- **Tonkin**: The French protectorate covering northern Vietnam.
- **Confucianism**: The philosophical and ethical system that formed the basis of the dynasty's state ideology, education, and bureaucracy.
- **Protectorate**: A territory under the control of a foreign power that maintains a local ruler as a figurehead.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The extent to which the Nguyễn dynasty's conservative, Sinicized policies (e.g., isolationism, persecution of minorities) directly caused its failure to resist French colonization is debated.
- The role of internal court politics, such as the succession crisis after Emperor Tự Đức's death, in facilitating the final French takeover is a point of historical analysis.
- The legacy of the dynasty is contested, viewed by some as a period of cultural consolidation and by others as one of oppressive feudalism that hindered modernization.

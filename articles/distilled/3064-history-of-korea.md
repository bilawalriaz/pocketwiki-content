# History of Korea

## Overview
Korean history spans from the Paleolithic era to the modern division into North and South Korea, marked by successive kingdoms, cultural development, and foreign influence. The modern era is defined by Japanese colonial rule, post-WWII division, the Korean War, and the divergent paths of a democratic South Korea and a totalitarian North Korea.

## Body

### Prehistory and Early Kingdoms
The Lower Paleolithic era began roughly half a million years ago. The Neolithic period followed, with the earliest pottery dating to c. 8000 BC. Agricultural societies emerged during the Mumun pottery period (c. 1500–300 BC). The Bronze Age began around 900–800 BC. The first Korean kingdom, Gojoseon, was mythically founded in 2333 BC. After its fall to the Han dynasty in 108 BC, the Chinese Lelang Commandery remained for four centuries. In the south, the Jin state arose c. 300 BC, later evolving into the Samhan confederacies.

### Three Kingdoms and Unification
The Proto-Three Kingdoms period saw states like Buyeo, Okjeo, and Dongye emerge. The Three Kingdoms—Goguryeo, Baekje, and Silla—rose to control the peninsula and Manchuria. Goguryeo, founded in 37 BC, became a powerful empire in the 5th century. Baekje, founded in 18 BC, was a maritime power that transmitted culture to Japan. Silla, traditionally founded in 57 BC, allied with Tang China. This alliance defeated Baekje in 660 and Goguryeo in 668. Silla expelled Tang forces by 676, unifying most of the peninsula.

### North and South States Period
Following unification, the "North and South States" period (698–926) saw Unified Silla coexist with Balhae. Unified Silla experienced a golden age of art and culture. Balhae, founded in 698 by the Goguryeo general Dae Jo-yeong, controlled former Goguryeo lands in Manchuria. It was conquered by the Khitan-led Liao dynasty in 926, and its refugees were absorbed by the Goryeo dynasty.

### Goryeo Dynasty
The Goryeo dynasty was founded by Wang Kŏn in 918, unifying the Later Three Kingdoms by 936. It is the source of the English name "Korea." Goryeo codified laws, introduced a civil service, and saw Buddhism flourish. It repelled a major Khitan invasion in 1018. From 1231, Mongol invasions brought Goryeo under Yuan overlordship for about 80 years. In the 14th century, General Yi Seong-gye seized power, founding the Joseon dynasty in 1392.

### Joseon Dynasty
The Joseon dynasty (1392–1897) was founded on Neo-Confucian ideology. King Sejong the Great (r. 1418–1450) created the Hangul alphabet. After repelling Japanese invasions (1592–1598) and Manchu invasions (1627, 1636), Joseon became increasingly isolationist. In the 19th century, it was forced to sign unequal treaties. The Donghak Peasant Revolution and Gabo Reforms (1894–1896) led to the Korean Empire (1897–1910), which attempted modernization.

### Japanese Rule and Division
Japan made Korea a protectorate in 1905 and annexed it in 1910. Japanese rule suppressed Korean culture. The March First Movement of 1919 was a major peaceful protest. After Japan's surrender in 1945, Korea was divided at the 38th Parallel. In 1948, this led to the Republic of Korea (South Korea) and the Democratic People's Republic of Korea (North Korea). The Korean War (1950–1953) ended in a ceasefire, cementing the division.

### Modern Korea
South Korea transitioned to democracy in 1987 and experienced rapid economic growth. North Korea maintained totalitarian rule under the Kim family. In 2018, the two nations agreed to work toward a formal end to the conflict, but this process was later suspended.

## Terms
- **Gojoseon**: The first Korean kingdom, founded mythically in 2333 BC.
- **Three Kingdoms of Korea**: Goguryeo, Baekje, and Silla, which dominated the peninsula from the 1st century BC to the 7th century AD.
- **Unified Silla**: The kingdom that controlled most of the peninsula after defeating Baekje and Goguryeo with Tang China's help in the 660s.
- **Balhae**: A Korean-Mohe state founded in 698 in former Goguryeo territories.
- **Goryeo**: The dynasty founded in 918 that unified the Later Three Kingdoms and is the source of the name "Korea."
- **Joseon**: The dynasty founded in 1392 that ruled until 1897, based on Neo-Confucianism.
- **Hangul**: The Korean alphabet created by King Sejong the Great in 1446.
- **March First Movement**: A nationwide peaceful protest for independence from Japanese rule that began on March 1, 1919.
- **38th Parallel**: The line of latitude that divided the Korean Peninsula in 1945 and later became the border between North and South Korea.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The exact dates and origins of the Paleolithic era in Korea are debated, with estimates ranging from 400,000 to 700,000 years ago.
- The historicity of Gojoseon's founding myths is challenged by contradicting evidence.
- The legitimacy of the 1910 Japan–Korea Annexation Treaty is disputed, as it was not signed by the Korean Emperor as required.

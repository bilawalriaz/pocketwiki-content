# Turkish War of Independence

## Overview
The Turkish War of Independence (1919–1923) was a military and political struggle led by the Turkish National Movement against Allied occupation and partition plans for the Ottoman Empire after World War I. It resulted in the abolition of the Ottoman sultanate and caliphate, the establishment of the Republic of Turkey, and the transfer of sovereignty from the dynasty to the nation, setting the stage for modern, secular Turkish statehood.

## Timeline
- **30 Oct 1918** — Armistice of Mudros ends Ottoman participation in WWI.
- **15 May 1919** — Greek forces land at Smyrna (İzmir), sparking widespread resistance.
- **19 May 1919** — Mustafa Kemal lands at Samsun, beginning organization of nationalist resistance.
- **23 Apr 1920** — Grand National Assembly (GNA) convenes in Ankara as a counter-government.
- **10 Aug 1920** — Treaty of Sèvres is signed by the Ottoman government, partitioning the empire.
- **26 Aug 1922** — Turkish forces launch the Great Offensive against Greek armies in the west.
- **11 Oct 1922** — Armistice of Mudanya ends fighting, leading to Greek evacuation of Thrace.
- **1 Nov 1922** — Ottoman sultanate is abolished by the GNA.
- **24 Jul 1923** — Treaty of Lausanne is signed, recognizing the sovereignty of the new Turkish state.
- **29 Oct 1923** — Republic of Turkey is officially proclaimed.

## Body

### Background and Outbreak
Following the Ottoman defeat in WWI, the Armistice of Mudros allowed Allied occupation of strategic points. The Committee of Union and Progress (CUP) regime collapsed, creating a power vacuum. Allied plans for partition, including a Greek landing at Smyrna on 15 May 1919, inflamed nationalist sentiment. Mustafa Kemal, sent by the Sultan to restore order, instead became the leader of a resistance movement, arguing the Istanbul government was collaborating with the occupiers.

### Organizing the National Movement
From 1919 to 1920, Mustafa Kemal organized resistance through a series of congresses. The Erzurum and Sivas Congresses drafted the National Pact (Misak-ı Millî), defining the desired borders of the Turkish state based on Wilsonian self-determination. When Allied authorities arrested nationalist politicians in Istanbul in March 1920, the GNA was established in Ankara as the legitimate government, creating a jurisdictional conflict with the Sultan's administration.

### Military Campaigns
The war was fought on multiple fronts. In the east, Turkish forces under Kâzım Karabekir defeated Armenia, leading to the Treaty of Kars (1921) which secured the eastern border with Soviet-backed states. In the south, irregular forces fought French troops, culminating in a French withdrawal and a border agreement. The decisive Western Front against Greece saw initial Greek advances checked at the Battles of İnönü. After a stalemate, the Turkish Great Offensive in August 1922 routed the Greek army, leading to the recapture of Smyrna and the Armistice of Mudanya.

### Political Outcome and Republic
The military victory led to the abolition of the Ottoman sultanate in November 1922. The subsequent Treaty of Lausanne (1923) replaced the punitive Treaty of Sèvres, recognizing the full sovereignty and borders of the new state. The Republic of Turkey was proclaimed on 29 October 1923, with Mustafa Kemal as its first president. The war concluded with a compulsory population exchange between Greece and Turkey and the end of the Ottoman era.

## Terms
- **Kuva-yi Milliye**: Irregular Turkish nationalist militia forces that formed the initial resistance against occupation.
- **Grand National Assembly (GNA)**: The parliament established in Ankara in 1920 as the sovereign body of the Turkish nationalist movement.
- **National Pact (Misak-ı Millî)**: The foundational document of the nationalist movement, outlining the desired borders and principles for the future Turkish state.
- **Treaty of Sèvres**: The 1920 treaty imposed by the Allies, which partitioned the Ottoman Empire but was never ratified and was rejected by the nationalists.
- **Treaty of Lausanne**: The 1923 treaty that replaced Sèvres, recognizing the sovereignty and borders of the Republic of Turkey.
- **Capitulations**: Historical privileges granting extraterritorial rights to foreign nationals in the Ottoman Empire, abolished by the new Turkish state.
- **Milne Line**: The demarcation line around the Greek-occupied zone of Smyrna, beyond which low-intensity guerrilla warfare began.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Continuity with the CUP**: Historians debate the extent to which the nationalist movement was a continuation of the CUP regime, given that many leaders were former members, versus a new popular movement that broke with the past.
- **Nature of the Conflict**: Some scholars, like Mesut Uyar, argue the war also constituted a civil war within Anatolia between different factions, a aspect often downplayed in official narratives.
- **Ethnic Cleansing**: The war's role in the final destruction of historic Christian communities in Anatolia (Armenian, Greek, Assyrian) through massacres, deportations, and population exchange is a major point of historical and political contention.
- **Historiographical Framing**: The traditional Turkish narrative of a "Liberation War" (Kurtuluş Savaşı) against imperialist forces is critiqued by some as obscuring the Ottoman Empire's own expansionist role in WWI and the nationalist movement's actions against minorities.

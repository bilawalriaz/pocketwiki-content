# History of Japan

## Overview
The history of Japan spans from prehistoric hunter-gatherer societies to a modern economic powerhouse, marked by periods of isolation, rapid modernization, and dramatic social transformation. Its trajectory is defined by the interplay between indigenous development and profound influences from the Asian continent and, later, the West, culminating in its emergence as a major global power after World War II.

## Timeline
- **c. 38,000 BC** — First human inhabitants arrive in the Paleolithic period.
- **c. 14,500 BC** — World's oldest known ceramics crafted during the Jōmon period.
- **c. 1,000–800 BC** — Yayoi period begins; iron technology and rice cultivation introduced from Asia.
- **3rd century AD** — Kingdom of Yamatai, ruled by Himiko, gains ascendancy.
- **538 AD** — Buddhism introduced from the Korean kingdom of Baekje.
- **794** — Heian period begins with the capital moved to Heian-kyō (Kyoto).
- **1185** — Minamoto no Yoritomo establishes the Kamakura shogunate after the Genpei War.
- **1543** — Portuguese become the first Europeans to reach Japan.
- **1600** — Tokugawa Ieyasu wins the Battle of Sekigahara, leading to the Edo period.
- **1853** — Commodore Perry's expedition ends Japan's isolationist policy.
- **1868** — Meiji Restoration restores imperial rule and begins rapid modernization.
- **1945** — Japan surrenders in World War II following atomic bombings.
- **1947** — New constitution establishes a constitutional monarchy and parliamentary democracy.
- **1989** — Heisei period begins; economic bubble bursts, leading to the "Lost Decade."
- **2019** — Reiwa period begins under Emperor Naruhito.

## Body

### Prehistoric and Classical Japan
The earliest inhabitants were Paleolithic hunter-gatherers. The Jōmon period (c. 13,000–1,000 BC) saw the creation of the world's oldest pottery. The Yayoi period (c. 1,000 BC–300 AD) brought transformative technologies like iron tools and wet-rice agriculture from the continent, leading to rapid population growth and social stratification. The Yamato clan gradually unified Japan, establishing the world's longest-reigning imperial dynasty. The introduction of Buddhism in 538 AD and the adoption of Chinese-style centralized government (ritsuryō state) during the Asuka and Nara periods profoundly shaped Japanese culture and administration. The Heian period (794–1185) is considered a golden age of classical culture, but imperial power waned as landholding aristocrats and warrior clans gained influence.

### Feudal Japan
Power shifted from the imperial court to military rulers. After the Genpei War, Minamoto no Yoritomo established the Kamakura shogunate (1185–1333), a feudal military government. Despite repelling Mongol invasions, the shogunate fell to a rebellion. The Muromachi period (1333–1568) saw the rise of regional warlords (daimyō) and a century of civil war (the Warring States period). Contact with Europeans in 1543 introduced firearms and Christianity. The Azuchi-Momoyama period (1568–1600) saw Japan reunified under Oda Nobunaga and Toyotomi Hideyoshi. Tokugawa Ieyasu's victory at Sekigahara (1600) initiated the Tokugawa shogunate and the Edo period (1600–1868).

### Early Modern and Modern Japan
The Tokugawa shogunate enforced strict social order and isolationist policies (sakoku), allowing limited trade with the Dutch and Chinese. This era of peace fostered economic growth, urbanization, and a flourishing of popular culture. The arrival of Commodore Perry in 1853 forced an end to isolation, leading to the shogunate's collapse and the Meiji Restoration (1868). The Meiji government rapidly modernized and Westernized Japan, establishing a constitutional monarchy and building an industrial, imperial power. After victories in the Sino-Japanese (1894–95) and Russo-Japanese (1904–05) wars, Japan became a major world power.

### World War II and Postwar Era
Rising militarism led to the invasion of Manchuria (1931) and a full-scale war with China (1937). Japan's attack on Pearl Harbor (1941) brought the U.S. into World War II. After devastating defeats and the atomic bombings of Hiroshima and Nagasaki, Japan surrendered in 1945. Under Allied occupation (1945–52), a new democratic constitution was enacted. The postwar "Yoshida Doctrine" focused on economic recovery with U.S. protection. Fueled by this alliance and government-industry cooperation, Japan experienced extraordinary economic growth from the 1950s, becoming the world's second-largest economy by 1968. The economic bubble burst in 1989, leading to prolonged stagnation. The Reiwa period (2019–present) has been marked by demographic challenges, political shifts, and evolving security concerns in the region.

## Terms
- **Shōgun**: The hereditary military dictator of Japan from 1185 to 1868.
- **Daimyō**: The powerful feudal lords who ruled their own domains during the feudal period.
- **Samurai**: The warrior class that served the daimyō and shōgun.
- **Ritsuryō**: A historical legal system based on Chinese Confucian and Legalist principles, used in Japan from the 7th to 12th centuries.
- **Sakoku**: The isolationist foreign policy of the Tokugawa shogunate (c. 1633–1853).
- **Meiji Restoration**: The 1868 political revolution that restored practical imperial rule and led to the Meiji era.
- **Zaibatsu**: Large family-controlled industrial and financial business conglomerates in the Empire of Japan.
- **Article 9**: A clause in Japan's 1947 constitution that renounces war and prohibits the maintenance of military forces.
- **Lost Decade**: A term for the period of economic stagnation in Japan following the asset price bubble's collapse in 1989.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The location and nature of the ancient kingdom of Yamatai, ruled by Queen Himiko, remain debated.
- The degree to which the spread of Yayoi culture was due to migration versus cultural diffusion is discussed.
- Emperor Hirohito's personal responsibility for and role in Japan's wartime actions is a subject of historical controversy.
- The adequacy and sincerity of Japan's official war apologies continue to cause diplomatic tensions with China and South Korea.

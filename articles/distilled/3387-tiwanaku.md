# Tiwanaku

## Overview
Tiwanaku is a major Pre-Columbian archaeological site in western Bolivia, near Lake Titicaca. It was the center of a powerful Andean civilization that flourished from approximately AD 200 to 1000, known for its monumental architecture, complex society, and eventual collapse, likely due to drought. Its study reveals key insights into pre-Inca urban life, agriculture, and cosmology.

## Body

### Site History and Dating
The site's age has been debated. Early 20th-century archaeologist Arthur Posnansky claimed it was 11,000–17,000 years old. Later, a date of 1580 BC was proposed but is now considered unreliable. The current consensus, based on radiocarbon dating, places the founding around AD 110. The city grew steadily, becoming a major urban center by AD 800 with a population over 10,000. Its complex agropastoral economy supported this growth. The civilization collapsed around AD 1000, with recent geological evidence pointing to a severe regional drought as a likely cause.

### Relationships and Influence
The Tiwanaku culture expanded its power in the Titicaca basin between AD 200-500. It shared iconography, known as the "Southern Andean Iconographic Series," with the contemporary Wari culture, suggesting a relationship based on trade or military contact. Later, the Inca Empire incorporated similar architectural styles, indicating Tiwanaku's lasting influence in the Andean region.

### Structures and Architecture
The site features monumental structures built with megalithic blocks. Key excavated buildings include the terraced platform mounds Akapana and Pumapunku, and enclosures like Kalasasaya and the Semi-Subterranean Temple. A theory suggests elites lived within a walled, moated area designed to resemble a sacred island. The purpose of the precise stonework is debated; researchers have not found a single standard unit of measurement like the proposed *luk’ a* (about 60 cm). Instead, they argue for a system where individual elements were scaled contextually. The Pumapunku platform contains the site's largest stone block, estimated at 131 metric tonnes.

### Cosmology and Ritual
Tiwanaku was a major religious center. The site is located between two sacred mountains, and its structures align with celestial events like solstices. The Akapana may have served as an astronomical observatory. Rituals included human and camelid sacrifice, as evidenced by remains at the Akapana. A "sacrificer" motif on artifacts represents transformation (*tucoy*). The Aymara people, likely descendants, believe in three cosmic spaces. Lake Titicaca is considered the spiritual birthplace of their world, and a figure on the Gateway of the Sun may represent the creator god Viracocha.

### Archaeology and Modern Study
The site has suffered from looting and quarrying since its fall. Early scientific study began in the 19th century. In the 1960s, the Bolivian government began reconstructing the Kalasasaya walls, though not always accurately. Major excavations from 1978-1990s by Alan Kolata and Oswaldo Rivera provided crucial data on the civilization's growth and drought-based collapse. Recent projects use technologies like ground-penetrating radar and drone imaging, which in 2016 revealed a vast complex of previously unknown structures. The site is a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

## Terms
*   **Agropastoral economy:** An economy based on a combination of agriculture and herding of animals.
*   **Ashlar masonry:** Stonework with square-cut stones.
*   **Chambranle:** A recessed frame around a doorway or gateway.
*   **Megalithic:** Relating to very large stones used in construction.
*   **Suka kollus:** Agricultural terraces rediscovered by archaeologists, likely used for farming.
*   **Tenon heads:** Sculpted heads that project from a wall, secured by a tenon (a projecting piece).
*   **UNESCO World Heritage Site:** A place listed by the United Nations as having special cultural or physical significance.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   The exact cause of the civilization's collapse around AD 1000 is debated, though drought is a leading theory.
*   The original name of the city for its inhabitants is unknown, as they had no written language.
*   The purpose and meaning of specific iconography, such as the carvings on the Gateway of the Sun, are speculative; theories include a calendar system, but there is no definitive evidence.
*   The precise methods and systems of measurement used in its monumental architecture remain unclear.

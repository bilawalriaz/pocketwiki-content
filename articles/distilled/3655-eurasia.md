# Eurasia

## Overview
Eurasia is the largest contiguous landmass on Earth, comprising the combined areas of Europe and Asia. While often treated as two separate continents, this division is a historical and cultural construct rather than a strict geographical one. The supercontinent is home to over 70% of the world's population and has been the cradle of many ancient civilizations, making it central to human history and geopolitics.

## Timeline
- **c. 375–325 million years ago** — Eurasia forms from the merging of Siberia, Kazakhstania, and Baltica.
- **c. 125,000 years ago** — Humans first settle in Eurasia from Africa.
- **Mid-first millennium BCE** — The Axial Age sees a belt of civilizations stretch across Eurasia's subtropical zone.
- **18th century** — Philip Johan von Strahlenberg defines the modern Europe-Asia boundary.
- **1869** — The Suez Canal is completed, connecting Eurasia and Africa.
- **1991** — The Cold War ends, altering the political landscape of Eurasia.
- **1996** — The Asia–Europe Meeting (ASEM) is first organized.
- **2001** — The Shanghai Cooperation Organization is founded.
- **2015** — The Eurasian Economic Union is established.

## Body

### Geography and Geology
Eurasia spans approximately 55 million square kilometers, or about 36.2% of Earth's land area. It is bordered by the Atlantic, Arctic, and Pacific Oceans, and is connected to Africa at the Suez Canal. The landmass exhibits all major climate types due to its vast size and latitude range. Geologically, it is often considered a single rigid mega block, though this is debated. The Eurasian Plate includes most of Europe and Asia but excludes the Indian subcontinent and the Arabian Peninsula.

The continent contains all of the world's 100 highest mountains, located in ranges like the Himalayas and Karakoram. The Alpide belt, a 15,000 km mountain chain, stretches across southern Eurasia from Java to the Iberian Peninsula. Major peninsulas include the Arabian, Korean, Indian, and Italian.

### Historical and Cultural Significance
Eurasia hosted ancient civilizations in Mesopotamia, Egypt, the Indus Valley, and China. The concept of Europe and Asia as distinct continents dates to antiquity, with the Greeks originally including Africa in their definition of Asia. The modern boundary, largely defined in the 18th century, follows the Ural Mountains, Ural River, and Caspian Sea, though its validity is debated.

From the Age of Discovery, new connections emerged, leading to Western European imperialism that dominated parts of Eurasia until the mid-20th century. The Soviet Union's communist presence then influenced much of the continent until 1991.

### Geopolitical Ideology and Modern Use
"Eurasia" is a key geopolitical concept. Russian "Eurasianism" initially corresponded to the 1914 borders of Imperial Russia and promotes integration among post-Soviet states. The term is also used to refer to the post-Soviet space, including Russia, Central Asia, and the Transcaucasus.

Several organizations aim for regional integration, such as the Eurasian Economic Union (2015), the Shanghai Cooperation Organization (2001), and the Asia–Europe Meeting (1996). Russia has proposed broader initiatives like the "Greater Eurasian Partnership" to create a pan-continental security and economic architecture.

## Terms
- **Supercontinent:** A large landmass comprising most or all of Earth's continental blocks. Eurasia is considered one, or part of the larger Afro-Eurasia.
- **Axial Age:** A period in the mid-first millennium BCE when major philosophical and religious ideas emerged across Eurasia.
- **Köppen classification:** A system for classifying climates based on temperature and precipitation patterns.
- **Eurasian Plate:** A major tectonic plate that includes most of Europe and Asia.
- **Alpide belt:** A 15,000 km mountain chain stretching across southern Eurasia.
- **Eurasianism:** A geopolitical ideology emphasizing the unique identity and integration of the post-Soviet Eurasian space.
- **Greater Eurasian Partnership:** A Russian initiative for broad continental integration in economics, security, and logistics.

## Debates and Open questions
- The precise geographical boundary between Europe and Asia is disputed, particularly whether it follows the Kuma-Manych Depression or the Caucasus Mountains.
- Geologists debate whether Eurasia is a single rigid mega block.
- The political and cultural definition of "Eurasia" varies, sometimes referring to the entire continent and other times specifically to the post-Soviet space.
- The feasibility and scope of pan-continental integration projects like the Greater Eurasian Partnership remain open questions.

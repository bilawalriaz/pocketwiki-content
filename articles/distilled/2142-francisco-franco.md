# Francisco Franco

## Overview
Francisco Franco was a Spanish military general who ruled Spain as a dictator from 1939 until his death in 1975. He led the Nationalist faction to victory in the Spanish Civil War (1936–1939), overthrowing the Second Spanish Republic. His long regime, known as Francoist Spain, was marked by severe political repression, economic autarky followed by growth, and a complex relationship with fascism and international politics.

## Body

### Early Life and Military Career
Franco was born into a seafaring family with a long tradition of naval service. He entered the Infantry Academy in Toledo in 1907 and was commissioned in 1910. He gained prominence as an effective officer in the Rif War in Morocco, where he was wounded in 1916. He rose rapidly, becoming a brigadier general in 1926 and director of the General Military Academy in Zaragoza in 1928. A conservative monarchist, he served the Second Spanish Republic after 1931 but clashed with its government. His career was boosted after the right-wing victory in the 1933 elections, and he led the brutal suppression of the 1934 Asturian miners' uprising.

### The Spanish Civil War and Rise to Power
Following the failed military coup of July 1936, which sparked the Spanish Civil War, Franco emerged as the leader of the Nationalist faction. He commanded the Army of Africa and, after the deaths of other rebel leaders, became the sole leader, appointed Generalísimo and Head of State in October 1936. He received crucial military support from Fascist Italy and Nazi Germany. Franco consolidated all Nationalist groups into a single party, the FET y de las JONS, and developed a cult of personality. The Nationalists won the war in April 1939, establishing Franco's dictatorship.

### Francoist Spain: Politics and Repression
Franco's regime was a personalist dictatorship with elements of fascism, though scholars debate its exact classification. It was characterized by extreme repression, with tens of thousands executed during and after the civil war in what is known as the White Terror. The regime suppressed all political opposition, trade unions, and regional cultures and languages, promoting a unitary Spanish identity. During World War II, Spain maintained official neutrality but supported the Axis powers, damaging its international reputation.

### Economic Policy and International Relations
Post-war Spain pursued autarky (economic self-sufficiency), leading to stagnation. In the 1950s, facing economic crisis, Franco replaced ideological ministers with technocrats, many linked to Opus Dei, who implemented liberal economic reforms. This led to the "Spanish miracle," a period of rapid growth from 1959 to 1973. Internationally, Spain was isolated until the Cold War, when its strategic location led to a 1953 alliance with the United States and admission to the United Nations in 1955.

### Succession and Death
In his final years, Franco named Prince Juan Carlos de Borbón as his successor in 1969. Franco died in 1975, and Juan Carlos became king, overseeing Spain's transition to democracy. Franco's remains were interred at the Valley of the Fallen, a monument built with forced labor. In 2019, after a political and legal battle, his body was exhumed and reburied at a state cemetery.

## Terms
*   **Autarky:** A policy of economic self-sufficiency, isolating a country from international trade.
*   **Caudillo:** A Spanish term for a military or political leader, used as Franco's official title.
*   **FET y de las JONS:** The single party of the Francoist state, formed by merging the Falange and Carlist movements.
*   **Movimiento Nacional:** The official name for the Nationalist political movement during and after the Spanish Civil War.
*   **Technocrats:** Apolitical experts, often with economics backgrounds, who implemented modernizing reforms in Franco's later government.
*   **White Terror:** The period of mass executions and repression carried out by the Nationalists during and after the Spanish Civil War.
*   **Valley of the Fallen (Valle de los Caídos):** A monumental basilica and memorial near Madrid, built using forced labor, where Franco was originally buried.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Fascist or Authoritarian?** Scholars debate whether Franco's regime was fully fascist, a para-fascist regime, or a traditional conservative military dictatorship. The regime exhibited fascist trappings but also relied on the military, the Church, and monarchists.
*   **World War II Neutrality:** Historians discuss whether Franco deliberately made excessive demands to Hitler in 1940 to avoid entering the war, or if Spain was simply too devastated and weak to join the Axis.
*   **Legacy and Historical Memory:** Franco remains a deeply divisive figure in Spain. The "Pact of Forgetting" after his death gave way to ongoing debates about exhumation, memorials, and officially acknowledging the victims of his regime.

# Huineng

## Overview
Huineng (638–713) is the semi-legendary Sixth Patriarch of Chan (Zen) Buddhism, central to the tradition's history. He is credited as the founder of the "Southern School," which teaches sudden enlightenment, and is the subject of the influential *Platform Sutra*. Modern scholarship suggests his legendary status was largely constructed by his disciple Shenhui in the 8th century to establish a rival lineage to the then-dominant Northern School.

## Timeline
- **c. 638** — Huineng is born in Fanyang; his father dies young, leaving the family in poverty.
- **c. 670s** — As a layman selling firewood, he hears the *Diamond Sutra* and is awakened.
- **c. 670s** — He travels to Huangmei to meet the Fifth Patriarch, Daman Hongren.
- **c. 670s** — After eight months of menial work, Hongren announces a poem contest to choose his successor.
- **c. 670s** — Shenxiu writes a famous stanza; the illiterate Huineng has one written for him in response.
- **c. 670s** — Hongren secretly transmits the Dharma and robe to Huineng, making him the Sixth Patriarch.
- **c. 670s** — Huineng escapes the monastery, is pursued, and converts his pursuer, Huiming.
- **713** — Huineng dies; his body is later mummified and enshrined at Nanhua Temple.
- **731** — Shenhui begins public campaigns promoting Huineng's lineage over Shenxiu's.
- **796** — An imperial commission officially recognizes Huineng's Southern lineage as orthodox.

## Body

### Sources and Historicity
The primary sources for Huineng's life are the preface to the *Platform Sutra* and the *Transmission of the Lamp*. Modern scholars doubt the historicity of his traditional biography, considering him a figure of "merely regional significance" whose legendary narrative was created after his death. The *Platform Sutra*, attributed to his disciple Fahai, is a composite text compiled over time, containing layers of writing from different periods. It is a repository of early Chan teachings, including the concept of an inherent Buddha-nature obscured by illusions.

### Legendary Biography
According to the *Platform Sutra*, Huineng was an uneducated layman who achieved sudden awakening upon hearing the *Diamond Sutra*. He sought out the Fifth Patriarch, Daman Hongren, who initially doubted the "barbarian from the south." Huineng impressed Hongren with his understanding of universal Buddha-nature. After working as a laborer, Hongren held a poem contest to select his successor. The leading disciple, Shenxiu, wrote a verse emphasizing gradual polishing of the mind. Huineng, with help, composed a counter-verse pointing to the inherent purity of mind. Hongren secretly transmitted the Dharma and robe to Huineng, who then fled to avoid persecution.

### Teachings: The Three Key Doctrines
Huineng's teachings, as recorded in the *Platform Sutra*, center on three interrelated doctrines: no-thought (*wunian*), non-attribute (*wuxiang*), and non-abiding (*wuzhu*). **No-thought** is not the absence of thinking but an unattached, non-conceptual state of mind that experiences reality directly. **Non-attribute** means being free from attachment to sensory characteristics while still engaging with the world, like space containing all objects. **Non-abiding** is the root principle, meaning the mind should not dwell or fixate on any past, present, or future experience, allowing for a state of "hyperintense motion" and free flow, which Huineng associated with enlightenment.

### Meditation and Wisdom
Huineng taught that meditation (*dhyāna*) and wisdom (*prajñā*) are not sequential but are two aspects of the same reality, forming an essence-function relationship. Wisdom is the function of meditation, and meditation is the essence of wisdom. He redefined "seated meditation" (*zuochan*) as the mind's internal motionlessness, not mere physical sitting. He criticized practices that objectify the mind or purity, arguing they are dualistic and obscure one's fundamental nature.

### Historical Impact and the Shenhui Controversy
Doctrinally, Huineng's Southern School is associated with sudden enlightenment, while Shenxiu's Northern School is linked to gradual enlightenment—a distinction modern scholars view as a polemical exaggeration. The historical Huineng was likely obscure until the 8th-century monk Shenhui launched a campaign to promote him as the true Sixth Patriarch. Shenhui's propaganda, which invented much of Huineng's hagiography, was eventually sanctioned by an imperial commission in 796. This established Huineng's lineage as orthodox, ensuring that all later Chan schools traced their origin to him and that "sudden enlightenment" became standard Chan doctrine.

## Terms
- **Chan (Zen):** A school of Mahayana Buddhism emphasizing meditation and direct insight.
- **Patriarch:** A revered teacher and lineage head in Chan Buddhism.
- **Sudden Enlightenment:** The doctrine that enlightenment can be attained in a single, direct moment of insight.
- **Platform Sutra:** A foundational Chan text attributed to Huineng, recording his teachings and life story.
- **No-thought (wunian):** A state of mind free from attachment, allowing direct experience of reality.
- **Non-abiding (wuzhu):** The principle that the mind should not fixate on any object or experience.
- **Dhyāna (Chan/Zen):** Meditation; in Huineng's teaching, it refers to the mind's inherent motionlessness.
- **Prajñā:** Wisdom or insight, especially into the nature of reality.
- **Southern School:** The Chan lineage associated with Huineng and sudden enlightenment.
- **Northern School:** The Chan lineage associated with Shenxiu and gradual enlightenment.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Historicity:** Was Huineng a significant teacher, or a marginal figure whose legend was created by Shenhui? Most scholars now lean toward the latter.
- **Authorship of the Platform Sutra:** The text is a composite work. Its core may have originated within the Oxhead school, not the Southern School as traditionally believed.
- **The Poem Contest:** The traditional interpretation of the verses as representing gradual vs. sudden approaches is considered incorrect by scholars like John McRae, who see them as complementary.
- **Shenhui's Role:** To what extent did Shenhui invent Huineng's biography and teachings to win imperial favor and establish his own lineage's authority?

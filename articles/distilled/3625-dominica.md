# Dominica

## Overview
Dominica is a volcanic island nation in the Caribbean's Lesser Antilles, known as the "Nature Island" for its lush rainforests and geothermal features. Its history is marked by Indigenous settlement, European colonization, and a struggle for self-governance, culminating in independence in 1978. Today, it is a parliamentary republic whose economy and identity are shaped by its natural environment, a diverse cultural heritage, and the challenges of hurricane vulnerability and economic diversification.

## Body

### Etymology and Early History
The Kalinago people called the island *Wai'tu kubuli* ("Tall is her body"). Christopher Columbus named it *Dominica* (Latin for "Sunday") after sighting it on 3 November 1493. The island was first settled by Arawak people from South America in the fifth century, who were later displaced by the Kalinago by the 15th century. European colonization began with Spanish claims, but permanent settlement was initiated by the French in 1690, who brought enslaved Africans to work on coffee plantations.

### Colonial Rule and Path to Independence
Great Britain gained control of Dominica in 1763 following the Seven Years' War. The British continued the plantation economy and established English as the official language. A pivotal moment came in 1838, when Dominica became the first British colony in the West Indies to have an elected legislature controlled by an ethnic African majority. This led to political tension with the planter class, resulting in the erosion of the elected assembly's power and a return to Crown colony government in 1896. The 20th century saw growing political consciousness, leading to Dominica becoming an associated state of the UK in 1967 and finally gaining full independence as a republic on 3 November 1978.

### Post-Independence Politics and Challenges
Independence was followed by political instability, including a civilian coup in 1979 and two attempted coups in 1981. Eugenia Charles of the Dominica Freedom Party served as prime minister from 1980 to 1995. The economy, historically dependent on bananas, has suffered from natural disasters and falling commodity prices. The Citizenship by Investment (CBI) programme, offering citizenship for economic investment, has become a major source of government revenue, particularly after the devastation of Hurricane Maria in 2017. Roosevelt Skerrit of the Dominica Labour Party has been prime minister since 2004, winning consecutive elections.

### Geography and Environment
Dominica is the youngest island in the Lesser Antilles, formed by volcanic activity and still geologically active, evidenced by features like Boiling Lake. It is mountainous, with rainforests covering much of the interior and 365 rivers. The island is a biodiversity hotspot, home to endemic species like the critically endangered Sisserou parrot, its national bird. Its location makes it highly vulnerable to hurricanes, which have caused repeated, severe damage to infrastructure and the economy.

### Economy and Society
The economy is transitioning from agriculture to services, including ecotourism and offshore finance. The CBI programme is a critical fiscal pillar. The population is predominantly of African descent, with a significant mixed-race minority and the last surviving pre-Columbian Kalinago community in the eastern Caribbean. English is the official language, but Dominican Creole (based on French) is widely spoken. The country is a member of the Commonwealth, the UN, and the Organisation of Eastern Caribbean States.

## Terms
- **Kalinago**: The Indigenous people of Dominica, also historically known as Caribs, who maintain a distinct territory on the island.
- **Dominican Creole (Kwéyòl)**: A French-based Creole language widely spoken alongside English, reflecting the island's colonial history.
- **Citizenship by Investment (CBI)**: A government programme that grants citizenship to foreign investors who make a significant financial contribution to the economy.
- **Morne Diablotins**: The highest mountain peak on Dominica, at 1,448 m (4,751 ft).
- **Boiling Lake**: The world's second-largest hot spring, a key geothermal feature indicating the island's volcanic activity.
- **Sisserou Parrot (Imperial Amazon)**: A critically endangered parrot endemic to Dominica and the island's national bird.
- **Windward Islands**: A group of islands in the Lesser Antilles, of which Dominica is the northernmost.
- **Commonwealth of Nations**: A political association of 56 member states, mostly former territories of the British Empire, of which Dominica is a member.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Name Change**: There is ongoing debate about changing the country's name from Dominica to Waitukubuli to better reflect Indigenous heritage and avoid confusion with the Dominican Republic. Proponents argue it strengthens local identity, while opponents cite the high cost.
- **Economic Citizenship Transparency**: The management and revenue use of the Citizenship by Investment programme have been frequent topics of domestic political controversy, with calls for greater transparency.
- **Territorial Dispute**: Dominica has a long-running dispute with Venezuela over the status of Isla de Aves, a tiny islet west of Dominica, though a statement by Prime Minister Skerrit in 2006 unofficially acknowledged Venezuelan sovereignty.

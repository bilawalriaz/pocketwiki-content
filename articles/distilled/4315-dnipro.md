# Dnipro

## Overview
Dnipro is Ukraine's fourth-largest city, located on the Dnipro River in eastern Ukraine. Its history reflects the region's complex past, from Cossack settlements and imperial Russian foundations to Soviet industrialization and its modern role as a strategic Ukrainian hub. The city's name and identity have changed multiple times, mirroring political shifts, and it remains a major industrial, logistical, and cultural center.

## Timeline
- **c. 1524** — Archaeological evidence suggests a Cossack trading settlement at the site.
- **1784** — Empress Catherine the Great orders the founding of Yekaterinoslav on the right bank of the Dnieper.
- **1926** — City renamed Dnipropetrovsk after Soviet leader Grigory Petrovsky.
- **1941-1943** — Under Nazi German occupation; the Holocaust reduces the Jewish population to 702.
- **1959** — City becomes a "closed city" due to its strategic missile industry (Yuzhmash).
- **1991** — 90.36% of voters approve Ukrainian independence in a referendum.
- **2014** — Euromaidan protests lead to a political shift away from pro-Russian parties.
- **2016** — City officially renamed Dnipro as part of decommunization.
- **2022** — Following Russia's full-scale invasion, Dnipro becomes a key humanitarian and logistical hub.

## Body

### Early History and Imperial Foundation
The area has been settled since the Paleolithic era. In the 17th century, the Cossack fortress of Kodak and the settlement of Novyi Kodak were established. After the liquidation of the Zaporozhian Sich in 1775, Empress Catherine the Great founded Yekaterinoslav ("Glory of Catherine") in 1784 as the administrative center of Novorossiya. Early growth was slow, but industrialization accelerated in the late 19th century with the discovery of the Kryvbas iron ore basin and the arrival of railways, attracting a multi-ethnic workforce.

### Soviet Era and Industrialization
Renamed Dnipropetrovsk in 1926, the city became a focus of Stalinist heavy industry. During the 1930s, its metallurgical plants produced a significant portion of the Ukrainian SSR's cast iron and steel. The city's population grew rapidly, and its ethnic composition shifted, with the Ukrainian percentage rising from 36% in 1926 to 54.6% in 1939. After World War II, the city was developed into a closed city for the Soviet space and arms race, centered on the Yuzhmash missile factory. This period also saw the rise of the "Dnipropetrovsk Mafia," a network of local elites who later gained national political influence.

### Modern Ukraine and the 2022 Invasion
Following independence, Dnipro's voters initially favored pro-Russian parties. The 2014 Euromaidan events triggered a political shift toward pro-European forces. In 2016, the city was renamed Dnipro, and extensive decommunization of street names and monuments followed. After the 2022 Russian invasion, Dnipro's strategic location made it a critical hub for humanitarian aid and military logistics. The city has been repeatedly targeted by Russian missile strikes, including a devastating attack on an apartment building in January 2023.

## Terms
*   **Hromada:** A Ukrainian term for a basic-level administrative unit, in this case, the Dnipro urban municipality.
*   **Korenizatsiya:** A Soviet policy of "nativization" in the 1920s-30s aimed at promoting local languages and cultures within the USSR.
*   **Holodomor:** The man-made famine in Soviet Ukraine (1932-33) that killed millions; Dnipropetrovsk Oblast was one of the most affected areas.
*   **Closed City:** A Soviet designation for a city with restricted access due to sensitive military or industrial facilities, as Dnipropetrovsk was from 1959 to 1987.
*   **Decommunization:** A Ukrainian process, accelerated by a 2015 law, to remove communist-era symbols and rename streets and cities.
*   **Yuzhmash:** A major Soviet and Ukrainian missile and space manufacturer, formally the Southern Machine-building Factory, founded in Dnipropetrovsk.
*   **Pale of Settlement:** The western region of the Russian Empire where Jewish residency was permitted, which included Yekaterinoslav from 1792.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Year of Foundation:** The official founding date is disputed. The Soviet-era date of 1776 was promoted for political reasons, while local historians argue for earlier Cossack settlements. As of December 2025, the Dnipro City Council was consulting experts to set a new official year.
*   **Political Identity:** The city's political trajectory has shifted dramatically, from a pro-Russian stronghold to a center of Ukrainian patriotism, a transition solidified by the 2014 Euromaidan and the 2022 invasion.
*   **Environmental Impact:** The city's heavy industrial base has led to significant air and water pollution, described by some sources as an environmental crisis.

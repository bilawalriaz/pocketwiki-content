# Danube

## Overview
The Danube is Europe's second-longest river, flowing 2,850 km from Germany's Black Forest to the Black Sea. It is a vital international waterway connecting ten countries, serving historically as a Roman frontier and today as a major trade route, source of drinking water, and ecological corridor.

## Timeline
- **c. 3000 BCE** — Vinča culture flourishes along the Danube.
- **336 BCE** — Alexander the Great advances to the Danube.
- **101-106 CE** — Roman Emperor Trajan conquers Dacia, crossing the Danube.
- **1396-1878** — Ottoman Empire competes with European powers for control of the river.
- **1856** — Paris Conference establishes international navigation regime.
- **1948** — Danube Commission established.
- **1991** — Danube Delta becomes a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
- **1992** — Rhine–Main–Danube Canal opens, linking North and Black Seas.
- **1998** — International Commission for the Protection of the Danube River (ICPDR) founded.
- **2026** — Record-low water levels expose historical wrecks and remains.

## Body

### Geography and Navigation
The Danube originates at the confluence of the Breg and Brigach rivers in Donaueschingen, Germany. It flows southeast through or along the borders of ten countries, passing four capitals: Vienna, Bratislava, Budapest, and Belgrade. Its drainage basin covers 817,000 km² and extends into nine additional countries. The river is divided into three main sections: Upper (to Devín Gate), Middle (to Iron Gates), and Lower (to the Black Sea). It is navigable for 2,415 km, with ocean ships reaching Brăila, Romania. The 1992 Rhine–Main–Danube Canal created a continuous waterway from Rotterdam to the Black Sea.

### Ecology and Economy
The Danube basin supports diverse fish species, including pike, catfish, and six sturgeon species, though sturgeon populations are severely threatened by dams and pollution. The river provides drinking water for about 20 million people. Its economic importance includes hydropower (e.g., Iron Gate dams), transport, and tourism, with popular cruise routes and cycling trails like the Danube Bike Trail. The Danube Delta, the EU's largest river delta, is a UNESCO site and Ramsar wetland, hosting over 300 bird species.

### History and Geology
Geologically, the Danube is older than the Rhine. Its upper course is an underfit stream; much of its water now flows underground to the Rhine via the Donauversickerung sinkholes. Historically, it was a Roman frontier (Danubian Limes) and a contested border between the Ottoman and Habsburg empires. In 2026, extreme drought exposed World War II shipwrecks, a mammoth skeleton, and the foundations of Constantine's Bridge.

## Terms
- **Danubian Limes**: The northeastern frontier of the Roman Empire along the Danube.
- **Drainage basin**: The area of land where all water drains into a river and its tributaries.
- **Euryhaline**: Fish that can tolerate a wide range of salinity, like those in the Danube Delta.
- **Huchen**: A large salmon species endemic to the Danube basin.
- **ICPDR**: International Commission for the Protection of the Danube River, coordinating basin-wide water management.
- **Iron Gates**: A gorge on the Danube forming the Serbia-Romania border, site of major hydroelectric dams.
- **Pan-European transport corridor**: A designated route in Central/Eastern Europe requiring infrastructure investment; the Danube is Corridor VII.
- **Stream capture**: A geological process where a river's flow is diverted to another channel, predicted for the upper Danube.
- **Zille**: A traditional flat-bottomed boat used on the upper Danube.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Name etymology**: The origin of the Romanian name "Dunărea" is debated; it may derive from a hypothetical Thracian *Donaris* or be a Turkic loanword.
- **Future of the upper Danube**: Geologists predict the upper Danube may eventually disappear entirely into underground channels, fully captured by the Rhine.
- **Ecological management**: The construction of dams like Iron Gates I and II, which lack fish passages, is a major point of contention regarding sturgeon conservation and river ecology.

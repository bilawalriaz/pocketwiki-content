# Josip Broz Tito

## Overview
Josip Broz Tito (1892–1980) was a Yugoslav communist revolutionary who led Yugoslavia from World War II until his death. He is significant for leading a successful partisan resistance, establishing a socialist state independent of Soviet control, and co-founding the Non-Aligned Movement, creating a unique "third way" during the Cold War.

## Timeline
- **7 May 1892** — Born in Kumrovec, Croatia-Slavonia, Austro-Hungarian Empire.
- **1913–1915** — Serves in Austro-Hungarian Army; wounded and captured by Russians in 1915.
- **1917** — Participates in the Russian Revolution after escaping a POW camp.
- **1920** — Returns to the Kingdom of Serbs, Croats and Slovenes; joins the Communist Party of Yugoslavia (CPY).
- **1937** — Becomes General Secretary of the CPY.
- **6 April 1941** — Axis invasion of Yugoslavia; Tito forms a military committee and leads the Partisan resistance.
- **29 November 1943** — AVNOJ session in Jajce establishes a provisional government with Tito as president.
- **28 June 1948** — Yugoslavia is expelled from the Cominform after the Tito–Stalin split.
- **14 January 1953** — Becomes President of Yugoslavia.
- **1961** — Co-founds the Non-Aligned Movement.
- **16 May 1974** — New constitution names him President for Life.
- **4 May 1980** — Dies in Ljubljana, Slovenia.

## Body

### Early Life and Revolutionary Path
Born to a Croat father and Slovene mother, Josip Broz worked as a metalworker before being conscripted into the Austro-Hungarian Army. Captured by Russia in 1915, he participated in the 1917 Russian Revolution and joined the Communist Party in 1920. Returning to the newly formed Kingdom of Yugoslavia, he became a professional revolutionary for the outlawed CPY, rising through its ranks despite frequent imprisonment. He adopted the pseudonym "Tito" in 1934 and survived Stalin's Great Purge in Moscow, where he was a Comintern official, to become the CPY's General Secretary in 1937.

### World War II Resistance
After the Axis invasion in 1941, Tito led the Yugoslav Partisans, a multi-ethnic communist resistance movement. The Partisans fought both the occupiers and the rival royalist Chetniks, establishing liberated territories and a provisional government (AVNOJ). By 1943, with Allied backing, the Partisans were recognized as Yugoslavia's legitimate government. Tito's leadership was crucial in uniting the country's diverse nations against fascism.

### Post-War Rule and the Tito–Stalin Split
After the war, Tito became Prime Minister and later President, establishing a communist state. Initially aligned with the Soviet Union, a major rift developed over Yugoslavia's independence. In 1948, after Tito refused to subordinate Yugoslav policy to Moscow, the Cominform expelled Yugoslavia. This "Tito–Stalin split" was the first major schism in the Eastern Bloc. Tito responded with internal repression of Stalinists but also sought Western economic aid, famously defying Stalin's assassination attempts.

### Non-Alignment and Domestic Policy
Tito pioneered the Non-Aligned Movement (1961), positioning Yugoslavia as a leader of nations independent of both Cold War superpowers. Domestically, he implemented "socialist self-management," a model of workplace democracy and profit-sharing. He balanced central authority with decentralization, granting significant autonomy to Yugoslavia's republics and provinces through successive constitutions, notably in 1974 when he was named President for Life. His rule combined economic liberalization and relative cultural openness with authoritarian control and suppression of nationalism.

### Death and Aftermath
Tito died in 1980 after a prolonged illness. His death removed the unifying figurehead of Yugoslavia. The complex power-sharing system he established proved unable to manage rising ethnic tensions, and within twelve years, the country dissolved into a series of brutal interethnic wars.

## Terms
- **Partisans**: The multi-ethnic communist resistance movement led by Tito against Axis occupation in Yugoslavia during WWII.
- **Cominform**: The Communist Information Bureau, an organization of communist parties from the Eastern Bloc, from which Yugoslavia was expelled in 1948.
- **Tito–Stalin split**: The political break between Yugoslavia and the Soviet Union in 1948, marking the first major division within the post-WWII communist bloc.
- **Non-Aligned Movement**: An international organization of states not formally aligned with or against any major power bloc, co-founded by Tito in 1961.
- **Socialist self-management**: Yugoslavia's unique economic system, introduced in the 1950s, where workers' councils managed socially-owned enterprises.
- **AVNOJ**: The Anti-Fascist Council of National Liberation of Yugoslavia, the wartime deliberative body that served as Yugoslavia's provisional government.
- **Goli Otok**: A political prison camp used from 1949 to 1956 for Stalinists and other political opponents after the split with the USSR.
- **Croatian Spring (Maspok)**: A political crisis in 1970–71 involving demands for greater Croatian autonomy and economic reform, which was suppressed by Tito.

## Debates and open questions
Historians debate whether Tito was a "benevolent dictator" who modernized Yugoslavia and maintained ethnic peace, or an authoritarian ruler who suppressed dissent and delayed necessary political reforms. His legacy is highly polarized in the former Yugoslav republics, viewed positively in Slovenia and Bosnia but more divisively in Croatia and Serbia. Scholars also analyze whether the decentralized 1974 constitution, intended to manage ethnic tensions, inadvertently created the structural weaknesses that made Yugoslavia's violent dissolution inevitable.

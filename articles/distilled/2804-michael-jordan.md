# Michael Jordan

## Overview
Michael Jordan is widely regarded as the greatest basketball player of all time. His career with the Chicago Bulls in the 1980s and 1990s, marked by six NBA championships and unparalleled individual dominance, was instrumental in globalizing the NBA. Beyond basketball, he became a cultural icon and a highly successful businessman, setting a new standard for athlete endorsements and wealth.

## Body

### Early Life and College Career
Michael Jeffrey Jordan was born in 1963 and raised in Wilmington, North Carolina. He was cut from his high school varsity basketball team as a sophomore for being too short, a motivation that fueled his development. He became a McDonald's All-American and accepted a scholarship to the University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill. As a freshman in 1982, he made the game-winning shot in the NCAA championship game. After winning the Naismith and Wooden College Player of the Year awards in 1984, he left college a year early for the NBA draft.

### Rise with the Chicago Bulls (1984–1990)
The Bulls selected Jordan with the third overall pick in 1984. He quickly became a star, winning Rookie of the Year. His early career was marked by prolific scoring, including a 63-point playoff game in 1986, and spectacular athleticism, earning him nicknames like "Air Jordan." Despite individual success, including his first MVP award in 1988, the Bulls were repeatedly eliminated from the playoffs by the Detroit Pistons' physical "Jordan Rules" defense.

### First Three-Peat and Retirement (1991–1995)
Under coach Phil Jackson, the Bulls implemented the triangle offense, which required Jordan to trust his teammates more. This led to the team's first championship in 1991, followed by titles in 1992 and 1993. Citing exhaustion and the murder of his father, Jordan abruptly retired in October 1993 to pursue a career in Minor League Baseball. He returned to the NBA in March 1995 but the Bulls were eliminated in the playoffs by the Orlando Magic.

### Second Three-Peat and Final Bulls Season (1996–1998)
Jordan returned for a full 1995–96 season, leading the Bulls to a then-record 72 wins and the NBA championship. The team won two more titles in 1997 and 1998, completing a second three-peat. The 1998 Finals concluded with Jordan's iconic game-winning shot in Game 6 against the Utah Jazz. He retired again in January 1999.

### Washington Wizards and Final Retirement (2001–2003)
After a stint as an executive for the Wizards, Jordan returned to play for the team from 2001 to 2003. He donated his salary to 9/11 relief efforts. Though no longer an All-Star caliber player, he was durable and occasionally dominant, becoming the first 40-year-old to score 43 points in a game. He retired for the final time in 2003.

### Post-Retirement and Business Career
Jordan became the majority owner of the Charlotte Bobcats (now Hornets) in 2010, the first former player to become a majority NBA team owner. He sold his majority stake in 2023. He co-owns the NASCAR team 23XI Racing. His most famous endorsement is the Air Jordan sneaker line with Nike, launched in 1984. His business acumen and endorsements have made him the wealthiest athlete in history, with a net worth estimated at $4.3 billion as of 2026.

## Terms
- **Triangle Offense:** An offensive basketball system implemented by coach Phil Jackson that emphasized spacing and ball movement, requiring stars like Jordan to share the ball.
- **"Jordan Rules":** A defensive strategy employed by the Detroit Pistons in the late 1980s to stop Michael Jordan by double-teaming him and forcing him to pass.
- **Three-peat:** Winning a championship in three consecutive years, achieved by Jordan's Bulls twice (1991–93 and 1996–98).
- **MVP (Most Valuable Player):** An annual award given to the best player in the NBA regular season. Jordan won it five times.
- **Finals MVP:** An award given to the best player in the NBA Finals series. Jordan won a record six times.
- **Triple Crown (Basketball):** Achieving an NCAA championship, an NBA championship, and an Olympic gold medal. Jordan did this twice.
- **Air Jordan:** The brand name for Michael Jordan's signature line of Nike basketball shoes, which became a cultural phenomenon.
- **Presidential Medal of Freedom:** The highest civilian honor in the United States, awarded to Jordan by President Barack Obama in 2016.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Greatest of All Time (GOAT):** While Jordan is widely considered the greatest basketball player, debates persist with other legends like LeBron James, Kareem Abdul-Jabbar, and Bill Russell.
- **First Retirement:** Persistent rumors suggested Jordan's first retirement in 1993 was a secret suspension by the NBA for gambling, a claim denied by then-commissioner David Stern and Jordan himself.
- **Executive Record:** Jordan's tenure as an NBA executive, particularly with the Charlotte Bobcats/Hornets, is viewed as mixed, highlighted by the team's historically poor 7–59 record in the 2011–12 season.

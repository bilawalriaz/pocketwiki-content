# Ion Antonescu

## Overview
Ion Antonescu was a Romanian military officer and marshal who ruled as Prime Minister and Conducător (leader) during most of World War II. He presided over a military dictatorship allied with Nazi Germany, which was responsible for the Holocaust in Romania, resulting in the deaths of up to 400,000 people. He was overthrown in a 1944 coup and executed for war crimes in 1946.

## Timeline
- **14 June 1882** — Born in Pitești, Romania.
- **1907** — Participated in suppressing the peasants' revolt.
- **1916-1918** — Served as chief of staff for General Prezan during World War I.
- **1933-1934** — Chief of the Romanian General Staff.
- **1937-1938** — Defence Minister in the cabinets of Octavian Goga and Miron Cristea.
- **5 September 1940** — Appointed Prime Minister after King Carol II's abdication.
- **14 September 1940** — National Legionary State proclaimed, a coalition with the Iron Guard.
- **January 1941** — Crushed the Legionary Rebellion, eliminating the Iron Guard as a political force.
- **22 June 1941** — Romania joined Operation Barbarossa, the invasion of the Soviet Union.
- **22 August 1941** — Promoted to Marshal of Romania.
- **23 August 1944** — Overthrown and arrested in King Michael's Coup.
- **1 June 1946** — Executed for war crimes.

## Body

### Rise to Power
Antonescu was a career army officer who gained prominence during World War I. His antisemitic and far-right sympathies brought him into conflict with King Carol II in the late 1930s, leading to his internment. During the political crisis of 1940, following territorial losses to the Soviet Union, Hungary, and Bulgaria, Antonescu was appointed Prime Minister. He forced Carol II to abdicate and was named Conducător, establishing an uneasy coalition government with the fascist Iron Guard, known as the National Legionary State.

### Alliance with Nazi Germany and the Holocaust
Antonescu aligned Romania with Nazi Germany, participating in the invasion of the Soviet Union to recover Bessarabia and Northern Bukovina. He also committed Romanian forces to occupy Transnistria, a territory beyond pre-war borders. His regime was independently responsible for the Holocaust in Romania, implementing policies of ethnic cleansing, pogroms (like the Iași pogrom), and mass deportations to Transnistria. While the Jewish population in the Old Kingdom largely survived, hundreds of thousands of Jews and Romani from annexed territories and Moldavia were killed. Historians note the regime's policies were inconsistent, prioritizing plunder and showing some leniency in certain areas, but were fundamentally driven by Antonescu's antisemitism and anti-Slavic racism.

### Downfall and Execution
Heavy military losses on the Eastern Front, particularly at Stalingrad, and Allied bombings of Romania weakened Antonescu's position. He began secret peace negotiations with the Allies but refused to break with Germany. On 23 August 1944, King Michael I led a coup, arresting Antonescu. Romania switched sides to the Allies. After the war, Antonescu was tried by a People's Tribunal for war crimes, crimes against peace, and treason. He was found guilty and executed by firing squad on 1 June 1946.

### Legacy and Historical Reassessment
Antonescu's legacy has been deeply contested. During the communist era, his role in the Holocaust was downplayed or denied, and he was sometimes rehabilitated as a nationalist figure. After the 1989 Revolution, he became a popular figure for some, with calls for his rehabilitation. The 2003 Wiesel Commission officially confirmed his regime's central role in the Holocaust, leading to the condemnation of his crimes and the removal of public monuments. His trial and conviction have also been subjects of legal and political debate.

## Terms
*   **Conducător:** Romanian for "leader," the title Antonescu used for his dictatorial role.
*   **National Legionary State:** The coalition government (September 1940 – January 1941) between Antonescu's military dictatorship and the fascist Iron Guard.
*   **Iron Guard:** A Romanian fascist, antisemitic, and ultranationalist movement.
*   **Transnistria:** A territory between the Dniester and Southern Bug rivers, annexed and administered by Romania during the war, where many deportees were killed.
*   **Operation Barbarossa:** The code name for the Axis invasion of the Soviet Union, launched in June 1941.
*   **People's Tribunal:** Post-war courts in Romania established to try war criminals, modeled on the Nuremberg Trials.
*   **Wiesel Commission:** An international commission of historians established in 2003 to study the Holocaust in Romania, whose report confirmed Romanian authorities' responsibility.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Degree of Autonomy:** Historians debate the extent to which Antonescu's policies, especially regarding the Holocaust, were independently driven versus influenced or pressured by Nazi Germany. The source notes Romania preserved political autonomy and its policies had "singular inconsistencies."
*   **Motivations for War:** Antonescu's primary geopolitical goal was recovering Northern Transylvania from Hungary. His decision to wage a war of aggression beyond pre-war borders into Transnistria is a key point of criticism and historical analysis.
*   **Post-Communist Rehabilitation:** The reasons for Antonescu's popularity in post-1989 Romania are debated, often linked to nationalist sentiment, "fantasies of persecution," and the legacy of communist-era historical revisionism.
*   **Legal Legacy:** The 2006 Bucharest Court of Appeal's decision to overturn Antonescu's conviction for crimes against peace (later reversed by the Supreme Court) sparked international debate about the legal justification of his actions in 1940.

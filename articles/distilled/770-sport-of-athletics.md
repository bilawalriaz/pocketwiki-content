# Sport of athletics

## Overview
Athletics is a collection of competitive human physical-feat events centered on running, jumping, and throwing. The most common forms are track and field, road running, cross-country running, and race walking. Its simplicity and minimal equipment needs make it one of the world's most widespread sports. Organized athletics trace back to the ancient Olympic Games of 776 BC, with modern rules standardized in 19th- and 20th-century Western Europe and North America, then spread globally under World Athletics, the sport's international governing body.

## Timeline
- **c. 2250 BC**: Running and high jumping depicted in Egyptian tombs at Saqqara.
- **c. 1800 BC**: Tailteann Games founded in Ireland, featuring running and stone-throwing.
- **776 BC**: First ancient Olympic Games held; stadion (stadium-length) running is the sole event.
- **1850**: Wenlock Olympian Games in England incorporate athletics events.
- **1857**: First modern athletics clubs founded at Cambridge and Oxford universities.
- **1880**: Amateur Athletic Association (AAA) established in England.
- **1896**: Athletics included in first modern Olympic Games in Athens.
- **1912**: International Amateur Athletics Federation (IAAF) founded.
- **1922**: Comité International Sports des Sourds formed to govern international deaf sports, including athletics.
- **1928**: Women's athletics events introduced at the Olympics.
- **1952**: First international Stoke Mandeville Games organized for athletes with physical disabilities.
- **1960**: First Paralympic Games held, including athletics.
- **1983**: First IAAF World Championships in Athletics held.
- **2001**: IAAF renamed International Association of Athletics Federations.
- **2019**: IAAF renamed World Athletics.

## Body

### History
Athletics events are among the oldest sports, with prehistoric roots. Ancient Egyptian tombs from 2250 BC depict running and high jumping. The Tailteann Games in Ireland (c. 1800 BC) included running and stone-throwing. The first ancient Olympics in 776 BC featured only the stadion race, later expanding to include throwing and jumping in the pentathlon. Other Panhellenic Games emerged around 500 BC.

The modern era began in 17th-century England with the Cotswold Olympic Games featuring sledgehammer throwing. The 1796 L'Olympiade de la République in France introduced the metric system. Organized competitions appeared at military academies and schools in the early 19th century. The first modern athletics clubs formed at Cambridge (1857) and Oxford (1860). The Amateur Athletic Association (1880) and similar bodies codified rules. Athletics joined the 1896 modern Olympics and has remained central since. The IAAF (1912) enforced amateur sport status on competitions during much of the 20th century. Professional competition continued at a low level, becoming increasingly more common as the century progressed. The International Track Association briefly formed a professional track and field circuit in the United States in the 1970s. Athletes used their increasing status to push for remuneration and the IAAF responded with the IAAF Golden Events series and the establishment of an outdoor World Championships in 1983, including track and field, racewalking and a marathon event. In modern times, athletes can receive money for racing, putting an end to the so-called "amateurism" that existed before.

### Events
World Athletics defines six disciplines: track and field, road running, race walking, cross country running, mountain running (added 2003), and trail running (added 2015). All are individual sports except relays, though team scoring occurs in cross country and road races. Additional forms exist outside World Athletics governance, including skyrunning (ISF), stair climbing (vertical/tower running), snowshoe running, and ultrarunning. Athletics also contributes to triathlon and modern pentathlon.

Track and field competitions emerged in the late 19th century between educational institutions, military organizations, and clubs. Events include sprints, middle-distance, long-distance, hurdles, steeplechase, relays, jumping (long jump, triple jump, high jump, pole vault), and throwing (shot put, discus, javelin, hammer). Combined events include decathlon (men) and heptathlon (women). Indoor and outdoor formats exist, with most indoor meets in winter and outdoor in summer.

Road running involves long-distance events on paved courses, with marathons being the most prominent. The 1896 Summer Olympics saw the birth of the modern marathon and the event led to the growth of road running competitions through annual events such as the Boston Marathon (first held in 1897) and the Lake Biwa Marathon and Fukuoka Marathons, which were established in the 1940s. Major marathons include New York, London, Chicago, Berlin, and Tokyo. The World Marathon Majors series features these six races. Ekiden relays are popular in Japan.

Cross country running takes place on natural surfaces like grass and trails, combining individual and team competition. The first organized event was the Crick Run in 1838. The World Cross Country Championships began in 1973. Mountain running is a variation with significant elevation changes.

Race walking requires one foot always in contact with the ground and a straight advancing leg. Originating from 18th-century English pedestrianism, it features 10 km, 20 km, and 50 km distances on roads, although women's 3 km and men's 5 km are held on indoor tracks. The 1908 Summer Olympics in London saw the introduction of the 3500-metre and 10-mile walks. The World Race Walking Cup has been held since 1961, and the IAAF World Race Walking Challenge forms the primary seasonal competition.

### Categories
Athletes are categorized by sex, age, disability, and sometimes nationality or occupation. Sex divisions have faced eligibility disputes, particularly for women. Sex verification began in 1950 after Foekje Dillema's refusal to test. The IAAF replaced sex chromatin tests with general medical exams in 1991. Controversies continue with intersex athletes like Caster Semenya and Dutee Chand, and transgender participation. The UN criticized IAAF's testosterone regulations in 2019.

Age categories include under-23, under-20, under-18, and masters (35+). Age falsification has occurred, notably Thomas Longosiwa's falsified passport in 2006. Masters records include Stanisław Kowalski (105+).

Disability categories use classifications: T/F codes with numbers (11-13 visual impairment, 20 intellectual disability, 31-38 cerebral palsy, 40-46 amputation, 51-58 wheelchair). The Paralympics began in 1960. Athletes with disabilities occasionally compete against able-bodied competitors, such as Oscar Pistorius and Marla Runyan.

### Venues
Professional athletics occurs in stadiums, grass/woodland courses, and road courses. Standard outdoor tracks are 400 meters with at least eight 1.22-meter lanes. Indoor tracks are 200 meters with four to eight lanes. Cross country courses vary by environment but must be looped (1750-2000m laps) at elite level. Road courses use man-made surfaces and come in looped or point-to-point configurations.

### Organizations
World Athletics (formerly IAAF, 1912-2019) has 215 member nations divided into six continental associations: Asian (AAA), African (CAA), European (EAA), Oceania (OAA), North/Central America/Caribbean (NACACAA), and South American (CONSUDATLE). National bodies regulate domestic competition.

### Competitions
Three types exist: international championships, national championships, and annual meetings. International championships are the pinnacle, with athletes selected by national governing bodies. The Olympics are the most prestigious, featuring 47 events (24 men, 23 women as of London 2012). The World Championships (since 1983) mirror the Olympic program. Other world championships include Indoor, Cross Country, Half Marathon, Race Walking, U20, and Masters. The Paralympics and World Para Championships serve athletes with disabilities.

## Terms
- **Stadion**: Ancient Olympic running event covering one stadium length (~192 meters).
- **Pentathlon**: Ancient Greek combined event of running, long jump, discus, javelin, and wrestling.
- **Decathlon**: Men's combined event of ten track and field disciplines.
- **Heptathlon**: Women's combined event of seven track and field disciplines.
- **Amateurism**: Historical rule requiring athletes to compete without payment, enforced by IAAF until late 20th century.
- **Classification**: System grouping athletes with disabilities by impairment type and severity.
- **Ekiden**: Japanese marathon relay race variation.
- **Diamond League**: Prestigious annual track and field competition series.
- **World Marathon Majors**: Elite marathon series featuring six major city races.
- **Masters athletics**: Competition category for athletes aged 35 and above in five-year age groups.

## Debates and open questions
Eligibility for the women's division remains contentious, particularly regarding intersex athletes and testosterone regulations. The IAAF's policies have faced criticism from the UN Human Rights Council and athletes' rights advocates, who cite harm to social well-being and test inaccuracies. Conversely, supporters argue regulations prevent countries from recruiting athletes with differences in sex development for competitive advantage. Transgender participation in women's events has also raised fairness concerns. Age verification in youth and masters categories faces challenges in regions with poor documentation. Athletes with disabilities occasionally compete against able-bodied competitors, raising questions about fair competition and classification accuracy.

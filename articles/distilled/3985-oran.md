# Oran

## Overview
Oran is Algeria's second-largest city and a major Mediterranean port, historically significant as a crossroads of Berber, Arab, Spanish, French, and Ottoman influences. Its strategic location made it a coveted prize for empires, shaping a complex history of conquest, cultural exchange, and conflict that culminated in its role during the Algerian War of Independence.

## Body

### Etymology and Founding
The city's name derives from the Berber expression *wa-iharan*, meaning "place of lions," linked to legends of Barbary lion sightings around AD 900. Present-day Oran was founded in 903 by Berbers.

### Islamic and Spanish Rule
Oran prospered under the Almohad Caliphate and later the Zayyanid dynasty of Tlemcen. It was captured by Spain in 1509, initiating a period of Spanish sovereignty (1509–1708, 1732–1792) marked by fortification building and conflict with regional powers. A devastating earthquake in 1790 weakened Spanish control, leading to its final recapture by Algerian forces in 1792.

### French Colonial Period (1831–1962)
France seized Oran in 1831. Under colonial rule, the city saw significant European settlement, becoming a majority-European city by the early 20th century. A discriminatory system favored Jewish residents with citizenship and opportunities while dispossessing Muslims. During WWII, Oran was a key landing site for the Allied Operation Torch in 1942.

### Independence and Modern Era
Following the 1962 ceasefire, the Oran massacre triggered a mass exodus of *pieds-noirs* (European settlers) and Algerian Jews to France. Post-independence, the city was rebuilt and industrialized. Today, it is a major commercial and cultural hub, hosting universities, a tramway system, and the 2022 Mediterranean Games.

### Culture and Economy
Oran is the birthplace of *Raï*, a popular music genre blending Arab and European influences that emerged in the 1930s. The city's economy is centered on trade, serving nearby oil/gas ports like Arzew, and tourism, with attractions including the historic Sidi El Houari district and the Great Mosque (built 1796).

## Terms
- ***Pieds-noirs***: French settlers and their descendants who lived in Algeria before independence.
- ***Beylik***: A territory or administrative division under the rule of a *bey*, an Ottoman governor.
- ***Raï***: A genre of Algerian folk music that originated in Oran, often featuring controversial themes.
- ***Maghrawa***: A Berber confederation that founded Oran in the 10th century.
- ***Operation Torch***: The Allied amphibious invasion of French North Africa in November 1942.
- ***Caliphate***: An Islamic state led by a caliph, a successor to the Prophet Muhammad.
- ***Casbah***: A citadel or old quarter in a North African city.
- ***Desalination***: The process of removing salt from seawater to make it drinkable.

## Debates and Open Questions
The exact death toll of the 1962 Oran massacre is debated, with estimates ranging from at least 95 to as many as 365 killed, with many others reported to have "disappeared." The city's name origin, while most plausibly linked to "lions," is part of broader scholarly discussion on Berber toponymy in North Africa.

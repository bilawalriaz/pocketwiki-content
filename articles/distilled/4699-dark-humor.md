# Dark humor

## Overview
Dark humor is a comedic style that makes light of serious, taboo, or painful subjects to provoke thought and discomfort. It serves as a coping mechanism and a tool for social commentary, with roots traceable to ancient literature and a formal definition emerging in the 20th century.

## Body

### Definition and Etymology
Dark humor, also called black comedy or gallows humor, makes light of subjects generally considered taboo or serious. The term "black humor" was coined by Surrealist theorist André Breton in 1935, derived from the French *humour noir*. Breton formalized the concept in his 1940 book *Anthology of Black Humor*, where he identified Jonathan Swift as its originator. The genre is distinct from blue comedy (focused on sex and nudity) and obscenity. While "dark humor" is a broad term, "gallows humor" specifically relates to death or dying situations.

### History and Literary Examples
Dark humor has ancient roots, with literary critics associating it with authors like Aristophanes. A famous early example is the story of Saint Lawrence, a 3rd-century martyr who allegedly joked, "Turn me over. I'm done on this side," while being burned alive. In modern literature, writers like Nathanael West and Vladimir Nabokov were among the first American practitioners. The genre gained widespread attention in the U.S. with Bruce Jay Friedman's 1965 anthology, which labeled authors such as Joseph Heller, Thomas Pynchon, and Kurt Vonnegut as black humorists. Evelyn Waugh is noted as "the first contemporary writer to produce the sustained black comic novel."

### Nature and Social Function
Psychologist Sigmund Freud, in his 1927 essay *Humor*, argued that the ego uses humor to refuse distress from external trauma, turning it into a source of pleasure. Sociologists like Paul Lewis note this "relieving" effect depends on context, especially whether the joke-teller is the threatened person. Socially, black comedy can strengthen the morale of the oppressed and undermine oppressors. It is a common coping mechanism in high-stress professions like emergency services, the military, and law enforcement, where it is used to manage exposure to dark subject matter. The German term *Galgenhumor* (cynical last words before hanging) and the French *rire jaune* ("yellow laughing") describe this type of laughter.

## Terms
- **Dark Humor / Black Comedy**: A style of comedy that treats serious, taboo, or painful subjects in a humorous way.
- **Gallows Humor**: A specific form of dark humor that deals directly with death or situations reminiscent of dying.
- **Blue Comedy**: Comedy focused on topics like nudity, sex, and body fluids, distinct from dark humor.
- **Humour Noir**: The French term for black humor, coined by André Breton.
- **Galgenhumor**: A German expression for cynical last words or humor in the face of death.
- **Rire Jaune / Groen Lachen**: French and Belgian Dutch terms for "yellow laughing" or "green laughing," describing the specific laughter aroused by grotesque satire.

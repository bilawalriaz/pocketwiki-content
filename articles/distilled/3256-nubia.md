# Nubia

## Overview
Nubia is a region along the Nile River between the confluence of the Blue and White Niles (near Khartoum, Sudan) and the First Cataract (south of Aswan, Egypt). It was the seat of one of Africa's earliest civilizations and home to several powerful empires, most notably the Kingdom of Kush, which conquered Egypt and ruled as its 25th Dynasty. The region's history is defined by its complex interactions with Egypt, its development of unique cultures, and its eventual division between modern Egypt and Sudan.

## Timeline
- **c. 2500–1500 BC** — Kerma culture flourishes in Upper Nubia.
- **c. 1500 BC** — New Kingdom of Egypt conquers Nubia under Pharaoh Thutmose I.
- **c. 750–590 BC** — Kingdom of Kush (25th Dynasty) rules Egypt.
- **275 BC** — Ptolemaic Egypt annexes northern Nubia (Dodekaschoinos).
- **25 BC** — Kushite Queen Amanirenas attacks Roman Egypt; Romans sack Napata.
- **c. 350 AD** — Kingdom of Aksum invades, leading to the collapse of Meroë.
- **c. 550–1400 AD** — Christian kingdoms of Nobatia, Makuria, and Alodia dominate Nubia.
- **1317** — Cathedral of Dongola is converted to a mosque.
- **1504** — Last Nubian kingdom collapses; region is partitioned.
- **1953–1956** — Nubia is divided between the newly independent Egypt and Sudan.

## Body

### Geography and Early History
Nubia is divided into Lower (north), Middle, and Upper (south) Nubia along the Nile. Archaeological evidence shows human presence from the Late Pleistocene, with sophisticated pottery produced by Mesolithic fisher-hunter-gatherers by the 9th millennium BC. By 5000 BC, populations participated in the Neolithic Revolution, domesticating animals. Megaliths at Nabta Playa (c. 5000 BC) are among the world's earliest astronomical devices. Scholars debate the relationship between early Nubian and Egyptian cultures, with some arguing for significant Nubian contributions to the foundations of pharaonic civilization.

### The A-Group and Kerma Culture
The A-Group culture (c. 3500–3000 BC) in Lower Nubia traded with Egypt and produced distinctive pottery. Royal burials at Qustul rivaled those of early Egyptian pharaohs, leading to debate about shared royal symbols and the origins of pharaonic iconography. After a hiatus, the Kerma culture emerged in Upper Nubia. By the Classic Kerma phase (c. 1650 BC), it was a powerful, militaristic kingdom with monumental architecture and rich royal tombs. Kerma nearly conquered Egypt during its Second Intermediate Period before being defeated by the New Kingdom.

### Egyptian Rule and the Kingdom of Kush
Egypt controlled Nubia from c. 1500 to 1070 BC, building fortresses and exploiting its gold. After Egyptian withdrawal, a unified Kushite state emerged at Napata. Kushite kings, deeply influenced by Egyptian culture, conquered Egypt in the 8th century BC, ruling as the 25th Dynasty. They promoted a religious and architectural revival (archaism) until expelled by the Assyrians around 590 BC. The Kushite kingdom then shifted its capital south to Meroë.

### The Meroitic Period
From c. 542 BC to 400 AD, the Kingdom of Kush was centered at Meroë. It developed its own script (Meroitic), worshipped a blend of Egyptian and local gods, and had a strong iron industry. Powerful queens (Kandakes) ruled, including Amanirenas, who fought a war with Rome in 25 BC. Meroë was eventually weakened by internal strife and defeated by the Kingdom of Aksum around 350 AD.

### Christian and Islamic Nubia
After Meroë's collapse, three Christian kingdoms arose: Nobatia, Makuria, and Alodia. Makuria became dominant and signed the Baqt treaty with Muslim rulers in the 7th century, ensuring centuries of peaceful coexistence. From the 14th century, Arab migrations and political fragmentation led to the gradual Islamization and Arabization of Nubia. The region was partitioned in the 16th century between the Ottoman Empire and the Sennar Sultanate, later reunified under Egyptian control in the 19th century.

## Terms
- **Nubiology**: The interdisciplinary study of ancient Nubia, combining archaeology, history, and philology.
- **Kerma culture**: One of the earliest urban civilizations in the Nile region, centered at Kerma in Upper Nubia (c. 2500–1500 BC).
- **Kingdom of Kush**: A powerful Nubian empire that ruled from Napata and later Meroë, and conquered Egypt as the 25th Dynasty.
- **Meroitic**: The language and script of the Kingdom of Kush during the Meroitic period (c. 300 BC–400 AD).
- **Kandake**: The Meroitic term for the sister of the king of Kush, who, due to matrilineal succession, was the queen mother and heir-bearer.
- **Baqt**: A treaty signed c. 7th century AD between the Christian kingdom of Makuria and Muslim rulers of Egypt, ensuring peaceful trade and coexistence for centuries.
- **Dodekaschoinos**: The "twelve-mile land," a territory in northern Nubia annexed by Ptolemaic Egypt in 275 BC.

## Debates and Open Questions
Scholars debate the origins of the 25th Dynasty Kushite kings, with theories ranging from them being Egyptianized Nubian officials to descendants of elite families. The relationship between the A-Group culture and early Egyptian civilization is contested, particularly regarding the origin of pharaonic iconography found at Qustul. The cause of Meroë's collapse is also debated, with theories including invasion by Aksum, economic decline, or internal rebellion. Finally, the precise linguistic identity of the ancient Kerma culture remains uncertain, with proposals for both Cushitic and Eastern Sudanic affiliations.

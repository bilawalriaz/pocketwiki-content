# World War II

## Overview
World War II (1939–1945) was a global conflict between the Allies and Axis powers, becoming the deadliest war in history with 60–75 million deaths, mostly civilians. It arose from unresolved post-WWI tensions, fascist expansionism in Europe, and Japanese militarism in Asia. The war reshaped global politics, ending European dominance and ushering in U.S.-Soviet superpower rivalry, decolonization, and the Cold War.

## Body

### Causes and Background
WWII stemmed from unresolved grievances after WWI. The Treaty of Versailles imposed harsh penalties on Germany, fostering revanchism. Fascism rose in Europe under Hitler and Mussolini, while Japan pursued militaristic expansion in Asia. The League of Nations proved ineffective in preventing aggression, such as Japan's invasion of Manchuria (1931) and Italy's invasion of Abyssinia (1935).

### Early Axis Expansion (1939–1941)
Germany's invasion of Poland on 1 September 1939 triggered declarations of war from Britain and France. The Soviet Union invaded eastern Poland under the Molotov-Ribbentrop Pact. Germany rapidly conquered Scandinavia, the Low Countries, and France by mid-1940. Italy joined the war but faced setbacks in North Africa and Greece. In Asia, Japan escalated its war with China, culminating in the attack on Pearl Harbor in December 1941, bringing the U.S. into the conflict.

### Turning Points (1942–1943)
The tide turned decisively against the Axis. Japan's defeat at Midway (June 1942) halted its Pacific expansion. In North Africa, Allied victories pushed Axis forces back. On the Eastern Front, the Soviet victory at Stalingrad (February 1943) marked the beginning of Germany's retreat. The Allies invaded Sicily and mainland Italy, leading to Mussolini's downfall.

### Allied Victory (1944–1945)
The D-Day landings in June 1944 opened a Western Front. The Soviets advanced into Eastern Europe, capturing Berlin in April 1945. Hitler committed suicide, and Germany surrendered in May. In the Pacific, the U.S. captured key islands, leading to the atomic bombings of Hiroshima and Nagasaki. Japan surrendered on 2 September 1945.

### Aftermath and Global Transformation
The war resulted in massive casualties and widespread destruction. The United Nations was established to promote peace. Europe was divided into Western and Soviet spheres, initiating the Cold War. Decolonization swept Africa and Asia. The U.S. and USSR emerged as superpowers, while Germany and Japan underwent reconstruction under Allied occupation.

## Terms
- **Blitzkrieg**: German military strategy using rapid combined arms to bypass strongpoints and encircle enemies.
- **Holocaust**: Systematic genocide of ~6 million Jews and millions of others by Nazi Germany.
- **Total War**: Military strategy mobilizing entire societies and economies for war effort.
- **Lend-Lease**: U.S. program providing military aid to Allied nations before direct involvement.
- **Operation Barbarossa**: Code name for Germany's 1941 invasion of the Soviet Union.
- **Atlantic Charter**: Joint declaration by Roosevelt and Churchill outlining post-war goals.
- **Iron Curtain**: Term describing Soviet-imposed division of Europe after the war.
- **Decolonization**: Process of former colonies gaining independence, accelerated post-WWII.
- **Cold War**: Period of geopolitical tension between the U.S. and Soviet Union.
- **Nuremberg Trials**: International tribunals prosecuting Axis leaders for war crimes.

## Debates and Open Questions
Historians debate the precise start date of WWII, with some citing Japan's 1931 invasion of Manchuria or the 1936 Spanish Civil War. The war's end date is also contested—V-J Day (15 August 1945) versus formal surrender (2 September 1945). Scholars continue discussing whether the atomic bombings were necessary to end the war, and debate the extent to which economic factors versus ideological expansionism drove Axis aggression. The effectiveness of appeasement policies and the role of the League of Nations remain contentious topics.

# Luxembourg

## Overview
Luxembourg is a small, landlocked Western European country with a unique history as a fortress state and a modern identity as a wealthy, multilingual financial hub. It is the world's only remaining sovereign grand duchy and a founding member of major international organizations like the EU and NATO. Its strategic location has made it a crossroads of cultures and a frequent subject of territorial disputes, shaping its path to full independence and its current trilingual society.

- **2020** — Luxembourg becomes the first country to offer free public transport nationwide.

## Body

### History and Formation
The region's recorded history begins in 963 with the acquisition of Luxembourg Castle. The County of Luxembourg grew in power within the Holy Roman Empire, culminating in Henry VII becoming Holy Roman Emperor in 1312. A succession crisis in 1443 ended the native dynasty, leading to nearly 400 years of foreign rule under the Houses of Burgundy and Habsburg. The territory was repeatedly partitioned and fought over by European powers. After the Napoleonic Wars, the Congress of Vienna (1815) restored it as a Grand Duchy in personal union with the Netherlands. Full independence and modern borders were established by the Treaty of London in 1839. The Luxembourg Crisis of 1867 reaffirmed its neutrality. A national awakening in the 19th century solidified a distinct Luxembourgish identity, culminating in the end of the personal union with the Netherlands in 1890. Luxembourg's neutrality was violated by Germany in both World Wars, leading to significant suffering and a post-war shift away from neutrality, joining NATO in 1949 and becoming a core EU member.

### Government and Politics
Luxembourg is a representative democracy and constitutional monarchy, headed by a Grand Duke. Executive power is held by the Grand Duke and a cabinet led by a Prime Minister. Legislative power resides in the unicameral Chamber of Deputies (60 members), advised by the appointed Council of State. The judiciary is independent, with a system of lower, district, and superior courts. The country is divided into 12 cantons and 100 communes. Luxembourg is a strong advocate for European integration and hosts key EU institutions, including the Court of Justice of the European Union. It maintains close ties with Belgium and the Netherlands through the Benelux union.

### Economy
Luxembourg has a highly developed, stable market economy with one of the world's highest GDPs per capita (PPP). Historically driven by steel, its economy has shifted to services, especially banking and finance. It is a leading global investment fund center and private banking hub. The government has also attracted tech companies and focuses on developing a knowledge economy. The labor market relies heavily on cross-border commuters from neighboring countries. Luxembourg has faced scrutiny as a tax haven but has adopted international standards on tax transparency.

### Society and Culture
Luxembourg is a multilingual and multicultural society. The 1984 Language Regimen Act established Luxembourgish as the national language, while French is the sole language of legislation, and both French and German are used administratively. The population includes a large proportion of foreign nationals, with Portuguese being the largest immigrant group. The education system is trilingual, transitioning from Luxembourgish to German in primary school and to French in secondary school. Culture reflects influences from France and Germany, with notable contributions in arts, media (e.g., RTL Group), and cuisine. The historic city of Luxembourg is a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

## Terms
*   **Grand Duchy:** A sovereign state headed by a monarch with the title of Grand Duke or Grand Duchess. Luxembourg is the world's only remaining one.
*   **Benelux:** A political and economic union of three neighboring countries: Belgium, the Netherlands, and Luxembourg.
*   **Personal Union:** A situation where two or more states share the same monarch but are otherwise independent. Luxembourg was in personal union with the Netherlands from 1815 to 1890.
*   **Luxembourgish:** A West Central Germanic language and the sole national language of Luxembourg, distinct from but related to German dialects.
*   **Chamber of Deputies:** The unicameral legislative body of Luxembourg, consisting of 60 directly elected members.
*   **Council of State:** An advisory body to the Chamber of Deputies in drafting legislation, with members appointed by the Grand Duke.
*   **Purchasing Power Parity (PPP):** An economic metric that adjusts GDP per capita for differences in the cost of living between countries, allowing for more accurate comparisons of living standards.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Language Future:** There is ongoing debate about the long-term status of Luxembourgish and German due to the strong influence of French in administration, business, and daily life, with concerns about potential "francization."
*   **Tax Haven Status:** Despite reforms, Luxembourg continues to face international scrutiny and debate over its role as a financial center and its corporate tax practices, as highlighted by the LuxLeaks scandal.
*   **Economic Model:** The country's economic growth model, heavily reliant on cross-border workers and the financial sector, raises questions about sustainability, tax revenue sharing with border regions, and social cohesion.

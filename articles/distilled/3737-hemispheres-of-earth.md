# Hemispheres of Earth

## Overview
Hemispheres of Earth are any division of the globe into two equal halves, used to study geographic, cultural, and demographic distributions. The most common divisions are the Northern and Southern Hemispheres, split by the Equator, and the Eastern and Western Hemispheres, split by the Prime Meridian. Alternative schemes can be based on cultural, religious, or geographic features.

## Timeline
- **c. 3000 BC** — Early civilizations begin conceptualizing the Earth as a sphere.
- **3rd century BC** — Eratosthenes calculates Earth's circumference, aiding the concept of hemispheres.
- **1884** — The International Meridian Conference establishes the Prime Meridian at Greenwich, formalizing the East-West division.

## Body

### Geographical Hemispheres
The primary geographical division splits Earth into the Northern and Southern Hemispheres along the Equator (0° latitude). The Eastern and Western Hemispheres are divided by the Prime Meridian (0° longitude) and its antimeridian (180° longitude). This East-West division is a convention, as the Prime Meridian is an arbitrary line.

### Alternative Hemispheres
Hemispheres can be defined by cultural or religious lines, such as the "Christian world" and "Islamic world." They can also be defined to maximize a geographic feature. The Land Hemisphere, centered near 47°N, 1°E (near Nantes, France), contains the largest possible area of land. The Water Hemisphere, centered near 47°S, 179°W (in the South Pacific Ocean), contains the largest possible area of water.

### Continents and Oceans by Hemisphere
The continents and oceans are distributed unevenly across the hemispheres. For example, Africa and Europe are primarily in the Northern Hemisphere, while South America is mostly in the Southern Hemisphere. The Pacific Ocean is the largest ocean and spans both the Eastern and Western Hemispheres.

## Terms
- **Hemisphere**: Any division of the globe into two equal halves.
- **Equator**: The imaginary line of latitude at 0°, dividing the Earth into Northern and Southern Hemispheres.
- **Prime Meridian**: The line of longitude at 0°, established in 1884, used as the basis for the Eastern and Western Hemispheres.
- **Antimeridian**: The line of longitude at 180°, which, with the Prime Meridian, divides the Eastern and Western Hemispheres.
- **Land Hemisphere**: The hemisphere containing the maximum possible land area, centered near France.
- **Water Hemisphere**: The hemisphere containing the maximum possible water area, centered in the South Pacific Ocean.

## Debates and Open questions
The division of the Eastern and Western Hemispheres is a cartographic convention with no single "correct" meridian. Alternative hemisphere schemes, like the Land and Water Hemispheres, are conceptual tools rather than standard geographical divisions. The "Global North" and "Global South" are socioeconomic and political concepts that do not align perfectly with the geographical hemispheres.

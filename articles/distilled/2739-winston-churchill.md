# Winston Churchill

## Overview
Winston Churchill was a British statesman who served as Prime Minister during the Second World War and again in the early 1950s. He is remembered as a defiant wartime leader who rallied Britain against Nazi Germany and as a key architect of the post-war world order. His long career spanned over six decades, during which he held numerous cabinet positions, switched political parties, and shaped British domestic and foreign policy.

## Timeline
- **30 November 1874** — Born at Blenheim Palace, Oxfordshire.
- **1895** — Commissioned as a second lieutenant in the British Army.
- **1900** — Elected Conservative MP for Oldham.
- **31 May 1904** — Defected from the Conservative Party to the Liberal Party.
- **1908–1910** — Served as President of the Board of Trade, championing social reforms.
- **1911–1915** — Served as First Lord of the Admiralty; oversaw the disastrous Dardanelles campaign.
- **1924–1929** — Served as Chancellor of the Exchequer; controversially returned Britain to the gold standard.
- **1930s** — During the "wilderness years," warned of the growing threat of Nazi Germany.
- **10 May 1940** — Became Prime Minister, forming a national government to prosecute the war.
- **1953** — Awarded the Nobel Prize in Literature.
- **5 April 1955** — Resigned as Prime Minister due to declining health.
- **24 January 1965** — Died in London; given a state funeral.

## Body

### Early Life and Military Career
Born into the aristocratic Spencer family, Churchill had a difficult childhood and was not a strong student. He attended the Royal Military College, Sandhurst, and joined the army in 1895. He saw action in Cuba, India, and Sudan, gaining fame as a war correspondent and author. During the Second Boer War, he was captured as a prisoner of war but made a celebrated escape, which boosted his public profile and political prospects.

### Early Political Career and Party Switches
Churchill was first elected as a Conservative MP in 1900 but defected to the Liberals in 1904, citing differences over free trade and protectionism. In the Liberal governments of H. H. Asquith and David Lloyd George, he held several senior posts, including President of the Board of Trade and Home Secretary, where he championed social welfare reforms like minimum wage and unemployment insurance. As First Lord of the Admiralty in World War I, he was held responsible for the failed Gallipoli campaign, which ended his ministerial career temporarily. He returned to government under Lloyd George, serving as Minister of Munitions and Secretary of State for War and Air, where he was a staunch anti-communist.

### Wilderness Years and Return to Power
After losing his cabinet seat and then his parliamentary seat in 1922, Churchill briefly sat out Parliament. He returned as a Conservative MP in 1924 and was appointed Chancellor of the Exchequer, a role for which he had no background. His most controversial decision was returning Britain to the gold standard in 1925, which contributed to economic deflation. During the 1930s, he was out of government and spent his time writing and warning about the threat of rearmament in Nazi Germany, a stance that was initially unpopular but proved prescient.

### World War II Leadership
Reappointed First Lord of the Admiralty at the war's outbreak, Churchill became Prime Minister in May 1940 after the Norway debate weakened Neville Chamberlain's government. He formed a national coalition and, through powerful oratory, galvanized British resistance during its darkest hour, rejecting any thought of peace with Hitler. He forged a close alliance with U.S. President Franklin D. Roosevelt, securing vital American aid through the Lend-Lease Act. Churchill worked closely with the Soviet Union after Hitler's invasion in 1941 and attended key conferences with Roosevelt and Stalin at Tehran and Yalta to plan the Allied strategy and post-war order. His government's handling of the Bengal famine of 1943 remains a subject of criticism.

### Post-War Career and Legacy
After leading Britain to victory in Europe, Churchill's Conservative Party was defeated in the 1945 general election. As Leader of the Opposition, he delivered his famous "Iron Curtain" speech in 1946, warning of Soviet expansion. He returned as Prime Minister from 1951 to 1955, a period focused on managing the decline of the British Empire and navigating the Cold War. His second term was marked by a successful housebuilding program but also by health issues. He is widely regarded as one of the 20th century's most significant figures, celebrated for his wartime leadership, though his imperialist views and some wartime decisions are subjects of ongoing debate.

## Terms
*   **MP (Member of Parliament):** An elected representative in the House of Commons of the United Kingdom.
*   **First Lord of the Admiralty:** The political head of the Royal Navy, a senior cabinet position.
*   **Appeasement:** A diplomatic policy of making political or material concessions to an aggressive power in order to avoid conflict.
*   **Lend-Lease:** A U.S. program providing military aid to foreign nations during World War II, enacted in 1941.
*   **Atlantic Charter:** A 1941 joint declaration by Churchill and Roosevelt outlining Allied goals for the post-war world, including self-determination and free trade.
*   **Iron Curtain:** A term coined by Churchill in 1946 to describe the ideological and physical boundary dividing Europe into Soviet-influenced and Western blocs.
*   **Gold Standard:** A monetary system where a country's currency has a value directly linked to gold. Churchill's 1925 decision to return Britain to it is widely seen as having deepened the Great Depression.
*   **Area Bombing:** A strategic bombing tactic aimed at destroying a city's industrial and residential areas, used by the Allies in WWII. The 1945 bombing of Dresden is a controversial example.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Responsibility for the Bengal Famine (1943):** Historians debate the extent to which Churchill's wartime policies, including the diversion of food supplies and refusal to approve more grain imports, exacerbated the famine that killed an estimated 2.1–3.8 million people.
*   **The "Percentages Agreement" (1944):** The secret deal with Stalin to divide spheres of influence in the Balkans is debated as either a pragmatic necessity of realpolitik or a cynical betrayal of Eastern European nations.
*   **Area Bombing Campaign:** The morality and military necessity of the Allied strategic bombing campaign, particularly the late-war bombing of Dresden, remains controversial. Churchill himself later expressed regret.
*   **Political Ideology:** Scholars debate whether Churchill was fundamentally a conservative, a liberal, or a pragmatic opportunist, given his party switches and record as both a social reformer and a staunch imperialist.
*   **Legacy on Race and Empire:** Churchill's legacy is contested due to his staunch imperialism, comments on race, and actions during colonial conflicts, which contrast with his role in defeating fascism.

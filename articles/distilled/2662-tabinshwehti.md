# Tabinshwehti

## Overview
Tabinshwehti (1516–1550) was the king of Toungoo and founder of the First Toungoo Empire. Through a series of military campaigns from 1534 to 1549, he created the largest Burmese kingdom since the fall of the Pagan Empire in 1287. His conquests, which united much of Burma, provided the foundation for the eventual reunification of the entire country under his successor and brother-in-law, Bayinnaung.

## Timeline
- **16 April 1516** — Born at Toungoo Palace to King Mingyi Nyo.
- **24 November 1530** — Ascends the throne at age 14 after his father's death.
- **c. April 1534** — Marries his sister to his deputy Ye Htut (later Bayinnaung), securing his loyalty.
- **Late 1534** — Launches first raids against the Hanthawaddy kingdom, breaking from his father's defensive policy.
- **1538/1539** — Captures the Hanthawaddy capital of Pegu after a stratagem.
- **May 1541** — Conquers the port city of Martaban, gaining control of Lower Burma's wealth and access to Portuguese firearms.
- **19 May 1542** — Captures the city of Prome after defeating relief forces from the Confederation of Shan States and Arakan.
- **July/August 1544** — Crowned at the ancient capital of Pagan (Bagan) in the tradition of Pagan kings.
- **1545** — Crowned again at Pegu in the Mon tradition, cementing his rule over Lower Burma.
- **30 January 1547** — Agrees to a truce with Arakan after a failed siege of its capital, Mrauk-U.
- **February 1549** — Negotiates a safe retreat from Siam after a failed invasion of Ayutthaya.
- **30 April 1550** — Assassinated on his 34th birthday by his adviser Smim Sawhtut.

## Body

### Early Life and Accession
Tabinshwehti was born the heir-apparent to the small, landlocked principality of Toungoo. A prophecy at his birth linked him to the legendary warrior Minye Kyawswa, shaping his education in military arts. He grew up with Ye Htut, the son of his wet nurse, who became his lifelong deputy. After ascending the throne in 1530, Tabinshwehti's first major decision was to pardon Ye Htut for an affair with his half-sister and instead marry them, securing an unshakeable alliance. Facing the threat of expansion from the Confederation of Shan States to the north, Tabinshwehti chose an aggressive strategy, attacking the wealthier but disunited Hanthawaddy kingdom to the south in 1534.

### Founding the Toungoo Empire
The initial raids on Hanthawaddy failed, but Toungoo forces eventually captured its capital, Pegu, in 1538/1539 after a successful stratagem. The subsequent conquest of Martaban in 1541 gave Tabinshwehti control of Lower Burma's manpower, trade wealth, and access to Portuguese mercenaries and firearms. He used these new resources to conquer Prome in 1542, defeating armies from both the Confederation of Shan States and Arakan. This expansion allowed him to be crowned at Pagan in 1544, claiming the legacy of ancient Burmese kings. He then pursued an east-west empire, though his campaigns against Arakan (1545–1547) and Siam (1547–1549) ultimately failed to achieve lasting conquest.

### Administration and Death
Tabinshwehti made no effort to centralize administration, instead relying on a decentralized model of appointing loyal local lords, many of whom were ethnic Mon, to govern conquered territories. He actively courted Mon support, appointing them to high offices and being crowned in Mon tradition at Pegu. This policy secured the loyalty needed for his constant military campaigns but left the kingdom administratively fragile. After returning from the failed Siamese campaign, the king became an alcoholic. In 1550, he was assassinated on the orders of his adviser, Smim Sawhtut. The empire he built promptly fragmented, requiring Bayinnaung two years to restore it.

## Terms
- **First Toungoo Empire**: The kingdom founded by Tabinshwehti through conquest, uniting much of Burma for the first time since the Pagan Empire's fall.
- **Hanthawaddy**: The wealthy, Mon-speaking kingdom in Lower Burma that was Tabinshwehti's first major conquest.
- **Confederation of Shan States**: A powerful alliance of Shan states in Upper Burma that was a rival to Toungoo.
- **Pegu (Bago)**: The capital of Hanthawaddy, which Tabinshwehti captured and later made his own capital.
- **Bayinnaung**: Tabinshwehti's deputy, childhood friend, and brother-in-law, who succeeded him and reunified the empire.
- **Mon**: An ethnic group dominant in Lower Burma; Tabinshwehti actively incorporated Mon elites into his government.
- **Pagan (Bagan)**: The ancient capital of the former Pagan Empire, where Tabinshwehti was crowned to legitimize his rule.
- **Nat**: A spirit venerated in traditional Burmese religion; Tabinshwehti is one of the 37 recognized nats.

## Debates and open questions
- The exact cause of the Burmese–Siamese Wars is debated. Burmese sources cite Siamese incursions into the Tavoy frontier, while Siamese sources claim Burma initiated hostilities to exploit a political crisis in Ayutthaya.
- The degree to which Tabinshwehti's early campaigns were directed by his older ministers versus his own decision is uncertain, as chronicles attribute the audacious war policy to the young king alone.

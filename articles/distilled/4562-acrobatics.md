# Acrobatics

## Overview
Acrobatics is the performance of human feats of balance, agility, and motor coordination. It is a fundamental component of various performing arts, sports, and martial arts, with its most extensive use found in disciplines like circus, gymnastics, and acro dance. The practice has deep historical roots across multiple cultures and continues to evolve in both artistic and competitive forms.

## Timeline
- **c. 2000 BC** — Minoan art depicts acrobatic bull-leaping.
- **Ancient Greece** — Acrobatics are practiced; tightrope walking is traced to this period.
- **618–907 AD** — Acrobatics are part of Chinese culture during the Tang dynasty, often in court displays.
- **European Middle Ages** — Noble courts include acrobatic performances with juggling.
- **19th century** — The term "acrobatics," initially for tightrope walking, expands to include circus acts.
- **Late 19th century** — Tumbling and gymnastic activities become competitive sports in Europe.

## Body

### History and Cultural Significance
Acrobatic traditions are ancient and cross-cultural. Evidence includes Minoan art from c. 2000 BC showing bull-leaping feats. The practice was present in Ancient Greece and medieval Europe, where it featured in courtly entertainment. In China, acrobatics have been integral since the Tang dynasty (618–907), initially as part of village festivals and later dominating court displays. The 19th century saw the term "acrobatics" broaden from tightrope walking to encompass circus performance, and by the late 19th century, activities like tumbling had become competitive sports in Europe. Acrobatics has also inspired fine art, such as paintings by Renoir and Vasnetsov.

### Major Forms and Disciplines
Acrobatics encompasses several distinct forms. **Acrobalance** is a floor-based art involving balances, lifts, and shapes performed in pairs or groups. **Acro dance** combines classical dance technique with precise acrobatic elements. **Aerial acrobatics** are performed on suspended apparatus, including the **trapeze** (a horizontal bar), **corde lisse** (a smooth vertical rope), **aerial silks** (long fabric), and the **aerial hoop** (a circular steel apparatus). Other specialized forms include **contortion**, which showcases extreme flexibility, and **rope and wire walking** (funambulism), which includes tightrope walking and slacklining. **Tumbling** involves whole-body rotational activities like somersaults and has origins in ancient China, Greece, and Egypt.

## Terms
- **Acrobatics**: The performance of human feats of balance, agility, and motor coordination.
- **Acrobalance**: A floor-based acrobatic art using balances, lifts, and shapes in pairs or groups.
- **Acro dance**: A dance style blending classical technique with precision acrobatics.
- **Aerial acrobatics**: Acrobatics performed on suspended apparatus like trapezes or silks.
- **Contortion**: A performance art highlighting extreme physical flexibility.
- **Funambulism**: The skill of walking along a thin wire or rope; tightrope walking.
- **Tumbling**: An acrobatic skill involving rolls, twists, and somersaults using the whole body.

# Ngorongoro Conservation Area

## Overview
The Ngorongoro Conservation Area (NCA) is a protected area and UNESCO World Heritage Site in Tanzania, centered on the world's largest intact volcanic caldera. It is globally significant for its unique "multi-use" model, which attempts to balance wildlife conservation with the traditional pastoralist lifestyle of the Maasai people, though this balance is a source of ongoing conflict. The area also contains Olduvai Gorge, a key site for understanding human evolution.

## Timeline
- **c. 2.5–1.9 Ma** — Ngorongoro Volcano active; caldera forms after eruption and collapse.
- **1892** — First European, Oscar Baumann, visits the crater.
- **1921** — British colonial government begins enacting game preservation ordinances restricting hunting.
- **1959** — Ngorongoro Conservation Area Ordinance separates the NCA from the newly created Serengeti National Park.
- **1979** — Ngorongoro Conservation Area inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site for natural significance.
- **2009** — Ngorongoro Wildlife Conservation Act places new restrictions on human settlement and farming in the Crater.
- **2010** — NCA receives UNESCO Mixed Heritage Status, recognizing cultural significance.
- **2021** — Tanzanian government begins implementing a plan to relocate Maasai from the NCA to Msomera.

## Body

### History and Human Use
The area has been occupied by hominids for at least 1.75 million years. It was later inhabited by hunter-gatherers, then pastoralists including the Iraqw, Datooga, and finally the Maasai, who dominate today. European colonization brought hunting, followed by game preservation laws. The 1959 Ngorongoro Conservation Area Ordinance was created to secure land rights for Maasai pastoralists relocated from the adjacent Serengeti National Park, establishing the NCA as a multi-use area. This model has been increasingly challenged by conservation pressures, leading to the 2009 Wildlife Conservation Act and a government plan, starting in 2021, to relocate Maasai to Msomera, a move criticized for lacking free, prior, and informed consent.

### Geology and the Crater
The NCA is part of the Crater Highlands, a volcanic complex. Its main feature is the Ngorongoro Crater, formed 2–3 million years ago when a volcano collapsed. It is 610 meters deep with a 260 km² floor. The crater contains a seasonal salt lake, Lake Magadi, fed by streams like the Munge and Lerai. Water extraction by lodges reduces flow into the Lerai by about 25%. The area also includes other volcanic features like Empakaai Crater and the Olduvai Gorge, a steep-sided ravine where the Leakeys pioneered research on early human evolution.

### Wildlife and Ecology
The crater floor hosts about 25,000 large animals, including elephants, buffalo, hippos, and a dense lion population. However, the crater is not a closed system; over 20% of wildebeest and half the zebra leave in the wet season. Key populations have fluctuated: the black rhino declined from ~108 in 1964–66 to 13 in 1993, recovering to 55 by 2018. The lion population crashed from 75–100 to 12 in 1962 due to disease from stable flies, and has been affected by canine distemper and inbreeding. The annual Great Migration of wildebeest, zebras, and gazelles passes through the wider NCA.

### Threats and Management
The NCA's unique multi-use mandate faces significant threats. The resident Maasai population grew from ~8,700 in 1966 to 93,136 in 2017, projected to reach 161,000 by 2027. This increases pressure from livestock grazing, lumber use, and construction. Tourism, which peaked at 752,232 visitors in 2023, is the primary funding source for management but is concentrated on the crater, prompting vehicle limits and higher fees. The Ngorongoro Conservation Area Authority (NCAA) manages these competing interests.

## Terms
- **Caldera:** A large volcanic crater formed by the collapse of a volcano after an eruption.
- **Multi-use protected area:** A conservation zone that allows for human habitation and traditional land use alongside wildlife protection.
- **Transhumance:** The seasonal movement of livestock between fixed pastures.
- **Inselberg:** An isolated rock hill, knob, or mountain that rises abruptly from a plain.
- **Paleoanthropology:** The study of human evolution through the fossil and archaeological record.
- **Ungulates:** Hoofed mammals, such as wildebeest, zebras, and gazelles.
- **FPIC (Free, Prior, and Informed Consent):** A principle requiring that indigenous peoples give voluntary consent to projects affecting them.

## Debates and Open Questions
The core debate is whether the NCA's multi-use model is sustainable. The government argues that relocating the growing Maasai population is necessary to preserve the area's natural values. The Maasai and human rights groups contend the relocation is coercive, violates their rights, and undermines the conservation area's founding purpose. The IUCN seeks solutions to improve collaborative conservation with local communities.

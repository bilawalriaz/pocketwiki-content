# Eritrea

## Overview
Eritrea is a multi-ethnic country in the Horn of Africa with a long history, from ancient kingdoms to Italian colonization and a decades-long war for independence. It gained de jure independence in 1993 but is now a one-party state with a highly restrictive government, facing significant international criticism for its human rights record and indefinite national service.

## Timeline
- **c. 100–940 AD** — Kingdom of Aksum, a major trading empire, flourishes in the region.
- **1869** — Italian missionary purchases land at Assab, beginning Italian colonial interest.
- **1889** — Italian Eritrea colony is formally established.
- **1941** — British forces expel Italians; Eritrea placed under British Military Administration.
- **1952** — UN federation plan links Eritrea with Ethiopia under Ethiopian sovereignty.
- **1961** — Eritrean Liberation Front (ELF) begins armed struggle for independence.
- **1962** — Ethiopian emperor dissolves Eritrean parliament, annexing the territory.
- **1991** — Eritrean People's Liberation Front (EPLF) defeats Ethiopian forces, gaining de facto independence.
- **1993** — Independence referendum passes; Eritrea gains de jure independence and UN membership.
- **1998–2000** — Eritrean–Ethiopian War is fought over border disputes.
- **2018** — Peace treaty formally ends the border conflict with Ethiopia.
- **2020** — Eritrean troops intervene in Ethiopia's Tigray War.

## Body

### History and Independence
Eritrea's history includes ancient human evolution sites and the Kingdom of Aksum, which adopted Christianity in the 4th century. From the 12th century, its highlands were the northern frontier of Ethiopian kingdoms. The modern state's boundaries were established during Italian colonization (1889–1941). After British administration and a UN-mandated federation with Ethiopia (1952), Ethiopia annexed Eritrea in 1962, sparking a 30-year war of independence led by the ELF and later the EPLF. Independence was achieved in 1991 (de facto) and 1993 (de jure) following a referendum.

### Government and Politics
Eritrea is a unitary one-party presidential republic under the People's Front for Democracy and Justice (PFDJ). President Isaias Afwerki has been in office since 1993. National legislative or presidential elections have never been held. The government is widely described as a totalitarian dictatorship with severe restrictions on political freedom, media, and assembly. The 1997 constitution, which provides for multi-party politics, remains unimplemented.

### Human Rights and National Service
Eritrea's human rights record is considered among the worst globally. The government practices arbitrary detention, torture, and extrajudicial killings. A key issue is indefinite national service, instituted in 1995. Officially 18 months, it has no time limit and averages 6.5 years, with some serving over 12 years. This has led to mass emigration, with over 500,000 refugees by 2018. All independent media have been banned since 2001.

### Economy and Society
Eritrea's economy is based on subsistence agriculture, mining (notably the Bisha gold mine), and remittances. It has rejected most foreign aid since 2005. The country is multi-ethnic, with nine recognized groups; the Tigrinya (c. 55%) and Tigre (c. 30%) are the largest. Tigrinya, Arabic, and English are working languages. Christianity and Islam are the main religions. Asmara, the capital, is a UNESCO World Heritage Site for its well-preserved Italian modernist architecture.

## Terms
*   **Bahr Negus:** The historical title for the governor of the Eritrean highlands under the Ethiopian empire.
*   **Dʿmt:** A pre-Aksumite kingdom (10th–5th centuries BC) in the region, known for irrigation and iron tools.
*   **Kingdom of Aksum:** A major trading empire (c. 100–940 AD) centered in Eritrea and northern Ethiopia, which adopted Christianity in the 4th century.
*   **Eritrean Liberation Front (ELF):** The organization that began the armed struggle for independence from Ethiopia in 1961.
*   **Eritrean People's Liberation Front (EPLF):** The successor to the ELF that led the final phase of the independence war and became the ruling party.
*   **National Service:** Mandatory, indefinite military and civil service program for all citizens aged 18–50, a major source of international criticism.
*   **People's Front for Democracy and Justice (PFDJ):** The sole legal political party in Eritrea.
*   **Treaty of Wuchale (1889):** Agreement where Ethiopian Emperor Menelik II recognized Italian control over Eritrean territories in exchange for aid and arms.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Population:** Sources disagree on Eritrea's population, with estimates ranging from 3.5 to 6.4 million, as no official census has been conducted since 1984.
*   **Religious Demographics:** The proportion of Christians and Muslims is debated, with estimates varying from near-equal splits to a Christian majority.
*   **Human Rights Allegations:** The Eritrean government consistently dismisses international reports of human rights abuses as "wild allegations" and politically motivated fabrications.
*   **Border Conflict with Ethiopia:** While a peace treaty was signed in 2018, the full demarcation of the border remains incomplete, and Eritrean troops remained involved in Ethiopian conflicts as of 2021.

# Rosario

## Overview
Rosario is Argentina's third-largest city and a major industrial, agricultural, and transportation hub located on the Paraná River. It is historically significant as the site where the Argentine flag was first raised and is a key center for the country's agro-industrial exports. The city's economy and politics are deeply tied to its role in national and international trade.

## Timeline
- **1793** — Traditional founding date of Rosario, then known as Pago de los Arroyos.
- **1812** — General Manuel Belgrano raises the Argentine flag for the first time on the Paraná River shore.
- **1852** — Rosario is formally declared a city.
- **1880** — Rosario becomes Argentina's first export outlet.
- **1969** — The "Rosariazo" protests occur against the military dictatorship.
- **2003** — The Rosario-Victoria Bridge opens, connecting the city to Entre Ríos province.
- **2025** — A municipal ordinance grants Rosario autonomy from Santa Fe Province.

## Body

### History and Foundation
Rosario's origins are in the colonial settlement of Pago de los Arroyos ("land of the streams"). The first permanent settlement was an estancia (farm) established in 1689. The city's official founding is traditionally dated to 1793, though it was not declared a city until 1852. A pivotal moment occurred on February 27, 1812, when General Manuel Belgrano first raised the Argentine flag on its shores, earning Rosario the title "Cradle of the Argentine Flag." The city's growth accelerated after 1852 when river trade was opened, and by 1880 it was Argentina's primary export port. Significant European immigration in the late 19th and early 20th centuries shaped its demographics and progressive political character.

### Government and Administration
Rosario is governed by a mayor and a Deliberative Council. Since 1983, the mayoralty was held continuously by the Socialist Party until 2019. The city is divided into six administrative districts. A long-standing movement for greater autonomy culminated in a 2025 ordinance making Rosario an autonomous city, separate from Santa Fe Province. The city pioneered the use of an unarmed Municipal Urban Guard for public safety.

### Economy and Industry
Rosario is the core of Argentina's major industrial corridor and a critical node for agricultural commerce. Its economy is based on services and industry, generating the country's second-largest urban gross regional product. The agro-industry is the principal manufacturing sector, with the city becoming a major global processor of vegetable oils. Key exports include wheat, corn, soy, and meat. The Port of Rosario, which requires periodic dredging, is a vital shipping center. The city also hosts significant petrochemical, chemical, and automotive manufacturing sectors. The Municipal Bank of Rosario, founded in 1896, is a key financial institution focused on small and medium enterprises.

### Culture and Landmarks
Rosario features preserved neoclassical, Art Nouveau, and Art Deco architecture. Its most prominent landmark is the National Flag Memorial, a monumental complex inaugurated in 1957 on the site where the flag was first raised. The city is home to numerous theaters, museums, and cultural centers. A major planned cultural project, the Puerto de la Música designed by Oscar Niemeyer, has been on indefinite hold since 2012. Rosario is also known as the birthplace of figures like Che Guevara and Lionel Messi.

### Demographics and Urban Structure
As of the 2010 census, Greater Rosario had a population of 1,193,605. The population is relatively aged compared to the national average. The city's ethnic makeup was transformed by late 19th and early 20th-century immigration, primarily from Italy and Spain. Rosario is divided into six districts, with the Centre District being the most densely populated. The city's urban structure includes major boulevards, parks along the recovered Paraná riverbank, and distinct neighborhoods.

### Transportation
Rosario is a major transportation hub. Its port connects to the Atlantic via the Paraná River. The city is linked by national highways and is a terminus for long-distance bus services. Passenger rail services are limited, operated by the state-owned Trenes Argentinos. The Rosario–Islas Malvinas International Airport provides domestic and some international flights. Public transport within the city relies on a bus and trolleybus system.

## Terms
- **Pago de los Arroyos**: The original name for the area meaning "land of the streams," referencing local waterways.
- **Estancia**: A large rural estate or farm, the first type of permanent settlement in the area.
- **Caudillo**: A political-military leader, often a provincial governor, prominent in 19th-century Argentina.
- **Rosariazo**: A series of worker and student protests and strikes against the military dictatorship in 1969.
- **Dirty War**: The period of state terrorism in Argentina (1976-1983) under military rule, during which many citizens "disappeared."
- **Agro-industry**: The business of processing agricultural products, a core sector of Rosario's economy.
- **Voseo**: The use of the pronoun "vos" instead of "tú" for the second-person singular, a feature of Rioplatense Spanish.
- **Sheísmo**: A pronunciation of the "ll" and "y" sounds as a voiceless "sh," characteristic of the local dialect.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The city's exact foundation date is not officially acknowledged, with 1793 being a traditional but debated date.
- Rosario was declared the federal capital of Argentina on three occasions, but each time the law was vetoed by the executive branch.
- The city has been at the center of a continuing debate over taxes levied on major agricultural exports like soy, which are critical to its economy.
- The future of the planned Puerto de la Música cultural complex remains uncertain after being put on indefinite hold in 2012 due to financial constraints.

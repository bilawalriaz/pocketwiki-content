# Zimbabwe

## Overview
Zimbabwe is a landlocked country in Southeast Africa with a complex history marked by ancient kingdoms, colonial exploitation, and post-independence political and economic turmoil. Its story is defined by the rise and fall of the Shona states, the imposition of white-minority rule as Rhodesia, a brutal liberation war, and the subsequent challenges of governance under Robert Mugabe, including economic collapse, hyperinflation, and authoritarianism. Understanding Zimbabwe is key to grasping the broader dynamics of decolonization, state failure, and resilience in modern Africa.

## Timeline
- **c. 1300** — Kingdom of Zimbabwe eclipses Mapungubwe, becoming a major trade center.
- **1890** — Cecil Rhodes's British South Africa Company establishes Fort Salisbury (Harare).
- **1923** — Southern Rhodesia becomes a self-governing British colony.
- **1965** — Ian Smith's government issues a Unilateral Declaration of Independence (UDI) as Rhodesia.
- **1979** — Lancaster House Agreement ends the guerrilla war, leading to a transition to majority rule.
- **1980** — Zimbabwe gains independence; Robert Mugabe becomes Prime Minister.
- **1987** — Mugabe becomes Executive President, consolidating power.
- **2000** — Government initiates Fast Track Land Reform, seizing white-owned farms.
- **2008** — Hyperinflation peaks; a power-sharing deal is signed between Mugabe and Morgan Tsvangirai.
- **2017** — Military coup leads to Robert Mugabe's resignation; Emmerson Mnangagwa becomes president.

## Body

### Pre-colonial and Colonial Foundations
The region was first inhabited by the San, with Bantu-speaking farmers arriving around 2,000 years ago. By the 13th century, the Shona people had built Great Zimbabwe, a major stone city and trade hub. Successive states like the Kingdom of Mutapa and the Rozwi Empire followed. In the late 19th century, Cecil Rhodes's British South Africa Company conquered the area, establishing the colony of Southern Rhodesia in 1923. Colonial rule was marked by land dispossession, epitomized by the 1930 Land Apportionment Act, which reserved the best land for the white minority.

### Liberation War and Independence
In 1965, the white-minority government under Ian Smith unilaterally declared independence as Rhodesia, triggering international sanctions and a 15-year guerrilla war led by ZANU (Robert Mugabe) and ZAPU (Joshua Nkomo). The conflict ended with the 1979 Lancaster House Agreement, which facilitated elections and the birth of independent Zimbabwe in 1980. Mugabe's ZANU-PF party won a landslide victory.

### Post-Independence Politics and Human Rights
Mugabe's rule became increasingly authoritarian. A violent campaign known as Gukurahundi (1983-1987) targeted the Ndebele minority, killing thousands. The state security apparatus dominated politics, suppressing dissent and committing widespread human rights violations. Opposition parties like the MDC faced severe repression, especially during contested elections in 2000, 2002, and 2008. In 2017, a military coup forced Mugabe's resignation, and his successor, Emmerson Mnangagwa, has continued ZANU-PF rule amid allegations of corruption and election fraud.

### Economic Crisis and Recovery
Zimbabwe's economy, once robust, collapsed after 2000 due to mismanagement, corruption, and the chaotic land reform program, which devastated agricultural exports. Hyperinflation peaked in 2008, rendering the Zimbabwean dollar worthless. The economy stabilized after 2009 when multiple foreign currencies were allowed. Growth returned, but remains fragile, with high unemployment and poverty. The mining sector, particularly platinum and diamonds, is a key resource, though revenues are often mismanaged.

## Terms
- **Shona**: The majority ethnic group (82%) in Zimbabwe, with a rich history of state-building and stone architecture.
- **Ndebele**: The second-largest ethnic group (14%), descended from 19th-century Zulu migrations.
- **Gukurahundi**: A series of massacres of Ndebele civilians by the Zimbabwean army from 1983 to 1987.
- **Fast Track Land Reform**: A government program begun in 2000 to seize white-owned farms for redistribution to black Zimbabweans, which crippled agricultural production.
- **Hyperinflation**: Extreme, out-of-control inflation; Zimbabwe's peaked at an estimated 11,200,000% in August 2008.
- **ZANU-PF**: The Zimbabwe African National Union – Patriotic Front, the ruling political party since independence.
- **MDC**: The Movement for Democratic Change, the main opposition political party formed in 1999.
- **Lancaster House Agreement**: The 1979 deal that ended the Rhodesian Bush War and paved the way for Zimbabwe's independence.
- **UDI**: Unilateral Declaration of Independence, issued by Rhodesia in 1965 to avoid majority rule.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Land Reform Impact**: Scholars debate whether the economic collapse was primarily caused by the Fast Track Land Reform program, Western sanctions, or a combination of both.
- **Ethnic Tensions**: The extent to which Zimbabwe functions as a nation-state versus a Shona-dominated entity remains contested, given the marginalization of the Ndebele and other minorities.
- **Political Legitimacy**: The legitimacy of elections since 2000, particularly the 2008 and 2018 polls, is frequently questioned by opposition groups and international observers.
- **Economic Recovery**: The sustainability of Zimbabwe's post-2009 economic recovery is uncertain, given ongoing challenges with corruption, debt, and reliance on mineral exports.

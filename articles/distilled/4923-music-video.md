# Music video

## Overview
A music video is a short film integrating a song or album with imagery, primarily created as a marketing tool to promote music recordings. It evolved from early 20th-century illustrated songs and promotional clips into a dominant art form and commercial force in the 1980s, driven by television and later the internet. Its development reflects changes in technology, censorship, and music industry practices.

## Timeline
- **1894** — "The Little Lost Child" uses projected still images, creating the "illustrated song."
- **1920s-30s** — Musical short films (Vitaphone, Screen Songs) and early animated cartoons feature performances.
- **1939-47** — "Soundies," musical films for Panoram jukeboxes, are produced.
- **1958** — Czechoslovak "Dáme si do bytu" is cited as an early abstract promotional video.
- **1964** — *Top of the Pops* premieres, creating demand for "promotional videos" as performance substitutes.
- **1966** — The Beatles' "Paperback Writer"/"Rain" promo films use sophisticated, non-performance techniques.
- **1975** — Queen's "Bohemian Rhapsody" video is central to its marketing, a key precursor to MTV.
- **1981** — MTV launches, airing "Video Killed the Radio Star," making music videos a 24/7 staple.
- **1983** — Michael Jackson's "Thriller" sets new production standards and breaks racial barriers on MTV.
- **1984** — The first MTV Video Music Awards are held.
- **2005** — YouTube launches, shifting video consumption online and enabling viral hits.
- **2009** — Vevo, a major-label music video service, debuts on YouTube.

## Body

### Origins and Precursors (1894–1960s)
The concept began with "illustrated songs" in 1894, where still images were projected during live performances. The arrival of sound film led to musical shorts (e.g., Vitaphone, Disney's *Silly Symphonies*) and "Soundies" (1939–47). Early promotional films emerged in the 1950s, with claims from artists like Tony Bennett and J.P. "The Big Bopper" Richardson (who possibly coined "music video" in 1959). The 1958 Czech clip "Dáme si do bytu" is noted as an early abstract example.

### The Promotional Clip Era (1960s–1970s)
The need for pre-recorded performances for TV shows like *Top of the Pops* (1964) drove the creation of "filmed inserts" or "promotional videos." The Beatles were pioneers, evolving from simple mimed performances to sophisticated films for songs like "Strawberry Fields Forever" (1967). Other British acts (The Who, Pink Floyd, The Kinks) and artists like Bob Dylan ("Subterranean Homesick Blues," 1965) and David Bowie also created innovative clips. By the late 1970s, music videos were regularly broadcast on TV shows in several countries.

### The MTV Era and Mainstream Dominance (1981–1990s)
The 1981 launch of MTV made music videos a central marketing tool. Artists like Michael Jackson, Madonna, and Duran Duran achieved massive success through visually compelling videos. Technological advances in cheaper video equipment and effects allowed for greater creativity. The period saw the rise of auteur directors (e.g., David Fincher, Spike Jonze) and the video album format. MTV's global expansion and the launch of the VMAs (1984) cemented the video's importance. Censorship became a recurring issue, with videos banned for sexual, violent, or controversial content.

### The Digital Shift (2005–Present)
The rise of the internet, particularly YouTube (2005), decentralized video distribution, allowing for viral hits and reducing reliance on TV channels like MTV. Services like Vevo (2009) emerged as official hubs. New formats gained prominence, including lyric videos, vertical videos for mobile, and low-budget "lo-fi" internet clips. The music video remains a key promotional and artistic medium, with its consumption and creation continually evolving with technology.

## Terms
*   **Promotional Video (Promo):** A short film created to promote a song, originally made for television broadcast when artists couldn't appear live.
*   **Video Album:** A long-form commercial release (on VHS, DVD, etc.) containing multiple music videos, similar to an audio album.
*   **Video Single:** A commercial release containing one to three music videos.
*   **Lyric Video:** A video where the song's lyrics are the primary visual element, often created as a supplemental or low-cost alternative.
*   **Visual Album:** A term popularized for a collection of music videos designed to be experienced as a cohesive visual work alongside an album.
*   **MTV (Music Television):** The American cable channel launched in 1981 that popularized the 24-hour music video format.
*   **Censorship:** The editing or banning of music videos by broadcasters or labels due to content deemed offensive (sexual, violent, political, etc.).
*   **Lo-fi Video:** A low-budget, often independently made music video, typically for internet distribution, with a raw aesthetic.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Racial Barriers on MTV:** The channel initially faced criticism for rarely playing videos by African-American artists. Michael Jackson's success with "Thriller" (1983) is credited with breaking this barrier, though debates about representation continued.
*   **Censorship Standards:** What constitutes "offensive" content varies by culture and era, leading to ongoing debates about artistic expression versus public morality, as seen with videos from Madonna, Lil Nas X, and others.
*   **Economic Models:** The shift from physical sales (video albums/singles) to online streaming has complicated revenue models, with debates over how royalties from platforms like YouTube are shared between labels, artists, and the platform.
*   **Artistic vs. Commercial Purpose:** While primarily a marketing tool, the music video is also an art form. Tensions exist between its commercial function and its potential for artistic innovation and social commentary.

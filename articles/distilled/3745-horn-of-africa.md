# Horn of Africa

## Overview
The Horn of Africa (HOA), or Somali Peninsula, is a large peninsula in easternmost mainland Africa. Its strategic location along the Red Sea and Gulf of Aden makes it critical for global trade and geopolitics. The region is home to distinct populations with unique genetic and linguistic heritage, with a history spanning from early human origins to complex modern state dynamics.

## Timeline
- **ca. 233,000 years ago** — Omo remains, among the earliest *Homo sapiens* fossils, found in Ethiopia.
- **25th century BCE** — First mention of the land of Punt by ancient Egyptians.
- **8th–7th centuries BCE** — Kingdom of Dʿmt flourishes in Eritrea and northern Ethiopia.
- **1st–7th centuries CE** — Kingdom of Aksum thrives, adopts Christianity, and becomes a major trading power.
- **1270** — Yekuno Amlak establishes the Solomonic dynasty.
- **1896** — Ethiopian forces defeat Italy at the Battle of Adwa, halting colonial invasion.
- **1941** — British and Ethiopian forces liberate Ethiopia from Italian occupation.
- **1960** — Union of British and Italian Somaliland forms the Somali Republic.
- **1977** — Djibouti gains independence from France.
- **1991** — Eritrea gains de facto independence after a 30-year war; Somali Civil War begins.
- **1993** — Eritrea gains international recognition following a UN referendum.
- **2012** — Federal Government of Somalia established, the first permanent central government since the civil war.

## Body

### Names and Usage
The peninsula has been known as *Regio Aromatica* (Romans), *Bilad al Barbar* (medieval), and *Geeska Afrika* (Somali). Geopolitically, the core region consists of Djibouti, Eritrea, Ethiopia, Somalia, and the partially recognized state of Somaliland. In anthropological studies, "Horn Africans" specifically refers to Cushitic and Ethio-Semitic speaking peoples.

### Peoples and Languages
Main demographic groups are Cushitic-speaking peoples (e.g., Oromo, Somali) and Ethio-Semitic-speaking Habesha peoples (e.g., Amhara, Tigrinya). The region has a long history of indigenous writing systems, including the Ge'ez script (an abugida) used for over 2,000 years, and the Osmanya script developed for Somali in the early 20th century.

### Genetics
Genome-wide studies show Horn Africans form a distinct genetic cluster. They derive ancestry from a local East African component ("Ethiopic") and a substantial non-African, West Eurasian component ("Ethio-Somali"), interpreted as an ancient back-migration. This places them in an intermediate genetic position between sub-Saharan African and Middle Eastern populations. A secondary, more recent southern Arabian-related component is also present. Paternal lineages are dominated by haplogroup E-M35, while maternal lineages are primarily African L haplogroups.

### Prehistory and Ancient History
The region is a source of early human fossils and evidence of early projectile technology. It is a likely route for the "Out of Africa" migration. Early agriculture included teff cultivation and the origin of coffee. The ancient land of Punt was likely located here. The Kingdom of Aksum was a major ancient power that adopted Christianity. Somali city-states were key suppliers of frankincense and myrrh.

### Middle Ages and Early Modern Era
Several powerful empires dominated regional trade, including the Adal Sultanate, the Ajuran Sultanate, and the Ethiopian Empire under the Solomonic dynasty. Conflicts such as the Ethiopian-Adal War involved Portuguese and Ottoman proxies. European contact began in the 15th century, and Jesuit influence led to a temporary shift to Catholicism in Ethiopia before their expulsion in 1632.

### Modern History
European colonialism shaped modern borders. Italy colonized Eritrea (1890) and Italian Somaliland, but was defeated by Ethiopia at Adwa (1896). After WWII, Eritrea was federated with and then annexed by Ethiopia, leading to a 30-year independence war (1961–1991). Djibouti gained independence from France in 1977. The Somali Republic formed in 1960 but collapsed into civil war in 1991, leading to the de facto independence of Somaliland and the later establishment of the Federal Government of Somalia in 2012. Ethiopia's modern borders were shaped by expansion under Emperor Menelik II, and its government was replaced by a Marxist-Leninist junta in 1974, which was overthrown in 1991.

### Geography and Ecology
The region is geologically defined by the Great Rift Valley and was once connected to Arabia. Climate varies from arid lowlands to wetter Ethiopian highlands. It is a biodiversity hotspot with high endemism in reptiles and plants, but faces severe, recurring droughts exacerbated by climate change and population pressure, leading to significant humanitarian crises.

### Economy
The economy is characterized by low GDP per capita. A vast, undocumented cross-border trade in livestock, primarily driven by pastoralists, is crucial for regional food security and integration but poses risks like disease spread and lost government revenue.

## Terms
- **Cushitic peoples**: A major linguistic group in the Horn, including Oromo and Somali speakers.
- **Ethio-Semitic peoples**: A linguistic group in the Ethiopian and Eritrean highlands, including Amhara and Tigrinya speakers.
- **Ge'ez script**: An ancient abugida writing system used in the Horn for over 2,000 years.
- **Abugida**: A writing system where each character represents a consonant with an inherent vowel.
- **Ethiopic component**: A distinct East African genetic ancestry found in Horn populations.
- **Ethio-Somali component**: A distinct West Eurasian genetic ancestry found in Horn populations, linked to an ancient back-migration.
- **Haplogroup E-M35**: A Y-chromosome lineage with high frequency in Horn African populations.
- **Punt**: An ancient land, likely in the Horn, known to Egyptians for its aromatic goods.
- **Aksumite Kingdom**: A major ancient trading empire in the Ethiopian/Eritrean highlands (1st–7th centuries CE).
- **Solomonic dynasty**: The ruling dynasty of the Ethiopian Empire from 1270 until 1974.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The precise location of the ancient land of Punt is debated, though the Horn of Africa is considered the most likely region.
- The timing, scale, and exact routes of the ancient back-migrations that created the "Ethio-Somali" genetic component are subjects of ongoing research.
- The long-term impacts of recurring severe droughts and the effectiveness of mitigation strategies like rangeland management remain critical concerns.

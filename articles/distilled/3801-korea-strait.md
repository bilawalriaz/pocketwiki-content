# Korea Strait

## Overview
The Korea Strait is a vital sea passage in East Asia separating the Korean Peninsula from Japan, connecting the East China Sea, Yellow Sea, and Sea of Japan. It has been a critical corridor for cultural exchange, military conflict, and modern commerce, shaping the historical and economic relationship between the two nations.

## Timeline
- **c. Pleistocene** — Land bridges may have connected Japan to the Eurasian continent via the strait.
- **6th century** — Buddhism is transmitted from Baekje (Korea) to Japan across the strait.
- **1274 & 1281** — Mongol-Korean fleets cross the strait in failed invasions of Japan.
- **1419** — Korean Joseon dynasty fleet suppresses Wokou (pirate) activity on Tsushima Island.
- **May 27–28, 1905** — Battle of Tsushima: Japanese navy destroys Russian fleet in the strait.
- **June 25–26, 1950** — Battle of Korea Strait: South Korean ship sinks North Korean transport in the war's first naval action.
- **Decades-long** — Proposal for a Japan–Korea Undersea Tunnel is discussed.

## Body
### Geography and Currents
The Korea Strait is approximately 200 km wide and 90–100 meters deep on average. It is divided by Tsushima Island into a deeper, narrower Western Channel and the Tsushima Strait (Eastern Channel). A warm branch of the Kuroshio Current, called the Tsushima Current, flows through it into the Sea of Japan, influencing regional climate and marine ecosystems.

### Economic and Strategic Importance
The strait hosts major international shipping lanes, including traffic for South Korean ports. Both Japan and South Korea limit their territorial claims in the strait to 3 nautical miles (5.6 km) to ensure free passage. Japan reportedly maintains this narrow limit to allow U.S. Navy nuclear vessels to transit without violating its non-nuclear policy. Passenger and commercial ferries connect numerous ports in South Korea, Japan, and China.

### Historical Role as a Corridor
During the Pleistocene glacial periods, lower sea levels may have created land bridges, allowing plants and animals like *Palaeoloxodon naumanni* to migrate to Japan. In recorded history, the strait was a conduit for the spread of Mahāyāna Buddhism to Japan in the 6th century. It later became a route for invasion, as seen in the Mongol attempts of 1274 and 1281, and for piracy, leading to the 1419 Korean suppression campaign on Tsushima.

### Modern Conflicts and Future
The strait was the site of the pivotal 1905 Battle of Tsushima during the Russo-Japanese War and one of the first naval engagements of the Korean War in 1950. For decades, a Japan–Korea Undersea Tunnel has been proposed to physically link the two countries.

## Terms
- **Korea Strait**: Sea passage between Korea and Japan connecting the East China Sea, Yellow Sea, and Sea of Japan.
- **Tsushima Island**: Island dividing the Korea Strait into its western and eastern channels.
- **Tsushima Current**: Warm branch of the Kuroshio Current flowing through the strait.
- **Wokou**: Japanese pirates active in the region, particularly after the Mongol invasions.
- **Pleistocene**: Geological epoch (c. 2.6 million to 11,700 years ago) characterized by glacial cycles.
- **Mahāyāna Buddhism**: A major branch of Buddhism transmitted to Japan via the strait.
- **Territorial waters**: Maritime zone extending from a nation's coast; here limited to 3 nautical miles for free passage.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The exact timing and frequency of land bridges connecting Japan to the continent during the Pleistocene are debated.
- The feasibility, cost, and political will for constructing a Japan–Korea Undersea Tunnel remain open questions.

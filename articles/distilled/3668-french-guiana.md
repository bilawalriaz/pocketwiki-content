# French Guiana

## Overview
French Guiana is an overseas department and region of France on the northern coast of South America, making it the only territory on the continental mainland of the Americas still under European sovereignty. It is a sparsely populated, heavily forested territory whose modern economy and strategic importance are largely defined by the European Space Agency's launch site, the Guiana Space Centre. Its society is a complex mix of diverse ethnic groups, shaped by a history of colonization, penal servitude, and successive waves of immigration.

## Timeline
- **1503** — First recorded French establishment in the region.
- **1643** — French colonists found Cayenne, establishing a durable presence.
- **1794** — French National Convention abolishes slavery in colonies, including French Guiana.
- **1852-1953** — French Guiana operates as a penal colony, including the infamous Devil's Island.
- **1946** — French Guiana is fully integrated into France as an overseas department.
- **1964** — President de Gaulle decides to construct the Guiana Space Centre near Kourou.
- **2010** — Voters reject becoming an autonomous overseas collectivity in a referendum.
- **2015** — The region and department are unified under a single territorial collectivity.
- **2017** — The Oyapock River Bridge opens, providing the first land link to Brazil.
- **2025** — The new European Ariane 6 rocket is successfully launched from Kourou.

## Body

### History and Governance
French Guiana was originally inhabited by Indigenous peoples like the Kalina and Lokono. French colonization began in earnest in the 17th century, developing the colony on a plantation economy using enslaved African labor. Slavery was abolished in 1794, but the colony later became infamous for its brutal penal system, which operated from 1852 to 1953 and included Devil's Island. French Guiana became a fully integrated department of France in 1946. Politically, it is governed as part of France, with a prefect appointed by the French president and a local assembly, the Assemblée de Guyane. A key modern political issue is the struggle against illegal gold mining by Brazilian *garimpeiros* in the remote interior, which has led to multiple military operations.

### Geography and Environment
French Guiana consists of a populated coastal strip and a vast, nearly inaccessible interior of primeval rainforest, which covers about 98.9% of its territory. The Guiana Amazonian Park, the largest national park in the EU, protects 41% of the land. The climate is equatorial, with high temperatures and heavy rainfall year-round. The territory is a global biodiversity hotspot, hosting thousands of plant and animal species. Major environmental threats include habitat fragmentation from roads, the impacts of the Petit-Saut Dam, and illegal gold mining.

### Economy and Infrastructure
The economy is heavily dependent on mainland France and the Guiana Space Centre, which accounts for a significant portion of GDP and employment. Traditional industries like fishing and gold mining (often illegal) exist alongside growing eco-tourism. Unemployment is persistently high. Infrastructure is concentrated on the coast; the road network is limited, and several interior communes are only accessible by air or river. The Oyapock River Bridge, opened in 2017, is the sole land border crossing with Brazil.

### Demographics and Society
French Guiana has a young, rapidly growing population with the highest fertility rate in Latin America. Its society is highly diverse, with people of African descent (Creole) forming the largest group, alongside Europeans, Maroons (descendants of escaped slaves), Amerindians, and immigrants from Brazil, Suriname, Haiti, and elsewhere. A significant portion of the population is of foreign origin, and undocumented immigration is a chronic issue. The official language is French, but French Guianese Creole is widely spoken, along with numerous Maroon, Amerindian, and immigrant languages.

## Terms
*   **Overseas department and region:** A first-order administrative division of France that is also part of the European Union, with the same status as departments in mainland France.
*   **Guiana Space Centre (Centre Spatial Guyanais):** The primary spaceport for the European Space Agency, located near Kourou, chosen for its proximity to the equator and ocean access.
*   **Garimpeiros:** Brazilian illegal gold miners who operate in the remote interior of French Guiana.
*   **Maroons:** Communities of African descent formed by escaped enslaved people, living primarily along the Maroni River.
*   **French Guianese Creole:** A French-based creole language and the most widely spoken language after French.
*   **Guiana Shield:** A 1.7-billion-year-old geological formation of which French Guiana is a part, characterized by elevated tablelands.
*   **Terra preta:** Anthropogenic (human-made) dark, fertile soils found in the Amazon, including near the French Guiana-Brazil border, created by ancient Indigenous practices.
*   **Assemblée de Guyane:** The single local assembly governing French Guiana since 2015, replacing the separate regional and departmental councils.

## Debates and open questions
*   **Autonomy vs. Integration:** There is ongoing discussion about granting French Guiana greater autonomy, similar to the "sui generis" status of Corsica, within the French Republic. A 2010 referendum rejected a move to become an autonomous overseas collectivity.
*   **Environmental vs. Economic Development:** A core tension exists between protecting French Guiana's immense biodiversity and the economic pressures of mining, infrastructure development (like road building), and agriculture.
*   **Illegal Immigration and Mining:** The effectiveness of French military and police operations (e.g., Operation Harpie) in permanently stopping illegal gold mining and immigration from Brazil and Suriname is a persistent and unresolved challenge.
*   **Border Dispute with Suriname:** A territorial dispute exists over a small area of the Maripasoula commune, with France and Suriname claiming different river boundaries. A bilateral commission is working to resolve it.

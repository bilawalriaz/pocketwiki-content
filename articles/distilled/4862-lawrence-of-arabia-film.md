# Lawrence of Arabia (film)

## Overview
*Lawrence of Arabia* (1962) is an epic biographical film directed by David Lean, depicting the life of T. E. Lawrence during the Arab Revolt of World War I. It is widely regarded as one of the greatest films ever made, celebrated for its sweeping cinematography, performances, and complex portrayal of its protagonist's psychological and moral struggles. The film's legacy lies in its cinematic grandeur and its enduring, if debated, influence on the public perception of Lawrence and the historical events.

## Timeline
- **1962** — *Lawrence of Arabia* premieres in London on 10 December.
- **1963** — Wins 7 Academy Awards, including Best Picture and Best Director.
- **1989** — A restored director's cut is released, supervised by David Lean.
- **1991** — Selected for preservation in the U.S. National Film Registry.
- **1998** — Ranked 5th on the American Film Institute's (AFI) list of greatest American films.
- **2002** — Theatrically re-released for its 40th anniversary.
- **2012** — A new 8K digital restoration is released for the 50th anniversary.

## Body

### Production and Cast
The film was produced by Sam Spiegel's Horizon Pictures and distributed by Columbia Pictures. Director David Lean cast Peter O'Toole as T. E. Lawrence, despite O'Toole being relatively unknown. The ensemble cast includes Alec Guinness as Prince Faisal, Omar Sharif as the composite character Sherif Ali, Anthony Quinn as Auda Abu Tayi, and Jack Hawkins as General Allenby. The screenplay, by Robert Bolt and Michael Wilson, was rewritten by Bolt to focus on Lawrence as a character study. Filming took place in Jordan, Morocco, and Spain, using Super Panavision 70 for its expansive desert cinematography. Maurice Jarre composed the iconic score.

### Plot and Themes
The film depicts Lawrence's experiences in the Ottoman provinces of Hejaz and Syria, focusing on his leadership in the Arab Revolt, including the capture of Aqaba and Damascus. It explores his emotional struggles with violence, his divided identity between British duty and Arab camaraderie, and the ultimate failure of his dream for Arab independence due to the secret Sykes-Picot Agreement. The narrative is framed by his death in 1935 and its impact on those who knew him.

### Historical Accuracy and Representation
The film takes significant liberties with history. It inaccurately depicts the chronology of the war, the geography of the region, and the composition of the Arab forces, which included a regular army, not just Bedouin irregulars. The character of Sherif Ali is a composite, and Colonel Brighton represents multiple British officers. The portrayal of Lawrence as an egotist and the depiction of his torture at Deraa are debated among biographers. The film also omits Lawrence's pre-war archaeological work and espionage. Characters like General Allenby and Auda Abu Tayi are portrayed differently from historical accounts, which upset their families and descendants.

### Reception and Legacy
Upon release, the film was a critical and commercial success, praised for its spectacle, performances, and score. It is consistently ranked among the greatest films by institutions like the AFI and the British Film Institute. Its visual style has influenced numerous directors, including Steven Spielberg, who cites it as the film that inspired him to become a filmmaker. The film has undergone several restorations, most notably a 1989 director's cut and a 2012 8K digital restoration.

## Terms
- **Epic film**: A genre of lengthy motion pictures, often with historical or fantasy settings, characterized by grand scale and spectacle.
- **Super Panavision 70**: A motion picture camera system using 65mm film negative and spherical lenses to produce high-resolution, wide-aspect-ratio images for theatrical projection.
- **Arab Revolt (1916–1918)**: A military uprising of Arab forces against the Ottoman Empire during World War I, supported by the British.
- **Sykes-Picot Agreement**: A 1916 secret treaty between the United Kingdom and France, with assent from the Russian Empire and the Kingdom of Italy, to define mutually agreed-upon spheres of influence and control in an eventual partition of the Ottoman Empire.
- **Arab Bureau**: A section of the British Foreign Office's Middle Eastern department, created in 1916, which organized the Arab Revolt.
- **Composite character**: A fictional character in a film or novel who combines the traits of several real people into one.
- **Director's cut**: A version of a film that represents the director's preferred vision, often restored after being altered for the original theatrical release.
- **National Film Registry**: The United States National Film Preservation Board's selection of films for preservation as "culturally, historically, or aesthetically significant."

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Lawrence's Characterization**: Historians debate whether the film's portrayal of Lawrence as an egotist and its implication of his homosexuality are accurate, or if they are influenced by later biographies like Richard Aldington's.
- **Historical Liberties**: Scholars question whether the film's dramatic license—such as conflating events, inventing characters, and altering timelines—unfairly distorts the historical record of the Arab Revolt and Lawrence's role.
- **The Deraa Incident**: The film's depiction of Lawrence's torture and possible rape at Deraa is a point of contention; some biographers accept Lawrence's account, while others argue contemporary evidence suggests the event did not happen as described.
- **Representation of Arabs**: The film has been critiqued for its "white saviour" narrative and for portraying the Arab forces primarily as Bedouin irregulars, omitting the regular Arab Army.

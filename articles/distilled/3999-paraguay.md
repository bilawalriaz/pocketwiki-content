# Paraguay

## Overview
Paraguay is a landlocked South American country whose history is defined by its indigenous Guaraní heritage, a devastating 19th-century war, and a long period of dictatorship before transitioning to democracy. Its culture is a unique fusion of Spanish and Guaraní influences, and its economy is heavily reliant on agriculture, hydroelectric exports, and trade with neighboring Brazil and Argentina.

## Timeline
- **1537** — Spanish explorer Juan de Salazar de Espinosa founds Asunción.
- **1767** — Spanish Crown expels the Jesuits from Paraguay.
- **1811** — Paraguay overthrows Spanish rule and declares independence.
- **1814–1840** — Rule of dictator José Gaspar Rodríguez de Francia ("El Supremo").
- **1864–1870** — Paraguayan War (Triple Alliance War) against Brazil, Argentina, and Uruguay; Paraguay loses half its population.
- **1932–1935** — Chaco War with Bolivia; Paraguay wins sovereignty over most of the disputed Chaco region.
- **1954–1989** — Dictatorship of Alfredo Stroessner.
- **1989** — Stroessner is overthrown in a military coup, beginning the democratic era.
- **1992** — A new constitution establishes a democratic system with improved human rights protections.
- **2008** — Fernando Lugo ends 61 years of Colorado Party rule, marking a peaceful transfer of power to the opposition.
- **2012** — President Lugo is impeached and removed from office.
- **2023** — Santiago Peña of the Colorado Party is elected president.

## Body

### History
The indigenous Guaraní inhabited eastern Paraguay for centuries before the Spanish founded Asunción in 1537. During the colonial era, Jesuit missions (reducciones) protected and converted Guaraní populations until their expulsion in 1767. After independence in 1811, Paraguay was ruled by authoritarian leaders, first José Gaspar Rodríguez de Francia, who isolated the country, and then the López family, who modernized it. This era ended catastrophically with the Triple Alliance War (1864–1870), which killed at least 50% of Paraguay's population and cost it 25–33% of its territory.

The 20th century was marked by political instability, the victorious Chaco War (1932–1935), and the long dictatorship of Alfredo Stroessner (1954–1989). Stroessner's regime, supported by the U.S. for its anti-communism, was marked by human rights abuses and participation in Operation Condor. His overthrow in 1989 initiated a transition to democracy, though political turbulence continued, including the controversial impeachment of President Fernando Lugo in 2012. The conservative Colorado Party has largely dominated politics since the end of Stroessner's rule.

### Geography and Climate
Paraguay is divided by the Paraguay River into two distinct regions: the eastern Región Oriental, with grassy plains and wooded hills, and the western Región Occidental (the Gran Chaco), which consists of low, marshy plains. The climate is semi-arid to subtropical, with significant temperature swings due to the lack of mountain barriers. Rainfall varies greatly, from 170 cm annually in the east to 50 cm in the arid western Chaco. The country has one of the world's highest deforestation rates.

### Government and Politics
Paraguay is a representative democratic republic with a presidential system. Executive power is held by the President, while legislative power is vested in a bicameral National Congress. Since the end of a 1947 civil war, politics have been dominated by the conservative Colorado Party. The country is divided into seventeen departments and a capital district.

### Economy
Paraguay is a developing country with a market economy. Its growth is driven by agriculture (it is the world's sixth-largest soybean producer), manufacturing, and the export of hydroelectric power from the Itaipu Dam. The economy has a large informal sector. Despite growth, poverty remains high (over 26% in 2018), and there is extreme inequality in land ownership, with 85% of agricultural land owned by 2.6% of landowners. The country is a founding member of Mercosur.

### Demographics and Culture
Paraguay's population of about 6 million is predominantly mestizo (mixed European and indigenous). The Guaraní language is spoken by over 90% of the population alongside Spanish, making Paraguay the Latin American country with the highest rate of indigenous language fluency. This cultural fusion is evident in arts like ñandutí lace and in music featuring the harp. Christianity, mainly Catholicism, is the dominant religion. Literacy rates are around 94%.

## Terms
*   **Guaraní:** An indigenous language and people of Paraguay; their language is co-official with Spanish and spoken by over 90% of the population.
*   **Mestizo:** A person of mixed European and indigenous ancestry; the majority of Paraguayans identify as mestizo.
*   **Gran Chaco:** The vast, semi-arid western region of Paraguay, part of a larger lowland plain shared with Bolivia and Argentina.
*   **Triple Alliance War (1864–1870):** A devastating conflict in which Paraguay fought against the alliance of Brazil, Argentina, and Uruguay, resulting in catastrophic population loss and territorial cession.
*   **Chaco War (1932–1935):** A war between Paraguay and Bolivia over the disputed Chaco region, which Paraguay won.
*   **Operation Condor:** A campaign of state terror and political repression in the 1970s and 1980s jointly conducted by the military dictatorships of several South American countries, including Paraguay.
*   **Colorado Party:** The conservative political party that has dominated Paraguayan politics for most of the period since 1947.
*   **Itaipu Dam:** A massive hydroelectric dam on the Paraná River border with Brazil; it is one of the world's largest producers of hydroelectric power, and Paraguay is a leading exporter of electricity from it.
*   **Mercosur:** A South American trade bloc; Paraguay is a founding member.

## Debates and open questions
*   The real causes of the Paraguayan War (1864–1870) are described as "highly debatable."
*   The 2012 impeachment of President Fernando Lugo is considered a "politically motivated coup d'état" by his supporters and some neighboring countries, while the Organization of American States concluded it was constitutional.
*   The origins of the name "Paraguay" are uncertain, with multiple theories based on Guaraní etymology.

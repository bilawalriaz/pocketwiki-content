# Chongqing

## Overview
Chongqing is a provincial-level direct-administered municipality in Southwestern China, notable for being the only one of China's four such municipalities located inland. It is the world's most populous city proper by administrative area, with a resident population exceeding 30 million, though this includes a large rural hinterland. Historically significant as the wartime capital of the Republic of China during the Second Sino-Japanese War (1937–1945), it was separated from Sichuan province in 1997 to spearhead development in China's western interior.

## Timeline
- **277 BC** — Qin general Zhang Ruo captures the area, establishing Qianzhong Commandery.
- **1189 AD** — Yu Prefecture is renamed Chongqing Fu to mark the "double celebration" of Prince Zhao Dun's enthronement.
- **1890** — British Consulate General opens; city becomes China's first inland commerce port open to foreigners.
- **Nov 1937** — Becomes the provisional wartime capital of the Republic of China after the retreat from Nanjing.
- **14 Mar 1997** — Chongqing is separated from Sichuan province and established as a direct-administered municipality.
- **8 Feb 2010** — Designated one of China's nine National Central Cities.

## Body
### History and Administrative Status
Chongqing's history is tied to the ancient State of Ba and was named Jiangzhou by a Qin general. Its current name, meaning "double celebration," was bestowed in 1189 during the Southern Song dynasty. The city served as the wartime capital of the Republic of China from 1937 to 1945, enduring massive Japanese bombing campaigns. After the Chinese Civil War, it was one of the last Kuomintang strongholds on the mainland. On 14 March 1997, the sub-provincial city was merged with adjacent prefectures and elevated to a direct-administered municipality, a provincial-level division, to drive development in western China and manage resettlement for the Three Gorges Dam project. It is now one of China's National Central Cities.

### Geography and Climate
Chongqing is located in the subtropical zone at the transition between the Sichuan Basin and the Yangtze Plain. Its terrain is mountainous and crisscrossed by rivers, including the Yangtze and Jialing, forming the famous Three Gorges. The central urban area is built on mountains surrounded by these rivers. The climate is a monsoonal humid subtropical type, known for very high humidity, hot and humid summers, and mild, damp winters. It is one of China's "Three Furnaces" due to summer heat and is famously called the "Fog City" for its frequent fog, which may have aided its defense during WWII.

### Economy
Chongqing is a major industrial base in western China, historically significant for military-industrial development during the "Third Front" campaign. Its economy is driven by manufacturing, particularly automobiles (it is China's third-largest motor vehicle production center) and motorcycles. The municipality is also a major producer of iron, steel, and aluminum. Agriculture and abundant natural resources, including coal and natural gas, are important. The government has pushed to develop it as a regional economic, trade, and financial center, attracting foreign direct investment in sectors like automotive and finance. Recent efforts focus on shifting towards high-technology and knowledge-intensive industries.

### Transportation and Infrastructure
Chongqing is a major transportation hub with a dramatically expanded infrastructure since 1997. It is known as the "Bridge Capital of China" due to its numerous bridges over the Yangtze and Jialing rivers. The Chongqing Rail Transit system includes the world's longest and busiest monorail network. Chongqing Jiangbei International Airport is a major aviation hub. The city is a key inland port on the Yangtze River, with improved access for cargo vessels due to the Three Gorges Dam. An extensive network of expressways and railways connects it to other regions.

### Culture and Demographics
The native language is Southwestern Mandarin, specifically the Sichuanese dialect. The population is predominantly Han Chinese, with smaller Miao and Tujia minorities in the southeast. Religion is dominated by Chinese folk religions, Taoism, and Buddhism. Chongqing cuisine is a branch of Sichuan cuisine, famous for its spicy and numbing flavors, with hot pot being a signature dish. The city has a rich cultural heritage, including UNESCO World Heritage Sites like the Dazu Rock Carvings and the South China Karst (Wulong Karst).

## Terms
- **Direct-administered municipality**: A provincial-level administrative division in China, directly governed by the central government.
- **National Central City**: A designation for major Chinese cities expected to drive regional economic development.
- **Third Front campaign**: A 1960s-70s Chinese government initiative to build industrial capacity in the remote interior for national defense.
- **Three Gorges Dam**: The world's largest hydroelectric power station, built on the Yangtze River, which affected Chongqing's population via resettlement.
- **Southwestern Mandarin**: A branch of Chinese spoken in Sichuan, Chongqing, and neighboring areas.
- **Sichuanese**: The primary dialect group of Southwestern Mandarin spoken in Sichuan and Chongqing.
- **Hot pot**: A Chinese cooking method where food is boiled in a shared pot of spicy broth at the table.
- **Karst**: A landscape formed from the dissolution of soluble rocks like limestone, characterized by caves and sinkholes.

## Debates and Open questions
- The source notes that while Chongqing is the largest city proper by population, it is not the most populous urban area, highlighting a distinction between administrative and functional urban boundaries.
- The municipality's large rural population (29.6% as of 2021) within its vast administrative area complicates its characterization as a single "city."
- The historical impact of Chongqing's frequent fog on its wartime defense is presented as a possibility ("possibly played a role") rather than a confirmed fact.

# Johnny Weissmuller

## Overview
Johnny Weissmuller (1904–1984) was an American Olympic swimming champion who became the definitive screen Tarzan. His unparalleled athletic career, marked by world records and an unbeaten amateur record, transitioned into a highly successful film career that cemented his pop culture legacy.

## Timeline

- **1905** — Immigrated with family to the United States, settling in Chicago.
- **c. 1913** — Contracted polio; began swimming on a doctor's recommendation.
- **August 6, 1921** — Won four Amateur Athletic Union races in his first major meet.
- **September 27, 1921** — Set his first two world records at the A.A.U. Nationals.
- **July 9, 1922** — Broke Duke Kahanamoku's 100m freestyle world record.
- **1924** — Won three gold medals (100m, 400m, 4x200m relay) and a water polo bronze at the Paris Olympics.
- **1927** — Set a 100-yard freestyle world record of 51.0 seconds; saved 11 people from a boat accident.
- **1928** — Won two gold medals (100m, 4x200m relay) at the Amsterdam Olympics.
- **1932** — Starred as Tarzan in *Tarzan the Ape Man*, his breakthrough film role.
- **1932–1948** — Played Tarzan in twelve feature films.
- **1942–1957** — Starred in sixteen *Jungle Jim* films and a TV series.
- **January 20, 1984** — Died of pulmonary edema in Acapulco, Mexico, at age 79.

## Body

### Early Life and Swimming Career
Born to an ethnic Banat Swabian family, Weissmuller immigrated to the U.S. as an infant. After contracting polio at age nine, swimming was recommended for recovery, sparking his lifelong passion. He excelled immediately, joining the Illinois Athletic Club. Under coach Bill Bachrach, he set his first world records in 1921 and broke Duke Kahanamoku's 100m freestyle record in 1922.

### Olympic Dominance
Weissmuller's Olympic career was spectacular. At the 1924 Paris Games, he won gold in the 100m and 400m freestyle and the 4x200m relay, plus a bronze in water polo. He repeated his 100m and relay victories at the 1928 Amsterdam Games. In total, he won five Olympic gold medals and one bronze, set 67 world records, and retired with an unbeaten amateur record. In 1950, he was named the greatest swimmer of the first half of the 20th century.

### Film Career as Tarzan and Beyond
After a brief modeling stint, Weissmuller was cast as Tarzan in 1932. He originated the iconic Tarzan yell and starred in twelve Tarzan films for MGM and RKO. Author Edgar Rice Burroughs was pleased with Weissmuller but disliked the studio's portrayal of Tarzan as barely articulate, leading Burroughs to create a separate film series. Weissmuller later starred in sixteen *Jungle Jim* movies and a TV series, retiring from acting in 1957.

### Personal Life and Later Years
Weissmuller was married five times and had three children with his third wife, Beryl. He was noted for saving lives, including 11 people from a 1927 boat accident. His health declined after a 1974 hip and leg fracture, revealing a serious heart condition. He died in 1984 in Acapulco, where he was buried with a 21-gun salute and the playing of his Tarzan yell.

## Terms
- **Banat Swabian**: An ethnic German community historically from the Banat region.
- **Amateur Athletic Union (AAU)**: A U.S. sports organization that governed amateur competitions.
- **World Record**: The best performance in a sport, officially recognized by the relevant governing body.
- **Pulmonary Edema**: A condition caused by excess fluid in the lungs, often leading to respiratory failure.
- **Holistic Lifestyle**: A health philosophy emphasizing the whole person (body, mind, spirit), as promoted by John Harvey Kellogg.

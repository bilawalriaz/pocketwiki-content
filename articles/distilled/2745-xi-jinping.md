# Xi Jinping

## Overview
Xi Jinping is the paramount leader of China, serving as General Secretary of the Chinese Communist Party (CCP) since 2012 and President of the People's Republic of China since 2013. His tenure is defined by power consolidation, a sweeping anti-corruption campaign, assertive foreign policy, and the promotion of "Xi Jinping Thought" as guiding state ideology. He is the first Chinese leader born after the PRC's founding and the first since Mao Zedong to serve beyond two presidential terms.

## Timeline
- **15 June 1953** — Born in Beijing.
- **1969–1975** — Sent to Liangjiahe village, Shaanxi, during the Cultural Revolution; joined CCP, became local party secretary.
- **1975–1979** — Studies chemical engineering at Tsinghua University.
- **22 October 2007** — Elected to Politburo Standing Committee (PSC), first-ranked Secretariat member.
- **15 November 2012** — Elected CCP General Secretary and Chairman of Party Central Military Commission (CMC).
- **14 March 2013** — Elected President of the People's Republic of China.
- **2013** — Announces Belt and Road Initiative (BRI).
- **2014** — Introduces "holistic national security concept"; launches Strike Hard Campaign in Xinjiang.
- **2018** — Constitutional amendment removes presidential term limits.
- **23 October 2022** — Re-elected CCP General Secretary for a third term.
- **10 March 2023** — Re-elected President for a third term, first to serve more than two terms.

## Body

### Early Life and Political Rise
Born to a revolutionary official, Xi's early life was marked by political turmoil. After his father was purged in 1963, Xi was sent to Liangjiahe during the Cultural Revolution, living there for seven years. He joined the CCP after several attempts and studied chemical engineering at Tsinghua. His career began in the Central Military Commission, followed by posts in Hebei, Fujian, and Zhejiang, where he gained a reputation for anti-corruption and economic development. In 2007, he was briefly Shanghai party secretary before joining the PSC. He became Vice President in 2008 and CMC Vice Chairman in 2010, positioning him as Hu Jintao's successor.

### Consolidation of Power and Governance
Xi centralized authority within the CCP and himself, departing from collective leadership and establishing a cult of personality. Designated "leadership core" in 2016, his anti-corruption campaign investigated over 2.3 million officials, including "tigers" like former PSC member Zhou Yongkang. This led to the National Supervisory Commission's creation in 2018. He restructured party and state bureaucracy, creating new leading groups to enhance CCP control over finance, technology, and cyberspace. "Xi Jinping Thought" was incorporated into both party and national constitutions.

### Economic and Social Policy
Xi's policies emphasize state control, technological self-reliance, and "common prosperity." His administration increased the role of state-owned enterprises and launched regulatory crackdowns on the private sector, particularly in technology, real estate, and tutoring. He declared a "complete victory" in the "battle against poverty" in 2021. Key initiatives include "Made in China 2025" for advanced manufacturing and the "Dual Circulation" strategy focusing on domestic consumption. Domestically, his rule has seen expanded censorship, mass surveillance, and tightened control over society and media.

### Foreign Policy and National Security
Xi pursued "major-country diplomacy" and the "rejuvenation of the Chinese nation." The Belt and Road Initiative (BRI) expanded China's influence in Eurasia and Africa. He took a harder line in territorial disputes, including in the South China Sea and with India. Relations with the U.S. deteriorated, marked by trade war and strategic competition. He introduced a "holistic national security concept" and passed laws expanding state power. His administration oversaw expanded mass surveillance and a severe crackdown in Xinjiang, including mass detention of Uyghurs, condemned by human rights groups.

### Hong Kong and Taiwan
Xi pushed for greater integration of Hong Kong. Following 2019–2020 protests, the NPCSC enacted a National Security Law in 2020, suppressing the pro-democracy movement. Regarding Taiwan, he increased pressure for unification, rejecting formal independence and stating "we make no promise to renounce the use of force." While he met Taiwanese President Ma Ying-jeou in 2015, relations have since deteriorated under the DPP government, with increased PLA military drills around the island.

## Terms
- **Paramount leader**: The most powerful political figure in China, holding top CCP, state, and military positions.
- **Politburo Standing Committee (PSC)**: The CCP's top decision-making body, currently seven members.
- **Central Military Commission (CMC)**: The body commanding the PLA; both party and state CMCs are chaired by Xi.
- **Princeling**: Descendants of senior CCP officials, referring to Xi's background.
- **Down to the Countryside Movement**: Cultural Revolution policy sending urban youth to rural areas for "re-education."
- **Xi Jinping Thought**: Official state ideology, formally "Xi Jinping Thought on Socialism with Chinese Characteristics for a New Era."
- **Belt and Road Initiative (BRI)**: Global infrastructure strategy investing in nearly 150 countries and organizations.
- **Common Prosperity**: Policy goal to reduce inequality, used to justify regulatory crackdowns on high-income sectors.
- **Wolf Warrior diplomacy**: More assertive, confrontational Chinese diplomacy style since the late 2010s.
- **Holistic national security concept**: Security doctrine encompassing political, economic, military, cultural, and social security.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Power and succession**: Xi's removal of term limits and absence of a clear PSC successor spark debate about long-term political stability and potential power vacuum.
- **Economic model**: Scholars debate whether policies represent a definitive shift from market-oriented reforms toward a state-dominated model and its long-term growth consequences.
- **Human rights**: The scale and nature of the Xinjiang crackdown, including allegations of genocide and crimes against humanity, remain major international contention points.
- **Foreign policy impact**: Analysts question if assertive foreign policy effectively advances China's interests or provokes a counterbalancing coalition that could isolate it.

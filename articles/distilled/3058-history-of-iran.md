# History of Iran

## Overview
Iran, historically known as Persia, is home to one of the world's oldest continuous civilizations, with urban settlements dating to the 5th millennium BC. Its history is defined by the rise and fall of powerful empires that shaped the cultural and political landscape of the Middle East and beyond. The region's identity evolved through successive conquests and cultural integrations, from the pre-Islamic Zoroastrian empires to the Islamic Golden Age and the establishment of a modern Islamic republic.

- **2022** — Protests erupt following the death of Mahsa Amini.

## Body

### Pre-Islamic Civilizations
The Iranian plateau was home to ancient civilizations like the Elamites, who established a state by 4000 BC. The first unified Iranian empire was the Median kingdom in the 7th century BC. The Achaemenid Empire (c. 550–330 BC), founded by Cyrus the Great, became the largest empire of its time, known for its tolerance and administrative innovations like the Royal Road. After its conquest by Alexander the Great, the region saw Greek (Seleucid), Parthian, and Sasanian rule. The Sasanian Empire (224–651 AD) was a major world power rivaling Rome/Byzantium and is considered the last great pre-Islamic Persian empire.

### Islamic Conquest and Persianization
The Muslim conquest of Persia (633–651) ended the Sasanian Empire and began the Islamization of Iran. While Zoroastrianism declined, Persian culture was not erased but absorbed into the new Islamic polity. During the Abbasid Caliphate, Persian administrators and scholars played a key role, and the Islamic Golden Age saw major contributions from Persian figures like Avicenna and Rumi. A process of "Persianization" revived the Persian language and culture within an Islamic context, culminating in works like the *Shahnameh*.

### Mongol Invasions and Later Empires
The Mongol invasion (1219–1221) was catastrophic, destroying cities and irrigation systems. The subsequent Ilkhanate eventually adopted Islam and Persian culture. Iran was later unified by the Timurid and then the Safavid dynasty (1501–1736). The Safavids established Twelver Shia Islam as the state religion, a defining feature of modern Iran. Their rival was the Ottoman Empire. After a period of instability, the Qajar dynasty (1796–1925) ruled, but Iran lost significant Caucasian territories to Imperial Russia in the 19th-century Russo-Persian Wars.

### Modern Iran and the Islamic Republic
The Pahlavi dynasty (1925–1979) pursued rapid, authoritarian modernization. A 1953 coup, aided by the US and UK, removed the elected Prime Minister Mosaddeq and strengthened the Shah's autocratic rule. This led to the 1979 Iranian Revolution, which established a theocratic Islamic republic under Ayatollah Khomeini. The new state faced the Iran–Iraq War (1980–1988) and ongoing tensions with the West, particularly over its nuclear program. Despite sanctions and internal unrest, including the 2009 Green Movement and 2022 protests, Iran remains a key regional power.

## Terms
- **Achaemenid Empire:** The first Persian Empire (c. 550–330 BC), founded by Cyrus the Great, known for its vast size and administrative system.
- **Sasanian Empire:** The last pre-Islamic Persian empire (224–651 AD), a major rival to the Roman/Byzantine Empire.
- **Persianization:** The process by which Persian culture, language, and identity were preserved and revived within a Muslim context after the Arab conquest.
- **Safavid Dynasty:** The ruling dynasty of Iran from 1501 to 1736 that established Shia Islam as the official state religion.
- **Qajar Dynasty:** The ruling dynasty of Iran from 1796 to 1925, during which Iran lost territory to Russia and faced increasing European influence.
- **Pahlavi Dynasty:** The last ruling dynasty of Iran (1925–1979), characterized by authoritarian modernization and close ties to the West.
- **Islamic Republic:** Iran's system of government since 1979, based on the principle of *velayat-e faqih* (guardianship of the Islamic jurist).
- **JCPOA:** The Joint Comprehensive Plan of Action (2015), an international agreement to limit Iran's nuclear program in exchange for sanctions relief.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The extent to which the 1979 revolution was a popular uprising versus a clerical takeover remains debated.
- The impact of the Islamic Golden Age on European Renaissance science is a subject of ongoing scholarly discussion.
- The future of Iran's nuclear program and its integration into the global community remains a central and unresolved geopolitical question.

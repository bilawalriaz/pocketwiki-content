# Saint Vincent and the Grenadines

## Overview
Saint Vincent and the Grenadines is an archipelagic country in the eastern Caribbean, part of the Windward Islands. Its history is marked by colonial contestation between European powers, the resistance and displacement of its indigenous Garifuna people, and a modern economy dependent on agriculture and tourism. The nation gained independence from Britain in 1979 and remains a Commonwealth realm, with its political and cultural institutions reflecting this colonial legacy.

## Timeline
- **c. 5000 BC** — First inhabited by Indigenous people from South America.
- **1498** — Christopher Columbus sights the island, naming it St. Vincent.
- **1719** — French establish the first European colony at Barrouallie.
- **1763** — British gain control of the island under the Treaty of Paris.
- **1795–1797** — Second Carib War; Garifuna defeated and largely deported.
- **1834** — Slavery abolished by the British Empire.
- **1902** — La Soufrière volcano erupts, killing 1,500–2,000 people.
- **1979** — Saint Vincent and the Grenadines achieves full independence.
- **2009** — A referendum to become a republic is defeated.
- **2021** — La Soufrière volcano erupts, forcing evacuation of 16,000 residents.
- **2025** — New Democratic Party wins general election, ending 24 years of ULP rule.

## Body

### History and Colonial Legacy
The island was first settled around 5000 BC, with later waves of Arawak and Kalinago (Carib) peoples. European contact began with Columbus in 1498. The French were the first to colonize it in 1719, importing enslaved Africans for plantations. Britain gained permanent control in 1763. The indigenous Garifuna (Black Caribs), an Afro-Indigenous creole people, resisted British rule in two major conflicts known as the Carib Wars. Their defeat in 1797 led to the deportation of nearly 5,000 people. British rule continued until independence in 1979, with the abolition of slavery in 1834 followed by the importation of indentured laborers from Portugal and India. The country has experienced multiple devastating eruptions of the La Soufrière volcano, in 1812, 1902, 1979, and 2021.

### Government and Politics
Saint Vincent and the Grenadines is a parliamentary democracy and constitutional monarchy, with King Charles III as head of state, represented by a Governor-General. The Prime Minister, currently Godwin Friday (since 2025), holds executive power. The legislature is the unicameral House of Assembly. The two main political parties are the centre-right New Democratic Party (NDP) and the centre-left Unity Labour Party (ULP). A 2009 referendum to replace the monarchy with a republic was defeated. The country has no formal armed forces.

### Economy and Society
The economy is lower-middle-income and heavily dependent on agriculture, particularly banana production, which is vulnerable to tropical storms. Tourism is a growing sector. Unemployment has historically been high. The population is predominantly of African descent, with minorities of mixed, East Indian, and European (mainly Portuguese) heritage. English is the official language, but Vincentian Creole is widely spoken. Christianity is the dominant religion, with Pentecostalism being the largest denomination as of 2023.

## Terms
- **Garifuna**: An Afro-Indigenous creole people descended from West African, Central African, and Arawak/Carib ancestors, who resisted British colonization.
- **Commonwealth realm**: A sovereign state within the Commonwealth of Nations that recognizes the British monarch as its head of state.
- **Kalinago**: The indigenous people of the Lesser Antilles, also known as Caribs, who inhabited Saint Vincent before European arrival.
- **La Soufrière**: An active stratovolcano on the island of Saint Vincent, responsible for major eruptions in 1812, 1902, 1979, and 2021.
- **Associate statehood**: A status granted by Britain in 1969 that gave Saint Vincent full control of internal affairs while remaining a dependent territory.
- **CARICOM**: The Caribbean Community, a regional organization promoting economic integration and cooperation among its member states.
- **OECS**: The Organisation of Eastern Caribbean States, a regional inter-governmental organization dedicated to economic harmonization and integration.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The proposal to transition from a constitutional monarchy to a republic was defeated in a 2009 referendum, but the debate over the country's head of state continues.
- Prime Minister Ralph Gonsalves has argued that European nations owe reparations to Caribbean nations for their role in the Atlantic slave trade, a position that has led to public protests during official visits.
- The country's heavy dependence on a single agricultural export (bananas) is cited as a major obstacle to sustainable economic development.

# Neolithic

## Overview
The Neolithic, or New Stone Age, is the final archaeological period of the Stone Age, spanning roughly from 10,000 BC to 2,000 BC. It is defined by the Neolithic Revolution, a fundamental shift from nomadic hunter-gatherer lifestyles to settled farming communities. This transition, involving the domestication of plants and animals, is considered one of the most significant developments in human history, laying the groundwork for complex societies.

## Timeline
- **c. 12,500–9,500 BC** — Natufian culture in the Levant shows early sedentism and use of wild cereals.
- **c. 10,200 BC** — Pre-Pottery Neolithic A (PPNA) begins in the Levant; true farming emerges.
- **c. 9500 BC** — Göbekli Tepe, a major ritual site, is constructed in southeastern Turkey.
- **c. 8800 BC** — Pre-Pottery Neolithic B (PPNB) begins; rectangular mud-brick houses appear.
- **c. 8000 BC** — Domestication of sheep, goats, cattle, and pigs becomes widespread.
- **c. 6400 BC** — Pottery Neolithic begins in the Fertile Crescent with cultures like Halaf.
- **c. 6500 BC** — Farming reaches southeastern Europe (e.g., Vashtëmi, Albania).
- **c. 4500 BC** — Neolithic begins in parts of Northwest Europe; Chalcolithic (Copper Age) starts in the Near East.
- **c. 3150 BC** — Neolithic ends in Egypt with the Protodynastic period.
- **c. 2000 BC** — Neolithic ends in China and Scandinavia.

## Body

### Origins and the Neolithic Package
The Neolithic began in the Levant around 10,200 BC, evolving from the Natufian culture. Climatic changes during the Younger Dryas (c. 10,000 BC) likely pressured populations to develop farming from their reliance on wild cereals. This "Neolithic package" included the cultivation of founder crops like wheat, lentils, and flax, and the domestication of animals. The transition was not uniform; for example, early farming societies in West Asia did not use pottery, while cultures in East Asia used pottery before agriculture.

### Regional Development
The Neolithic spread from the Fertile Crescent at different rates. In Europe, it was strongly linked to the migration of Anatolian farmers beginning around 9,000 years ago. In Northeast Africa, the first evidence for domestic plants and animals appears in the early fifth millennium BC, possibly through indigenous development or exchange. In Sub-Saharan Africa, the initial form of food production was mobile pastoralism, known as the Pastoral Neolithic. In East Asia, early sites like Pengtoushan (c. 7500 BC) show independent development of rice cultivation.

### Social and Cultural Characteristics
Neolithic societies were generally organized into small, relatively egalitarian tribes, though social inequality increased with the domestication of large animals (c. 8000 BC), which allowed for inherited wealth. People lived in permanent settlements, often in mud-brick houses, as seen at Çatalhöyük. The period is marked by polished stone tools, which enabled large-scale forest clearance for agriculture. While most societies remained simple, some late Neolithic cultures formed complex chiefdoms, and fortified settlements indicate increased inter-group conflict.

## Terms
- **Neolithic Revolution**: The wide-ranging set of developments, including farming and animal domestication, that marked the transition from hunter-gatherer to settled agricultural societies.
- **Domestication**: The process of adapting wild plants and animals for human use through selective breeding.
- **Sedentism**: The practice of living in one place for a long time, a key feature of Neolithic settlements.
- **Pre-Pottery Neolithic (PPN)**: An early phase of the Neolithic in the Levant characterized by farming and mud-brick architecture but before the widespread use of pottery.
- **Mudbrick**: Sun-dried brick made from clay and straw, a primary building material in early Neolithic settlements.
- **Polished stone tools**: Stone tools shaped by grinding and polishing, a defining technology of the Neolithic that allowed for more efficient woodworking and farming.
- **Pastoralism**: A form of agriculture centered on the herding of livestock, which was the primary food-producing strategy in some regions like Sub-Saharan Africa.
- **Chalcolithic**: The Copper Age, a transitional period following the Neolithic marked by the early use of copper metallurgy.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The primary stimulus for the adoption of agriculture in regions like Egypt is debated: was it driven by migration from the Near East or by indigenous development?
- The causes of the "Neolithic decline," a population collapse across Europe after 5000 BC, are uncertain, with possible explanations including climate change, plague, or mass migration.
- The social structure of Neolithic societies is debated; while some evidence suggests egalitarianism, other findings indicate the existence of influential individuals who could organize labor and the presence of warfare.
- The extreme drop in Y-chromosomal diversity during the Neolithic is poorly understood, with possible causes ranging from increased male mortality to the rise of patrilineal social structures.

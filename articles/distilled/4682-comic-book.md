# Comic book

## Overview
A comic book is a publication featuring sequential art in panels, often with dialogue in speech balloons, that tells a story. It is a major global entertainment medium with distinct regional traditions, a complex history of artistic evolution and social controversy, and a significant collector's market.

## Timeline
- **1842** — *The Adventures of Mr. Obadiah Oldbuck* printed in the US, the first known American prototype comic book.
- **1884** — *Ally Sloper's Half Holiday* published in the UK, notable for using sequential cartoons to unfold narrative.
- **1933** — *Famous Funnies: A Carnival of Comics* released in the US, considered the first true newsstand American comic book.
- **1938** — Superman debuts in *Action Comics #1*, launching the Golden Age of Comic Books and the superhero archetype.
- **1954** — Psychiatrist Fredric Wertham's *Seduction of the Innocent* prompts a US Senate investigation, leading to the industry's self-censoring Comics Code.
- **1956** — The Flash debuts in *Showcase #4*, beginning the Silver Age of Comic Books and a revival of superheroes.
- **Late 1960s** — Underground comix movement emerges, reflecting youth counterculture and distributed outside mainstream channels.
- **1978** — Will Eisner popularizes the term "graphic novel" with *A Contract with God*.
- **1980s** — The "British Invasion" of writers and artists (e.g., Alan Moore, Neil Gaiman) brings mature themes to mainstream comics.
- **2007** — Marvel launches its digital comics subscription service, marking a shift in distribution.
- **2015** — Eiichiro Oda's *One Piece* sets a Guinness World Record for most copies published for a single comic series.

## Body

### Origins and Structure
The modern comic book evolved from 19th-century sequential art and newspaper comic strips. Its core structure relies on panels, speech balloons, and text to convey narrative. In the US, the term "comic book" originally referred to compilations of humorous strips, but it now encompasses all genres. The medium's visual language, including panel size and layout, is fundamental to its storytelling.

### The American Comic Book Industry
The debut of Superman in 1938 created a major industry and defined the Golden Age. Historians divide American comics into eras: Golden (1938–), Silver (1956–), Bronze (early 1970s–), and Modern (mid-1980s–present). A pivotal event was the 1954 Senate investigation into comics' link to juvenile delinquency, which led to the strict, self-imposed Comics Code. This censorship stifled content until the 1970s. The industry later diversified with underground comix (late 1960s), alternative comics (1970s), and the graphic novel format.

### Global Markets and Traditions
Japan is the largest comic book market, with manga (漫画) being a dominant cultural form. South Korea has a thriving market for manhwa (만화) and the digitally-native webtoon format. France and Belgium have a strong tradition of bandes dessinées (BDs). British comics, from *The Beano* to *2000 AD*, have a distinct history, often targeting younger audiences but also producing influential mature works. Other significant markets include Spain (historietas/tebeos) and Italy (fumetti).

### Social Representation and Controversy
The history of American comics includes problematic racial and ethnic stereotyping. Early Black characters often relied on dehumanizing tropes like the "Sambo" or "coon" stereotypes. During WWII, government boards promoted narratives of racial tolerance for propaganda, but stereotypes persisted, particularly for Asian characters depicted as subhuman enemies. Later, Black characters were sometimes used to critique militant activism. Hispanic and Native American representation has also been limited, often relying on stereotypes like the "noble savage."

### Collecting and Market
Comic book collecting became a significant hobby with the rise of specialty stores in the 1970s. Key collectibles include first issues, first character appearances, and original artwork pages. High-value sales are driven by rarity and character popularity; for example, *Action Comics #1* (Superman's first appearance) has sold for over $3 million. Professional grading services like CGC now "slab" comics in protective cases, creating standardized valuation. The global comic book market was valued at $9.21 billion in 2021.

## Terms
- **Sequential Art**: The art of depicting a series of images in a specific order to tell a story or convey information.
- **Speech Balloon (or Bubble)**: A convex container in a comic panel that holds dialogue or other information, typically connected to a character by a tail.
- **Golden Age of Comic Books**: The period beginning in 1938 with the debut of Superman, which established the superhero archetype.
- **Comics Code Authority (CCA)**: A self-censorship body established by the US comic industry in 1954 in response to public concern over content.
- **Underground Comix**: Independent comics from the late 1960s/early 1970s that reflected counterculture and often featured explicit content.
- **Graphic Novel**: A term for a standalone comic book or a collected edition of a comic series, often implying a more literary or artistic ambition.
- **Manga**: Japanese comics or graphic novels, typically read right-to-left, with a distinct artistic style and genre range.
- **Manhwa**: Korean comics, influenced by manga but with distinct features, including the popular vertical-scroll webtoon format.
- **Bandes Dessinées (BD)**: The Franco-Belgian term for comics, literally meaning "drawn strips."
- **Slabbing**: The process of encasing a comic book in a hard plastic shell for preservation and grading by companies like CGC.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The precise demarcation between the Silver and Bronze Ages of American comics is less well-defined than other eras.
- The impact of Wertham's *Seduction of the Innocent* and the subsequent Comics Code on stifling artistic expression versus its role in industry self-preservation is debated.
- The evolution of digital distribution continues to reshape the market, with debates over its impact on traditional print comic stores and collector culture.
- The historical and ongoing struggle for authentic representation of racial and ethnic minorities in comics remains a critical area of scholarly and public discussion.

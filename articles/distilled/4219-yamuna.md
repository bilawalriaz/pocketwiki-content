# Yamuna

## Overview
The Yamuna is the second-largest tributary of the Ganges by discharge and India's longest tributary river. It is a vital water source for nearly 57 million people, supplying over 70% of Delhi's water, and is central to Hindu religious life, worshipped as the goddess Yamuna. However, severe pollution, particularly in its Delhi stretch, threatens its ecological health and utility.

## Timeline
- **c. 1500–1000 BCE** — Earliest mentions of the Yamuna in the Rig Veda.
- **305 BCE** — Greek officer Seleucus I Nicator records the river as Iomanes.
- **Before 288 BCE** — Greek traveller Megasthenes mentions the river in his *Indica*.
- **1335 CE** — The Western Yamuna Canal is built by Firuz Shah Tughlaq.
- **1873** — The Tajewala Barrage is constructed on the Yamuna.
- **1993** — The Yamuna Action Plan (YAP) is initiated to address pollution.
- **1994** — A water-sharing agreement is signed among five basin states and Delhi.
- **1999** — The modern Hathni Kund Barrage replaces the Tajewala Barrage.
- **2017** — Uttarakhand High Court declares the Yamuna a "legal person."
- **2025** — The 'Yamuna Master Plan' is introduced to clean the river's Delhi section.

## Body

### Course and Basin
The Yamuna originates from the Yamunotri Glacier at about 4,500 m on the southwestern slopes of the Bandarpunch peaks in Uttarakhand. It flows 1,376 km through Himachal Pradesh, Haryana, Delhi, and Uttar Pradesh, draining an area of 366,223 km² (40.2% of the Ganges Basin). It merges with the Ganges at Triveni Sangam in Prayagraj. The river creates the fertile Ganges-Yamuna Doab region, which supports a significant portion of India's population and agriculture. Its major tributaries include the Tons (its largest), Chambal, Sindh, Betwa, and Ken.

### Water Use and Infrastructure
The Yamuna's waters are heavily managed for irrigation and municipal supply. The Hathni Kund Barrage diverts water into the Western and Eastern Yamuna Canals. A 1994 Memorandum of Understanding governs water sharing among basin states. The river is designated as National Waterway NW110, with sections developed for navigation. Several barrages and dams, including the Wazirabad and Okhla barrages in Delhi, regulate its flow.

### Religious Significance
The Yamuna is one of Hinduism's seven sacred rivers, personified as the goddess Yamuna, daughter of the sun god Surya and twin sister of Yama, the god of death. Bathing in her waters is believed to free one from the torments of death. The river is intimately associated with the deity Krishna, and the Braj region (Mathura, Vrindavan) is a major pilgrimage center. Despite spiritual reverence, physical pollution has led many Hindus to stop ritually bathing in or using the river's water.

### Pollution and Ecology
The Yamuna is one of the world's most polluted rivers. The 22 km stretch in Delhi from Wazirabad to Okhla barrage, less than 2% of its length, accounts for nearly 80% of its pollution due to untreated sewage and industrial effluents. The river's ecology supports fish species, notably Cyprinidae, but populations of turtles and gharials have declined. Cleanup efforts include the Yamuna Action Plan (YAP) since 1993 and the 2025 'Yamuna Master Plan'.

## Terms
- **Tributary:** A river that flows into a larger river.
- **Doab:** A tract of land lying between two converging rivers.
- **Barrage:** A type of dam with gates that regulate water flow.
- **Palaeochannel:** An ancient or former course of a river.
- **Alluvial:** Soil, clay, or silt deposited by flowing water.
- **BOD (Biochemical Oxygen Demand):** A measure of organic pollution in water; lower values indicate cleaner water.
- **DO (Dissolved Oxygen):** Oxygen dissolved in water, essential for aquatic life; higher values indicate healthier water.
- **National Waterway:** A designated inland waterway for navigation in India.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Cause of Course Change:** Geological evidence suggests the Yamuna was once a tributary of the Ghaggar River (possibly the Vedic Sarasvati). While some argue a tectonic shift caused its eastward diversion to the Ganges, potentially contributing to the decline of the Harappan civilization, recent research suggests the diversion occurred during the Pleistocene, much earlier.
- **Spiritual vs. Physical Purity:** A debate exists among Hindus regarding whether physical pollution affects the river's spiritual purity. Some religious figures maintain the water remains spiritually pure, while others no longer use it for rituals due to contamination.
- **Effectiveness of Cleanup:** The success of long-term initiatives like the Yamuna Action Plan and the 2025 Master Plan in achieving significant pollution reduction is a subject of ongoing scrutiny and public concern.

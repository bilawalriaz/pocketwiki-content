# Lagos

## Overview
Lagos is a megacity and the economic engine of Nigeria, generating an estimated 30–35% of national GDP. Originating as a 15th-century Awori (Yoruba) settlement named Èkó, it was renamed by Portuguese explorers after Lagos, Portugal. It served as Nigeria’s federal capital until 1991, when the government moved to Abuja. Today, Metropolitan Lagos spans 16 of Lagos State’s 20 Local Government Areas (LGAs) and, as of late 2025, hosts an estimated 17–21 million residents, making it Africa’s most populous urban area. The city handles 88% of Nigeria’s exports through three ports and Murtala Muhammed International Airport, and anchors West Africa’s finance, technology (five of seven African "unicorns"), pharmaceutical, and entertainment (Nollywood, Afrobeats) sectors. Rapid, largely informal growth has created severe infrastructure deficits in housing, transport, water, and waste management, while climate change threatens the low-lying coast with sea-level rise and subsidence.

## Body

### Historical Development and Administration
Lagos began as an Awori farmstead (Èkó) in the 15th century. Portuguese contact (c. 1472) introduced the name "Lagos," linking the lagoon port to European maritime networks. British annexation (1861) made it a colonial hub; the 1914 Amalgamation Ceremony at Tinubu Square created modern Nigeria. Lagos served as the federal capital until December 1991, when the seat of government moved to planned city Abuja (decision legislated 1976).

Administratively, Lagos is not a single municipality. Metropolitan Lagos comprises 16 of Lagos State’s 20 LGAs; the state government provides region-wide services (roads, power, water, health, education). The former Lagos City Council (covering Lagos Island, Ikoyi, Victoria Island, and parts of mainland) was disbanded in 1976. Rapid post-1970s oil-boom growth absorbed outlying towns (Mushin, Ikeja, Agege) into a continuous conurbation. By 2006, the "Greater Metropolitan Lagos" or "Lagos Megacity Region" spilled into neighbouring Ogun State LGAs (Obafemi Owode, Sagamu, Ifo, Ado-Odo/Ota, Ewekoro), adding ~1,535 km² of built-up land.

### Demography and Population Disputes
Population figures are highly contested. The 2006 federal census recorded ~8–9 million for the conurbation; Lagos State’s parallel census claimed ~16 million (later 17.5 million for the state, implying ~15.5 million for the metro area). Independent research (Africopis/e-Geopolis) supported the federal figure, suggesting state estimates are inflated. UN projections (1999) predicted >20 million by 2010. Growth is driven by ~275,000–3,000 daily arrivals (1.1M/yr) seeking economic opportunity. The Yoruba are indigenous and demographically dominant, but all Nigerian ethnic groups are present. Estimates for late 2025 range 17–21 million.

### Geography: Island and Mainland
Lagos sits on islands and mainland separated by Lagos Lagoon, protected from the Atlantic by barrier islands and sand spits (e.g., Bar Beach, stretching 100 km).
*   **Island (Lagos Island, Eti-Osa LGAs):** Historic core, Central Business District (high-rises, banking on Marina), major markets (Idumota, Balogun), upscale neighbourhoods (Ikoyi, Victoria Island), and the Lekki Peninsula (Ajah, Lekki Phase 1, Ibeju-Lekki). Connected to Mainland by Carter, Eko (Second), and Third Mainland Bridges; Fourth Mainland Bridge construction slated 2022.
*   **Mainland (Surulere, Apapa, Lagos Mainland, Agege, Mushin, Oshodi-Isolo, Ikeja LGAs):** Densely populated, industrial, hosts Murtala Muhammed International Airport (Ikeja) and state capital Ikeja. Nightlife historically centred here (Yaba, Surulere) but shifted toward Island (Victoria Island, Lekki) recently.

### Climate and Climate Change
Tropical savanna (Aw): wet season May–Oct (peak June, 316 mm), dry season Nov–Apr; short dry spell July–Aug. Temperatures 28–32°C (82–90°F) year-round; hottest March (32–26°C), coolest August (28–24°C).
*   **Change impacts:** Under RCP 4.5 (~2.5–3°C warming by 2100), 2050 climate resembles present-day Panama City (+1.6°C annual mean). IPCC AR6 (2022) lists Lagos among 12 African cities most vulnerable to sea-level rise. Cumulative damages by 2050: US$65B (RCP 4.5) to US$86.5B (RCP 8.5); with ice-sheet instability, up to US$397B. Subsidence (up to 87 mm/yr) exacerbates relative sea-level rise.

### Economy: Structure and Key Sectors
Lagos generates 30–35% of Nigeria’s GDP. GaWC rates it "beta minus" (globalisation level of Manchester/Edinburgh), the highest in West/Central Africa. Central Business District on Lagos Island hosts HQs of major banks (Access, Zenith, GTB, First Bank—each >US$2B capital) and insurers. Ports handle 88% of exports: Apapa (oldest, largest, >5.7Mt/yr), Tin Can Island (1975), Lekki Deep Sea Port (commissioned early 2023, 50 km east, state-level not urban). Lekki Free Trade Zone (155 km² total, 30 km² developed) integrates industry, trade, logistics, tourism.

**Technology ("Africa’s Silicon Valley"):** 5 of 7 African unicorns (Flutterwave, Opay, Interswitch, Andela, Jumia) based in Lagos, concentrated in Yaba district. >90M internet users; only African city with both Google and Microsoft offices. MTN, Airtel provide 4G; Paystack, ULesson, Hotels.ng are key fintech/edtech/travel platforms.

**Industry:**
*   **Oil Refining:** Dangote Refinery (Lekki) started Dec 2023 at 650k bpd; Oct 2025 plan to double to 1.4M bpd (US$350M), becoming world’s largest. Ogun (Tongeji Island) and Ondo (Ilaje) announced rival refineries.
*   **Fertiliser:** Dangote plant (2022) produces 3 Mt/yr (~Germany’s consumption); filled gap after 2022 Russian export halt.
*   **Pharmaceuticals:** ~60% of Africa’s production capacity (2022); major firms (Emzor, Fidson, May & Baker, Swiss Pharma) in North Lagos.
*   **Automotive:** Nord Automobile (Sangotedo, Epe) assembles 8 models; faces demand/import-cost challenges post-2023 naira devaluation.
*   **Food/Beverage:** Nigerian Breweries (Heineken, Surulere) & Guinness (Ikeja) dominate; malt beer cultural staple. Imota rice hulling mill (largest sub-Saharan) targets 2.5M 50-kg bags/yr, 250k jobs. Dangote Sugar Refinery (Apapa) Q3 2022 turnover ₦288.3B (€590M), capacity 1.44 Mt/yr. New food hubs: Ikosi Fruit Market (Ketu, May 2023), Fresh Food Hub (Idi-Oro, Dec 2023); Ketu-Ereyun Food Logistics Park (1.2M m², target 2024 completion).
*   **Timber:** Sawmill moved Oko Baba → Timberville (2022) for modern facilities.
*   **Leather/Fashion:** Lagos Leather Fair largest in West Africa; Nigeria 6th global leather exporter (sources for Prada, Gucci, LV).

**Entertainment/Media:** Nollywood (Surulere) ranks 2nd/3rd globally by volume; PwC forecasts 85% growth to $15B. Quality/commercial shift post-*The Figurine* (2009) drove box-office records (*Half of a Yellow Sun* 2013, *The Wedding Party* 2016, *Battle on Buka Street* 2023). Major newspapers (Punch, Vanguard, Guardian, Nation, Sun, Tribune—founded 1949) produced in Lagos. TV: Channels TV (top rated), Arise, NTA, AIT; pidgin/Yoruba content moving to streaming.

### Transportation Infrastructure
**Roads:** Extensive network; Lagos–Ibadan & Lagos–Abeokuta Expressways are major inter-state controlled-access highways. Terminus for Trans–West African Coastal Highway (Badagry Expwy → Benin/Dakar) and Trans-Sahara Highway (Lagos–Ibadan Expwy → Algiers). Chronic peak-hour congestion.
**Bus:** BRT (2008, dedicated lanes Ikorodu Rd, Funsho Williams Ave, e-payment, Ashok Leyland/Yutong buses, operated by Primero) and LBSL (2019, Marcopolo buses). Cashless card (2021) works on both. Oshodi Bus Terminal (2019) largest in West Africa. Electric buses introduced May 2023. Legacy "Molue" buses nearly extinct.
**Rail:**
*   *Light Rail (LAMATA):* Blue Line (Mile 2–Marina, E-W, operational Feb 2023); Red Line (Agbado–Oyingbo, N-S, inaugurated Feb 29 2024). Extensions (Blue to Okokomaiko, Red to Marina) under construction. Planned: Green, Yellow, Purple, Brown, Orange lines. 583k passengers in first 4 months (early 2024).
*   *Standard Gauge:* Lagos–Ibadan double-track (June 2021), Mobolaji Johnson station. 3 trips Fri/Sat, 2 trips other days; cash tickets (e-ticketing planned). Operated by Nigerian Railway Corporation.
**Ferries:** ~2M passengers/month (2021). Five Cowries Terminal (commissioned Aug 2018, HQ of LASWA) connects Marina, Ikoyi, Victoria Island, Lekki, Apapa, Ikorodu, Badagry. Car park 800+ spaces. Operates 8am–5pm daily.
**Air:** Murtala Muhammed Intl Airport (MMIA) – largest/busiest in Africa, terminal 1978/opened 1979, major remodel 2013–2014 (lounge 4x, conveyors >1k pax/hr). Lekki-Epe Intl Airport approved Apr 2023.

### Utilities and Environment
**Waste:** 13,000 tonnes/day generated; only 40% collected, 13% recycled. Open burning common, worsening pollution.
**Water:** Tap water non-potable (distribution network contaminated; lagoon raw water too polluted). Supply drawn from Ogun and Owo rivers. Large slum populations lack piped access.
**Sewage:** No efficient system. Rain flushes open sewers into rivers/lagoon. Leaking septic tanks/latrines contaminate groundwater, wells, boreholes, and street-vended water.

### Culture and Creative Industries
**Architecture:** Tropical modernist (reinforced concrete, louvres, sun-screens) pioneered by Onafowokan Michael Olutusen, Oluwole Olumuyiwa. Brazilian-style (creole returnees) seen in Water House, Shitta Bey Mosque. Spatial logic 1960s–80s: core-periphery (Lagos Island nucleus; Yaba, Apapa, Surulere as growth zones).
**Monuments:** National Arts Theatre (Iganmu, oval/Metabolist, renovated Mar 2023, Blue Line station adjacent); Lekki-Ikoyi Link Bridge (first cable-stayed in Nigeria); Cathedral Church of Christ (1867–69, oldest Anglican); The Bookshop House (1973, sun-breaker facade cuts AC load 75%); Synagogue Church of All Nations (2004).
**Art:** Sculptors Ben Enwonwu (wood/bronze), Dotun Popoola (scrap metal), Peju Alatise, Idowu Sonaya, Collins Abinoro Akporode. Galleries: Nike Art Gallery (5-storey, ~8k works, largest West Africa), Red Door, Ovuomaroro, Alexis (Victoria Island). Documenta 11 platform (2002).
**Museums:** National Museum (Onikan, archaeology/ethnography); John K. Randle Centre (Yoruba culture, 2023, interactive, leads artifact repatriation); Kalakuta Republic (Fela Kuti); Mindscape Children’s Museum (2015, Nigeria’s first).
**Performance:** MUSON Centre (Musical Society of Nigeria) – choir (since 1995), Nigeria’s only pro symphony orchestra (since 2005), annual festival.
**Festivals:** Lagos Carnival (Jan), Eko Intl Film Festival (Mar), Lagos Black Heritage Carnival (Apr), Lagos Photo/Book & Art Festivals (Nov), Lagos Food Festival (Dec), Lagos Jazz Festival, The Experience (gospel, Dec, Tafawa Balewa Sq), Eyo Carnival (irregular, origin Iperu Remo).
**Cuisine:** Staples: eba/egusi, amala/ewedu, jollof rice, ofada rice, dodo (plantain), suya (spiced beef), iyan (pounded yam), asaro, egusi (melon soup). Wide international availability.
**Music:** Birthplace of Sakara, Nigerian hip hop, highlife, juju, fuji, Afrobeats. Historic recordings: Paul McCartney *Band on the Run* (EMI Lagos, 1973), James Brown (1970). Hosted global stars (Beyoncé, Ed Sheeran, Shakira, Jay-Z, etc.). Afrobeats stars (Wizkid, Tems, Davido, Asake) launched from Lagos studios.
**Film:** Nollywood centred in Surulere/Festac. Location for *Captain America: Civil War* (2016), *La unidad* (2020–23), *The Last Tree* (2019), *93 Days* (Ebola 2014, Danny Glover).

### Social Dynamics, Quality of Life, and Crime
**Inequality/Informal Economy:** Extreme wealth disparity. IMF: ~5.5M employed in informal sector in Lagos State (~75% of workforce)—street vendors, artisans, okada (motorbike taxi) drivers, market traders, domestic workers. Informal activity excluded from GDP/unemployment stats, causing significant underestimation of both welfare and joblessness.
**Quality of Life:** Contradictory indices. Statista (2023): 2nd worst globally (after Manila). Time Out (2024–26): Top 50 Best Cities (96% happy, >2/3 say easy to make friends). Brand Finance (2023): 5th most desirable in Africa. Caveat: indices often reflect Western subjective biases.
**Crime:** Reliable stats unavailable (underreporting, police underfunding/corruption). Council on Foreign Relations Nigeria Security Tracker (2012–2023): Lagos State 135 violent deaths in 24 months to July 2023 (5.6/million/yr) — safer than UK (10) or Turkey (24). Nigeria overall: 93/million/yr (62% police violence). Boko Haram negligible in Lagos (2023).

### Education and Healthcare
**Education:** 6-3-3-4 system (Primary, JSS, SSS, University). State + federal schools (e.g., King’s College, Queen’s College, FGC Ijanikin). Vocational: Institute for Industrial Technology (2000, Dual Training). Polytechnics: Yaba College of Technology (1934, Nigeria’s first, 16k+ students), Lagos City Polytechnic (1990, first private), Grace, Wolex, Ronik, Federal College of Fisheries, Federal College of Education (Tech) Akoka.
**Universities:** Public: UNILAG (1962, 55k+, 13 faculties), LASU (1983, multi-campus Ojo), LASUSTECH (Ikorodu, converted from LASPOTECH by Gov Sanwo-Olu). Private: Pan-Atlantic (1996, univ. status 2002, LBS, SMC, Virtual Museum of Modern Nigerian Art), National Open University (Victoria Island), Caleb (Imota). Medical: LASUCOM (Ikeja), CMUL (UNILAG), LASCOHET (Yaba, health tech).
**Healthcare:** Oldest Nigerian hospital in city; West Africa’s first air-operated emergency service. Public/private split across primary, secondary, tertiary levels.

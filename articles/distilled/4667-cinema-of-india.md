# Cinema of India

## Overview
Indian cinema is not a single industry but a collection of distinct film industries, each producing films in a specific language (e.g., Hindi, Telugu, Tamil). This multilingual, multi-ethnic art form has had a profound global influence since the mid-20th century, with India consistently ranking as the world's largest film producer by volume. Its evolution reflects India's social, political, and cultural history, from the silent era to the modern "pan-Indian" blockbuster.

## Timeline
- **1896** — First public film screenings in Bombay (Mumbai).
- **1913** — *Raja Harishchandra*, the first Indian feature film, is released.
- **1931** — *Alam Ara*, the first Indian sound film (talkie), is released.
- **1937** — *Kisan Kanya*, the first Indian colour film, is released.
- **Late 1940s–1960s** — The "Golden Age" of Indian cinema, featuring parallel cinema and social realism.
- **1957** — *Mother India* becomes the first Indian film nominated for an Academy Award.
- **1970s** — The term "Bollywood" is coined; the "angry young man" genre emerges.
- **1986** — India's annual film output reaches 833 films, making it the world's largest producer.
- **2015–2017** — S. S. Rajamouli's *Baahubali* duology pioneers the "pan-Indian film" phenomenon.
- **2022** — The song "Naatu Naatu" from *RRR* wins an Oscar, the first for an Indian film.
- **2024** — Regional film industries collectively account for ~60% of India's box office share.

## Body

### Structure and Scale
Indian cinema comprises numerous language-specific industries, with major production centres in Mumbai, Hyderabad, Chennai, and Kolkata. The term "Bollywood" specifically denotes the Hindi-language film industry based in Mumbai, not Indian cinema as a whole. Historically, Hindi cinema led in box office performance, but in recent years, Telugu cinema has emerged as a major competitor. As of 2022, the combined revenue of South Indian film industries surpassed that of Bollywood. Overseas Indians account for 12% of the industry's revenue.

### Historical Development
The silent era (1890s–1920s) began with Dadasaheb Phalke's *Raja Harishchandra* (1913). The sound era started with *Alam Ara* (1931), launching the tradition of song-and-dance musicals. The post-independence "Golden Age" (late 1940s–1960s) saw the rise of parallel cinema, a social realist movement led by Bengali filmmakers like Satyajit Ray, whose *Apu Trilogy* gained international acclaim. Commercial Hindi cinema thrived with stars like Dilip Kumar and Raj Kapoor.

From the 1970s, Hindi cinema was revitalized by screenwriters Salim–Javed, who created the "angry young man" archetype, famously portrayed by Amitabh Bachchan. This era also saw the consolidation of the masala film genre—a mix of action, comedy, romance, and drama. The late 1990s saw a resurgence of parallel cinema with "Mumbai noir" crime films. Since the 2000s, the "Three Khans" (Aamir, Shah Rukh, Salman) have dominated Bollywood, while a new generation of actors and female-centric films have gained prominence.

### Regional Industries and Pan-Indian Cinema
While Hindi cinema is prominent, other industries have rich histories and distinct identities. Telugu cinema is noted for technical innovation and the pan-Indian film movement, pioneered by S. S. Rajamouli's *Baahubali* films and *RRR*. Tamil cinema, based in Chennai, has a global diaspora audience and a tradition of addressing social issues. Malayalam cinema is acclaimed for its Golden Age of art films and technological firsts. Kannada, Marathi, and other industries have their own significant cinematic traditions.

The "pan-Indian film" is a mainstream commercial style, originating in Telugu cinema, designed for simultaneous release in multiple languages to appeal to a national audience. This model has reshaped the industry's economics and star system.

### Cultural Influences and Style
Indian popular cinema is shaped by ancient epics (Mahabharata, Ramayana), Sanskrit drama (with its *rasa* method of emotional conveyance), folk theatre, Parsi theatre, and Hollywood musicals. Parallel cinema, in contrast, was influenced by European neorealism. The masala film is a defining style, blending multiple genres. Music and dance are integral, with playback singers being major stars. Film music accounts for 48% of India's net music sales.

### International Influence
Indian films gained early popularity in the Soviet Union, the Middle East, and Southeast Asia. Filmmakers like Satyajit Ray influenced global cinema, inspiring directors such as Martin Scorsese. In recent decades, Bollywood musicals have influenced Western films like *Moulin Rouge!*, and Indian films have achieved major success in China. Indian productions have received multiple Academy Award nominations and wins.

## Terms
- **Bollywood**: The Hindi-language film industry based in Mumbai; often mistakenly used to refer to all of Indian cinema.
- **Masala film**: A genre that blends multiple elements (action, comedy, romance, drama, song, dance) in one film.
- **Parallel cinema**: Also called art cinema or the Indian New Wave; a movement emphasizing social realism and distinct from mainstream commercial cinema.
- **Pan-Indian film**: A mainstream commercial film style, originating in Telugu cinema, designed for simultaneous multi-language release to appeal to a national audience.
- **Playback singer**: A singer whose voice is recorded for actors to lip-sync on screen.
- **Rasa method**: A traditional Indian performance technique where the actor conveys emotions to the audience through empathy, rather than Western method acting.
- **Talkie**: A film with synchronized recorded sound, as opposed to a silent film.
- **Dacoit film**: A genre focusing on bandits or outlaws, often with social themes.

## Debates and Open questions
- The relative decline of Hindi cinema's box office share versus the rise of regional industries (especially Telugu and Tamil) is an ongoing shift, with regional films collectively accounting for ~60% of revenue in 2024.
- The impact of the pan-Indian film model on the diversity and financial health of smaller regional industries is debated.
- The influence of OTT (streaming) platforms on traditional theatrical distribution and film-making practices is a significant and evolving topic.

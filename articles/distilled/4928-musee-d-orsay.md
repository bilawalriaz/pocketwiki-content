# Musée d'Orsay

## Overview
The Musée d'Orsay is a major art museum in Paris, France, housed in a former Beaux-Arts railway station. It is renowned for holding the world's largest collection of Impressionist and post-Impressionist masterpieces, bridging the chronological gap between the collections of the Louvre and the Centre Pompidou.

## Timeline
- **1898–1900** — The Gare d'Orsay railway station is built for the 1900 Exposition Universelle.
- **1970** — Permission is granted to demolish the station, but the plan is halted.
- **1978** — The station is listed as a Historic Monument; a competition to convert it into a museum is organized.
- **1981** — Gae Aulenti is chosen to design the museum's interior.
- **December 1986** — The Musée d'Orsay is officially opened by President François Mitterrand.
- **2016** — The museum agrees to keep the donated Marlene and Spencer Hays Collection together.
- **2020** — A decade-long renovation project, "Orsay Grand Ouvert," begins.
- **May 2026** — A permanent exhibition of artworks looted or sold under duress during the Nazi occupation opens.

## Body
### From Station to Museum
The museum building was originally the Gare d'Orsay, a railway station designed by Lucien Magne, Émile Bénard, and Victor Laloux for the 1900 World's Fair. By the 1970s, the station was slated for demolition, but cultural authorities intervened. The idea to convert it into a museum, championed by the Directorate of the Museum of France and accepted by President Georges Pompidou, was to create a space for art from 1848 to 1914. A 1978 competition was won by ACT Architecture, with interior design by Gae Aulenti. The museum opened in 1986.

### The Collection
The Musée d'Orsay's collection focuses on French art from 1848 to 1914, including paintings, sculptures, furniture, and photography. It is particularly celebrated for its Impressionist and post-Impressionist holdings, featuring major works by artists such as Monet, Renoir, Degas, Van Gogh, Cézanne, and Gauguin. Many of these works were previously housed at the Galerie nationale du Jeu de Paume. The sculpture collection, which fell out of fashion in the mid-20th century, was revitalized by the museum's spacious nave and now includes over 2,200 works, with key pieces by Rodin and Claudel.

### Recent Developments
In 2016, the museum received the significant Hays Collection of about 600 post-Impressionist works, the largest donation of foreign art to France since World War II. To accommodate this and other acquisitions, a major renovation project, "Orsay Grand Ouvert," began in 2020, funded in part by a €20 million anonymous donation. The museum also opened a permanent exhibition in 2026 for artworks looted during the Nazi occupation.

## Terms
- **Beaux-Arts**: An academic art style characterized by grandeur, rich detail, and elaborate ornamentation, exemplified by the museum's original station architecture.
- **Impressionism**: A 19th-century art movement characterized by small, thin brush strokes and an emphasis on the accurate depiction of light in its changing qualities.
- **Post-Impressionism**: A late 19th-century art movement that extended Impressionism while rejecting its limitations, emphasizing more symbolic content and formal order.
- **Gare d'Orsay**: The former railway station building that now houses the museum, constructed for the 1900 Exposition Universelle.
- **Exposition Universelle**: A world's fair held in Paris in 1900, for which the Gare d'Orsay was built.
- **Réseau Express Régional (RER)**: A hybrid suburban commuter rail and rapid transit system serving Paris and its suburbs; its Line C runs beneath the museum.
- **Orsay Grand Ouvert**: The name for the museum's decade-long renovation project beginning in 2020.

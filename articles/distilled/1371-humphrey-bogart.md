# Humphrey Bogart

## Overview  
Humphrey Bogart (December 25, 1899 – January 14, 1957) was an American actor whose performances in classic Hollywood cinema—including *The Maltese Falcon*, *Casablanca*, and *The African Queen*—cemented his status as a cultural icon. In 1999, the American Film Institute named him the greatest male star of classic American cinema. His transition from supporting gangster roles to leading man, combined with his off-screen persona and marriages to Mayo Methot and Lauren Bacall, shaped both his legacy and the evolution of film noir.

## Body  

### Early Life and Background  
Born into a well-to-do family, Bogart was the son of Belmont DeForest Bogart, a cardiopulmonary surgeon, and Maud Humphrey, a commercial illustrator who later became art director of the fashion magazine *The Delineator* and a militant suffragette. Raised in Manhattan and upstate New York, he was teased as a child for his appearance and mannerisms. His parents were emotionally distant, fostering a rebellious streak. He attended Phillips Academy but left after one semester, possibly due to disciplinary issues or academic failure.

### Naval Service  
In 1918, during World War I, Bogart enlisted in the U.S. Navy. He served until 1919, reaching the rank of Petty Officer Second Class. Stories about the origin of his lip scar vary—some attribute it to wartime injury, others to a childhood accident or a bar fight. During World War II, he attempted re-enlistment but was rejected due to age; instead, he volunteered for the Coastal Picket Force in 1944.

### Rise in Hollywood  
After working odd jobs and dabbling in theater, Bogart made his film debut in *The Dancing Town* (1928). For over a decade, he was typecast as a gangster in B-movies, earning praise for roles in *The Petrified Forest* (1936) and *Dead End* (1937). His breakthrough came with *High Sierra* (1941), followed by his iconic portrayal of Sam Spade in *The Maltese Falcon* (1941), widely regarded as one of the first great film noirs.

### Film Noir and Stardom  
Bogart became synonymous with film noir, starring in classics such as *The Maltese Falcon*, *The Big Sleep* (1946), and *Dead Reckoning* (1947). His private detective characters set the template for future noir protagonists. In *Casablanca* (1942), he played Rick Blaine, a role that elevated him to international stardom. His performance earned him an Academy Award nomination and established him as a leading man.

### Personal Life and Marriages  
Bogart married three times: to Helen Menken on May 20, 1926 (divorced 1927), Mary Philips on April 3, 1928 (divorced 1937), and Mayo Methot on August 21, 1938 (divorced 1945). His marriage to Methot was tumultuous, marked by mutual alcoholism and violence. In 1945, he divorced Methot and married actress Lauren Bacall, with whom he had two children. Their partnership, both personal and professional, became legendary, highlighted by collaborations in *Dark Passage* (1947) and *Key Largo* (1948).

### Later Career and Legacy  
In his later years, Bogart formed Santana Productions and starred in critically acclaimed films such as *The Treasure of the Sierra Madre* (1948) and *The African Queen* (1951), for which he won the Academy Award for Best Actor. He was a founding member and the original leader of the Hollywood Rat Pack. Despite declining health, he continued acting until his death in 1957 from esophageal cancer. His legacy endures through his iconic roles, enduring popularity, and influence on American cinema.

## Terms  
- **Film Noir**: A cinematic genre characterized by dark themes, moral ambiguity, and stylized visuals, often featuring hard-boiled detectives and femme fatales.  
- **Typecasting**: The practice of casting actors repeatedly in similar roles based on their previous performances.  
- **Santana Productions**: Bogart’s independent film production company, named after his yacht.  
- **Rat Pack**: A group of entertainers, including Frank Sinatra and Sammy Davis Jr., known for their performances in Las Vegas and Hollywood.  
- **Code of Honor**: A personal moral framework guiding behavior, often depicted in Bogart’s characters.  
- **Supporting Role**: A minor acting part that supports the main characters.  
- **Leading Man**: The primary male actor in a film, typically playing the hero or romantic lead.  
- **Cinematography**: The art and technology of motion-picture photography.  
- **Box Office**: A measure of a film’s commercial success based on ticket sales.  
- **Eulogy**: A speech or piece of writing praising someone who has died.

## Debates and Open Questions  
- **Birth Date Dispute**: Some sources claim Bogart was born on January 23, 1900, but biographers confirm December 25, 1899, based on birth announcements and census records.  
- **Origin of Lip Scar**: Multiple conflicting accounts exist regarding how Bogart acquired his distinctive scar, including wartime injury, bar fights, and childhood accidents.  
- **Phillips Academy Departure**: The exact reason for Bogart leaving Phillips Academy remains unclear, with various theories including expulsion, academic failure, or family withdrawal.  
- **Casablanca’s Ending**: The final scene of *Casablanca* was rewritten multiple times, with some debate over whether the original ending was more pessimistic.  
- **The Big Sleep Plot**: The film’s complex narrative has led to ongoing speculation about unresolved plot points, including the identity of the chauffeur’s killer.  
- **Legacy of *Beat the Devil***: Initially poorly received, the film has gained critical appreciation over time, raising questions about its place in Bogart’s filmography.

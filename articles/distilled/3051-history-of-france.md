# History of France

## Overview
The history of France spans from prehistoric settlements to its current status as a major global power. It is a story of territorial consolidation, the rise and fall of monarchies and empires, revolutionary upheaval, and the development of a modern nation-state. Understanding this history is key to grasping the foundations of European politics, culture, and the concept of the nation-state itself.

## Timeline
- **c. 600 BC** — Ionian Greeks found Massalia (Marseille).
- **58–51 BC** — Julius Caesar conquers Gaul in the Gallic Wars.
- **486** — Clovis I unites most of Gaul, founding the Merovingian dynasty.
- **843** — Treaty of Verdun divides the Carolingian Empire, creating West Francia (future France).
- **987** — Hugh Capet founds the Capetian dynasty, beginning the Kingdom of France.
- **1214** — Philip II defeats the Angevin Empire at the Battle of Bouvines, consolidating royal power.
- **1337–1453** — Hundred Years' War between the Houses of Valois and Plantagenet.
- **1789** — French Revolution begins with the storming of the Bastille.
- **1804** — Napoleon Bonaparte crowned Emperor, founding the First French Empire.
- **1870** — The Third Republic is established after the Franco-Prussian War.
- **1914–1918** — World War I; France suffers massive casualties on the Western Front.
- **1940** — Nazi Germany conquers France; the Vichy regime is established.
- **1944** — Liberation of France; Charles de Gaulle leads the Provisional Government.
- **1958** — Charles de Gaulle establishes the Fifth Republic.

## Body

### From Gaul to the Frankish Kingdom
The territory of modern France was known to the Romans as Gaul, inhabited by Celtic tribes. After Roman conquest (58–51 BC), a Gallo-Roman culture emerged. Following the decline of Roman authority, Frankish king Clovis I united most of Gaul in the late 5th century. His conversion to Catholicism secured clerical support. The Frankish realm later reached its peak under Charlemagne, but was divided by the Treaty of Verdun in 843, with West Francia forming the core of the future French kingdom.

### The Medieval Kingdom and the Hundred Years' War
The Capetian dynasty, founded in 987, gradually strengthened royal authority from a limited base. Key victories like Bouvines (1214) under Philip II enhanced royal power. A succession crisis in 1328 led to the Hundred Years' War (1337–1453) with England. The war, marked by figures like Joan of Arc, ended with a Valois victory, strengthening French nationalism and monarchical power. This period saw the emergence of a centralized state.

### The Ancien Régime and Revolution
Over the following centuries, France became a centralized absolute monarchy. Religious conflicts culminated in the French Wars of Religion, resolved by the Edict of Nantes (1598). Under Louis XIV (reigned 1643–1715), France became Europe's dominant power, though costly wars strained its treasury. The Enlightenment challenged traditional authority. A financial crisis led to the Estates General of 1789, which evolved into the National Assembly and sparked the French Revolution. The monarchy was abolished, leading to the radical Reign of Terror and the rise of Napoleon Bonaparte.

### Empire, Republic, and War
Napoleon's First Empire (1804–1815) spread revolutionary ideals across Europe before his defeat. The 19th century saw political instability, with regimes changing frequently until the establishment of the Third Republic (1870). France built a vast colonial empire. It played a decisive role in World War I but suffered devastating losses. In World War II, France was conquered by Nazi Germany in 1940. The Vichy regime collaborated, while General Charles de Gaulle led the Free French Forces. France was liberated in 1944.

### The Fifth Republic and Modern France
The post-war Fourth Republic faced crises in its colonies, notably in Indochina and Algeria. In 1958, Charles de Gaulle established the Fifth Republic with a strong presidency. He granted independence to Algeria and pursued an independent foreign policy, developing a nuclear deterrent and challenging U.S. dominance within NATO. France became a founding member of the European Economic Community (later the EU). Since the 1970s, France has navigated economic challenges, social tensions related to immigration, and its role as a global power within the European framework.

## Terms
*   **Gaul:** The region covering most of modern France, Belgium, and parts of Germany and Italy, inhabited by Celtic tribes before Roman conquest.
*   **Capetian Dynasty:** The royal dynasty founded by Hugh Capet in 987, which ruled France continuously until 1328 and through its cadet branches until 1848.
*   **Hundred Years' War:** A series of conflicts (1337–1453) between the House of Valois (France) and the House of Plantagenet (England) over the French throne and feudal territories.
*   **Ancien Régime:** The political and social system of the Kingdom of France before the French Revolution of 1789, characterized by absolute monarchy and feudal privileges.
*   **Reign of Terror:** The period of the French Revolution (1793–94) during which the Revolutionary Tribunal executed thousands of perceived enemies of the revolution.
*   **Napoleonic Code:** The civil legal code established under Napoleon I in 1804, which spread French legal principles across Europe and influenced many other legal systems.
*   **Vichy France:** The collaborationist government (1940–1944) that administered the unoccupied zone of France during World War II under Marshal Philippe Pétain.
*   **Fifth Republic:** The current republican government of France, established in 1958 by Charles de Gaulle, characterized by a strong executive presidency.

## Debates and Open questions
*   The extent to which the Hundred Years' War awakened a modern sense of French nationalism versus being a series of feudal struggles is debated.
*   The causes and legacy of the French Revolution remain subjects of intense scholarly debate, particularly regarding its radical phase and long-term impact on democracy.
*   The success and failure of France's colonial "civilizing mission" and the long-term effects of decolonization on French society and identity are ongoing topics of discussion.

# Monrovia

## Overview
Monrovia is the capital and largest city of Liberia, serving as the nation's political, economic, and cultural center. Founded in 1822 by the American Colonization Society as a settlement for formerly enslaved and freeborn African Americans, it became the capital upon Liberia's independence in 1847. The city is the country's most densely populated region, housing roughly one-third of Liberia's total population, and has been shaped by its colonial origins, civil wars, and ongoing reconstruction.

## Body

### Founding and Early History
The area around Cape Mesurado was a pre-existing trade crossroads inhabited by various ethnic groups. In 1821, the ACS brought the first African American settlers to West Africa. They were relocated to Providence Island in January 1822 and established a settlement initially called Christopolis. The city was renamed Monrovia in 1824 in honor of President James Monroe, a supporter of the ACS. It became the capital of the Republic of Liberia in 1847. By the early 20th century, the population was predominantly Americo-Liberian, and the city's architecture reflected American Southern styles.

### Civil Wars and Reconstruction
Monrovia experienced rapid population growth during the Liberian civil wars (1989–2003), driven by internal displacement. The conflicts caused extensive damage to infrastructure and public services, particularly during the siege of Monrovia. Key battles included clashes in 1990 and an assault in 1992. The wars ended following the Women of Liberia Mass Action for Peace movement, which began in Monrovia in 2002. Since 2003, the city has undergone gradual reconstruction, though it faces ongoing challenges in housing, sanitation, and transportation.

### Geography and Climate
Monrovia lies on the Cape Mesurado peninsula between the Atlantic Ocean and the Mesurado River, which forms a large natural harbor. It has a tropical monsoon climate and is the wettest capital city in the world, with average annual rainfall of 182 inches (4,600 mm). Temperatures are fairly constant year-round, averaging around 79.5 °F (26.4 °C).

### Economy and Infrastructure
The city's economy is dominated by the Freeport of Monrovia, the country's principal seaport, and government offices. Main exports include latex and iron ore. The port is a central component of the economy, and Monrovia is the home port for 10-15% of the world's merchant shipping registered under Liberia's Flag of Convenience. The nearest airport is Spriggs Payne Airport, with the larger Roberts International Airport 37 miles (60 km) away. Road networks have been rebuilt by the World Bank and government since 2005.

### Administration and Culture
Greater Monrovia is divided into 16 zones and 161 communities, administered by the Monrovia City Corporation (MCC). The city is Liberia's hub for education, hosting the University of Liberia and other institutions, and for media, with a newspaper industry dating to the 1820s. Cultural attractions include the Liberian National Museum and major sports complexes. Pollution and inadequate sanitation are significant challenges, with irregular waste collection and limited access to clean toilets.

## Terms
- **American Colonization Society (ACS):** An organization founded to facilitate the emigration of freeborn and formerly enslaved African Americans to Africa.
- **Flag of Convenience:** A business practice where a ship's owner registers a vessel in a foreign country to take advantage of different regulations, often used by Liberia.
- **Köppen climate classification:** A system for classifying the world's climates based on temperature and precipitation patterns.
- **Americo-Liberian:** A term for the descendants of the African American settlers who founded Liberia and dominated its early political and social elite.
- **Tropical monsoon climate (Am):** A climate type characterized by a short dry season and heavy annual rainfall, as seen in Monrovia.
- **Freeport of Monrovia:** Liberia's principal seaport and a major economic hub, significantly expanded during World War II.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The circumstances and nature of the 1821 land purchase at Cape Mesurado by the ACS from local leaders are subject to historical debate, with modern scholarship revisiting earlier claims.
- The origin of the "-via" suffix in Monrovia's name is debated, with theories linking it to either a West African deity or a mythological figure.

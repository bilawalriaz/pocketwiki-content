# Hernán Cortés

## Overview
Hernán Cortés (c. 1485–1547) was a Spanish conquistador whose expedition caused the fall of the Aztec Empire, bringing mainland Mexico under Spanish rule. This pivotal event in the Spanish colonization of the Americas established New Spain and demonstrated the complex interplay of military strategy, indigenous alliances, and imperial ambition.

## Timeline
- **c. 1485** — Born in Medellín, Spain, to lesser nobility.
- **1504** — Arrives in Hispaniola as a colonist.
- **1511** — Joins the expedition to conquer Cuba.
- **October 1518** — Appointed captain-general for a Mexico expedition by Governor Velázquez.
- **February 1519** — Ignores Velázquez's recall order and sails for Mexico.
- **November 1519** — Enters Tenochtitlán, received by Emperor Moctezuma II.
- **June–July 1520** — "Noche Triste" escape from Tenochtitlán after a rebellion.
- **August 1521** — Captures Cuauhtémoc, completing the conquest.
- **1524–1526** — Leads expedition to Honduras, executing Cuauhtémoc en route.
- **1528** — Returns to Spain; in 1529 is granted the title Marquess of the Valley of Oaxaca.
- **1535** — Antonio de Mendoza is appointed Viceroy of New Spain, limiting Cortés's authority.
- **2 December 1547** — Dies in Castilleja de la Cuesta, Spain.

## Body

### Early Life and Career in the Indies
Born to *hidalgos* (lesser nobility) in Medellín, Cortés briefly studied law before leaving for Hispaniola in 1504. He gained an *encomienda* and served as a notary. In 1511, he joined the conquest of Cuba under Diego Velázquez, who later appointed him to high offices. A strained relationship with Velázquez, partly due to a romantic involvement with the governor's sister-in-law Catalina Suárez (whom he married), set the stage for future conflict.

### Conquest of the Aztec Empire (1519–1521)
In 1518, Velázquez appointed Cortés to lead a Mexican expedition but revoked the order. Cortés ignored the recall, sailing in February 1519 with about 500 men. He allied with indigenous groups hostile to the Aztecs, such as the Tlaxcalans, and used interpreters like Gerónimo de Aguilar and Doña Marina (La Malinche). After founding Veracruz and scuttling his ships to prevent retreat, he marched on Tenochtitlán. He was peacefully received by Moctezuma II in November 1519 but soon took him hostage. A rebellion, followed by a Spanish massacre at the Great Temple, led to a siege. After the disastrous "Noche Triste" retreat and a victory at Otumba, Cortés regrouped with Tlaxcalan allies. A prolonged siege ended with Tenochtitlán's capture and destruction on August 13, 1521.

### Governance and Conflict with the Crown
Cortés was appointed governor and captain general of New Spain, but royal officials were sent to oversee him. He founded Mexico City on Tenochtitlán's ruins and imposed the *encomienda* system. His authority was challenged by rivals like Velázquez and Bishop Fonseca. After a turbulent period, he returned to Spain in 1528, was honored with the Order of Santiago and, in 1529, the title Marquess of the Valley of Oaxaca, but was not reinstated as governor. Returning to Mexico in 1530, his civil power was limited by the 1535 appointment of Antonio de Mendoza as viceroy. Cortés focused on exploration, discovering the Baja California peninsula in 1536, and managing his estates and silver mines.

### Later Life and Legacy
Cortés returned to Spain in 1541, heavily in debt and facing lawsuits. He participated in a failed 1541 naval expedition against Algiers. He died in 1547, requesting burial in Mexico. His body was moved multiple times, reflecting his contested legacy. He had numerous children, including Martín Cortés, his son with La Malinche. His *Cartas de Relación* (letters to King Charles V) are his primary surviving writings. His life is depicted in sources ranging from the laudatory biography by Francisco López de Gómara to the critical accounts of Bartolomé de las Casas and indigenous perspectives in the Florentine Codex.

## Terms
- **Conquistador**: A Spanish soldier and explorer involved in the military conquest of the Americas in the 16th century.
- **Encomienda**: A Spanish colonial system granting a colonist the right to the labor and tribute of indigenous people, often justified as a means to Christianize them.
- **Alcalde**: A municipal magistrate or judge in Spanish towns and colonies.
- **Tlatoani**: The Nahuatl term for the ruler or emperor of an Aztec city-state.
- **Mestizo**: A person of mixed European and Indigenous American ancestry. Martín Cortés is often called "the first mestizo."
- **Noche Triste**: The "Sad Night" of June 30–July 1, 1520, when the Spanish suffered heavy losses fleeing Tenochtitlán.
- **Juez de residencia**: A Spanish judicial officer appointed to investigate a colonial official's conduct at the end of their term.
- **Cartas de Relación**: The five official letters written by Cortés to King Charles V describing his expedition and conquest.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Moctezuma's Reception**: Whether Moctezuma initially believed Cortés was the returning god Quetzalcoatl is a contested interpretation, with some modern historians challenging this traditional narrative.
- **Cortés's Character**: Historical sources portray him either as a ruthless, ambitious mutineer or as a noble cavalier who expanded the Spanish Empire and facilitated Christian evangelization.
- **Treatment by the Crown**: While Cortés claimed he was unjustly treated, other evidence suggests his disobedience and vanity contributed to his loss of political authority.
- **Death of Catalina Suárez**: The mysterious death of his first wife in 1522 led to accusations and an investigation, with suspicions of murder never fully resolved.

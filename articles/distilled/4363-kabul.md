# Kabul

## Overview
Kabul is the capital and largest city of Afghanistan, serving as its political, cultural, and economic center for centuries. Situated at a high altitude in a strategic mountain valley, it has been a crossroads of empires and trade routes, including the Silk Road. Its history is marked by repeated conquest, periods of prosperity, and devastating modern conflicts, making its story central to understanding Afghanistan's complex past.

## Timeline
- **c. 1500–2000 BC** — The Kabul River and a settlement called Kubha are mentioned in the Rigveda.
- **549 BC** — Kabul becomes part of the Achaemenid Empire after the fall of the Median Empire.
- **c. 45 AD** — Conquered by Kushan Emperor Kujula Kadphises, beginning centuries of Kushan rule.
- **870** — Ya'qub bin Laith as-Saffar conquers Kabul, establishing the first Islamic dynasty in the region.
- **1504** — Babur captures Kabul, making it his headquarters and later a principal Mughal city.
- **1776** — Timur Shah Durrani moves the capital of the Durrani Empire from Kandahar to Kabul.
- **1919** — King Amanullah Khan announces Afghanistan's full independence from British influence at Eidgah Mosque.
- **1978** — The Saur Revolution occurs; President Daoud is assassinated.
- **1979** — The Soviet Union invades Afghanistan, occupying Kabul heavily.
- **1992** — Following the fall of the communist government, civil war between mujahideen factions devastates the city.
- **1996** — The Taliban captures Kabul and establishes the Islamic Emirate of Afghanistan.
- **2001** — The Northern Alliance captures Kabul after a U.S.-led invasion.
- **2021** — The Taliban recaptures Kabul following the withdrawal of U.S. and coalition forces.

## Body

### Antiquity and Early History
Kabul's origins are ancient but obscure, with references in texts like the Rigveda and Avesta. It was part of successive empires including the Achaemenid, Seleucid, Mauryan, and Kushan. The city's strategic location made it a prize for rulers and a hub for trade and cultural exchange between India, Persia, and Central Asia. By the 7th century, the Chinese pilgrim Xuanzang recorded it as "Gāofù."

### Islamic Conquest and Mongol Invasion
The Islamic conquest reached Kabul in 642 AD, but the city, ruled by the Hindu Shahis, resisted Islamization for over two centuries. It was permanently conquered by the Ghaznavids around 977. Subsequent centuries saw rule by the Ghurids, Khwarazmshahs, and others. The Mongol invasion in the 13th century caused major destruction, leading to a population exodus toward the Indian subcontinent.

### Timurid, Mughal, and Durrani Eras
In the late 14th century, Kabul became a major trading center in Timur's kingdom. In 1504, Babur captured it, making it the base for his Mughal Empire. Under the Mughals, Kabul prospered as a summer capital, mint, and military base. After Nader Shah's invasion in 1738, the city eventually became the capital of the new Afghan state under Ahmad Shah Durrani in 1747, with Timur Shah formally moving the capital there in 1776.

### 19th and Early 20th Century
Kabul was the site of conflict during the First (1839–1842) and Second (1878–1880) Anglo-Afghan Wars, suffering destruction and occupation. The 20th century brought modernization under kings like Habibullah Khan and Amanullah Khan, who introduced electricity, telephones, and modern education. Amanullah's ambitious plan for a new capital at Darulaman was largely abandoned after his overthrow in 1929. Under Zahir Shah, Kabul continued to develop with foreign assistance, building infrastructure and industries.

### Late 20th Century: War and Taliban Rule
The 1978 Saur Revolution and the 1979 Soviet invasion ended a period of relative tranquility. Kabul became a Soviet command center, its population swelling with refugees. After the Soviet withdrawal in 1989 and the fall of the communist government in 1992, rival mujahideen factions engaged in a brutal civil war that destroyed much of the city. The Taliban captured Kabul in 1996, imposing strict Islamic law until their ouster in 2001.

### 21st Century
Following the 2001 U.S.-led invasion, Kabul experienced rapid population growth and reconstruction, with new housing, businesses, and restored landmarks. Despite frequent terrorist attacks, it became one of the world's fastest-growing cities. A high-security "Green Zone" was established. The city fell to the Taliban again in August 2021, leading to a new period of Taliban rule.

## Terms
- **Primate city**: A city that is disproportionately larger and more influential than any other in the country.
- **Silk Road**: An ancient network of trade routes connecting the East and West, crucial for commerce and cultural exchange.
- **Mujahideen**: Islamic guerrilla fighters, notably those who resisted the Soviet occupation in the 1980s.
- **Saur Revolution**: The 1978 communist coup in Afghanistan that overthrew the government of Daoud Khan.
- **Sharia**: Islamic law derived from the Quran and the teachings of the Prophet Muhammad.
- **Mikrorayon**: A type of high-density, Soviet-style residential housing complex.
- **Gozar**: The neighborhood-level administrative unit in Kabul's municipal structure.
- **Dari**: A dialect of Persian that serves as the lingua franca in Kabul and much of Afghanistan.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The exact origin and etymology of the name "Kabul" remain unknown, with various theories involving Sanskrit, Greek, and Persian roots.
- The precise population of Kabul is uncertain due to the lack of a recent, comprehensive census, with various estimates provided by different sources.
- The historical impact of the 2021 Taliban takeover on Kabul's social fabric, economy, and future development is still unfolding and subject to ongoing analysis.

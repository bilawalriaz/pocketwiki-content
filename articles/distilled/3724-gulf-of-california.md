# Gulf of California

## Overview
The Gulf of California, also known as the Sea of Cortés, is a marginal sea of the Pacific Ocean separating the Baja California peninsula from the Mexican mainland. It is renowned for its exceptional ecological diversity, complex geology, and significant economic and conservation challenges.

## Timeline
- **c. 5.3 million years ago** — The gulf forms as tectonic forces rift the Baja California peninsula from the North American plate.
- **1540** — Melchior Díaz reaches the northern boundary of the gulf.
- **1605** — Juan de Oñate reaches the gulf overland via the Colorado River.
- **August 2, 1978** — The gulf's islands are enacted as a "Area Reserve and Migratory Bird Refuge and Wildlife."
- **June 2000** — The islands are designated a flora and fauna protection area.
- **2000** — A single management program for the islands is published.
- **2019** — The gulf is added to the List of World Heritage in Danger due to the imminent extinction of the vaquita.

## Body

### Geography and Formation
The gulf is approximately 1,126 km long and 48–241 km wide, with a surface area of about 177,000 km². Its complex geology, linked to plate tectonics, results in maximum depths exceeding 3,000 meters. The gulf formed around 5.3 million years ago when tectonic forces rifted the Baja California peninsula from the North American plate, with the East Pacific Rise propagating up its center. The Colorado River Delta blocks the sea from extending further north into the Mexicali and Imperial Valleys.

### Islands and Shores
The gulf contains over 900 islands and islets, with 37 major islands, the largest being Isla Ángel de la Guarda and Isla Tiburón. Most islands are volcanic in origin. All islands are part of a federal protected area and the UNESCO World Reserve Network. The shores include rocky, sandy, and tidal flat types. Species richness is higher on shores with softer, porous rocks (like coquina limestone) than on harder rocks (like basalt). The northern gulf experiences tidal ranges up to 5 meters.

### Oceanography and Climate
Water temperatures generally range from 16°C in winter to 24°C in summer, but can vary significantly. The northern gulf can experience cold winters with water temperatures dropping below 8°C, causing marine die-offs. The climate is generally sheltered from continuous wave shock but is subject to brief, damaging storms called "chubascos."

### Marine Life and Ecology
The gulf is one of the most ecologically diverse seas, home to over 5,000 species of micro-invertebrates. It hosts endemic species like the critically endangered vaquita, as well as migratory species including humpback whales, blue whales, and leatherback sea turtles. The Colorado River Delta area has a small remnant population of totoaba. The region is a major source of sardines and anchovies for Mexico.

### Human Impact and Conservation
Coastal communities are highly reliant on commercial and sport fishing. However, the gulf faces threats from overfishing, bottom trawling, and water pollution. The reduced flow of the Colorado River due to dams has negatively impacted northern fisheries. Conservation efforts, including UNESCO World Heritage status, are hampered by lack of enforcement, political consensus, and the economic pressures of the fishing industry. In 2019, the gulf was placed on the List of World Heritage in Danger.

## Terms
- **Marginal sea:** A sea partially enclosed by islands, archipelagos, or peninsulas.
- **Endemic:** A species native and restricted to a certain place.
- **Tectonic rift:** A place where the Earth's crust is being pulled apart.
- **East Pacific Rise:** A mid-ocean ridge, a divergent tectonic plate boundary.
- **Negative estuary:** An estuary where evaporation exceeds freshwater input, resulting in higher salinity than the ocean.
- **Mixed semidiurnal tides:** Tides with two high and two low waters each lunar day, with significant inequality in height.
- **Chubasco:** A violent, squally windstorm, especially on the Pacific coast of Mexico.
- **Vaquita:** A critically endangered species of porpoise endemic to the Gulf of California.

## Debates and open questions
- The gulf's ability to recuperate after years of overfishing remains uncertain, with data varying widely by species.
- The impacts of the Mexican government's macro-level tourist development vision for the gulf on local ecology and society are uncertain.
- The existence of a giant creature known as the "Black Demon" is alleged by locals but is not scientifically verified.

# Copenhagen

## Overview
Copenhagen is the capital and most populous city of Denmark, situated on the islands of Zealand and Amager. It is the country's cultural, economic, and governmental center, and a major financial hub in Northern Europe. The city's history as a Viking fishing village evolved into its role as a regional power center, and today it is renowned for its high quality of life, sustainable urban planning, and integration with the Swedish city of Malmö via the Øresund Bridge.

## Body

### Early History and Medieval Period
Copenhagen originated as a Viking fishing village, with archaeological evidence of 11th-century habitation at Gammel Strand. The traditional founding date is 1167, when Bishop Absalon built a fortress on Slotsholmen to defend against Wendish pirates. The city received its first city charter in 1254. It became the capital of Denmark by about 1416 and was repeatedly attacked by the Hanseatic League. The University of Copenhagen was founded in 1479.

### Rise as a Regional Capital (16th–17th Centuries)
During the Reformation, Copenhagen was besieged and changed hands between Catholic and Lutheran forces. The city prospered in the second half of the 16th century from Baltic trade. Under King Christian IV (r. 1588–1648), it experienced dramatic growth, with major projects like the Tøjhus Arsenal, Børsen (stock exchange), and the district of Christianshavn. By 1661, Copenhagen had asserted itself as the capital of Denmark and Norway, housing all major institutions and the fleet.

### 18th Century: Plague, Fire, and Trade
The 18th century was marked by catastrophe and rebuilding. A plague outbreak in 1711 killed about 22,000 residents. The Great Fire of 1728 destroyed 28% of the city. Major redevelopment followed, including the construction of Christiansborg Palace and the prestigious Frederiksstaden district. Copenhagen became the center of the Danish slave trade, with merchants transporting approximately 120,000 enslaved Africans to the Danish West Indies between 1660 and 1806.

### 19th Century: War and Golden Age
Copenhagen was bombarded by the British in 1807 during the Napoleonic Wars, leading to the Gunboat War. The subsequent Danish Golden Age brought a Neoclassical architectural style to the city. In the late 19th century, the old ramparts were opened for expansion, leading to the development of new districts like Nørrebro and Vesterbro to accommodate industrialization and population growth.

### 20th Century: World War II and Modernization
Copenhagen was occupied by German troops from 1940 to 1945. A notable event was the 1945 British air raid, "Operation Carthage," targeting the Gestapo headquarters, which accidentally hit a school. Post-war, the 1947 Finger Plan shaped development along five "fingers" of railway lines. The city modernized, replacing trams with buses in 1972 and creating pedestrian zones. Freetown Christiania was established in 1971.

### 21st Century: Integration and Sustainability
The 2000 opening of the Øresund Bridge integrated Copenhagen with Malmö, forming the Øresund Region. The Copenhagen Metro opened in 2002. The city has focused on green initiatives, aiming to be carbon-neutral by 2025 through investments in cycling infrastructure, renewable energy, and sustainable urban planning. It hosted the COP15 climate conference in 2009.

## Terms
*   **Finger Plan:** A 1947 urban planning strategy that encouraged development along five "fingers" extending from the city center along S-train routes, with green wedges in between.
*   **Kalmar Union:** A personal union that from 1397 to 1523 united Denmark, Norway, and Sweden under a single monarch.
*   **Øresund Region:** A metropolitan area spanning eastern Denmark and southern Sweden, integrated by the Øresund Bridge.
*   **Flexicurity:** A Danish model of labor market policy combining flexible hiring and firing rules with strong social security and active labor market policies.
*   **Smørrebrød:** A traditional Danish open-faced sandwich, typically on rye bread.
*   **Bournonville:** A style of ballet developed by Danish choreographer August Bournonville, characterized by its joyous, virtuosic style.
*   **New Nordic Cuisine:** A culinary movement emphasizing regional, seasonal, and sustainable ingredients, pioneered by Copenhagen restaurants like Noma.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   The exact founding date of Copenhagen is debated; while 1167 is traditional, archaeological evidence points to earlier settlements in the 11th century.
*   The city's role and the extent of its involvement in the transatlantic slave trade during the 18th century is a subject of historical study and public discourse.
*   The future of Freetown Christiania, an autonomous neighborhood since 1971, remains a topic of debate regarding its legal status and governance.

# Central African Republic

## Overview
The Central African Republic (CAR) is a landlocked nation in Central Africa, rich in natural resources yet consistently ranked among the world's poorest and least developed countries. Its history is marked by colonial exploitation, post-independence autocracy, and recurring civil wars, which have severely hampered its development and stability.

## Timeline
- **c. 3500–2700 BCE** — Bouar Megaliths indicate advanced habitation in the late Neolithic period.
- **1894** — France seizes and colonizes the territory, naming it Ubangi-Shari.
- **13 August 1960** — The Central African Republic gains independence from France.
- **31 December 1965** — Colonel Jean-Bédel Bokassa overthrows President Dacko in a coup.
- **4 December 1976** — Bokassa declares himself Emperor, renaming the country the Central African Empire.
- **September 1979** — France overthrows Bokassa, restoring the republic and Dacko to power.
- **1981** — General André Kolingba overthrows Dacko in a coup.
- **1993** — Ange-Félix Patassé wins the first multi-party presidential election.
- **March 2003** — General François Bozizé overthrows Patassé in a coup.
- **2004** — The Central African Bush War begins.
- **March 2013** — The Séléka coalition seizes the capital, overthrowing Bozizé and starting a civil war.
- **December 2015** — Faustin-Archange Touadéra is elected president.

## Body

### Colonial and Early Post-Independence History
The region was inhabited for millennia, with farming and ironwork developing early. The modern borders were established by France, which colonized the area as Ubangi-Shari in 1894. Colonial rule was exploitative, relying on forced labor for resource extraction and infrastructure projects like the Congo–Ocean Railway, which caused thousands of deaths. After independence in 1960, the CAR was ruled by a series of autocratic leaders. David Dacko, the first president, was overthrown in 1965 by Jean-Bédel Bokassa, who later declared himself Emperor of the Central African Empire (1976–1979). His regime ended after French forces intervened following the massacre of schoolchildren protesting a uniform decree.

### Civil Wars and Modern Conflict
Following Bokassa, the country experienced further coups and unstable transitions to democracy in the 1990s. The 2003 coup by François Bozizé led to the Central African Bush War (2004–2007), which was paused by peace agreements. A major crisis erupted in 2012 when the Séléka coalition, a group of rebel factions, overthrew Bozizé. This triggered a devastating civil war characterized by sectarian violence between Séléka fighters and Christian "anti-balaka" militias, leading to widespread human rights abuses, displacement, and warnings of genocide. A transitional government and international peacekeepers struggled to restore order. Faustin-Archange Touadéra was elected president in 2016, but his government has relied heavily on Russian mercenaries (first the Wagner Group, later the Africa Corps) to fight rebel groups, raising concerns about sovereignty and human rights.

### Geography, Economy, and Demographics
The CAR is a landlocked plateau country, with its southern border formed by tributaries of the Congo River. Its climate is tropical, with distinct wet and dry seasons. Despite vast natural resources (diamonds, gold, uranium, timber), the economy is one of the world's weakest, with extreme poverty and a heavy reliance on subsistence agriculture and foreign aid. Diamonds are the primary export, but much of the production is smuggled. The population is ethnically diverse, with over 80 groups, and is predominantly Christian (about 80-90%) with a Muslim minority (about 9-10%). Sango and French are the official languages. The country faces severe challenges in health, education, and infrastructure, with very low human development indicators.

## Terms
- **Séléka**: A coalition of predominantly Muslim rebel groups that seized power in the CAR in 2013, triggering a major civil war.
- **Anti-balaka**: Christian militias that formed in opposition to the Séléka, engaging in widespread retaliatory violence against Muslim civilians.
- **Ubangi-Shari**: The name of the CAR during the French colonial period, derived from the Ubangi and Chari rivers.
- **CFA franc**: The currency used in the CAR, which is pegged to the euro and used across several former French West African colonies.
- **Human Development Index (HDI)**: A composite statistic of life expectancy, education, and per capita income indicators, used to rank countries' development levels. The CAR consistently ranks near the bottom.
- **Wagner Group**: A Russian paramilitary organization that has been contracted by the CAR government to provide security and fight rebels, accused of human rights abuses and resource exploitation.
- **GIBAFOR**: An international group (France, US, Germany, Japan, EU, World Bank, UN) that pressured for democratic reforms in the CAR during the 1990s.
- **Neolithic Revolution**: The transition from hunting and gathering to farming and settlement, which occurred in the region before 3000 BCE.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The role and impact of Russian military personnel (Wagner Group/Africa Corps) in the CAR is highly debated, with allegations of human rights abuses and creating a "vassal state" versus the government's view of them as essential for security.
- The effectiveness and legacy of numerous international peacekeeping missions in the CAR, which has hosted "almost a dozen," are questioned given the persistent instability.
- The causes and sectarian nature of the conflict since 2012 are analyzed, with debates on whether it is primarily religious, political, or driven by competition over resources and power.

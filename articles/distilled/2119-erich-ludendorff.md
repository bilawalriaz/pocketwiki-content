# Erich Ludendorff

## Overview
Erich Ludendorff was a Prussian-born German general and politician who became a central figure in World War I and the early Nazi movement. As First Quartermaster General from 1916, he effectively directed Germany's military strategy and war effort, achieving major victories before the 1918 collapse. After the war, he was a key promoter of the "stab-in-the-back" myth and participated in failed far-right coups, significantly contributing to Adolf Hitler's rise to power.

## Body

### Early Life and Pre-War Career
Ludendorff was born to a non-noble family and entered the Prussian military cadet system. He rose rapidly through the ranks, joining the prestigious Great General Staff in 1894. By 1904, he led the Mobilisation Section, where he helped develop the Schlieffen Plan, a strategy for a two-front war. His pre-war career was marked by a talent for logistics and a controversial intervention in politics; he was transferred to a regimental command in 1913 after lobbying the Reichstag for additional army corps.

### World War I: Eastern Front and Rise to Power
At the war's outbreak, Ludendorff's knowledge of Belgian fortifications led to his role in the capture of Liège. He was then sent to the Eastern Front as chief of staff to General Hindenburg. Together, they orchestrated the decisive German victory at Tannenberg in August 1914 and subsequent battles. Their success made them national heroes. In August 1916, amid crises on the Western Front, they were given supreme command. Ludendorff, as First Quartermaster General, became the de facto military dictator, directing all strategy and increasingly influencing the home front and government policy.

### Military Strategy and the Home Front
Ludendorff implemented innovative defensive tactics like "elastic defense in depth" and later masterminded the 1918 Spring Offensives using infiltration tactics. These initial successes failed to win the war, and the Allied Hundred Days Offensive led to Germany's collapse. On the home front, the military leadership attempted to control the economy through the Hindenburg Program but struggled with labor and food shortages. Ludendorff's political interventions included forcing the resignation of Chancellor Bethmann Hollweg and supporting unrestricted submarine warfare, which brought the United States into the war.

### Post-War Politics and the "Stab-in-the-Back" Myth
After the war, Ludendorff became a leading nationalist figure, promoting the *Dolchstosslegende* (stab-in-the-back myth). This theory falsely claimed Germany's defeat was caused by internal betrayal by Marxists, Jews, and others on the home front, not military failure. He participated in the 1920 Kapp Putsch and the 1923 Beer Hall Putsch with Hitler. Though he later broke with Hitler and espoused extreme anti-Semitic and anti-Christian conspiracy theories, his actions and rhetoric helped legitimize the far-right and Hitler's early career.

### Later Years and Death
Ludendorff retired from active politics after his failed presidential run in 1925. He published works on military theory, most famously *The Total War*, which argued for the permanent mobilization of a nation's resources. He died in 1937 and was given a state funeral by Hitler, despite his earlier opposition to the Nazi regime.

## Terms
- **Schlieffen Plan**: Germany's pre-war military strategy for a two-front war, involving a rapid defeat of France before turning to fight Russia.
- **First Quartermaster General**: The second-highest position in the German Army command, which Ludendorff used to exercise dictatorial control over strategy and the war effort.
- **Pour le Mérite**: Prussia's highest military order for gallantry, awarded to Ludendorff after Liège.
- **Elastic Defense in Depth**: A defensive tactic involving a lightly held front line and a battle zone of counterattacks, designed to absorb and punish enemy assaults.
- **Hindenburg Line**: A heavily fortified defensive position built by Germany in 1917 on the Western Front.
- **Dolchstosslegende (Stab-in-the-Back Myth)**: The false theory that the German army was undefeated in the field but betrayed at home by civilians, socialists, and Jews.
- **Kapp Putsch**: A 1920 right-wing coup attempt in Berlin, which Ludendorff supported.
- **Beer Hall Putsch**: Hitler's failed 1923 coup attempt in Munich, in which Ludendorff was a leading participant.
- **The Total War**: Ludendorff's 1935 book arguing that modern war requires the complete mobilization of a nation's physical and moral resources.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Ludendorff's Role in Defeat**: Historians debate whether his aggressive strategy, especially the 1918 offensives, hastened defeat by exhausting the army, or if defeat was inevitable due to material shortages and the Allied blockade.
- **Political Responsibility**: Scholars assess the extent to which his political meddling, such as ousting chancellors and promoting annexationist aims, undermined Germany's ability to seek a negotiated peace.
- **The "Dictator" Label**: While often called a dictator from 1916–1918, his power was technically shared with Hindenburg and dependent on the Kaiser, leading to debate about the precise nature of his authority.
- **Authenticity of the 1933 Telegram**: The famous telegram he allegedly sent to Hindenburg protesting Hitler's appointment as Chancellor is considered by some historians to be a forgery.

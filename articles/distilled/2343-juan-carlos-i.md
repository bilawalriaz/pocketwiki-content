# Juan Carlos I

## Overview
Juan Carlos I was King of Spain from 1975 to 2014. Initially chosen by dictator Francisco Franco as his successor, Juan Carlos instead oversaw Spain's transition to democracy, earning widespread acclaim. His legacy is now complicated by later scandals involving corruption and personal conduct, which led to his abdication and self-imposed exile.

## Timeline
- **5 January 1938** — Born in Rome, Italy, to the exiled Spanish royal family.
- **1969** — Named by Francisco Franco as his successor with the title "Prince of Spain."
- **22 November 1975** — Proclaimed King of Spain after Franco's death.
- **15 June 1977** — Spain holds its first post-Franco democratic elections.
- **6 December 1978** — Spanish Constitution of 1978 approved by referendum, establishing a constitutional monarchy.
- **23 February 1981** — Plays a key role in thwarting a military coup attempt (23-F).
- **19 June 2014** — Abdicates in favor of his son, Felipe VI.
- **3 August 2020** — Leaves Spain for self-imposed exile in the United Arab Emirates amid corruption investigations.

## Body

### Early Life and Designation as Heir
Born in exile, Juan Carlos moved to Spain in 1947 for his education. He received military training and university studies. In 1969, General Francisco Franco, seeking a successor who would maintain his authoritarian system, bypassed Juan Carlos's father (whom he considered too liberal) and designated Juan Carlos as his personal successor. Juan Carlos swore loyalty to Franco's regime.

### The Spanish Transition to Democracy
Upon Franco's death in 1975, Juan Carlos became king. Contrary to expectations, he initiated reforms to dismantle the Francoist system. He appointed Adolfo Suárez as Prime Minister to lead the transition. Key steps included the legalization of political parties, the first democratic elections in 1977, and the ratification of a new democratic constitution in 1978. His authority was challenged in 1981 by a military coup attempt, which he publicly and successfully opposed in a televised address, cementing his role as a defender of democracy.

### Reign and Later Controversies
For decades, Juan Carlos was a popular and largely respected figure, seen as a symbol of national unity. His reputation began to decline due to personal scandals, most notably a 2012 elephant-hunting trip in Botswana during a severe economic crisis in Spain. This, combined with corruption allegations involving his family, eroded public support. He abdicated in 2014 in favor of his son, Felipe VI.

### Post-Abdication and Exile
After abdicating, Juan Carlos faced multiple investigations into alleged financial improprieties, including kickbacks related to a Saudi Arabian high-speed rail contract and the use of undeclared credit cards. In 2020, amid these investigations, he left Spain for self-imposed exile in the United Arab Emirates. Spanish and Swiss prosecutors eventually closed their cases against him in 2021 and 2022, respectively.

## Terms
- **Francoist regime**: The authoritarian government of Spain led by General Francisco Franco from the end of the Spanish Civil War in 1939 until his death in 1975.
- **Spanish transition to democracy**: The period following Franco's death in which Spain peacefully transformed from a dictatorship into a democratic constitutional monarchy.
- **Constitutional monarchy**: A system of government where a monarch acts as head of state within the parameters of a constitution.
- **23-F**: The failed coup d'état attempt on 23 February 1981, named for the date.
- **Movimiento Nacional**: The sole legal political party during the Francoist dictatorship.
- **Sovereign immunity**: A legal doctrine that protects a sovereign (like a king) from being sued or prosecuted in the courts of another sovereign state.
- **Abdication**: The formal renunciation of a throne by a monarch.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Role in the 1981 coup**: While Juan Carlos publicly opposed the coup, some theories question his full knowledge of the plot beforehand and whether his actions were purely democratic conviction or a reaction to the coup's failure.
- **Motives for the transition**: Scholars debate the extent to which Juan Carlos's push for democracy was a long-held personal conviction versus a pragmatic response to political pressures and the unsustainability of the Francoist system.
- **Legacy**: His historical reputation is contested, balancing his crucial role in establishing Spanish democracy against the damage caused by later corruption scandals and his departure from the country.

# Trần Hưng Đạo

## Overview
Trần Hưng Đạo (1228–1300) was a Vietnamese prince, statesman, and supreme military commander of the Trần dynasty. He is a national hero for his decisive role in repelling two major Mongol invasions of Đại Việt (medieval Vietnam) in the late 13th century, preserving the kingdom's independence. His strategic brilliance, particularly in guerrilla warfare and naval tactics, is central to Vietnamese historical identity.

## Timeline

- **1258** — Serves as an officer during the first Mongol invasion.
- **1285** — Commands Đại Việt forces against the second Mongol invasion led by Prince Toghan.
- **1285** — After initial defeats, his forces drive the overextended Yuan army back to China.

- **1288** — His forces ambush and capture the Mongol supply fleet at Vân Đồn.

- **Posthumous** — Deified as Đức Thánh Trần; given the title Hưng Đạo Đại Vương (Grand Prince Hưng Đạo).

## Body

### Origins and Early Life
Trần Hưng Đạo was born Trần Quốc Tuấn in 1228, the son of Prince Trần Liễu. His father was the elder brother of Emperor Trần Thái Tông, who came to power after the Trần dynasty replaced the Lý dynasty in 1225. A family grudge existed because their uncle, Imperial Regent Trần Thủ Độ, forced Trần Liễu to give up his wife, Princess Thuận Thiên, to Emperor Thái Tông to secure the dynasty's stability.

### Second Mongol Invasion (1285)
Following failed diplomatic missions, Kublai Khan ordered a punitive invasion. In January 1285, Prince Toghan led Mongol forces into Đại Việt. Trần Hưng Đạo, as commander of the combined land and naval forces, initially suffered defeats and lost the capital, Thăng Long. Emperor Trần Nhân Tông considered surrender, but Trần Hưng Đạo resisted and rallied the private armies of the Trần princes. As the Mongols advanced south, Trần Hưng Đạo's forces inflicted major defeats during the enemy's retreat, killing the deputy commander Sodu and forcing Toghan to withdraw to China with heavy losses.

### Third Mongol Invasion and the Battle of Bạch Đằng (1287-1288)
In 1287, Kublai Khan sent an even larger army, estimated between 120,000 and 500,000 troops, under Prince Toghan. The Mongols quickly captured border posts and advanced on Thăng Long. Trần Hưng Đạo's strategy involved scorched-earth tactics (burning the capital to deny resources) and guerrilla warfare. A critical turning point was the ambush and capture of the Mongol supply fleet at Vân Đồn by General Trần Khánh Dư. The campaign culminated in the Battle of Bạch Đằng River. Trần Hưng Đạo predicted the Mongol naval route and ordered steel-tipped wooden stakes planted in the riverbed, hidden by high tide. Luring the Mongol fleet into the trap, the receding tide left the vessels stuck and vulnerable. Đại Việt forces destroyed an estimated 400 Mongol ships, capturing their admiral Omar. The Mongol land army, also ambushed during its retreat, lost half its remaining forces.

### Death and Legacy
Trần Hưng Đạo died of natural causes in 1300. He declined a lavish state burial, requesting a simple private ceremony; his ashes were scattered under his favorite oak tree. He was posthumously honored as Grand Prince Hưng Đạo. He is revered as a deity, Đức Thánh Trần, with numerous shrines dedicated to him. Major streets in Vietnamese cities like Hanoi, Ho Chi Minh City, and Da Nang bear his name, and a modern naval frigate is named in his honor.

## Terms
- **Đại Việt**: The historical name for Vietnam during the Trần dynasty.
- **Yuan dynasty**: The Mongol-led dynasty of China, founded by Kublai Khan, which launched the invasions.
- **Guerrilla warfare**: A form of irregular warfare using small, mobile forces to harass a larger, conventional army through ambushes and sabotage.
- **Scorched-earth tactics**: A military strategy of destroying resources (like crops, infrastructure, or cities) that might be useful to the enemy.
- **Bạch Đằng River**: The site of the decisive naval ambush that destroyed the Mongol fleet in 1288.
- **Đức Thánh Trần**: The deified name ("Holy Saint Trần") by which Trần Hưng Đạo is reverentially known in Vietnamese folk religion.
- **Hịch tướng sĩ**: A famous "Proclamation to the Officers" attributed to Trần Hưng Đạo, rallying his troops.

# Naples

## Overview
Naples is the capital of Campania in southern Italy and one of the world's oldest continuously inhabited cities. Founded by Greeks in the first millennium BC, it has been a major cultural, political, and economic center for millennia, serving as the capital of several kingdoms before Italian unification. Today, it is Italy's third-largest city, renowned for its dense historical heritage, distinctive cuisine (especially pizza), and significant contributions to music, art, and philosophy.

## Timeline
- **8th century BC** — Colony of Parthenope founded by Cumaeans on Pizzofalcone hill.
- **6th century BC** — City refounded as Neápolis, becoming a key city of Magna Graecia.
- **536 AD** — Byzantine General Belisarius recaptures Naples from the Ostrogoths.
- **661–1139** — Naples serves as the capital of the independent Duchy of Naples.
- **1224** — University of Naples Federico II founded by Frederick II, the first state-funded secular university in Europe.
- **1282** — Following the Sicilian Vespers, Naples becomes capital of the Angevin Kingdom of Naples.
- **1503** — Spain wins Naples from France at the Battle of Garigliano, beginning Spanish Habsburg rule.
- **1815** — Congress of Vienna creates the Kingdom of the Two Sicilies, with Naples as capital.
- **1861** — Naples becomes part of the unified Kingdom of Italy after the Expedition of the Thousand.
- **1943** — The Four Days of Naples uprising (27-30 September) liberates the city from Nazi occupation.
- **1995** — Historic centre of Naples designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

## Body

### Ancient and Medieval Foundations
Naples was founded as the Greek colony of Parthenope in the 9th century BC and refounded as Neápolis in the 6th century BC. It was a prominent city of Magna Graecia and later a respected Roman colony that preserved its Greek language and culture. After the fall of Rome, Naples was contested by Ostrogoths and Byzantines before becoming the capital of an independent Duchy of Naples in the 7th century. The duchy maintained its Greco-Roman culture and eventually gained full independence from Byzantium by the early 9th century, at times allying with Muslim Saracens against rivals. Norman mercenaries were later employed, leading to Naples coming under Norman control in 1137 and joining the Kingdom of Sicily.

### Capital of Kingdoms and Spanish Rule
After the Sicilian Vespers in 1282, Naples became the capital of the separate Angevin Kingdom of Naples, attracting Renaissance artists and scholars. Following a period of Aragonese rule, Spain conquered Naples in 1503. Under Spanish viceroys, Naples grew into Europe's second-largest city by the 17th century and a major Baroque cultural center, though it also suffered from plague and a brief popular revolution in 1647. Spanish rule ended in 1714 due to the War of the Spanish Succession, leading to Austrian and then Bourbon rule. The Congress of Vienna (1815) united Naples and Sicily into the Kingdom of the Two Sicilies, with Naples as its capital.

### Unification, Modern Challenges, and Renewal
Naples joined the unified Kingdom of Italy in 1861. The post-unification period saw economic struggle, mass emigration, and severe public health crises, including a major cholera epidemic in 1884 that prompted a large-scale urban renewal project (*risanamento*). Naples was heavily bombed in World War II and was the first Italian city to rise up against Nazi occupation in 1943. Post-war reconstruction was aided by government funds, but the city continued to face high unemployment and organized crime (the Camorra), which has been linked to illegal waste disposal problems. Since the late 20th century, Naples has experienced economic growth, tourism revival, and infrastructure development, including a modern metro system and high-speed rail links.

## Terms
- **Magna Graecia**: The name for the coastal areas of Southern Italy that were extensively settled by Greek colonists in the 8th and 7th centuries BC.
- **Duchy of Naples**: An autonomous Byzantine-ruled duchy that existed from 661 to 1139, with Naples as its capital.
- **Angevin**: Referring to the House of Anjou, a French royal dynasty that ruled the Kingdom of Naples from 1282.
- **Risanamento**: A major urban renewal and public health improvement project undertaken in Naples after the 1884 cholera epidemic.
- **Four Days of Naples**: The popular uprising against Nazi German occupation forces in Naples from 27 to 30 September 1943.
- **Camorra**: An Italian organized crime syndicate based in the Campania region, with Naples as its historic center.
- **Canzone napoletana**: The traditional popular music genre of Naples, with a repertoire of folk songs dating back centuries.
- **Opera buffa**: A genre of comic opera that originated in Naples in the 18th century.
- **Sceneggiata**: A Neapolitan popular musical theatre genre, often depicting melodramatic stories of love and crime.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The exact founding date and nature of the earliest Greek settlement (Parthenope vs. Neápolis) involves archaeological interpretation.
- The scale and impact of the *risanamento* project are debated, with sources noting it was hampered by corruption and land speculation, leading to "contrasting results."
- The economic and social impact of the Camorra and the waste management crisis on Naples' development remains a significant ongoing issue.

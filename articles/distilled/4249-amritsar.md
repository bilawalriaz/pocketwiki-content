# Amritsar

## Overview
Amritsar is the second-largest city in Punjab, India, and the holiest city in Sikhism. Its significance stems from being the site of the Golden Temple, making it a major pilgrimage and tourist center. The city has a rich history from its 16th-century founding to its role in key events of the Indian independence movement and post-independence conflicts.

## Timeline
- **1574** — Guru Ram Das founds the city, originally named Ramdaspur.
- **1604** — The Sikh scripture is installed in the newly built Harmandir Sahib (Golden Temple).
- **1762, 1766–67** — Ahmad Shah Durrani invades, massacres the populace, and destroys the city.
- **1822** — Maharaja Ranjit Singh begins fortifying the city.
- **1849** — British annex Punjab; Amritsar is a walled city.
- **13 April 1919** — Jallianwala Bagh massacre occurs under British Colonel Dyer.
- **1947** — Partition of India leads to an influx of Hindu and Sikh refugees.
- **1–6 June 1984** — Operation Blue Star: Indian military removes Sikh militants from the Golden Temple.
- **31 October 1984** — Prime Minister Indira Gandhi is assassinated by her Sikh bodyguards.
- **2014** — The craft of the Thatheras of Jandiala Guru is listed as UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage.

## Body

### Foundation and Early History
In Sikh tradition, Guru Ram Das founded Amritsar in 1574, purchasing land for a new town centered on a man-made pool. The town, initially called Ramdaspur, expanded under Guru Arjan Dev, who completed the Harmandir Sahib (Golden Temple) and installed the Sikh scripture there in 1604. The city's early development is documented in the semi-historical text *Mahima Prakash Vartak*, likely composed in 1741.

### 18th to 19th Centuries
Amritsar was devastated by invasions from Ahmad Shah Durrani's Durrani Empire in 1762 and 1766–67. Under the Sikh Empire, Maharaja Ranjit Singh fortified the city starting in 1822, constructing walls and gates. After the British annexed Punjab in 1849, they further modified the city's fortifications and supported the development of a textile industry, establishing Amritsar as a key colonial city.

### The Jallianwala Bagh Massacre
On 13 April 1919, during the Sikh festival of Baisakhi, thousands gathered peacefully in Jallianwala Bagh. British Colonel Reginald Dyer ordered his troops to fire on the unarmed crowd without warning, blocking the exits. A British inquiry placed the death toll at 379, while the Indian National Congress estimated around 1,000 killed. This event was a pivotal moment in the Indian independence movement.

### Post-Independence and Operation Blue Star
Following the 1947 partition, Amritsar received many refugees from West Punjab. In the 1980s, the city became a center of unrest. In June 1984, Prime Minister Indira Gandhi ordered Operation Blue Star to remove Sikh militants who had occupied the Golden Temple. The military operation was controversial, with official reports stating 493 civilians and 83 soldiers died, while independent estimates are much higher. This led to Gandhi's assassination and the subsequent 1984 anti-Sikh riots.

### Geography and Climate
Amritsar is located in the Majha region of Punjab, about 28 km from the India-Pakistan border. It has a hot semi-arid climate with four seasons: winter, summer, monsoon, and post-monsoon. Temperatures range from −1°C to 45°C, with annual rainfall averaging 726 mm.

### Demographics and Religion
According to the 2011 census, the city had a population of 1,159,227. The main religions are Hinduism (49.36%) and Sikhism (48.00%). Punjabi is the primary language, spoken by 91.12% of the population. An estimated 30% of the city's population descends from partition-era refugees.

### Government and Economy
Amritsar is governed by a Municipal Corporation with 85 councillors. The city is the economic capital of Punjab, with a strong tourism sector centered on the Golden Temple, which attracts about 30 million visitors annually. Historically, it was known for textiles, and the craft of the Thatheras of Jandiala Guru is a UNESCO-listed heritage.

### Transport
The city is served by Sri Guru Ram Das Ji International Airport and the busy Amritsar Junction railway station. It is located on the historic Grand Trunk Road (NH 3), with new expressways under development to improve connectivity.

## Terms
- **Gurudwara**: A Sikh place of worship.
- **Satyagraha**: A form of nonviolent resistance or civil disobedience.
- **Operation Blue Star**: The 1984 Indian military operation to remove Sikh militants from the Golden Temple.
- **Partition of India**: The 1947 division of British India into India and Pakistan, causing massive migration and violence.
- **Majha**: A historical region in the Punjab, part of which is in India and part in Pakistan.
- **Koppen BSh**: A climate classification for a hot semi-arid climate.
- **HRIDAY**: Heritage City Development and Augmentation Yojana, a Government of India program.
- **Thatheras**: A community of traditional brass and copper craftsmen from Jandiala Guru.

## Debates and Open questions
- The death toll of the Jallianwala Bagh massacre is disputed between British and Indian sources.
- The justification for the timing and style of Operation Blue Star is "hotly debated."
- The exact number of casualties during Operation Blue Star varies widely between official and independent estimates.

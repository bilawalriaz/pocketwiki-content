# Ramana Maharshi

## Overview
Ramana Maharshi (1879–1950) was an Indian Hindu sage and a principal exponent of experiential Advaita Vedanta. He is widely regarded by his followers as a *jivanmukta* (liberated being) and is renowned for teaching the path of Self-enquiry (*vichara*) as the direct means to realize the true Self. His life and teachings, centered on the sacred hill Arunachala, have had a significant influence on both Indian and Western spirituality.

## Timeline
- **30 December 1879** — Born Venkataraman Iyer in Tiruchuzhi, Tamil Nadu.
- **July 1896** — At age 16, experiences a "death-experience" and realizes his true Self.
- **1 September 1896** — Arrives at Tiruvannamalai, beginning his lifelong residence near Arunachala.
- **1902** — First teachings on Self-enquiry given to Sivaprakasam Pillai, later published as *Nan Yar?* ("Who am I?").
- **1907** — Vedic scholar Ganapati Sastri proclaims him "Bhagavan Sri Ramana Maharshi."
- **1911** — First Western visitor, Frank Humphreys, writes about him.
- **1916** — His mother and brother join him at Tiruvannamalai.
- **1922** — His mother dies; he settles permanently at the site that becomes Sri Ramanasramam.
- **1934** — Paul Brunton's *A Search in Secret India* popularizes his teachings in the West.
- **14 April 1950** — Dies at Sri Ramanasramam.

## Body

### Early Life and Realization
Born into a Brahmin family, Venkataraman showed an early aptitude for memory and learning. In 1895, his attraction to the sacred hill Arunachala intensified. In July 1896, he had a sudden "death-experience" where he became aware of a "current" or "force" (*avesam*) which he recognized as his true Self, later identifying it with God (Shiva). This event, which he called *akrama mukti* (sudden liberation), led him to renounce his family and move to Tiruvannamalai, where he lived as a silent sage for the rest of his life.

### Life at Arunachala and the Ashram
Initially living in temples and caves on Arunachala, he attracted devotees who valued his silent presence and austere life. In 1922, after his mother's death, he settled permanently at the base of the hill, and Sri Ramanasramam grew around him. Though he lived a modest, renunciate life, he was actively involved in the ashram's development. His fame spread in the West through Paul Brunton's 1934 book, attracting visitors like Somerset Maugham. He died in 1950 after refusing treatment for cancer, stating, "Where can I go? I am here."

### Teachings: The Real Self and Self-Enquiry
Ramana Maharshi taught that the "real I" is a non-personal, all-inclusive awareness, often called *sat-chit-ananda* (truth-consciousness-bliss) or the Heart. This true Self is permanent and distinct from the illusory individual ego or "I-thought." The primary method to realize this is *vichara* or Self-enquiry: the constant attention to the source of the "I"-thought. This practice, he taught, dissolves the ego and leads to *sahaja samadhi*—a natural, continuous state of Self-awareness maintained during daily activities.

### Paths of Devotion and Surrender
While advocating Self-enquiry as the fastest path, he also approved of *bhakti* (devotion) and complete surrender to God or the Guru. He taught that these paths ultimately converge, as true devotion is love for the Self, which is God. Surrender, he emphasized, must be desireless and complete, leading to the effacement of the ego, which is the same goal as Self-enquiry.

### Scriptural Context and Legacy
Though his authority came from direct experience, he engaged with Hindu scriptures like the *Bhagavad Gita* and Shaiva texts, using them to explain his insights. He is strongly associated with Shaivism, considering the mountain Arunachala to be Shiva himself. He never appointed a successor, and his teachings have been popularized in the West through the neo-Advaita movement, though this has been criticized for sometimes omitting preparatory practices.

## Terms
- **Jivanmukta**: A liberated being who is free from ignorance while still living in a physical body.
- **Advaita Vedanta**: A school of Hindu philosophy teaching non-dualism, that the individual Self (*Atman*) and the ultimate reality (*Brahman*) are one.
- **Vichara (Self-enquiry)**: The practice of turning attention inward to inquire "Who am I?" to discover the source of the "I"-thought.
- **Sat-chit-ananda**: A Sanskrit term meaning "truth-consciousness-bliss," describing the nature of the ultimate reality or true Self.
- **Sahaja samadhi**: A natural, effortless state of continuous Self-awareness maintained while engaged in daily life.
- **Darshan**: The act of seeing or being in the presence of a holy person or deity, considered spiritually beneficial.
- **Upadesa**: Spiritual instruction or guidance given by a guru.
- **Vasanas**: Subconscious tendencies or karmic impressions that shape the ego and cause the "I"-thought to arise.
- **Arunachala**: A sacred hill in Tiruvannamalai, Tamil Nadu, regarded as a manifestation of the god Shiva and central to Ramana Maharshi's life and teachings.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Neo-Advaita Interpretation**: The Western neo-Advaita movement, influenced by students of H.W.L. Poonja, emphasizes insight alone and is criticized for omitting the preparatory practices Ramana Maharshi sometimes recommended.
- **Scriptural Authority vs. Direct Experience**: While his teachings align with Advaita Vedanta, he emphasized personal experience over philosophical argumentation and scripture, leading some scholars to classify him as a neo-Vedantin rather than a traditional Advaitin.
- **Reincarnation**: He taught that reincarnation is a concept based on the illusion of a separate self; when the Self is realized, notions of birth and death become inapplicable.

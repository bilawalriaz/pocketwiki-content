# Tianjin

## Overview
Tianjin is a major port city and direct-administered municipality in Northern China, serving as a gateway to Beijing and a key economic hub. Its history as a treaty port and its modern development as a financial and industrial center make it a significant case study in China's urban and economic transformation.

## Timeline
- **1404** — Walled city of Tianjin built during the Ming dynasty.
- **1858** — Treaty of Tientsin signed, opening Tianjin to foreign trade.
- **1860** — Tianjin formally opened to Great Britain and France.
- **1900** — Boxer Rebellion; city seat of the Tianjin Provisional Government.
- **1927** — Tianjin established as a municipality of China.
- **1937** — Tianjin falls to Japan during the Second Sino-Japanese War.
- **1949** — Chinese Communist Party captures Tianjin.
- **1976** — Tangshan Earthquake devastates the city.
- **1984** — Tianjin listed as one of 14 coastal open cities.
- **2008** — Beijing-Tianjin Intercity Railway opens; city co-hosts Olympics.
- **2015** — Tianjin port explosion causes major casualties and damage.
- **2025** — Tianjin hosts the Shanghai Cooperation Organization summit.

## Body

### Name and Early History
The name "Tianjin" (天津) means "the Ford of the Emperor." Its origin is debated, with theories linking it to a poem by Qu Yuan, a Chinese constellation, or a place name in historical records. The most common theory credits the Ming dynasty's Yongle Emperor. The city's land was formed by river sediments between 900 and 1300 CE. The Grand Canal, completed by 609 CE, facilitated its development as a grain transport hub. It became a garrison town and commercial center by the Ming dynasty.

### Treaty Port Era and Foreign Concessions
Following the Second Opium War, the 1858 Treaty of Tientsin opened the city to foreign trade. By 1900, multiple nations, including Britain, France, Japan, Germany, and Russia, had established self-contained concessions with their own institutions. This period saw violent incidents like the 1870 Tianjin Church Massacre and the 1900 Boxer Rebellion, during which the city was governed by an Eight-Nation Alliance provisional government until 1902. Western nations maintained garrisons in the city into the 20th century.

### Modern Development and Challenges
After the founding of the People's Republic of China, Tianjin was a direct-controlled municipality until 1958, when it was incorporated into Hebei Province. It was restored to direct control in 1967. The city suffered greatly from the 1976 Tangshan Earthquake, which killed over 24,000 people and destroyed 60% of its buildings. Economic reforms began in 1984, and the Binhai New Area was developed from 1994 onward. A major industrial shift and national strategy for the area were solidified in the mid-2000s. The city continues to develop as a financial and logistics center, though it faced a major setback with the 2015 port explosion.

### Geography and Climate
Tianjin is located on the Bohai Gulf, bordering Beijing and Hebei. The Hai River system, connected to the Grand Canal, is central to its geography. The terrain is generally flat and coastal, with hills in the north. The climate is a semi-arid monsoon type with four distinct seasons, featuring cold, dry winters and hot, humid summers. Air quality has been a significant concern, leading to pollution control measures.

### Economy and Administration
The economy is based on petrochemicals, textiles, car manufacturing, and finance. Key zones include the Tianjin Economic-Technological Development Area (TEDA), founded in 1984, and the Tianjin Port Free Trade Zone. The Port of Tianjin is China's largest artificial deep-water harbor. Administratively, the municipality is divided into 16 districts. The political system follows the standard parallel party-government structure of mainland China.

### Culture and Demographics
The majority of residents are Han Chinese, with significant Hui, Korean, Manchu, and Mongol minorities. The Tianjin dialect is distinct from the Beijing dialect. The city is a center for Beijing opera and the comedic art of *xiangsheng*. Traditional crafts include Yangliuqing New Year paintings and clay figurines. Cuisine features seafood and famous snacks like *jianbing guozi* and Goubuli baozi.

### Transport and Infrastructure
Tianjin has a comprehensive transport system, including Binhai International Airport, the extensive Port of Tianjin, and a metro system that began service in 1984. The Beijing-Tianjin high-speed railway, opened in 2008, reduces travel time to 30 minutes. The city has an extensive road network with ring roads and expressways. It received the 2024 Sustainable Transport Award for its public and non-motorized transport improvements.

## Terms
- **Direct-administered municipality**: A Chinese administrative division equivalent to a province, under the direct control of the central government.
- **Treaty port**: A port opened to foreign trade and settlement by treaty, often with extraterritorial concessions.
- **Concession**: A territory within a country administered by a foreign power, with its own laws and institutions.
- **Grand Canal**: A series of waterways in eastern China, the world's longest, connecting the Yellow and Yangtze rivers.
- **Hukou**: A system of household registration in China that ties access to social services to one's place of registered residence.
- **TEDA**: Tianjin Economic-Technological Development Area, a state-level zone for attracting investment and developing technology-oriented industries.
- **Binhai New Area**: A major national development zone and urban core east of Tianjin's main city, focused on port and industrial growth.
- **Xiangsheng**: A traditional Chinese comedic performance art, often involving dialogue and monologues.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The origin of the name "Tianjin" remains disputed among scholars, with multiple historical theories.
- The city's economic development pace has been a point of discussion, noted as slower than that of special economic zones and southeast coastal areas after initial reforms.
- The long-term impact of the 2015 Tianjin port explosion on the city's port economy and safety regulations is an ongoing subject of analysis.

# Mass in B minor

## Overview
The Mass in B minor (BWV 232) is a comprehensive setting of the Catholic Mass ordinary by Johann Sebastian Bach, completed in 1749. It is a monumental synthesis of Bach's lifelong musical output, reworking earlier compositions into a unified, large-scale sacred work. Though likely never performed in its entirety during Bach's lifetime, it is now considered one of the supreme achievements of classical music.

## Timeline
- **1724** — Bach composes a Sanctus for six voices, later revised for the Mass.
- **1733** — Bach composes the Missa (Kyrie and Gloria) and presents it to Augustus III in Dresden.
- **1736** — Bach is made court composer to Augustus III.
- **c. 1743–46** — Bach reuses Gloria movements in cantata BWV 191.
- **1748–49** — Bach composes/assembles the Symbolum Nicenum (Credo) and completes the full Mass.
- **1786** — First public performance of the Symbolum Nicenum section by C.P.E. Bach.
- **1859** — First documented complete public performance in Leipzig.
- **1900** — American premiere of the complete Mass in Bethlehem, Pennsylvania.
- **1929** — First complete recording made by Albert Coates.
- **2015** — Bach's autograph manuscript inscribed in UNESCO's Memory of the World Register.

## Body
### Composition and Structure
The Mass is a *Missa tota* (a complete setting of the Latin Ordinary), which was exceptional for a Lutheran composer like Bach, who typically set only the Kyrie and Gloria. It is structured in four major sections: "Missa" (Kyrie and Gloria), "Symbolum Nicenum" (Credo), "Sanctus", and a final section containing the Osanna, Benedictus, Agnus Dei, and Dona Nobis Pacem. The work is scored for five soloists, a five-part choir, and a Baroque orchestra including natural trumpets and horns. Its scale is unusual, with a performance time of around two hours.

The composition is largely based on earlier works by Bach. He reworked movements from his cantatas and other compositions, a common practice known as parody. For example, the "Gratias agimus" is from a 1731 cantata, and the "Crucifixus" is from a 1714 cantata. Only a few movements, like the "Et incarnatus est" and parts of the "Confiteor", appear to be newly composed for the final version. Scholars debate the exact purpose of the completed work; it may have been intended for the dedication of the Dresden Hofkirche or for a performance in Vienna.

### Manuscripts and Editions
Two primary autograph sources exist: the 1733 Kyrie-Gloria parts and the complete 1748–50 score. The latter was inherited by C.P.E. Bach, who made performance alterations, such as adding an introduction and changing instrumentation. This complicates modern editing. Major critical editions include those by the Bach Gesellschaft (1857), Friedrich Smend (1954), Christoph Wolff (1997), Joshua Rifkin (2006), and Uwe Wolf (2010), each using different methods to reconstruct Bach's original intent.

### Performance History
The Sanctus was performed by Bach in 1724. Scholars disagree on whether the 1733 Missa was performed in Dresden. The first public performance of any part was in 1786. The first complete public performance was in 1859. The work gained popularity in the 20th century, with the first recording in 1929. The historically informed performance movement, using period instruments and smaller forces, began in the late 1960s. As of 2022, over 350 complete recordings exist.

## Terms
- **Missa tota**: A complete setting of the Latin Mass ordinary (Kyrie, Gloria, Credo, Sanctus, Agnus Dei).
- **Mass ordinary**: The set of texts in the Catholic Mass that are the same every day.
- **Parody**: The common Baroque practice of reworking existing vocal music for a new text.
- **Symbolum Nicenum**: The Nicene Creed; the text for the Credo section of the Mass.
- **Urtext edition**: A critical edition of a musical work that aims to present the composer's original text as accurately as possible.
- **Basso continuo**: The bass line and harmonic accompaniment in Baroque music, typically played by keyboard and bass instrument.
- **Oboe d'amore**: A Baroque oboe pitched a minor third lower than the standard oboe.
- **Natural trumpet/horn**: Valves-less brass instruments that can only play notes from the harmonic series of their fundamental pitch.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Purpose of the work**: Was the completed Mass intended for a specific event, such as the Dresden Hofkirche dedication (1751) or a performance in Vienna (1749), or was it a more general "encyclopedic" project?
- **Performance in 1733**: Did Bach ever perform the initial Kyrie-Gloria Missa in Dresden in 1733? Scholars like Christoph Wolff argue yes, while others like Joshua Rifkin are skeptical.
- **Original scoring**: For some movements like "Quoniam", scholars debate whether the final scoring (e.g., horn) represents the original conception or a later adaptation from a different lost source.
- **Editorial challenges**: How should editors handle C.P.E. Bach's alterations to the autograph score? Different editions (Wolff, Rifkin, Wolf) prioritize different source materials and methodologies to reconstruct J.S. Bach's final intentions.

# Shivaji

## Overview
Shivaji I (c. 1630–1680) was the founder of the Maratha Kingdom in western India. He rose from a minor feudal vassal to establish an independent Hindu sovereign realm, or *Hindavi Swaraj*, through military innovation and administrative skill. His legacy is a central element of Maharashtra's identity and modern Indian nationalism.

## Timeline
- **c. 1630** — Born at Shivneri fort to Shahaji Bhonsle and Jijabai.
- **1647** — Captures Torna Fort, beginning independent military campaigns.
- **1659** — Defeats and kills Bijapur general Afzal Khan at the Battle of Pratapgarh.
- **1663** — Leads a night raid on the Mughal camp of Shaista Khan near Pune.
- **1664** — Sacks the wealthy Mughal port of Surat for the first time.
- **1665** — Defeated by Mughal general Jai Singh I; signs the Treaty of Purandar.
- **1666** — Escapes house arrest in Agra after being summoned by Emperor Aurangzeb.
- **1670** — Resumes hostilities with the Mughals; sacks Surat a second time.
- **1674** — Crowned *Chhatrapati* (sovereign king) at Raigad Fort.
- **1677** — Invades Karnataka, seizing Vellore and Gingee forts.
- **1680** — Dies at Raigad Fort, succeeded by his son Sambhaji.

## Body

### Early Life and Conflict with Bijapur
Shivaji was born into the Bhonsle clan, a Maratha family serving the Deccan Sultanates. His father, Shahaji, was a general for Bijapur, while his mother, Jijabai, instilled in him a vision of Hindu sovereignty. After his father's administrator died in 1647, Shivaji took control of Pune and began capturing nearby forts like Torna and Purandar, using their treasure to build his power base. The Bijapur Sultanate, alarmed by his expansion, sent the general Afzal Khan to arrest him in 1659. Shivaji lured Khan to a meeting at Pratapgarh fort, where he killed him in a premeditated attack, then decisively defeated the Bijapuri army. This victory established his military reputation.

### Conflict with the Mughal Empire
Shivaji's relationship with the Mughal Empire shifted between alliance and war. After initial raids against Mughal territory, he was confronted by Emperor Aurangzeb's forces. A major Mughal army under Shaista Khan seized Pune in 1660, but Shivaji executed a daring night raid in 1663, wounding Khan and forcing his retreat. To replenish his treasury, Shivaji sacked the Mughal port of Surat in 1664. Aurangzeb then sent the Rajput general Jai Singh I, who besieged Shivaji's forts. Facing defeat, Shivaji signed the Treaty of Purandar (1665), surrendering 23 forts and agreeing to serve as a Mughal *mansabdar* (ranked noble). He was later summoned to the Mughal court at Agra in 1666, where he was placed under house arrest. Shivaji escaped in August 1666, likely by bribing guards, and after a period of peace, resumed hostilities in 1670, recovering most of his lost territories.

### Coronation and Administration
Despite his de facto control, Shivaji lacked a formal royal title. In 1674, after overcoming opposition from Brahmins who questioned his *kshatriya* (warrior) lineage, he was crowned king at Raigad Fort. The ceremony, officiated by a Varanasi priest, declared him *Chhatrapati* and *Haindava Dharmodhhaarak* (protector of the Hindu faith). He established the *Ashta Pradhan*, a council of eight ministers, and promoted Marathi and Sanskrit over Persian in administration. His governance was notably inclusive, employing Muslims and Europeans in his forces and administration.

### Military Strategy and Navy
Shivaji mastered guerrilla warfare, or *Ganimi Kawa*, using his light cavalry and intimate knowledge of the Western Ghats terrain to harass larger, conventional armies. His strategy relied on a network of hill forts, each guarded by three officers to prevent betrayal. He also built a coastal navy to control the Konkan shoreline, constructing forts like Sindhudurg and employing diverse crews, including local sailors and European experts. This naval power allowed him to challenge regional maritime powers.

### Death and Succession
Shivaji died in 1680 after a brief illness. A succession dispute followed: his wife Soyarabai had their young son Rajaram crowned, but Shivaji's elder son Sambhaji seized Raigad Fort and took the throne, executing Soyarabai for conspiracy.

## Terms
- **Chhatrapati**: A sovereign ruler; the title Shivaji adopted at his coronation.
- **Mansabdar**: A ranked noble in the Mughal administrative system.
- **Zamindar**: A feudal landholder or chief, often serving a larger sovereign.
- **Jagir**: A fiefdom granted in lieu of a salary, from which the holder collected revenue.
- **Swaraj**: Self-rule or independence; *Hindavi Swaraj* referred to Shivaji's independent Hindu kingdom.
- **Ashta Pradhan**: The council of eight ministers that formed Shivaji's administrative core.
- **Ganimi Kawa**: The Marathi term for Shivaji's guerrilla warfare tactics.
- **Kshatriya**: The warrior varna (social class) in the traditional Hindu caste system.
- **Sati**: The practice of a widow immolating herself on her husband's funeral pyre.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Date of Birth**: Scholars disagree on his exact birth date; the Government of Maharashtra officially observes 19 February.
- **Cause of Death**: Contemporary sources conflict, citing bloody flux (dysentery), anthrax, or fever. Allegations of poisoning by his wife Soyarabai are doubted by later scholars.
- **Lineage and Coronation**: The Brahmin opposition to his coronation centered on his claimed descent. His asserted Rajput (Sisodia) ancestry is considered by historians to be "tenuous at best, or even being purely invented."
- **Historical Interpretation**: Shivaji's legacy is contested. While celebrated as a national hero and secular ruler by many, some modern Hindu nationalist groups portray him primarily as a "Hindu king" who fought Muslim rulers, a view that contrasts with historical accounts of his alliances and inclusive policies.

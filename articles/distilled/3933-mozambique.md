# Mozambique

## Overview
Mozambique is a Southeast African nation with a long history of Indian Ocean trade, followed by over four centuries of Portuguese colonial rule. After independence in 1975, it endured a devastating civil war before transitioning to a multiparty democracy. Today, it is a resource-rich but poor country facing challenges of corruption, inequality, and insurgencies, while its economy grows from agriculture, fishing, and emerging natural gas projects.

- **1977–1992** — A protracted civil war between the FRELIMO government and RENAMO rebels.
- **October 1992** — The Rome General Peace Accords end the civil war.

- **2017** — An Islamist insurgency begins in the northern province of Cabo Delgado.
- **15 January 2025** — Daniel Chapo is sworn in as the fifth president.

## Body

### Pre-Colonial and Colonial History
Bantu-speaking peoples migrated into the area as early as the 4th century BC, bringing iron-smelting technology. From the late first millennium AD, the coast became part of Indian Ocean trade networks, leading to the development of Swahili port towns like Sofala and Mozambique Island. The Portuguese arrived in 1498, gradually displacing Arab traders and establishing a colonial presence. Their rule was often indirect, using land grants called *prazos* administered by settlers who became Africanized. In the early 20th century, administration was largely handed to private chartered companies, which built infrastructure to supply forced labor to neighboring colonies.

### Independence and Civil War
FRELIMO launched a guerrilla campaign against Portuguese rule in 1964. Following the 1974 Carnation Revolution in Portugal, Mozambique gained independence in 1975. The new Marxist government, led by Samora Machel, expelled most Portuguese residents. A brutal civil war soon erupted between the FRELIMO regime, backed by the Soviet Union and Cuba, and the RENAMO rebels, supported by Rhodesia and apartheid South Africa. The conflict caused an estimated one million deaths, massive displacement, and economic collapse. It ended with the 1992 Rome General Peace Accords, brokered by the Community of Sant'Egidio.

### Democratic Era and Modern Challenges
Since 1994, Mozambique has held regular elections, though later ones have been marred by fraud allegations. The FRELIMO party has remained in power. The country experienced strong economic growth after the war, driven by reforms and foreign investment. However, since 2014/15, growth has been accompanied by rising inequality. A low-intensity insurgency by RENAMO occurred from 2013 to 2019. More significantly, since 2017, a violent Islamist insurgency in the northern Cabo Delgado province has threatened major liquefied natural gas projects and displaced hundreds of thousands. The government has responded with military force, including a major bilateral intervention by Rwanda.

### Economy and Resources
Mozambique is one of the world's poorest countries, with an economy based on agriculture, fishing, and growing industry. It has vast natural resources, including major coal deposits and enormous offshore natural gas reserves in the Rovuma Basin, which could make it a top global LNG exporter. Despite high GDP growth rates in the 2000s, development is hampered by widespread corruption, poor infrastructure, and low human development indicators. The metical is the official currency, but foreign currencies are widely used.

### Demographics and Society
The population of over 34 million is composed of more than 2,000 ethnic groups, predominantly Bantu peoples. Portuguese is the official language and a lingua franca, while numerous local languages like Makhuwa and Sena are spoken. Christianity is the largest religion, followed by Islam, which is predominant in the north. The country has a very young population but faces severe health challenges, including high rates of HIV/AIDS and maternal mortality. Literacy rates are improving but remain low, especially among women.

## Terms
*   **FRELIMO:** The Front for the Liberation of Mozambique, the ruling party since independence, originally a Marxist-Leninist liberation movement.
*   **RENAMO:** The Mozambican National Resistance, originally an anti-communist rebel group backed by foreign powers during the civil war, now the main opposition party.
*   **Prazos:** Land grants established by the Portuguese in central Mozambique, administered by settlers (*prazeiros*) who often developed hybrid African-Portuguese cultures.
*   **Chartered Companies:** Private companies (e.g., Mozambique Company) granted concessions by Portugal in the early 20th century to administer territory and exploit resources, often using forced labor.
*   **Rome General Peace Accords:** The 1992 agreement, mediated by the Community of Sant'Egidio, that ended the Mozambican Civil War and established a framework for multiparty democracy.
*   **Cabo Delgado Insurgency:** An ongoing violent Islamist insurgency that began in 2017 in northern Mozambique, threatening regional stability and major energy projects.
*   **Liquefied Natural Gas (LNG):** Natural gas cooled to a liquid state for transport; major offshore discoveries in Mozambique's Rovuma Basin are central to its future economic plans.
*   **Bantu Peoples:** A large and diverse group of African peoples who speak Bantu languages and form the overwhelming majority of Mozambique's population.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Electoral Integrity:** While the first post-war elections were considered free and fair, there is significant evidence and opposition claims that subsequent elections have been fraudulent, challenging the country's democratic credentials.
*   **Economic Development vs. Inequality:** Mozambique has experienced periods of high GDP growth, but this has not consistently translated into broad-based development. A key debate is how to manage its vast natural resource wealth (especially gas) to reduce poverty and inequality rather than enriching elites.
*   **Security and Governance:** The government's response to the Cabo Delgado insurgency, including reliance on foreign military intervention (Rwanda, SADC), raises questions about national sovereignty and long-term security strategy. The conflict's roots in local grievances versus global jihadism are also debated.
*   **Corruption:** Widespread corruption scandals, including the "tuna bond" scandal, have undermined donor confidence and economic stability, prompting ongoing debate about the effectiveness of anti-corruption measures.

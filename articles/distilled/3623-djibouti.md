# Djibouti

## Overview
Djibouti is a small, strategically located country in the Horn of Africa, at the southern entrance to the Red Sea. Its modern identity is shaped by its role as a key maritime hub and its history as a French colony, which ended in 1977. The nation's economy and politics are heavily influenced by its control of vital shipping lanes and its hosting of multiple foreign military bases.

## Timeline
- **c. 3000 BC** — The region is part of the ancient Land of Punt, trading with Egypt.
- **9th century** — The Adal Sultanate, centered at Zeila, is established by Somali clans.
- **1529-1543** — Ahmad ibn Ibrahim al-Ghazi leads the Adal Sultanate's conquest of much of Christian Abyssinia.
- **1862** — France signs a treaty with Afar sultans, establishing its first foothold at Obock.
- **1894** — French Somaliland is formally established with Djibouti City as its capital.
- **1967** — The territory is renamed the French Territory of the Afars and the Issas.
- **1977** — A referendum approves independence; the Republic of Djibouti is established.
- **1991-2001** — The Djiboutian Civil War between the government and the FRUD opposition ends in a power-sharing agreement.
- **2002** — The United States leases the former French base, Camp Lemonnier.
- **2018** — The new Addis Ababa–Djibouti Railway begins operation.

## Body

### History and Colonial Era
The area has been inhabited since the Neolithic period and was part of the ancient Land of Punt. In the medieval era, it was home to the Adal and Ifat Sultanates, which engaged in prolonged conflict with the Christian Ethiopian Empire. In the late 19th century, during the Scramble for Africa, France established a colony by signing treaties with local Afar and Somali sultans. The construction of a railway to Ethiopia turned Djibouti into a major port, superseding older caravan routes. The colony was renamed French Somaliland in 1894 and again to the French Territory of the Afars and the Issas in 1967. After two earlier referendums favored continued association with France, a third in 1977 approved independence by a landslide.

### Politics and Governance
Djibouti is a unitary presidential republic where executive power is dominant. The government has been controlled since independence by the People's Rally for Progress (RPP) party. A civil war in the 1990s between the government and the Front for the Restoration of Unity and Democracy (FRUD) ended with a 2000 power-sharing agreement, after which FRUD became a political party. The country's strategic location has led to the establishment of foreign military bases, including those of the United States, France, China, Japan, and Italy. These leases are a significant part of the national economy.

### Economy and Strategic Location
Djibouti's economy is service-based, centered on its strategic position near the Bab-el-Mandeb Strait, a critical chokepoint for global shipping. The Port of Djibouti handles over 95% of the transit trade for landlocked Ethiopia. The government has invested in port expansion and the new Addis Ababa–Djibouti Railway to solidify this role. Revenue from foreign military base leases and port services are major contributors to GDP. The country also has potential in geothermal energy and salt production from Lake Assal.

### Demographics and Culture
Djibouti is a multi-ethnic nation with a population of about 1.07 million (2024 census). The two largest groups are the Somali (60%) and Afar (35%), both speaking Cushitic languages. French and Arabic are official languages. The population is predominantly Sunni Muslim. Culture reflects a blend of Somali, Afar, Yemeni, and French influences, with a strong oral tradition in poetry and music. Traditional attire includes the macawiis for men and the dirac for women.

## Terms
- **Adal Sultanate**: A medieval Muslim state in the Horn of Africa, centered at Zeila, that warred with the Ethiopian Empire.
- **Bab-el-Mandeb**: A strait between Djibouti and Yemen, a key shipping lane connecting the Red Sea and the Gulf of Aden.
- **Cushitic**: A branch of the Afroasiatic language family, which includes Somali and Afar.
- **FRUD**: The Front for the Restoration of Unity and Democracy, an opposition group that fought a civil war against the government in the 1990s.
- **IGAD**: The Intergovernmental Authority on Development, a regional development organization headquartered in Djibouti.
- **Issa**: A major Somali clan that forms a significant portion of Djibouti's population.
- **Lake Assal**: A crater lake in Djibouti, the lowest point in Africa and a major salt source.
- **Xeer**: A traditional system of customary law used by Somali and Afar communities.

## Debates and open questions
- The exact location of the ancient Land of Punt is debated, though the region including Djibouti is considered a likely candidate.
- The etymology of the name "Djibouti" is disputed, with theories linking it to Afar words for "plate" or "upland," or to the Egyptian god Thoth.
- The circumstances surrounding the death of independence advocate Mahmoud Harbi in a 1960 plane crash are considered suspicious.

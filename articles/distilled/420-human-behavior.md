# Human behavior

## Overview
Human behavior is the capacity of individuals or groups to respond to internal and external stimuli throughout life, driven by a complex interplay of genetic predispositions, environmental influences, psychological traits, and cultural norms. It spans domains from basic physiological maintenance to complex social, cognitive, moral, and digital interactions. The study of human behavior is inherently interdisciplinary, drawing on psychology, sociology, neuroscience, economics, and anthropology to understand how biological maturation, social learning, and cultural evolution shape actions across the lifespan. Central to this field is the nature versus nurture debate, now largely resolved into an interactionist perspective recognizing that behavioral outcomes emerge from dynamic gene-environment interplay.

## Timeline
- **c. 70,000–100,000 years ago** — Early modern humans migrate out of Africa, initiating cultural evolution and adaptation to diverse global environments.
- **c. 50,000 years ago** — Colonization of Australia demonstrates advanced navigation and cultural transmission capabilities.
- **Neolithic Revolution (c. 10,000 BC)** — Development of agriculture enables resource surplus, labor specialization, and complex economic behavior.
- **Industrial Revolution (18th–19th century)** — Factory system transforms work structure, enabling mass production, urbanization, and modern consumer behavior.
- **2018** — Pew Research Center reports 54% of global adults state religion is "very important" in their lives.
- **Contemporary** — Average human spends 6.5 hours daily online; smartphone addiction affects ~6.3% of global users (higher in subgroups).

## Body

### Social Behavior and Norms
Social behavior involves actions directed at others, underpinned by advanced **theory of mind** (the ability to attribute mental states to oneself and others). It is governed by **social norms**—unwritten, culturally ingrained expectations regulating decorum, morality, property, and justice. Norms facilitate coordination but are enforced through social pressure and risk of exclusion. **Ethical systems** (derived from divine law, natural law, reason, or civil authority) guide moral behavior, enabling **altruism** (welfare consideration for others beyond biological kin). **Deviance**—violation of norms—is subjective and culturally relative; it is sanctioned via stigma, violence, or criminal justice systems to prevent harm or enforce worldviews.

### Interpersonal Relationships and Politics
Relationships form through communication, creating intimacy and identity. **Extraverts** actively seek interaction; **introverts** do not. **Romantic love** varies culturally but often links to sexual attraction and marriage. **Family** units based on **consanguinity** (blood relation) involve extended **parental investment** due to prolonged human development. In **politics**, humans form **in-groups** (cooperation) and **out-groups** (competition), leading to conformity, authority obedience, and hostility toward outsiders. **Conflict** arises from disagreement, obstruction, or negative emotion; it ranges from interpersonal to international (diplomacy or war), with social conflict often driven by marginalization or resource scarcity.

### Cultural and Cross-Cultural Behavior
Cultural behavior—patterns shaped by shared values and practices—evolved rapidly via **cultural transmission** (social learning), allowing colonization of diverse environments (e.g., Polynesian Pacific expansion). A key distinction is **individualistic** (analytic thinking, independent self) versus **collectivistic** (holistic thinking, interdependent self) orientations, affecting cognition, emotion, and morality. Research faces a **WEIRD** sampling bias (Western, Educated, Industrialized, Rich, Democratic populations represent <12% of humanity but >95% of samples). **Acculturation** describes behavioral and identity changes during cultural contact, ranging from assimilation to integration, influenced by cultural distance and social support.

### Developmental Behavior
Behavior changes systematically across the lifespan via biological maturation and environmental interaction.
*   **Infancy/Early Childhood:** Rapid emergence of social interaction, emotional regulation, and **attachment** (bond with caregiver forming internal working models for future relationships). **Theory of mind** emerges ~age 4–5.
*   **Adolescence:** Prefrontal cortex reorganization drives identity formation and risk-taking; ~1 in 7 adolescents globally face mental health challenges.
*   **Adulthood:** Young adulthood focuses on intimacy/career; middle adulthood on **generativity** (contributing to next generation). **Neuroplasticity** (brain's ability to reorganize) supports lifelong adaptation.
*   **Late Life:** **Socioemotional selectivity theory** posits older adults prioritize emotionally meaningful relationships to maximize well-being.
Key frameworks: Piaget (cognitive stages), Erikson (psychosocial stages), Bronfenbrenner (ecological systems: nested environmental contexts from family to culture).

### Moral Behavior
Moral behavior involves decisions guided by principles of right/wrong, distinct in humans for its complexity. It arises from interaction between emotional intuitions and rational deliberation, mediated by the **ventromedial prefrontal cortex (VMPFC)**. **Kohlberg’s stages** progress from obedience (childhood) to universal ethical principles (adulthood). **Moral Foundations Theory** identifies six universal concerns (care/harm, fairness/cheating, loyalty/betrayal, authority/subversion, sanctity/degradation, liberty/oppression) weighted differently across cultures. Situational factors like the **bystander effect** (reduced helping in groups) modulate expression.

### Antisocial and Criminal Behavior
Actions violating norms/laws involve genetic (~50% variance), environmental (childhood trauma, disadvantage), and neurobiological factors (prefrontal cortex/amygdala imbalance).
*   **Deception/Fraud:** Average 1–2 lies/day; **pathological lying** is compulsive. Fraud (e.g., Ponzi schemes) exploits cognitive biases (authority bias, social proof). Conspiracy theories exploit confirmation bias.
*   **Exploitation:** Economic (wage theft, gig economy risk transfer), sexual (trafficking), environmental (degradation imposing costs on marginalized groups), and digital (**surveillance capitalism**: converting experience into behavioral data for prediction products). Historical forms: slavery, unethical experimentation (prompting Nuremberg Code).
*   **Violence:** Failure of prefrontal regulation over limbic aggression. Includes domestic violence, hate crimes (group dynamics amplify via **deindividuation**—loss of self-awareness in groups—and **moral disengagement**), and serial killing.
*   **Sexual Crimes/Organized Crime:** Non-consensual acts causing trauma; structured groups (cartels) using violence/corruption for profit.
*   **Extremism:** Ideologies justifying violence; radicalization involves quest for significance, extremist narratives, and network integration. **Antisocial personality disorder/psychopathy** involve empathy/remorse deficits.

### Cognitive Behavior
Human cognition is distinguished by **cultural accumulation** of knowledge, **language**-based transmission, and **neuroplasticity**.
*   **Learning/Habit:** Practice modifies neural pathways; repetition ingrains **habits** (automatic behaviors).
*   **Reasoning:** Mostly automatic. **Deductive** (certain conclusions from premises) vs. **inductive** (probable conclusions from context).
*   **Emotion:** Basic emotions (joy, fear, anger) are universal; complex emotions (guilt, pride) are culturally shaped. Humans maintain a **hedonic set point** (baseline happiness) influenced by health/relationships.
*   **Creativity:** Combining existing resources for novelty; drives art/science. Requires domain-specific knowledge.
*   **Religion:** Traditions involving myths, rituals, taboos, symbolism, morality, altered states, and supernatural belief. Costly signaling (high time/energy cost) conflicts with rational choice but provides community benefits. 54% globally rate religion "very important" (2018).

### Psychological Behavior
Encompasses mental well-being continuum: emotional regulation, stress response, trauma, resilience, biases, addiction, personality.
*   **Regulation/Stress:** **Emotional dysregulation** is transdiagnostic (across disorders). Effective strategies: mindfulness, cognitive reappraisal. Positive coping predicts well-being (β=0.43); negative coping predicts distress (β=0.81).
*   **Trauma/Resilience:** Responses: fight, flight, freeze, fawn. **PTSD** affects ~3.5% adults/year. **Resilience** involves cognitive flexibility, optimism, social connection, meaning-making; buildable via CBT/mindfulness.
*   **Cognitive Biases:** Systematic deviations from rationality (confirmation bias, availability heuristic, anchoring). Explain 30–45% variance in judgment errors.
*   **Addiction/Compulsion:** **Addiction** hijacks dopamine system (nucleus accumbens), shifting behavior from impulsive to compulsive. **Compulsion**: repetitive acts felt as mandatory but misaligned with goals. ~10% show sub-threshold OCD symptoms.
*   **Personality:** **Big Five** traits (OCEAN: Openness, Conscientiousness, Extraversion, Agreeableness, Neuroticism). Neuroticism predicts anxiety/depression; Conscientiousness predicts self-regulation.
*   **Interventions:** **ACT** (psychological flexibility), **DBT** (distress tolerance, emotion regulation), digital therapeutics. **Positive psychology**: gratitude, optimism, forgiveness enhance well-being.
*   **Cognitive/Health:** **ADHD** impairs attention/regulation. **Sleep** causally affects mental health (poor sleep ↑ negative emotion, ↓ positive). Sleep, activity, diet are interconnected health pillars.

### Physiological Behavior
Biological maintenance behaviors shared with animals, plus uniquely human health practices.
*   **Basics:** Eating (nutrition/pleasure, preparation), waste elimination (taboo in urban settings), sleep (90-min cycles, 3–5/night, regulated by **circadian rhythm**—internal 24-hr clock—and **homeostasis**—pressure increasing with deprivation).
*   **Health:** Medicine, nutrition, hygiene, exercise, birth control. **Disgust** evolved as pathogen avoidance (feces, rot, vectors). Hygiene: grooming, corpse disposal, sewage, cleaning.
*   **Reproduction:** Sexual intercourse for reproduction/pleasure. Unique intentional fertility control. Mating structures: monogamy, polygyny, polyandry (culturally shaped). Spontaneous ovulation (menstrual cycle 25–35 days), not seasonal.
*   **Movement/Tool Use:** Bipedal gait (learned 7–9 years, unique individual patterns). **Endurance running hypothesis**: humans outpace animals over distance via sweating (thermoregulation). **Prehensile hand** enables complex tool use.

### Economic Behavior
Decisions often **reference-dependent** (vs. status quo) and **loss-averse** (fear loss > seek gain).
*   **Work:** Hunter-gatherers: work = life. **Neolithic Revolution**: agriculture → surplus → specialization (crafts, admin) → non-laborious work. **Industrial Revolution**: factory system → collaboration, employer authority, end of forced labor. **Post-industrial**: service/knowledge economy. Motivation: material gain, self-esteem, identity, creativity. Blue-collar (laborious) vs. white-collar (non-laborious).
*   **Leisure:** Non-work activity for relaxation/identity. **Serious leisure** (hobbies, volunteering) vs. **casual leisure** (passive/active entertainment, sensory stimulation). Risks: boredom, substance abuse.
*   **Consumption:** Humans as consumers. Mass consumption began with Industrial Revolution. Decisions influenced by product nature, cost, convenience, advertising, culture, social class, reference groups.

### Digital Behavior
Actions via smartphones, social media, internet (avg. 6.5 hrs/day).
*   **Social Media:** Alters communication/relationships. Heavy use linked to brain structure changes (reward/addiction areas), accelerated cerebellum development in children (9–11 yrs), and 2–3x higher odds of perceived social isolation (strongest in 19–32 yr olds). Yet, internet use ↑ family communication (~102 min/week per SD usage increase).
*   **Cyberbullying:** Digital harassment (anonymity, 24/7 access, viral spread). Victimization: 13.99%–57.5% of youth globally. Females, frequent users more vulnerable. Image/video bullying most harmful (32% stress, 38% distress).
*   **Addiction/Wellness:** Smartphone addiction ~6.3% global (up to 81.1% in medical students); women > men. Linked to emotion dysregulation, impulsivity, cognitive decline, sleep disturbance, reduced gray matter. Paradox: moderate use in older adults ↓ cognitive impairment risk.
*   **Digital Consumerism:** AI/ML algorithms personalize marketing, predict preferences in real-time, mediate product discovery.

### Gender and Sexual Behavior
Complex interaction of biological, psychological, social, cultural factors. Dimensions: biological sex, gender identity, gender expression, sexual orientation (continua, not binaries).
*   **Gender Identity:** Stable sense by age 3–5. Cross-cultural non-binary categories: **hijra** (South Asia), **fa'afafine** (Samoa), **Two-Spirit** (Native American). Neurobiology: prenatal hormones, genetics, brain structure (gender dysphoria brains resemble experienced gender). Supportive environments → better mental health.
*   **Sexual Orientation:** Continuum of attraction. Factors: genetics, prenatal hormones, **fraternal birth order effect** (each older brother ↑ homosexuality odds ~33% in men, suggesting maternal immune response). Epigenetics likely involved.
*   **Intimate Relationships:** **Pair bonding** (intimacy, affection, commitment) serves psychological/social functions. **Attachment theory**: early caregiving shapes adult styles (secure → better satisfaction/communication). Sexual behavior varies widely; comprehensive education → delayed initiation, ↑ contraception, ↓ STIs. Diverse structures (beyond monogamy) validated; satisfaction depends on communication/respect/values.
*   **Implications:** Acceptance → better mental health. **Minority stress theory**: stigma/prejudice → chronic stress → disparities (depression, anxiety, suicide) in LGBTQ+ populations, attributable to social factors, not identity. Family/peer/community support is protective. Historical attitudes vary; globalization brings awareness and, in some contexts, persecution.

### Ecological Behavior
Humans interact with ecosystems (natural and human-made: urban, agricultural). Geography/landscape ecology determine distribution. Control over animals: **domestication** (pets for companionship, livestock for products, lab animals for testing), **nature reserves/zoos** (tourism/conservation). Human habitation alters environments; environments shape behavior.

### Causes and Factors
*   **Structure vs. Agency:** Debate on whether behavior is driven by individual impulses (agency) or external forces (structure).
*   **Behavioral Genetics:** Inherited traits increase likelihood of behaviors/personalities; not deterministic. Gene-environment interaction is key.
*   **Age:** Infants develop object permanence, categorization, imitation, gestures (9–10 mo), speech (2nd yr). Children (3–6 yr): fine motor skills, complex emotions (empathy, humor), play-based learning, peer influence, self-concept (gender/ethnicity). Adolescents: puberty hormones (testosterone/estradiol → sensation seeking, risk-taking), social reorientation, self-concept solidification, egocentrism.
*   **Culture/Environment:** **Neuroplasticity** allows brain restructuring via experience. Culture/language distinctively shape human behavior via **social learning** (observation/imitation) and norm definition.
*   **Physiology:** Neurotransmitters, hormones, metabolism drive behavior. Physical disabilities necessitate alternative behaviors/accommodations (healthcare, assistive tech); severe disability → ↑ leisure time but ↓ satisfaction, ↓ productivity/health. Mental disabilities (mood, anxiety, personality, substance disorders) directly affect cognitive/social behavior.

## Terms
- ****Theory of Mind**** — The cognitive ability to attribute mental states—beliefs, intents, desires, knowledge—to oneself and others, understanding that others have perspectives different from one's own.
- ****Social Norms**** — Unwritten, culturally shared expectations for behavior that regulate social life (decorum, morality, justice) and are enforced through social pressure and exclusion.
- ****Neuroplasticity**** — The brain's ability to reorganize its structure, functions, and connections in response to learning, experience, or injury throughout the lifespan.
- ****WEIRD**** — Acronym for Western, Educated, Industrialized, Rich, and Democratic populations, which are vastly overrepresented in psychological research (<12% of world, >95% of samples).
- ****Attachment**** — The enduring emotional bond between an infant and primary caregiver that establishes "internal working models" influencing social and emotional behavior across life.
- ****Ventromedial Prefrontal Cortex (VMPFC)**** — A brain region critical for integrating emotional and cognitive information to generate moral judgments and guide decision-making.
- ****Deindividuation**** — A psychological state in group settings where reduced self-awareness and accountability lead to disinhibited, often aggressive or antisocial behavior.
- ****Surveillance Capitalism**** — An economic system where human behavioral data is extracted, predicted, and sold as futures to third parties for profit, often without explicit consent.
- ****Minority Stress Theory**** — A framework explaining how chronic stress from stigma, prejudice, and discrimination creates mental health disparities in sexual and gender minority populations.
- ****Fraternal Birth Order Effect**** — The finding that each older biological brother increases the odds of homosexuality in men by ~33%, hypothesized to result from maternal immune responses to male fetal antigens.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Nature vs. Nurture / Structure vs. Agency:** The relative weighting of genetic predispositions versus environmental/cultural determinism, and individual free will versus structural constraint, remains a central theoretical tension.
*   **WEIRD Generalizability:** The extent to which psychological theories based on WEIRD samples apply universally is a major methodological crisis; integrating Indigenous epistemologies and diverse populations is an ongoing challenge.
*   **Digital Technology Effects:** Causality direction is debated: does heavy social media use cause depression/isolation, or do vulnerable individuals self-select into heavy use? Long-term neurodevelopmental impacts on children are unknown.
*   **Moral Universality vs. Relativity:** While Moral Foundations Theory proposes universal categories, the cultural weighting and expression of these foundations (e.g., sanctity, loyalty) vary widely, challenging claims of a single human moral nature.
*   **Antisocial Behavior Etiology:** The precise gene-environment interactions (e.g., MAOA gene × childhood maltreatment) producing psychopathy or extreme violence are not fully mapped; prediction and prevention remain limited.
*   **Gender/Sex Complexity:** The biological substrates of gender identity (prenatal hormones, brain structure) are incompletely understood; the clinical and social management of gender diversity in minors is intensely debated.
*   **Consciousness and Free Will:** Philosophy of mind questions (mind-body problem, determinism vs. libertarian free will) remain unresolved by neuroscience, affecting concepts of moral responsibility.

# Río de la Plata

## Overview
The Río de la Plata is a vast funnel-shaped estuary on the southeastern coast of South America, formed by the confluence of the Uruguay and Paraná rivers. It is a major geographical and political feature, forming part of the border between Argentina and Uruguay, and its basin is the second largest in South America. The estuary's name, meaning "River of Silver," stems from early myths of regional wealth, which also gave the country Argentina its name.

## Timeline
- **1512–13** — First explored by the Portuguese.
- **1516** — Spanish explorer Juan Díaz de Solís traverses it, calling it the "Mar Dulce" (freshwater sea).
- **1526–29** — Sebastian Cabot explores the Paraná and Uruguay rivers and renames the estuary "Río de la Plata."
- **2 February 1536** — First European colony, Buenos Aires, founded by Pedro de Mendoza (and quickly abandoned).
- **1537** — Founding of Asunción, following the failure to settle Buenos Aires.
- **11 June 1580** — Buenos Aires is re-founded by Juan de Garay.
- **1776** — The region is elevated to the Viceroyalty of the Río de la Plata under the Bourbon monarchy.
- **1806–07** — British invasion of the region is defeated by local forces.
- **1826–28** — The estuary is blockaded by the Imperial Brazilian Navy during the Cisplatine War.
- **December 1939** — Battle of the River Plate: The German pocket battleship *Admiral Graf Spee* is engaged by Allied cruisers and later scuttled in the estuary.

## Body

### Geography and Hydrology
The Río de la Plata begins at Punta Gorda and flows about 290 km east into the Atlantic Ocean, widening from 2 km to a maximum of 220 km. Its classification is debated; it is considered the world's widest river by some, while others define it as a bay or marginal sea. A submerged shoal, the Barra del Indio, divides it into an inner freshwater zone and an outer brackish estuary. The estuary behaves as a "salt wedge" system, where denser seawater flows beneath the freshwater. Its massive drainage basin, covering about one-fourth of South America, includes parts of five countries and is drained primarily by the Paraná, Paraguay, and Uruguay rivers.

### History and Colonial Trade
The region was inhabited by nomadic indigenous peoples for millennia before European arrival. Spanish exploration in the 16th century, led by figures like Solís and Cabot, was driven by the search for a passage to the Pacific and legends of silver. The first settlement at Buenos Aires (1536) failed, leading to the founding of Asunción. Buenos Aires was successfully re-founded in 1580 and became a center for trade, particularly in silver from Potosí. Much of this trade was "illicit" under Spanish Crown regulations but was tolerated. The region was organized as the Governorate, and later the Viceroyalty, of the Río de la Plata in 1776 as part of the Bourbon Reforms to increase Crown revenue.

### Conflicts and Navigation Rights
Control of the estuary and its navigation rights was a source of conflict after the independence of Spanish and Portuguese colonies. The river was blockaded multiple times: by Brazil (1826–28), France (1838–40), and an Anglo-French alliance (1845–50). During the Cisplatine War (1825–28), the Brazilian Navy blockaded the estuary, leading to several naval battles between Brazilian forces and the Argentine squadron led by Admiral William Brown. The estuary was also the site of the 1939 Battle of the River Plate, the first naval battle of World War II, where the German ship *Admiral Graf Spee* was scuttled after being damaged.

## Terms
- **Estuary**: A partially enclosed coastal body of brackish water with one or more rivers or streams flowing into it, and with a free connection to the open sea.
- **Salt wedge estuary**: A type of estuary where denser seawater flows in as a distinct layer beneath the freshwater, which floats on top.
- **Drainage basin**: An area of land where all surface water from precipitation converges to a single point at a lower elevation, usually the exit of the basin.
- **Halocline**: A subtype of chemocline caused by a strong vertical salinity gradient within a body of water.
- **Tidal prism**: The volume of water in an estuary or inlet between mean high tide and mean low tide.
- **Viceroyalty**: A territory or domain ruled by a viceroy, a deputy of a monarch.
- **Bourbon Reforms**: A series of economic and political changes in the Spanish Empire implemented by the House of Bourbon in the 18th century.
- **Potosí**: A city in present-day Bolivia, famous for its silver mines which were a major source of wealth for the Spanish Empire.

## Debates and Open Questions
The primary scholarly debate concerns the geographical classification of the Río de la Plata. Geographers disagree on whether it should be considered a river, an estuary, a gulf, or a marginal sea. This classification has implications for its status as the "widest river in the world."

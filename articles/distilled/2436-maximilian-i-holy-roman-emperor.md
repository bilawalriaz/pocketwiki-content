# Maximilian I, Holy Roman Emperor

## Overview
Maximilian I (1459–1519) was Holy Roman Emperor from 1508 to 1519 and a pivotal figure in the rise of the Habsburg dynasty. Through strategic marriages and wars, he vastly expanded Habsburg influence across Europe, laying the foundations for the empire of his grandson, Charles V. He is remembered both as "the last knight," embodying medieval chivalric ideals, and as a modernizing ruler who implemented significant administrative and legal reforms within the Holy Roman Empire.

## Timeline
- **22 March 1459** — Born in Wiener Neustadt, son of Emperor Frederick III.
- **19 August 1477** — Marries Mary of Burgundy, gaining control of the Burgundian Netherlands.
- **16 February 1486** — Elected King of the Romans.
- **27 March 1482** — Death of Mary of Burgundy; Maximilian becomes regent for their son Philip.
- **1490** — Recaptures Vienna and Austrian lands from Hungary.
- **1493** — Death of Frederick III; Maximilian becomes de facto Holy Roman Emperor.
- **1495** — Diet of Worms initiates Imperial Reform, including the *Ewiger Landfriede* (Eternal Peace).
- **22 September 1499** — Signs Treaty of Basel, granting independence to the Swiss Confederacy.
- **1508** — Proclaims himself "Elected Roman Emperor" at Trento, ending the tradition of papal coronation.
- **1515** — First Congress of Vienna arranges double marriages securing Habsburg succession in Hungary and Bohemia.
- **12 January 1519** — Dies in Wels, Upper Austria.

## Body

### Burgundy and the Netherlands
Maximilian's marriage to Mary of Burgundy in 1477 brought him the wealthy Burgundian Netherlands but also conflict with France. After Mary's death in 1482, he fought to secure the inheritance for their son, Philip the Handsome. His rule was marked by rebellions from the Netherlandish Estates, who resisted his centralizing policies and heavy taxation to fund his wars. Though he eventually suppressed the revolts by 1492, his relationship with the provinces remained strained. He established the first permanent Dutch naval institution in 1488 and, through the Treaty of Senlis (1493), recovered key territories like Artois and Franche-Comté for Habsburg rule.

### Imperial Rule and Reform
As Emperor, Maximilian presided over a major reform movement in the Holy Roman Empire. The 1495 Diet of Worms established the *Reichskammergericht* (Imperial Chamber Court) and the *Ewiger Landfriede* to end private feuds. He created a rival court, the *Reichshofrat*, and attempted to institute a common tax, the *Gemeine Pfennig*, though this largely failed due to opposition from the princes. His reforms aimed to modernize administration and strengthen imperial authority, but he often clashed with the Estates over control and funding. In 1508, he took the title "Elected Roman Emperor," breaking with the tradition of requiring a papal coronation.

### Wars and Dynastic Strategy
Maximilian was a near-constant warrior, fighting France over Burgundy and Italy, the Swiss Confederacy, and Hungary. His campaigns in Italy were often financially ruinous, earning him the nickname "Maximilian the Moneyless." His greatest success was dynastic. The marriage of his son Philip to Joanna of Castile in 1496 brought Spain into the Habsburg sphere. Later, the 1515 Congress of Vienna arranged marriages that would eventually place his grandson Ferdinand on the thrones of Hungary and Bohemia. This strategy of "Bella gerant alii, tu felix Austria nube" ("Let others wage war; you, happy Austria, marry") made the Habsburgs the dominant dynasty in Europe.

### Finance, Culture, and Legacy
Maximilian was perpetually short of money, relying heavily on loans from German banking families like the Fuggers. His debts totaled over six million gulden by his death. Despite this, he was a lavish patron of the arts and humanists, commissioning works like the *Triumphal Procession* and the *Theuerdank* epic to craft his image as "the last knight." He implemented the first codified penal law in the German-speaking world (1499) and a military code (1508). Historians debate whether he was a visionary modernizer or a reckless spendthrift, but his administrative reforms and dynastic marriages fundamentally shaped the Holy Roman Empire and European politics for centuries.

## Terms
- **King of the Romans**: The title for the elected ruler of the Holy Roman Empire before being crowned Emperor by the Pope.
- **Doppelregierung**: A "double government," referring to the period (1486–1493) when Maximilian co-ruled with his father, Frederick III.
- **Reichskammergericht**: The Imperial Chamber Court, established in 1495 as a supreme court largely independent of the Emperor.
- **Ewiger Landfriede**: The "Eternal Public Peace" proclaimed in 1495, aiming to abolish private warfare and feuds.
- **Gemeine Pfennig**: A common tax levied on the Empire's inhabitants to fund imperial institutions, first attempted in 1495.
- **Reichshofrat**: The Imperial Aulic Council, a rival court to the *Reichskammergericht* established by Maximilian in Vienna.
- **Landsknechte**: German mercenary pikemen and foot soldiers, whose tactics evolved from the Burgundian pikemen Maximilian employed.
- **Tu felix Austria nube**: A Latin motto meaning "You, happy Austria, marry," summarizing the Habsburg strategy of dynastic marriage for territorial gain.

## Debates and Open Questions
Scholars debate Maximilian's historical persona: was he the idealized "last knight" (*der letzte Ritter*), a chivalric dreamer, or a pragmatic, amoral Renaissance prince who pioneered modern statecraft? His financial practices are also contested; while he left enormous debts, some historians argue his investments in administration and military innovation were forward-looking. The effectiveness of his imperial reforms is another point of discussion—while he strengthened certain institutions, his failure to secure a permanent common tax hampered the Empire's development. Finally, his personal rule in the Netherlands is viewed by some historians as a period of political misstep and instability, contrasting with his later imperial legacy.

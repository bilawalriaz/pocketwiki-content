# James Watt

## Overview
James Watt (1736–1819) was a Scottish inventor and engineer whose improvements to the steam engine were a key driver of the Industrial Revolution. His introduction of a separate condenser dramatically increased engine efficiency, and his later adaptations for rotary motion made steam power applicable to factories and mills, fundamentally transforming industry and transportation.

## Timeline
- **1736** — Born in Greenock, Scotland.
- **1757** — Establishes a workshop at the University of Glasgow.
- **1763** — Repairs a model Newcomen engine, identifying its inefficiency.
- **1765** — Has the insight for a separate condenser while walking in Glasgow Green.
- **1769** — Patents the separate condenser (Patent 913).
- **1775** — Forms a partnership with Matthew Boulton; patent extended to 1800.
- **1776** — First commercial engines installed, primarily for pumping mines.
- **1781** — Patents the sun and planet gear for rotary motion.
- **1782** — Patents the double-acting and expansive steam engine.
- **1784** — Patents the parallel motion linkage.
- **1788** — Patents the centrifugal governor.
- **1800** — Retires; partnership and fundamental patent expire.
- **1819** — Dies at Heathfield Hall, Staffordshire.

## Body

### Early Life and Instrument Making
Born to a shipwright and contractor, Watt was educated at home and at Greenock Grammar School, showing an early aptitude for mathematics. After training as an instrument maker in London, he returned to Scotland. In 1757, he set up a workshop at the University of Glasgow, repairing scientific instruments and befriending professors Joseph Black and Adam Smith. This academic environment sparked his interest in the science of steam.

### The Steam Engine Improvement
In 1763, while repairing a model Newcomen engine, Watt recognized that its design wasted energy by repeatedly heating and cooling the cylinder. His critical insight, conceived in 1765, was to use a separate condenser to avoid this thermal waste. This "separate condenser" design radically improved power, efficiency, and cost-effectiveness. Financial and manufacturing difficulties followed until he partnered with Matthew Boulton in 1775. With access to superior ironworking via John Wilkinson, the firm of Boulton and Watt began installing commercial engines in 1776.

### Commercialization and Further Innovations
Boulton and Watt initially charged a fee based on the coal saved compared to Newcomen engines. To broaden the engine's use beyond pumping, Watt developed methods for rotary motion, notably the sun and planet gear (1781) to circumvent a crank patent. He continued to innovate, patenting the double-acting engine (1782), the parallel motion linkage (1784), and the centrifugal governor (1788). These improvements made his engines up to five times more fuel-efficient than Newcomen's.

### Other Inventions and Later Life
Watt also invented a copying machine for letters (patented 1780) and conducted chemical experiments on bleaching processes. In retirement, he continued inventing, including machines for copying sculptures. He was a member of the Lunar Society of Birmingham and was highly respected for uniting scientific theory with practical application. He died in 1819, having become a wealthy man.

## Terms
*   **Separate Condenser:** Watt's key innovation, a chamber separate from the main cylinder where steam is condensed, keeping the cylinder hot and avoiding energy waste.
*   **Newcomen Engine:** An early atmospheric steam engine (c. 1712) used mainly for pumping water from mines, which Watt sought to improve.
*   **Rotary Motion:** The conversion of the piston's back-and-forth (reciprocating) motion into circular motion, enabling engines to power machinery like mills and looms.
*   **Sun and Planet Gear:** A gearing system Watt patented to produce rotary motion, designed to avoid infringing on a separate patent for the crank.
*   **Parallel Motion Linkage:** A mechanism Watt invented to convert the circular arc of the engine's rocking beam into the straight-line motion needed for the piston rod.
*   **Centrifugal Governor:** A device using spinning weights to automatically regulate the speed of an engine, preventing it from "running away."
*   **Horsepower:** A unit of power developed by Watt to compare the output of his engines with the power of draft horses.
*   **Latent Heat:** The thermal energy released or absorbed during a constant-temperature phase change (like condensation), a concept crucial to understanding steam engine efficiency.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **The Kettle Story:** The popular tale that Watt was inspired by a boiling kettle is considered apocryphal, possibly created by his son. While Watt did use kettles as boilers in experiments, the story is seen as a myth akin to Newton's apple.
*   **Murdoch's Contributions:** There is debate over the extent of William Murdoch's contributions to inventions like the sun and planet gear and early steam locomotive experiments, which were patented by Watt. Watt's discouragement of high-pressure steam work may have delayed locomotive development.
*   **Patent Enforcement:** Watt faced significant legal battles to enforce his patents against infringers like Edward Bull and the Hornblower brothers, which were costly but ultimately successful in protecting the firm's revenue.

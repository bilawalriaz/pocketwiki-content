# Nagoya

## Overview
Nagoya is the capital of Aichi Prefecture and the fourth-most populous city in Japan, serving as the core of the Chūkyō metropolitan area. Its strategic location on the Nōbi Plain and its port have made it a major industrial, economic, and transportation hub since the early modern period. The city is historically significant as the seat of the powerful Owari Tokugawa lords and as a center for the automotive, aviation, and ceramics industries.

## Body

### History and Development
Nagoya's origins trace to ancient settlements, with the revered Atsuta Shrine dating back centuries. The modern city's foundation was laid in 1610 when shogun Tokugawa Ieyasu relocated the Owari capital and built Nagoya Castle, moving approximately 60,000 people from Kiyosu. The castle and the nearby Atsuta Shrine, a waystation on the Tōkaidō road, formed the city's core. During the Meiji Restoration, Nagoya became an industrial hub, producing pottery, textiles, and later automobiles and aircraft. Its industrial importance made it a major target in World War II, with air raids devastating the city and destroying Nagoya Castle. Post-war reconstruction allowed Nagoya to re-emerge as a leading industrial center.

### Economy and Industry
Nagoya's economy is dominated by the automotive industry, with Toyota and its affiliates (Lexus, Denso, Aisin) headquartered in or near the city. The aviation sector, historically producing the Mitsubishi A6M Zero fighter, continues with Mitsubishi Aircraft Corporation. The region has a long tradition of ceramics, from historical Seto and Tokoname ware to modern industrial ceramics by companies like NGK. Nagoya is also a center for the MICE (meetings, incentives, conferences, exhibitions) industry, hosting events like Expo 2005 and the Nagoya Protocol. The Nagoya Stock Exchange and headquarters of companies like Brother Industries are located here.

### Geography and Transport
Nagoya lies on the fertile Nōbi Plain north of Ise Bay, with the Kiso and Shōnai Rivers facilitating trade. It has a humid subtropical climate. The city is a major transport node, served by Chubu Centrair International Airport on an artificial island and Nagoya Airfield. Nagoya Station, the world's largest by floor area, is a hub for the Tōkaidō Shinkansen and conventional rail lines operated by JR Central and Meitetsu. The city has an extensive subway system and expressway network. Nagoya Port is Japan's largest by international trade value.

### Culture and Society
Nagoya's culture was fostered by the patronage of the Owari Tokugawa lords. Key landmarks include Atsuta Shrine, which houses the legendary Kusanagi sword, and Nagoya Castle, famous for its golden shachihoko ornaments. The city is known for its distinct "Nagoya meshi" cuisine, including dishes like miso katsu and tebasaki chicken wings. Traditional handicrafts include Arimatsu tie-dye, cloisonné enamelware, and the Nagoya obi sash. The city is home to numerous museums, such as the Tokugawa Art Museum and the Toyota Commemorative Museum of Industry and Technology, and is recognized as a UNESCO "Design City."

## Terms
- **Chūkyō**: An alternative name for Nagoya, meaning "central capital," used for the metropolitan area and related institutions.
- **Owari Province**: The historical province whose capital was moved to Nagoya in 1610.
- **MICE**: An acronym for Meetings, Incentives, Conferences, and Exhibitions, a growing industry sector for the city.
- **Nagoya meshi**: The unique local cuisine of Nagoya, featuring distinct flavors like red miso.
- **Shachihoko**: A mythical tiger-headed carp ornament, the golden ones on Nagoya Castle are a city symbol.
- **Kusanagi no Tsurugi**: One of the three Imperial Regalia of Japan, a legendary sword said to be enshrined at Atsuta Shrine.
- **Nōbi Plain**: The fertile alluvial plain on which Nagoya is built.
- **Tōkaidō Shinkansen**: The high-speed rail line connecting Nagoya to Tokyo and Osaka.

## Debates and open questions
The sister city relationship with Nanjing, China, was suspended in 2012 following public comments by Nagoya's mayor denying the Nanjing Massacre. The city's bid to host the 1988 Summer Olympics was unsuccessful, losing to Seoul.

# Gaza Strip

## Overview
The Gaza Strip is a small, densely populated Palestinian territory on the Mediterranean coast, bordered by Israel and Egypt. It has been a central flashpoint in the Israeli-Palestinian conflict for decades, marked by cycles of conflict, blockade, and humanitarian crisis. Its political and humanitarian status is a major subject of international law and debate.

## Timeline
- **1948** — Following the 1948 Arab–Israeli War, Gaza's borders are set by the Green Line; it is administered by Egypt.
- **1967** — Israel captures and occupies the Gaza Strip during the Six-Day War.
- **1987** — The First Intifada, a Palestinian uprising, begins in the Jabalia refugee camp in Gaza.
- **1994** — The Oslo Accords establish the Palestinian Authority (PA) to govern parts of Gaza and the West Bank.
- **2005** — Israel unilaterally withdraws its military and settlements from Gaza.
- **2006** — Hamas wins Palestinian legislative elections.
- **2007** — Hamas takes full control of Gaza after fighting with Fatah; Israel and Egypt impose a blockade.
- **2008-2009** — The Gaza War (Operation Cast Lead) between Israel and Hamas.
- **2014** — The Gaza War (Operation Protective Edge) occurs.
- **2018-2019** — The Great March of Return protests are held near the Israel-Gaza barrier.
- **2023** — The Gaza war begins following a Hamas attack on Israel on 7 October.
- **2025** — A provisional ceasefire begins in January; a US-led peace plan is agreed in October.

## Body

### History and Conflicts
Historically part of Palestine, Gaza was administered by Egypt after the 1948 war. Israel occupied it in 1967, establishing settlements and exercising military control. The First Intifada (1987) began in Gaza. The Oslo Accords (1994) created the Palestinian Authority, but political division led to Hamas seizing control of Gaza in 2007. This triggered a blockade by Israel and Egypt. Multiple major wars between Israel and Hamas occurred in 2008-2009, 2014, and from 2023 onwards. The 2023 war has caused massive destruction, displacement, and a humanitarian crisis, with scholars and rights groups alleging genocide. A peace plan was agreed in October 2025.

### Political Status and Governance
The UN and most countries consider Gaza occupied by Israel due to its control over borders, airspace, and utilities, a view contested by Israel. Hamas governed Gaza from 2007 until it dissolved its administration in July 2026 as part of a peace deal. The territory is now under a transitional administration per UN Security Council Resolution 2803 (2025). Gaza is divided into five governorates.

### Blockade and Economy
Since 2007, Israel and Egypt have maintained a blockade, severely restricting the movement of people and goods. This has crippled the economy, leading to extremely high unemployment (46% overall, 70% youth) and poverty, with over 75% of the population dependent on humanitarian aid. The economy is characterized by "de-development," with limited capacity for production or innovation. Key industries are small-scale textiles and agriculture (citrus, olives).

### Demographics and Society
Gaza is one of the world's most densely populated areas, with a population of about 2.1 million (2024 estimate). It has a very young population (43.5% are children 14 or younger). The population is predominantly Sunni Muslim, with a small Christian minority. Literacy rates are high (97%), but health and living conditions are dire, exacerbated by conflict and blockade.

## Terms
- **Green Line**: The 1949 armistice line separating Israel from the territories it occupied in the 1948 war, including Gaza.
- **Intifada**: A Palestinian uprising against Israeli occupation; the First (1987) and Second (2000) were major periods of conflict.
- **Oslo Accords**: A series of agreements (1990s) between Israel and the PLO that created the Palestinian Authority for limited self-rule.
- **Blockade**: The restriction by Israel and Egypt on the movement of goods and people into and out of Gaza since 2007.
- **De-development**: A term describing the deliberate undermining of an economy's capacity for self-sustaining growth.
- **Buffer Zone**: An area along Gaza's borders where access is restricted by Israel for security reasons, reducing usable land.
- **UNRWA**: The United Nations Relief and Works Agency for Palestine Refugees in the Near East, which provides aid to Palestinian refugees.
- **Philadelphi Route**: A narrow buffer zone along the Gaza-Egypt border, controlled by Israel until 2005.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Occupation Status**: The core legal debate is whether Gaza remains occupied by Israel after its 2005 withdrawal. The UN, EU, and most legal scholars say yes, citing Israeli control over key aspects of life. Israel and some legal commentators argue it does not.
- **Genocide Allegations**: There is significant scholarly and international debate over whether Israel's actions in the 2023 Gaza war constitute genocide. Multiple human rights organizations and UN bodies have concluded there are reasonable grounds to believe genocidal acts have occurred, while Israel and others dispute this.
- **Governance Future**: The long-term political future of Gaza is unresolved. The 2025 peace plan envisions a transitional international administration before a reformed Palestinian Authority takes over, but its acceptance and implementation are contested.

# Tallinn

## Overview
Tallinn is the capital and most populous city of Estonia, located on the Gulf of Finland. It is the country's primary governmental, financial, industrial, and cultural center. Historically known as Reval, the city's well-preserved medieval Old Town is a UNESCO World Heritage Site, and it has emerged as a major European hub for information technology and startups.

## Timeline
- **c. 3000 BCE** — Earliest archaeological traces of settlement (comb ceramic pottery).
- **c. 1050 AD** — A fortress is built on Toompea hill in central Tallinn.
- **1219** — Danish King Valdemar II conquers the area after the Battle of Lyndanisse.
- **1248** — The town receives Lübeck city rights from the Danish king.
- **1285** — Reval (Tallinn) becomes the northernmost member of the Hanseatic League.
- **1346** — Denmark sells Reval to the Teutonic Order.
- **1561** — Reval becomes a dominion of Sweden.
- **1710** — Tallinn capitulates to Tsardom of Russia during the Great Northern War.
- **1918** — The Estonian Declaration of Independence is proclaimed in Tallinn.
- **1940** — Estonia is annexed by the Soviet Union; Tallinn is occupied.
- **1944** — A devastating Soviet bombing raid kills 757 people.
- **1991** — Estonian independence is restored; Tallinn becomes its capital again.
- **1997** — Tallinn Old Town is designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

## Body

### History and Names
The area shows evidence of human settlement from c. 5,000 years ago. A fortress existed on Toompea hill by c. 1050 AD. The medieval indigenous inhabitants were Christianized during the 13th-century Northern Crusades. Denmark first claimed the area in 1219, leading to a period of Scandinavian and Teutonic rule. The city's strategic port made it a significant Hanseatic trade hub. Known internationally as Reval until the 20th century, the name Tallinn (possibly derived from "Taani-linna," meaning "Danish-castle") was adopted after Estonian independence in 1918. The city was part of Sweden from 1561, then the Russian Empire from 1710, before becoming the capital of independent Estonia in 1918. It was occupied by the Soviet Union (1940), Nazi Germany (1941–1944), and the Soviet Union again until 1991, when independence was restored.

### Geography and Climate
Tallinn is situated on the southern coast of the Gulf of Finland. Its largest lake, Lake Ülemiste, is the main source of drinking water. The city has a humid continental climate (Köppen Dfb) with cold, snowy winters and warm, rainy summers. The average temperature in February is −3.6 °C (25.5 °F), and in July it is 17.6 °C (63.7 °F). Annual precipitation is 700 mm (28 in).

### Government and Demographics
The city is governed by the Tallinn City Council. As of 2025, its population is 456,518. Ethnic Estonians make up over 53% of the population, a decrease from over 80% before World War II due to Soviet-era immigration. The official language is Estonian, though Russian is also widely spoken.

### Economy and Technology
Tallinn has a diversified economy, creating more than half of Estonia's GDP. It is a major European IT center, home to startups like Skype and Bolt, and hosts the NATO Cooperative Cyber Defence Centre of Excellence. The city is also a key financial and logistics hub, with one of the busiest cruise ports in the Baltic Sea. Tourism is significant, with 4.3 million annual visitors.

### Culture and Tourism
Tallinn's main attraction is its medieval Old Town, divided into the upper Toompea and the lower town. Key sites include Toompea Castle (housing the Parliament), the Town Hall square, and historic churches. The city hosted the 2011 European Capital of Culture. Cultural highlights include the Estonian Song Festival, a UNESCO-listed choral event, and the Tallinn Black Nights Film Festival. Traditional cuisine features marzipan and "vürtsikilu" (spiced sprat).

### Transport
Tallinn operates an extensive public transport system of buses, trams, and trolleybuses, which has been fare-free for registered residents since 2013. Lennart Meri Tallinn Airport is close to the city center. Ferry services connect Tallinn to Helsinki, Stockholm, and Mariehamn. Rail links connect to other Estonian cities and international destinations like Riga.

## Terms
- **Hanseatic League**: A medieval commercial and defensive confederation of merchant guilds and market towns in Northern Europe.
- **Northern Crusades**: A series of 12th–13th century military campaigns by German and Scandinavian forces to Christianize pagan peoples in the Baltic region.
- **Toompea**: The historic upper town of Tallinn, traditionally the seat of governing power.
- **Linnaosa**: An administrative district of Tallinn; the city is divided into eight.
- **Humid continental climate**: A climate characterized by large seasonal temperature differences, with warm to hot summers and cold winters.
- **UNESCO World Heritage Site**: A place listed by the United Nations as having special cultural or physical significance.
- **Vürtsikilu**: A traditional Estonian dish of salted sprats pickled with spices like black pepper and allspice.
- **Laulupidu**: The Estonian Song Festival, a massive choral event held every five years.

## Debates and Open questions
- The etymology of the name "Tallinn" is debated; while "Taani-linna" ("Danish-castle") is a common theory, other hypotheses exist.
- The connection between the 1154 Arab cartographer al-Idrisi's recorded place "Qlwn" and modern Tallinn is considered by some historians to be erroneous or speculative.
- The city's 2023 European Green Capital Award has been criticized, with detractors claiming the winning "15-minute city" concept was not implemented in the final program.

# Jordan

## Overview
Jordan, officially the Hashemite Kingdom of Jordan, is a semi-arid country in the Southern Levant of West Asia with a population of 11.5 million. Strategically located at the crossroads of Asia, Africa, and Europe, it is a constitutional monarchy with a pro-Western foreign policy. Jordan is noted for its relative regional stability, its role as a major refugee host, and a tourism sector centered on historical and natural sites.

## Timeline
- **c. 14,500 BC** — World's oldest evidence of bread-making found at a Natufian site.
- **840 BC** — Mesha Stele erected by the Moabite king, a key archaeological parallel to the Bible.
- **169 BC** — Nabataeans establish an independent kingdom centered at Petra.
- **106 AD** — Emperor Trajan annexes Nabataea; Romans form the Decapolis league.
- **636 AD** — Byzantines defeated by Muslims at the Battle of the Yarmuk, leading to Islamic rule.
- **1115–1187** — Crusaders establish the Lordship of Oultrejordain, building castles like Montreal.
- **1516** — Ottoman Empire conquers the region for 400 years.
- **1916–1918** — The Great Arab Revolt against Ottoman rule, led by Sharif Hussein and his sons.
- **1921** — Emirate of Transjordan established under British mandate with Abdullah as emir.
- **1946** — Jordan gains independence as the Hashemite Kingdom of Jordan.
- **1948** — Jordan captures and annexes the West Bank during the 1948 Palestine war.
- **1967** — Israel occupies the West Bank during the Six-Day War.
- **1988** — Jordan renounces its claim to the West Bank.
- **1994** — Israel–Jordan peace treaty is signed.
- **2005** — Al-Qaeda bombings in Amman kill 60 people.
- **2011–present** — Jordan absorbs over 1.4 million Syrian refugees, straining national resources.

## Body

### Ancient and Classical Periods
Inhabited since the Paleolithic, the area saw Iron Age kingdoms of Ammon, Moab, and Edom. The Arab Nabataeans later established a wealthy kingdom controlling trade routes, with Petra as its capital. After Roman annexation in 106 AD, the region became part of the Decapolis league. Under Byzantine rule, Christianity spread.

### Islamic Era and Ottoman Rule
Muslim forces defeated the Byzantines in 636 AD, incorporating Transjordan into the Islamic caliphates. The region saw Umayyad desert castles and became a pilgrimage route. After periods under Crusaders, Ayyubids, and Mamluks, the Ottoman Empire conquered the area in 1516. Ottoman rule was largely neglectful, leading to tribal anarchy until the 19th century.

### Modern Statehood and Conflicts
Following the 1916 Arab Revolt, Britain established the Emirate of Transjordan in 1921. Jordan gained independence in 1946. It annexed the West Bank in 1950 but lost it to Israel in 1967. Internal conflict with Palestinian militias culminated in "Black September" in 1970. Jordan renounced its West Bank claims in 1988 and signed a peace treaty with Israel in 1994. King Abdullah II, who ascended in 1999, has pursued reforms while navigating regional instability and the Syrian refugee influx.

### Geography and Climate
Jordan covers 89,342 km². Its western highlands drop into the Jordan Rift Valley, containing the Jordan River and the Dead Sea—the lowest point on Earth. The climate varies from Mediterranean in the northwest to arid desert in the east and south. The country is among the most water-scarce nations globally.

### Government and Politics
Jordan is a unitary semi-constitutional monarchy where the king retains wide executive and legislative powers. The bicameral parliament has an appointed Senate and an elected House of Representatives. Political parties are underdeveloped, with tribal identity strong. Freedom House ranks Jordan as "Not Free." It maintains a pro-Western foreign policy and is a key U.S. ally.

### Economy
Jordan is a lower-middle-income economy with a GDP of $39.453 billion (2016). It lacks significant natural resources, relying on services, tourism, and a skilled workforce. The economy has been hampered by regional turmoil, the Great Recession, and the cost of hosting refugees, which reached over $2.5 billion annually. Jordan is a regional leader in medical tourism.

### Demographics and Society
The population is 95% Sunni Muslim and about 4% Christian. Arabs constitute 98% of the population. Jordan has hosted large refugee populations for decades, including 2.1 million Palestinians and 1.4 million Syrians as of 2015. The official language is Arabic, but English is widely used. Literacy rates are high, and the health system is considered among the best in the region.

## Terms
- **Transjordan**: The historical name for the lands east of the Jordan River, constituting modern Jordan.
- **Decapolis**: A league of ten autonomous Greek cities in the Roman province of Syria, several in Transjordan.
- **Nabataeans**: An Arab people who established a wealthy kingdom controlling trade routes, with Petra as their capital.
- **Hashemite**: The royal dynasty of Jordan, descended from the Prophet Muhammad, which also briefly ruled Syria and Iraq.
- **Black September**: The 1970 conflict between the Jordanian monarchy and Palestinian militias, resulting in the expulsion of fighters to Lebanon.
- **Semi-constitutional monarchy**: A system where a monarch retains significant executive power, but a constitution and parliament exist.
- **Water scarcity**: A condition where freshwater availability is insufficient; Jordan faces "absolute water scarcity."
- **Oil shale**: A fine-grained sedimentary rock containing kerogen, from which liquid hydrocarbons can be extracted; Jordan has the world's fifth-largest reserves.
- **Remittances**: Money sent home by workers employed abroad, a major source of external funding for Jordan.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The origin of the name "Jordan" is debated, though it most plausibly derives from the Semitic word *Yarad* ("flow down").
- The role and future of Jordan's large refugee populations, particularly Palestinians and Syrians, in relation to national identity and resources.
- The balance of power between the monarchy and elected parliament, with ongoing efforts to transition to a more parliamentary system hampered by weak political parties.
- The long-term economic sustainability of Jordan given its lack of natural resources, high debt, and dependence on foreign aid and remittances.

# Ibadan

## Overview
Ibadan is the capital and most populous city of Oyo State, Nigeria. It is a major commercial, research, and educational hub in southwestern Nigeria, historically significant as a powerful 19th-century Yoruba military state and the administrative centre of the old Western Region.

- **1840** — Ibadan's armies decisively defeat the Sokoto Caliphate's expansion southward.
- **1853** — Reverend David and Anna Hinderer establish the first Western mission and school.
- **1893** — The Ibadan area becomes a British Protectorate via a treaty with the Baale.
- **1939** — Ibadan is chosen as the headquarters of the Western Provinces.
- **1948** — The University of Ibadan is established as a college of the University of London.

- **2016** — Ibadan joins the UNESCO Global Network of Learning Cities.
- **2021** — A new standard gauge railway connection to Lagos is completed.

## Body

### History and Founding
Ibadan was founded in 1829 during a period of turmoil in Yorubaland, which saw the decline of older cities like Old Oyo. It was established by Lagelu as a strategic military camp on a forested, hilly site. The city grew rapidly, becoming a dominant military, political, and economic power in the region by the late 19th century. It was administered as a military republic, unlike the hereditary kingship of most Yoruba cities. The British established a protectorate in 1893, and the city's central location led to its selection as the administrative centre for the Western Provinces in 1939.

### Geography and Climate
Located in southwestern Nigeria, Ibadan lies within the tropical forest zone. Its elevation ranges from 150 to 275 metres. The city is drained by five rivers, including the Ona and Ogunpa. It has a tropical wet and dry climate (Köppen Aw) with a wet season from March to October and a dry season from November to February. The mean annual rainfall is about 1,230 mm.

### Administration and Demographics
The Ibadan metropolitan area comprises 11 local government areas (LGAs): five urban and six semi-urban. The city's land area has expanded dramatically, from about 103.8 km² in 1952 to an estimated 400 km² by 2000. Principal inhabitants are Yoruba, with significant communities from other Nigerian ethnic groups.

### Economy and Infrastructure
Ibadan is a major trade centre for agricultural products like cocoa and cassava, supported by industries in agro-allied processing, textiles, and manufacturing. It is a key transport hub with an airport, major freeways, and a railway terminus on the Lagos-Kano line. A new standard gauge railway to Lagos began operations in 2021.

### Education and Research
The city hosts Nigeria's premier university, the University of Ibadan (established 1948), and the first teaching hospital, University College Hospital. It is also home to the International Institute of Tropical Agriculture (IITA) and several other national research institutes. The first Western school was started in 1853 by missionaries David and Anna Hinderer.

### Architecture and Landmarks
Notable landmarks include Cocoa House (Nigeria's first skyscraper), Mapo Hall, and Bower's Tower (1936). The city has the first television station in Africa (1959) and the Obafemi Awolowo Stadium, the first standard stadium in Nigeria.

## Terms
- **Military Republic**: A system of government where the warrior class held dominant political and economic power, as practiced in 19th-century Ibadan.
- **Baale**: A traditional title for a chief or ruler in some Yoruba cities, including Ibadan before it obtained a crown.
- **Köppen climate classification Aw**: A climate type characterized by a tropical wet and dry climate with a distinct dry season.
- **Local Government Area (LGA)**: The third tier of government in Nigeria, responsible for local administration.
- **Standard Gauge Railway**: A railway track with a standard distance between the rails (1,435 mm), as opposed to the older narrow gauge.
- **Derived Savanna**: A landscape resulting from the transformation of tropical forest by human activity, such as farming and burning.

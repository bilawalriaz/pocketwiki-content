# Recep Tayyip Erdoğan

## Overview
Recep Tayyip Erdoğan is a Turkish politician who has been the dominant figure in Turkish politics since 2002, serving as Prime Minister (2003–2014) and President (2014–present). His tenure is marked by significant economic growth and infrastructure development, but also by a pronounced shift towards authoritarianism, democratic backsliding, and the suppression of dissent. His leadership has fundamentally reshaped Turkey's political system, foreign policy, and societal norms.

## Timeline
- **26 Feb 1954** — Born in Beyoğlu, Istanbul.
- **1994–1998** — Serves as Mayor of Istanbul.
- **Dec 1997** — Convicted for inciting religious hatred; banned from politics.
- **2001** — Co-founds the Justice and Development Party (AKP).
- **2002** — AKP wins landslide election victory; Erdoğan becomes de facto leader.
- **Mar 2003** — Becomes Prime Minister after a by-election.
- **2014** — Elected Turkey's first directly elected President.
- **15 Jul 2016** — Survives a military coup attempt.
- **Apr 2017** — Constitutional referendum approves a shift to a presidential system.
- **2023** — Wins re-election in a runoff; oversees response to a devastating earthquake.

## Body

### Early Life and Political Rise
Erdoğan was born to a modest family and worked as a street vendor in his youth. He became active in Islamist politics, joining the Welfare Party (RP) in 1983. He was elected Mayor of Istanbul in 1994, where he focused on pragmatic infrastructure and service improvements. In 1998, he was imprisoned and banned from politics for reciting a poem deemed to incite religious hatred. While banned, he co-founded the AKP in 2001, strategically rebranding it as a conservative democratic party rather than an explicitly Islamist one, which proved electorally successful.

### Premiership (2003–2014)
The AKP won a landslide in 2002, and Erdoğan became Prime Minister in 2003 after a legal change ended his political ban. His early tenure was characterized by economic recovery from the 2001 crisis, the initiation of EU membership negotiations, and the reduction of the military's influence on politics. He won two more general elections in 2007 and 2011. Key domestic policies included a major infrastructure overhaul, education reforms, and the Health Transformation Program. In 2009, he launched a peace process with the Kurdistan Workers' Party (PKK), which collapsed in 2015. His government also faced the 2013 Gezi Park protests against perceived authoritarianism and a major corruption scandal.

### Presidency (2014–Present)
Erdoğan became President in 2014 and oversaw a 2017 constitutional referendum that abolished the parliamentary system, creating a powerful executive presidency. His rule has been marked by a severe crackdown on dissent, especially after the failed 2016 coup attempt, which led to a state of emergency, mass arrests, and purges of the judiciary, media, and civil service. Economically, his unorthodox policies of lowering interest rates despite high inflation have contributed to a currency crisis and soaring inflation. Foreign policy has been assertive, involving military interventions in Syria and Libya, mediating in the Ukraine conflict, and complex relations with the US, EU, and regional powers. In 2025, he oversaw the arrest of Istanbul's opposition mayor, Ekrem İmamoğlu.

### Ideology and Governance
Erdoğan's political style, sometimes termed "Erdoğanism," blends conservative democracy with a strong cult of personality and Ottoman revivalism. He has centralized power, curtailed judicial independence, and systematically suppressed media freedom and political opposition. His government has promoted socially conservative values, withdrawn from international treaties like the Istanbul Convention on violence against women, and taken a hardline stance against LGBTQ+ rights. His tenure has seen a significant erosion of democratic institutions and civil liberties.

## Terms
*   **AKP (Justice and Development Party):** The conservative political party co-founded by Erdoğan in 2001, which has governed Turkey since 2002.
*   **Erdoğanism:** A term describing the political ideology and system centered on Erdoğan's leadership, combining conservative democracy, economic liberalism, and an increasingly authoritarian, populist style.
*   **Presidential System:** The system of government adopted in Turkey via a 2017 referendum, which abolished the office of Prime Minister and concentrated executive power in the presidency.
*   **Democratic Backsliding:** The gradual deterioration of democratic norms and institutions, characterized by the weakening of checks and balances, suppression of opposition, and erosion of civil liberties.
*   **PKK (Kurdistan Workers' Party):** A militant organization designated as a terrorist group by Turkey, the US, and the EU, which has waged an insurgency against the Turkish state since 1984.
*   **Gülen Movement:** A social and religious movement led by the US-based cleric Fethullah Gülen, once allied with Erdoğan's AKP but later accused by the government of forming a "parallel state" and orchestrating the 2016 coup attempt.
*   **Ottomanism:** A cultural and political revival of Ottoman traditions and symbols, promoted by Erdoğan to emphasize Turkey's historical and regional influence.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Democratic Legitimacy vs. Authoritarianism:** Scholars and critics debate whether Erdoğan's consolidation of power represents a legitimate response to Turkey's instability or a definitive shift to authoritarian rule.
*   **Economic Policy:** There is intense debate over the causes and sustainability of Turkey's economic crisis, with critics blaming Erdoğan's unorthodox insistence on low interest rates and his interference with the Central Bank's independence.
*   **Role of Religion in Politics:** Erdoğan's promotion of conservative Islamic values in a historically secular state continues to fuel deep societal polarization and debate over the country's identity.
*   **Foreign Policy Direction:** Analysts question whether Turkey is strategically reorienting itself away from the West towards a more independent, multi-polar foreign policy, or if its actions are primarily transactional.

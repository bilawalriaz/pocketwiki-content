# Lake Titicaca

## Overview
Lake Titicaca is a large, high-altitude lake in the Andes on the border of Peru and Bolivia. It is the largest lake in South America by volume and surface area, and is famously known as the highest navigable lake in the world. The lake is ecologically significant, home to hundreds of species, and has been a center of human culture for millennia, from pre-Inca civilizations to the Inca Empire.

## Timeline
- **c. 27–20 million years ago** — Volcanic rocks accumulate in the Tinajani Basin, indicating its initial development.
- **18–14 million years ago** — Ancestral Lake Titicaca exists within the basin.
- **c. 370,000 BP** — Continuous sediment record from the lake bottom begins.
- **120,000–98,000 BP** — Lake Ballivián, a prehistoric high lake, exists.
- **18,100–14,100 BP** — Lake Tauca, another prehistoric high lake, exists.
- **c. 650–1000 AD** — Tiwanaku people build a major temple on Isla de la Luna.
- **15th–16th centuries** — The Inca use the lake as a pilgrimage site, with offerings at Khoa reef.
- **1862** — The iron-hulled ships SS Yavari and SS Yapura are built in the UK for the Peruvian Navy.
- **1931** — The SS Ollanta, then the lake's largest ship, is launched.
- **1998** — Lake Titicaca is designated a Ramsar Site.
- **2009** — Water level drops to its lowest since 1949.
- **2012** — The Global Nature Fund nominates the lake "Threatened Lake of the Year."

## Body

### Physical Characteristics and Hydrology
Located at 3,812 m (12,507 ft) elevation, the lake consists of two subbasins connected by the Strait of Tiquina. The larger, deeper subbasin is Lago Grande; the smaller, shallower one is Wiñaymarka (or Lago Pequeño). Five major rivers feed it, but its single outlet, the Río Desaguadero, accounts for only about 10% of water loss; the rest is lost to evapotranspiration. The water is slightly brackish. Since 2000, water levels have receded due to shortened rainy seasons and glacial melt, and pollution from growing cities is a major threat.

### Geology and Climate
The lake lies in the Tinajani Basin, a pull-apart basin formed by fault movement. Sediment cores show that for the last 370,000 years, the lake has been fresher and higher during glacial periods and lower during interglacial periods. Evidence exists for five major prehistoric lakes that occupied the basin during the Pliocene and Pleistocene. The climate is cold subtropical highland, with an average annual precipitation of 610 mm (24 in).

### Ecology
The lake hosts over 530 aquatic species, with about 90% of its fish species being endemic, including 23 species of *Orestias*. It is a critical habitat for water birds and contains unique species like the Titicaca water frog and the flightless Titicaca grebe. Introduced species like rainbow trout have caused extinctions, such as the Titicaca orestias. Aquatic vegetation like totora sedges and macrophytes are widespread.

### Human History and Islands
The lake's name likely derives from a corruption of the 15th- to 16th-century name for the Sacred Rock on Isla del Sol. The region was a major pilgrimage center for the Inca, who believed the sun god was born on Isla del Sol. Several islands are inhabited, including the Uros' human-made "Floating Islands" constructed from totora reeds. Other notable islands are Amantani, Taquile (known for its textile art), and Suriqui, where traditional reed boat construction survives.

### Transport and Archaeology
Historically, steamships built in the UK were transported in pieces and assembled on the lake. The largest vessel today is likely the train barge Manco Capac. Underwater excavations at Khoa reef have recovered thousands of Inca-era offerings, including ceramic feline incense burners and metal ornaments.

## Terms
- **Endorheic basin:** A closed drainage basin that retains water and allows no outflow to other external bodies of water.
- **Monomictic:** A lake that mixes from top to bottom only once per year.
- **Brackish:** Water that has more salinity than fresh water, but not as much as seawater.
- **Ramsar Site:** A wetland site designated to be of international importance under the Ramsar Convention.
- **Endemic:** A species native and restricted to a certain place.
- **Macrophytes:** Aquatic plants that are visible to the naked eye.
- **Totora:** A thick, buoyant sedge (*Schoenoplectus californicus*) used to build the Uros' floating islands.
- **Tiwanaku:** A pre-Inca civilization that flourished near Lake Titicaca from about 500 to 1000 AD.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The origin of the name "Titicaca" is debated, with theories linking it to Aymara words for "puma" or "lead," or to a corruption of the name of the Sacred Rock (*thakhsi cala*) on Isla del Sol.
- The age of the prehistoric Lake Mataro is uncertain and may date back to the Late Pliocene.
- The lake's biodiversity is under severe threat from pollution and invasive species, with its long-term ecological health a major concern.

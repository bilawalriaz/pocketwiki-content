# Khartoum

## Overview
Khartoum is the capital and largest city of Sudan, situated at the confluence of the White and Blue Niles. Its strategic location has made it a historical center for trade, administration, and conflict, shaping its development from an Egyptian outpost to a modern metropolis scarred by recent civil war.

## Timeline
- **1821** — Founded by Isma'il Kamil Pasha as an Egyptian army outpost.
- **1823** — Became the seat of the colonial government.
- **1884–1885** — Siege and capture by Mahdist forces; city largely destroyed.
- **1898** — Reoccupied by British forces; rebuilt as capital of Anglo-Egyptian Sudan.
- **1956** — Designated capital of independent Sudan.
- **1973** — Hostage crisis at the Saudi Embassy; three diplomats killed.
- **1991** — Osama bin Laden purchased property in the city.
- **1998** — U.S. cruise missile attack on the al-Shifa pharmaceutical factory.
- **2008** — Attack by the Justice and Equality Movement repelled by government forces.
- **2019** — Khartoum massacre during the Sudanese Revolution.
- **2023–2025** — Extensive combat between Sudanese Armed Forces and Rapid Support Forces (RSF).
- **2025** — Sudanese armed forces recaptured the city from the RSF.

## Body

### Founding and 19th-Century History
Khartoum was established in 1821 by Isma'il Kamil Pasha, son of Egypt's Muhammad Ali Pasha, 24 km north of the ancient city of Soba. It quickly grew into a regional trade center and a major hub for the slave trade. The city was largely destroyed by floods in 1854 and rebuilt. In 1884, Mahdist forces under Muhammad Ahmad besieged the city, capturing it in 1885 and massacring the Anglo-Egyptian garrison. The city was abandoned until its reconstruction began after the British reconquest in 1898.

### British Colonial Rule and Independence
Under Anglo-Egyptian administration, Khartoum was rebuilt with a street plan in the shape of the Union Jack. It developed rapidly with new infrastructure, including the Gordon Memorial College (now the University of Khartoum) and a railway to Port Sudan. The city became the capital of independent Sudan in 1956. Its population grew significantly due to rural-urban migration and, from the 1970s onward, an influx of refugees from neighboring conflicts.

### Modern Conflicts and Civil War
Khartoum has been a focal point for political violence. In 1973, Black September militants attacked the Saudi Embassy. In 1998, the U.S. bombed the al-Shifa factory, alleging it produced chemical weapons. The 2019 Khartoum massacre saw over 100 protesters killed by the RSF. Most recently, from April 2023 to March 2025, the city experienced devastating combat between the Sudanese Armed Forces and the RSF, causing widespread destruction before the army recaptured it.

### Geography and Climate
The city is located at the confluence of the White and Blue Niles, a point known as al-Mogran. It is relatively flat, at an elevation of 385 m (1,263 ft). Khartoum has a hot desert climate with extremely high temperatures year-round, averaging around 30°C (86°F). The rainy season is very brief, lasting about one month in August.

### Economy and Infrastructure
Khartoum is Sudan's primary economic and trade center. Its economy was bolstered by oil wealth until South Sudan's independence in 2011. Key industries include printing, glass manufacturing, food processing, and textiles. The city is served by Khartoum International Airport and is a hub for Sudan Railways, with lines connecting to Port Sudan and El Obeid. The Souq al Arabi is its largest open-air market.

### Education and Culture
Khartoum hosts Sudan's top educational institutions, including the University of Khartoum and Sudan University of Science and Technology. Cultural landmarks include the National Museum of Sudan, which houses ancient Egyptian temples relocated from Lake Nasser, and the National Botanical Garden. The city's architecture reflects its diverse history, blending Sudanese, Turkish, British, and modern styles.

## Terms
- **Confluence**: The junction of two rivers; here, the meeting point of the White and Blue Niles.
- **Mahdist War**: A late 19th-century conflict in which Mahdist forces overthrew Egyptian and later Anglo-Egyptian rule in Sudan.
- **Anglo-Egyptian Sudan**: A condominium (joint sovereignty) of the United Kingdom and Egypt that governed Sudan from 1899 to 1956.
- **Rapid Support Forces (RSF)**: A powerful paramilitary group in Sudan, originally formed from Janjaweed militias, which fought the Sudanese Armed Forces in the 2023–2025 civil war.
- **Souq**: An open-air market or bazaar, common in Arab cities.
- **Köppen BWh**: A climate classification for hot desert climates, characterized by very low precipitation and high temperatures.
- **Al-Mogran**: The Arabic name for the confluence of the White and Blue Niles.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The etymology of "Khartoum" is uncertain, with multiple scholarly theories proposing origins from Dinka, Arabic, Nubian, Beja, or Maasai languages.
- The death toll of the 2019 Khartoum massacre is disputed, with the government stating 61 were killed while other sources report over 100.

# Laos

## Overview
Laos is a landlocked country in Mainland Southeast Asia, the only one in the region. Its history is marked by the medieval Lan Xang kingdom, French colonization, a devastating civil war during the Vietnam War era, and its current status as a one-party communist state. The country's political and economic development remains closely tied to its powerful neighbors, particularly Vietnam and China.

## Timeline
- **14th century** — Prince Fa Ngum founds the kingdom of Lan Xang ("a million elephants").
- **1707** — Lan Xang splits into three kingdoms: Luang Prabang, Vientiane, and Champasak.
- **1893** — The three kingdoms are unified under French rule as part of French Indochina.
- **1945** — Laos gains brief independence during Japanese occupation before returning to French control.
- **1953** — Laos gains full independence as a constitutional monarchy under King Sisavang Vong.
- **1959-1975** — Civil war between the Royal Lao Government and the communist Pathet Lao.
- **1964-1973** — The United States drops over 2 million tons of bombs on Laos, making it the most heavily bombed country per capita in history.
- **1975** — The Pathet Lao overthrows the monarchy, establishing the Lao People's Democratic Republic.
- **1997** — Laos joins the Association of Southeast Asian Nations (ASEAN).
- **2016** — Laos accedes to the World Trade Organization (WTO).

## Body

### Etymology and Prehistory
The name "Laos" was introduced by French colonial administrators in 1893 to pluralize the name across the former kingdoms. The origin of the ethnonym "Lao" is debated, with theories linking it to the Pali word for bottle gourd, Tai-Lao migration legends, or an Austroasiatic root for "person." Archaeological evidence shows modern human presence in the area for at least 46,000 years, with agricultural societies developing by the 4th millennium BCE and bronze artifacts appearing around 1500 BCE.

### Lan Xang and Colonial Era
The Lan Xang kingdom, founded in the 14th century, became a regional trade hub. After splitting in 1707, the successor kingdoms were gradually brought under French control by 1893. French rule saw Vietnamese migration to Laos and the development of a small export economy. During World War II, Laos was occupied by Japan before briefly declaring independence. The First Indochina War led to French withdrawal, and Laos gained full independence in 1953.

### Civil War and Communist Rule
A civil war erupted in 1959 between the U.S.-backed Royal Lao Government and the communist Pathet Lao, which was supported by North Vietnam and the Soviet Union. The conflict was intensified by the Vietnam War, with North Vietnam using Laotian territory for the Ho Chi Minh Trail supply route. In response, the U.S. conducted a massive aerial bombing campaign. The Pathet Lao won in 1975, abolished the monarchy, and established the Lao People's Democratic Republic, a one-party state led by the Lao People's Revolutionary Party (LPRP). A 1977 treaty formalized close ties with Vietnam.

### Politics and Government
The LPRP holds a legal monopoly on state power. The political system is structured around the principle of democratic centralism, with the party's Central Committee and Politburo as the highest decision-making bodies. The National Assembly is the supreme state organ, but candidates are vetted by the party. The president serves as head of state and LPRP general secretary. Human rights organizations have raised concerns about restrictions on freedoms of expression, assembly, and the press.

### Economy and Society
The economy is transitioning from subsistence agriculture, which still employs 80% of the population. Key sectors include mining, hydroelectric power (with significant exports to Thailand and Vietnam), and a growing tourism industry. Laos faces challenges with poverty and human development, ranking 139th on the Human Development Index in 2018. The population is ethnically diverse, with the lowland Lao Loum (over 50%), midland Lao Theung (30%), and highland Lao Soung (10%). Theravada Buddhism is the predominant religion (66%).

## Terms
- **Lan Xang**: The historical kingdom of "a million elephants" that existed from the 14th to 18th centuries.
- **Pathet Lao**: The communist political movement and organization that fought in the civil war and has ruled Laos since 1975.
- **Lao People's Revolutionary Party (LPRP)**: The founding and ruling communist party of Laos, holding a legal monopoly on state power.
- **Democratic Centralism**: The organizing principle of the LPRP, where decisions are binding on lower members once made by higher bodies.
- **Lao Loum**: The lowland-dwelling ethnic groups, primarily the ethnic Lao, who constitute over half the population.
- **Lao Theung**: The midland-dwelling ethnic groups, predominantly Mon-Khmer speakers like the Khmu.
- **Lao Soung**: The highland-dwelling ethnic groups, including the Hmong and Yao.
- **Ho Chi Minh Trail**: A network of supply routes used by North Vietnam during the Vietnam War, passing through Laos and Cambodia.
- **Unexploded Ordnance (UXO)**: Remnants of bombs and munitions, particularly from the U.S. bombing campaign, that continue to cause casualties in Laos.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The exact origin and meaning of the ethnonym "Lao" is a subject of linguistic and historical debate.
- The human rights situation in Laos, particularly regarding freedom of expression and enforced disappearances, is a point of contention between the government and international observers.
- The long-term economic and political impact of Laos's close ties with Vietnam and its growing investment relationship with China is a significant question for its future sovereignty and development.

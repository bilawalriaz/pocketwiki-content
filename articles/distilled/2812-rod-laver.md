# Rod Laver

## Overview
Rodney "Rocket" Laver is an Australian former professional tennis player, widely regarded as one of the greatest in history. His significance stems from his unparalleled achievement of winning the Grand Slam (all four major tournaments in a calendar year) twice—once as an amateur in 1962 and again in 1969, the latter being the only time a man has done so in the Open Era. His career bridged the amateur and professional eras, and he won a record 200 singles titles across all surfaces.

## Body

### Amateur Career (1956–1962)
Laver left school as a teenager to pursue tennis, coached by Charlie Hollis and later Harry Hopman. He won junior titles in 1957 and reached the Wimbledon singles final in 1959. His first major singles title was the 1960 Australian Championships. In 1962, he became the first man since Don Budge in 1938 to win all four Grand Slam singles titles in one year, a feat he accomplished by winning 22 titles that season. He was ranked the world No. 1 amateur for both 1961 and 1962.

### Professional Career: Pre-Open Era (1963–1968)
After turning professional in December 1962, Laver was initially outmatched by established pros Ken Rosewall and Lew Hoad. By 1964, he had risen to challenge Rosewall, winning the prestigious Wembley Championships. From 1965 to 1969, he was ranked indisputably as the world No. 1 professional. In 1967, he achieved a "Pro Slam" by winning all three professional majors (Wimbledon Pro, US Pro, and French Pro). During this period, professionals were banned from the amateur Grand Slam tournaments.

### Professional Career: Open Era (1968–1976)
The Open Era began in 1968, allowing pros to compete in Grand Slams. Laver won the first Open Era Wimbledon in 1968. In 1969, he completed his second Grand Slam, winning all four majors against fields that included the world's best amateurs and professionals. He won a record 18 singles titles that year. In the early 1970s, he remained a top player on the WCT circuit, becoming the first player to earn over $1 million in career prize money in 1971. He continued to win titles until semi-retiring from the main tour in 1976.

### Playing Style and Rivalries
Laver was a technically complete left-handed serve-and-volley player with aggressive, topspin groundstrokes. He was known for his speed, wrist strength, and ability to adapt his game to all surfaces. His primary rival was Ken Rosewall, with whom he played at least 164 professional matches, leading 89–75 in documented results. He also held significant head-to-head leads over Pancho Gonzales (43–22), Roy Emerson (49–18), and Arthur Ashe (21–3).

### Legacy
Laver is frequently cited by experts and former champions as the greatest tennis player of all time. He is the only player, male or female, to have won titles on all court surfaces of his time (grass, clay, hard, carpet, wood). He contributed to five Davis Cup titles for Australia. His legacy is commemorated by the Rod Laver Arena at the Australian Open and the Laver Cup team tournament.

## Terms
- **Grand Slam**: Winning all four major tennis tournaments (Australian, French, Wimbledon, US) in a single calendar year.
- **Pro Slam**: Winning all three major professional tournaments in one year during the pre-Open Era.
- **Open Era**: The period in tennis history beginning in 1968 when professional players were allowed to compete in amateur Grand Slam tournaments.
- **Serve-and-volley**: A playing style where a player serves and immediately moves to the net to volley the return.
- **Topspin**: A type of spin applied to a tennis ball, causing it to dip and bounce higher.
- **Davis Cup**: The premier international team event in men's tennis.
- **WCT (World Championship Tennis)**: A professional tennis circuit and governing body founded in 1968.
- **NTL (National Tennis League)**: A rival professional tennis circuit to WCT in the early Open Era.

## Debates and open questions
- **World No. 1 in 1970**: There was no clear-cut world No. 1 in 1970. Different journalists and panels ranked Ken Rosewall, John Newcombe, or Laver as the top player, with Laver winning the most titles and head-to-head records against the others.
- **Greatest of all time**: While many experts rank Laver as the greatest player ever, others, including tennis promoter Jack Kramer, have placed him outside their top six. The debate continues, with modern comparisons often involving players like Roger Federer.

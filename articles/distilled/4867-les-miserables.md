# Les Misérables

## Overview
*Les Misérables* is a French epic historical novel by Victor Hugo, first published in 1862. It is considered one of the greatest novels of the 19th century, exploring themes of justice, mercy, redemption, and social struggle through the lives of several characters in early 19th-century France. Its enduring popularity stems from its profound moral questions and has led to numerous adaptations, most famously the stage musical.

## Timeline
- **1815** — Jean Valjean is released from prison after 19 years; encounters Bishop Myriel.
- **c. 1818** — Valjean, as Monsieur Madeleine, becomes a wealthy factory owner and mayor of Montreuil-sur-Mer.
- **c. 1818** — Fantine is fired from Valjean's factory, falls into poverty and prostitution, and dies.
- **1818** — Valjean rescues Cosette from the Thénardiers and flees to Paris.
- **1823** — Valjean and Cosette hide in the Petit-Picpus convent.
- **1832** — Marius Pontmercy falls in love with Cosette; the Friends of the ABC plan a revolt.
- **June 5-6, 1832** — The June Rebellion in Paris; the barricade is erected and falls.
- **1832** — Éponine dies at the barricade; Valjean saves Marius and escapes through the sewers.
- **1832** — Javert commits suicide after Valjean spares his life.
- **1833** — Marius and Cosette marry; Valjean confesses his past and is reconciled with them before his death.

## Body

### Hugo's Sources and Composition
Hugo drew inspiration from real-life incidents and people. He witnessed a man arrested for stealing bread in 1829, which inspired Jean Valjean, and a mother and daughter watching, who became Fantine and Cosette. The character of Valjean is loosely based on the ex-convict Eugène François Vidocq, who later founded a detective agency. Hugo began writing the novel in 1845 after extensive research, including visiting the Bagne of Toulon prison in 1839 and the Waterloo battlefield in 1861. He incorporated personal experiences, such as saving a prostitute from arrest in 1841 and witnessing a bread thief's arrest in 1846.

### Novel Form and Structure
The novel is divided into five volumes, totaling 365 chapters. It is one of the longest ever written, with over 655,000 words in the original French. Hugo's stated purpose was to address social problems like poverty, injustice, and ignorance. A significant portion of the text consists of digressions on topics such as the Paris sewers, convents, and the Battle of Waterloo, which Hugo saw as a pivotal historical moment that shifted revolutionary struggle from military to intellectual forces.

### Major Characters and Plot
The central narrative follows Jean Valjean, an ex-convict seeking redemption after a bishop shows him mercy. He assumes a new identity as Monsieur Madeleine but is relentlessly pursued by the rigid police inspector Javert. Valjean rescues the abused orphan Cosette from the corrupt Thénardiers and raises her. Years later, Cosette falls in love with the student revolutionary Marius Pontmercy. The plot culminates during the 1832 June Rebellion, where Valjean saves Marius's life at a barricade, spares Javert, and carries Marius through the Paris sewers. Javert, unable to reconcile his duty to the law with Valjean's mercy, commits suicide. Valjean eventually reconciles with Marius and Cosette before dying.

### Contemporary Reception and Legacy
The novel was a massive commercial success upon its 1862 publication, translated into multiple languages immediately. Critical reception was mixed; some praised its focus on social problems, while others like Flaubert and Baudelaire criticized it as sentimental, artificial, or propagandistic. Its enduring legacy is cemented by countless adaptations, including the globally successful 1980 musical and the 2012 film adaptation.

## Terms
*   **Bagne:** A French penal colony or prison, like the one in Toulon where Valjean was held.
*   **Grisette:** A young, working-class woman, often a seamstress, in 19th-century Paris.
*   **Friends of the ABC (Les Amis de l'ABC):** A fictional club of republican student revolutionaries. The name is a pun, as "ABC" is pronounced like "abaissés" (the abased or downtrodden) in French.
*   **June Rebellion (1832):** An anti-monarchist insurrection in Paris, the historical setting for the novel's climactic barricade scenes.
*   **Patron-Minette:** A quartet of dangerous criminals who assist the Thénardiers.
*   **Yellow Passport:** A document issued to released convicts in France, marking them as ex-prisoners and leading to social ostracism.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   The novel's extensive digressions, such as the lengthy essay on the Battle of Waterloo, are a point of critical debate. Some view them as essential to Hugo's moral and historical vision, while others see them as disruptive to the narrative.
*   The character of Javert and his suicide are analyzed as a profound exploration of the conflict between rigid legalism and moral mercy.
*   The political sympathy for the revolutionaries of 1832 was controversial upon publication and remains a subject of discussion regarding Hugo's intent and the novel's message.

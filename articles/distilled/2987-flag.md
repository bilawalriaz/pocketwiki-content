# Flag

## Overview
A flag is a piece of fabric, typically rectangular, with a distinctive design used for identification, signalling, or decoration. The study of flags is called vexillology. Flags originated for military coordination and have evolved into powerful symbols of national identity, used in maritime signalling, sports, and political expression.

## Timeline
- **c. 3500 BC** — Ships with vexilloids (flag-like objects) depicted on predynastic Egyptian pottery.
- **c. 2400 BC** — Oldest discovered flag, a bronze Derafsh from Shahdad, Iran.
- **4th century** — Roman Emperor Constantine adopts the Christian labarum, a version of the vexillum.
- **1478** — The Dannebrog, the flag of Denmark, is attested; it is the oldest national flag still in use.
- **1572** — The Dutch tricolour first appears as a state flag (orange-white-blue).
- **1794** — The French tricolour flag is designed.
- **Early 17th century** — Ships begin routinely flying national flags, a practice that later becomes a legal requirement.
- **19th-20th centuries** — Political change and nationalism lead to the creation of many new national flags worldwide.

## Body

### History and Origins
The origin of the flag is unknown. Early "flag-like" objects (vexilloids) appear in ancient Egypt and Assyria for warfare. The oldest physical flag is a bronze Derafsh from Iran (c. 2400 BC). Cloth flags likely originated independently in the Indian subcontinent or China. The Roman *vexillum* was a cloth military flag that influenced the later European tradition. In medieval Europe, flags developed alongside heraldry, warfare, and maritime identification. Modern national flags emerged with early modern state formation and nationalism, particularly after 1789.

### National Flags and Their Influence
National flags are potent patriotic symbols. Several have inspired global "flag families":
*   **Denmark's Dannebrog** inspired the Nordic cross design.
*   **The Netherlands' tricolour** (red, white, blue) is the oldest and likely inspired the Russian flag and pan-Slavic colours.
*   **France's tricolour** inspired many revolutionary and national flags (e.g., Italy, Ireland).
*   **The United Kingdom's Union Flag** was the basis for flags of many former colonies (e.g., Australia, New Zealand).
*   **The United States' Stars and Stripes** was imitated by nations like Liberia and Chile.
*   **Ethiopia's flag** provided the pan-African colours for many post-colonial states.
*   **The Ottoman/Turkish flag** inspired the crescent symbol on many Muslim nations' flags.
*   **The Soviet flag** inspired communist state flags (e.g., China, Vietnam).

### Types and Uses of Flags
Flags serve specific functions beyond national symbolism:
*   **Maritime Flags:** An ensign flown at sea denotes a ship's nationality. A courtesy flag is flown when visiting a foreign port. International maritime signal flags form a communication system.
*   **War and Civil Flags:** Some nations have distinct war flags for their armed forces (e.g., UK, USSR). A civil flag is flown by civilians on non-government buildings.
*   **Sports Flags:** Used by officials (e.g., linesmen's flags in football, penalty flags in American football) and fans. The checkered flag signals the end of a motor race.
*   **Religious and Political Flags:** Represent faiths (e.g., Jain, Sikh flags) or movements (e.g., socialist red flags, anarchist black flags, the LGBT rainbow flag).
*   **Safety Flags:** Beach warning flags (e.g., red-yellow for safe swimming) and dive flags mark hazards or locations.

### Design and Physical Characteristics
Flags are usually rectangular but can be any practical shape (e.g., Nepal's double-pennant). Designs often derive from heraldry, using crosses, stripes, and divisions of the field. A flag's parts include the canton (upper inner section), field (main area), hoist (edge attached to the pole), and fly (opposite edge). Flags can be manufactured as "through and through" (reverse is a mirror image) or with a distinct reverse design (e.g., Paraguay). Vertical flags are common in central and eastern Europe.

## Terms
*   **Vexillology:** The study of flags.
*   **Vexilloid:** A flag-like object, such as a standard or banner, that is not made of fabric.
*   **Ensign:** A national flag flown at sea by a ship.
*   **Courtesy Flag:** A flag of a host nation flown by a visiting vessel.
*   **Canton:** The upper inner section of a flag, often a different colour or bearing a design.
*   **Field:** The main background colour or area of a flag.
*   **Hoist:** The edge of the flag attached to the flagpole.
*   **Fly:** The edge of the flag furthest from the flagpole.
*   **Semaphore:** A signalling system using two flags held in different positions to represent letters or numbers.

## Debates and Open Questions
The exact origin of the first flag remains unknown and debated. The development of cloth flags is attributed to either ancient India or China, with the possibility of independent invention or transmission via silk. The precise inspiration for some flag designs, like the Dutch tricolour's influence on Russia, is noted as probable but not definitively proven.

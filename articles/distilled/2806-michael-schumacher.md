# Michael Schumacher

## Overview
Michael Schumacher is a German former Formula One driver, widely regarded as one of the greatest in the sport's history. He won a record seven World Drivers' Championship titles and held numerous records upon his first retirement in 2006. His career, marked by exceptional skill, intense rivalries, and a transformative impact on Ferrari, ended with a skiing accident in 2013 that left him with severe injuries.

## Timeline
- **3 January 1969** — Born in Hürth, West Germany.
- **1987** — Wins European Kart Championship.
- **1991** — Makes Formula One debut with Jordan at the Belgian Grand Prix; signs with Benetton.
- **1992** — Achieces first F1 victory at the Belgian Grand Prix.
- **1994** — Wins first World Drivers' Championship with Benetton.
- **1995** — Wins second consecutive World Championship with Benetton.
- **1996** — Joins Ferrari.
- **2000** — Wins first World Championship with Ferrari, ending the team's 21-year title drought.
- **2002** — Wins fifth title, equaling Juan Manuel Fangio's record.
- **2003** — Wins sixth title, breaking Fangio's record.
- **2004** — Wins seventh title, setting a record of 13 wins in a season.
- **2006** — Announces retirement after finishing runner-up to Fernando Alonso.
- **2010** — Returns to F1 with the Mercedes team.
- **29 December 2013** — Suffers a traumatic brain injury in a skiing accident in the French Alps.

## Body

### Early Life and Career
Born into a working-class family, Schumacher began karting at age four. His talent was evident early, winning the German Junior Kart Championship in 1982 and the European Championship in 1987. He progressed through junior formulae, winning the German Formula Three Championship in 1990. An unusual move to the Mercedes-Benz junior programme in the World Sportscar Championship provided experience with professional racing and powerful cars before his F1 debut.

### Formula One Debut and Rise with Benetton (1991–1995)
Schumacher debuted in F1 with Jordan in 1991, impressing by qualifying seventh at Spa-Francorchamps. He was immediately signed by Benetton. He scored his first win in 1992 at the Belgian Grand Prix. His first championship in 1994 was controversial, decided by a collision with rival Damon Hill at the final race. He successfully defended his title in 1995, winning nine races and securing Benetton's first Constructors' Championship.

### Ferrari Era: Challenge and Dominance (1996–2006)
Schumacher joined Ferrari in 1996, a team in a long title drought. After challenging in 1997 (and being disqualified from the championship for a collision with Jacques Villeneuve) and 1998, he won his first Ferrari title in 2000, ending a 21-year wait. He then dominated, winning five consecutive championships from 2000 to 2004. The 2002 season was particularly dominant, with Schumacher winning 11 races and clinching the title with six races remaining. His seventh title in 2004 set a record of 13 wins in a season. Rule changes in 2005 disadvantaged Ferrari, and he finished third. In 2006, he fought closely with Fernando Alonso before announcing his retirement.

### Mercedes Comeback and Final Years (2010–2012)
Schumacher returned in 2010 with the new Mercedes team, reuniting with Ross Brawn. The comeback was challenging, with the car not initially competitive. He achieved his only podium of this period at the 2012 European Grand Prix. He retired for a second time at the end of 2012, having been credited with laying the groundwork for Mercedes's future success.

### Driver Profile and Legacy
Schumacher was renowned for his relentless work ethic, pioneering fitness regimen, and ability to push his car to the limit, especially in wet conditions. He was a masterful tactician who could galvanize a team. His win-at-all-costs mentality led to both triumphs and controversies. He is credited with popularizing F1 globally, especially in Germany, and transforming Ferrari into the sport's most dominant team. He held numerous records, including most wins (91), pole positions (68), and fastest laps (77), the last of which he still holds.

### Personal Life and Accident
Schumacher married Corinna Betsch in 1995; they have two children, Gina-Maria and Mick (who also became an F1 driver). He was known for his extensive philanthropy, donating over $65 million to various causes. On 29 December 2013, he suffered a severe traumatic brain injury while skiing in the French Alps. He was placed in a medically induced coma and has since received private rehabilitation at home. His family maintains strict privacy regarding his condition.

## Terms
*   **Formula One (F1):** The highest class of international auto racing for single-seater formula racing cars.
*   **World Drivers' Championship (WDC):** The premier competition in Formula One, awarded to the most successful driver of the season.
*   **Constructors' Championship:** Awarded to the most successful constructor (team) of the season.
*   **Pole Position:** The first starting position on the grid, earned by the fastest qualifier.
*   **Podium:** A top-three finish in a race.
*   **Team Orders:** Instructions from a team to its drivers to affect the race result, often to favor one driver's championship bid.
*   **Safety Car:** A vehicle deployed to temporarily neutralize a race after an accident or hazardous conditions, bunching up the field.
*   **Tifosi:** The passionate fans of the Ferrari Formula One team.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **The 1994 Championship:** The collision with Damon Hill in the final race remains debated. Public opinion was divided, with Schumacher vilified in the British media, though the stewards judged it a racing incident.
*   **The 1997 Disqualification:** Schumacher was disqualified from the entire 1997 championship after deliberately causing a collision with Jacques Villeneuve. He later admitted it was a mistake.
*   **Team Orders Controversy:** The 2002 Austrian Grand Prix, where Rubens Barrichello was ordered to let Schumacher win, sparked widespread criticism and led to a temporary ban on such orders.
*   **Legacy and "Greatest" Status:** While statistically one of the most successful, his legacy is sometimes debated due to the controversies. However, he is consistently ranked among the top drivers by mathematical models and is widely considered the greatest of his era.

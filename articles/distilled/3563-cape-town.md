# Cape Town

## Overview
Cape Town is South Africa's legislative capital and oldest city, renowned for its natural beauty, including Table Mountain and the Cape Floristic Region. It is the country's second-largest city by population and a major economic, cultural, and tourist hub, though it grapples with significant socioeconomic inequality and crime stemming from its apartheid history.

## Timeline
- **1488** — Bartolomeu Dias, the first European, reaches the area and names it the "Cape of Storms."
- **1652** — Jan van Riebeeck establishes a Dutch East India Company (VOC) way-station, founding the settlement.
- **1795** — Britain captures Cape Town; it is returned to the Dutch in 1803.
- **1806** — British forces re-occupy Cape Town after the Battle of Blaauwberg.
- **1814** — Cape Town is permanently ceded to the United Kingdom in the Anglo-Dutch Treaty.
- **1833** — Slavery is abolished in the colony, freeing over 5,500 slaves in the city.
- **1910** — The Union of South Africa is established; Cape Town becomes its legislative capital.
- **1948** — The National Party wins elections on an apartheid platform, leading to racial segregation.
- **1965** — District Six is declared a whites-only area, leading to the forced removal of over 60,000 residents.
- **1990** — Nelson Mandela delivers his first public speech after release from prison at Cape Town City Hall.
- **1994** — South Africa's first democratic election is held.
- **2015-2018** — Cape Town experiences a severe water shortage, narrowly avoiding "Day Zero."

## Body

### History
The earliest human occupation in the region dates to the mid-Holocene. European contact began with Portuguese explorers in the late 15th century. The city was founded in 1652 as a Dutch refreshment station, with enslaved people imported from Indonesia and Madagascar forming the basis of the modern Cape Coloured and Cape Malay communities. British colonial rule began in 1795, leading to the abolition of slavery and the expansion of the Cape Colony. Cape Town became the legislative capital of the Union of South Africa in 1910. The apartheid era (post-1948) enforced strict racial segregation, exemplified by the destruction of the multi-racial District Six. The city was a center of anti-apartheid resistance, with Robben Island serving as a political prison. Nelson Mandela's 1990 speech heralded the end of apartheid. Post-apartheid, the city has seen economic growth and good governance but continues to face deep-seated inequality and crime.

### Geography and Natural Environment
Cape Town is located at the convergence of the Atlantic Ocean and False Bay, dominated by the flat-topped Table Mountain. The city's geography includes the City Bowl, the Cape Flats, and the Cape Peninsula. It lies within the Cape Floristic Region, a UNESCO World Heritage Site and biodiversity hotspot with exceptionally high levels of endemic plant species. The climate is Mediterranean, with warm, dry summers and mild, wet winters. Sea surface temperatures vary dramatically between the cold Atlantic Seaboard and the warmer False Bay. Climate change projections suggest significant future impacts, including sea-level rise.

### Government and Economy
Cape Town is governed by a city council and is noted for its good governance, being the only South African metro to receive consecutive clean financial audits. Its economy is diverse, with major sectors including finance, tech, tourism, and retail. The city has South Africa's highest household incomes and lowest unemployment rate. However, it also has high inequality, with a Gini coefficient of 0.58. The property market is the country's strongest, with high prices and growth, particularly in affluent coastal suburbs.

### Demographics and Society
The 2011 census recorded a population of 3.74 million in the metropolitan municipality, with Afrikaans, Xhosa, and English as the main languages. The population is young and has grown through internal migration, a phenomenon termed "semigration." The city is religiously diverse, predominantly Christian, with a significant Muslim community. Crime is a major issue, with high murder rates concentrated in specific, impoverished areas like the Cape Flats, driven by gang violence and socioeconomic factors.

### Infrastructure and Services
Cape Town has a well-developed infrastructure, including an international airport, a major port, and extensive road networks. The city is investing heavily in public transport, including the MyCiTi bus rapid transit system. It has a more stable energy supply than the rest of the country, thanks to the Koeberg nuclear power station and initiatives to procure independent power. Water supply is managed through a system of dams, and the city is developing new sources like desalination following the 2017-2018 water crisis.

## Terms
- **Cape Dutch style:** An architectural style combining Dutch, German, French, and Indonesian traditions, visible in historic buildings.
- **Cape Floristic Region:** A UNESCO World Heritage Site and biodiversity hotspot containing thousands of endemic plant species.
- **Cape Flats:** A flat, sandy, low-lying area east of the Cape Peninsula, home to many townships and informal settlements.
- **Gini coefficient:** A measure of income inequality, where 0 represents perfect equality and 1 represents perfect inequality.
- **Group Areas Act:** A 1950 apartheid law that classified and segregated urban areas by race.
- **Khoikhoi:** Indigenous pastoralist clans who inhabited the Cape region before European colonization.
- **MyCiTi:** Cape Town's city-owned bus rapid transit (BRT) system.
- **Robben Island:** A UNESCO World Heritage Site in Table Bay, used as a political prison during apartheid.
- **Table Mountain:** A flat-topped mountain forming a dramatic backdrop to the city, part of a national park.
- **"Day Zero":** A term used during the 2017-2018 water crisis for the projected day municipal water supplies would be shut off.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The legacy and ongoing impact of apartheid-era spatial planning and forced removals on current inequality and segregation.
- The effectiveness and ethics of various policing strategies, including the use of advanced surveillance technology like drones and CCTV.
- The balance between promoting tourism and short-term rentals (like Airbnb) versus ensuring housing availability and affordability for residents.
- The long-term sustainability of the city's water supply and the necessity of expensive new infrastructure like desalination plants.
- The potential and implications of the small but growing secessionist movement advocating for "Cape Independence."

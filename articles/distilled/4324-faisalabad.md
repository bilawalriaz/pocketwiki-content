# Faisalabad

## Overview
Faisalabad, formerly Lyallpur, is Pakistan's third-most populous city and its primary industrial center, often called the "Manchester of Pakistan" for its dominant textile industry. Established in the 1890s as a planned British colonial city, its population surged after the 1947 partition of India, transforming it into a major economic hub that contributes over 20% to Pakistan's GDP.

## Timeline
- **1849** — British East India Company annexes Punjab after the Second Anglo-Sikh War.
- **1880s** — British Punjab government begins the Chenab Colony irrigation scheme; site for future city identified.
- **1890** — City of Lyallpur founded and named after Sir James Broadwood Lyall.
- **1895** — Railway link between Wazirabad and Lyallpur completed.
- **1904** — New Lyallpur District created in Multan Division.
- **1947** — Partition of British India; Lyallpur sees mass migration of Muslim refugees from East Punjab.
- **1979** — City renamed Faisalabad in honor of King Faisal of Saudi Arabia.
- **1982** — City district upgraded to a division.
- **2001** — Local Government Ordinance restructures city into a city district.
- **2005** — City reorganized into eight Tehsil Municipal Administrations (TMAs).
- **2017** — Census records city population at 3,210,158.
- **2023** — Census records city population at 3,691,999.

## Body

### Founding and Colonial Development
The region was originally inhabited by forest-dwelling tribes. In the 1880s, the British Punjab government launched the Chenab Colony, a major irrigation project to cultivate western Punjab. Popham Young, the colonisation officer, identified the site for the colony's headquarters. The city was founded in 1890 and designed with a central clock tower and eight radiating roads, forming a Union Jack pattern. It became a key agricultural and rail hub, with a railway link completed in 1895 and a new district created in 1904.

### Post-Partition Growth and Renaming
Following the 1947 partition, Lyallpur's population exploded as hundreds of thousands of Muslim refugees from East Punjab settled there. The city's population grew from 69,930 in 1941 to 179,000 in 1951. Industrial growth, particularly in textiles, accelerated in the 1960s. In 1979, the city was renamed Faisalabad. The 1980s brought foreign investment in textiles and increased remittances from residents working abroad, funding major infrastructure projects.

### Economy and Industry
Faisalabad is the center of Pakistan's textile industry, contributing to half of the country's total textile shipments. Its economy is driven by agriculture (cotton, rice, wheat) and industry, including textiles, chemicals, and food processing. The historic Clock Tower and its eight bazaars remain a major trading zone. The city has a major dry port, an international airport, and is a key node in the national road and rail network.

### Geography and Climate
The city lies in the flat plains of the Rachna Doab, between the Chenab and Ravi rivers, at an elevation of about 186 meters. It has a semi-arid to humid subtropical climate with hot summers and cool, foggy winters. The monsoon season (July-September) brings heavy rain and potential flooding, while the average annual rainfall is about 650 mm.

### Government and Administration
Faisalabad is a city district governed by seven departments. It is divided into eight Tehsil Municipal Administrations (TMAs), each with functions like land-use planning and municipal services. The Faisalabad Development Authority (FDA), established in 1976, regulates city development, building codes, and parks. The Water and Sanitation Agency (WASA) provides water and sewerage services.

### Demographics and Society
The population is overwhelmingly Muslim (97.22% in 1998) and Punjabi-speaking (90.86% in 2023). Social structures are traditionally joint-family based, with arranged marriages common, though modern influences are growing. Studies note persistent gender discrimination. Religious minorities, particularly Christians, face vulnerability. Literacy rates have improved, with the city holding its first literary festival in 2014.

### Culture and Recreation
Faisalabadi culture blends Punjabi, Mughlai, and Anglo-Indian influences, evident in its cuisine (e.g., halwa poori, tandoori barbecue). Traditional attire is the shalwar kameez, with Western styles also adopted. The city celebrates national and religious festivals like Eid and Basant (kite-flying). Key recreational sites include Jinnah Gardens (the oldest park), Gatwala Wildlife Park, and sports venues like Iqbal Stadium (cricket) and the Faisalabad Hockey Stadium.

### Education and Healthcare
Faisalabad is a regional hub for higher education, with institutions like the University of Agriculture (est. 1906), Government College University, and the National Textile University. Healthcare is provided by large public hospitals, including Allied Hospital and the Faisalabad Institute of Cardiology.

## Terms
- **Canal Colony:** A British colonial irrigation project to cultivate arid land by building canals, which spurred the founding of Lyallpur.
- **Rachna Doab:** The "doab" (land between two rivers) of the Chenab and Ravi rivers where Faisalabad is located.
- **Tehsil:** An administrative subdivision of a district in Pakistan.
- **Tehsil Municipal Administration (TMA):** A local government body responsible for municipal services within a tehsil or town.
- **Faisalabad Development Authority (FDA):** The statutory body responsible for planning and regulating the city's development.
- **Water and Sanitation Agency (WASA):** The agency under the FDA that manages water supply and sewerage services.
- **Union Council:** The smallest administrative unit in Pakistan, responsible for local governance and data collection.
- **Shalwar Kameez:** The traditional attire consisting of loose trousers (shalwar) and a long tunic (kameez).

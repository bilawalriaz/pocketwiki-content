# Israeli–Palestinian conflict

## Overview
The Israeli–Palestinian conflict is an ongoing struggle over land, self-determination, and security in the territory of the former British Mandate of Palestine. It began with the rise of political Zionism in the late 19th century and has evolved through wars, uprisings, and failed peace processes. The conflict's core issues include borders, the status of Jerusalem, Israeli settlements, security, and the right of return for Palestinian refugees.

## Timeline
- **1917** — British Balfour Declaration supports a "Jewish homeland" in Palestine.
- **1947** — UN General Assembly adopts Partition Plan (Resolution 181).
- **1948** — State of Israel is founded; 1948 Palestine war begins; Nakba occurs.
- **1967** — Six-Day War; Israel occupies West Bank, Gaza Strip, East Jerusalem.
- **1987** — First Intifada (Palestinian uprising) begins.
- **1993** — Oslo Accords signed; Palestinian Authority is created.
- **2000** — Camp David Summit fails; Second Intifada begins.
- **2005** — Israel disengages from Gaza.
- **2007** — Hamas takes control of Gaza; Israel imposes a blockade.
- **2023** — Hamas-led attack on Israel on 7 October; ensuing Gaza war begins.
- **2024** — International Court of Justice rules Israel's occupation is illegal.

## Body

### Origins and Early Conflict
The conflict originated in the late 19th century with the rise of Zionism, a movement to establish a Jewish state in Palestine. British support was secured via the 1917 Balfour Declaration. Following World War I, Britain established Mandatory Palestine. Increased Jewish immigration and land purchases heightened tensions with the Arab population. The 1936–1939 Arab revolt against British rule and Zionist settlement was suppressed, severely weakening Palestinian leadership. After World War II and the Holocaust, Britain referred the issue to the United Nations, which voted for a partition plan in 1947. The plan was rejected by Arab leaders, leading to civil war.

### 1948 War and Its Aftermath
The 1948 Palestine war followed the establishment of Israel. It resulted in Israeli control over most of the mandate territory and the displacement of over 700,000 Palestinians, an event Palestinians call the Nakba. The West Bank and Gaza Strip came under Jordanian and Egyptian control, respectively. No independent Palestinian state was formed. The Palestine Liberation Organization (PLO) was established in 1964 to pursue Palestinian national aims.

### 1967 War and Occupation
In the 1967 Six-Day War, Israel occupied the West Bank, Gaza Strip, and East Jerusalem. This began a prolonged military occupation, during which Israel constructed settlements in the occupied territories. The occupation is considered illegal under international law by the International Court of Justice and most of the international community. The PLO's base shifted from Jordan to Lebanon after 1970.

### Uprisings and Peace Processes
The First Intifada (1987–1993) was a Palestinian uprising against the occupation. It led to the Oslo peace process and the 1993 Oslo Accords, which created the Palestinian Authority (PA) and established a framework for negotiations. Key issues were deferred to "final status" talks. The process stalled after the assassination of Israeli PM Yitzhak Rabin in 1995 and the failure of the 2000 Camp David Summit. The Second Intifada (2000–2005) followed.

### Gaza, Hamas, and Recent Developments
In 2005, Israel withdrew settlers and soldiers from Gaza but maintained control over its borders, airspace, and coastline. In 2006, Hamas won Palestinian elections, leading to a split with the Fatah-led PA. Hamas seized full control of Gaza in 2007, prompting an Israeli-Egyptian blockade. Israel and Hamas have fought several wars since. The current war began after a Hamas-led attack on Israel on 7 October 2023, which killed nearly 1,200 people. Israel's subsequent military campaign in Gaza has caused widespread destruction, mass displacement, and a humanitarian crisis, with over 70,000 Palestinians reported killed. In 2024, the ICJ ruled Israel's occupation of Palestinian territories is illegal and must end.

## Terms
- **Zionism**: A nationalist movement among Jews that emerged in the late 19th century with the aim of re-establishing a Jewish homeland in Palestine.
- **Nakba**: Arabic for "catastrophe," referring to the displacement of Palestinians during the 1948 war.
- **Intifada**: Arabic for "shaking off," referring to Palestinian uprisings against Israeli occupation (First: 1987–1993; Second: 2000–2005).
- **PLO (Palestine Liberation Organization)**: Established in 1964, it is the recognized representative of the Palestinian people internationally.
- **Oslo Accords**: A set of agreements (1993, 1995) between Israel and the PLO that created the Palestinian Authority and a framework for peace talks.
- **Palestinian Authority (PA)**: An interim self-governance body established in 1994 following the Oslo Accords, administering parts of the West Bank and Gaza.
- **Hamas**: An Islamist militant group and political party that won the 2006 Palestinian elections and has controlled Gaza since 2007.
- **Two-state solution**: The proposed resolution to the conflict involving the creation of an independent State of Palestine alongside the State of Israel.
- **Right of return**: The principle, supported by Palestinians and UN Resolution 194, that Palestinian refugees and their descendants have a right to return to their homes in what is now Israel.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Causes of the 1948 Nakba**: Historians debate the relative impact of direct expulsions by Zionist forces versus flight due to the "flail of war" and Arab leaders' advice.
- **Apartheid designation**: Major human rights organizations (e.g., Human Rights Watch, Amnesty International) describe Israeli policies as apartheid. Israel and some others reject this characterization.
- **Nature of the Gaza blockade**: Israel frames its blockade as a security measure against a hostile entity. The UN and many NGOs consider it a form of collective punishment and part of an ongoing occupation.
- **Genocide allegations**: Some international law experts and human rights organizations have described Israel's actions in the 2023 Gaza war as genocide. Israel and its supporters strongly dispute this.
- **Viability of a two-state solution**: Public support for a two-state solution has declined among both Israelis and Palestinians. The expansion of Israeli settlements and the political split between Fatah and Hamas are major obstacles.

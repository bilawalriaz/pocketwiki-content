# Yunnan–Guizhou Plateau

## Overview
The Yunnan–Guizhou Plateau (Yungui Plateau) is a major highland region in southwest China, primarily spanning Yunnan and Guizhou provinces. It is a geographically and economically significant area, characterized by rugged karst topography, deep gorges, and a history of diverse ethnic habitation. The plateau acts as a physical barrier separating the Sichuan Basin from South China and is home to some of the world's most challenging engineering projects.

## Timeline
- **c. 3000 BC** — The region is home to many minority peoples who traditionally engage in intensive hill and valley agriculture.
- **Historical** — The plateau has long been considered a backwater region of China.
- **Modern** — The Yungui region becomes one of the most economically depressed areas of China, with Yunnan and Guizhou ranking in the bottom three for Human Development Index.
- **Modern** — Major cities like Kunming, Guiyang, and Zunyi develop on the plateau.
- **Modern** — Extreme engineering feats are undertaken to build railways and expressways across the terrain.
- **Modern** — The Beipanjiang Bridge, the world's highest bridge, is constructed on the Yunnan-Guizhou border.

## Body

### Geography and Definition
The Yungui Plateau is defined by the Red River Fault in the southwest and the Wuling Mountains in the northeast, encompassing most of eastern Yunnan and Guizhou. A broader definition includes parts of Sichuan, Chongqing, Hubei, Hunan, and Guangxi. The terrain is a mix of flatter highland areas in the southwest and mountainous, rolling hills with karst topography in the northeast. It is buttressed by major mountain ranges, including the Hengduan, Wumeng, Dalou, Wuling, and Ailao Mountains.

### Physical Features and Hydrology
The plateau is a source for major Asian rivers, which split around it; the Salween and Mekong flow south, while the Yangtze turns northeast. The western plateau is drained by the Nanpan and Beipan Rivers (headwaters of the Pearl River), and the eastern plateau by the Wu River (a Yangtze tributary). Significant lakes, such as Dian Chi, Fuxian Lake, and Erhai Lake, are found in the Yunnan portions.

### Climate and Ecology
The climate transitions from semi-arid in east-central Yunnan to humid subtropical in most of Guizhou. Vegetation reflects this, with subtropical evergreen forests dominating in Yunnan and mixed broadleaf forests in Guizhou.

### Human Geography
The plateau has historically been home to many minority peoples. Today, it is one of China's most economically depressed regions, with many residents living traditionally in rural villages. Despite this, it features major cities and has been the site of extreme engineering projects to overcome its challenging terrain.

## Terms
- **Karst topography**: A landscape formed from the dissolution of soluble rocks like limestone, characterized by sinkholes, caves, and steep peaks.
- **Human Development Index (HDI)**: A composite statistic of life expectancy, education, and per capita income indicators, used to rank regions by development.
- **Red River Fault**: A major geological fault line in Yunnan that defines the southwestern boundary of the plateau.
- **Hengduan Mountains**: A large mountain range in northwest China that buttresses the Yungui Plateau to the northwest.
- **Wuling Mountains**: A mountain range in the northeast that forms a transitional terrain between the plateau and the Yangtze Plain.
- **Nanpan and Beipan Rivers**: Headwaters of the Pearl River that drain the western Yungui Plateau.
- **Wu River**: A major tributary of the Yangtze River that drains the eastern Yungui Plateau.

## Debates and Open Questions
The source notes a common discrepancy in the plateau's definition: while the strict definition is geographically precise, the term "Yunnan–Guizhou Plateau" is often used more broadly to refer to surrounding highland areas that lack plateau-like characteristics.

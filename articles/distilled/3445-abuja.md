# Abuja

## Overview
Abuja is the purpose-built capital city of Nigeria, located at the country's geographic center within the Federal Capital Territory (FCT). It was designed to be a neutral, unifying seat of government, replacing the overcrowded commercial hub of Lagos, which it officially did on 12 December 1991. As Nigeria's administrative and political capital, it hosts key national institutions and is a major conference center on the African continent.

## Timeline
- **1970s** — Location for a new capital is designated in central Nigeria to signify neutrality and national unity.
- **4 February 1976** — Federal Military Government promulgates Decree No. 6, initiating the capital's move from Lagos to Abuja.
- **June 1977** — International Planning Associates (IPA) commissioned to create the Abuja Master Plan.
- **15 February 1979** — IPA submits the final master plan report.
- **1979** — Construction begins under the administration of President Shehu Shagari.
- **12 December 1991** — Abuja officially replaces Lagos as Nigeria's capital.
- **2003** — Demolition and eviction campaigns begin in some areas under FCT minister Nasir Ahmad el-Rufai.
- **2016** — Abuja joins the UNESCO Global Network of Learning Cities.
- **2018** — Abuja light rail opens, becoming the first rapid transit in Western Africa.
- **2020** — Light rail system shuts down due to the COVID-19 pandemic.
- **29 May 2024** — Light rail traffic resumes.

## Body

### Planning and Construction
The move to a central capital was driven by a need for ethnic and religious neutrality and to relieve pressure on Lagos. The Federal Military Government initiated the project in 1976. The city's master plan was created by a consortium led by International Planning Associates (IPA) and submitted in 1979. Detailed design of the central areas was later accomplished by Japanese architect Kenzo Tange. Construction began in 1979 but was delayed by economic and political instability, with initial stages not complete until the late 1980s. The move was controversial, notably opposed by politician Obafemi Awolowo.

### Geography and Climate
Abuja is defined by two prominent monoliths: Aso Rock, a 400-metre (1,300 ft) rock formation near which the Presidential Complex and other government buildings are located, and Zuma Rock, a 725-metre (2,379 ft) monolith north of the city. The city has a tropical wet and dry climate with a rainy season from April to October and a dry season. Daytime temperatures can reach 40 °C (104 °F) in the dry season. Studies indicate climate change effects, including rising average minimum temperatures and a downward trend in rainfall between 1986 and 2016.

### Administration and Layout
The city is administered by the Abuja Municipal Area Council (AMAC) under the Federal Capital Territory Administration (FCTA). For development, the city is divided into concentric "Phases." Phase 1 contains the core districts like the Central Business District, Maitama, Asokoro, Wuse, and Garki. The Central Business District is the principal business zone and houses the Three Arms Zone (executive, legislative, judicial offices). Asokoro is the most secure district, hosting the Presidential Palace (Aso Rock) and many embassies. The city has experienced rapid, unplanned growth, leading to the emergence of satellite towns like Karu, Lugbe, and Kuje.

### Demographics and Economy
At the 2006 census, Abuja's population was 776,298. The United Nations reported it grew by 139.7% between 2000 and 2010, making it the world's fastest-growing city at that time. As of 2015, it was experiencing annual growth of at least 35%. The urban agglomeration had an estimated population of 3,770,000 in 2022. The economy is driven by real estate, government services, and international conferences. It hosts the headquarters of ECOWAS and the regional headquarters of OPEC.

### Infrastructure and Landmarks
Abuja is served by Nnamdi Azikiwe International Airport and a network of federal highways. The Abuja light rail opened in 2018. Major landmarks include the Millennium Tower (construction halted in 2017), the National Mosque, the National Christian Centre, and the National Stadium. The city has several parks, including Millennium Park, which was opened in 2003.

## Terms
- **Federal Capital Territory (FCT):** The administrative territory in central Nigeria that contains the capital city, Abuja.
- **Purpose-built capital:** A city constructed from scratch to serve as a country's capital, as opposed to an existing city that was designated.
- **Monolith:** A large single, upright block of stone, such as Aso Rock and Zuma Rock.
- **Three Arms Zone:** The area in Abuja's Central District housing the administrative offices of the executive, legislative, and judicial branches of government.
- **Satellite towns:** Smaller urban areas that have developed around a major city due to rapid population growth and sprawl.
- **ECOWAS:** Economic Community of West African States, a regional political and economic union of 15 countries in West Africa, headquartered in Abuja.
- **Harmattan:** A dry and dusty wind that blows from the Sahara over West Africa, typically from November to March, causing haze.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The relocation of the capital from Lagos to Abuja was highly controversial, with significant opposition led by Yoruba politician Obafemi Awolowo.
- The city's rapid growth has led to challenges, including the spread of squatter settlements and large-scale evictions and demolitions, such as the campaign that began in 2003.
- The government's plans for newer districts like Maitama II, created in 2018 from Mpape Hills, are not fully clear, and the process involved revoking plots from previous allottees.

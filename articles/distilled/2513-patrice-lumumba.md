# Patrice Lumumba

## Overview
Patrice Lumumba was a Congolese politician and independence leader who became the first prime minister of the newly independent Democratic Republic of the Congo in 1960. His brief tenure was defined by the Congo Crisis—a period of intense political upheaval, secessionist rebellions, and foreign intervention. He is widely remembered as a martyr for anti-colonial liberation and pan-Africanism, whose assassination, with the involvement of Western powers, became a defining event of the Cold War era in Africa.

## Timeline
- **2 July 1925** — Born as Isaïe Tasumbu Tawosa in Onalua, Belgian Congo.
- **1958** — Co-founds and leads the Mouvement National Congolais (MNC).
- **May 1960** — MNC wins a plurality in national elections.
- **30 June 1960** — Congo gains independence; Lumumba delivers a famous, defiant speech.
- **5 July 1960** — Army mutiny begins the Congo Crisis.
- **11 July 1960** — Katanga secedes with Belgian support.
- **5 September 1960** — President Kasa-Vubu dismisses Lumumba from office.
- **14 September 1960** — Mobutu Sese Seko launches a coup, neutralizing the government.
- **1 December 1960** — Lumumba is captured while attempting to flee to Stanleyville.
- **17 January 1961** — Lumumba is executed in Katanga by a firing squad including Belgian mercenaries.
- **20 June 2022** — Belgium returns Lumumba's tooth to his family.

## Body

### Early Life and Political Rise
Lumumba was born into a farming family in the Kasaï province and was a member of the Tetela ethnic group. He was educated in Catholic and Protestant schools and worked as a postal clerk. Influenced by Enlightenment thinkers, he developed anti-imperialist views. After a brief imprisonment for embezzlement, he co-founded the MNC in 1958. Unlike other parties, the MNC was not based on a single ethnicity, promoting instead a platform of national unity, independence, and neutralism. His popularity grew after he represented the MNC at the 1958 All-African Peoples' Conference in Accra, where he aligned with pan-Africanist leader Kwame Nkrumah.

### Independence and the Congo Crisis
Following the MNC's election victory, complex negotiations led to Lumumba becoming prime minister in a coalition government. His government's goals were to improve Congolese quality of life through "Africanisation" of the administration and to unify the country. However, his tenure was immediately challenged by a mutiny in the army (Force Publique) and the secession of Katanga and South Kasai provinces, backed by Belgian interests and mining companies. Belgium intervened militarily, ostensibly to protect its citizens, which Lumumba opposed. He sought UN assistance to expel the Belgians and suppress the rebellions but grew frustrated with the UN's limited mandate. His appeal for Soviet aid, though framed as a request for technical assistance, alarmed Western powers during the Cold War.

### Downfall and Assassination
A political crisis erupted when President Kasa-Vubu dismissed Lumumba, a move Lumumba declared illegal. This stalemate was broken by a coup led by Colonel Mobutu Sese Seko on 14 September 1960, which neutralized both leaders. Lumumba was placed under house arrest. In late November, he attempted to escape to Stanleyville to join supporters but was captured on 1 December. He was transferred to Katanga, where, on 17 January 1961, he was tortured and executed by a firing squad commanded by Belgian contract officer Julien Gat and Katangan authorities. His body was subsequently dissolved in acid.

### Foreign Involvement and Legacy
Investigations, notably the 2001 Belgian parliamentary inquiry and the 1975 US Church Committee, revealed significant foreign involvement in Lumumba's downfall and death. Belgium, seeking to protect its economic interests, actively worked to remove him. The CIA, under the Eisenhower administration, plotted his assassination, viewing him as a potential communist ally of the Soviet Union, though later assessments found this threat was exaggerated. The UK also expressed a desire to see him removed. Lumumba's death cemented his status as a martyr for anti-colonialism. His legacy, known as "Lumumbisme," emphasizes nationalism, pan-Africanism, and non-alignment. He remains a potent symbol of Congolese national unity and the struggle for self-determination.

## Terms
- **Mouvement National Congolais (MNC):** The political party founded by Lumumba in 1958, which advocated for Congolese independence and national unity beyond ethnic lines.
- **Congo Crisis:** The period of political upheaval, secessionist conflicts, and foreign intervention that followed the Congo's independence in 1960.
- **Force Publique:** The Belgian colonial army in the Congo, which mutinied shortly after independence, triggering the crisis.
- **Africanisation:** Lumumba's policy goal of replacing European colonial administrators with Congolese nationals.
- **Katanga:** A mineral-rich province that seceded from the Congo in 1960 with Belgian support, leading to a major conflict.
- **ONUC (United Nations Operation in the Congo):** The UN peacekeeping mission deployed to the Congo in 1960 to help restore order and mediate the crisis.
- **Lumumbisme:** The political ideology associated with Lumumba, emphasizing Congolese nationalism, pan-Africanism, and non-alignment in foreign affairs.
- **Church Committee:** A US Senate committee that investigated CIA covert operations in the 1970s, revealing plots to assassinate Lumumba.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Degree of Western Responsibility:** While Belgium's "moral responsibility" is acknowledged, debates continue about the precise roles of the CIA, MI6, and the UN in Lumumba's capture and death. Some accounts suggest direct operational involvement, while official inquiries often stop short of ordering the execution.
- **Lumumba's Political Legacy:** Scholars debate whether Lumumba was a pragmatic nationalist or an uncompromising radical whose actions contributed to his downfall. His political program is often seen as less developed than his symbolic importance.
- **Impact of His Death:** Historians analyze whether Lumumba's assassination directly radicalized movements like the American civil rights movement and how his martyrdom shaped post-colonial African politics.
- **Congolese Perception:** While internationally revered, Lumumba's legacy within the Congo is complex. He was unpopular in some regions (e.g., Katanga, South Kasai) due to the army's actions there, and his image was later co-opted by leaders like Mobutu.

# Mount Vesuvius

## Overview
Mount Vesuvius is a somma–stratovolcano on the Gulf of Naples, Italy, famous for its catastrophic eruption in AD 79 that buried the Roman cities of Pompeii and Herculaneum. It is considered one of the world's most dangerous volcanoes due to the large population living nearby, and it remains the only volcano on mainland Europe to have erupted in the last century.

## Timeline
- **c. 25,000 years ago** — Vesuvius begins forming in the Codola Plinian eruption.
- **c. 18,300 years ago** — Basal Pumice eruption (VEI 6) forms the original Somma caldera.
- **c. 3,800 years ago** — Avellino eruption (VEI 6) destroys Bronze Age settlements.
- **62 AD** — A major earthquake causes widespread damage around the Bay of Naples.
- **17 October 79 AD** — Eruption of Vesuvius begins, destroying Pompeii, Herculaneum, and other settlements.
- **1631** — Major eruption buries villages, kills ~3,000 people, and begins a new phase of frequent activity.
- **5 April 1906** — Eruption kills over 100 people and ejects the most lava ever recorded from Vesuvius.
- **18–24 March 1944** — Last major eruption destroys several villages and damages Allied aircraft.
- **5 June 1995** — Area around Vesuvius is declared a national park.

## Body

### Formation and Topography
Vesuvius is a stratovolcano formed by the subduction of the African tectonic plate beneath the Eurasian plate. It is part of the Campanian volcanic arc. The volcano's distinctive "humpbacked" profile consists of a large cone (Gran Cono) partially encircled by the steep rim of a summit caldera. This caldera, named Monte Somma, resulted from the collapse of an earlier, higher structure. The term "somma volcano" now describes any volcano with a summit caldera surrounding a newer cone.

### Eruption History Before AD 79
Vesuvius has a long history of explosive activity. The Avellino eruption around 1800 BC (c. 3,800 years ago) was a major Plinian event that buried Bronze Age settlements. After this, the volcano entered a period of less violent eruptions until a long period of quiescence. Roman writers like Strabo and Vitruvius described the mountain as having a volcanic nature, with fertile slopes possibly due to past activity, though it was covered in gardens and vineyards before the AD 79 eruption.

### The Eruption of AD 79
The eruption, which began after 17 October AD 79, was a multi-phase Plinian event. It ejected a column of ash and pumice 33 km high, followed by the collapse of this column into devastating pyroclastic surges. The cities of Pompeii and Herculaneum were destroyed; Pompeii was buried by ashfall and surges, while Herculaneum was buried by 23 meters of material from pyroclastic flows. The only surviving eyewitness account is from Pliny the Younger, who described the death of his uncle, Pliny the Elder, while attempting a rescue. Over 1,000 people are thought to have died, with around 1,044 body casts found at Pompeii and 332 remains at Herculaneum.

### Later Eruptions and Modern Activity
Vesuvius has erupted dozens of times since AD 79. A major eruption in 1631 killed about 3,000 people and initiated a period of near-continuous activity. The last major eruption occurred in March 1944. The volcano is still active, producing sulfur-rich steam, and is closely monitored. It is classified as a Decade Volcano due to the risk it poses; about 3,000,000 people live nearby, with 600,000 in the danger zone. Emergency plans assume a worst-case eruption similar to 1631 (VEI 4) and foresee the evacuation of 600,000 people from the "red zone."

## Terms
- **Somma–stratovolcano**: A volcano with a central cone (stratovolcano) partially surrounded by the rim of an older, collapsed caldera.
- **Caldera**: A large, basin-shaped depression formed by the collapse of a volcano after a major eruption.
- **Plinian eruption**: A highly explosive volcanic eruption characterized by a sustained column of gas and ash, named after Pliny the Younger's description of AD 79.
- **Pyroclastic surge**: A fast-moving, ground-hugging current of hot gas and volcanic debris that is less dense than a flow.
- **Tephra**: Fragmented material ejected into the air during a volcanic eruption, including ash, pumice, and rocks.
- **Decade Volcanoes**: A list of 16 volcanoes identified by the IAVCEI as worthy of particular study due to their history of large, destructive eruptions and proximity to populated areas.
- **Volcanic Explosivity Index (VEI)**: A scale (0-8) that measures the explosiveness of volcanic eruptions based on volume of ejected material and eruption column height.
- **Zona rossa ("red zone")**: The area at greatest risk from pyroclastic flows during an eruption, for which evacuation is planned.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The exact death toll of the AD 79 eruption remains unknown.
- The precise date of the AD 79 eruption is debated, though evidence points to after 17 October.
- The cause of Pliny the Elder's death is uncertain; while traditionally attributed to toxic gases, it may have been a stroke or heart attack.
- The timing and triggers for Vesuvius's next major eruption are subjects of ongoing monitoring and scientific modeling.

# Jimi Hendrix

## Overview
James Marshall "Jimi" Hendrix (1942–1970) was an American guitarist, singer, and songwriter widely regarded as the greatest instrumentalist in rock history. In a brief four-year mainstream career, he revolutionized the electric guitar by transforming feedback, distortion, and effects pedals—previously considered nuisances—into a controlled, fluid vocabulary. He synthesized blues, R&B, soul, and psychedelic rock, pioneering techniques like thumb-fretting, stereophonic phasing, and the integration of rhythm and lead playing that made a trio sound like an orchestra. His landmark albums *Are You Experienced* (1967), *Axis: Bold as Love* (1967), and *Electric Ladyland* (1968) redefined studio production, while his performances at Monterey (1967) and Woodstock (1969) became cultural touchstones. He died at 27 from barbiturate-related asphyxia, leaving a legacy that shaped hard rock, funk, hip-hop, and modern guitar playing.

## Body

### Ancestry and Childhood
Hendrix was of African-American and Cherokee descent. His paternal grandparents, Bertran Philander Ross Hendrix and Zenora "Nora" Rose Moore, relocated to Vancouver, Canada, where their son James Allen "Al" Hendrix was born in 1919. Al met Lucille Jeter in Seattle in 1941; they married in March 1942 before Al deployed for WWII. Johnny Allen Hendrix was born November 27, 1942; Al, denied military furlough, was confined to stockade for two months to prevent him from going AWOL. After Al’s 1945 discharge, the family struggled with poverty and alcoholism; Hendrix witnessed domestic violence and hid in closets. His parents divorced in 1951; Al gained custody of Jimi and brother Leon. Three younger siblings were given up for adoption. Lucille died of cirrhosis in 1958; Al gave the boys whiskey instead of taking them to the funeral.

### First Instruments and Early Music
A social worker failed to secure school funding for a guitar; Hendrix played a one-string ukulele found in trash (1957), learning Elvis songs by ear. He acquired a $5 acoustic guitar at 15 (1958), studying blues artists Muddy Waters, B.B. King, Howlin' Wolf, and Robert Johnson. He formed the Velvetones, then the Rocking Kings. After his guitar was stolen, Al bought him a red Silvertone Danelectro. In 1959, he met Billy Davis of Hank Ballard & The Midnighters, who taught him licks and gave him a short gig.

### Military Service
Caught twice in stolen cars, Hendrix chose Army over prison (May 1961). At Fort Campbell, Kentucky, he neglected duties for guitar, leading to peer abuse. He befriended bassist Billy Cox; they formed the Casuals. Hendrix earned his Screaming Eagles patch (Jan 1962) but was cited for napping on duty and poor marksmanship. His platoon sergeant recommended discharge: "Private Hendrix will never come up to the standards required of a soldier." He received a general discharge under honorable conditions June 29, 1962. Hendrix later claimed a medical discharge for a broken ankle; Army records show no such injury.

### Early Career and Sideman Years (1962–1966)
Hendrix and Cox formed the King Kasuals in Clarksville, TN, moving to Nashville’s Jefferson Street R&B scene. They toured the Chitlin' Circuit (TOBA), backing Wilson Pickett, Sam Cooke, Ike & Tina Turner, and Jackie Wilson. In 1964, Hendrix moved to Harlem, won the Apollo Theater amateur contest, and joined the Isley Brothers' backing band (I.B. Specials). He recorded "Testify" (March 1964) and played on Don Covay’s "Mercy Mercy" (#35 Billboard). He toured with Little Richard (Upsetters), recording "I Don't Know What You Got" (Feb 1965), and made his TV debut on *Night Train* (July 1965). Clashing with Richard, he was fired. He signed with Sue Records (Ed Chalpin) and joined Curtis Knight and the Squires (Oct 1965), earning first composer credits ("Hornets Nest", "Knock Yourself Out"). A conflicting three-year contract with Chalpin caused later legal disputes. In 1966, he formed Jimmy James and the Blue Flames in Greenwich Village, developing his style at Cafe Wha?.

### The Jimi Hendrix Experience: UK Breakthrough
Linda Keith (Keith Richards' girlfriend) saw Hendrix at Cafe Wha? and recommended him to Chas Chandler. Chandler brought Hendrix to London (Sep 24, 1966), signing him with Michael Jeffery. Chandler recruited Noel Redding (bass) and Mitch Mitchell (drums). At the London Polytechnic (Oct 1, 1966), Hendrix jammed with Cream; Eric Clapton later said, "He walked off, and my life was never the same again." The Experience debuted in France (Oct 13, 1966). In Munich (Nov 1966), Hendrix smashed a guitar for the first time; Chandler made it a permanent stage feature. A Bag O'Nails show (Nov 1966) stunned London’s elite (Clapton, Lennon, McCartney, Beck, Townshend, Jagger). "Hey Joe" hit UK #6 (Dec 1966), followed by "Purple Haze" (#3, Mar 1967) and "The Wind Cries Mary" (#6, May 1967). At the London Astoria (Mar 31, 1967), Hendrix set his guitar on fire, earning press labels "Black Elvis" and "Wild Man of Borneo."

### *Are You Experienced* (1967)
Recorded at De Lane Lea and Olympic Studios, the album blended blues ("Red House"), R&B ("Remember"), and experimental pieces ("Third Stone from the Sun," title track with backward tapes). Released UK May 12, 1967, it spent 33 weeks on charts, peaking at #2 behind *Sgt. Pepper*. At Saville Theatre (Jun 4), Hendrix opened with *Sgt. Pepper*'s title track, impressing McCartney. US release (Aug 23) reached #5 on Billboard 200. *Rolling Stone* later ranked it 15th greatest album ever.

### Monterey Pop Festival (June 18, 1967)
Recommended by McCartney, Hendrix closed the festival. Introduced by Brian Jones, he performed "Killing Floor," "Rock Me Baby," "Wild Thing," "Like a Rolling Stone," and originals. He destroyed his guitar and set it ablaze; photographer Ed Caraeff captured the iconic image. *Los Angeles Times* wrote he "graduated from rumor to legend." The D.A. Pennebaker film *Monterey Pop* brought him US fame. The Experience then outperformed Jefferson Airplane at the Fillmore and opened for the Monkees (left after six shows due to audience hostility).

### *Axis: Bold as Love* (1967)
The album opened with "EXP" (feedback/stereo panning) and featured the first recorded stereo phasing on the title track. Hendrix lost the side-one master tape in a taxi; he, Chandler, and Eddie Kramer remixed overnight, using a wrinkled backup tape for "If 6 Was 9" smoothed with an iron. Hendrix disliked the rushed remix. Cover art depicted the band as avatars of Vishnu; Hendrix objected: "I'm not that kind of Indian." UK release Dec 1, 1967 (#5); US Feb 1968 (#3).

### *Electric Ladyland* (1968)
Sessions began at Olympic (Dec 1967), moved to Record Plant NYC (Apr 1968). Chandler quit over Hendrix’s perfectionism, chaotic studio guests, and Redding’s unreliability (Redding formed Fat Mattress). Hendrix played many bass parts himself; album credited "produced and directed by Jimi Hendrix." Guests included Jack Casady, Steve Winwood ("Voodoo Chile"), B.B. King, Al Kooper. Released Oct 25, 1968: US #1 (2 weeks), UK #6. Included "All Along the Watchtower" (US #20, highest charting single) and "Burning of the Midnight Lamp" (first wah-wah recording). Critics call it his masterpiece and a contender for rock's greatest album.

### Break-up of the Experience (1969)
Jan 1969: On BBC's *Happening for Lulu*, the Experience played past their slot into "Sunshine of Your Love"; Hendrix allegedly banned from BBC. Feb 1969: Final European shows (Royal Albert Hall). Redding quit after Denver Pop Festival (Jun 29, 1969), citing Hendrix’s plan to expand the band without his input and learning of his replacement (Billy Cox) from a journalist. Hendrix moved to Ashokan House near Woodstock; Jeffery hoped for new material. Mitchell was often unavailable.

### Woodstock (August 18, 1969)
World’s highest-paid rock musician. Headlined with expanded lineup (Larry Lee, Juma Sultan, Jerry Velez) as "Gypsy Sun and Rainbows" / "Band of Gypsys." Played Monday 8:00 AM to ~30,000 remaining (peak 400,000). "Star-Spangled Banner" used feedback/distortion to mimic rockets/bombs; called "single greatest moment of the sixties" (Al Aronowitz). *Guitar World* (2011) named it greatest performance ever. He collapsed after encore "Hey Joe."

### Band of Gypsys (1969–1970)
Legal settlement with Ed Chalpin required an album. Hendrix formed all-black power trio with Billy Cox and Buddy Miles (ex-Wilson Pickett, Electric Flag). Four shows at Fillmore East (Dec 31, 1969 – Jan 1, 1970) yielded *Band of Gypsys* (Apr 1970, US/UK top 10). "Machine Gun" featured Uni-Vibe, Octavia, Fuzz Face to sonically depict warfare; Andy Aledort called it "pinnacle of Hendrix's career." Jan 28, 1970: Final show at Madison Square Garden (Winter Festival for Peace). Hendrix appeared drugged (Miles/Redding alleged Jeffery gave LSD), snapped at audience, played briefly, left. Jeffery fired Miles; Cox quit.

### Cry of Love Tour and Electric Lady Studios (1970)
Jeffery attempted to reunite original Experience; Hendrix refused Redding, keeping Cox. Lineup (Hendrix, Mitchell, Cox) toured as "Jimi Hendrix Experience" or "Jimi Hendrix." Cry of Love tour began L.A. Forum; 32 US dates. Second Atlanta Pop Festival (Jul 4) drew ~500,000 (largest US audience). NY Pop Festival (Jul 17) deemed a disaster due to Hendrix's drug intake. US leg ended Honolulu Aug 1, 1970 (final US show).

Electric Lady Studios: Hendrix and Jeffery bought Generation Club (1968), converted to studio designed by John Storyk (round windows, ambient lighting, $1M cost). First session Jun 15, 1970 ("Night Bird Flying"). Official opening Aug 25; Hendrix left for England next day, never returned.

### European Tour and Death
Sep 2, 1970: Abandoned Aarhus show after three songs ("I've been dead a long time"). Sep 6: Final concert at Isle of Fehmarn, West Germany; booed for prior cancellation. Sep 16: Last public appearance, jam at Ronnie Scott’s with Eric Burdon and War. Sep 17: Spent day with Monika Dannemann in London. Sep 18: Found unconscious; pronounced dead 12:45 PM at St Mary Abbots Hospital. Coroner Gavin Thurston ruled open verdict: asphyxia from vomit while intoxicated with barbiturates (9 Vesparax tablets, 18x dosage). Buried Oct 1 at Greenwood Cemetery, Renton, WA, near mother.

### Substance Abuse and Behavioral Problems
**Alcohol:** From 1962, caused "foolish" generosity and "fits of pique, even rare bursts of atypical, physical violence." **LSD/Other:** First use debated: Linda Keith (late 1966) vs. Monterey (June 1967). After 1967, regularly used cannabis, hashish, LSD, amphetamines. **Violence:** Drunken brawl in Gothenburg (Jan 1968) smashed window, injured hand, large fine. 1969: Accused friend Paul Caruso of burglary, assaulted him. Hit girlfriend Carmen Borrero with vodka bottle (stitches). **Possession:** Toronto Airport (May 3, 1969): heroin/hashish found. Acquitted Dec 1969; testified fan gave him vial. Mitchell/Redding claimed warned of bust, believed drugs planted.

### Posthumous Releases and Estate
Pre-Experience recordings (controlled by Ed Chalpin/PPX) released as hundreds of unauthorized "Jimi Hendrix" albums; Hendrix denounced them as "malicious." Unfinished fourth album split into *The Cry of Love* (1971, US #3), *Rainbow Bridge* (1971), *War Heroes* (1972). Reassembled as *First Rays of the New Rising Sun* (1997). Alan Douglas (1974) criticized for adding studio musicians. Al Hendrix regained control of song/image rights (1995) via Experience Hendrix LLC. Licensed to MCA (1995), then Sony Legacy (2010). *Valleys of Neptune* (2010). *Black Gold* demos unreleased.

### Equipment
**Guitars:** Primarily right-handed Fender Stratocasters played upside-down/restrung (first bought 1966). Bridge pickup slant reversed tonal brightness. Also used Fender Jazzmasters, Duosonics, Gibson Flying Vs (2), Les Paul, SGs (3), Gretsch Corvette, Jaguar. White SG Custom (*Dick Cavett*), black Flying V (Isle of Wight). **Amplifiers:** Fender Twin Reverb (85W, 1965–66). In UK, Chandler supplied Burns (30W); Hendrix switched to Marshall after meeting Jim Marshall via Mitch Mitchell. Bought 4 cabinets, 3x 100W Super Leads (Oct 11, 1966). Used all three at max ("Hendrix setting"). Purchased 50–100 Marshalls in four years. **Effects:** Wah-wah pedal (first heard on Cream's "Tales of Brave Ulysses", May 1967; met Frank Zappa Jul 1967). Signature riff on "Voodoo Child (Slight Return)." Dallas Arbiter Fuzz Face, Vox wah. Collaborated with Roger Mayer ("secret of my sound"): Octavia (octave doubler, first on "Purple Haze" solo, Dec 1966), Uni-Vibe (Leslie simulator, used at Woodstock, "Machine Gun"). Signal chain: Guitar → Wah → Fuzz Face → Uni-Vibe → Marshall.

### Influences
**Early Rock/Roll:** Elvis Presley, Little Richard, Chuck Berry. **Electric Blues:** Muddy Waters (first electric guitarist heard, "scared me to death"), Elmore James, B.B. King, Howlin' Wolf ("Killing Floor" opened Monterey set), Albert King (Cox: "very powerful influence"), Jimmy Reed. **Soul/R&B:** Curtis Mayfield (guitar style), Isley Brothers. **Songwriting:** Bob Dylan (played *Highway 61 Revisited*, *Blonde on Blonde* repeatedly; covered "All Along the Watchtower," "Like a Rolling Stone"). **Other:** Eddie Cochran, Bob Wills (western swing), Grand Ole Opry.

### Legacy
**Musical Innovation:** Transformed feedback/distortion into controlled vocabulary. Pioneered wah-wah, Octavia, Uni-Vibe, stereo phasing (first in rock recordings). Thumb-fretting low E string (piano-style) allowed sustained roots with melody. Combined rhythm/lead in trio format. Synthesized blues, R&B, soul, British rock, folk, jazz, psychedelia. **Influence:** Hard rock, heavy metal, funk, post-punk, grunge, hip-hop. Directly influenced: Prince, George Clinton, John Frusciante, Eddie Hazel, Ernie Isley, Stevie Ray Vaughan, Kurt Cobain, Jerry Cantrell, Mike McCready, Stone Gossard, Robert Smith, John McGeoch, Yngwie Malmsteen ("created modern electric playing"), Joe Satriani, Kirk Hammett, Brad Whitford, Richie Faulkner, Ace Frehley, Doug Pinnick, Adrian Belew, Miles Davis (compared to Coltrane), Mdou Moctar, Tinariwen. **Cultural:** Reclaimed rock's Black origins (Little Richard, Chuck Berry); role model for Black rockers. Soulquarians used Electric Lady Studios. **Recognition:** Rock and Roll Hall of Fame (1992), UK Music Hall of Fame (2005), Native American Music Hall of Fame (1998). *Rolling Stone*: #1 guitarist, #6 artist. 7 songs in 500 Greatest Songs; 3 in 100 Greatest Guitar Songs. 6 solos in *Guitar World* top 100. Grammy Lifetime Achievement (1992), Hall of Fame Grammys for albums/songs ("Purple Haze," "Star-Spangled Banner," "All Along the Watchtower"). Hollywood Walk of Fame (1991). Blue plaque at 23 Brook Street, London (now Handel Hendrix House). Jimi Hendrix Park (Seattle), historic marker (Atlanta Pop Festival). US Post Office renamed (Renton, 2019). Commemorative stamp (2014). Rhythm and Blues Hall of Fame (2016, 2019 for Band of Gypsys). West 8th St, NYC named Jimi Hendrix Way (2026).

## Terms
- ****Chitlin' Circuit**** — A network of venues (Theater Owners Booking Association/TOBA) throughout the South that hosted Black entertainers during segregation.
- ****Feedback (Guitar Amplifier)**** — A sustained, howling sound caused when amplified sound from a speaker re-enters the guitar pickups; Hendrix turned this undesirable noise into a musical vocabulary.
- ****Fuzz Distortion / Fuzz Face**** — An effects pedal that clips the guitar signal to create a thick, saturated, "broken" tone; a staple of Hendrix's sound (e.g., "Purple Haze" solo).
- ****Octavia**** — An octave-doubling effect pedal designed by Roger Mayer; mixes the original signal with a version one octave higher, creating a ringing, synthetic texture.

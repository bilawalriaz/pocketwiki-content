# Tokhtamysh

## Overview
Tokhtamysh (c. 1342–1406) was the Khan of the Golden Horde from 1380 to 1395. He is significant for briefly reunifying the divided Golden Horde, reasserting Mongol dominance over the Russian principalities, and then fatally provoking a war with his former protector, the Turco-Mongol conqueror Timur (Tamerlane), which led to the Horde's devastation and his own downfall.

## Timeline
- **c. 1342** — Tokhtamysh is born into the House of Borjigin, descended from Genghis Khan.
- **1376** — After repeated defeats by Urus Khan, Tokhtamysh flees to the court of Timur for protection.
- **1379** — With Timur's military support, Tokhtamysh defeats and executes Tīmūr Malik, securing the eastern wing (White/Blue Horde) of the Golden Horde.
- **1380-1381** — Tokhtamysh conquers the western wing, defeating the warlord Mamai and becoming the first khan in decades to rule both halves of the Horde.
- **1382** — Tokhtamysh sacks Moscow, reasserting Mongol tribute payments from the Russian principalities.
- **1385** — Tokhtamysh's forces capture Tabriz in Azerbaijan, marking the height of his power.
- **1387** — Tokhtamysh attacks Timur's forces in Azerbaijan, initiating their open conflict.
- **1391** — Timur invades the Golden Horde, defeating Tokhtamysh at the Battle of the Kondurcha River.
- **1395** — Timur invades again, defeating Tokhtamysh at the Battle of the Terek River and subsequently sacking the Horde's capital, Sarai.
- **1399** — Tokhtamysh, allied with Lithuanian Grand Prince Vytautas, is defeated at the Battle of the Vorskla River by his rival Tīmūr Qutluq and the Horde's powerful emir, Edigu.
- **1406** — Tokhtamysh is killed near Tyumen in Siberia, likely on the orders of Edigu or the puppet khan Shādī Beg.

## Body

### Rise to Power and Reunification
Tokhtamysh spent his early years as a fugitive, fighting against Urus Khan, who had executed his father. After repeated failures, he sought and gained the crucial support of the warlord Timur. With Timur's armies, Tokhtamysh defeated his rivals in the eastern Golden Horde by 1379. He then swiftly conquered the western portion, defeating the warlord Mamai in 1381. This made him the first khan in over twenty years to rule both the eastern (White/Blue) and western (Blue/White) wings of the Golden Horde, promising a restoration of its power.

### Campaigns in Russia and Relations with Timur
Consolidating his rule, Tokhtamysh turned to the Russian principalities. Although Grand Prince Dmitry Donskoy of Moscow had submitted, he withheld tribute. In 1382, Tokhtamysh besieged and sacked Moscow, forcing the resumption of tribute payments and reasserting Mongol hegemony. Later, he adopted a more conciliatory policy, ultimately strengthening Moscow's position by granting it the hereditary Grand Principality of Vladimir in 1389. Encouraged by his successes, Tokhtamysh grew bold. In 1385, he invaded Azerbaijan, capturing Tabriz. This expansion, along with other provocations, led to a rupture with his former protector, Timur.

### Conflict with Timur and Downfall
The conflict with Timur proved catastrophic. Timur invaded the Golden Horde twice, defeating Tokhtamysh at the Kondurcha River in 1391 and the Terek River in 1395. The second invasion was devastating; Timur sacked the Horde's major cities, including the capital Sarai, crippling its trade-based economy. Tokhtamysh was left a fugitive, competing with rival khans like Tīmūr Qutluq and the powerful emir Edigu. He briefly allied with Lithuania's Grand Prince Vytautas, but their joint army was crushed at the Battle of the Vorskla River in 1399. Reduced to an adventurer, Tokhtamysh was eventually killed in Siberia in 1406.

### Legacy
Tokhtamysh was the last great khan to reunify the Golden Horde and the last to mint coins with Mongolian script. His sack of Moscow reversed the setback of the Battle of Kulikovo. However, his conflict with Timur destroyed the Horde's urban centers and trade wealth. Paradoxically, his policies strengthened Moscow and established a precedent for Lithuanian intervention in Horde politics. His death led to another period of civil war that ended in the Golden Horde's disintegration.

## Terms
- **Golden Horde**: The Mongol and later Turkicized khanate established in Eastern Europe, the western part of the Mongol Empire.
- **House of Borjigin**: The royal clan of Genghis Khan and his descendants.
- **White Horde / Blue Horde**: The two main divisions of the Golden Horde. The eastern wing was called the White Horde in Persian sources and the Blue Horde in Turkic ones; the western wing had the reverse naming.
- **Beglerbeg**: A governor-general or high-ranking military commander in the Mongol and Turkic empires.
- **Timur (Tamerlane)**: A 14th-century Turco-Mongol conqueror who founded the Timurid Empire in Persia and Central Asia.
- **Edigu**: A powerful Manghit emir who became the de facto ruler of the Golden Horde, often controlling puppet khans.
- **Ushkuyniks**: Russian river pirates who raided along the Volga and other waterways.
- **Investiture**: The formal bestowal of a title or office, in this context by the khan upon Russian princes.

## Debates and open questions
- The exact tribal affiliation of Tokhtamysh's mother, Kutan-Kunchek of the Khongirad tribe, is based on the account of the historian Muʿīn-ad-Dīn Naṭanzī, whose testimony is sometimes considered inaccurate.
- The precise motivations behind Timur's leniency toward Tokhtamysh's captured warriors after the 1387 skirmish are unclear—whether it was respect for his royal lineage or a strategic attempt to avoid a wider conflict.
- The exact terms of the 1399 treaty between Tokhtamysh and Vytautas are debated, particularly whether it stipulated that Vytautas would pay tribute to Tokhtamysh once the khan regained his throne.

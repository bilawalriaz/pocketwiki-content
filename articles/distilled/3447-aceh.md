# Aceh

## Overview
Aceh is a special autonomous province on the northern tip of Sumatra, Indonesia. It is historically significant as a key early center for the spread of Islam in Southeast Asia and has a long history of political independence and resistance to outside control. The province was devastated by the 2004 Indian Ocean tsunami, which killed approximately 170,000 people and ultimately helped precipitate a peace agreement ending a decades-long separatist conflict.

## Timeline
- **c. 10,000 BCE** — First evidence of human habitation in Aceh.
- **c. 1250** — Islam reaches the region (Kingdoms of Fansur and Lamuri).
- **1297** — Earliest clear evidence of a Muslim dynasty in the area (gravestone of Sultan Malikussaleh of Samudra).
- **1511** — Sultanate of Aceh is established by Sultan Ali Mughayat Syah.
- **17th Century** — Aceh Sultanate reaches its golden era as the wealthiest and most powerful state in the Strait of Malacca.
- **1873** — The Dutch colonial government declares war on Aceh, beginning the Aceh War.
- **1942** — Acehnese ulama (Islamic clerics) revolt against both the Dutch and the Japanese during World War II.
- **1953–1959** — Acehnese Rebellion against the Indonesian central government.
- **1976** — Dr. Hasan Muhammad di Tiro proclaims independence, founding the Free Aceh Movement (GAM).
- **26 December 2004** — The Indian Ocean earthquake and tsunami devastates Aceh's western coast.
- **15 August 2005** — The Helsinki Agreement peace deal is signed between GAM and the Indonesian government.

## Body

### History and Conflict
Aceh has a pre-Islamic history of Hindu-Buddhist kingdoms like Lambri. Islam arrived around 1250, and by the early 17th century, the Aceh Sultanate was a major regional power. Its strategic location for trade made it a target for colonial powers. The Dutch declared war in 1873, leading to a costly, four-decade guerrilla war that only saw wider Dutch control after 1908. After Indonesian independence, Aceh was incorporated into North Sumatra province, violating promises of autonomy and sparking the Acehnese Rebellion (1953–59). In 1976, the Free Aceh Movement (GAM) was founded to fight for independence, leading to a conflict that lasted until the 2004 tsunami.

### The 2004 Tsunami and Peace
On 26 December 2004, a magnitude 9.2 earthquake triggered a tsunami that killed over 170,000 people in Aceh and left about 500,000 homeless. The disaster's scale and international response created a new environment for peace. The Helsinki Agreement, signed on 15 August 2005, granted Aceh special autonomy and the right to establish local political parties in exchange for GAM's disarmament. A European Union monitoring mission oversaw the process, which concluded with local elections in 2006.

### Governance and Sharia Law
Aceh is governed as a special autonomous province with increased authority over local affairs. Following the 2001 Special Autonomy Law, the provincial legislature enacted a series of *qanuns* (local laws) implementing Sharia. These laws criminalize acts such as gambling, alcohol consumption, and "seclusion" (*khalwat*), with penalties including fines, imprisonment, and public caning. Enforcement is carried out by both the national police and the *Wilayatul Hisbah* (Sharia police). The implementation of these laws, particularly caning and punishments targeting LGBT individuals, has drawn international human rights criticism.

### Economy and Demographics
Aceh's economy, historically reliant on oil and natural gas, shifted toward services and reconstruction after the tsunami. Poverty remains higher than the national average. The population was approximately 5.55 million in mid-2024. The Acehnese are the largest ethnic group, but the province is home to ten indigenous groups, including the Gayo, Alas, and Tamiang Malays. The Acehnese language is co-official with Indonesian. The population is over 98% Muslim, making religious issues highly sensitive.

## Terms
*   **Special Autonomous Province (*Daerah Istimewa*):** An administrative designation granting Aceh increased autonomy from Indonesia's central government, including the right to implement Sharia law.
*   **Sharia (Islamic Law):** A legal framework derived from Islamic religious texts. In Aceh, it is enforced through locally enacted *qanuns*.
*   **Qanun:** A local law enacted by Aceh's provincial legislature to implement Sharia.
*   **Wilayatul Hisbah:** Aceh's Sharia police force responsible for enforcing *qanuns*.
*   **Free Aceh Movement (GAM):** A separatist group that fought for Acehnese independence from Indonesia from 1976 until the 2005 peace agreement.
*   **Helsinki Agreement:** The peace deal signed on 15 August 2005 that ended the conflict between GAM and the Indonesian government.
*   **Ulama:** Islamic clerics or scholars. In Aceh, they have historically held significant political and military influence.
*   **Mukim:** A traditional administrative unit grouping villages in Aceh, with some recognized customary forest rights.

## Debates and open questions
*   **Human Rights:** The use of caning and the targeting of LGBT individuals under Aceh's Sharia laws are subjects of ongoing international condemnation and debate over cultural relativism versus universal human rights.
*   **Autonomy vs. Central Control:** Despite the peace agreement, tensions persist regarding the practical limits of Aceh's autonomy and the implementation of its special status.
*   **Post-Tsunami Reconstruction:** While significant progress has been made, challenges remain in economic development, poverty reduction, and addressing the long-term psychological impact of the disaster.
*   **Historical Justice:** Human rights advocates note that previous human rights violations during the conflict have not been seriously addressed.

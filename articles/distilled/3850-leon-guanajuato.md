# León, Guanajuato

## Overview
León, officially León de Los Aldama, is the most populous city in the Mexican state of Guanajuato and a major national economic center. It is renowned for its large leather and shoe manufacturing industry, which has earned it the nickname "panzaverde" (green belly). The city is also recognized for its environmental initiatives, extensive cycling network, and integrated public transportation system.

## Timeline
- **1576** — Juan Bautista de Orozco founds the village of León.
- **1731** — Jesuits arrive, founding the "Compañía Vieja" (now the Temple of the Immaculate).
- **1732** — An image of Our Most Holy Mother of Light arrives; she is declared the city's patron saint in 1849.
- **1810** — Independence forces under José Rafael de Iriarte arrive; royalists retake the town two months later.
- **1827** — León becomes one of the four "departments" of the newly created state of Guanajuato.
- **1859** — General José Iniesta attacks the town during the La Reforma war; León temporarily separates from Guanajuato.
- **1862** — The bishopric of León is founded.
- **1866** — The Basilica Cathedral is consecrated.
- **1946** — A mob protesting elections is fired upon in the plaza, which is later named the "Plaza of the Martyrs."
- **2003** — The Optibús, Mexico's first Bus Rapid Transit (BRT) system, begins operation.
- **2012** — León receives the "City Water Champion" award for sanitation and wastewater reuse progress.

## Body

### History and Founding
The León area has ten pre-classic Mesoamerican archaeological zones, mostly linked to the Chupícuaro culture. By the 13th century, Chichimeca groups overran the area. Spanish settlement began in the 1530s, but constant conflict with the Chichimecas led Viceroy Martín Enríquez de Almanza to order the city's founding in 1575. It was officially founded in 1576 and gained the rank of *alcadía mayor* (a town with governing authority over surrounding lands) in 1580. The city's history includes periods of Jesuit influence, epidemics, and droughts. It saw conflict during the Mexican War of Independence, the La Reforma war, and the French Intervention. A significant modern event was the 1946 "Plaza of the Martyrs" massacre during contested elections.

### Economy and Industry
León's economy is dominated by its leather industry, producing shoes, boots, belts, and other accessories for national and international markets. About 70% of all shoes made in Mexico come from the León area. The city also has industrial chemical, plastic, polymer, and transportation companies. The financial sector includes the national headquarters of Banco del Bajío and regional offices for major banks. Major trade fairs include Sapica (Shoe Fair) and ANPIC. Retail is represented by several large malls, including Plaza Mayor, which will host a Palacio de Hierro department store.

### Climate and Geography
León has a humid subtropical climate (Köppen: Cwa) bordering on semi-arid, with summer rainfall. The average annual temperature is 19.9 °C (67.8 °F). Annual precipitation averages 681 mm (26.8 in), mainly from late May through October. There is debate over whether the valley is sub-humid or semi-arid, depending on the methodology and years studied. Snowfall is extremely rare; the last recorded snow was in 1997.

### Transportation and Environment
León is considered one of Mexico's most environmentally friendly cities, with a high number of cyclists. Its Integrated Transportation System (ITS), known as Optibús, was Mexico's first BRT system, launched in 2003. It uses articulated "caterpillar" buses and a network of feeder routes. The system reduces CO2 emissions by 5,600 tons annually. The city's Del Bajío International Airport is located in neighboring Silao.

### Historic Sites and Culture
Key landmarks include the Cathedral Basilica of Our Lady of the Light (begun 1746, consecrated 1866), the neo-Gothic Expiatorio sanctuary (construction began 1920), and the Arch of the Causeway of the Heroes topped by a bronze lion. The Forum Cultural Guanajuato complex houses a state library, art museum, and the Bicentennial Theater. The city hosts the headquarters of the Rally Mexico (WRC) and is home to the eight-time champion football club León.

## Terms
- **Alcaldía mayor**: A Spanish colonial administrative division with governing authority over a town and its surrounding territory.
- **Chichimecas**: A collective term for various semi-nomadic Indigenous groups that inhabited northern Mexico.
- **La Reforma**: A period of liberal reforms in Mexico (1858–1861) that led to a civil war between Liberals and Conservatives.
- **Panzaverde**: Spanish for "green belly," a nickname for León's inhabitants due to the green taint from treating leather.
- **Optibús**: León's Bus Rapid Transit (BRT) system, the first of its kind in Mexico.
- **BRT (Bus Rapid Transit)**: A high-capacity bus-based public transport system with dedicated lanes and stations.
- **Köppen climate classification**: A system for classifying the world's climates based on temperature and precipitation patterns.
- **Cwa**: The Köppen code for a humid subtropical climate with dry winters and hot summers.

## Debates and Open Questions
- There is a discrepancy about whether León's valley is sub-humid or semi-arid, depending on the methodology and the specific years of data used for analysis.
- The construction of the Expiatory Sanctuary of the Sacred Heart of Jesus has been ongoing since 1920 and is considered 90% complete, with its funding partly reliant on the sale of crypts.

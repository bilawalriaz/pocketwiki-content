# Barranquilla

## Overview
Barranquilla is Colombia's fourth-largest city and the principal port on the Caribbean coast, strategically located near the mouth of the Magdalena River. Its importance stems from its role as a major economic, industrial, and transportation hub, as well as its vibrant cultural identity, epitomized by the UNESCO-recognized Carnival of Barranquilla.

## Timeline
- **c. 1629** — Settlement established on the site, though the area was inhabited earlier.
- **April 7, 1813** — Legally established as a town ("villa") and made capital of the department of Barlovento.
- **1825** — Steamship navigation begins on the Magdalena River, boosting trade.
- **1869-1871** — The Bolívar Railway, Colombia's first, is built to link the city to its port.
- **1919** — Ernesto Cortissoz International Airport, South America's first, is built.
- **1919** — SCADTA (later Avianca), the first commercial airline in the Americas, is founded.
- **1946** — Hosts the 5th Central American and Caribbean Games.
- **1993** — Granted the category of "Special District, Industrial and Port."
- **2001** — Carnival of Barranquilla declared a National Cultural Heritage of Colombia.
- **2003** — Carnival of Barranquilla recognized by UNESCO as a Masterpiece of Oral and Intangible Heritage of Humanity.
- **2018** — Hosts the 23rd Central American and Caribbean Games.

## Body

### Origins and Economic Rise
Barranquilla was not founded during the Spanish colonial period on a pre-Columbian site. Its settlement grew from haciendas on the banks of the Magdalena River in the 17th century. Its strategic location near the river's mouth made it a natural port. The introduction of steamships on the Magdalena in 1825 and the construction of the Bolívar Railway (1869-1871) cemented its role as Colombia's primary gateway for river and maritime trade, leading to rapid growth and immigration in the late 19th and early 20th centuries.

### 20th Century: Boom and Decline
In the early 20th century, Barranquilla was a modern, cosmopolitan city and Colombia's main port, earning the nickname "Golden Gate of the Republic." It was a pioneer in aviation, hosting South America's first airport (1919) and the first commercial airline. However, from the 1960s to the early 1980s, the city experienced economic decline due to political mismanagement and the collapse of industrial sectors. Government investment shifted to other cities, and Barranquilla's national prominence waned.

### Culture and Identity
The city's culture is a blend of influences from its diverse immigrant populations. Its most famous cultural expression is the Carnival of Barranquilla, a major folk festival featuring music, dance, and costumes. The city is also known for its Caribbean Spanish dialect and folk music rhythms like cumbia and vallenato. Notable cultural institutions include the Teatro Amira de la Rosa and the Parque Cultural del Caribe.

### Geography and Climate
Barranquilla is located on the west bank of the Magdalena River, 7.5 km from the Caribbean Sea. It has a tropical savanna climate (Köppen Aw), hot year-round with an average temperature of 28.4°C. The rainy seasons are from April to June and August to November. The city's terrain is a slightly inclined plain, with the Mallorquín Swamp being a significant natural feature.

## Terms
*   **Magdalena River:** Colombia's principal river, flowing north to the Caribbean Sea; Barranquilla's location near its mouth made it a key port.
*   **SCADTA:** *Sociedad Colombo-Alemana de Transportes Aéreos*, the first commercial airline in the Americas, founded in Barranquilla in 1919, later becoming Avianca.
*   **Carnival of Barranquilla:** One of Colombia's most important folk festivals, a multicultural event held annually before Lent, recognized by UNESCO.
*   **Bocas de Ceniza:** The mouth of the Magdalena River where it meets the Caribbean Sea, critical for the port's access.
*   **Special District, Industrial and Port:** The administrative category granted to Barranquilla in 1993, recognizing its economic importance.
*   **TransMetro:** Barranquilla's metropolitan mass transit system using articulated buses on exclusive lanes, launched in 2001.
*   **Republican Architecture:** A style of architecture in Colombia from the post-independence period (c. 1819-1930), featuring neoclassical and other European influences, prominent in Barranquilla's historic center.

## Debates and Open Questions
The exact origins of the settlement are debated. While traditionally dated to 1629, some historians argue it was founded by farmers from the neighboring town of Galapa. The city's decline in the mid-20th century is attributed to widespread corruption in local administrations and a subsequent lack of investment compared to other Colombian cities.

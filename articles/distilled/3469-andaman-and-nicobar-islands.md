# Andaman and Nicobar Islands

## Overview
The Andaman and Nicobar Islands are a strategically vital union territory of India, comprising 836 islands in the Bay of Bengal. They are geographically significant as India's southernmost point and are ecologically unique, hosting endemic species and uncontacted tribes. Their history spans ancient isolation, medieval trade, colonial exploitation, and modern strategic development.

## Timeline
- **c. 30,000 years ago** — Indigenous Andamanese people become isolated during the Middle Paleolithic era.
- **11th century** — Chola dynasty uses the islands as a naval base for Southeast Asian expeditions.
- **1755** — Danish East India Company settlers arrive on the Nicobar Islands.
- **1858** — British re-establish a penal colony at Port Blair for Indian rebels.
- **1868** — Denmark sells Nicobar Islands to the British.
- **1942-1945** — Japanese occupation during World War II; islands renamed Shaheed-Dweep and Swaraj-dweep.
- **1947** — Islands automatically become part of the Dominion of India after independence.
- **1950** — Designated a union territory under the Constitution of India.
- **2004** — A tsunami causes over 2,000 casualties; indigenous groups largely survive due to oral traditions.

## Body

### Geography and Nature
The territory is split into the Andaman and Nicobar island clusters by the 150 km-wide Ten Degree Channel. It has a total land area of 8,249 km², with only 31 of its 836 islands inhabited. The landscape varies from sandy beaches to marshy mangroves, with a tropical climate. The islands are a biodiversity hotspot; 86.2% of the land is forested, hosting over 2,200 plant species (200 endemic) and 8,300+ animal species (1,117 endemic). Notable fauna includes the endangered Indian elephant (introduced in 1883) and the Nicobar pigeon, the closest living relative to the extinct dodo.

### Indigenous Peoples and Demographics
The islands are home to distinct indigenous groups. The Andamanese (including the Great Andamanese, Onge, Jarawa, and Sentinelese) have been isolated for millennia and speak unrelated Andamanese languages. The Nicobarese and Shompen of the southern islands speak Austroasiatic languages. The Sentinelese of North Sentinel Island remain uncontacted. The 2011 census recorded a total population of 380,581, with Bengali, Tamil, and Telugu as major spoken languages. Hinduism is the majority religion (69.5%). The indigenous population has drastically declined due to colonization and disease, with government efforts now focused on their protection.

### History and Administration
Archaeological evidence dates civilization to 2,200 years ago. The islands were used as a naval base by the 11th-century Chola dynasty. European colonization began with the Danish in 1755, but malaria thwarted early settlements. The British established a permanent penal colony in 1858, using the Cellular Jail (built 1896-1906) to imprison political activists. Japan occupied the islands during WWII (1942-1945). Post-independence, the territory became a union territory in 1956. It is administered by a Lieutenant Governor and is strategically important, hosting the joint-service Andaman and Nicobar Command.

### Economy and Infrastructure
Agriculture engages about 50% of the population, though only 6% of land is arable; rice is the main crop. Fishing and tourism are major economic drivers, with over 400,000 visitors in 2016. The islands have limited industrial development. Infrastructure includes the Veer Savarkar International Airport, 23 ports, and national highways. Power is generated primarily by diesel plants, and high-speed internet was enabled by a submarine cable to Chennai in 2020.

## Terms
- **Union Territory**: A type of administrative division in India, governed directly by the central government.
- **Ten Degree Channel**: The 150 km-wide strait separating the Andaman and Nicobar island groups.
- **Cellular Jail**: A colonial prison in Port Blair used to exile political prisoners from mainland India.
- **Endemic**: A species native and restricted to a certain place.
- **Uncontacted tribe**: An indigenous group that has chosen to remain isolated from the outside world.
- **Andamanese languages**: A group of endangered, unrelated languages spoken by the indigenous peoples of the Andaman Islands.
- **Austroasiatic languages**: A language family that includes the Nicobarese languages spoken in the Nicobar Islands.
- **Restricted Area Permit (RAP)**: A permit required for foreign tourists to visit specific, restricted areas of the islands.

## Debates and Open Questions
The source highlights ongoing scholarly debates regarding the precise origins and linguistic relationships of the indigenous Andamanese peoples. A major open question concerns the future of uncontacted tribes like the Sentinelese, balancing protection from disease and external influence with the right to self-determination. The long-term ecological impact of the 2004 tsunami and subsequent human development on the islands' unique biodiversity also remains a subject of study.

# Isle of Man

## Overview
The Isle of Man is a self-governing British Crown Dependency in the Irish Sea, with its own parliament and government but relying on the UK for defence and international representation. It has a distinct Celtic and Norse cultural heritage, a unique language (Manx), and an economy heavily reliant on financial services, online gambling, and tourism, including the famous TT motorcycle races.

## Timeline
- **c. 6500 BC** — Humans colonise the island by sea.
- **5th century AD** — Gaelic influence and settlement; Manx language emerges; Christianity spreads.
- **627** — King Edwin of Northumbria conquers the Isle of Man.
- **9th century** — Norsemen found the Kingdom of the Isles, including Man.
- **1266** — Treaty of Perth: Norway cedes Man to Scotland.
- **1399** — Island comes under the feudal lordship of the English Crown.
- **1765** — Act of Revestment: feudal rights revested into the British Crown.
- **1866** — Isle of Man obtains limited home rule.
- **1881** — Tynwald becomes the first national legislature to grant women voting rights in a general election (excluding married women).
- **2016** — UNESCO awards the Isle of Man biosphere reserve status.

## Body

### History and Governance
Humans have inhabited the island since before 6500 BC. Gaelic culture and the Manx language (a Goidelic language) became established by the 5th century AD, alongside the spread of Christianity. The island was conquered by Northumbria in 627 and later became part of the Norse Kingdom of the Isles in the 9th century. Following the Treaty of Perth in 1266, sovereignty passed from Norway to Scotland, and after periods of alternating rule, the island came under English feudal lordship in 1399. The feudal rights were purchased by the British Crown in 1765 (the Act of Revestment), but the island never became part of the United Kingdom, retaining its internal self-government. The UK is responsible for defence and external affairs, while the island's own parliament, Tynwald (claimed to be the oldest continuous governing body), handles domestic matters.

### Economy
The Isle of Man's economy is characterised by low taxation and a focus on offshore financial services. It has no capital gains, wealth, stamp duty, or inheritance tax, with a top income tax rate of 22% and a standard corporation tax rate of 0%. Key sectors include insurance and online gambling (each 17% of GNI), and information technology and banking (9% each). This status has attracted wealth but also brought challenges like money laundering and financial crime. Tourism, once a mainstay, has declined, though the TT motorcycle races remain a major economic event. The island is in a customs union with the UK under the Common Purse Agreement.

### Culture and Society
The Manx are a Celtic nation, with culture influenced by Norse and British traditions. The Manx language, closely related to Irish and Scottish Gaelic, is an official language and has seen a revival since the death of the last native speaker in 1974. The island's symbol is the "three legs of Man" triskelion. Christianity is the predominant religion (54.7% in 2021), with the Anglican diocese of Sodor and Man being historic. The Manx cat (a tailless breed) and the Manx Loaghtan sheep (a multi-horned breed) are iconic fauna. The island was designated a UNESCO biosphere reserve in 2016.

## Terms
- **Crown Dependency**: A territory for which the UK is responsible for defence and international representation, but which is not part of the UK itself.
- **Tynwald**: The parliament of the Isle of Man, consisting of the House of Keys and the Legislative Council.
- **Goidelic languages**: A branch of the Celtic languages, including Manx, Irish, and Scottish Gaelic.
- **Act of Revestment (1765)**: The purchase of the feudal rights to the Isle of Man by the British Crown from the Dukes of Atholl.
- **Common Purse Agreement**: An arrangement where the Isle of Man and the UK pool and share customs and excise revenues.
- **Triskelion**: A motif of three interlocked spirals or legs, the national symbol of the Isle of Man.
- **Manx Loaghtan**: A rare, multi-horned breed of sheep native to the Isle of Man.
- **Biosphere Reserve**: A designation by UNESCO for areas that balance conservation with sustainable development.

## Debates and open questions
- The exact origin of the island's name is debated; it may derive from the Brittonic *Manaua* ("mountain island") or be associated with the sea god Manannán mac Lir, though the latter is considered less likely.
- The historical extent of Northumbrian and later English influence on the island is unclear, as few Old English place names exist.
- The impact of legal measures to combat money laundering and financial crime remains uncertain, and as of 2023, the island was not fully compliant with international anti-money laundering standards.

# Novosibirsk

## Overview
Novosibirsk is the administrative center of Novosibirsk Oblast and the Siberian Federal District in Russia. It is the third-most populous city in Russia (1,633,595 as of the 2021 census) and the largest city in North Asia. Its importance stems from its founding as a key transport hub on the Trans-Siberian Railway, which catalyzed its rapid growth into a major industrial, scientific, and cultural center for Siberia.

## Body

### Founding and Early Growth
Novosibirsk was founded on April 30, 1893, at the site of a future Trans-Siberian Railway bridge over the Ob River. Originally named Novonikolayevsk, the settlement grew rapidly due to the bridge's completion in 1897 and the later Turkestan–Siberia Railway. By 1907, it had town status and over 47,000 people, becoming a major commercial and industrial hub with banks, factories, and schools. By 1917, its population reached 80,000.

### Civil War and Soviet Era
The Russian Civil War (1917–1923) caused significant damage, including the destruction of the Ob bridge and population decline due to epidemics. Reconstruction began in 1921 under Lenin's New Economic Policy. The city was renamed Novosibirsk in 1926. Under Stalin, it became a key industrial center, with plants like 'Sibkombain' producing heavy equipment. The 1932–33 famine brought over 170,000 rural refugees. During World War II (1941–1942), more than 50 factories were relocated to the city, making it a major supply base.

### Post-War Development and Science
Post-war growth included the construction of a hydroelectric station in the 1950s, creating the "Ob Sea" reservoir. In 1957, the government established Akademgorodok, a scientific research complex 30 km south of the city center, housing over 35 institutes and Novosibirsk State University. The city's population hit one million in 1962. The Novosibirsk Metro opened in 1985.

### Geography and Climate
The city is located on the Ob River in the West Siberian Plain. Its climate is humid continental (Köppen Dfb) with warm summers (avg. +15°C to +26°C) and bitterly cold winters (avg. −20°C to −12°C), though less severe than eastern Siberia. It is the second-farthest substantially populated city from an ocean.

### Economy and Transportation
Novosibirsk is a large industrial center, producing over two-thirds of Novosibirsk Oblast's industrial output. Key industries include aerospace, nuclear fuel, and machinery. It is a major transport hub served by Tolmachevo Airport (S7 Airlines hub) and a key stop on the Trans-Siberian Railway. The city has an extensive public transport network, including a metro, trams, trolleybuses, and buses.

### Governance and Culture
Until 2023, the mayor was elected by residents. In 2023, direct elections were abolished, and in April 2024, Maxim Kudryavtsev was elected mayor by the city council. The city is a cultural center with numerous theatres, including the Novosibirsk Opera and Ballet Theatre, museums, and the world-renowned Novosibirsk Zoo, which houses over 11,000 animals.

## Terms
- **Trans-Siberian Railway**: A network of railways connecting Moscow with the Russian Far East, the construction of which prompted Novosibirsk's founding.
- **Ob River**: The great Siberian river on whose banks Novosibirsk is located.
- **Akademgorodok**: A scientific research complex and suburb of Novosibirsk, founded in 1957, housing numerous institutes and Novosibirsk State University.
- **Ob Sea**: The large reservoir created by the Novosibirsk Hydroelectric Station dam on the Ob River.
- **Humid continental climate (Köppen Dfb)**: A climate type characterized by large seasonal temperature differences, with warm to hot summers and cold winters.
- **Novosibirsk Metro**: The city's rapid transit system, opened in 1985, the first in Siberia.

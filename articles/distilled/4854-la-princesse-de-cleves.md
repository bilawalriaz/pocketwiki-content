# La Princesse de Clèves

## Overview
*La Princesse de Clèves* is a French novel published anonymously in 1678, generally attributed to Madame de La Fayette. It is considered a foundational work of the psychological novel and early historical fiction, renowned for its realistic portrayal of the 16th-century French court and its deep analysis of characters' inner emotions and moral conflicts.

## Body

### Plot and Setting
The story is set primarily at the royal court of Henry II of France between October 1558 and November 1559. It follows Mademoiselle de Chartres, a sheltered sixteen-year-old heiress. Following her mother's advice, she marries the stable but less exciting Prince de Clèves. She soon meets the charismatic Duke de Nemours, and they fall in love but refrain from acting on their feelings. After a series of misunderstandings involving a letter and court intrigues, the Princess confesses her emotional attachment to her husband. The Prince, devastated, becomes ill and dies, blaming the Duke on his deathbed. Now free, the Princess rejects the Duke's advances, choosing instead a life of duty and partial seclusion in a convent.

### Literary Significance and Style
The novel is a landmark in literary history. It is considered one of the first *romans d'analyse* (analysis novels), which prioritized the exploration of characters' psychological states and moral dilemmas over complex plots and adventures. This introspective style marked a major turning point from the prevailing romance novels of the era. The work is also a pioneering example of Western historical fiction, recreating the 16th-century court with remarkable precision and using nearly all historical figures as characters, except for the heroine.

### Characters and Historical Basis
The central character is the Princess of Clèves, who embodies the novel's core conflict between passion and duty. Her mother, Madame de Chartres, serves as an early advisor for restraint. Her husband, the Prince de Clèves, is a man of "prudence rare in the young" who loves her deeply. The object of her affection, the Duke de Nemours, is based on the historical figure Jacques of Savoy. The court is populated with real historical figures, including King Henry II (seen as a representation of Louis XIV), Diane de Poitiers, and Mary, Queen of Scots. The Princess's uncle, the Vidame de Chartres, is based on François de Vendôme.

### Reception and Legacy
The novel was an immediate commercial success, sparking public debates about its authorship and the Princess's decision to confess her feelings. Its influence extends to modern culture, inspiring several film adaptations. In 2006, it became a symbol of cultural debate when politician Nicolas Sarkozy mocked its inclusion in civil service exams. This led to public readings and a surge in sales, cementing its status as a classic of French literature.

## Terms
- **Psychological novel**: A genre focusing on the inner thoughts, feelings, and motivations of characters.
- **Roman d'analyse**: "Analysis novel," a subtype of the psychological novel that delves deeply into characters' emotional and moral conflicts.
- **Historical fiction**: A literary genre where the plot takes place in a setting from the past, often incorporating real historical events and figures.
- **Ingénue**: An innocent and unsophisticated young woman, especially in a play or novel.
- **Salon**: A regular social gathering of literary, artistic, or political figures, often hosted in a private home.
- **Vidame**: A feudal title in France, originally a deputy of a bishop.
- **Chef d’oeuvre de la nature**: French for "masterpiece of nature," used to describe the Duke de Nemours's exceptional charm.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Authorship**: The novel was published anonymously, and while Madame de La Fayette is generally held to be the author, this was a subject of early debate.
- **The Princess's Confession**: A major contemporary debate concerned the wisdom and morality of the Princess's decision to confess her adulterous feelings to her husband, a central and controversial plot point.
- **Historical Fidelity**: While the novel is praised for its precise recreation of the era, scholars examine the balance between its historical accuracy and its fictional narrative.

# Hebei

## Overview
Hebei is a province in North China that uniquely surrounds the municipalities of Beijing and Tianjin. Its strategic location has made it historically significant as a political and military frontier, and today it forms the core of the Jing-Jin-Ji megalopolis, a major economic and transportation hub. The province is a leading industrial center, particularly for steel production, but faces challenges related to pollution and development disparities.

## Timeline
- **c. 200,000–700,000 years ago** — Peking Man (*Homo erectus*) lives in the region.
- **771–221 BC** — Region is ruled by the states of Yan and Zhao during the Spring and Autumn and Warring States periods.
- **1421** — Ming dynasty capital moves to Beijing; the surrounding province is first called North Zhili.
- **1928** — Republic of China renames Zhili province to Hebei.
- **1948–1949** — Xibaipo serves as the CCP's headquarters during the final stages of the Chinese Civil War.
- **1976** — The Tangshan earthquake kills over 240,000 people.
- **2017** — Xiong'an New Area is established to integrate Beijing, Tianjin, and Hebei.
- **2022** — Beijing–Tangshan and Beijing–Binhai intercity railways open.
- **2026** — First circular high-speed rail service linking Beijing, Baoding, Xiong'an, and Tianjin begins operation.

## Body

### Etymology and History
The name "Hebei" means "north of the river," referring to its location north of the Yellow River. Historically, the region was known as Jizhou and later as Zhili ("Directly Ruled") when it surrounded the imperial capital. The modern province was created in 1928. The area has a deep history, from prehistoric Peking Man to its role as the heartland of the Yan and Zhao states. It was a frequent battleground during periods of division, such as the Sixteen Kingdoms and the Five Dynasties. The cession of the Sixteen Prefectures of Yanyun to the Khitan Liao dynasty in the 10th century created a long-term strategic weakness for Chinese states.

### Geography and Climate
Hebei is geographically diverse, containing plateaus, mountains, hills, plains, and a coastline on the Bohai Sea. The Taihang Mountains run along its western border, and the Yan Mountains cross the north. The province has a monsoon-influenced humid continental climate with cold, dry winters and hot, humid summers. Annual precipitation ranges from 400 to 800 mm.

### Economy
Hebei's economy is driven by agriculture and heavy industry, making it China's premier steel producer. Key industries include mining (coal and iron ore), petroleum refining, and manufacturing. The Kailuan coal mine in Tangshan is one of China's oldest and produces over 20 million tonnes annually. Despite its industrial output, the province ranks relatively low in per capita income. About 40% of the labor force works in agriculture, supplying crops like wheat and maize to Beijing and Tianjin.

### Demographics and Culture
The population is predominantly Han Chinese (96%), with significant Manchu, Hui, and Mongol minorities. The dominant religions are Chinese folk religions, Taoism, and Buddhism. Hebei has China's largest Catholic population, with many loyal to the Pope rather than the state-sanctioned church. The province is known for traditional arts like Pingju opera and Ding ware ceramics. Its cuisine features wheat-based dishes like the donkey burger.

### Infrastructure and Transportation
Hebei is a central node in China's rail network. The Jing-Jin-Ji regional integration initiative has built a dense high-speed rail grid, connecting all prefecture-level cities. Major lines include the Beijing–Zhangjiakou high-speed railway (2019) and the Beijing–Xiong'an intercity railway (2019–20). The province's expressway network exceeded 9,000 km by 2026. Key ports include Qinhuangdao, one of China's busiest.

## Terms
- **Zhili**: "Directly Ruled"; the name of the province when it surrounded the imperial capital during the Ming and Qing dynasties.
- **Jing-Jin-Ji**: The megalopolis region integrating Beijing, Tianjin, and Hebei.
- **Xiong'an New Area**: A state-level new area established in 2017 to relieve non-capital functions from Beijing.
- **Pingju**: A popular form of Chinese opera in Hebei known for its colloquial language.
- **Ding ware**: A type of creamy white Chinese ceramic historically produced in Quyang County, Hebei.
- **Sixteen Prefectures of Yanyun**: Strategic territory in northern Hebei ceded to the Khitan Liao dynasty in 936, creating a defensive vulnerability.
- **Ji Lu Mandarin**: A subdivision of Chinese dialects spoken in most of Hebei.
- **Kailuan coal mine**: One of China's oldest modern coal mines, located in Tangshan, Hebei.

## Debates and open questions
The source notes that the exact location of the mythical Battle of Zhuolu, which is said to have started Huaxia civilization, is debated but placed in Zhangjiakou, Hebei. The political status of the Catholic Church in Hebei is a point of tension, with a significant "underground" church loyal to the Pope existing alongside the state-approved Catholic Patriotic Church.

# Philip V of Spain

## Overview
Philip V (1683–1746) was the first Bourbon king of Spain, whose accession triggered the War of the Spanish Succession. His reign, the longest in Spanish history, was marked by efforts to centralize royal power and reverse Spain's imperial decline, though it was also affected by his personal mental health struggles.

## Timeline
- **19 Dec 1683** — Born at Versailles, France, as Duke of Anjou.
- **1 Nov 1700** — Succeeds Charles II as King of Spain, beginning the Bourbon dynasty.
- **1701–1714** — War of the Spanish Succession fought over his accession.
- **2 Nov 1701** — Marries Maria Luisa of Savoy.
- **1707–1713** — Issues Nueva Planta decrees, centralizing Spanish administration.
- **11 Apr 1713** — Treaty of Utrecht ends the war; Philip is recognized as king but loses European territories.
- **24 Dec 1714** — Marries Elisabeth Farnese after Maria Luisa's death.
- **14 Jan 1724** — Abdicates in favor of his son, Louis I.
- **31 Aug 1724** — Louis I dies; Philip is restored to the throne.
- **9 Jul 1746** — Philip V dies at El Escorial.

## Body
**Accession and War of the Spanish Succession**
Philip was the grandson of Louis XIV of France. In 1700, the childless Spanish Habsburg king Charles II named Philip his heir. This threatened to unite the French and Spanish crowns, upsetting Europe's balance of power. A Grand Alliance of England, the Dutch Republic, and Austria fought to place the Habsburg claimant, Archduke Charles, on the throne. The 13-year War of the Spanish Succession ended with the Treaty of Utrecht (1713), which confirmed Philip as King of Spain but required him to renounce any claim to the French throne. Spain also lost the Spanish Netherlands and its Italian territories.

**Domestic Reforms and Centralization**
To consolidate power during and after the war, Philip issued the Nueva Planta decrees starting in 1707. These abolished the separate legal and administrative systems of the Crown of Aragon and other regions, imposing the centralized Castilian model. Only Navarre and the Basque region, which had supported him, retained their semi-autonomous status. These reforms, modeled on Louis XIV's France, aimed to strengthen the monarchy at the expense of regional privileges.

**Later Reign and Personal Struggles**
Philip's second wife, Elisabeth Farnese, became influential in government, especially as the king suffered from recurring bouts of depression. He briefly abdicated in 1724, but was restored after his son and successor, Louis I, died of smallpox after only seven months. In his later years, Philip sought to reclaim lost Spanish territories in Italy, leading to the War of the Quadruple Alliance (1718–1720), which ended in defeat. His reign saw some economic recovery and naval rebuilding, but the state treasury was often depleted by court expenses and relied on New World silver shipments, leading to a debt suspension in 1739.

## Terms
*   **Nueva Planta decrees:** A series of laws issued by Philip V that abolished the traditional charters and separate administrations of the Crown of Aragon and other Spanish kingdoms, centralizing governance under the Castilian model.
*   **War of the Spanish Succession (1701–1714):** A major European conflict triggered by Philip V's accession, fought to determine if the Spanish and French crowns would unite under the Bourbon dynasty.
*   **Treaty of Utrecht (1713):** The peace agreement that ended the War of the Spanish Succession, recognizing Philip V as King of Spain but forcing him to renounce claims to France and cede Spanish territories in Europe.
*   **Grand Alliance:** A coalition formed against the union of the French and Spanish crowns, primarily consisting of England, the Dutch Republic, and Austria.
*   **Crown of Aragon:** A composite monarchy with its own laws and institutions, which was dissolved by the Nueva Planta decrees.
*   **Régnicole:** A legal status, granted to Philip by Louis XIV, meaning a natural-born Frenchman with rights to the French throne, which was later repealed.
*   **Semi-Salic law:** A succession law introduced in Spain under Philip V, limiting the throne to the male line before it could pass to a female, designed to prevent a future union with France.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Motivation for Abdication:** The text notes that Philip's 1724 abdication was believed by some to be a tactic to circumvent the Treaty of Utrecht and claim the French throne, but states the actual reason was his declining mental health.
*   **Historical Assessment:** Historians are generally critical of Philip V. One view, cited from historian John Lynch, is that he advanced Spanish government only marginally and was more of a liability than his Habsburg predecessor, Charles II, often favoring French interests over Spanish ones.
*   **Effectiveness of Reforms:** While Philip initiated centralizing reforms, the text indicates that below the elite level, the inefficiency and corruption of the previous era persisted, and his reforms were only fully realized under his later successor, Charles III.

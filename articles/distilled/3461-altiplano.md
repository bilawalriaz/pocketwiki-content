# Altiplano

## Overview
The Altiplano, or Andean Plateau, is the most extensive high plateau on Earth outside Tibet, located in west-central South America. It is a region of significant geographical, geological, and cultural importance, hosting major pre-Columbian civilizations and modern economic activities like mining and herding. Its extreme elevation and unique climate create a distinct environment that has shaped human settlement and ecology for millennia.

## Timeline
- **c. 3000 BC** — Chiripa culture emerges on the Altiplano.
- **c. 500–1000 AD** — Tiwanaku civilization flourishes near Lake Titicaca.
- **c. 1438–1533 AD** — The Inca Empire incorporates the Altiplano.
- **16th century** — Spain conquers the region.
- **Pleistocene epoch** — Vast pluvial lakes cover parts of the Altiplano; remnants include Lake Titicaca.
- **Recent history** — Lake Poopó, Bolivia's second-largest lake, dries up and is declared defunct by December 2015.

## Body

### Geography and Climate
The Altiplano is an area of inland drainage (endorheism) in the central Andes, averaging about 3,750 meters (12,300 ft) in elevation. It is flanked by massive active volcanoes to the west and the humid Amazon rainforest to the east. The climate is cold and dry with marked daily temperature fluctuations. Mean annual temperatures range from 3°C (37°F) near the western mountains to 12°C (54°F) near Lake Titicaca. Annual rainfall varies from less than 200 mm (8 in) in the arid southwest to over 800 mm (31 in) near Lake Titicaca, which acts as a thermoregulator, moderating local temperatures. The rainy season is concentrated between December and March.

### Geology and Formation
The formation of the Altiplano is explained by several hypotheses. These include pre-existing weaknesses in the Earth's crust that partitioned tectonic uplift, magmatic processes from the asthenosphere, and climate-controlled erosion and sediment deposition. The internal drainage and sediment trapping may have blocked tectonic deformation in the central area. During the Pleistocene, vast pluvial lakes covered the region; their remnants are Lake Titicaca and the now-defunct Lake Poopó. Large dry salt flats, like Salar de Uyuni, formed after these paleolakes dried out.

### Human History and Economy
The Altiplano was home to pre-Columbian cultures such as the Chiripa, Tiwanaku, and the Inca Empire before Spanish conquest in the 16th century. Today, major economic activities include mining, llama and vicuña herding, and urban services in cities like El Alto, Oruro, Juliaca, and Puno. The area also attracts international tourism. Communities such as the Qulla, Uros, Quechua, and Aymara inhabit the plateau.

## Terms
- **Altiplano**: Spanish for "high plain"; a high plateau in the central Andes.
- **Endorheism**: A closed drainage basin where water does not flow to the ocean, often forming salt lakes.
- **Puna**: An alternate name for the high, cold plateau climate zone of the Andes.
- **Salar**: A salt flat, formed from the evaporation of ancient lakes.
- **Pluvial lake**: A lake formed during a period of increased rainfall, often in the past.
- **Tierra fría**: A Spanish term for a "cold land" climate zone at high elevations.
- **Tierra helada**: A Spanish term for a "frozen land" climate zone at very high elevations.
- **Hypoxic**: Having a deficiency of oxygen in the tissues, due to the thin air at high altitude.

## Debates and Open questions
The primary scholarly debate concerns the precise geological mechanisms that formed the Altiplano's high, low-relief topography within the Andean orogen. Multiple hypotheses involving crustal weaknesses, magmatic processes, climate, and lithospheric removal are proposed. Additionally, the future of the recently dried Lake Poopó is uncertain; it is unclear whether this former second-largest lake in Bolivia can be restored.

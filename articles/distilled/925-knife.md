# Knife

## Overview
A knife is a cutting tool with a blade attached to a handle, among humanity's oldest implements, dating back at least 2.5 million years to Oldowan stone tools. Over millennia, blade materials evolved from stone and bone to copper, bronze, iron, steel, ceramic, and titanium, driven by advances in metallurgy and manufacturing. Knives serve diverse functions across hunting, combat, food preparation, dining, ritual, and utility applications, with designs varying widely by culture, purpose, and maker.

## Timeline
- **2.5 million years ago** — Oldowan stone tools represent earliest known knives
- **c. 3000 BC** — Copper smelting enables first metal knife blades
- **Bronze Age** — Bronze alloys replace copper for stronger blades
- **Iron Age** — Iron becomes dominant blade material
- **Medieval period** — Steel-making techniques improve blade quality
- **19th century** — Mass production and standardized knife types emerge
- **20th century** — Stainless steel, ceramics, and titanium introduced

## Body

### Construction and Materials
Modern knives consist of a blade, handle, and optional components like guards, bolsters, and lanyards. Blades are made from carbon steel (sharp but rust-prone), stainless steel (corrosion-resistant but less sharp), high-carbon stainless steel (combines both properties), laminated steel (layered construction), Damascus steel (pattern-welded), titanium (lightweight but less hard), ceramic (maintains edge long but brittle), and plastic (disposable). Manufacturing methods include forging (shaping heated steel) and stock removal (grinding from solid stock), both followed by heat treatment involving quenching and tempering.

### Blade Geometry
Blades are ground into flat (tapering from spine to edge), hollow (concave bevels for thinner edges), or serrated (wavy edges for sawing motions) profiles. Edge configurations include plain, serrated, or combination edges. Some single-edged knives feature reverse or false edges on the spine for enhanced function.

### Handle Design
Handles are crafted from wood (warm but water-sensitive), plastic (easy-care but slippery), reinforced polymers like Zytel or Grivory (durable), rubber (grippy and cushioned), Micarta/G-10 (water-resistant composite), leather (traditional on military knives), or metal (durable but conducts temperature). Exotic materials like bone, antler, and mother-of-pearl appear on ceremonial pieces. Handles may be full tang (blade extends entire length) or partial tang (stick tang), with specialized designs accommodating users with disabilities.

### Folding Mechanisms
Folding knives use various locking systems: slip joints (spring-held, no lock), lockbacks (spine-mounted latch), linerlocks (side-spring against tang), frame locks (handle material itself acts as lock), compression locks (metal tab engages blade impression), collar locks (used by Opinel), button locks (push-button release), axis locks (cylindrical bearing), arc locks (curved bearing path), ball bearing locks, Tri-Ad locks (reinforced lockback), and PickLocks (post-and-spring mechanism). Opening methods include nail nicks, thumb studs/holes, flippers, and Emerson's "wave" feature that catches pocket fabric during drawing.

### Specialized Types
Knives are categorized by function: weapons (daggers, combat knives, switchblades, machetes, throwing knives), culinary tools (chef's knives, paring knives, cleavers, fillet knives), utility implements (pocketknives, multi-tools, box cutters), and religious/ceremonial objects (kirpan, athame, kris, sgian-dubh). Traditional forms include the Japanese tanto, Finnish puukko, Nepalese kukri, and Inuit ulu.

## Terms
- **Tang**: Portion of blade extending into handle; full tang runs entire handle length
- **Grind**: Cross-sectional shape of blade (flat, hollow, convex)
- **Serration**: Wavy or scalloped edge for sawing action
- **Bolster**: Heavy material at handle-blade junction for strength and balance
- **Ricasso**: Flat blade section at base, sometimes used for finger placement
- **Damascus steel**: Pattern-welded steel with distinctive layered appearance
- **Laminated blade**: Multiple metal layers bonded together for combined properties
- **Out-the-front (OTF)**: Switchblade where blade extends fully from handle front
- **Sheath knife**: Fixed-blade knife carried in protective sheath
- **Ball bearing lock**: Locking mechanism using ball bearing to secure blade

## Debates and Open Questions
The exact timeline of early knife development remains debated, with Oldowan tools representing the earliest evidence but questions about their classification as true knives. The superiority of forging versus stock removal manufacturing methods continues among makers, with forged blades often commanding premium prices despite both methods producing functional knives. Legal restrictions on automatic knives vary significantly by jurisdiction, creating ongoing debates about effectiveness versus civil liberties. The classification of assisted-opening knives versus switchblades remains legally contentious in some regions. Additionally, the optimal steel alloys for specific applications involve trade-offs between hardness, corrosion resistance, and edge retention that manufacturers continue to refine.

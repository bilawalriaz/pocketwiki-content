# Tashkent

## Overview
Tashkent is the capital and largest city of Uzbekistan, serving as the country's economic, cultural, and political center. As the most populous city in Central Asia, it has been a historic crossroads on the Silk Road, shaped by successive empires and modern development. Its significance lies in its role as a regional hub for trade, governance, and urban modernization, though it faces ongoing environmental and infrastructural challenges.

## Timeline
- **9th–4th centuries BC** — Burgulik culture inhabits the Tashkent oasis; hill fort Shashtepa is built.
- **2nd century BC** — Settlement exists near the Chirchik River, possibly part of the Kangju state.
- **5th–3rd centuries BC** — Square citadel of Chach is constructed south of the Syr Darya.
- **Early 8th century** — Chach is conquered by the Umayyad Caliphate.
- **1219** — City is destroyed by Genghis Khan during the Mongol conquest.
- **14th–15th centuries** — Tashkent is restored and becomes part of Timur's empire.
- **1784** — Yunus Khoja creates an independent Tashkent state.
- **1809** — Tashkent is annexed by the Khanate of Kokand.
- **May 1865** — City is captured by Russian forces under Mikhail Chernyayev.
- **April 1918** — Tashkent becomes the capital of the Turkestan Autonomous Soviet Socialist Republic.
- **26 April 1966** — A major earthquake destroys much of the old city; it is rebuilt as a model Soviet city.
- **1991** — Tashkent becomes the capital of independent Uzbekistan.

## Body

### Early History and Etymology
The city's name derives from Turkic *tash* and Persian *kent*, meaning "Stone City." Its ancient name, Chach, may trace to Old Iranian roots related to water or stone. Archaeological evidence shows the Burgulik culture (Late Bronze and Early Iron Ages) as the first known occupants, living in basement huts and practicing animal husbandry. By the 7th century AD, Chach had over 30 towns and a trade network between Sogdian and Turkic peoples.

### Islamic and Mongol Periods
Chach was conquered by the Umayyad Caliphate in the early 8th century, introducing Islamic influence. Under the Samanid Empire, the city was called Binkath, while the region retained the name Chach. After the 11th century, the name evolved into Tashkent. The city was destroyed by Genghis Khan in 1219, leading to a significant population loss.

### Timurid to Kokand Rule
Under Timur (14th–15th centuries), Tashkent was restored as a strategic center for scholarship and trade. It later passed through Shaybanid and Kazakh rule before becoming an independent state in 1784. In 1809, it was annexed by the Khanate of Kokand, which taxed it heavily but benefited from Russian trade.

### Russian and Soviet Eras
In 1865, Russian forces captured Tashkent, making it the capital of Russian Turkestan. The city became a center for espionage during the Great Game between Russia and Britain. After the 1917 Russian Revolution, it became the capital of the Turkestan ASSR. During World War II, factories were relocated to Tashkent, boosting its industry and population. The 1966 earthquake destroyed much of the old city; it was rebuilt with Soviet urban planning, including wide streets and apartment blocks.

### Modern Tashkent
Since independence in 1991, Tashkent has retained a multi-ethnic population, with Uzbeks forming the majority. It contributes 19% of Uzbekistan's GDP and leads in healthcare, education, and infrastructure. The city faces air pollution challenges and has implemented a master plan through 2045 for balanced development. Tourism has increased due to visa reforms, and the city hosts modern business districts and cultural sites.

## Terms
- **Silk Road**: Ancient network of trade routes connecting East and West.
- **Sogdian**: An Iranian people who were prominent traders along the Silk Road.
- **Kangju**: A Central Asian kingdom mentioned in Chinese sources, existing from about 140 BC to the 5th century AD.
- **Karakhanids**: A Turkic dynasty that ruled parts of Central Asia from the 9th to 13th centuries.
- **Timurid Empire**: A Persianate Turco-Mongol empire founded by Timur in the 14th century.
- **Shaybanid dynasty**: A Uzbek dynasty that ruled Central Asia in the 16th century.
- **Basmachi**: A resistance movement against Soviet rule in Central Asia in the early 20th century.
- **Turkestan ASSR**: An autonomous Soviet republic established in 1918, with Tashkent as its capital.
- **Tashkent Metro**: The first metro system in Central Asia, opened in 1977, known for its ornate stations.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The identification of Ptolemy's "Stone Tower" with Tashkent is debated among scholars.
- The etymology of the city's ancient name, Chach, has multiple proposed origins, including Iranian and Turkic roots.
- The cause of Indian Prime Minister Lal Bahadur Shastri's death in Tashkent in 1966 is speculated to be poisoning, though officially attributed to a heart attack.

# Mollusca

## Overview
Mollusca is the second-largest animal phylum (after Arthropoda), with roughly 86,600 recognized extant species and an estimated 60,000–100,000 fossil species. They dominate marine ecosystems (23% of named marine organisms) but have also colonized freshwater and terrestrial habitats. The phylum is defined by four universal features: a soft, muscular body; a mantle with a cavity for respiration and excretion; a radula (a chitinous, tooth-bearing "tongue" absent only in bivalves); and a specific nervous system structure. Despite this shared ground plan, molluscs exhibit extreme morphological disparity across 7–8 classes (two extinct), ranging from neurologically advanced cephalopods (squid, octopuses) to the species-rich gastropods (snails, slugs; 80% of species) and filter-feeding bivalves. Their evolutionary origins from the ancestral Lophotrochozoa and the relationships among classes remain vigorously debated. Humans have long exploited molluscs for food, luxury goods (pearls, Tyrian purple, sea silk), and currency, while a few species pose hazards as venomous biters, disease vectors (schistosomiasis), or invasive agricultural pests.

## Timeline
- **c. 555 Ma** — *Kimberella* appears; interpreted by some as "mollusc-like," by others as a probable bilaterian.
- **c. 540–530 Ma** — Helcionellids (early snail-like shelled molluscs) appear in Early Cambrian rocks, predating trilobites.
- **c. 500 Ma** — Good evidence for stem-lineage gastropods (*Aldanella*), cephalopods (*Plectronoceras*), and bivalves (*Pojetaia*, *Fordilla*) in the middle Cambrian.
- **488–443 Ma (Ordovician)** — "Modern-looking" bivalves appear.
- **c. 65 Ma (end Cretaceous)** — All externally shelled cephalopods except nautiloids go extinct; shell-less Coleoidea (squid, octopus, cuttlefish) survive and diversify.
- **20th–18th c. BC** — Minoans possibly pioneer extraction of Tyrian purple from murex shells (Middle Minoan period).
- **1990s** — Invasive giant African snail (*Achatina fulica*) reaches the West Indies; biocontrol attempt with *Euglandina rosea* backfires, extirpating native snails.
- **2004** — IUCN Red List includes nearly 2,000 endangered non-marine molluscs (vs. 41 marine); ~42% of recorded extinctions since 1500 are molluscs (mostly non-marine).
- **2005** — China accounts for 80% of global mollusc catch (~11 million tonnes).

## Body

### Definition and Body Plan
Molluscs are unsegmented, bilaterally symmetrical protostomes. Because modern groups vary radically, textbooks describe a **hypothetical ancestral mollusc (HAM)**: a single, limpet-like shell secreted by a dorsal mantle covering the upper surface; a ventral muscular foot; and a visceral mass (visceropallium) containing organs. The mantle overlaps the body to form a **mantle cavity** housing the anus, genitals, gills (ctenidia), nephridia (kidneys), and osphradia (chemical sensors). The shell comprises three layers: an outer organic periostracum (conchiolin), a middle prismatic calcite layer, and an inner nacreous laminated calcite layer (often aragonite). Molluscs never use phosphate for hard parts (except questionably in *Cobcrephora*). The coelom is reduced to small spaces around the heart and gonads; the main body cavity is a **hemocoel** (open circulatory system) through which blood (hemolymph) circulates, also functioning as a hydrostatic skeleton.

### Organ Systems
**Feeding & Digestion:** Most molluscs possess a **radula**—a ribbon of chitinous teeth on a cartilaginous odontophore, unique to the phylum—used to scrape algae/bacteria or rasp tissue. Mucus glands and cilia form a "food string" that transports particles to the stomach. A rotating **prostyle** (cone of feces/mucus) winds the string; cilia sort particles: small minerals to the prostyle for excretion, larger food to the cecum for digestion. Bivalves lack a radula; they use labial palps to collect mucus-trapped detritus. Carnivores have simpler guts.  
**Circulation:** Primarily open (hemocoel), except cephalopods (closed). Blood uses copper-based **hemocyanin** for oxygen transport (colorless when deoxygenated, blue when oxygenated); exception: *Planorbidae* (ram's horn snails) use iron-based hemoglobin. The heart has 1+ pairs of atria (auricles) receiving oxygenated blood from gills, and a ventricle pumping into a short aorta opening into the hemocoel. Atria also filter waste into the coelom; metanephridia reclaim useful materials and dump waste into the mantle cavity.  
**Respiration:** Typically one pair of feather-like gills (ctenidia) in the mantle cavity. Cilia drive water current (in bottom, out top) and clean gills. Osphradia can halt ciliary beating if noxious chemicals/sediment are detected.  
**Nervous System:** **Cephalic** molluscs (gastropods, cephalopods) have two main nerve cord pairs (visceral, pedal) with paired ganglia (cerebral, pleural, visceral above esophagus; pedal below) linked by commissures. Pedal and cerebral connectives form a **circumesophageal nerve ring**. **Acephalic** bivalves have three ganglia pairs (cerebral, pedal, visceral); the large visceral ganglion is the main processing center. Some scallops have mantle-edge eyes connected to looped nerves for light/shadow detection.  
**Reproduction:** Primitive state: dioecious, external fertilization. Gonads shed gametes into coelom; nephridia extract and release them into mantle cavity. Eggs hatch as **trochophore larvae** (planktonic, two ciliary bands for feeding/swimming), often followed by a **veliger stage** (protrotroch becomes ciliated velum for swimming), then settlement and metamorphosis. Cephalopods exhibit **direct development** (hatchling = miniaturized adult). Variations include hermaphroditism and internal fertilization.

### Diversity and Classification
Estimates of described living species range 50,000–120,000; total living estimated ~200,000. **Gastropoda** (snails, slugs) dominate at ~80% of species. **Bivalvia** (clams, mussels) ~14%. **Cephalopoda** (squid, octopus, cuttlefish, nautilus) are neurologically advanced; giant/colossal squid are largest extant invertebrates. Other classes: **Polyplacophora** (chitons, 8 shell plates), **Scaphopoda** (tusk shells), **Monoplacophora** (cap-like shells, segmented organs), **Aplacophora** (worm-like, spicules, no shell: Caudofoveata, Solenogastres). Two classes extinct: **Rostroconchia**, **Helcionelloida** (possibly stem gastropods).  
**Phylogeny** is highly contentious. Mollusca sits within **Lophotrochozoa** (trochophore larvae, lophophore in some). Traditional split: **Conchifera** (shelled: gastropods, bivalves, cephalopods, scaphopods, monoplacophorans) vs. **Aculifera** (spiculate: chitons, aplacophorans). Competing hypotheses: **Testaria** (chitons + conchiferans), **Serialia** (chitons + monoplacophorans). Molecular vs. morphological analyses conflict; a 2009 study even suggested Mollusca is polyphyletic (scaphopods, bivalves separate), but a 2010 analysis recovered monophyly citing contaminated data. Current data insufficient to resolve deep nodes; methods prone to overestimating clade confidence.

### Evolutionary History
**Ediacaran/Early Cambrian:** *Kimberella* (555 Ma) debated as mollusc-like or basal bilaterian. *Wiwaxia* (505 Ma) debated: radula-like feeding apparatus vs. polychaete-like. Microfossils (515–510 Ma) suggest genuine radula fragments, contradicting a mineralized ancestral radula.  
**Cambrian Explosion:** **Helcionellids** (>540 Ma, Siberia/China) are earliest probable shelled molluscs (snail-like, predate trilobites). **Stem gastropods** (*Aldanella*), **stem cephalopods** (*Plectronoceras* – septa, siphuncle, but benthic with ballast), **stem bivalves** (*Fordilla*, *Pojetaia*) appear ~500 Ma. Torsion (gut twisting) in early gastropods is unclear in fossils. *Volborthella* (pre-530 Ma) once thought cephalopod; shell built from silica grains, not secreted. *Nectocaris* reinterpreted as chaetognath relative.  
**Post-Cambrian:** Modern bivalves in Ordovician (488–443 Ma). Rudists (bivalves) major Cretaceous reef-builders; extinct at K-Pg boundary (65 Ma). All shelled cephalopods except nautiloids extinct by 65 Ma; Coleoidea thrive. **Hyolitha** (shell + operculum) possibly molluscs; some assign separate phylum.

### Human Interaction
**Food & Fisheries:** Major protein source since anatomically modern humans. 2005: China 80% of catch (~11 Mt). Bivalves (clams, mussels, oysters, scallops), cephalopods, gastropods (whelks) commonly eaten. Overfishing common. Regulations manage toxin accumulation (e.g., algal bloom biotoxins).  
**Luxury Goods:** **Pearls** from nacre-lined shells (bivalves, some gastropods); best from *Pinctada* pearl oysters (Pacific). Cultured via "seed" (ground freshwater mussel shell – overharvesting endangers US mussels) or bead insertion. **Tyrian purple** from murex ink glands (weight in silver, 4th c. BC); Minoans may have pioneered 20th–18th c. BC. **Sea silk** from byssus threads of *Pinna nobilis*; reserved for rulers (Procopius, 550 CE). **Shell money** (cowries, etc.) in preindustrial societies; non-standardized, status-display or exchange media, vulnerable to transport-driven inflation.  
**Bioindicators:** Bivalves (sessile) monitor aquatic health (population, physiology, contamination). *Potamopyrgus antipodarum* tests estrogen-mimicking pollutants. *Crassostrea virginica*, *Dreissena polymorpha*, *Mytilus edulis* indicate DNA-damaging stress.  
**Hazards:** **Venom:** All octopuses venomous; blue-ringed (*Hapalochlaena*, Australia/New Guinea) 25% human fatality. *Octopus apollyon* inflammation >1 month; *O. rubescens* necrosis >1 month. **Cone snails** (Conidae): all venomous; complex toxin cocktails (fast/slow); precise neurotoxins valuable for neurology research; easily synthesized. Deaths <10% of jellyfish fatalities. **Disease:** **Schistosomiasis** (bilharzia/snail fever): fluke *Schistosoma* uses freshwater snails as intermediate hosts; ~200 million infected in 74 countries (100M Africa); second only to malaria in tropical parasitic burden. **Pests:** Snails/slugs damage crops. Invasive *Achatina fulica* (giant African snail) spread across Asia, Indian/Pacific islands, West Indies (1990s). Biocontrol with *Euglandina rosea* failed—predator ignored target, extirpated native snails.

## Terms
- ****Mantle (pallium)**** — Dorsal body wall that secretes shell/spicules and forms a cavity (mantle cavity) housing gills, anus, gonads, nephridia.
- ****Radula**** — Chitinous, tooth-bearing ribbon on a cartilaginous odontophore; unique to Mollusca (absent in bivalves); used for scraping/rasping food.
- ****Hemocoel**** — Primary body cavity in open circulatory systems; blood (hemolymph) bathes organs directly; also acts as hydrostatic skeleton.
- ****Hemocyanin**** — Copper-based respiratory pigment in molluscan blood (blue when oxygenated); exception: *Planorbidae* use hemoglobin.
- ****Trochophore larva**** — Planktonic, ciliated larva (two equatorial bands) typical of Lophotrochozoa; feeds on suspended particles; often first molluscan stage.
- ****Veliger larva**** — Later larval stage in many molluscs; prototroch develops into ciliated velum (swimming lobes); precedes settlement/metamorphosis.
- ****Torsion**** — 180° twisting of visceral mass in gastropod development, bringing anus/mantle cavity over head; defining but debated in early fossils.
- ****Nacre (mother of pearl)**** — Inner iridescent shell layer of laminated aragonite/conchiolin; source of valuable pearls.
- ****Byssus**** — Silky protein threads secreted by some bivalves (e.g., *Pinna nobilis*) for attachment; source of sea silk.
- ****Lophotrochozoa**** — Major protostome clade defined by trochophore larvae and/or lophophore feeding structure; includes Mollusca, Annelida, and seven marine phyla.

## Debates and open questions
- **Phylogeny:** Relationships among the 7–8 classes remain unresolved. Competing hypotheses (Conchifera/Aculifera, Testaria, Serialia) receive conflicting support from morphological vs. molecular data. A 2009 study suggested polyphyly (Scaphopoda, Bivalvia separate); 2010 study recovered monophyly but cited data contamination. Current methods may overestimate clade confidence.
- **Ancestral state:** Was the last common ancestor **metameric** (segmented)? Giribet et al. (2006) say repeated gills/retractor muscles are later; Sigwart (2007) argues ancestral mollusc was metameric with creeping foot and mineralized shell.
- **Shell origin:** Did the conchiferan shell evolve from aplacophoran spicules? Embryological origins of spicules vs. shell fields complicate this.
- **Early fossils:** *Kimberella* (555 Ma): mollusc-like or basal bilaterian? *Wiwaxia* (505 Ma): mollusc (radula?) or polychaete relative? Helcionellids: earliest gastropods or stem molluscs? Torsion evidence in Early Cambrian gastropods is ambiguous.
- **Cephalopod origins:** *Volborthella* (silica shell) and *Nectocaris* (chaetognath?) are not cephalopods. *Plectronoceras* (Late Cambrian) is earliest undisputed cephalopod (septa, siphuncle) but was benthic, not nektonic.
- **Conservation bias:** Non-marine molluscs suffer vastly higher extinction rates (42% of extinctions since 1500) and endangerment (2,000+ on 2004 Red List vs. 41 marine), yet receive far less study; many regions unsurveyed, specialist shortage acute.

# Flavius Aetius

## Overview
Flavius Aetius (c. 390–454) was the dominant Roman general and statesman of the Western Empire for two decades (433–454). He is best known for assembling a coalition that defeated Attila the Hun at the Battle of the Catalaunian Plains in 451, an achievement that has led to him being called the "Last of the Romans." His career and assassination highlight the fragility of the late Western Empire, which rapidly collapsed after his death.

## Timeline
- **c. 390** — Born in Durostorum, Moesia Secunda.
- **405–408** — Held as a hostage at the courts of the Visigoths and Huns.
- **425** — Serves the usurper Joannes; after Joannes's defeat, compromises with the new regime.
- **432** — Defeated by Bonifacius at Rimini; flees to the Huns for support.
- **433** — Returns to power, beginning his two-decade dominance.
- **436–437** — Defeats the Burgundians; sends Hunnic foederati to destroy them.
- **451** — Commands the Roman-Visigothic coalition at the Battle of the Catalaunian Plains against Attila.
- **452** — Unable to stop Attila's invasion of Italy; Attila withdraws after meeting Pope Leo I.
- **21 September 454** — Assassinated by Emperor Valentinian III in Ravenna.
- **16 March 455** — Valentinian III is assassinated in revenge by Aetius's followers.

## Body

### Rise to Power and Early Campaigns
Aetius was born to a Roman general and a wealthy aristocratic woman. His youth as a hostage among the Visigoths and Huns provided him with military experience and crucial barbarian contacts. After the death of Emperor Honorius in 423, Aetius supported the usurper Joannes, fetching Hunnic mercenaries. Following Joannes's defeat, he skillfully negotiated with the new regent, Galla Placidia, trading his Hunnic army for the command in Gaul. He spent the following years campaigning successfully against the Visigoths, Franks, and other groups in Gaul and on the Danube frontier.

### Dominance and Civil Conflict
Aetius's rise involved intense rivalry with other powerful generals, notably Bonifacius and Felix. After engineering Felix's death in 430, Aetius faced Bonifacius, who was recalled by the court and given senior command. In 432, Aetius was stripped of his post and defeated by Bonifacius at Rimini. He fled to his Hunnic allies and returned to power in 433, marking the start of his unchallenged dominance. He then marginalized Bonifacius's faction, exiled his successor, and married Bonifacius's widow.

### Management of Gaul and the Hunnic Threat
From 433 to 454, Aetius was the effective ruler of the Western Empire, managing a complex web of barbarian federates (foederati). He settled groups like the Burgundians and Alans within the empire to secure borders and suppress revolts, such as those of the Bagaudae. His greatest challenge came from Attila the Hun. In 451, Aetius forged a remarkable coalition of Visigoths, Franks, and others to defeat Attila at the Catalaunian Plains. In 452, he could not prevent Attila's invasion of Italy but helped coordinate the diplomatic and military pressure that led to the Hun's withdrawal.

### Assassination and Aftermath
Despite his power, Aetius was vulnerable to court intrigue. Emperor Valentinian III, feeling threatened, conspired with senator Petronius Maximus and the chamberlain Heraclius. On 21 September 454, Valentinian personally killed Aetius during a meeting. This act was widely condemned; a contemporary reportedly told the emperor, "You have cut off your right hand with your left." In March 455, Valentinian himself was assassinated by Aetius's followers, Optila and Thraustila. The empire descended into chaos and was sacked by the Vandals later that year.

## Terms
*   **Foederati:** Barbarian groups (e.g., Huns, Visigoths) who provided military service to the Roman Empire under treaty agreements.
*   **Magister Militum:** "Master of the Soldiers," a high-ranking military commander in the late Roman Empire.
*   **Patricius:** "Patrician," the highest honorific title for a non-emperor, often held by the most powerful general or statesman.
*   **Bagaudae:** Peasant rebels or bandits who revolted in Gaul and Hispania during the 3rd–5th centuries.
*   **Federate:** A barbarian leader or group allied with Rome by a formal treaty (*foedus*).
*   **Usurper:** A person who takes imperial power without legal authority, like the emperor Joannes.
*   **Limes:** The fortified frontier borders of the Roman Empire.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **The Battle's Decisiveness:** Historians debate whether the Battle of the Catalaunian Plains was a truly decisive Roman victory or merely a tactical check. J. B. Bury argued it was not decisive, as Attila retreated from Gaul anyway and the more important Hunnic defeat came later at the Battle of Nedao. Others, like Arther Ferrill, see it as a crucial victory that shattered Attila's aura of invincibility.
*   **Aetius's Legacy:** Scholars compare Aetius to the earlier general Stilicho, noting both left the empire weakened. Critics point to his failure to retake Africa from the Vandals and mixed success in Spain. Defenders argue he was managing impossible problems and that the empire's rapid collapse after his death proves his stabilizing importance.
*   **Motives for the 452 Italian Campaign:** The reasons for Attila's withdrawal from Italy in 452 are debated. While medieval sources credit Pope Leo I's intercession and divine intervention, modern historians emphasize practical factors like disease, logistical problems, and military harassment by Aetius and the Eastern Emperor Marcian.

# Illustration

## Overview
Illustration is the art of creating visual images—drawings, paintings, or digital designs—to explain, decorate, or interpret a text, concept, or process for integration into media like books, magazines, websites, and films. It is a functional art form that has evolved alongside printing and publishing technologies, serving both aesthetic and informational purposes.

## Timeline
- **c. 1294–1279 BC** — Illustrations exist from the Tomb of Pharaoh Seti I in ancient Egypt.
- **15th century** — Invention of the printing press leads to wider distribution of books illustrated with woodcuts.
- **1600s** — Ukiyo-e, an influential Japanese illustration style, originates.
- **18th century** — William Blake uses relief etching for illustration in England.
- **Early 19th century** — Introduction of lithography improves reproduction quality.
- **1841** — The British magazine *Punch* is founded, later coining the term "cartoon" for a humorous illustration.
- **c. 1880s–early 1900s** — The "Golden Age of Illustration" occurs in America, driven by advances in printing.
- **2006** — Norman Rockwell's painting "Breaking Home Ties" sells at auction for US$15.4 million. |

## Body
### Styles and Applications
Contemporary illustration employs a wide range of techniques, including drawing, painting, printmaking, collage, and digital design. Its style can be expressive, stylized, realistic, or technical, depending on the purpose. Specialist areas include book illustration, concept art for games, information graphics, and medical or scientific illustration.

### Technical and Scientific Illustration
This specialized field communicates technical or scientific information through images like diagrams, exploded views, and reconstructions. Its primary aim is to convey complex information clearly to a nontechnical audience, enhancing understanding and interest. Modern practice often uses 2D and 3D software for accurate, reusable visuals. Professional organizations exist for practitioners, with median salaries reported around $70,000–$72,000.

### Illustration as Fine Art
Historically, illustration was sometimes considered less important than graphic design or fine art. However, its status has risen due to growth in industries like graphic novels and video games, and its increased use in publications. Original illustration art now commands high prices at auction, as seen with works by artists like Norman Rockwell and pinup illustrators.

### Historical Development
The art of illustration is closely linked to printing and publishing. Early examples include medieval illuminated manuscripts and ancient Egyptian hieroglyphs. The 15th-century printing press enabled wider book distribution with woodcut illustrations. In 17th-century Japan, Ukiyo-e woodblock printing flourished. In Europe, engraving and etching were dominant until lithography improved quality in the 19th century.

The 19th century saw illustrators like John Leech and George Cruikshank contribute to satirical and serious publications. The magazine *Punch* (1841–2002) was particularly influential, employing well-regarded illustrators and shifting from caricature to topical observation. The "Golden Age of Illustration" in America (c. 1880s–early 1900s) was fueled by advances in printing technology, which increased production and reduced costs. This era produced iconic illustrators like N.C. Wyeth and Maxfield Parrish, whose work reflected American aspirations.

## Terms
- **Illustration**: A visual image created to explain, decorate, or interpret a text or concept for media.
- **Illumination**: The medieval term for hand-drawn and painted illustrations in codices.
- **Woodcut**: An early printmaking technique using a carved wooden block.
- **Lithography**: A printing method from the early 19th century that improved reproduction quality.
- **Ukiyo-e**: A Japanese illustration style from the 1600s characterized by expressive line and vivid color.
- **Cartoon**: A term for a humorous illustration, first used by the magazine *Punch*.
- **Golden Age of Illustration**: A period in America (c. 1880s–early 1900s) of high achievement in illustration, driven by printing advances.
- **Technical illustration**: Images designed to convey complex technical or scientific information clearly.

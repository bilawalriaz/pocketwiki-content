# Enugu (city)

## Overview
Enugu is a major city in southeastern Nigeria, serving as the capital of Enugu State. Founded in the early 20th century as a British colonial coal-mining hub, it became a pivotal center for Igbo culture, politics, and the struggle for Nigerian independence. Its history, from pre-colonial settlements through the Nigerian Civil War, has shaped its identity as the "Coal City" and a cultural heartland of Igboland.

## Timeline
- **Pre-1901** — Area settled by Nike people, allied with the Aro Confederacy which controlled local slave trade.
- **1901-1902** — British defeat the Aro Confederacy, ending its control and opening the hinterland.
- **1909** — Coal discovered under the village of Enugwu Ngwo by British geologists.
- **1915** — British begin acquiring land for a colliery and the Eastern Line railway.
- **1917** — Enugu officially gains township status.
- **1928** — City renamed from Enugwu-Ngwo to Enugu.
- **1938** — Enugu becomes the administrative capital of the Eastern Region.
- **18 November 1949** — British police massacre striking miners in the Iva Valley Shooting, fueling nationalist sentiment.
- **30 May 1967** — Enugu declared the capital of the short-lived Republic of Biafra.
- **4 October 1967** — Nigerian military captures Enugu during the civil war.
- **27 August 1991** — Enugu becomes the capital of the newly created Enugu State.

## Body

### Early History and Colonial Foundation
The area was first settled by the Nike people, who acquired land through slave raiding and were allied with the Aro Confederacy, a powerful Igbo organization that controlled regional slave trading. British military campaigns ended the Aro Confederacy by 1902. In 1909, British geologists discovered commercially viable coal deposits. To exploit this resource, the British acquired land, built the Enugu Coal Camp, and constructed the Eastern Line railway to Port Harcourt for coal export. This industrialization led to rapid settlement growth, with distinct European and African residential areas established by 1917.

### Industrialization and Political Significance
Enugu became West Africa's only significant coal-mining area. The harsh colonial working conditions led to labor tensions, culminating in the 1949 Iva Valley Shooting, where British police killed 21 striking miners. This event galvanized the "Zikist" nationalist movement and accelerated calls for independence. After Nigerian independence in 1960, Enugu served as the capital of the Eastern Region. During the Nigerian Civil War (1967-1970), it was declared the capital of the secessionist Republic of Biafra and housed Radio Biafra before being captured by federal forces in October 1967.

### Post-War Development and Modern City
After the war, Enugu resumed as a state capital, eventually becoming the capital of Enugu State in 1991. While coal mining was once the economic driver, the industry declined after the civil war due to damaged infrastructure. The city's economy now includes manufacturing, services, and a growing tech startup ecosystem. Enugu is a major educational hub, home to institutions like the University of Nigeria and the Institute of Management and Technology. It is also a center for Igbo culture, hosting major festivals like the Mmanwu and New Yam festivals, and has a vibrant arts and film scene.

## Terms
*   **Aro Confederacy:** A powerful Igbo political and economic organization (c. 1690–1901) that dominated trade, including the slave trade, in southeastern Nigeria.
*   **Zikism:** A post-World War II Nigerian nationalist movement inspired by Nnamdi Azikiwe, advocating for independence from British rule.
*   **Iva Valley Shooting:** The 1949 massacre of striking coal miners by British police in Enugu, a key event that fueled Nigerian nationalism.
*   **Republic of Biafra:** The short-lived secessionist state in southeastern Nigeria (1967–1970), for which Enugu served as the initial capital.
*   **Mmanwu Festival:** An annual Igbo festival in Enugu featuring parades of traditional masquerades, supported by the Enugu Council of Arts and Culture.
*   **Face-me-I-face-you:** A colloquial term for low-rent, one-bedroom flats arranged in a square around a common compound entrance.
*   **Okada:** A common term for motorcycle taxis used for public transportation in many Nigerian cities, including Enugu until a 2009 ban.
*   **Coal City Cabs:** A 2009 Enugu State government initiative to provide registered taxis on loan to unemployed citizens to alleviate poverty.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Population Figures:** The 2006 census population estimate for the Enugu metropolitan area (722,664) is disputed, with accusations of population inflation and deflation in favor of northern Nigeria.
*   **Economic Transition:** The city's economy has struggled to fully transition from its historical dependence on coal mining, which is no longer a major income source.
*   **Infrastructure Challenges:** Despite recent investments, Enugu's water supply infrastructure has not kept pace with population growth, and reticulation remains a challenge due to aged pipes.

# Kolkata

## Overview
Kolkata (formerly Calcutta) is the capital of West Bengal, India, and a major cultural, historical, and economic hub in eastern India. Once the de facto capital of British India, it is renowned for its colonial architecture, intellectual heritage, and role in the Indian independence movement. The city's fortunes have shifted from pre-eminence to post-independence stagnation and recent economic revitalization.

## Timeline
- **1690** — East India Company establishes a trading post in the villages of Kalikata, Sutanuti, and Govindapur.
- **1756-57** — Nawab Siraj ud-Daulah captures Fort William; British recapture the city after the Battle of Plassey (1757).
- **1773** — Calcutta becomes the headquarters of the East India Company.
- **1905** — Partition of Bengal along religious lines sparks mass protests.
- **1911** — British capital of India is moved from Calcutta to New Delhi.
- **1943** — Bengal famine causes millions of deaths.
- **1947** — Partition of India leads to massive demographic shifts and refugee influx.
- **1971** — City hosts the government-in-exile of Bangladesh during its Liberation War.
- **1977-2011** — Left Front (Communist-led) government rules West Bengal from Kolkata.
- **2001** — Official name changed from Calcutta to Kolkata.

## Body

### Historical Development
The area was inhabited for millennia, with early villages like Kalikata mentioned in 15th-century texts. The modern city began with the East India Company's trading license in 1690. After the Battle of Plassey (1757), British control solidified, and Calcutta became the Company's headquarters by 1773. It served as the de facto capital of British India until 1911. The city was a centre of the Bengal Renaissance and the Indian nationalist movement. Following the 1947 partition, Kolkata absorbed a massive influx of Hindu refugees from East Bengal (now Bangladesh), transforming its demographics and politics. Decades of political violence, strikes, and economic stagnation followed, earning it the label "dying city" in the 1980s. Economic recovery began after India's 1990s market liberalization.

### Geography and Climate
Kolkata lies on the eastern bank of the Hooghly River in the lower Ganges Delta, with low elevation (1.5–9 m). Much of the city was reclaimed from wetlands; the remaining East Kolkata Wetlands are a Ramsar-designated site. The climate is tropical savanna (Aw), with hot, humid summers (March–June) and mild winters (Dec–Jan). Annual rainfall is about 1,850 mm, mostly from the southwest monsoon (June–September). The city is in seismic zone III and faces high cyclone risk.

### Demographics and Society
The 2011 census recorded a city population of about 4.5 million, with a metropolitan population over 15 million. Bengali is the dominant language, with English widely used in professional contexts. The population is predominantly Hindu (76.51%), with a significant Muslim minority (20.60%). The city is demographically diverse, with historic communities including Chinese, Armenian, and Anglo-Indian groups. Social life is characterized by close-knit neighbourhoods (*paras*) and leisurely intellectual conversations (*adda*).

### Economy and Infrastructure
Kolkata is the commercial hub of eastern India. Once India's leading city, its economy declined post-independence due to industrial decay and political instability. Revival began in the 1990s, with growth in IT, manufacturing, and services. The informal sector employs over 40% of the labour force. Key institutions include the Calcutta Stock Exchange and the Port of Kolkata (India's oldest operating port). Transport includes the Kolkata Metro (India's oldest), a suburban railway network, trams, and the Netaji Subhas Chandra Bose International Airport.

### Governance and Services
The city is administered by the Kolkata Municipal Corporation (KMC). It is the seat of the West Bengal government and the Calcutta High Court (India's oldest). The Kolkata Police district has a crime rate below the national average. Utility services include water from the Hooghly River and electricity supplied primarily by the private CESC. The city faces challenges with air pollution and inadequate sewerage in some areas.

### Culture and Education
Kolkata is regarded as India's cultural capital, with a legacy in literature, cinema (Tollywood), and the arts. It hosts the Indian Museum, Victoria Memorial, and the University of Calcutta (South Asia's first modern university, 1857). The city is a centre for Bengali literature and the Bengal Renaissance. Major festivals include Durga Puja (UNESCO-recognized). Education is provided in Bengali and English, with numerous schools and universities, including the Indian Institute of Management Calcutta.

## Terms
- **Adda**: Freestyle, leisurely intellectual conversation, a characteristic social practice in Kolkata.
- **Bhadralok**: The educated, urban Bengali middle class, historically influential in the city's culture and politics.
- **Bengali Renaissance**: A 19th-20th century cultural, social, and intellectual movement in Bengal, centered in Kolkata.
- **Fort William**: The British East India Company's fort, completed in 1712, which became the nucleus of colonial Calcutta.
- **Hooghly River**: A distributary of the Ganges, on whose eastern bank Kolkata is situated.
- **Kolkata Metropolitan Area (KMA)**: The urban agglomeration encompassing Kolkata and its suburbs, with a population over 15 million.
- **Naxalite**: A radical Maoist communist movement active in Kolkata during the 1960s-70s, contributing to political violence.
- **Paras**: Close-knit neighbourhoods in Kolkata, each with a strong community identity.
- **Tollywood**: The Bengali-language film industry based in Kolkata's Tollygunge area.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Founder of Kolkata**: Job Charnock was traditionally regarded as the founder, but a 2003 Calcutta High Court ruling stated the city has no single founder.
- **Economic Trajectory**: Scholars debate the causes and timeline of Kolkata's post-independence economic decline and the effectiveness of its recent revival.
- **Cultural Capital Status**: While widely called India's "cultural capital," the basis and contemporary relevance of this title are subjects of discussion.
- **Urban Challenges**: The city's ability to address issues like air pollution, inadequate infrastructure, and slum conditions while sustaining economic growth remains a key question.

# Parenting

## Overview
Parenting encompasses the physical, cognitive, social, emotional, and educational support of a child from infancy to adulthood, performed by biological parents or diverse caregivers such as relatives, guardians, or state institutions. Research identifies distinct parenting styles—authoritative, authoritarian, permissive, and uninvolved—defined by varying levels of responsiveness and demandingness, with authoritative parenting generally linked to the most positive child outcomes. Cultural values, socioeconomic status, and parental history profoundly shape practices, while specific skills like consistency, communication, and emotional regulation mediate long-term developmental trajectories across the lifespan.

## Timeline
- **1960s–1970s** — Diana Baumrind proposes three parenting styles (authoritative, authoritarian, permissive); later expanded to four with uninvolved style.
- **1983** — Baumrind finds children in authoritarian homes are less cheerful, moodier, and more vulnerable to stress.
- **Post-WWII** — Permissive parenting becomes more popular in middle-class families.
- **2005** — Alfie Kohn publishes *Unconditional Parenting*, popularizing the concept of love as a gift rather than a reward.
- **2014** — US data shows women spend ~1 hour/day on physical care for children under 6; men spend 23 minutes.
- **Contemporary** — Meta-analyses confirm ethnic-racial socialization positively correlates with psychosocial well-being in children of color; parent training programs (PCIT, Triple P, PMT) validated for improving skills and child conduct.

## Body

### Parenting Styles
Developmental psychologist Diana Baumrind’s typology categorizes styles along two dimensions: **responsiveness** (warmth/support) and **demandingness** (control/expectations).
*   **Authoritative ("just right"):** Medium demands, medium responsiveness. Parents use positive reinforcement, support autonomy within limits, and maintain give-and-take communication. Linked to higher competence, mental health, and social development. Critiques note "firm control" risks conflating structure with coercive power assertion.
*   **Authoritarian:** High demands, low responsiveness. Rigid rules enforced via punishment (often corporal) without explanation. Associated with lower cheerfulness, higher stress vulnerability, passive hostility, and negative educational/career impacts. More common in working-class families.
*   **Permissive:** Low demands, high responsiveness. High value on freedom; few rules or punishments. Children often happy but may lack self-control and self-reliance. Critics argue categorization blurs "confused" parents with deliberately democratic ones.
*   **Uninvolved/Neglectful:** Low demands, low responsiveness. Emotional/physical absence; survival needs met but little engagement. Children suffer in social competence, academics, and psychosocial development, with higher deviant behavior risk.

**Additional Styles:**
*   **Intrusive:** Psychological control via guilt induction, love withdrawal, and manipulation. Linked to adolescent under-eating, risky cyber behavior, substance use, and depression.
*   **Unconditional (Alfie Kohn):** Focuses on whole child, problem-solving, and love as a gift; rejects rewards/punishments as conditional control.
*   **Trustful (Peter Gray):** Child-centered; trusts children to explore, play, and learn from mistakes. Argues this was dominant in hunter-gatherer societies, replaced by "directive-domineering" (agriculture/industry) and later "directive-protective" (late 20th century) styles.
*   **Material:** Uses possessions to express love or shape behavior (rewards/punishments). Research links it to potential adult reduced self-esteem, marital problems, and financial hardship.

### Parenting Practices and Skills
**Practices** are specific socialization behaviors (e.g., reading aloud, storytelling in oral-tradition cultures). **Skills** are broader competencies classified by the Canadian Council on Learning and others:
*   **Core domains:** Relationship skills (quality time, affection), encouraging desirable behavior (praise), teaching (modeling, role-play), managing misbehavior (firm limits, authoritative time-outs), anticipating/planning, self-regulation, mood/coping, and partner support.
*   **Consistency** is the "backbone"; **overprotection** is a key weakness.
*   **The Parenting Pyramid (Arbinger Institute):** Hierarchical foundation: Ways of Being → Spouse Relationship → Child Relationship → Teaching → Corrections. Prioritizing foundation makes corrections more receptive.

**Parent Training:** Group programs (PCIT, PMT, Triple P, Incredible Years, BEST) improve short-term parental psychosocial well-being and child conduct. **Jay Belsky’s Process Model** links parental well-being (work, social life, cognitions) to parenting quality; positive parental cognitions predict supportive parenting and child self-perceptions.

### Cultural Values
Culture defines "what is best" and interprets behaviors differently.
*   **Hunter-gatherer/Subsistence:** Early practical survival skills (e.g., knife use before age 1).
*   **Individualistic (e.g., Germany):** Face-to-face baby talk focused on the infant.
*   **Communal (e.g., West Africa):** Baby worn facing outward; talk focused on others.
*   **Value differences:** European Americans prize intellectual "book learning" (questions = intelligence); Italians value emotional competence (questions = social skill); Dutch value independence (questions = dependence).
*   **Shared prosocial goals:** Hispanic (respect, familism), East Asian (order, sometimes via psychological control), Kipsigis (Kenya: *ng/om*—responsible innovation), Sweden/Spain (sociality, happiness).

#### Indigenous American Cultures
*   **Tools:** Storytelling (myths preserve language, identity, animistic respect), *Consejos* (narrative advice fostering independence), educational teasing (fabricated threats for safety), nonverbal communication, observational learning.
*   **LOPI (Learning by Observing and Pitching In):** Children integrated into adult activities.
*   **Navajo:** "Respect" for all relations learned via nonverbal cues; early morning runs encouraged through community humor and gentle physical guidance (placing child in snow).
*   **Mayan:** Sacred activities (corn/tortillas) restricted but observed; girls practice movements on plastic, mastering skill without verbal instruction.
*   **Threats:** Forced conversion, land loss, and displacement diminished traditional techniques.

#### Ethnic-Racial Socialization (US Immigrants/Minorities)
Transmission of cultural resources to support children of color against racism. Dimensions: **Cultural socialization** (customs), **Preparation for bias** (coping with discrimination), **Promotion of mistrust** (caution toward other groups), **Egalitarianism** (emphasizing similarities). Meta-analyses show small positive correlations with psychosocial well-being (self-perceptions, interpersonal relationships), stronger for child reports than parent reports. Causation and bidirectionality require experimental study.

### Across the Lifespan
*   **Pre-pregnancy:** Family planning assesses finances, stability, readiness. ~40% of pregnancies unplanned globally. Preconception health (weight, anemia, infections) affects outcomes.
*   **Prenatal:** Maternal lifestyle (nutrition, activity, stress) affects fetal development. Disparities: wealthy mothers may overeat/rest; poor/abused mothers may be overworked/malnourished.
*   **Newborns/Infants:** Needs: food, sleep, comfort, cleaning. Crying is primary communication. **Attachment** (distinct from love) forms immediately; secure attachment predicts relationship success, self-esteem; disrupted attachment risks PTSD, oppositional-defiant disorder. Breastfeeding recommended; alternatives exist.
*   **Toddlers (12–36 months):** Mimicry, vocabulary building, emotion management, social etiquette. "Terrible Twos" tantrums stem from frustration/communication gaps. Parents guide via routines, not threats. Parental trauma history can impair regulation support. Gender gap: US mothers 1 hr/day physical care vs. fathers 23 min (2014).
*   **Childhood:** Growing independence, friendships, reasoning. Learning via play/household duties. Education decisions diverge (structured vs. free). Allowance teaches money responsibility. Consistent, explanatory discipline reduces later problems. Group behavioral interventions effective for conduct issues.
*   **Adolescence:** High risk/opportunity. Brain: emotional center mature; frontal cortex (rational control) not. Peer time increases; parent time decreases. Parents remain influential (e.g., on drinking). Trust built via behavioral control, monitoring, warmth, inductive reasoning, communication enables resistance to peer pressure and identity formation. Open adolescents granted more freedom.
*   **Adulthood:** Parenting continues (financial support, wisdom, inheritance). Grandparenting mirrors parenting. Role reversal: adult children care for elderly parents.

### Rights and Responsibilities
Obligations: home, food, security, education, healthy atmosphere. **Universal Declaration of Human Rights, Article 25.2:** Motherhood/childhood entitled to special care; all children enjoy equal social protection regardless of birth status.

### Effects of Parenting
*   **Happiness:** British/German panel data: up to two children increases happiness near birth, mostly for those postponing childbearing; third child adds no happiness.
*   **Values:** US "Success Index": parenting deemed integral to "American Dream," especially for ages 65+.
*   **Economics:** More children sustain pay-as-you-go pension systems; some systems offer lower contributions for parents vs. voluntarily childless for same benefits.

## Terms
- ****Parenting Style**** — The overall emotional climate of the home, defined by combinations of responsiveness (warmth) and demandingness (control).
- ****Authoritative Parenting**** — High responsiveness + high demandingness; balances autonomy support with clear limits and reasoning.
- ****Authoritarian Parenting**** — Low responsiveness + high demandingness; strict obedience enforced via punishment without explanation.
- ****Permissive Parenting**** — High responsiveness + low demandingness; few rules, high autonomy, reliance on reasoning over punishment.
- ****Uninvolved Parenting**** — Low responsiveness + low demandingness; emotional/physical absence, minimal expectations or engagement.
- ****Attachment**** — The enduring emotional bond forming in infancy; foundation for lifelong relationship capacity, distinct from affection.
- ****LOPI (Learning by Observing and Pitching In)**** — Indigenous practice integrating children into mature daily activities for learning via observation and contribution.
- ****Consejos**** — Narrative advice (Spanish "advice") using stories to guide child decision-making without direct commands.
- ****Ethnic-Racial Socialization**** — Parental transmission of cultural resources to prepare children of color for racism and foster positive group identity.
- ****Parent Training**** — Structured programs (e.g., PCIT, Triple P) teaching skills to improve parent-child interaction and child behavior.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Baumrind Typology Validity:** Critiques argue categories are overly broad, "authoritative" is imprecisely idealized, and "firm control" conflates positive structure with coercive power.
*   **Permissive Definition:** Debate whether the category conflates neglectful/confused parenting with intentional democratic/anti-authoritarian approaches.
*   **Causality in Ethnic-Racial Socialization:** Meta-analyses show only correlations; experimental studies needed to establish causation and test bidirectional child effects.
*   **Generalizability:** Need for further research to increase generalizability of ethnic-racial socialization findings across diverse populations.
*   **Material Parenting Long-term Effects:** Emerging concerns link material rewards to adult overconsumption, reduced self-esteem, marital issues, and financial hardship; causal pathways require clarification.
*   **Trustful vs. Directive Histories:** Gray’s evolutionary claim (trustful = hunter-gatherer norm; directive = agriculture/industry) contrasts with modern "directive-protective" dominance; empirical validation of historical prevalence is debated.

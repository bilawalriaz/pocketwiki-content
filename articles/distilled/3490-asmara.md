# Asmara

## Overview
Asmara is the capital and largest city of Eritrea, situated at a high altitude in the country's Central Region. It is globally renowned for its exceptionally well-preserved early 20th-century modernist architecture, a legacy of Italian colonial rule, which led to its designation as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2017.

## Body
### History and Colonial Development
According to Tigrinya oral tradition, Asmara was founded when four warring clans united for peace, giving it the name "Arbate Asmera" ("the four made them unite"). The city's modern prominence began with Italian occupation in 1889 and its designation as the colonial capital in 1897. Under Italian rule, Asmara underwent rapid urbanization and modernization, becoming known as "Piccola Roma" (Little Rome). A large Italian settler community grew, and the city became an experimental ground for radical modernist architecture, with most of its central buildings constructed between 1935 and 1941 under Mussolini's colonial ambitions.

### Post-Colonial Era and UNESCO Status
Following World War II, Eritrea was federated with Ethiopia in 1952, and Asmara ceased to be the capital. The city was home to the US Army's Kagnew Station from 1943 to 1977. After the Eritrean War of Independence (1961–1991), Asmara was restored as the capital of independent Eritrea. In 2017, UNESCO recognized the city as an "exceptional example of early modernist urbanism" in an African context, making it the first entire modernist city to receive World Heritage status.

### Geography and Climate
Asmara is located at 2,325 meters (7,628 ft) on the Eritrean Highlands, making it the sixth-highest capital in the world. It has a cool semi-arid climate (BSk) with mild temperatures year-round, averaging 518 mm (20.4 in) of precipitation annually, mostly in July and August. The city faces environmental challenges including deforestation, desertification, and soil erosion.

### Architecture and Culture
Asmara is celebrated for its early 20th-century architecture, featuring Art Deco, Futurist, Cubist, and Rationalist styles. Notable buildings include the Cinema Impero (1937), the Fiat Tagliero Building, and the Enda Mariam Cathedral. The city's Italian colonial heritage is evident in its wide streets, piazzas, and numerous cafes and restaurants serving Italian-Eritrean cuisine. It is home to the Eritrean National Museum and is the starting point for the Tour of Eritrea cycling race.

### Economy, Transport, and Education
Asmara is the economic hub of Eritrea, hosting the headquarters of Eritrean Airlines and other major corporations. The Eritrean Railway, rebuilt after independence, connects the city to the port of Massawa. Asmara International Airport provides air links. The city has been a national center of education, formerly home to the University of Asmara (1958–2006) and now the Eritrea Institute of Technology.

## Terms
- **Modernist Architecture**: An architectural style from the early 20th century characterized by simple forms, lack of ornamentation, and use of new materials like concrete and glass.
- **Art Deco**: A decorative style of the 1920s and 1930s featuring bold geometric shapes and vibrant colors.
- **Futurism**: An early 20th-century Italian art movement that emphasized speed, technology, and modernity.
- **UNESCO World Heritage Site**: A landmark or area with legal protection for having cultural, historical, or scientific significance.
- **Cool Semi-Arid Climate (BSk)**: A climate classification for regions with low precipitation and moderate temperatures, often at high elevations.
- **Kebessa Plateau**: The highland region where Asmara is located.
- **Arbate Asmera**: The Tigrinya name for Asmara, meaning "the four made them unite."
- **Piccola Roma**: Italian for "Little Rome," a nickname for Asmara during the Italian colonial period.

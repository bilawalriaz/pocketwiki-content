# Jomo Kenyatta

## Overview
Jomo Kenyatta (c. 1897–1978) was Kenya's first president and a central figure in its transition from British colony to independent republic. He led the anti-colonial movement, was imprisoned by the British, and governed Kenya from 1963 until his death. His legacy is complex, marked by successful nation-building and economic growth, but also by authoritarian rule, corruption, and policies favoring his own Kikuyu ethnic group.

## Timeline
- **c. 1897** — Born Kamau in Kiambu, British East Africa.
- **1929** — Travels to London to lobby for Kikuyu land affairs.
- **1938** — Publishes *Facing Mount Kenya*, an anthropological study of Kikuyu life.
- **1945** — Co-organizes the Pan-African Congress in Manchester.
- **1947** — Elected President of the Kenya African Union (KAU).
- **1952** — Arrested and charged with masterminding the Mau Mau Uprising (the Kapenguria Six).
- **1959** — Released from prison after serving a seven-year sentence.
- **1961** — Becomes President of the Kenya African National Union (KANU).
- **1963** — Leads KANU to election victory; becomes Prime Minister of autonomous Kenya.
- **12 Dec 1963** — Kenya gains independence; Kenyatta becomes Prime Minister of the sovereign state.
- **1964** — Kenya becomes a republic; Kenyatta is sworn in as its first President.
- **1969** — Bans the opposition Kenya People's Union (KPU), establishing a de facto one-party state.
- **22 Aug 1978** — Dies in office; succeeded by Daniel arap Moi.

## Body

### Early Life and Political Awakening
Born into the Kikuyu people, Kenyatta was educated at a Church of Scotland mission, taking the name Johnstone. He later worked in Nairobi, adopting the name "Kenyatta." He became politically active through the Kikuyu Central Association (KCA), advocating for Kikuyu rights, and in 1929 traveled to London to lobby on their behalf.

### Overseas Activism and Education
Kenyatta spent much of the 1930s in Europe, influenced by anti-colonialist and Pan-African ideas, notably through friendship with Marxist George Padmore. He studied at Moscow's Communist University, University College London, and the London School of Economics. In 1938, he published *Facing Mount Kenya*, using anthropology to argue for the integrity of traditional Kikuyu society. During WWII, he lived in Sussex and helped organize the 1945 Pan-African Congress.

### Return to Kenya and Imprisonment
Kenyatta returned to Kenya in 1946 and became president of the KAU in 1947, advocating for independence through peaceful means. Despite opposing the violent Mau Mau Uprising, he was arrested in 1952 with five others (the Kapenguria Six) and charged with masterminding it. Convicted in a trial later seen as politically motivated, he served a prison sentence until 1959, followed by internal exile. His imprisonment transformed him into a political martyr and symbol of African nationalism.

### Path to Independence and Premiership
Upon release, Kenyatta became president of KANU. The party won the 1963 election, and Kenyatta became Prime Minister. He oversaw the transition to independence, emphasizing reconciliation between indigenous groups and the white minority. His government negotiated a federal constitution but later centralized power. In 1964, Kenya became a republic with Kenyatta as its executive president.

### Presidential Rule and Domestic Policy
Kenyatta's presidency aimed for national unity under a strong central government. He suppressed dissent, banning the opposition KPU in 1969 and using detention laws to silence critics. His capitalist economic policy promoted foreign investment and "Africanisation"—transferring businesses from non-citizen (largely Asian) to African ownership. Land redistribution from white settlers favored KANU loyalists, exacerbating ethnic tensions. His government expanded education and healthcare, improving life expectancy.

### Foreign Policy and Final Years
Kenya pursued a pro-Western, anti-communist foreign policy during the Cold War, maintaining close ties with the UK and joining the Commonwealth. Kenyatta's health declined in his later years; by 1975 he had largely ceased active governance, with power concentrated in a small Kikuyu inner circle. He died in 1978, and Vice President Daniel arap Moi succeeded him.

## Terms
*   **Kikuyu Central Association (KCA):** Political organization founded in 1924 to advocate for Kikuyu rights, particularly land.
*   **Mau Mau Uprising:** Violent anti-colonial insurgency in Kenya (1952–1960), primarily involving the Kikuyu, against British rule.
*   **Kapenguria Six:** Six nationalists, including Kenyatta, arrested and tried in 1952 for alleged role in the Mau Mau Uprising.
*   **Kenya African Union (KAU):** Primary political party advocating for Kenyan independence before KANU.
*   **Kenya African National Union (KANU):** Political party led by Kenyatta that won the 1963 election and dominated Kenyan politics for decades.
*   **Majimbo:** Federalist political system advocated by KADU and favored by the British, which KANU initially opposed.
*   **Africanisation:** Policies transferring economic control from non-citizen (primarily Asian) to African ownership.
*   **Mzee:** Swahili term of respect meaning "elder," which became Kenyatta's honorary title.
*   **Shifta War:** Low-level conflict (1963–1967) between the Kenyan government and ethnic Somali separatists in the North Eastern Province.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Role in the Mau Mau Uprising:** Kenyatta consistently protested his innocence, and later historians largely agree he was not a leader. However, his trial and conviction were pivotal in his political career and the colonial government's strategy.
*   **Ideological Consistency:** Scholars debate whether Kenyatta was a committed Pan-Africanist and socialist who became a conservative pragmatist, or always fundamentally a capitalist and nationalist.
*   **Legacy of Governance:** Assessments are sharply divided. He is praised as the "Father of the Nation" who achieved independence and economic growth, but criticized for establishing an authoritarian, corrupt, and ethnically biased regime that laid groundwork for future dictatorial rule.
*   **Land Redistribution:** The process under Kenyatta is criticized for favoring political loyalists and his own family, creating long-term ethnic tensions and land grievances that erupted in violence in the 1990s.

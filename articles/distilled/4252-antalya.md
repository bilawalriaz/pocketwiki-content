# Antalya

## Overview
Antalya is a major Turkish city on the Mediterranean coast, serving as the capital of Antalya Province and a pivotal hub for tourism on the Turkish Riviera. Its history spans from its Hellenistic founding to its modern status as one of the world's most visited cities, reflecting a succession of empires and cultural influences.

## Body

### History and Etymology
The city was founded as "Attaleia" around 150 BC by King Attalus II of Pergamon, serving as a naval base. It became part of the Roman Empire in 133 BC and prospered, with nearby cities in Lycia, Pamphylia, and Pisidia also flourishing. Christianity arrived early, with visits from Paul of Tarsus and Barnabas. The city was a major Byzantine naval and commercial center before being conquered by the Seljuk Turks in 1207, who built its first mosque. It later became the capital of the Turkish beylik of Teke until Ottoman conquest in 1391. Ottoman rule brought stability for five centuries. After a brief Italian occupation (1919–22), it was returned to Turkey. Large-scale development from the 1970s onward transformed it into a major metropolitan and tourist center.

### Geography and Climate
Antalya sits on Anatolia's southwest coast, flanked by the Taurus Mountains. This topography traps moisture, resulting in high winter rainfall and very hot summers. The city has a hot-summer Mediterranean climate (Köppen: Csa) with nearly 3,000 hours of sunlight annually. Snow is rare, and the highest recorded temperature was 45.4 °C (113.7 °F) in 2017 (later adjusted to 45°C in 2000).

### Demographics and Economy
The city's population is around 1.5 million, with a provincial population of over 2.7 million. Historically, it had a Greek minority until the 1920s population exchange. The economy is dominated by tourism; Antalya is the fourth-most visited city globally and Turkey's biggest international sea resort. Agriculture includes citrus, cotton, and olives. The city hosts shipyards specializing in pleasure yachts and is the headquarters for several airlines.

### Cityscape and Architecture
The historic core, Kaleiçi, features narrow cobblestone streets, Ottoman houses, and a walled harbor. Architectural heritage includes Hellenistic, Roman, Byzantine, and Seljuk structures, such as Hadrian's Gate and the Yivli Minare Mosque. The modern city surrounds this core, with large *gecekondu* (shantytown) neighborhoods developed since the 1970s. Major beaches include Konyaaltı and Lara.

### Government and Politics
Antalya has been a Metropolitan Municipality since 1993. Politically, it has been a stronghold for the center-left Republican People's Party (CHP), though the right-wing Justice and Development Party (AKP) has also won control in recent decades, making it a competitive battleground. The CHP regained control of the municipality in 2019 and solidified its position in 2024.

### Culture and Transportation
Antalya's cuisine features dishes like Piyaz and şiş köfte. The city hosts the Golden Orange Film Festival and has numerous museums, including the Antalya Museum. Transportation is primarily by air and land, with Antalya Airport being a major international hub. The city has a tram system and a light rail line (Antray).

## Terms
- **Attaleia**: The ancient Greek name for Antalya, founded by King Attalus II.
- **Turkish Riviera**: A popular name for the southwestern coast of Turkey, of which Antalya is a key part.
- **Gecekondu**: Turkish term for informal, rapidly built housing, often on the outskirts of cities.
- **Beylik**: A Turkish principality or state, especially those that existed after the decline of the Seljuk Empire.
- **Kaleiçi**: The historic old town center of Antalya, within ancient walls.
- **Dolmuş**: Privately owned, shared minibuses that operate on fixed routes.
- **Halkkart**: The public transportation smart card system used in Antalya.
- **Antray**: The light rail system in Antalya.

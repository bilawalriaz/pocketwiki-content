# Yekaterinburg

## Overview
Yekaterinburg, Russia's fourth-largest city, is a major economic, cultural, and industrial hub in the Ural Federal District. Founded in 1723 as a mining and metallurgical center, it has evolved into a key transport junction between Europe and Asia, known for its constructivist architecture and role in pivotal historical events, including the execution of the Romanov family.

## Timeline
- **18 Nov 1723** — Official founding date; named after Catherine I, wife of Peter the Great.
- **1781** — Granted town status by Catherine the Great; becomes administrative center of Perm Province.
- **17 Jul 1918** — Execution of Tsar Nicholas II and his family in the Ipatiev House.
- **19 Oct 1920** — First university (Ural State University) established by decree of Lenin.
- **1924** — Renamed Sverdlovsk after Bolshevik leader Yakov Sverdlov.
- **1929** — Tram network completed.
- **Apr 1991** — Sverdlovsk Metro opens.
- **23 Sep 1991** — City regains its historical name, Yekaterinburg.
- **2009** — Hosts first official BRIC summit.
- **2018** — Hosts four matches of the FIFA World Cup.

## Body

### Founding and Imperial Era
The area was settled in prehistory, with archaeological sites dating to the Mesolithic period (8000–7000 BC). The first Russian settlements appeared in the late 17th century. In 1720, statesman Vasily Tatishchev and mining specialist Johann Blüher were sent to the Urals to develop the mining industry. After inspecting the area, Tatishchev selected a site on the Iset River for a new plant. Though initially removed from his post, his project was later supported by Georg Wilhelm de Gennin, and construction resumed in 1723. The city was founded as a fortress and manufacturing center, becoming a strategic "window to Asia" via the Siberian Route (operational 1763). It was a major mining capital, with 45% of the world's gold mined there between 1820–1845.

### Soviet Era and World War II
Following the 1917 Revolution, the city became a center of revolutionary activity. In 1924, it was renamed Sverdlovsk. Under Stalin, it was developed into a heavy industry powerhouse, with its population tripling. During World War II, it served as a major rear base; factories and institutions were evacuated there from war-torn cities, and the Uralmash plant became a key producer of armored vehicles. The city's industrial specialization in defense was extreme, with industry accounting for 90% of its economy in the Soviet era. A major anthrax outbreak occurred in 1979, attributed to a release from a military facility.

### Contemporary Era
After the 1991 coup attempt, the city was briefly designated a reserve capital for the Russian Federation. It regained its historical name in September 1991. The 2000s saw economic and population growth, highlighted by hosting major international summits (SCO, BRIC) and the 2018 FIFA World Cup. The economy has diversified from its industrial base into finance, retail, and tourism. The city is now a major financial center with over 100 banks and is home to the headquarters of the Central Military District.

### Geography and Climate
Located on the eastern side of the Ural Mountains, the city is bisected by the Iset River. It has a humid continental climate (Dfb) with sharp seasonal temperature fluctuations, ranging from −44.6 °C to 40.0 °C. The average annual temperature is 2.1 °C.

### Demographics and Religion
The 2021 Census recorded a population of 1,544,376. The predominant religion is Russian Orthodox Christianity. The city has a significant Muslim community and a planned "area of the three religions" (mosque, cathedral, synagogue), though the mosque's construction is frozen. Many religious buildings were destroyed during the Soviet era, but some, like the Great Zlatoust Church, have been restored.

### Economy and Transportation
Yekaterinburg is Russia's third-largest economy. Its economy has shifted from heavy industry (metallurgy, machinery) to include finance, retail, and services. It is a major transport hub with six federal highways, seven railway lines (part of the Trans-Siberian Railway), and Koltsovo International Airport. Public transport includes a metro, tram network, and buses, though traffic congestion is a persistent issue.

### Culture and Architecture
The city is famous for its constructivist architecture, with over 140 structures in that style. It is a major cultural center with numerous theaters, museums (including the Yeltsin Center), and libraries. It is considered a capital of Russian rock music and street art. The city hosted the 2018 FIFA World Cup and regularly hosts the INNOPROM industrial exhibition.

## Terms
- **Constructivism**: An architectural style prominent in 1920s–30s Soviet Union, emphasizing geometric forms and industrial materials.
- **Siberian Route**: A major trade road built in the 18th century connecting European Russia to Siberia, passing through Yekaterinburg.
- **Uralmash**: A major heavy machinery plant in Yekaterinburg, crucial for Soviet industrialization and WWII tank production.
- **Ipatiev House**: The building where the Romanov family was imprisoned and executed in 1918; demolished in 1977.
- **Humid continental climate (Dfb)**: A climate type with warm summers and cold winters, featuring significant seasonal temperature variation.
- **Trans-Siberian Railway**: The world's longest railway line, connecting Moscow with the Russian Far East; the Perm–Yekaterinburg–Tyumen section is a key part.
- **BRIC**: An acronym for the group of major emerging national economies: Brazil, Russia, India, and China.
- **INNOPROM**: An annual international industrial exhibition held in Yekaterinburg, among the world's largest.

## Debates and Open questions
- The founding of the first Old Believers village in the area (1672) is disputed by historians due to a lack of contemporary source evidence.
- The exact cause and circumstances of the 1979 Sverdlovsk anthrax outbreak were initially concealed by Soviet authorities and are still a subject of study regarding biological weapons safety.

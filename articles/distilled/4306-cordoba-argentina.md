# Córdoba, Argentina

## Overview
Córdoba is Argentina's second-largest city, a major educational, industrial, and cultural hub in the country's geographic center. Founded in 1573, it is renowned for its colonial history, the National University of Córdoba (the oldest in Argentina), and its pivotal role in national political and social movements.

## Body

### Foundation and Colonial Era
Córdoba was founded on 6 July 1573 by Jerónimo Luis de Cabrera, who named it after Córdoba, Spain. The settlement was established on the Suquía River in territory inhabited by the Comechingones people. After early conflicts, the city was relocated to its current site in 1577. The Jesuits arrived in 1599 and established a major religious and educational complex, founding the National University of Córdoba in 1613. This complex, the Jesuit Block, became a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2000.

### Political and Social History
Córdoba played a key role in Argentina's formation. During the May Revolution of 1810, local authorities initially supported the Spanish crown, but figures like Dean Gregorio Funes advocated for independence. The city was a center of conflict between federalist caudillos in the 1820s. In the 20th century, it was the birthplace of the influential 1918 University Reform movement and the 1969 Cordobazo protests, which challenged authoritarian rule.

### Industrial and Economic Development
Industrialization began in the late 19th century with European immigration. The city's economy diversified from processing agricultural goods to manufacturing. The 1927 inauguration of the Military Aircraft Manufacturer (FMA) marked a shift toward heavy industry. Post-WWII, the automotive sector became dominant, with factories for Renault, Volkswagen, and others. Córdoba is also a technological center, hosting the national spaceport and a growing software industry.

### Geography and Climate
The city lies in the Humid Pampa plain at the foothills of the Sierras Chicas, on the Suquía River. It has a humid subtropical climate (Cwa) with four distinct seasons. Summers are hot with thunderstorms, while winters are mild with occasional cold snaps from Antarctic air masses. Annual rainfall averages 750 mm.

### Demographics and Urban Structure
Córdoba's population is largely of Italian and Spanish descent, with significant European and Middle Eastern immigrant communities. The city is the core of the second-largest metropolitan area in Argentina. Its urban layout is radial, centered on San Martín Square. Key districts include the historic downtown, the university-focused Ciudad Universitaria, and affluent suburbs like Cerro de Las Rosas.

## Terms
- **Caudillos:** Regional strongmen or military-political leaders who dominated Argentine provinces in the 19th century.
- **Comechingones:** The indigenous people inhabiting the Córdoba region before Spanish colonization.
- **Jesuit Block (Manzana Jesuítica):** A 17th-century complex of religious, educational, and residential buildings founded by the Jesuits, now a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
- **La Reforma del '18:** The University Reform movement of 1918, which began in Córdoba and spread across Latin America, advocating for university autonomy and student participation.
- **Cordobazo:** A violent labor and student uprising in Córdoba in May 1969 against the military government.
- **Humid Pampa:** A fertile, low-lying plain in central-eastern Argentina, characterized by its humid climate.
- **Sierras Chicas:** A low mountain range east of Córdoba city, part of the larger Sierras de Córdoba system.
- **Suquía River (Río Primero):** The main river flowing through Córdoba, originating in the Sierras and flowing east to the Mar Chiquita salt lake.

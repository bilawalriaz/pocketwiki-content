# Edgar Allan Poe

## Overview  
Edgar Allan Poe (1809–1849) was an American writer, poet, editor, and critic whose works helped define Gothic fiction, detective fiction, and early science fiction. Orphaned young and fostered by the Allan family, he struggled financially throughout his life, becoming one of the first Americans to live solely by his pen. His macabre tales and innovative literary theories left a lasting impact on global literature, while his mysterious death at age 40 sparked enduring speculation.

## Timeline  
- **1809**: Born in Boston to actors David and Eliza Poe  
- **1810–1811**: Father abandons family; mother dies; taken in by John and Frances Allan  
- **1826**: Enters University of Virginia; leaves after one year due to debt  
- **1827**: Enlists in U.S. Army under alias "Edgar A. Perry"; publishes *Tamerlane and Other Poems*  
- **1831**: Court-martialed and dismissed from West Point  
- **1836**: Marries 13-year-old cousin Virginia Clemm  
- **1845**: Publishes “The Raven,” achieving instant fame  
- **1847**: Virginia dies of tuberculosis  
- **1849**: Dies mysteriously in Baltimore at age 40  

## Body  

### Early Life and Education  
Born into a theatrical family, Poe lost both parents early and was raised by the Allans, who never formally adopted him. His relationship with John Allan was strained, especially over money and gambling debts. After brief schooling in England and at the University of Virginia, he left academia in debt.

### Military Career  
Unable to support himself, Poe enlisted in 1827 under the name "Edgar A. Perry." He published his first poetry collection anonymously and rose to sergeant major. Discharged in 1829, he briefly attended West Point but was expelled in 1831 after deliberately courting dismissal.

### Publishing Career  
Working for journals like the *Southern Literary Messenger* and *Graham’s Magazine*, Poe gained recognition as a sharp critic. He launched the *Broadway Journal* but failed financially. His poem “The Raven” (1845) brought sudden fame, though he remained poor. He planned a literary journal, *The Stylus*, which never materialized.

### Death and Aftermath  
Found delirious in Baltimore in October 1849, Poe died days later. His death certificate was lost, and causes ranged from alcoholism to rabies to cooping. Literary rival Rufus Griswold wrote a defamatory obituary, shaping a damaging public image that persisted for decades.

### Literary Style and Themes  
Poe pioneered detective fiction with C. Auguste Dupin and explored themes of death, madness, and premature burial. He championed the “unity of effect” and originality in art, criticizing didacticism. His interest in cryptography led to “The Gold-Bug,” and his cosmological essay *Eureka* anticipated the Big Bang theory.

### Legacy  
Though underappreciated in his lifetime, Poe influenced global literature. Writers like Conan Doyle, Lovecraft, and Verne drew from his work. His home is preserved as a museum, and the Edgar Awards honor mystery writing. A mysterious “Poe Toaster” visited his grave annually from 1949 to 2009.

## Terms  
- **Gothic fiction**: Genre emphasizing mystery, horror, and the supernatural  
- **Macabre**: Material dealing gruesomely with death or decay  
- **Detective fiction**: Stories centered on solving crimes through deduction  
- **Unity of effect**: Poe’s principle that every element in a work should contribute to a single emotional impact  
- **Cooping**: Form of electoral fraud involving forcing people to vote under threat  
- **Delirium tremens**: Severe alcohol withdrawal syndrome  
- **Substitution cryptogram**: Cipher where each letter is replaced by another symbol  
- **Dark romanticism**: Literary movement focusing on human darkness and psychological horror  
- **Transcendentalism**: 19th-century philosophical movement emphasizing intuition and nature  
- **Obal man**: Slang term used by Poe for followers of transcendentalism  

## Debates and Open Questions  
The cause of Poe’s death remains unknown, with theories including alcoholism, rabies, carbon monoxide poisoning, and cooping. Whether Poe was a drug user is debated; Griswold’s claims lack evidence. Some scholars question whether Poe’s “Philosophy of Composition” was retroactively constructed. The identity of the “Poe Toaster” remains unconfirmed despite claims by Sam Porpora.

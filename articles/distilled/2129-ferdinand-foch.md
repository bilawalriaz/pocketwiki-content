# Ferdinand Foch

## Overview
Ferdinand Foch (1851–1929) was a French general and military theorist who became the Supreme Allied Commander on the Western Front in the final year of World War I. His leadership was pivotal in coordinating Allied forces to halt the 1918 German spring offensive and launch the counter-offensive that led to victory. His post-war advocacy for a harsh peace treaty against Germany, which he believed was too lenient, remains a significant part of his legacy.

## Timeline
- **2 October 1851** — Born in Tarbes, France.
- **1870** — Enlists in the French Army during the Franco-Prussian War.
- **1885–1901** — Instructor at the École Supérieure de Guerre, shaping French military doctrine.
- **1907** — Promoted to Général de Brigade; takes command of the French War College.
- **August 1914** — Commands XX Corps at the outbreak of WWI; fights at the Battle of the Frontiers.
- **September 1914** — Commands the Ninth Army during the First Battle of the Marne.
- **26 March 1918** — Appointed "Commander-in-Chief of the Allied Armies" (Généralissime).
- **6 August 1918** — Promoted to Marshal of France.
- **11 November 1918** — Accepts the German armistice at Compiègne.
- **January 1919** — Presents a memorandum at the Paris Peace Conference advocating for the Rhine as France's frontier.
- **20 March 1929** — Dies in Paris; buried at Les Invalides.

## Body

### Early Life and Military Thought
Foch was born into a middle-class Catholic family and enlisted in the army after the Franco-Prussian War. He became a distinguished instructor at the French War College, where he was known as a masterful military thinker. His lectures, published as *Des Principes de la Guerre* (1903) and *De la Conduite de la Guerre* (1904), reintroduced the concept of the offensive to French military theory, emphasizing "the will to conquer." However, his nuanced ideas were later distorted by contemporaries into the extreme "cult of the offensive" (*l'offensive à outrance*), which contributed to disastrous French losses in 1914.

### World War I: Rise to Supreme Command
At the war's outbreak, Foch commanded XX Corps. He gained prominence for his defensive actions during the First Battle of the Marne in 1914, where he helped stabilize the Allied line. He was then given command of the Northern Army Group, coordinating with British forces at Ypres and the Somme. After the costly Somme offensive, he was transferred to Italy in late 1916. Recalled in 1917, he became chief of the general staff. In March 1918, facing the German spring offensive, he was appointed Supreme Allied Commander. He successfully coordinated French, British, and American forces, stopping the German advance and planning the Grand Offensive that led to Allied victory.

### The Armistice and Peace Conference
Foch accepted the German armistice on 11 November 1918. At the Paris Peace Conference, he argued forcefully that the Rhine River should be France's permanent military frontier to ensure security. He considered the resulting Treaty of Versailles too lenient, believing its terms would only lead to a future conflict. He is famously, though perhaps apocryphally, quoted as saying, "This is not Peace. It is an Armistice for twenty years."

### Post-War Legacy
After the war, Foch was made a British Field Marshal (1919) and a Marshal of Poland (1923). He toured the United States, where he was celebrated. He died in 1929 and was buried at Les Invalides in Paris. His legacy is complex: celebrated as the victorious Allied commander, but also criticized for the pre-war doctrines that contributed to early French defeats and for his uncompromising stance at Versailles.

## Terms
*   **Généralissime:** A supreme military commander of multiple armies or nations.
*   **Cult of the offensive (*l'offensive à outrance*):** A pre-WWI French military doctrine emphasizing relentless attack, which led to massive casualties.
*   **Plan XVII:** The French Army's battle plan for WWI, influenced by Foch's offensive theories.
*   **Race to the Sea:** The period in late 1914 when both sides tried to outflank each other, leading to trench warfare.
*   **Supreme War Council:** An Allied political-military body established in 1917 to coordinate strategy.
*   **Military Board of Allied Supply (MBAS):** An agency created in 1918 to coordinate logistical support for Allied armies.
*   **Armistice of 11 November 1918:** The agreement that ended fighting on the Western Front.
*   **Treaty of Versailles:** The 1919 peace treaty that formally ended WWI, which Foch opposed as too lenient.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Foch's Pre-War Doctrines:** Historians debate whether Foch's pre-war teachings on the offensive were correctly interpreted or if they were distorted by others, leading to the disastrous 1914 strategies.
*   **Contribution to the 1918 Victory:** While credited as the architect of victory, some argue his counter-attacks at the Marne in 1914 failed, and his 1918 success was due more to Allied material superiority and German exhaustion.
*   **The Armistice Delay:** Foch's refusal to declare an immediate ceasefire after the armistice was signed resulted in thousands of casualties on the final day, a decision still questioned.
*   **Versailles Treaty Judgment:** His prediction that the treaty was merely a "20-year armistice" is often cited as prescient given the outbreak of WWII in 1939, though the treaty's actual role in causing the war is a major historical debate.

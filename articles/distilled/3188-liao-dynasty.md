# Liao dynasty

## Overview
The Liao dynasty (916–1125) was a Khitan-led empire that ruled over Manchuria, the Mongolian Plateau, and parts of northern China. It was the first state to control all of Manchuria and established a dual administrative system for governing both nomadic and sedentary populations. Its legacy includes a unique cultural synthesis and the term "Cathay" for China, derived from "Khitai."

## Timeline
- **907** — Abaoji becomes supreme Khitan leader, ending the triennial election system.
- **916** — Abaoji proclaims a Chinese-style dynasty, adopting the title "Celestial Emperor."
- **936** — The Later Jin cedes the Sixteen Prefectures to the Liao, granting access to northern China.
- **947** — Emperor Taizong conquers the Later Jin, formally adopts the name "Great Liao."
- **1004** — The Chanyuan Treaty ends major warfare with the Northern Song, establishing peace for ~120 years.
- **1066** — Emperor Daozong reintroduces the dynastic name "Great Liao."
- **1114** — Aguda of the Jurchen Wanyan clan begins a rebellion against the Liao.
- **1125** — The Jurchen-led Jin dynasty captures Emperor Tianzuo, ending the Liao dynasty.
- **1130s** — Yelü Dashi establishes the successor Western Liao (Qara Khitai) state in Central Asia.

## Body

### Origins and Rise to Power
The Khitans emerged from the Xianbei people, splitting from the Kumo Xi tribe in the 4th century. They formed a tribal federation with eight tribes sharing power through triennial elections. After periods of vassalage to the Tang and Uighur empires, the Khitans expanded under leaders like Xianzhi Khagan. The Yila tribe, led by Abaoji, became dominant due to its adoption of agriculture and Han Chinese refugees. In 907, Abaoji abolished the elective system, and in 916, he proclaimed a Chinese-style imperial state, founding the Liao dynasty.

### Government and Administration
The Liao employed a dual administration: a Northern Administration for Khitan and nomadic peoples, and a Southern Administration modeled on the Tang dynasty for the Han Chinese population. This system was formalized under Emperor Shizong. The empire was divided into five circuits, each with a capital city. The military was based on the *orda* (emperor's private army) and tribal cavalry, with Khitan forces organized into heavy armored units. The Khitan language was written in two scripts developed in the 920s, but Chinese remained the administrative language of the Southern Establishment.

### Foreign Relations and Warfare
The Liao engaged in frequent warfare with neighboring states, including the Song dynasty, Goryeo, and Western Xia. The Chanyuan Treaty of 1004/1005 with the Song was a landmark agreement, establishing a century of peace and mutual recognition as equals. Relations with Goryeo fluctuated between war and tributary status. The Liao's decline began with the Jurchen rebellion in 1114. Internal strife and military defeats led to the fall of the northern capitals and the capture of Emperor Tianzuo by the Jin in 1125.

### Society and Culture
Khitan society exhibited a synthesis of nomadic and sedentary cultures. Women held significant status, managing herds and sometimes commanding armies. Elite burials featured personalized metal masks and wire suits. Religion was a mix of Buddhism, Confucianism, Daoism, and tribal beliefs, with Buddhism becoming dominant. The Liao left a cultural legacy in statuary, ceramics, and music that influenced subsequent Chinese, Jurchen, and Mongol traditions.

## Terms
- **Khitan**: A nomadic people from the Mongolian Plateau and Manchuria who founded the Liao dynasty.
- **Dual Administration**: The Liao's system of separate governments for nomadic (Northern) and sedentary (Southern) populations.
- **Sixteen Prefectures**: Strategic northern Chinese territories ceded to the Liao in 938, including modern Beijing.
- **Chanyuan Treaty**: A 1004/1005 agreement between the Liao and Song dynasties that established peace and mutual recognition.
- **Orda**: The emperor's private army and household, forming the core of the Liao military.
- **Qara Khitai**: The Western Liao state established by Khitan refugees in Central Asia after 1124.
- **Cathay**: A name for China derived from "Khitai," used in medieval Europe via Russian and Islamic sources.
- **Jinshi**: The highest degree in the Chinese imperial examination system, adopted by the Liao.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The exact meaning of the word "Liao" (Hura) in the Khitan language is uncertain, with theories including "black," "broad," or a reference to the Liao River.
- The extent of Liao cultural influence on subsequent dynasties, particularly in music and theater, remains a subject of scholarly inquiry.
- The precise nature of the relationship between the Liao and its successor state, the Qara Khitai, is debated, as the latter considered itself the legitimate continuation of the Liao.

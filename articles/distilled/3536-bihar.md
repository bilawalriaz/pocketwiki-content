# Bihar

## Overview
Bihar is a state in eastern India, historically a cradle of major religions and empires, but which has faced significant socio-economic challenges in the modern era. Its identity is deeply tied to the Ganges River, ancient Buddhist learning centers like Nalanda, and a complex caste-based political landscape. Despite recent governance improvements and economic growth, it remains one of India's less developed states.

## Timeline
- **c. 2500–1345 BCE** — Neolithic archaeological record at Chirand.
- **c. 1100–500 BCE** — Videha Kingdom becomes a major political/cultural center; Vajji established as a republic.
- **684 BCE** — Haryanka dynasty founded, ruling Magadha from Rajgriha.
- **321 BCE** — Maurya Empire, India's first empire, founded by Chandragupta Maurya in Magadha.
- **240 CE** — Gupta Empire originates in Magadha, marking a "Golden Age" for India.
- **12th century** — Destruction of Nalanda and Vikramashila universities by Bakhtiyar Khalji.
- **1764** — British East India Company obtains diwani rights for Bihar after the Battle of Buxar.
- **1912** — Bihar and Orissa carved out of the Bengal Presidency to form a new province.
- **1917** — Mahatma Gandhi leads the Champaran Satyagraha against indigo planters.
- **1990** — Lalu Prasad Yadav becomes Chief Minister, marking a shift in caste-based politics.
- **2005** — Nitish Kumar becomes Chief Minister, focusing on development and governance.
- **2026** — Samrat Chaudhary (BJP) becomes the first BJP Chief Minister of Bihar.

## Body

### Historical Significance
Bihar was the heartland of ancient Indian empires and religions. Regions like Magadha, Mithila, and Anga are mentioned in ancient texts. The Maurya Empire (321 BCE), founded in Magadha, was India's first empire, and Emperor Ashoka, born in Pataliputra (modern Patna), is a world-historical figure. The Gupta Empire (240 CE), also originating in Magadha, is considered a "Golden Age" of Indian science, mathematics, and philosophy. Crucially, Bihar was central to Buddhism; the Buddha attained enlightenment at Bodh Gaya, and ancient universities like Nalanda and Vikramashila were major international centers of learning until their destruction in the 12th century.

### Modern Political and Social Landscape
Since independence, Bihar's politics have been dominated by caste dynamics. Initially controlled by Forward Castes (Brahmin, Rajput, Bhumihar, Kayastha), power shifted after 1990 following the Mandal Commission's reservation policy, which mobilized Other Backward Classes (OBCs). Leaders like Lalu Prasad Yadav and Nitish Kumar emerged from this shift. The state experienced severe political instability, violence, and economic decline, becoming a symbol of poverty and corruption by the early 2000s. Since 2005, under Nitish Kumar and now Samrat Chaudhary, governance has improved with a focus on infrastructure, education, crime reduction, and economic revival, though challenges persist.

### Economy and Development
Bihar's economy is primarily agrarian, with about 80% of the population employed in agriculture. It is a major producer of vegetables and fruits but is hampered by floods and droughts. The state has one of India's fastest-growing economies in recent years, with a GSDP growth rate of 13.5% in FY 2024-25. However, it ranks very low in per capita income compared to other Indian states. Industrial development is supported by entities like the Bihar Industrial Area Development Authority, with key industrial cities including Begusarai, Hajipur, and Muzaffarpur. A new Startup Policy (2022-2027) aims to foster entrepreneurship.

### Culture and Demographics
Bihar is the second most populous state in India, with a young population (58% under 25). Hindi and Urdu are official languages, but major native languages include Bhojpuri, Maithili, and Magahi. The state has a rich cultural heritage, including Mithila (Madhubani) and Bhojpuri folk paintings, and the Patna School of Painting. Major festivals include Chhath Puja and Durga Puja. Tourism is significant, centered on UNESCO sites like the Mahabodhi Temple (Bodh Gaya) and Nalanda Mahavihara, as well as wildlife sanctuaries.

## Terms
- **Vihāra**: A Sanskrit and Pali word meaning "abode," referring to a Buddhist monastery; the name "Bihar" is derived from this.
- **Zamindari System**: A land tenure system where landlords (zamindars) collected taxes from peasants; its abolition and subsequent land reform failures were key post-independence issues.
- **Mandal Commission**: A 1980 Indian government commission that recommended reservations (quotas) for Other Backward Classes (OBCs) in government jobs and education, implemented in 1990.
- **Naxalism**: A far-left Maoist insurgent movement in India, which in Bihar often took the form of violent caste-based conflict between landowners and the landless.
- **GSDP (Gross State Domestic Product)**: The total value of goods and services produced within a state in a year, used to measure its economic size.
- **Per Capita Income**: The average income earned per person in a given area, calculated by dividing the state's total income by its population.
- **Chhath Puja**: A major Hindu festival in Bihar dedicated to the Sun God, involving rituals at water bodies over four days.
- **Satyagraha**: A philosophy and practice of non-violent resistance, famously used by Mahatma Gandhi, as in the Champaran Satyagraha.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Causes of Underdevelopment**: Economists debate whether Bihar's lag is primarily due to central government policies (e.g., freight equalisation), lack of Bihari sub-nationalism, the legacy of the Permanent Settlement of 1793, or internal factors like caste conflict and poor governance.
- **Historical Destruction of Universities**: Historians debate the nature of the 12th-century destruction of Nalanda and Vikramashila. While traditionally attributed to Bakhtiyar Khalji's invasion, some scholars like D. N. Jha suggest it may have resulted from Buddhist–Brahmin skirmishes.
- **Impact of Prohibition**: The state's 2016 alcohol ban is debated for its linked effects, including a reported drop in tourism and a rise in substance abuse.
- **Caste Politics Evolution**: Political scientists analyze whether the dominant political divide in Bihar has shifted from Forward vs. Backward Castes to conflicts between different groups within the OBC category itself, as seen in the 1995 election.

# Jazz dance

## Overview
Jazz Dance is a performance dance style that originated in the United States in the early 20th century, evolving from African American vernacular dance forms alongside jazz music. It encompasses a range of styles, from social vernacular dances like the Charleston and Lindy hop to theatrical forms used on Broadway and in film. The style is significant as a dynamic expression of African American cultural identity and experience, blending African traditions with European influences and continually adapting to new musical and social contexts.

## Timeline
- **c. 1917** — Spencer Williams' song "Shim-Me-Sha-Wabble" inspires the shimmy dance.
- **1920s** — The Charleston dance becomes a national craze, peaking in popularity between 1926-1927.
- **1920s** — The Harlem Renaissance celebrates Black art, with figures like Josephine Baker popularizing jazz dance.
- **1929** — The Great Depression begins, leading many to turn to social dances like the Lindy hop.
- **1931** — The New Negro Art Theatre presents a recital with interpretive dances based on Southern spirituals.
- **1940s** — Hollywood and Broadway begin using "Jazz Dance" to describe choreography by figures like Bob Fosse.
- **1944-45** — Katherine Dunham opens her schools of arts and research in New York.
- **1980s** — MTV popularizes high-energy music videos combining jazz with other styles.
- **1990s** — Colleges apply the term "Jazz Dance" to physical education classes set to pop music.

## Body

### Origins and Early History
Jazz Dance's roots are in African dance traditions, characterized by bent knees, complex rhythms, and body isolations. In the late 19th and early 20th centuries, black performance art gained visibility through Vaudeville and minstrel shows, which provided platforms for entertainers and influenced evolving dance styles. The Harlem Renaissance of the 1920s was a pivotal cultural movement where jazz music's popularity spurred dance evolution, incorporating African traditions, social dances, and jazz rhythms.

### Vernacular and Swing Dances
Vernacular jazz dances emerged from everyday community life. The Charleston, named after Charleston, South Carolina, incorporated hand clapping and foot stamping linked to African origins. Its popularity surged after the 1923 song "The Charleston" by James P. Johnson. The Lindy hop was a wild, rhythmically conscious partner dance that became a core part of the "swing dance" umbrella during the Great Depression. These dances were often improvised, allowing dancers to "riff" by incorporating personal movements into established patterns, reflecting cultural resilience.

### Theatrical and Modern Evolution
From the 1940s, choreographers like Jack Cole and Bob Fosse promoted dramatic jazz dance on stage and screen. Cole is credited with popularizing its theatrical form, while Fosse revolutionized it with sexually suggestive movements. Katherine Dunham, an anthropologist and choreographer, introduced isolations and created the Dunham technique. In the late 20th century, styles like contemporary jazz (seen on shows like *So You Think You Can Dance*) and commercial jazz (combining hip hop and jazz for pop music videos) emerged. Latin jazz, emphasizing hip movement, was also developed.

## Terms
- **Vernacular Dance:** Dance forms that emerge from the everyday life and cultural practices of a specific community.
- **Isolation:** A dance technique where one part of the body moves independently while the rest remains still.
- **Improvisation:** A spontaneous, structured form of choreography where dancers create movements in the moment, drawing on established patterns.
- **Swing Dance:** An umbrella term for social dances that developed with swing jazz music, including the Lindy hop and Charleston.
- **Dunham Technique:** A codified jazz dance technique created by Katherine Dunham, characterized by simple lines, torso movement, and varied tempos.
- **Luigi Technique:** The first codified jazz dance technique, created by Eugene Louis Faccuito (known as Luigi).
- **Commercial Jazz:** A style popular since the 1980s that combines aspects of hip hop and jazz, often performed to pop music in media like music videos.

## Debates and Open Questions
The term "Jazz Dance" is used broadly and sometimes controversially. Since the 1940s, it has been applied to Broadway and Hollywood choreography that may have little direct connection to jazz music. Furthermore, since the 1990s, colleges have used the term for physical education dance classes set to various pop music, further diluting its original association with jazz music and African American vernacular roots. This raises ongoing questions about the definition and ownership of the style.

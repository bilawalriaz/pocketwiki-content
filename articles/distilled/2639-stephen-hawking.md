# Stephen Hawking

## Overview
Stephen Hawking (1942–2018) was a British theoretical physicist and cosmologist whose work fundamentally shaped our understanding of black holes and the origins of the universe. Despite being diagnosed with motor neurone disease at age 21 and living for over 50 years with the condition, he became one of the most celebrated scientists of his time, known for groundbreaking theoretical predictions and for making complex science accessible to the public.

## Timeline
- **8 January 1942** — Born in Oxford, England.
- **1959** — Begins undergraduate studies at University College, Oxford.
- **1962** — Begins graduate work at Trinity Hall, Cambridge.
- **1963** — Diagnosed with motor neurone disease (MND).
- **1966** — Awarded PhD in applied mathematics and theoretical physics.
- **1974** — Proposes that black holes emit radiation (Hawking radiation).
- **1979** — Elected Lucasian Professor of Mathematics at Cambridge.
- **1988** — Publishes *A Brief History of Time*.
- **2004** — Concedes the black hole information paradox bet with John Preskill.
- **14 March 2018** — Dies at his home in Cambridge at age 76.

## Body

### Early Life and Education
Hawking was born into an educated but financially constrained family. He attended St Albans School and then University College, Oxford, where he found the work "ridiculously easy" but initially lacked motivation. He earned a first-class BA degree in physics in 1962. He began his PhD at Cambridge in 1962, but his first year was difficult; he was diagnosed with MND in 1963 and initially fell into a depression. With encouragement from his supervisor, Dennis Sciama, he returned to his work, which focused on applying Roger Penrose's singularity theorems to the entire universe.

### Scientific Career and Key Contributions
Hawking's early work, in collaboration with Penrose, proved that if the universe obeys general relativity, it must have begun as a singularity. In the early 1970s, he helped establish the laws of black hole mechanics. His most famous breakthrough came in 1974 with the theoretical prediction that black holes emit thermal radiation, now called Hawking radiation, implying they can eventually evaporate. This was initially controversial but became widely accepted. He later proposed the Hartle-Hawking state (1983), a model suggesting the universe has no boundary or beginning in spacetime, replacing the initial singularity of the Big Bang.

### Public Profile and Later Work
Hawking became a global celebrity with his 1988 bestseller *A Brief History of Time*. He engaged in several high-profile scientific wagers, including conceding a bet on the black hole information paradox in 2004, stating that information is not lost in black holes. He also lost a bet on the existence of the Higgs boson, discovered in 2012. In his later career, he developed top-down cosmology and continued to write popular science books. He retired as Lucasian Professor in 2009 but remained director of research at Cambridge.

### Personal Life and Disability
Hawking married Jane Wilde in 1965, and they had three children. His physical abilities declined steadily; he lost his speech after a tracheotomy in 1985 and communicated via a speech-generating device, eventually using a cheek muscle. He divorced Jane in 1995 and married his nurse, Elaine Mason, divorcing her in 2006. He later reconciled with his first family. Despite his disability, he traveled widely, including a zero-gravity flight in 2007, and became an inspirational figure for disability rights.

## Terms
- **General Relativity:** Einstein's theory describing gravity as the curvature of spacetime caused by mass and energy.
- **Cosmology:** The study of the origin, evolution, and eventual fate of the entire universe.
- **Singularity:** A point in spacetime where gravitational forces cause matter to have an infinitely small, dense volume, as predicted by general relativity.
- **Black Hole:** A region of spacetime where gravity is so strong that nothing, not even light, can escape from it.
- **Hawking Radiation:** The theoretical thermal radiation predicted to be emitted by black holes due to quantum effects near the event horizon.
- **Event Horizon:** The boundary around a black hole beyond which nothing can escape.
- **Quantum Mechanics:** A fundamental theory in physics describing the physical properties of nature at the scale of atoms and subatomic particles.
- **No-Boundary Proposal (Hartle-Hawking state):** A cosmological model suggesting the universe has no initial boundary or singularity in spacetime.
- **Motor Neurone Disease (MND):** A progressive neurodegenerative disease that affects nerve cells in the brain and spinal cord, leading to loss of muscle control.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Black Hole Information Paradox:** A major debate over whether information that falls into a black hole is permanently lost when it evaporates via Hawking radiation, which would violate quantum mechanics. Hawking initially argued information was lost but later conceded the bet, proposing it could be retrieved.
- **The No-Boundary Proposal:** The idea that the universe has no initial boundary remains a significant but debated model in quantum cosmology, with implications for the origin of time.
- **Existence of God:** Hawking's work, particularly the no-boundary proposal, led him to argue that a creator God was not necessary to explain the universe's origin, though he acknowledged the question of why the universe exists at all.

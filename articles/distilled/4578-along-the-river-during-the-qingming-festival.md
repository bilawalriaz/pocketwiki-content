# Along the River During the Qingming Festival

## Overview
*Along the River During the Qingming Festival* is a renowned Chinese handscroll painting attributed to the Song dynasty artist Zhang Zeduan (1085–1145). It is celebrated for its detailed depiction of daily life, commerce, and landscape in the Northern Song capital of Bianjing (modern Kaifeng), capturing the bustling activity of all social classes. The painting is considered a national treasure and one of the most famous works in Chinese art history.

## Timeline
- **c. 1085–1145** — Life of the attributed painter, Zhang Zeduan.
- **1127** — Fall of the Northern Song dynasty to the Jin; many records destroyed.
- **1186** — Earliest known attribution to Zhang Zeduan via an inscription.
- **Yuan dynasty (1271–1368)** — An early, faithful copy is made by Zhao Mengfu.
- **Ming dynasty (14th–17th c.)** — Painter Qiu Ying creates several influential copies with updated details.
- **Late Ming / Early Qing** — Dozens of copies are produced in Suzhou, often with forged signatures.
- **1737** — The "Qing Court Version" is completed and presented to the Qianlong Emperor.
- **1924** — The last emperor, Puyi, takes the Song original from the Forbidden City.
- **1945** — The Song original is recovered and returned to public ownership.
- **2007** — The Song original is exhibited in Hong Kong.
- **2010** — A 3D animated digital version, *River of Wisdom*, is featured at the World Expo.
- **2012** — The Song original is exhibited in Tokyo, its first attested journey outside China.

## Body
### The Song Original
The original scroll, attributed to Zhang Zeduan, is a handscroll 25.5 cm high and 5.25 m long. It depicts a panoramic view of the capital, with the Bian River flowing through the scene. The right section shows a rural landscape with farmers and herders, while the left section portrays the densely populated city, filled with shops, businesses, and people from all walks of life. The central focus is the bustling Rainbow Bridge, where a boat narrowly avoids a collision. The painting contains hundreds of figures, animals, and vehicles, offering a detailed snapshot of Northern Song society. Its accuracy is partially corroborated by contemporary writings like the *Dongjing Meng Hua Lu*.

### Copies and Versions
The original was frequently copied, creating a lineage of works that adapt the composition to different eras. Notable versions include a faithful Yuan dynasty copy by Zhao Mengfu and several Ming dynasty copies by Qiu Ying, which updated costumes, architecture, and technology. A distinct "Qing Court Version," completed in 1737, is much larger and features over 4,000 figures, including an added palace scene. These later works, once dismissed as mere copies, are now recognized for their own artistic and historical value in visualizing idealized societies.

### Modern Legacy and Exhibition
The painting's fame has led to rare public exhibitions that draw massive crowds. The Song original was shown in Hong Kong in 2007 and in Tokyo in 2012. A major digital adaptation, *River of Wisdom*, was a centerpiece of the 2010 Shanghai World Expo and has since toured internationally. The delicate nature of the original scroll means it is exhibited only intermittently and for brief periods.

## Terms
- **Handscroll:** A traditional Chinese painting format designed to be viewed section by section from right to left as it is unrolled.
- **Jiehua:** A technique of using a ruler to draw straight lines, particularly for architectural elements.
- **Qingming Festival:** A traditional Chinese festival for honoring ancestors, also known as Tomb-Sweeping Day.
- **Bianjing:** The capital city of the Northern Song dynasty, now modern Kaifeng.
- **Rainbow Bridge (Hong Qiao):** The prominent wooden bridge that serves as the central focal point of the painting's composition.
- **Qing Court Version:** A large, detailed copy of the painting completed in 1737 by court painters for the Qianlong Emperor.
- **Dongjing Meng Hua Lu:** A Song dynasty text describing life in the capital, used to corroborate details in the painting.

## Debates and Open Questions
Scholars debate several fundamental aspects of the painting. The meaning of "Qingming" in the title is contested—it may refer to the Qingming Festival, the "peace and order" of the era, or the solar term. Some question whether the city depicted is an accurate portrayal of Kaifeng or an idealized vision. There is also disagreement about whether the painting was created before the fall of the Northern Song in 1127 or after, and whether it depicts a spring festival or an autumn scene. One allegorical interpretation suggests the painting was a subtle warning to the emperor about the fragility of prosperity.

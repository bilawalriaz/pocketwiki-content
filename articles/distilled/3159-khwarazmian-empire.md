# Khwarazmian Empire

## Overview
The Khwarazmian Empire was a culturally Persianate, Sunni Muslim state of Turkic origin that dominated Central Asia, Afghanistan, and Iran from 1077 to 1231. It rose from a Seljuk vassal state to become the most powerful realm in the region, only to be catastrophically destroyed by the Mongol invasion of 1219–1221, an event that reshaped the history of the Islamic world.

## Timeline
- **1077** — Anushtegin Gharachai, a Turkic mamluk, becomes governor of Khwarazm for the Seljuks.
- **1127–1156** — Reign of Ala ad-Din Atsiz, who maneuvers for independence between the Seljuks and Qara Khitai.
- **1194** — Tekish defeats the Seljuk Sultan Toghrul III, ending Seljuk power in Iran.
- **c. 1190** — The empire becomes fully independent from the Qara Khitai.
- **1213–1217** — Muhammad II conquers the Western Kara-Khanids, vassalizes the Ghurids, and expands into Persia.
- **1218** — Mongols under Genghis Khan reach the Khwarazmian border after defeating the Qara Khitai.
- **1219** — Genghis Khan invades the Khwarazmian Empire following a diplomatic and trade dispute.
- **1220** — The Mongols sack major cities including Bukhara, Samarqand, and the capital Gurganj; Shah Muhammad II flees and dies.
- **1221** — Jalal al-Din Mangburni is defeated at the Battle of the Indus; the Mongols capture his grandmother Terken Khatun.
- **1231** — Jalal al-Din Mangburni, the last Khwarazmshah, is murdered, ending the empire.

## Body

### Origins and Rise
The Anushtegin dynasty was founded by Anushtegin Gharachai, a Turkic slave (mamluk) who became a commander and governor for the Seljuk Empire. His son, Qutb al-Din Muhammad, became the first hereditary Khwarazmshah. Independence was gradually achieved under Ala ad-Din Atsiz (r. 1127–1156), who skillfully navigated between his suzerains, the Seljuq Sultan Sanjar and the Qara Khitai gurkhan, while expanding his territory.

### Expansion and Zenith
Under Il-Arslan (r. 1156–1172) and especially Tekish (r. 1172–1200), the empire broke free from Qara Khitai suzerainty and defeated the Seljuks, becoming the dominant power in Iran. The empire reached its maximum extent under Muhammad II (r. 1200–1220), who conquered the Ghurids and expanded into Persia. However, his reign was marked by a diarchy with his powerful mother, Turkan Khatun, whose influence and conflicts with Muhammad may have weakened the state's response to the coming Mongol threat.

### The Mongol Invasion and Fall
The invasion was triggered by a trade dispute: Muhammad II's governor seized a Mongol merchant caravan, and the Shah subsequently executed Mongol envoys. In 1219, Genghis Khan invaded with a massive force, exploiting internal divisions. The Mongols systematically besieged and destroyed the empire's wealthy cities. Muhammad II fled and died in 1220. His son, Jalal al-Din Mangburni, fought a brilliant but ultimately futile resistance, winning the Battle of Parwan in 1221 before being defeated and fleeing to India. He re-established a rump state in western Iran but was killed in 1231, ending the dynasty.

### State and Society
The empire was modeled on the Seljuk system, with a Persianate bureaucracy led by a vizier and a Turkic military elite. Its population, estimated at 5 million in 1220, was sparse for its vast area (2.3–3.6 million km²). The society was a blend of Turkic military culture and Persian civilian administration, with Persian as the official language of state. The army consisted of about 40,000 Turkic cavalry, supplemented by poor-quality city militias.

### Culture and Legacy
The Khwarazmian era fostered a dual Turkic-Persian culture. Persian was the language of administration and literature, while Turkic was the rulers' mother tongue. The period saw notable achievements in ceramics, particularly the production of Mina'i and lusterware. After the empire's fall, Khwarazmian mercenaries, known as the Khwarazmiyya, played a significant role in the region, famously capturing Jerusalem in 1244 and helping destroy a Crusader army at the Battle of La Forbie.

## Terms
- **Mamluk**: A soldier of slave origin, often Turkic, who served in Islamic armies and could rise to high positions.
- **Khwarazmshah**: The title of the ruler of the Khwarazmian Empire.
- **Anushtegin dynasty**: The Turkic mamluk dynasty that ruled the Khwarazmian Empire from 1077 to 1231.
- **Qara Khitai (Western Liao)**: A Mongol and Buddhist dynasty that ruled parts of Central Asia and was a suzerain of the early Khwarazmians.
- **Diwan**: A government bureau or administrative department.
- **Vizier**: The chief minister or head of the bureaucracy in an Islamic state.
- **Hajib**: A high-ranking court official, often a chamberlain or commander of the royal guard.
- **Mina'i ware**: A type of finely decorated Persian ceramic produced in the late 12th and early 13th centuries.
- **Khwarazmiyya**: The name given to Khwarazmian mercenaries and soldiers after the empire's fall.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The exact founding date of the Khwarazmian state is debated, with the title "Khwarazmshah" first appearing in 305 AD under the Afrighids, but the Anushtegin dynasty's rule beginning in 1077.
- The degree to which the diarchy between Muhammad II and his mother Turkan Khatun contributed to the empire's military weakness and rapid collapse against the Mongols is a point of scholarly discussion.
- The precise population of the empire and its major cities at the time of the Mongol invasion is estimated, with figures for major cities ranging from 520,000 to 850,000 people.

# Managua

## Overview
Managua is the capital and largest city of Nicaragua, situated on the southern shore of Lake Managua. It serves as the nation's political, economic, and cultural hub. The city's history is marked by rapid growth, devastating earthquakes, and periods of reconstruction, making it a case study in urban resilience and development in Central America.

## Body

### Origins and Early History
The Managua area has been inhabited since at least 12,000 BC, as evidenced by the 2,100-year-old Ancient footprints of Acahualinca. The city was founded as a pre-Columbian fishing village and incorporated in 1819. Its selection as the national capital in 1852 was a political compromise, as its location between the rival cities of León and Granada was seen as neutral. Between 1852 and 1930, Managua underwent extensive urbanization, becoming Central America's most developed city.

### 20th Century: Earthquakes and Conflict
Managua's growth was severely impacted by two major earthquakes. The 1931 earthquake killed between 1,000 and 2,450 people. Under the Somoza family dictatorship (1936–1979), the city was rebuilt and industrialized. However, the 1972 earthquake destroyed 90% of downtown, killed over 19,120 people, and crippled the city's infrastructure. Corruption in the relief effort hindered reconstruction. The subsequent Nicaraguan Revolution (1979) and the Contra War (1980s) further devastated the economy.

### 21st Century: Reconstruction and Modernization
After the 1990 election, reconstruction began in earnest, aided by returning Nicaraguans. New infrastructure, including an expanded airport and shopping malls, was built. Since 2006, the Sandinista government has expanded social programs and revitalized parts of downtown with new government buildings and cultural venues. However, commercial activity in the old center remains low, and rapid growth on the city's outskirts, often near Lake Managua, poses environmental concerns. A major German-funded wastewater treatment plant, inaugurated in 2009, was built to decontaminate the lake.

### Geography and Climate
Managua is located on the southern shore of Lake Managua (also known as Lake Xolotlán) at an altitude of 55 meters (180 ft), rising to over 700 meters (2,297 ft) in the southern Sierras de Managua. The city lies on fault lines, leading seismologists to predict a severe earthquake every 50 years or less. It has a tropical wet and dry climate (Aw) with constant temperatures averaging 28–32 °C (82–90 °F). The rainy season is from mid-May to mid-November.

### Economy and Transportation
Managua is Nicaragua's primary economic center, hosting major national and international businesses, factories, and banks. The Augusto C. Sandino International Airport is the country's only international airport. The city is a hub for the Pan-American Highway system, with good road connections to the rest of the country. Public transportation consists of an extensive bus network. There are no operating railroads in the country.

### Culture and Landmarks
Managua is the cultural capital, home to the National Palace of Culture, the Rubén Darío National Theatre, and the Old Cathedral (Catedral de Santiago), all of which survived the 1972 earthquake. The Plaza de la Revolución is the historic center. The city hosts the annual Miss Nicaragua pageant and the patron saint festival of Santo Domingo de Guzmán in August. Notable landmarks also include the Tiscapa Lagoon Natural Reserve and the Dennis Martínez National Stadium.

## Terms
- **Paleo-Indians:** The earliest known inhabitants of the Americas.
- **Nahuatl:** An Uto-Aztecan language spoken in Central Mexico, from which the name "Managua" may derive.
- **Mangue (Chorotega):** An extinct indigenous language of Nicaragua.
- **Somoza regime:** The family dictatorship that ruled Nicaragua from 1936 to 1979.
- **Sandinista National Liberation Front (FSLN):** The left-wing political party that led the Nicaraguan Revolution and has governed Nicaragua since 2007.
- **Contra War:** A U.S.-backed guerrilla war fought by the Contras against the Sandinista government in the 1980s.
- **Tropical wet and dry climate (Aw):** A climate type characterized by a pronounced dry season.
- **Pan-American Highway:** A network of roads connecting the mainland countries of the Americas.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The etymology of "Managua" is debated, with possible origins in the Nahuatl term "Mana-ahuac" ("adjacent to the water") or the Mangue word for "place of the big man."
- The reconstruction of Managua's historic downtown after the 1972 earthquake remains incomplete and is a subject of ongoing urban planning debates, including proposed projects for a light rail system.

# Saladin

## Overview
Saladin (c. 1137–1193) was a Kurdish Muslim commander who founded the Ayyubid dynasty and became the first sultan of Egypt and Syria. He is most famous for recapturing Jerusalem from the Crusaders in 1187, a pivotal event that triggered the Third Crusade and cemented his legacy as a chivalrous and unifying figure in both Muslim and Western history.

## Timeline
- **c. 1137** — Born in Tikrit, Iraq, to a Kurdish family.
- **1164** — Sent with his uncle Shirkuh to Fatimid Egypt on orders from Nur ad-Din.
- **1169** — Appointed vizier of Egypt after the deaths of Shawar and Shirkuh.
- **1171** — Abolished the Fatimid Caliphate and realigned Egypt with the Abbasid Caliphate.
- **1174** — Entered Damascus after the death of Nur ad-Din, beginning his conquest of Syria.
- **1175** — Defeated the Zengids at the Battle of the Horns of Hama; proclaimed Sultan of Egypt and Syria.
- **1187** — Defeated the Crusaders at the Battle of Hattin and captured Jerusalem.
- **1189–1192** — Faced the Third Crusade led by Richard I of England.
- **1192** — Treaty with Richard I ended the Third Crusade.
- **4 March 1193** — Died in Damascus, having given away most of his wealth.

## Body

### Rise to Power in Egypt
Saladin's military career began under his uncle Shirkuh, a commander for the Zengid ruler Nur ad-Din. They were sent to Egypt in 1164 to restore the vizier Shawar. After a power struggle, Shirkuh and then Saladin (in 1169) became vizier to the Shia Fatimid caliph al-Adid. As a Sunni, Saladin worked to undermine the Fatimid establishment. Following al-Adid's death in 1171, he abolished the Fatimid Caliphate and realigned Egypt with the Sunni Abbasid Caliphate in Baghdad, consolidating his power.

### Conquest of Syria and Unification
After Nur ad-Din's death in 1174, Saladin moved to secure Syria. He peacefully entered Damascus and then conquered Hama and Homs. He defeated rival Zengid lords at the Battle of the Horns of Hama (1175) and was proclaimed sultan by the Abbasid caliph. By 1182, he had completed the conquest of Syria after capturing Aleppo, though he failed to take Mosul. This period involved complex diplomacy and warfare with other Muslim rulers, including the Zengids and the Assassin order.

### The Battle of Hattin and Capture of Jerusalem
Provoked by Crusader raids, especially by Raynald of Châtillon, Saladin launched a major campaign. On 4 July 1187, his army decisively defeated the combined Crusader forces at the Battle of Hattin. Saladin personally executed Raynald for his violations of truces. Following this victory, Saladin captured nearly every Crusader city. Jerusalem surrendered on 2 October 1187 after a siege. Saladin offered generous terms, allowing Christian inhabitants to leave for a ransom or remain under Muslim rule, and he permitted Jews to resettle in the city.

### The Third Crusade and Later Years
The fall of Jerusalem prompted the Third Crusade (1189–1192), led by European monarchs including Richard I of England. Key engagements included the Siege of Acre and the Battle of Arsuf (1191), where Saladin's forces were defeated. Despite military setbacks, Saladin's forces prevented Richard from retaking Jerusalem. The conflict ended with the Treaty of Jaffa (1192), which left Jerusalem under Muslim control but allowed unarmed Christian pilgrims to visit. Saladin died in Damascus in 1193, having distributed his wealth.

## Terms
*   **Ayyubid dynasty:** The Sunni Muslim dynasty founded by Saladin, ruling Egypt, Syria, and parts of Mesopotamia and Yemen from 1171 to 1341.
*   **Fatimid Caliphate:** An Isma'ili Shia caliphate based in Cairo that ruled Egypt and varying parts of the Levant until Saladin abolished it in 1171.
*   **Zengid dynasty:** A Sunni Muslim dynasty of Turkic origin that ruled parts of Syria and Mesopotamia; Saladin began his career in their service.
*   **Jihad:** An Islamic term meaning "struggle" or "striving," often interpreted as a holy war in defense of Islam. Saladin's campaigns were framed as jihad against the Crusaders.
*   **Order of Assassins:** A Nizari Isma'ili Shia sect known for targeted killings; Saladin fought against them but later formed a cooperative alliance.
*   **Third Crusade (1189–1192):** A military expedition by European Christian leaders to recapture Jerusalem after Saladin's conquest in 1187.
*   **Mamluk:** A soldier of slave origin, often of Turkic or Circassian descent, who served in elite military units in the medieval Islamic world.
*   **Madrasa:** An Islamic educational institution, often focused on religious instruction. Saladin founded many Sunni madrasas to counter Fatimid Shia influence.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Motives for abolishing the Fatimid Caliphate:** Scholars debate whether Saladin's primary motivation was religious (promoting Sunni orthodoxy) or political (consolidating personal power and aligning with the Abbasid Caliphate).
*   **Relationship with the Assassins:** The exact nature of Saladin's relationship with the Assassin leader Rashid ad-Din Sinan is debated. Sources conflict on whether their interactions ended in a truce, a peace agreement, or a more cooperative alliance.
*   **Legacy in the Muslim World:** While celebrated as a hero in modern Arab nationalism, Saladin's legacy is more complex. Among Egyptian Shias, he is sometimes derided as "Kharab ad-Din" (the destroyer of religion) for ending Fatimid rule.

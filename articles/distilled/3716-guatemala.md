# Guatemala

## Overview
Guatemala is a Central American nation with a rich Maya heritage and a complex history marked by colonialism, political instability, and a devastating civil war. It is the most populous country in Central America and is characterized by significant biodiversity, deep social inequalities, and a large indigenous population. Its modern history is defined by the struggle between authoritarian rule and democratic movements, with the legacy of the 1960–1996 civil war and ongoing challenges like corruption and poverty shaping its current political and social landscape.

## Body

### Pre-Columbian and Colonial Era
The territory was the core of the Maya civilization, which flourished during the Classic period (250–900 AD) before a collapse often attributed to drought. The Spanish conquest began in 1524 under Pedro de Alvarado, leading to the establishment of the Captaincy General of Guatemala, a colonial administrative unit. The capital was moved multiple times due to natural disasters, finally settling in present-day Guatemala City in 1776.

### Independence and Instability (1821–1944)
Guatemala gained independence in 1821, briefly joining Mexico and then the Central American Federation. The 19th century was marked by conflict between liberal and conservative factions. Conservative Rafael Carrera dominated politics until his death in 1865. The Liberal Revolution of 1871, led by Justo Rufino Barrios, initiated modernization and expanded coffee production. The late 19th and early 20th centuries saw the rise of dictators like Manuel Estrada Cabrera (1898–1920) and Jorge Ubico (1931–1944), who granted major concessions to the United Fruit Company and ruled with authoritarian efficiency.

### Revolution, Coup, and Civil War (1944–1996)
A pro-democratic coup in 1944 ended Ubico's rule, leading to a decade of reform under presidents Juan José Arévalo and Jacobo Árbenz. Árbenz's agrarian reform (Decree 900) threatened the United Fruit Company's interests. In 1954, a CIA-backed coup (Operation PBSuccess) overthrew Árbenz, installing a military-dominated government. This reversal sparked a 36-year civil war (1960–1996) between the state (backed by the U.S.) and leftist guerrillas (backed by the USSR/Cuba). The conflict involved widespread human rights abuses, including genocide against the Maya population, with over 200,000 killed or disappeared. A UN truth commission attributed 93% of violations to state forces.

### Contemporary Guatemala (1996–Present)
The 1996 peace accords ended the war, leading to economic growth and democratic elections. However, challenges persist, including corruption, crime, and poverty. A major anti-corruption movement, supported by the UN-backed CICIG, led to the resignation of President Otto Pérez Molina in 2015. In 2023, Bernardo Arévalo of the anti-corruption Semilla party won the presidency, facing attempts by the outgoing administration to block his inauguration before assuming office in January 2024.

## Terms
- **Maya civilization**: A Mesoamerican civilization noted for its hieroglyphic script, mathematics, and calendar systems, which reached its height in the Classic period.
- **Captaincy General of Guatemala**: The Spanish colonial administrative unit governing Central America from 1542 until independence.
- **Decree 900**: A 1952 agrarian reform law under President Jacobo Árbenz that redistributed uncultivated land to landless peasants, affecting large landholders like the United Fruit Company.
- **United Fruit Company (UFCO)**: A powerful American corporation that owned vast plantations and infrastructure in Guatemala, wielding significant political influence.
- **Operation PBSuccess**: The 1954 CIA covert operation that orchestrated the coup against President Jacobo Árbenz.
- **Scorched-earth warfare**: A military strategy of destroying anything useful to the enemy, used extensively by the Guatemalan army against Maya communities during the civil war.
- **CICIG (International Commission against Impunity in Guatemala)**: A UN-backed judicial body that investigated and prosecuted high-level corruption and clandestine security networks in Guatemala from 2007 to 2019.
- **Femicide**: The murder of a woman because of her sex; Guatemala officially recognized it as a crime in 2008 and has one of the world's highest rates.

## Debates and open questions
- The cause of the Classic Maya collapse (c. 900 AD) is debated, with drought-induced famine being a leading theory supported by archaeological evidence.
- The extent of U.S. support for the Guatemalan military during the civil war is a subject of historical scrutiny, with evidence suggesting aid continued even after official bans.
- The precise indigenous population percentage is contested; the 2018 census reported 43.6%, while indigenous rights activists claim it is closer to 61%.
- The effectiveness and legacy of the CICIG's anti-corruption work remain debated, especially after its mandate was terminated in 2019.

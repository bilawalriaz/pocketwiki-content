# Uganda

## Overview
Uganda is a landlocked East African nation in the Great Lakes region, with a population of 45.9 million as of 2024. Its history is marked by pre-colonial kingdoms, British colonial rule, post-independence political instability, and a long period of rule under President Yoweri Museveni since 1986. The country faces challenges including corruption, human rights issues, and regional conflicts, but has made progress in health and education.

## Timeline
- **c. 3000 BC** — Bantu and Nilotic groups arrive in the region.
- **1830s** — Arab traders arrive from the Indian Ocean coast.
- **1894** — The British establish the Uganda Protectorate.
- **9 October 1962** — Uganda gains independence from the UK.
- **1966** — The Mengo Crisis: President Obote suspends the constitution, attacks the Buganda palace, and abolishes traditional kingdoms.
- **25 January 1971** — Idi Amin overthrows Milton Obote in a military coup.
- **1979** — Amin is overthrown during the Uganda–Tanzania War.
- **1986** — Yoweri Museveni's National Resistance Movement wins the Ugandan Bush War.
- **2005** — Presidential term limits are abolished; a referendum restores multi-party politics.
- **2014** — President Museveni signs the Anti-Homosexuality Act, later annulled by the Constitutional Court.
- **2023** — Parliament passes a new Anti-Homosexuality Act, which is signed into law.

## Body

### Pre-colonial and Colonial History
Before 3000 BC, the region was inhabited by Central Sudanic and Kuliak-speaking peoples. The arrival of Bantu and Nilotic speakers led to the assimilation of earlier groups and the rise of influential kingdoms like the Empire of Kitara. Arab traders arrived in the 1830s, followed by British explorers and missionaries in the late 19th century. Religious wars in Buganda and the financial failure of the Imperial British East Africa Company led the British government to establish the Uganda Protectorate in 1894. The protectorate system allowed kingdoms to retain some self-government.

### Independence and Political Instability
Uganda gained independence in 1962 with Milton Obote as prime minister. The early years were dominated by tensions between the central government and the powerful Buganda kingdom. This culminated in the 1966 Mengo Crisis, where Obote suspended the constitution, attacked the Kabaka's palace, and abolished traditional kingdoms. Obote was overthrown by Idi Amin in 1971. Amin's brutal dictatorship (1971–1979) saw mass killings and the expulsion of the Indian minority. He was overthrown in 1979, leading to a period of instability and the Ugandan Bush War.

### Museveni Era and Contemporary Uganda
Yoweri Museveni took power in 1986 after a guerrilla war. His rule brought initial stability and economic growth but has been marked by political repression, the abolition of presidential term limits in 2005, and allegations of electoral fraud. Uganda has been involved in regional conflicts, including the Congo Wars and the fight against the Lord's Resistance Army (LRA). Human rights concerns persist, particularly regarding the treatment of political opponents and the LGBTQ+ community, highlighted by the passage of anti-homosexuality laws in 2014 and 2023. Despite these issues, Uganda has made notable progress in reducing HIV infection rates and improving literacy.

### Geography and Economy
Uganda is geographically diverse, featuring volcanic hills, mountains, and major lakes including a portion of Lake Victoria. It lies within the Nile basin. The economy has transitioned from being agriculture-dominated to one where the services sector is largest. Key exports include coffee, fish, and tea. The country has significant untapped oil reserves, with a major crude oil pipeline project underway. Tourism, focused on wildlife and national parks, is a vital economic sector. However, poverty remains deep-rooted in rural areas, and corruption is a severe problem.

## Terms
- **Protectorate**: A territory administered by an outside power, which in Uganda's case allowed kingdoms to retain some self-government compared to a full colony.
- **Mengo Crisis**: The 1966 conflict between President Obote and the Buganda kingdom, leading to the suspension of the constitution and abolition of kingdoms.
- **Bush War**: The guerrilla war (1981–1986) fought by Yoweri Museveni's National Resistance Army that brought him to power.
- **Lord's Resistance Army (LRA)**: A violent rebel group led by Joseph Kony that has committed widespread human rights abuses in northern Uganda and neighboring countries.
- **Anti-Homosexuality Act**: Laws passed in 2014 and 2023 that criminalize homosexual acts, drawing international condemnation.
- **Bantu**: A large group of African languages and the ethnic groups who speak them, predominant in southern Uganda.
- **Nilotic**: A group of African languages and the ethnic groups who speak them, historically dominant in northern Uganda's security forces.
- **Kabaka**: The traditional king of the Buganda kingdom.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The political legacy of the "North/South" ethnic divide, rooted in colonial recruitment patterns and post-independence politics, continues to influence Ugandan governance.
- The long-term impact of President Museveni's rule, including the abolition of term limits and allegations of electoral fraud, on Uganda's democratic future is a subject of ongoing concern.
- The effectiveness and human rights implications of Uganda's progressive refugee policy, which hosts more refugees than any other African country, are debated.
- The potential economic benefits versus the risks of corruption and environmental impact from the development of Uganda's oil reserves.

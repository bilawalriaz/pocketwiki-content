# Latin America

## Overview
Latin America is a cultural region of the Americas defined by the predominant use of Romance languages, primarily Spanish and Portuguese. It encompasses most of Central and South America, Mexico, and parts of the Caribbean, shaped by Indigenous civilizations, European colonization, African slavery, and immigration. The region's identity is complex, marked by diverse societies and significant economic and social challenges, including high inequality.

## Timeline
- **c. 1500 BCE – 1500 CE** — Advanced Indigenous civilizations flourish, including the Olmec, Maya, Aztec, and Inca.
- **1492** — Christopher Columbus's voyage begins the era of European colonization.
- **1500s** — Spain and Portugal establish colonies, imposing Roman Catholicism and their languages.
- **1810s–1820s** — Most Spanish American colonies achieve independence through armed struggle.
- **1822** — Brazil gains independence from Portugal, initially as a monarchy.
- **1856** — The term "Latin America" is first used publicly by Chilean politician Francisco Bilbao in Paris.
- **Late 19th century** — Abolition of slavery (Brazil, 1888) and large-scale European immigration.
- **1959** — The Cuban Revolution establishes a socialist state, influencing regional politics.
- **1970s–1980s** — Military dictatorships rule several countries; the "Lost Decade" economic crisis occurs.
- **1990s–2000s** — A wave of democratization and neoliberal economic reforms.
- **2000s–2010s** — The "Pink Tide" sees left-wing governments elected across the region.
- **2010s–present** — A "Conservative Wave" follows, with right-wing leaders gaining power in several nations.

## Body

### Etymology and Definition
The term "Latin America" was coined in 1856 by Chilean politician Francisco Bilbao to promote unity among Spanish and Portuguese-speaking nations. It was later promoted by Napoleon III's France to justify its influence. The definition is cultural, not geographic, typically including countries where Spanish or Portuguese are the main languages, excluding English- and Dutch-speaking nations like Belize, Guyana, and Suriname. Related terms include "Hispanic America" (Spanish-speaking only) and "Ibero-America" (including Spain and Portugal).

### History
Before European contact, the region hosted advanced Indigenous civilizations. Colonization by Spain and Portugal from the late 15th century imposed European languages, religion, and social structures, causing massive Indigenous population decline. The colonial economy was mercantilist, controlled by the Spanish Crown. Independence movements in the early 19th century led to new nations but often political instability, the rise of caudillos (strongmen), and continued economic dependence. The 20th century saw U.S. intervention, Cold War conflicts, military dictatorships, and later a shift toward democracy. The early 21st century saw the "Pink Tide" of left-wing governments, followed by a conservative resurgence.

### Economy
Latin America's economy is diverse, with significant agricultural, mining, and manufacturing sectors. Brazil, Argentina, and Mexico are the largest economies. The region is a major global producer of agricultural commodities (e.g., soy, coffee, beef) and minerals (e.g., copper, silver, lithium). Manufacturing is concentrated in Brazil, Mexico, and Argentina. Economies are often dependent on commodity exports, making them vulnerable to global price fluctuations. The 2000s commodities boom brought growth, but the 2008 Great Recession caused stagnation. China has become a major trading partner and investor. Wealth inequality is among the highest in the world, with deep historical roots.

### Demographics and Society
Latin America is ethnically diverse, with populations descended from Indigenous, European, African, and Asian ancestors. Mestizo (mixed European and Indigenous) populations are the majority in many countries. Spanish is the predominant language, followed by Portuguese. The region is overwhelmingly Christian, mostly Roman Catholic, though Protestantism is growing. Urbanization is advanced, with large metropolitan areas. Public health has improved, but challenges remain in education access, crime, and violence, with some of the world's highest homicide rates.

### Culture
Latin American culture is a synthesis of Indigenous, European, African, and Asian influences, evident in its vibrant arts, literature, music, and cuisine. The region has produced globally renowned artists, writers (e.g., Gabriel García Márquez), and musicians (e.g., Shakira, Juan Gabriel). Musical genres like salsa, tango, samba, and reggaeton have international reach. Cultural institutions include major film industries in Mexico, Argentina, and Brazil. The region has six Nobel Prize in Literature laureates.

## Terms
- **Caudillo**: A military and political leader, especially in 19th-century Latin America, who exercises power in a charismatic and often authoritarian manner.
- **Mestizo**: A person of mixed European and Indigenous ancestry; the largest demographic group in many Latin American countries.
- **Mercantilism**: An economic system dominant in Europe from the 16th to 18th centuries, where colonies existed to enrich the mother country through controlled trade and resource extraction.
- **Pink Tide**: A political wave of left-wing governments elected in Latin America during the first decade of the 21st century.
- **Ibero-America**: A cultural and geopolitical concept referring to the countries in the Americas that were once part of the Spanish or Portuguese Empires.
- **Casta System**: A colonial racial classification system in Spanish America that categorized people based on their ancestry and skin color.
- **Dependency Theory**: An economic theory suggesting that resources flow from a "periphery" of poor and underdeveloped states to a "core" of wealthy states, enriching the latter at the expense of the former.
- **Neotropical realm**: One of Earth's eight biogeographic realms, covering most of Latin America, characterized by high biodiversity and tropical rainforests.

## Debates and Open questions
- **Definition Boundaries**: There is ongoing debate about whether to include French-speaking territories (like Haiti and French Guiana) or English-speaking countries with similar socioeconomic histories (like Belize and Guyana) in the definition of Latin America.
- **Racial Identity**: Scholars debate the legacy of colonial racial categories like the *casta* system and the concept of "racial democracy" (e.g., in Brazil), examining how race continues to influence social inequality and political representation.
- **Economic Model**: There is a persistent debate over the effectiveness of neoliberal economic policies versus state-led development models in addressing the region's chronic inequality and fostering sustainable growth.
- **Impact of China**: The rapid increase in Chinese trade and investment raises questions about its long-term impact on Latin American economies, sovereignty, and development patterns.

# Walt Disney

## Overview
Walt Disney (1901–1966) was an American animator, film producer, and entrepreneur who pioneered the modern animation industry. His innovations—including synchronized sound, full-color Technicolor, and the multiplane camera—revolutionized animated filmmaking. Beyond animation, he created Disneyland, helped launch the theme park industry, and built a multimedia empire that remains one of the world’s largest entertainment conglomerates.

## Timeline
- **1901** — Born in Chicago, Illinois
- **1923** — Moved to Hollywood; founded Disney Brothers Studio
- **1928** — Created Mickey Mouse; released *Steamboat Willie*, first synchronized-sound cartoon
- **1937** — Released *Snow White and the Seven Dwarfs*, first full-length animated feature
- **1941** — Animators’ strike; U.S. enters WWII
- **1950** — Released *Cinderella*; began live-action films
- **1955** — Opened Disneyland in Anaheim, California
- **1964** — Released *Mary Poppins*; developed exhibits for New York World’s Fair
- **1966** — Died of lung cancer; EPCOT and Disney World plans unfinished

## Body

### Early Life and Career Foundations
Born in Chicago and raised in Missouri, Disney showed early talent for drawing. He worked as a commercial illustrator and co-founded a short-lived animation studio in Kansas City. After moving to Hollywood in 1923, he partnered with his brother Roy and Ub Iwerks to create the Disney Brothers Studio.

### Rise to Prominence: Mickey Mouse and Technological Innovation
After losing rights to Oswald the Lucky Rabbit, Disney and Iwerks created Mickey Mouse in 1928. *Steamboat Willie* (1928) became the first synchronized-sound cartoon, launching Mickey to fame. Disney embraced new technologies: three-strip Technicolor (*Flowers and Trees*, 1932), the multiplane camera (*The Old Mill*, 1937), and CinemaScope (*Lady and the Tramp*, 1955).

### Golden Age of Animation
From 1934 to 1941, Disney produced landmark features: *Snow White* (1937), *Pinocchio* (1940), *Fantasia* (1940), *Dumbo* (1941), and *Bambi* (1942). These films advanced storytelling, visual depth, and emotional resonance in animation. However, financial strain from *Pinocchio* and *Fantasia*, compounded by WWII-era losses, led to a 1941 strike and significant debt.

### Expansion into Live-Action and Television
Post-war, Disney diversified into live-action films (*Treasure Island*, 1950) and nature documentaries (*True-Life Adventures* series). He launched the *Disneyland* TV series (1954) to fund his theme park ambitions. The *Davy Crockett* miniseries sparked a national craze, boosting merchandising and record sales.

### Theme Parks and Final Projects
Disneyland opened in 1955 as the first modern theme park, featuring themed lands and immersive design. In 1964, he contributed exhibits to the New York World’s Fair, including *It’s a Small World* and *Great Moments with Mr. Lincoln*. He began planning Walt Disney World and EPCOT—a futuristic city concept—before his death in 1966.

### Personal Life and Legacy
Disney was known for his public charisma contrasting with private shyness. He was politically conservative, involved in anti-communist efforts, and faced posthumous accusations of prejudice, though colleagues and archival evidence largely refute claims of racism or antisemitism. His legacy includes 22 Academy Awards, a global entertainment empire, and enduring cultural influence.

## Terms
- **Cel animation**: Technique using hand-drawn images on transparent sheets layered over backgrounds.
- **Multiplane camera**: Device allowing multiple layers of artwork to move independently, creating depth.
- **Three-strip Technicolor**: Color process producing vibrant, stable hues, used in Disney’s color cartoons.
- **Silly Symphonies**: Series of animated shorts scored to classical music, used to experiment with storytelling and technique.
- **Imagineers**: Designers and engineers who develop Disney theme park attractions and experiences.
- **EPCOT**: Experimental Prototype Community of Tomorrow; Disney’s unrealized vision for a futuristic urban community.
- **Buena Vista**: Disney’s film distribution company, established in 1953.
- **Nine Old Men**: Core group of Disney animators who shaped the studio’s artistic direction.
- **WED Enterprises**: Company formed by Disney to design Disneyland; later became Walt Disney Imagineering.
- **Cinephone**: Sound-on-film system used by Disney for early synchronized cartoons.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Prejudice allegations**: Accusations of antisemitism and racism persist, but biographers like Neal Gabler argue there is no evidence Disney held such views personally. His association with anti-communist groups may have fueled reputational damage.
- **EPCOT’s intent**: Whether EPCOT was meant as a real city or an elaborate theme park remains debated; posthumous development favored the latter.
- **Cultural impact**: Scholars debate whether Disney represented wholesome American values or cultural imperialism, particularly through Cold War-era propaganda and global expansion.

# Jiangsu

## Overview
Jiangsu is a coastal province in East China, a national economic and commercial center since the Sui and Tang dynasties. It is one of China's most developed and densely populated provinces, with a leading economy, extensive higher education system, and a history shaped by its location on the Yangtze River Delta and the Grand Canal.

## Timeline
- **484 BC** — The state of Wu, in southern Jiangsu, defeats the state of Qi.
- **473 BC** — The state of Wu is subjugated by the state of Yue.
- **222–280** — Southern Jiangsu is the base of the Eastern Wu kingdom, with its capital at Jianye (modern Nanjing).
- **4th–6th c.** — Nomadic invasions cause a northern imperial court to move to Jiankang (Nanjing), spurring economic growth.
- **581 onward** — After reunification, the Grand Canal is built through Jiangsu, linking it to the north.
- **1127** — The Huai River becomes the border between the Jurchen Jin dynasty in the north and the Southern Song in the south.
- **1368** — The Ming dynasty establishes its capital in Nanjing.
- **1666** — Jiangsu and Anhui are split into separate provinces under the Qing dynasty.
- **1853–1864** — The Taiping Rebellion establishes its capital in Nanjing (renamed Tianjing).
- **1927** — Chiang Kai-shek establishes a government in Nanjing; Shanghai is separated from Jiangsu.
- **1937–1945** — Nanjing falls to Japan; the "Rape of Nanjing" occurs. The province is under Japanese occupation.
- **1949** — Communist takeover; Jiangsu is dissolved into administrative regions.
- **1952** — Jiangsu Province is reconstituted with Nanjing as its capital.
- **1990s onward** — Economic reforms accelerate development, especially in southern cities like Suzhou and Wuxi.

## Body

### Name and Geography
The name "Jiangsu" combines elements from the cities of Jiangning (now Nanjing) and Suzhou. The province is predominantly flat, with plains covering 68% of its area and water 18%. The Yangtze River flows through its south, dividing it into a more urban, prosperous southern half and a poorer, rural northern half. The Grand Canal traverses the province north-south. Major lakes include Lake Tai and Lake Hongze. The Huai River's course was altered by Yellow River silting after 1194, causing it to flood southward.

### History
The area was home to ancient states like Wu and Yue before being unified under Qin in 221 BC. During periods of northern invasion (e.g., 4th century), southern Jiangsu, centered on Nanjing, became a political and economic hub. The construction of the Grand Canal after 581 cemented its role as a national commercial center. This prosperity continued through the Song, Ming, and Qing dynasties. In the modern era, Jiangsu was a battleground in the Taiping Rebellion, the Second Sino-Japanese War (including the 1937 Nanjing Massacre), and the Chinese Civil War, culminating in the Communist victory in 1949. Post-1990s economic reforms led to rapid growth, particularly in cities near Shanghai.

### Economy
Jiangsu has China's second-highest GDP and the highest GDP per capita among provinces. Its economy is driven by manufacturing (electronics, chemicals, textiles), agriculture (rice, wheat, silk), and it is China's largest recipient of foreign direct investment. The Suzhou Industrial Park is a major Sino-Singaporean project. A significant economic disparity exists between the wealthy south and the less developed north.

### Education and Research
Jiangsu hosts 168 institutions of higher education, the most in China. It has 16 Double First-Class Construction universities. Six of its cities rank among the world's top 200 for scientific research output (Nature Index), with Nanjing ranked 5th globally.

### Culture and Demographics
The province's culture is segmented into four subdivisions (Wu, Jinling, Huaiyang, Xuhuai). It is known for traditional arts like Kunqu opera and Pingtan storytelling, as well as Suzhou's classical gardens and silk. The population is predominantly Han Chinese, with a high urbanization rate (73.94% in 2021).

### Transportation
Jiangsu has a highly developed transport network. The Beijing–Shanghai high-speed railway and expressway are key arteries. Numerous bridges and tunnels cross the Yangtze River. Six cities have operational metro systems, with Nanjing's being the first in the province (opened 2005).

## Terms
- **Grand Canal**: A major historical waterway linking northern and southern China, built through Jiangsu after 581.
- **Double First-Class Construction**: A Chinese government initiative to develop world-class universities and disciplines.
- **Nature Index**: A database tracking research output in high-quality scientific journals.
- **HDI (Human Development Index)**: A composite statistic of life expectancy, education, and per capita income indicators.
- **Prefecture-level city**: An administrative division in China, ranking above a county but below a province.
- **Kunqu**: One of the oldest and most influential forms of Chinese opera, originating in Kunshan, Jiangsu.
- **Pingtan**: A form of Chinese storytelling accompanied by music, with regional variants.
- **Suzhou Industrial Park**: A major collaborative economic zone between China and Singapore, located in Suzhou.

## Debates and open questions
- The source notes a "tense division" between the more urban, prosperous south and the poorer, rural north of Jiangsu, indicating an ongoing regional disparity.
- The historical trajectory of "incipient industrialization and capitalism" in the late Ming Jiangnan textiles industry is noted as a trend that was "aborted," suggesting scholarly debate on its potential.

# Moldova

## Overview
Moldova is a landlocked parliamentary republic in Eastern Europe, bordered by Romania and Ukraine. Its modern history is defined by shifting sovereignty between the Ottoman Empire, Russian Empire, Romania, and the Soviet Union, culminating in independence in 1991. The country faces ongoing challenges from the breakaway region of Transnistria, significant emigration, and a geopolitical tug-of-war between pro-Western and pro-Russian influences, with its current government strongly pursuing European Union integration.

## Timeline
- **1359** — Foundation of the Principality of Moldavia.
- **1812** — Eastern half of Moldavia (Bessarabia) ceded to the Russian Empire.
- **1918** — Bessarabia declares independence and integrates into Romania.
- **1940** — Soviet Union annexes Bessarabia, creating the Moldavian SSR.
- **1990** — De facto control of Transnistria established by a breakaway government.
- **1991** — Moldavian SSR declares independence as the Republic of Moldova.
- **1992** — Transnistria War ends, solidifying the region's de facto separation.
- **2020** — Maia Sandu elected president on a pro-Western, anti-corruption platform.
- **2022** — Moldova granted EU candidate status.
- **2023** — Formal EU accession talks begin.

## Body

### History and Identity
Most of Moldovan territory was part of the Principality of Moldavia from the 14th century until 1812, when it was ceded to the Russian Empire as Bessarabia. After a brief period of autonomy and union with Romania following World War I, the region was re-annexed by the Soviet Union in 1940. Independence was declared in 1991, but the eastern strip of territory, Transnistria, has been under de facto separatist control since 1990. The country's name derives from the Moldova River, and a historical debate exists over whether "Moldovan" constitutes a distinct ethnic and linguistic identity separate from Romanian.

### Government and Politics
Moldova is a parliamentary democratic republic. The 1994 constitution establishes a unicameral Parliament, an independent judiciary, and a Constitutional Court. The president is the head of state, elected by popular vote. Under President Maia Sandu, elected in 2020 and re-elected in 2024, the government has prioritized anti-corruption measures and EU membership. This pro-Western shift has heightened tensions with Russia, which has been accused of destabilization efforts. In 2023, Moldova suspended participation in the Commonwealth of Independent States (CIS) and expelled Russian diplomats.

### Economy and Society
Moldova is Europe's poorest country by GDP per capita, with an economy reliant on agriculture, services, and remittances from its large diaspora. The wine industry is a major export sector, though markets have shifted from Russia to the EU. The country faces severe demographic decline due to emigration and low birth rates. The population is predominantly ethnic Moldovan (77%), with significant Ukrainian, Gagauz, and Russian minorities. Romanian is the official language, though Russian is widely spoken. Eastern Orthodox Christianity is the dominant religion.

### Regional Tensions
The unresolved conflict in Transnistria, where Russian troops are stationed, remains a core challenge to sovereignty. The autonomous region of Gagauzia, with a Turkic-speaking population, also presents tensions, having voted in 2014 to reject closer EU ties in favor of the Russian-led Eurasian Economic Union. Relations with Romania are exceptionally strong, with Romania providing significant economic and energy support. While unification is a topic of public debate, most in both countries believe the timing is not right.

## Terms
- **Bessarabia:** The historical name for the region that is now the majority of Moldova, ceded by the Ottoman Empire to Russia in 1812.
- **Transnistria:** An unrecognised breakaway state on the eastern bank of the Dniester river, under de facto control since 1990 with Russian military presence.
- **Gagauzia:** An autonomous territorial unit in southern Moldova, home to the Turkic-speaking Gagauz people, who are predominantly Orthodox Christian and often favor closer ties with Russia.
- **Chernozem:** A highly fertile black soil that covers about three-quarters of Moldova's land area, making it ideal for agriculture.
- **Moldavian SSR:** The Moldavian Soviet Socialist Republic, the administrative unit of the Soviet Union from 1940 to 1991.
- **Sfatul Țării:** The parliament of the Moldavian Democratic Republic that voted for union with Romania in 1918.
- **CIS:** The Commonwealth of Independent States, a regional organization of former Soviet republics, which Moldova is in the process of leaving.

## Debates and Open questions
- **Identity:** A persistent debate concerns whether the Moldovan language and people are distinct from Romanian, a question with political implications for national identity and potential unification.
- **EU Accession:** Moldova has set a 2030 target for EU membership, but the process is complex, requiring extensive reforms. The EU has stated the pathway does not depend on resolving the Transnistria conflict.
- **Transnistria:** The final status of the breakaway region remains unresolved, with the central government in Chișinău lacking control over the territory.
- **Geopolitical Alignment:** The country is deeply divided between pro-European and pro-Russian factions, a tension exacerbated by Russia's invasion of neighboring Ukraine.

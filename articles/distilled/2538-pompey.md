# Pompey

## Overview
Gnaeus Pompeius Magnus (106–48 BC), known as Pompey the Great, was a Roman general and statesman whose career epitomized the late Republic's turmoil. He rose as a protégé of Sulla, achieved extraordinary military success, and formed a pivotal alliance with Julius Caesar. His conflict with Caesar culminated in civil war and his assassination, marking a decisive step in the Republic's collapse.

## Timeline
- **106 BC** — Born in Picenum.
- **83 BC** — Joins Sulla's civil war, raising a legion.
- **82 BC** — Defeats Marian forces in Sicily and Africa; celebrates first triumph.
- **76–71 BC** — Fights the Sertorian War in Hispania.
- **70 BC** — First consulship (with Crassus); restores tribunician powers.
- **67 BC** — Defeats Mediterranean pirates via the Lex Gabinia.
- **66–62 BC** — Wins Third Mithridatic War; reorganizes the Roman East.
- **61 BC** — Celebrates third triumph; Senate refuses to ratify his eastern settlements.
- **59 BC** — Forms First Triumvirate with Caesar and Crassus; marries Caesar's daughter Julia.
- **52 BC** — Serves as sole consul; marries Cornelia Metella.
- **49 BC** — Caesar crosses the Rubicon; Pompey retreats to Greece.
- **48 BC** — Defeated at Battle of Pharsalus; assassinated in Egypt.

## Body

### Early Career and Sulla's Civil War
Born to a provincial noble family, Pompey began military service under his father in the Social War (91–87 BC). After his father's death, he raised a legion and joined Sulla in 83 BC. His early victories earned him the title *imperator*. Sulla compelled Pompey to divorce his first wife and marry his stepdaughter. Pompey's ruthless campaigns in Sicily and Africa against the Marian faction earned him the nickname *adulescentulus carnifex* ("teenage butcher") but also secured his first triumph and the cognomen *Magnus* ("the Great").

### The Sertorian War and First Consulship
After suppressing a rebellion by Marcus Aemilius Lepidus, Pompey was given command in Hispania (76–71 BC) to fight the rebel general Quintus Sertorius. Despite initial defeats, Pompey's persistence and Sertorius's assassination by his own officers led to victory. His return to Italy coincided with the end of Spartacus's slave revolt, which he claimed credit for, angering his rival Crassus. In 70 BC, Pompey and Crassus served as consuls, restoring powers to the plebeian tribunes that Sulla had removed.

### Pirate War and Eastern Reorganization
Pompey's greatest strategic success came with his command against the Mediterranean pirates (67 BC). Using the Lex Gabinia, he systematically cleared the seas in three months. He then received an even larger command in the East (66 BC) to finish the war against Mithridates VI of Pontus. Pompey defeated Mithridates, annexed Syria, and intervened in the Hasmonean civil war in Judea, capturing Jerusalem in 63 BC. His reorganization of the East created new provinces and client kingdoms, vastly increasing Roman revenue and his own personal wealth and patronage.

### The First Triumvirate and Drift to Civil War
Upon returning to Rome, Pompey found the Senate, led by the conservative *optimates*, hostile to ratifying his eastern settlements and granting land to his veterans. To overcome this, he allied with Julius Caesar and Crassus in the informal First Triumvirate (59 BC). This alliance secured Pompey's settlements and Caesar's rise, but its bonds weakened after Julia's death (54 BC) and Crassus's defeat in Parthia (53 BC). Pompey aligned with the *optimates*, and as sole consul in 52 BC, he passed laws that threatened Caesar's legal immunity. The breakdown of their alliance led Caesar to cross the Rubicon in 49 BC, igniting civil war.

### Civil War and Death
Pompey, though backed by the Senate and superior numbers, was outmaneuvered by Caesar's speed and veteran troops. He evacuated Italy for Greece, where he built a large army. After a prolonged standoff near Dyrrhachium, Pompey was decisively defeated by Caesar at the Battle of Pharsalus (48 BC). Fleeing to Egypt, he was assassinated by courtiers of the young Pharaoh Ptolemy XIII, who hoped to curry favor with Caesar. His death ended the last major opposition to Caesar's dominance.

## Terms
*   **Cursus honorum**: The sequential order of public offices required for political advancement in the Roman Republic.
*   **Optimates**: A conservative political faction in the late Roman Senate that sought to preserve senatorial authority and traditional norms.
*   **First Triumvirate**: An informal political alliance (59 BC) between Pompey, Julius Caesar, and Marcus Licinius Crassus to counter the Senate's conservative *optimates*.
*   **Lex Gabinia**: A law (67 BC) granting Pompey extraordinary command and resources to suppress piracy across the Mediterranean.
*   **Lex Manilia**: A law (66 BC) granting Pompey supreme command in the war against Mithridates VI of Pontus.
*   **Proconsul**: A governor of a Roman province, often a former consul, granted military command.
*   **Imperator**: A title of honor bestowed on a victorious general by his troops.
*   **Client kingdom**: A state that maintained internal autonomy but was politically and militarily dependent on Rome.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Pompey's Ambition**: Scholars debate whether Pompey's actions were driven by a consistent desire for monarchical power or by a more reactive defense of his status and the traditional Republican order against rivals like Caesar.
*   **The Triumvirate's Nature**: Historians question whether the First Triumvirate was a formal, binding agreement or a series of ad-hoc political deals based on mutual self-interest.
*   **Responsibility for Civil War**: The extent to which Pompey's political miscalculations and alliance with the *optimates* provoked Caesar's invasion, versus Caesar's own ambition, is a central point of contention.
*   **Generalship**: While praised as a superb strategist and organizer, Pompey's tactical caution and reluctance to risk open battle, especially against Caesar, are frequently criticized by ancient and modern sources.

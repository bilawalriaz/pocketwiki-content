# History of Saudi Arabia

## Overview
The history of Saudi Arabia traces the transformation of the Arabian Peninsula from a region of ancient tribes and civilizations into a modern, oil-rich kingdom. It is defined by the rise of the Al Saud dynasty, the unification of its historical regions, and the profound impact of Islam and petroleum on its development and global influence.

## Timeline
- **c. 63,000 years ago** — Evidence of early human habitation in the Arabian Peninsula.
- **c. 570** — Birth of the Prophet Muhammad in Mecca.
- **632** — Death of Muhammad; beginning of the Rashidun Caliphate and rapid Muslim expansion.
- **1727** — Emergence of the Al Saud dynasty in Diriyah.
- **1744** — Alliance between Muhammad ibn Saud and Muhammad ibn Abd al-Wahhab, founding the First Saudi State.
- **1818** — Ottoman-Egyptian forces destroy the First Saudi State.
- **1902** — Abdulaziz Al Saud (Ibn Saud) captures Riyadh, beginning the Third Saudi State.
- **1925** — Abdulaziz conquers the Hejaz, including Mecca and Medina.
- **1932** — The Kingdom of Saudi Arabia is officially proclaimed.
- **1938** — Commercial oil is discovered in the Eastern Province.
- **1973** — Saudi-led oil embargo dramatically increases global oil prices and Saudi political leverage.
- **2015** — King Salman ascends the throne; his son Mohammed bin Salman becomes crown prince in 2017.

## Body

### Pre-Islamic and Early Islamic Arabia
The territory of modern Saudi Arabia was home to ancient cultures, including the Dilmun and Al-Magar civilizations. Before Islam, the region was religiously diverse, with polytheistic tribes worshipping deities like Hubal and al-'Uzzā, alongside Jewish and Christian communities. The rise of Islam in the 7th century, led by the Prophet Muhammad, unified the Arabian tribes. Following his death in 632, the Rashidun and subsequent Umayyad caliphates rapidly expanded Muslim rule far beyond Arabia, though Mecca and Medina remained the faith's spiritual centers.

### The Saudi-Wahhabi Alliance and Unification
The modern Saudi state began in 1727 with the Al Saud dynasty. A pivotal 1744 alliance with the religious reformer Muhammad ibn Abd al-Wahhab provided the ideological foundation for expansion. The First Saudi State (1727–1818) briefly controlled much of the peninsula before being destroyed by Ottoman-Egyptian forces. After a period of Ottoman domination and rivalry with the Al Rashid dynasty, Abdulaziz Al Saud returned from exile in 1902. Through a series of conquests, he united the regions of Najd, Al-Hasa, Asir, and finally the Hejaz (1925), culminating in the proclamation of the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia in 1932.

### Oil, Modernization, and Regional Influence
The discovery of oil in 1938 transformed Saudi Arabia's economy and geopolitical standing. A 1945 agreement with the United States established a security-for-oil partnership. Under King Faisal (1964–1975), oil wealth fueled modernization, and the 1973 oil embargo cemented Saudi Arabia's role as a global energy power. The 1979 seizure of the Grand Mosque by extremists prompted a stricter enforcement of Islamic norms. King Fahd (1982–2005) maintained close ties with the U.S., notably during the 1991 Gulf War, which increased internal tensions. Subsequent kings, Abdullah and Salman, pursued cautious reforms. The current de facto ruler, Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman, has launched Saudi Vision 2030 to diversify the economy and enacted social reforms, while also centralizing power and facing criticism over human rights issues.

## Terms
*   **Al Saud:** The ruling royal family of Saudi Arabia, founded in 1727.
*   **Wahhabism:** A conservative, puritanical form of Sunni Islam founded by Muhammad ibn Abd al-Wahhab, allied with the Al Saud.
*   **Ikhwan:** The Wahhabi-Bedouin tribal army used by Abdulaziz to conquer the Hejaz and other regions in the 1920s.
*   **Hejaz:** The western region of Saudi Arabia containing the holy cities of Mecca and Medina.
*   **Najd:** The central, arid plateau of Saudi Arabia, the traditional heartland of the Al Saud.
*   **Sharifate of Mecca:** The Hashemite-ruled state in the Hejaz that predated Saudi control.
*   **Caliphate:** The political-religious state succeeding Muhammad, led by a caliph. The Rashidun, Umayyad, and Abbasid were major caliphates.
*   **Saudi Vision 2030:** A strategic framework announced in 2016 to diversify Saudi Arabia's economy away from oil dependence.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   The exact nature and extent of pre-Islamic religious practices in Arabia remain a subject of archaeological and historical study.
*   The long-term social and political consequences of the centralization of power under Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman are debated.
*   The balance between economic modernization (Vision 2030) and the preservation of the traditional religious-social order continues to be a key challenge.

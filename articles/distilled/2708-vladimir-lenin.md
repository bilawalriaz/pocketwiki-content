# Vladimir Lenin

## Overview
Vladimir Lenin was a Russian revolutionary and political theorist who led the Bolshevik seizure of power in 1917, becoming the first head of Soviet Russia and the Soviet Union. He adapted Marxism into Leninism, emphasizing a vanguard party and revolutionary violence to establish a dictatorship of the proletariat. His government's policies, including the Red Terror and the New Economic Policy, laid the foundation for the Soviet state, making him one of the 20th century's most influential and controversial figures.

## Timeline
- **22 April 1870** — Born Vladimir Ilyich Ulyanov in Simbirsk, Russia.
- **May 1887** — Brother Aleksandr executed for plotting to assassinate Tsar Alexander III.
- **1893** — Moves to Saint Petersburg; becomes a senior Marxist activist.
- **1897–1900** — Exiled to Siberia; marries Nadezhda Krupskaya.
- **1902** — Publishes *What Is to Be Done?*, outlining the need for a vanguard party.
- **1903** — Key role in the RSDLP split, leading the Bolshevik faction.
- **1917 (April)** — Returns to Russia after the February Revolution; issues the "April Theses."
- **1917 (October)** — Leads the Bolsheviks in the October Revolution, overthrowing the Provisional Government.
- **March 1918** — Signs the Treaty of Brest-Litovsk, taking Russia out of World War I.
- **1918–1922** — Oversees the Red Terror and the Russian Civil War.
- **1921** — Introduces the New Economic Policy (NEP) in response to famine and uprisings.
- **30 December 1922** — Becomes Chairman of the Council of People's Commissars of the newly formed USSR.
- **21 January 1924** — Dies at Gorki; succeeded by Joseph Stalin.

## Body

### Early Life and Revolutionary Path
Born to an upper-middle-class family, Lenin embraced revolutionary socialism after his brother's 1887 execution. Expelled from Kazan Imperial University for protesting, he later studied law and became a Marxist activist in Saint Petersburg. After exile in Siberia, he lived in Western Europe, becoming a leading theorist in the Russian Social Democratic Labour Party (RSDLP). In 1903, he led the Bolshevik faction in a party split, advocating a disciplined vanguard party to lead the proletariat to revolution.

### Seizure of Power and Early Government
Following the February Revolution of 1917, Lenin returned to Russia and called for the overthrow of the Provisional Government. The Bolsheviks seized power in the October Revolution. Lenin's government initially shared power with Left Socialist Revolutionaries but quickly centralized control in the Communist Party. It issued decrees nationalizing land and banks, and withdrew from World War I via the Treaty of Brest-Litovsk, which ceded significant territory. Opponents were suppressed in the Red Terror, a campaign of state violence administered by the Cheka secret police.

### Civil War, Economic Policy, and the Soviet State
Lenin's government fought and won the Russian Civil War (1917–1922) against anti-Bolshevik White armies and foreign interventionists. The regime promoted world revolution through the Communist International (Comintern). Facing famine and popular uprisings, Lenin introduced the New Economic Policy (NEP) in 1921, allowing limited private enterprise to revive the economy. In 1922, several non-Russian nations were forcibly re-united into the Soviet Union. Lenin's health declined after strokes in 1922 and 1923; he died in 1924, with Stalin emerging as his successor.

### Ideology and Legacy
Lenin was a devout Marxist whose ideas, termed Leninism, emphasized the role of a vanguard party, revolutionary violence, and the dictatorship of the proletariat. He viewed liberal democracy as a facade for bourgeois rule and advocated for a centralized, socialist state. His administration established a one-party state and a pervasive cult of personality after his death. Historians debate whether his government was a totalitarian dictatorship or a flawed experiment in proletarian democracy, with its legacy shaping communist movements worldwide.

## Terms
- **Bolsheviks**: The radical, majority faction of the RSDLP led by Lenin, which advocated a disciplined vanguard party to seize power.
- **Mensheviks**: The more moderate, minority faction of the RSDLP that favored a broader party membership and a gradual transition to socialism.
- **Dictatorship of the proletariat**: A Marxist concept, championed by Lenin, describing a transitional state where the working class holds political power to suppress the bourgeoisie and build socialism.
- **Cheka**: The first Soviet state security organization, established in 1917, responsible for political repression and the Red Terror.
- **Red Terror**: A campaign of mass arrests, executions, and internments carried out by the Cheka against perceived enemies of the revolution, primarily from 1918 to 1922.
- **New Economic Policy (NEP)**: Introduced in 1921, it was a strategic retreat from war communism, allowing limited private trade and small-scale industry to revive the economy after civil war devastation.
- **Communist International (Comintern)**: An international organization founded in 1919 to coordinate the world communist movement and promote world revolution.
- **Vanguard party**: Lenin's concept of a small, disciplined, and centralized party of professional revolutionaries to lead the working class in overthrowing capitalism.
- **War communism**: The economic and political policies implemented by the Bolsheviks during the Russian Civil War (1918–1921), characterized by nationalization, forced grain requisitions, and suppression of private trade.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Dictatorship vs. Democracy**: Historians debate whether Lenin's government was a totalitarian dictatorship from its inception or a flawed but genuine attempt at proletarian democracy that was later corrupted by Stalin.
- **Continuity with Stalinism**: A major scholarly debate concerns whether Stalinism was a natural continuation of Leninist principles or a fundamental betrayal of them. Traditionalists see continuity, while revisionists emphasize a rupture.
- **Role of Violence**: Scholars analyze whether Lenin's endorsement of revolutionary violence was a pragmatic necessity for survival or an intrinsic part of his ideology, and how it paved the way for later mass repression.
- **Legacy and Cult of Personality**: The development of the Lenin cult, contrary to his own stated wishes, and its function in legitimizing Soviet power remains a subject of study, as does his enduring symbolic legacy in post-Soviet states.

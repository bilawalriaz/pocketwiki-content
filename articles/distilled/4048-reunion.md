# Réunion

## Overview
Réunion is a volcanic island in the Indian Ocean and an overseas department and region of France. Its strategic location has made it a key French military and economic outpost, while its history of colonization and slavery has created a diverse, multicultural Creole society. Today, it is fully integrated into France and the European Union, with an economy transitioning from sugar production to tourism and services.

## Timeline
- **c. 1507** — First European discovery by Portuguese explorer Diogo Fernandes Pereira; island is uninhabited.
- **1642** — France officially claims the island, naming it Île Bourbon.
- **1793** — Renamed "Réunion" during the French Revolution to break with the Bourbon monarchy.
- **1810** — British forces capture the island; it is renamed Isle Bourbon.
- **1814** — Returned to France under the Treaty of Paris.
- **1848** — Slavery is abolished on 20 December; the island is renamed "Réunion" again.
- **1946** — Réunion becomes an overseas department of France.
- **1998** — Opening of Pierrefonds Airport catalyzes tourism growth.
- **2005-2006** — Major chikungunya epidemic infects over 250,000 people.
- **2010** — Part of the island is designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
- **2025** — Cyclone Garance causes significant damage and power outages.

## Body

### History and Colonization
Before French settlement in the 17th century, Réunion was uninhabited. The French developed a plantation economy based on coffee and later sugar, relying on enslaved people from East Africa and Madagascar. Slavery was abolished in 1848, but was replaced by a system of indentured labor (*engagés*) from India, China, and other regions, creating a diverse population. The island's name changed multiple times, reflecting political shifts in France. It became a French overseas department in 1946, granting it the same status as departments in mainland France.

### Politics and Administration
Réunion is governed under Article 73 of the French Constitution, meaning French laws apply directly. It is an outermost region of the European Union and part of the eurozone. The island is represented in the French National Assembly and Senate. Administratively, it is divided into 24 communes grouped into four arrondissements. France maintains a significant military presence, hosting the French Armed Forces of the Southern Indian Ocean Zone (FAZSOI).

### Geography and Climate
The island is 2,512 km² and dominated by two volcanoes: the dormant Piton des Neiges (3,070 m), the highest point in the Indian Ocean, and the highly active Piton de la Fournaise. The terrain features deep canyons and three large calderas (*cirques*). The climate is tropical, with a rainy season (January–March) and a dry season (May–November). Rainfall varies dramatically, from 447.7 mm annually on the dry west coast to nearly 11,000 mm in the highlands, making it one of the wettest places on Earth.

### Demographics and Culture
The population is predominantly of mixed descent, with a Creole culture blending African, European, Indian, Chinese, and Malagasy influences. French is the official language, but Réunion Creole is widely spoken. Christianity is the main religion, with significant Hindu and Muslim minorities. Traditional music includes *sega* (a ballroom dance) and *maloya*, a ritual dance with African roots that was once suppressed but is now a symbol of cultural identity. Cuisine features dishes like *cari* (curry) and *rougail*.

### Economy
The economy has shifted from a sugar monoculture to a service-based model, with tourism now a primary resource. In 2019, the GDP per capita was 61.7% of metropolitan France's level. Unemployment, though improved, remains high at 19.0% in 2023. Agriculture, mainly sugarcane, employs 10% of the workforce. The island's main port in Le Port combines commercial, passenger, and naval functions.

## Terms
- **Overseas department and region (DROM):** A French administrative division with the same status as departments in mainland France, governed by Article 73 of the Constitution.
- **Creole:** A person born on the island regardless of ethnic origin; also refers to the blended culture and language.
- **Réunion Creole:** A French-based creole language widely spoken informally.
- **Engagés:** A system of indentured labor that replaced slavery after 1848.
- **Maloya:** A traditional Réunionese music and dance form with African origins, once suppressed but now a cultural symbol.
- **Cirque:** A large, steep-walled amphitheater-like valley formed by erosion or volcanic collapse.
- **Piton de la Fournaise:** One of the world's most active shield volcanoes, located on Réunion.
- **Outermost region (OMR):** A designation for EU territories located far from the continent, which benefit from certain adaptations to EU law.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The origin of the name "Réunion" is debated; it may symbolically refer to the 1792 meeting of federated troops in Paris, but no document confirms this.
- The "Children of Creuse" relocation program (1963–1982), where over 1,600 Réunionese children were sent to mainland France, is a source of ongoing controversy. Lawsuits alleging kidnapping were dismissed by French and European courts.
- The environmental impact of climate change on the island's unique ecosystems and coral reefs is a growing concern, with studies indicating adaptation strategies are incompletely integrated into planning.

# Wayne Gretzky

## Overview
Wayne Gretzky is a Canadian former professional ice hockey player widely regarded as the greatest in the sport's history. His unparalleled career records, particularly in assists and total points, and his transformative impact on the style of play in the National Hockey League (NHL) cement his legacy. He later served as a head coach and executive in the NHL.

## Body

### Early Life and Junior Career
Born in Brantford, Ontario, Gretzky honed his skills on a backyard rink built by his father, Walter. He was a prodigy, playing at levels far above his age group. Despite being undersized, his intelligence, stamina, and ability to anticipate the play were exceptional. He often set up behind the opponent's net, an area nicknamed "Gretzky's office." After a standout junior career, he was drafted third overall by the Sault Ste. Marie Greyhounds in 1977, where he first wore jersey number 99.

### WHA and NHL Debut with the Edmonton Oilers
Gretzky turned professional in 1978 by signing with the WHA's Indianapolis Racers. After eight games, he was traded to the Edmonton Oilers. He won the WHA's rookie of the year award. When the WHA merged with the NHL in 1979, Gretzky joined the Oilers. In his first NHL season, he won the Hart Trophy as league MVP, the first of a record nine. He quickly shattered scoring records, becoming the only player to score over 200 points in a season (a feat he accomplished four times). He led the Oilers to four Stanley Cup championships between 1984 and 1988.

### "The Trade" and the Los Angeles Kings
In a move that caused national outrage in Canada, Gretzky was traded to the Los Angeles Kings on August 9, 1988. He led the Kings to their first Stanley Cup Final in 1993. His arrival is credited with popularizing hockey in California and the American Sun Belt, leading to NHL expansion in the region. He broke Gordie Howe's career goal-scoring record in 1994.

### Later Career and Retirement
Gretzky played briefly for the St. Louis Blues in 1996 before finishing his career with the New York Rangers (1996–1999). He retired in 1999, holding 61 NHL records. He was immediately inducted into the Hockey Hall of Fame, and the NHL retired his number 99 league-wide.

### Post-Retirement and Legacy
After retiring, Gretzky served as executive director for Canada's 2002 Olympic gold-medal team. He became part-owner and later head coach of the Phoenix Coyotes (2000–2009). He returned to the Oilers in an executive role in 2016. His career totals of 894 goals and 1,963 assists remain NHL records. He is widely considered the greatest ice hockey player of all time.

## Terms
- **Hart Memorial Trophy:** Awarded annually to the NHL's most valuable player.
- **Art Ross Trophy:** Awarded annually to the player who leads the league in points at the end of the regular season.
- **Stanley Cup:** The championship trophy awarded to the winner of the NHL playoffs.
- **Conn Smythe Trophy:** Awarded annually to the most valuable player in the NHL playoffs.
- **Lady Byng Memorial Trophy:** Awarded for sportsmanship and performance.
- **Hockey Hall of Fame:** An institution that honors and preserves the history of ice hockey.
- **World Hockey Association (WHA):** A professional ice hockey league that competed with the NHL from 1972 until a merger in 1979.
- **Gretzky's Office:** The area behind the opponent's net, where Gretzky often positioned himself to set up plays.

## Debates and open questions
- **Greatest of All Time:** While the NHL and numerous polls name Gretzky the greatest player, debates persist among fans and analysts comparing him to other legends like Bobby Orr and Gordie Howe.
- **The "Gretzky Rule":** A 1985 NHL rule change that eliminated offsetting penalties was dubbed the "Gretzky Rule" because it was seen as targeting the Oilers' success in open-ice situations. The rule was later reversed.
- **Order of Canada:** Gretzky has not attended his investiture ceremony as a Companion of the Order of Canada, the country's highest civilian honor, drawing criticism for not fulfilling the ceremonial duty.

# Theodoric I

## Overview
Theodoric I was king of the Visigoths from 418 to 451. He is most famous for leading his people into a crucial alliance with Rome to defeat Attila the Hun at the Battle of the Catalaunian Plains, where he was killed. His reign marked the consolidation of the Visigothic kingdom in Gaul and its complex, often adversarial, relationship with the Western Roman Empire.

## Timeline
- **418** — Theodoric I succeeds King Wallia as ruler of the Visigoths.
- **423** — After Roman Emperor Honorius's death, Theodoric attempts to capture Arelate but is repelled by Aëtius with Hunnic aid.
- **436** — Theodoric conquers Narbo Martius, gaining Mediterranean access, but is later defeated by Litorius and the Huns.
- **444** — Theodoric's daughter is mutilated and returned by her husband, Huneric of the Vandals, creating lasting enmity.
- **449** — Theodoric allies with the Suevic king Rechiar, marrying him to his daughter.
- **451** — Theodoric joins a Roman-led coalition against Attila the Hun.
- **451** — Theodoric is killed at the Battle of the Catalaunian Plains.
- **453** — His son Thorismund is murdered by his brother Theodoric II.
- **466** — Theodoric II is murdered by his brother Euric.

## Body

### Early Reign and Expansion
Theodoric I completed the Visigothic settlement in Roman Gaul (Aquitania, Novempopulana, and Narbonensis) begun by his predecessor. He exploited Roman internal weakness after 423 to expand southward, though initial attempts to seize key cities like Arelate were checked by the Roman general Aëtius. A subsequent treaty was concluded, and the Roman noble Avitus later lived at Theodoric's court, teaching his sons.

### Conflict with Rome and the Vandals
Theodoric's attempts to expand further, such as capturing Narbo Martius in 436, were initially resisted by Roman forces aided by Huns. After a defeat at Tolosa, a peace treaty was accepted, possibly acknowledging Visigothic sovereignty. His foreign policy was also shaped by conflict with the Vandal king Geiseric, whose son Huneric mutilated Theodoric's daughter in 444. This enmity, along with the Vandal alliance with Rome, eventually led Theodoric to ally with the Suevi in Iberia.

### Alliance Against the Huns and Death
Facing the existential threat of Attila's invasion of Gaul in 451, Theodoric formed an alliance with his long-time rival Aëtius. He led the Visigothic army, including his sons Thorismund and Theodoric, to join the Roman coalition. The allied forces successfully defended Aurelianorum and then engaged Attila at the Catalaunian Plains. The Visigothic contribution was decisive for the victory, but Theodoric was killed in the battle, either thrown from his horse or slain by an Ostrogothic spearman.

### Succession and Legacy
Theodoric's death led to a violent succession among his sons. Thorismund was elected king immediately but was murdered by Theodoric II in 453. Theodoric II ruled until 466, when he was killed by his brother Euric. Euric's reign ended the cycle of fratricide, lasting until 484. Theodoric I's sacrifice at the Catalaunian Plains made him a revered figure in Western history, later inspiring J.R.R. Tolkien's character King Théoden.

## Terms
- **Visigoths**: A Germanic people who established a kingdom in Gaul and later Hispania.
- **Battle of the Catalaunian Plains (451)**: A major battle where a Roman-Visigothic coalition defeated Attila the Hun, halting his invasion of Western Europe.
- **Aëtius**: The powerful Roman *magister militum* (master of soldiers) who was both an enemy and, ultimately, an ally of Theodoric.
- **Huns**: A nomadic people from Central Europe, led by Attila, who posed a major threat to the Roman Empire in the 5th century.
- **Suevi**: A Germanic tribe in Iberia, with whom Theodoric formed a late alliance.
- **Civitas Aurelianorum**: The Roman city of Orléans, which was saved from Attila by the allied forces.
- **Tolosa**: The Visigothic capital, modern-day Toulouse.

## Debates and Open questions
- The exact date of Theodoric's birth is uncertain, given as c. 390 or 393.
- It is debated whether Theodoric I issued his own legislation.
- The circumstances of his death are recorded with two different accounts by the historian Jordanes.
- The precise nature of the peace treaty after 436 is unclear; it may have involved Roman recognition of Visigothic sovereignty.

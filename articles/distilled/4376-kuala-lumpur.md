# Kuala Lumpur

## Overview
Kuala Lumpur (KL) is the capital and a federal territory of Malaysia, serving as the nation's cultural, financial, and political center. It evolved from a 19th-century tin-mining settlement into a major global metropolis, known for its rapid development, multicultural population, and iconic modern architecture like the Petronas Towers.

## Timeline
- **c. 1857** — Founded as a tin-mining town at the confluence of the Gombak and Klang rivers.
- **1880** — Becomes the capital of the state of Selangor.
- **1896** — Chosen as the capital of the Federated Malay States.
- **1942** — Captured by the Imperial Japanese Army during World War II.
- **1957** — Hosts the Declaration of Independence for the Federation of Malaya.
- **1972** — Granted city status.
- **1974** — Becomes a Federal Territory, ceded by Selangor.
- **1998** — Hosts the Commonwealth Games; Petronas Towers completed.
- **1999** — Federal government administrative and judicial functions relocate to Putrajaya.
- **2020** — Named UNESCO World Book Capital.

## Body

### Founding and Early Development
Kuala Lumpur was established around 1857 as a mining settlement. The official account credits Malay chief Raja Abdullah with sending Chinese miners to open tin mines. The town grew as a collection point for tin, with early leaders like Kapitan Cina Yap Ah Loy and British Resident Frank Swettenham instrumental in its development. Yap Ah Loy rebuilt the town after fires and floods, while Swettenham modernized infrastructure, stipulating brick construction and improving sanitation. A railway line completed in 1886 spurred rapid growth.

### Political Evolution and Modernization
Kuala Lumpur became the capital of Selangor in 1880 and of the Federated Malay States in 1896. It was the founding capital of Malaya (1957) and Malaysia (1963). In 1974, it was separated from Selangor to become a Federal Territory. While the executive and judicial branches of the federal government moved to Putrajaya in 1999, Kuala Lumpur remains the legislative capital and home to the national parliament and the monarch's official residence. The city has hosted major international events, including the 1998 Commonwealth Games.

### Economy and Urban Landscape
Kuala Lumpur is Malaysia's primary economic hub, with a service-dominated economy centered on finance, tourism, and retail. It is home to Bursa Malaysia (the stock exchange) and the headquarters of many major corporations. The city's skyline is defined by modern skyscrapers, most notably the Petronas Towers, once the world's tallest buildings. Tourism is a key industry, with the city being one of the most visited in the world. The urban area has expanded into the wider Klang Valley metropolitan region, known as Greater Kuala Lumpur.

### Demographics and Culture
Kuala Lumpur is Malaysia's most populous city, with a highly diverse population historically dominated by Chinese communities but now with a Bumiputera (Malay and other indigenous groups) majority. The city is pluralistic, with Malay as the principal language and English widely used. It is a center for arts and culture, hosting numerous festivals, museums, and performing arts venues. The architecture blends colonial, Islamic, and modern styles.

## Terms
- **Federal Territory**: A Malaysian federal area administered directly by the central government, not part of any state.
- **Bumiputera**: A constitutional term for Malays and other indigenous peoples of Malaysia, granting certain privileges.
- **Kapitan Cina**: A historical title for the headman of the Chinese community, appointed by Malay rulers.
- **Mukim**: A administrative subdivision or district in Malaysia.
- **Klang Valley**: An urban conglomeration in Malaysia centered on Kuala Lumpur and including surrounding cities like Shah Alam and Petaling Jaya.
- **Petronas Towers**: Iconic twin skyscrapers in Kuala Lumpur, a symbol of Malaysia's modern development.
- **Putrajaya**: The federal administrative capital of Malaysia, where executive and judicial government functions are based.
- **Rapid KL**: The brand name for the integrated public rail and bus network in the Klang Valley.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The exact origin of the name "Kuala Lumpur" is debated, with theories involving a "muddy river" (Sungai Lumpur) or a corruption of other words.
- The precise identity of the first settlers is unclear, with evidence of pre-1857 settlements by indigenous Temuan people, Chinese miners, and Sumatran traders.
- The accuracy of early population censuses, such as the 1891 census, is considered uncertain by historians.

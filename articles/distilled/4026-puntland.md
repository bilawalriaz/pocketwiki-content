# Puntland

## Overview
Puntland is a semi-autonomous federal member state in northeastern Somalia, established in 1998. It is significant as a relatively stable region that has pursued state-building and democratization while maintaining it is part of Somalia, unlike the separatist Somaliland. Its political trajectory and recent rejection of the federal government's authority highlight ongoing tensions within Somalia's federal system.

## Body

### History and Establishment
The region's history includes the Majeerteen and Hobyo Sultanates, which signed protectorate treaties with Italy in the late 19th century before being annexed into Italian Somaliland. Following the 1991 Somali Civil War, a constitutional conference in Garoowe led to the establishment of Puntland in 1998 as an autonomous state seeking to provide services and security. Unlike Somaliland, Puntland was formed as a descendant-based entity for the Harti community and seeks unity within a federal Somalia. It has been in territorial disputes with Somaliland over the Sool, Sanaag, and Ayn regions.

### Politics and Governance
Puntland uses a parliamentary system. Its political history includes a brief unrest in 2001 over a presidential term extension. The Farole administration (2009-2014) implemented reforms in security, justice, and public finance, and initiated a democratization process. This process included passing a new constitution in 2009, inaugurating it in 2012, and opening political party registration. In 2014, Abdiweli Mohamed Ali Gaas was elected president, followed by Said Abdullahi Deni in 2019. In April 2024, amid a constitutional crisis, Puntland announced it would operate independently until a federal constitution is agreed upon via a referendum in which it participates.

### Geography and Demographics
Puntland occupies 212,510 km² in northeastern Somalia, featuring a semi-arid climate with sparse rainfall. Key geographical points include Cape Guardafui and Ras Hafun. As of 2016, its population was 4,334,633, primarily from the Somali ethnic group (especially Darod), with 39% being nomads. Islam is the main religion. The population density is 20/km².

### Economy and Infrastructure
The economy relies on livestock, fishery, frankincense, and trade, with Bosaso and Galkayo as key centers. Puntland has the lowest poverty rate in Somalia (27%). Infrastructure includes a 750 km north-south highway, the seaport at Bosaso, and airports like Bender Qassim International. Oil exploration has been pursued since the 2000s, with agreements signed with companies like Range Resources and CCECC. The Puntland Maritime Police Force combats piracy and illegal fishing.

### Education and Media
The education system follows a 2-4-4-4 structure and is considered progressive, with efforts to close the gender gap. The Ministry of Education oversees this system, which includes seven major universities. Media includes the public Puntland TV and Radio, private stations like ETN TV, and online outlets such as Puntland Post and Horseed Media.

## Terms
- **Federal member state**: A constituent state of the Federal Republic of Somalia.
- **Sultanate**: A historical Muslim state led by a sultan.
- **Protectorate**: A state or territory under the protection and partial control of another power.
- **Clan-based entity**: A political or social organization structured around kinship and lineage.
- **Parliamentary system**: A system of government where the executive is derived from and accountable to the legislature.
- **Democratization process**: The transition toward a political system with multi-party elections and constitutional governance.
- **Semi-arid**: A climate with low and variable rainfall, supporting limited agriculture.
- **Pastoralism**: A form of agriculture based on herding livestock.
- **Product Sharing Agreement (PSA)**: A contract between a government and an oil company detailing the division of production and profits.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The exact location of the ancient "Land of Punt" mentioned in Egyptian sources is unknown, with Somalia being one proposed location.
- The status of the Sool, Sanaag, and Ayn (SSC) regions is disputed between Puntland and Somaliland.
- The implications and sustainability of Puntland's 2024 declaration to "act independently" amid Somalia's constitutional crisis are unclear.

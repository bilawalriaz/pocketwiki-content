# Kumasi

## Overview
Kumasi is the capital of Ghana's Ashanti Region and the historic heart of the Asante Empire. As the nation's second-largest city and a major commercial hub, it blends deep cultural heritage with modern economic activity, serving as the seat of the Asantehene (Ashanti king) and home to West Africa's largest market.

## Timeline
- **1680s** — Founded by Asantehene Osei Kofi Tutu I as the capital of the Ashanti Empire.
- **1695** — Rose to prominence as the empire's capital.
- **1701** — Ashanti victory over Denkyira established the empire's dominance.
- **1874** — British forces burned parts of the city during the Third Anglo-Ashanti War.
- **1896** — British took control of Kumasi.
- **1926** — Ashanti King Prempeh I returned from exile; Kumasi gained ceremonial control over sub-states.
- **1935** — Colonial administration fully restored the Asantehene's role.
- **1945** — Maxwell Fry published the "Garden City of West Africa" plan for Kumasi.
- **1952** — Kumasi College of Technology (now KNUST) opened.
- **1957** — Ghana's independence; Kumasi became the Ashanti Regional capital.
- **2021** — Population recorded as 443,981 in the city proper.

## Body

### History and Etymology
The name "Kumasi" derives from the Twi word *Kumase*, meaning "under the kum tree." Oral traditions debate its founding, with most attributing the site selection to the priest Okomfo Anokye, who planted kum seeds and decreed the one that grew would mark the capital. The city was built on the eastern slopes of a ridge. It became the capital of the expanding Ashanti Empire in 1695 under Osei Tutu I. The city's population during the empire's peak is debated, with Ashanti sources estimating 100,000 in the early 19th century versus European estimates of 12,000–15,000. British colonial forces sacked the city in 1874, leading to a period of decline. After the return of King Prempeh I from exile in 1926, the city's traditional governance was gradually restored.

### Geography and Climate
Kumasi is located in a rainforest region about 200 km from Accra, near Lake Bosomtwe. It has a tropical savanna climate (Köppen *Aw*) with two rainy seasons: a major season from March to July and a minor one from September to November. Annual rainfall averages about 1,200 mm. The city faces environmental pressures, including water pollution in rivers like the Wiwi and Subin due to encroachment for industrial and residential use.

### Culture and Economy
Kumasi is the center of Asante culture, known for traditional kente cloth and the Kejetia Market, the largest in West Africa with over 10,000 stalls. The city's economy is a national hub; the Ashanti Region produced 21% of Ghana's national output in 2014, with 48% of that originating in Kumasi. Key sectors include services, manufacturing, and timber processing, with half of the country's timber processed in the city. The "Kumawood" film industry produces local-language films on low budgets.

### Infrastructure and Governance
The city is administered by the Kumasi Metropolitan Assembly, led by a mayor appointed by Ghana's president. It is divided into 10 sub-metros. Infrastructure includes the Prempeh I International Airport, which was renovated for international flights by 2022, and a road network with interchanges like the Asokwa Interchange. Public transport includes "tro-tros" (privately owned mini-buses) and a metro bus service. Rail service to Accra and Sekondi-Takoradi is currently suspended. The city is home to the Kwame Nkrumah University of Science and Technology (KNUST) and the Komfo Anokye Teaching Hospital, a 750-bed facility.

## Terms
- **Asantehene**: The king of the Ashanti people and ruler of the Ashanti Empire.
- **Kente**: A traditional, brightly colored woven textile of the Asante people.
- **Kejetia Market**: The largest open-air market in West Africa, located in Kumasi.
- **Twi**: The principal language of the Ashanti people, widely spoken in Kumasi.
- **Kumawood**: The local film industry in Kumasi, producing films in the Twi language.
- **Tro-tro**: A commonly used privately owned mini-bus providing public transport in Ghana.
- **Compound house**: A popular housing style in Kumasi, consisting of single rooms around a central courtyard, housing multiple households.
- **Garden City**: A nickname for Kumasi, originating from a 1945 urban plan inspired by the Garden City movement.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Founding Figures**: Oral traditions conflict on whether Osei Tutu I, Nana Oti Akenten, or Nana Obiri Yeboa was the primary founder of Kumasi.
- **Historical Population**: There is a significant discrepancy between Ashanti and European estimates of the city's population during the 19th century.
- **Urban Green Space**: The effectiveness of the 1945 "Garden City" plan is debated, as studies show many green spaces have been lost to urbanization and weak land control.

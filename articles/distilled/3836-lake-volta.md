# Lake Volta

## Overview
Lake Volta is the world's largest artificial reservoir by surface area, created by the Akosombo Dam in Ghana. It is a critical source of hydroelectric power for Ghana and neighboring countries, but its creation involved massive social disruption and continues to present environmental and safety challenges.

## Timeline
- **1915** — Geologist Albert Ernest Kitson first proposes a dam on the Volta River for bauxite smelting.
- **Post-WWII** — Kwame Nkrumah revives the dam project as part of Ghana's modernization drive.
- **1957** — Ghana gains independence; main industrial sponsor, Aluminium Limited, withdraws.
- **Late 1950s** — Kaiser Aluminum joins; funding secured, but resettlement planning is deprioritized.
- **1964** — Chaotic resettlement of 78,000 people begins as the dam is constructed.
- **1965** — Akosombo Dam is completed, flooding 740 villages and forming Lake Volta.
- **2011** — Volta River Authority allocates $2.5M to develop eco-tourism on Dodi Island. |
- **Oct 11, 2025** — A boat accident near Kete Krachi kills 15 people, including 11 children.

## Body

### Creation and Resettlement
The Akosombo Dam was conceived in 1915 to exploit the Volta River's energy for smelting locally-sourced bauxite. The project was revived after World War II by President Kwame Nkrumah for national modernization. A preparatory commission noted the need to relocate over 60,000 people but also highlighted benefits like increased fishing. After Ghana's independence in 1957, the original sponsor withdrew, and Kaiser Aluminum entered the project. Funding was secured with cost-cutting that deprioritized resident relocation. Construction began before resettlement planning was complete. The 1964 resettlement was chaotic, displacing 78,000 people from 740 villages to build 52 new towns. This disruption caused long-term hardship, broke traditional fishing and farming practices, and led to a spike in water-related diseases like malaria and schistosomiasis.

### Economy and Environment
The dam produces 912 MW of electricity for Ghana and export to Togo and Benin. The lake is vital for transportation, fisheries, and tourism, with Dodi Island as a major site. A recent enterprise harvests submerged tropical hardwood from the flooded forests, aiming to create a sustainable hardwood source while improving navigation. However, the lake's water level is decreasing due to global warming, reducing power generation. An estimated 7,000 to 10,000 children work in the lake's fishing industry. Reports have described this as slavery, a claim contested by some academics who argue it is often exploitative child labor within a traditional apprenticeship system.

### Safety and Disasters
The lake's vast size and heavy use lead to safety incidents. A major disaster occurred on October 11, 2025, when a grossly overloaded passenger boat capsized near Kete Krachi, killing 15 people, including 11 children. The Ghana Maritime Authority launched an investigation and pledged to intensify safety enforcement.

## Terms
- **Artificial reservoir**: A large, human-made lake created by damming a river.
- **Akosombo Dam**: The hydroelectric dam on the Volta River that created Lake Volta.
- **Bauxite**: The primary ore from which aluminium is refined.
- **Resettlement**: The organized relocation of people from their homes, often due to a large infrastructure project.
- **Hydroelectric power**: Electricity generated from the energy of falling or flowing water.
- **Schistosomiasis**: A disease caused by parasitic worms released by freshwater snails.
- **Eco-tourism**: Responsible travel to natural areas that conserves the environment and improves the well-being of local people.

## Debates and open questions
- **Child labor vs. apprenticeship**: There is debate over whether child labor in Lake Volta's fishing industry constitutes slavery or is a form of exploitative but traditional apprenticeship.
- **Project legacy**: The long-term success of the dam's modernization goals is questioned, as government-led development failed and communities had to adapt on their own.

# Flanders

## Overview
Flanders is the Dutch-speaking northern region of Belgium, encompassing a distinct cultural, linguistic, and political community. It is a federal entity within Belgium with its own government and parliament, yet its official capital is Brussels, a bilingual enclave. Historically a medieval economic powerhouse, Flanders is now one of Europe's wealthiest regions, though its identity and political status remain subjects of ongoing debate within Belgium.

## Timeline
- **c. 900 AD** — The County of Flanders emerges, stretching from the Strait of Dover to the Scheldt estuary.
- **1302** — The Battle of the Golden Spurs sees Flemish cities defeat a French annexation attempt.
- **1384** — The County of Flanders passes to Philip the Bold, Duke of Burgundy, beginning Burgundian rule.
- **1500** — Charles V, future Holy Roman Emperor, is born in Ghent.
- **1556** — The Low Countries pass from the Habsburg Empire to the Spanish Crown under Philip II.
- **1585** — Antwerp falls to Spanish forces, ending the Eighty Years' War for the Southern Netherlands.
- **1795** — France annexes Flanders, dividing it into départements.
- **1815** — The Congress of Vienna places Flanders in the United Kingdom of the Netherlands.
- **1830** — The Belgian Revolution leads to the independence of Belgium, including Flanders.
- **1960s** — Belgium begins state reforms, establishing official Dutch- and French-speaking regions.
- **1993** — Belgium becomes a federal state, formally recognizing communities and regions.

## Body

### Terminology and Definitions
The term "Flanders" has multiple overlapping meanings. It can refer to the historical County of Flanders, which once included parts of modern France and the Netherlands. In modern Belgium, it primarily denotes the Dutch-speaking community and the political entities that govern it: the Flemish Region (competent in economic matters) and the Flemish Community (competent in cultural matters). The Flemish Community also covers the Dutch-speaking inhabitants of the Brussels-Capital Region, which is not part of the Flemish Region. The ambiguity between the historical county and the modern political region persists.

### History
The area's history is marked by shifting sovereignty. After Roman and Frankish periods, the medieval County of Flanders became a wealthy, urbanized trading and cloth-making center. It later passed through Burgundian, Habsburg, and Spanish rule. The Eighty Years' War (1568–1648) split the Low Countries, with the north becoming the Dutch Republic and the south (including Flanders) remaining under Spanish control. This period saw economic decline and emigration, notably from Antwerp. After French annexation and a brief union with the Netherlands, Flanders became part of independent Belgium in 1830. The 20th century saw the rise of the Flemish Movement, advocating for Dutch-language rights, which culminated in state reforms transforming Belgium into a federal state.

### Government and Politics
Flanders is governed by a merged institution of the Flemish Community and Region, with a directly elected parliament and government based in Brussels. The official language is Dutch. Politically, Flanders is characterized by a spectrum of parties, from traditional Christian Democrats, Liberals, and Socialists to nationalist parties like the New Flemish Alliance (N-VA), which advocates for greater autonomy or independence. While some advocate for secession, sociological studies indicate that a majority of Flemings remain proud to be Belgian and oppose dissolution.

### Economy and Geography
Flanders is a highly urbanized, densely populated, and fertile region. Its economy, once based on textiles, modernized rapidly after World War II and is now service-oriented and export-driven, with a high GDP per capita. Key industries include chemicals, diamonds (centered in Antwerp), and food processing. The Port of Antwerp is Europe's second-largest. Geographically, it is mostly flat, with a coastal plain and a central plain, and includes complex exclaves and enclaves with the Netherlands and Wallonia.

### Culture and Society
The standard language is Dutch, with regional variations known as Flemish. The educational system is split into secular and subsidized religious (mostly Catholic) branches, both enjoying high quality and freedom. Historically Catholic, Flanders is now religiously diverse, with a significant Muslim minority. The region has a vibrant cultural scene, with internationally recognized film, music festivals (like Tomorrowland), and sports, particularly cycling.

## Terms
- **Flemish Region**: The federal region of Belgium comprising the five northern provinces, competent in territorial matters like economy and infrastructure.
- **Flemish Community**: The federal community of Belgium competent in cultural, educational, and language-related matters for all Dutch speakers, including those in Brussels.
- **Flemish Movement**: A political and cultural movement advocating for the rights and autonomy of Dutch speakers in Belgium.
- **State Reforms**: A series of constitutional changes since the 1970s that transformed Belgium from a unitary state into a federal state with communities and regions.
- **Pillarisation (Verzuiling)**: The historical division of Flemish society into ideological "pillars" (Catholic, Socialist, Liberal), each with its own institutions.
- **Flemish Diamond**: The highly urbanized and economically powerful area between Brussels, Antwerp, Ghent, and Leuven.
- **Beeldenstorm**: The wave of Protestant iconoclasm in 1566 that was a prelude to the Eighty Years' War.
- **Franco-Flemish School**: A style of Renaissance polyphonic vocal music from the Low Countries, also known as the Dutch School.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Political Status**: The extent of Flanders' autonomy is a central issue in Belgian politics, with parties ranging from those favoring the current federal system to those advocating for confederalism or full independence.
- **Identity and Nationalism**: There is a debate about the strength of Flemish versus Belgian identity. While nationalist parties have gained electoral support, surveys show a majority of Flemings still identify as Belgian and oppose secession.
- **Linguistic Landscape**: The status of French in Brussels and the municipalities with language facilities along the language border remains a point of contention between linguistic communities.

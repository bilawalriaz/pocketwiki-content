# Republic of the Congo

## Overview
The Republic of the Congo (Congo-Brazzaville) is a Central African nation whose history is defined by pre-colonial Bantu kingdoms, French colonization, a post-independence Marxist-Leninist state, and a modern era dominated by oil wealth and political instability under long-serving President Denis Sassou Nguesso. Its economy is heavily dependent on petroleum, making it vulnerable to global price fluctuations, while its society is diverse, with Christianity as the predominant faith.

## Timeline
- **c. 1500 BC** — Bantu-speaking peoples expand into the region, displacing earlier inhabitants.
- **13th century** — Three main state federations dominate the western Congo Basin, including the Vungu-led confederation.
- **14th century** — The Tio Kingdom emerges.
- **1484** — Portuguese explorer Diogo Cão reaches the mouth of the Congo River.
- **1880** — France establishes sovereignty north of the Congo River via a treaty with Tio King Iloo I.
- **1908** — France organizes French Equatorial Africa (AEF), with Brazzaville as its capital.
- **15 August 1960** — The Republic of the Congo gains independence from France.
- **1969** — Marien Ngouabi proclaims the People's Republic of the Congo (PRC), a Marxist-Leninist state.
- **1992** — Multi-party elections are held; Pascal Lissouba becomes the first elected president.
- **1997** — Civil war erupts; Denis Sassou Nguesso returns to power with Angolan military support.
- **2002** — Sassou Nguesso wins a presidential election in a vote criticized by international observers.
- **2015** — A constitutional referendum allows Sassou Nguesso to run for another term.
- **2018** — The Republic of the Congo joins OPEC.

## Body

### Pre-colonial and Colonial Era
The region was home to Bantu-speaking tribes for millennia, who established trade networks. By the 13th century, powerful federations like the Vungu confederation and later kingdoms such as the Tio (14th century) and Loango (16th century) dominated the area. European contact began with the Portuguese in 1484, leading to commercial relationships and the transatlantic slave trade. French colonization formalized in 1880 with a treaty with the Tio king. The territory became part of French Equatorial Africa in 1908, with Brazzaville as the federal capital. Colonial economic development focused on resource extraction, including the construction of the Congo–Ocean Railway, which cost an estimated 14,000 lives. During World War II, Brazzaville served as the symbolic capital of Free France.

### Independence and Political Instability
The Republic of the Congo became autonomous in 1958 and fully independent on 15 August 1960. Its early post-independence years were marked by political turmoil. The first president, Fulbert Youlou, was ousted in 1963. His successor, Alphonse Massamba-Débat, adopted "scientific socialism" and aligned with the Eastern Bloc. In 1969, Marien Ngouabi established Africa's first "people's republic," the PRC. After Ngouabi's assassination in 1977, Denis Sassou Nguesso eventually took power in 1979, ruling until 1992. The collapse of the Soviet Union led to a transition to multi-party democracy, but a civil war in 1997 brought Sassou Nguesso back to power. He has remained president since, winning elections in 2002, 2009, and 2016, the latter following a controversial 2015 constitutional referendum.

### Economy and Geography
The economy is dominated by petroleum, which in 2008 accounted for 65% of GDP and 85% of government revenue. This dependence has caused economic instability, particularly after the 2015 drop in oil prices. The country has attempted economic reforms and privatization with international support, but progress has been repeatedly disrupted by conflict. Geographically, it is located on the Equator, featuring coastal plains, central plateaus, and dense tropical forests. The Forest Massif of Odzala-Kokoua is a UNESCO World Heritage Site. The population is concentrated in the southwest, with 70% living in urban areas like Brazzaville and Pointe-Noire.

### Society and Demographics
The population is ethnically diverse, with the Kongo (about half the population), Teke, and Mbochi as major groups. French is the official language, with Kituba and Lingala as national languages. Christianity is the most widely professed faith. The country faces social challenges, including high maternal and infant mortality rates, and issues related to the rights of indigenous Pygmy peoples, who have historically faced discrimination.

## Terms
- **Bantu expansions:** A series of migrations of Bantu-speaking peoples across central, eastern, and southern Africa over millennia.
- **French Equatorial Africa (AEF):** A federation of French colonial territories in Central Africa, of which the Congo was a part, with Brazzaville as its capital.
- **People's Republic of the Congo (PRC):** The Marxist-Leninist state name of the country from 1969 to 1992.
- **Congolese Labour Party (PCT):** The ruling political party of Denis Sassou Nguesso, originally the National Revolutionary Movement.
- **Semi-presidential system:** A system of government where a president exists alongside a prime minister and cabinet, with the latter being responsible to the legislature.
- **OPEC:** The Organization of the Petroleum Exporting Countries, which the Republic of the Congo joined in 2018.
- **Kimberley Process:** An international certification scheme to prevent the trade of "conflict diamonds."

## Debates and Open Questions
The fairness and legitimacy of elections held under President Sassou Nguesso, particularly the 2015 constitutional referendum and the 2016 presidential election, are widely disputed by opposition groups and international observers. The equitable distribution of oil wealth and its impact on poverty and inequality remains a critical economic and social challenge. The long-term political stability of the country is uncertain given its history of civil conflict and the dominance of a single political figure.

# Narendra Modi

## Overview
Narendra Modi is the Prime Minister of India, serving since 2014, and the longest-serving PM outside the Indian National Congress. A member of the Hindu nationalist Bharatiya Janata Party (BJP) and the Rashtriya Swayamsevak Sangh (RSS), his tenure is marked by economic reforms, a strong centralization of power, and a controversial Hindu nationalist agenda. His leadership has been credited with driving economic growth and infrastructure development but also criticized for democratic backsliding, social polarization, and the handling of the 2002 Gujarat violence.

## Body

### Early Life and Political Rise
Modi was born into a Gujarati family of Other Backward Class (OBC) background. He was introduced to the RSS at age eight, becoming a full-time worker in 1971. During the 1975-77 Emergency, he worked underground for the RSS. The RSS assigned him to the BJP in 1985, where he rose through the ranks, becoming a key organizer for party campaigns. His electoral strategy was central to BJP victories in Gujarat in the late 1990s, leading to his appointment as BJP general secretary in 1998.

### Chief Minister of Gujarat (2001–2014)
Modi became Chief Minister in 2001. His tenure was defined by the 2002 Gujarat violence, which began after a train carrying Hindu pilgrims burned near Godhra, killing approximately 60 people. Subsequent riots resulted in over 1,000 deaths, mostly Muslims. A Supreme Court-appointed Special Investigation Team (SIT) found no evidence to prosecute Modi, though scholars and critics have described the violence as a pogrom with state complicity. Economically, his administration promoted industrial growth, investment, and infrastructure, but was criticized for poor human development indices in health, poverty, and education.

### Premiership: First Term (2014–2019)
Modi led the BJP to a parliamentary majority in 2014. His government centralized power, replaced the Planning Commission with NITI Aayog, and launched initiatives like Digital India and Make in India. Key policies included the 2016 demonetization, which caused economic disruption, and the 2017 GST rollout. Socially, his administration emphasized sanitation (Swachh Bharat Mission) and reduced spending on healthcare and education. Foreign policy focused on improving ties with the US and East Asia.

### Premiership: Second and Third Terms (2019–Present)
Re-elected in 2019, Modi's government revoked the special status of Jammu & Kashmir and passed the controversial Citizenship Amendment Act (CAA), which sparked protests and the 2020 Delhi riots. The COVID-19 pandemic saw strict lockdowns and a devastating second wave; the WHO estimated 4.7 million Indian deaths, a figure the government disputed. In 2024, the BJP lost its majority in the lower house, forcing it to lead a coalition. In 2025, a terrorist attack in Pahalgam led to a brief military conflict with Pakistan.

### Governance, Economy, and Society
Modi's economic policies favored privatization, liberalization, and attracting foreign direct investment. His tenure saw a decline in GDP growth rates compared to the previous government and rising unemployment. Socially, his administration has been accused of promoting a Hindu nationalist agenda, weakening democratic institutions and press freedom, and presiding over democratic backsliding. He has maintained consistently high public approval ratings throughout his premiership.

## Terms
*   **Hindutva:** A right-wing Hindu nationalist ideology advocating for India as a Hindu nation.
*   **Rashtriya Swayamsevak Sangh (RSS):** A right-wing Hindu nationalist paramilitary volunteer organization.
*   **Bharatiya Janata Party (BJP):** The right-wing political party to which Modi belongs.
*   **Demonetization:** The 2016 policy of invalidating high-value banknotes to curb black money and corruption.
*   **Goods and Services Tax (GST):** A comprehensive indirect tax on the supply of goods and services implemented in 2017.
*   **Citizenship Amendment Act (CAA):** A 2019 law providing a path to citizenship for non-Muslim refugees from neighboring countries.
*   **Democratic Backsliding:** The gradual decline in the quality of democracy, including weakening of institutions and civil liberties.
*   **Swachh Bharat Mission:** A national sanitation campaign launched in 2014.
*   **NITI Aayog:** The public policy think tank of the Government of India, replacing the Planning Commission.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Role in 2002 Gujarat Violence:** While an SIT found no evidence to prosecute him, scholars and critics debate the extent of his administration's complicity in the riots.
*   **Economic Impact:** Assessments are divided on whether Gujarat's economic growth accelerated under his chief ministership and whether his national policies (like demonetization) helped or harmed the economy.
*   **Democratic Health:** There is significant debate over whether his tenure has strengthened or weakened India's democratic institutions, press freedom, and secular fabric.
*   **Social Agenda:** His government's policies, such as the CAA and the revocation of Kashmir's autonomy, are debated as either necessary reforms or steps toward a majoritarian, exclusionary social order.

# Balochistan, Pakistan

## Overview
Balochistan is Pakistan's largest province by area but its least populated, located in the country's southwest. It is a region of rugged plateaus and deserts with a complex history of migrations, empires, and political struggles, making it a geographically strategic but economically underdeveloped area.

## Timeline
- **c. 7000 BCE** — Earliest known farming settlement at Mehrgarh in the Kach Gandava Plain.
- **302 BCE** — Seleucus I Nicator cedes Gedrosia to the Mauryan emperor Chandragupta.
- **644 CE** — Arab army invades Makran during the caliphate of Omar.
- **11th century** — Baloch people first noted in Makran; large-scale migrations begin by the 14th century.
- **17th century** — Brahui Qambrani dynasty establishes the Khanate of Kalat.
- **1876** — Robert Sandeman negotiates the Treaty of Kalat, bringing the region under British protection.
- **1879** — Afghan Emir cedes Quetta, Pishin, Harnai, and Sibi to the British after the Second Afghan War.
- **29 June 1947** — The Shahi Jirga votes to join Pakistan, though the vote's legitimacy is disputed.
- **27 March 1948** — The Khan of Kalat accedes to Pakistan.
- **1958** — Pakistan purchases Gwadar, an Omani colony since 1783.
- **1970** — The modern province of Balochistan is established.
- **2003–present** — An ongoing insurgency by autonomy-seeking Baloch groups.

## Body

### History and Migrations
The region's history predates the Baloch people, with ancient settlements like Mehrgarh and rule by empires including the Achaemenid, Mauryan, and Sasanian. The Baloch people themselves migrated east from Kerman (in present-day Iran) into Makran, with large-scale movement occurring by the 14th century. The medieval period saw Arab conquests, followed by the rise of local dynasties like the Khanate of Kalat, which expanded under Mir Nasir Khan I in the 18th century.

### Colonial and Modern Political History
British influence began with the 1876 Treaty of Kalat, which brought the Khanate and princely states under British protection while allowing internal autonomy. After the 1947 partition of British India, the province's accession to Pakistan was contentious. The Shahi Jirga voted to join Pakistan, but this is disputed as representatives from the Khanate of Kalat were excluded. The Khan of Kalat initially sought independence but acceded in 1948, sparking revolts and a series of insurgencies (1948–50, 1958–60, 1962–69, 1973–77) that continue today, driven by demands for autonomy and resource control.

### Geography and Climate
Balochistan is an extensive, rugged plateau covering 347,190 km², 44% of Pakistan's land. It borders Iran, Afghanistan, and the Arabian Sea, with the strategic Gwadar Port on its coast. The climate is predominantly arid desert, with extreme temperatures ranging from below -20°C in northern highlands to over 50°C in the plains. Only about 5% of the land is arable.

### Demographics and Society
The province has a low population density. The 2023 census recorded 14.9 million people. The major ethnic groups are Baloch (concentrated in the west and south) and Pashtuns (in the north). The primary languages are Balochi (39.91%) and Pashto (34.34%), with Brahui (17.22%) also significant. The population is overwhelmingly Muslim, with small Hindu and Christian minorities.

### Economy and Resources
Agriculture and livestock contribute about 47% of the provincial GDP, but the economy is hampered by aridity and underdevelopment. Balochistan is rich in natural resources, including natural gas and minerals, but their extraction has not significantly benefited the local population. The province has the highest poverty and lowest literacy rates in Pakistan. Major projects like the Gwadar Port and the China-Pakistan Economic Corridor (CPEC) aim to boost development, but progress is uneven.

## Terms
- **Khanate of Kalat**: A Brahui princely state established in the 17th century that ruled much of Balochistan until its accession to Pakistan in 1948.
- **Shahi Jirga**: A grand council of tribal elders in British Balochistan that voted on the province's accession to Pakistan in 1947.
- **Instrument of Accession**: The legal document through which princely states joined either India or Pakistan after the 1947 partition.
- **Gedrosia**: The ancient Greek name for the arid region of southeastern Balochistan.
- **Makran**: A historical and geographical region along the Arabian Sea coast, part of modern Balochistan.
- **Brahui**: A Dravidian language spoken mainly in central Balochistan, associated with some of the region's earliest inhabitants.
- **CPEC (China–Pakistan Economic Corridor)**: A major infrastructure and trade project linking Gwadar Port to China, passing through Balochistan.
- **NFC Award**: A formula-based mechanism for distributing financial resources between Pakistan's federal government and its provinces.

## Debates and open questions
- The legitimacy of the 1947 Shahi Jirga vote on accession to Pakistan is disputed, with claims that representatives from the Khanate of Kalat were excluded.
- The causes and nature of the ongoing Baloch insurgency are debated, with factors including demands for autonomy, grievances over resource control, and allegations of external interference.
- The effectiveness and equity of development projects like CPEC in benefiting the local Baloch population versus serving broader national or international interests are contested.

# Kaduna

## Overview
Kaduna is a major city in north-western Nigeria, serving as the capital of Kaduna State and a key historical political center for Northern Nigeria. Its strategic location on the Kaduna River and its role as a transportation hub have made it a vital trade and industrial center for the region.

- **1917** — Became the capital of Nigeria's former Northern Region.
- **1967** — Lost its status as the Northern Region capital.
- **1976** — North Central State was renamed Kaduna State, with Kaduna as its capital.
- **1982** — Kaduna International Airport commenced operations.

## Body

### History and Administration
Kaduna was founded in 1900 by British colonists, who selected the site for its proximity to the Lagos-Kano Railway. It became the capital of the Northern Region in 1917, a status it held until 1967. The city remains influential as a headquarters for political, military, and cultural organizations in Northern Nigeria. The modern Kaduna State was formed in 1976 from the former North Central State, and its current boundaries were established in 1991 when Katsina was separated into its own state. The state is administered through 23 Local Government Areas (LGAs).

### Economy
Kaduna is a major industrial center, manufacturing textiles, machinery, steel, aluminum, petroleum products, and bearings. The textile industry has declined due to foreign imports and neglect. Other industries include plastics, pharmaceuticals, leather goods, and automobile assembly, with a Peugeot plant located in the city. The Kaduna Refining and Petrochemical Company (KRPC) is one of Nigeria's four main oil refineries. Agriculture is also significant, with the Bank of Agriculture headquartered in the city; key exports include cotton, peanuts, sorghum, and ginger. A 2009 World Bank survey indicated Kaduna had one of the highest unemployment rates in Nigeria at 20%.

### Infrastructure and Transport
The city is a major transportation hub. It is served by Kaduna International Airport (opened 1982) and is a key junction on Nigeria's railway network. A branch line connects the Lagos–Nguru and Port Harcourt–Maiduguri railways. The completed Abuja–Kaduna segment of the planned Lagos–Kano Standard Gauge Railway provides passenger service. Infrastructure upgrades under Governor Nasir el-Rufai included flyovers, underpasses, and streetlights. The Nigerian military has a significant presence, including the Nigerian Defence Academy and the Air Force Institute of Technology.

### Education and Culture
Kaduna hosts numerous educational institutions, including Kaduna State University, the Nigerian Defence Academy, Kaduna Polytechnic (established 1968), and the National Open University of Nigeria's study center. The city has diverse places of worship, with the Sultan Bello Mosque as a central mosque and various Christian denominations represented. Sports facilities include the Ahmadu Bello Stadium, Ranchers Bees Stadium, and the Kaduna Polo Club.

## Terms
- **LGA (Local Government Area):** The administrative division system introduced in 1976 to delegate responsibilities to local councils.
- **Cape gauge railway:** A railway track gauge with a width of 1,067 mm (3 ft 6 in), forming part of Nigeria's existing rail network.
- **Standard Gauge Railway:** A railway track gauge with a width of 1,435 mm (4 ft 8½ in), used for the new Lagos–Kano line.
- **KRPC (Kaduna Refining and Petrochemical Company):** One of Nigeria's four major oil refineries, supplied by pipeline from the Niger Delta.
- **Northern Region:** A former administrative region of Nigeria for which Kaduna served as capital from 1917 to 1967.

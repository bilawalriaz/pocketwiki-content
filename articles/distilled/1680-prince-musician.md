# Prince (musician)

## Overview
Prince Rogers Nelson (1958–2016) was an American singer, songwriter, multi-instrumentalist, and filmmaker widely regarded as one of the greatest musicians of his generation. A pioneer of the "Minneapolis sound"—a synthesis of funk, rock, R&B, synth-pop, and new wave—he achieved global superstardom with the 1984 film and soundtrack *Purple Rain*, becoming the first artist to simultaneously hold the number-one film, album, and single in the United States. Known for his flamboyant androgynous image, prolific output (39 studio albums released during his lifetime), and virtuosic musicianship, he sold over 100 million records worldwide. His career was marked by a fierce battle for artistic ownership, culminating in a 1993 name change to an unpronounceable "Love Symbol" to escape contractual constraints with Warner Bros. He died at age 57 from an accidental fentanyl overdose at his Paisley Park estate.

## Body

### Early Life and Musical Foundations
Prince Rogers Nelson was born at Mount Sinai Hospital in Minneapolis to Mattie Della Shaw, a jazz singer, and John Lewis Nelson, a pianist and songwriter who performed as "Prince Rogers." All four grandparents were from Louisiana. The jazz drummer Louis Hayes was his paternal cousin. Prince was named after his father's stage name; he disliked it as a child and went by "Skipper." He stated he was born epileptic and had seizures until an angel told him he would be well. Prince wrote his first song, "Funk Machine," on his father's piano at age seven. His parents divorced when he was ten; his mother remarried Hayward Baker, who took Prince to see James Brown—a pivotal influence. His younger sister, Tyka Nelson, was born May 18, 1960; both siblings developed a keen interest in music encouraged by their father. After a turbulent adolescence moving between parents and the basement of neighbors the Andersons, he befriended André Cymone (future bandmate). He attended Central High School, played sports, and studied classical ballet at the Minnesota Dance Theatre, later becoming a patron of the Joffrey Ballet.

### 1975–1983: Breakthrough and the Minneapolis Sound
Prince's professional start came via Pepe Willie's band 94 East (1975). A demo tape produced by Chris Moon led to management by Owen Husney, who signed Prince, then aged 19, to a management contract. Husney helped create a demo at Sound 80 Studios, resulting in interest from Warner Bros., A&M, and Columbia. With Husney's help, Prince signed a recording contract with Warner Bros. Records in 1977. The record company agreed to give Prince creative control for three albums and retain his publishing rights. *For You* (1978) showcased his one-man-band approach. He formed his first touring band in 1979 (including Cymone, Dez Dickerson, Gayle Chapman, Doctor Fink, Bobby Z.). The self-titled *Prince* (1979) went platinum, driven by "I Wanna Be Your Lover." *Dirty Mind* (1980), recorded in his home studio, stripped the sound down to a raw, explicit fusion of funk, new wave, and R&B. *Controversy* (1981) introduced his sensational spelling (U, 2, 4) and saw him open for the Rolling Stones, where he was booed off stage in LA. He launched side project The Time (1981), writing and playing most instruments under pseudonyms like "Jamie Starr." *1999* (1982) was a commercial breakthrough; the title track protested nuclear proliferation, and "Little Red Corvette" was one of the first two videos by Black artists—along with Michael Jackson's "Billie Jean"—played in heavy rotation on MTV, which had been perceived as against "black music" until CBS President Walter Yetnikoff threatened to pull all CBS videos.

### 1984–1987: The Purple Rain Era and the Revolution
With the *1999* tour, the backing band was christened the Revolution (Lisa Coleman, Doctor Fink, Bobby Z., Brown Mark, Wendy Melvoin replacing Dickerson). Prince leveraged his contract for a film deal, resulting in *Purple Rain* (1984). The semi-autobiographical film grossed $68M US; the soundtrack sold 13M+ US copies, spent 24 weeks at #1, and yielded #1 hits "When Doves Cry" and "Let's Go Crazy." Prince simultaneously held the #1 film, album, and single—a historic first. The album won an Academy Award and two Grammys. Tipper Gore's objection to "Darling Nikki" spurred the creation of the Parents Music Resource Center and the "Parental Advisory" label. *Around the World in a Day* (1985) and *Parade* (1986, soundtrack to the critically panned *Under the Cherry Moon*) followed. "Kiss" hit #1; "Manic Monday" (written for the Bangles) hit #2. After the Parade Tour, Prince disbanded the Revolution, firing Melvoin and Coleman.

### 1987–1990: Sign o' the Times, The Black Album, and Batman
Post-Revolution, Prince merged material from the band project *Dream Factory* and his solo *Camille* project (featuring a sped-up, female-voiced persona) into the triple album *Crystal Ball*. Warner Bros. forced a trim to a double LP: *Sign o' the Times* (March 1987). It peaked at #6 US but was critically hailed (Pazz & Jop #1), selling 3.2M copies. Singles "Sign o' the Times" (#3), "U Got the Look" (#2, duet with Sheena Easton), and "I Could Never Take the Place of Your Man" (#10) charted well. A European tour was filmed for a theatrical release (Nov 1987) with poor box office. The dark, funk/hip-hop *The Black Album* was pressed (500,000 copies) but recalled after a spiritual epiphany; it became a legendary bootleg. *Lovesexy* (1988), a spiritual counterpoint, reached #11 US; the 84-show world tour lost money due to lavish staging. Prince contributed to Madonna's *Like a Prayer* (1989) uncredited. He then produced the *Batman* soundtrack (1989) in weeks, hitting #1 with "Batdance," but signed away publishing rights to Warner Bros. *Graffiti Bridge* (1990), a *Purple Rain* sequel film, flopped ($4.2M gross), though the soundtrack hit #6.

### 1991–1996: New Power Generation, Name Change, and Contract War
Prince formed the New Power Generation (NPG) in 1991 (Sonny T., Tommy Barbarella, Rosie Gaines, Michael Bland, Hornheads). *Diamonds and Pearls* (1991) hit #3, yielding five US hits including #1 "Cream." He signed a reported $100M Warner Bros. deal for six albums in 1992. The follow-up, *Love Symbol* (1992), sold 2.8M but disappointed the label. In 1993, rebelling against Warner's refusal to release his backlog faster, he changed his name to the "Love Symbol," becoming "The Artist Formerly Known as Prince." Warner mailed floppy disks with a custom font for the symbol. *The Hits/The B-Sides* compilation followed. "The Most Beautiful Girl in the World" (1994) hit #3 US via independent Bellmark Records. Prince wrote "slave" on his face to protest contract terms. *Come* (1994) and *The Gold Experience* (1995, delayed by Warner) followed. *Chaos and Disorder* (1996) and *The Vault: Old Friends 4 Sale* (released 1999) fulfilled the contract.

### 1996–2004: Independence, Internet Pioneer, and Musicology
Free of Warner, Prince released *Emancipation* (1996), a 36-song triple album on NPG Records/EMI, notable for covering other artists' songs. *Crystal Ball* (1998), a 5-CD vault set, suffered distribution chaos. He signed with Arista for *Rave Un2 the Joy Fantastic* (1999). In 2000, he reclaimed the name "Prince" after his Warner publishing deal expired. He pioneered direct-to-fan distribution via NPGOnlineLtd.com/NPGMusicClub.com (2000–2006), releasing *The Rainbow Children* (2001), *One Nite Alone...* (2002), *Xpectation* (2003). Inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 2004, he delivered a legendary guitar solo on "While My Guitar Gently Weeps." *Musicology* (2004, Columbia) was a commercial comeback (top 5 globally, 2 Grammys), boosted by a tour where CDs were bundled with tickets (Pollstar's top US draw, $56.5M income).

### 2005–2010: 3121, Super Bowl, and Vault Experiments
*3121* (2006, Universal) debuted at #1 on Billboard 200. He performed at the Brit Awards and BET Awards, won a Webby Lifetime Achievement Award, then shut down NPG Music Club amid a trademark dispute. He opened a 3121 nightclub in Las Vegas (2006–07). His Super Bowl XLI halftime show (Feb 2007) is widely ranked the greatest ever (140M viewers, performed "Purple Rain" in the rain). A 21-night residency at London's O2 Arena (Earth Tour, 2007) capped tickets at £31.21. *Planet Earth* (2007) was given away free with a UK newspaper, angering distributors. He settled a lawsuit over a canceled Dublin concert (2008) for $2.95M. *Lotusflow3r/MPLSoUND/Elixer* triple set (2009) was sold via his LotusFlow3r.com site.

### 2010–2016: Final Years and Streaming Battles
*20Ten* (2010) was a free covermount in Europe; he rejected digital downloads and closed LotusFlow3r.com. He headlined festivals (Hop Farm 2011) and returned to streaming with "Extraloveable" (2011). He re-signed with Warner Bros. in 2014 (regaining master ownership) for a *Purple Rain* 30th-anniversary deluxe edition. He launched "Hit N Run" tours (2014) with surprise pop-up shows. *Hit n Run Phase One* (Sept 2015) and *Phase Two* (Dec 2015) were released via Tidal. He supported Black Lives Matter, releasing "Baltimore" (2015) after Freddie Gray's death and holding a "Rally 4 Peace" concert. His final tour, *Piano & A Microphone* (2016), stripped the show to solo piano; it was cut short by illness in April 2016.

### Death and Estate
On April 7 and 20, 2016, Prince saw Dr. Michael Schulenberg. On April 14, he performed his final show. On April 15, his plane made an emergency landing in Moline, IL, after he became unresponsive; he received naloxone (opioid reversal) but left against medical advice. On April 21, he was found unresponsive in an elevator at Paisley Park; paramedics pronounced him dead at 10:07 AM. The Midwest Medical Examiner ruled accidental fentanyl overdose (counterfeit hydrocodone/acetaminophen pills). Investigations (Carver County, DEA, FDA) closed in 2018 with no criminal charges, confirming opioid addiction. Prince died intestate. His estate (valued at millions in cash, real estate, stocks, cars) went to his full sister Tyka Nelson and five half-siblings under Minnesota law. After protracted probate, Primary Wave Music acquired interests from several heirs. Ashes are in a 3D-printed Paisley Park urn at the estate.

### Posthumous Releases and Legacy Management
The estate has managed extensive vault releases: *4Ever* (2016), *Piano and a Microphone 1983* (2018), *Originals* (2019, demos for other artists), *Sign o' the Times* Super Deluxe (2020, Pitchfork 10/10), *Welcome 2 America* (2021, recorded 2010), *Diamonds and Pearls* Super Deluxe (2023). Distribution deals shifted: Universal (2017, voided), Sony/Legacy (US post-2019 for 1978–1996), Warner (international). "The Most Beautiful Girl in the World" returned to streaming in 2022 after Italian plagiarism suit resolution. A planned Netflix documentary by Ezra Edelman (*The Book of Prince*) was cancelled in Feb 2025 after estate objections ("sensationalized"); an estate-produced replacement was announced. *Timeless* compilation announced for Aug 2026.

### Personal Life: Relationships, Faith, and Activism
Prince married backup dancer Mayte García (1996–2000); their son Amiir died days after birth from Pfeiffer syndrome. He married Manuela Testolini (2001–2007). He was linked to Kim Basinger, Madonna, Vanity, Sheila E., Susannah Melvoin, Carmen Electra. Raised Seventh-day Adventist, he became a Jehovah's Witness in 2001 (influenced by bassist Larry Graham), calling it a "realization." This faith influenced *The Rainbow Children* (2001) and led to reported tensions with former bandmates Wendy & Lisa (later reconciled for 2004/2006 performances). He expressed ambiguous views on homosexuality (denied anti-gay quotes in 2008) but is considered a gay icon. Politically, he addressed themes in songs ("Ronnie Talk to Russia," "Sign o' the Times," "Baltimore"), supported Black Lives Matter later in life, donated to Trayvon Martin/Eric Garner families, but did not vote (per 2009 Tavis Smiley interview) and expressed belief in chemtrail conspiracy. He was a vegan/vegetarian animal rights activist (enforced meat-free Paisley Park) and a secretive philanthropist (donated to Louisville Western Branch Library, Clyde Stubblefield medical bills, YesWeCode, Green for All, PARSA Afghanistan).

### Artistry: Musicianship, Influences, and Equipment
Prince synthesized influences: James Brown, Jimi Hendrix, Sly Stone, Joni Mitchell, Duke Ellington, Miles Davis, George Clinton, Stevie Wonder, The Beatles. Miles Davis called him a blend of Brown, Hendrix, Gaye, Stone, Richard, Ellington, Chaplin. Prince had a vast vocal range (falsetto to baritone) and was a multi-instrumentalist virtuoso (guitar, drums, bass, keys, synth), playing nearly all instruments on early albums. He pioneered drum machines (Linn LM-1) and "new-generation synthesizer sounds in service of the groove" (LA Times). He wrote hits for others: "Manic Monday" (Bangles), "I Feel for You" (Chaka Khan), "Nothing Compares 2 U" (Sinéad O'Connor/Family), "Love... Thy Will Be Done" (Martika), "With This Tear" (Celine Dion). His guitar collection (121+) included Cloud Guitars (white/yellow/purple), Love Symbol guitars, the H.S. Anderson Madcat (Telecaster copy, main career guitar), and later the Vox HDC-77.

### Legacy
Prince is cited as a "pop polymath" (Simon Reynolds) and "master architect of funk, rock, R&B and pop" (Jon Pareles), defying race, genre, and gender categories. *Rolling Stone* ranks him #27 Greatest Artist, #16 Greatest Singer (2023), #14 Greatest Guitarist (2023). *Billboard* called him "greatest musician of his generation." Influenced Beyoncé, Justin Timberlake, Bruno Mars, Rihanna, The Weeknd, Janelle Monáe, St. Vincent, D'Angelo, H.E.R., and countless others. Bono called him a favorite 20th-century composer. Awards: 7 Grammys (incl. President's Merit, Lifetime Achievement 2025), 7 Brits, 4 VMAs, Oscar, Golden Globe, Billboard Icon Award. *Purple Rain* in National Film Registry (2019). Honored with gold star on Minneapolis's First Avenue mural.

## Terms
- **Minneapolis Sound**: A hybrid genre pioneered by Prince in the late 1970s/early 1980s, blending funk, rock, R&B, synth-pop, and new wave, characterized by heavy use of drum machines (Linn LM-1), synthesizers, and guitar.
- **Love Symbol (O(+>)**: An unpronounceable glyph combining male (♂) and female (♀) signs; adopted as Prince's legal stage name (1993–2000) during his contract dispute with Warner Bros.
- **The Revolution**: Prince's primary backing band (1983–1986), featuring Wendy Melvoin, Lisa Coleman, Bobby Z., Brown Mark, Doctor Fink; credited on *Purple Rain*, *Around the World in a Day*, *Parade*.
- **New Power Generation (NPG)**: Prince's backing band from 1991 onward, featuring a larger ensemble including horns, rappers (Tony M.), and multiple keyboardists; credited on *Diamonds and Pearls* through later albums.
- **Paisley Park**: Prince's 65,000 sq. ft. home, recording studio, and creative complex in Chanhassen, Minnesota (opened 1987); site of his death and current museum.

# Visayas

## Overview
The Visayas are one of the three principal geographical divisions of the Philippines, located in the central archipelago. The region is historically significant as the heartland of the Visayan peoples, known for their seafaring culture and early resistance to Spanish colonization. It remains a major cultural and economic center, with a population of over 20 million across its four administrative regions.

## Timeline
- **Late 12th century** — Chinese sources first document raids by people called "Pi-sho-ye" (Visayans).
- **1521** — Ferdinand Magellan's expedition is defeated at the Battle of Mactan by Lapu-Lapu.
- **1565** — Miguel López de Legazpi begins Spanish colonization of the Visayas.
- **1621–1622** — The Tamblot Uprising occurs in Bohol.
- **1744–1829** — The Bohol Rebellion, led by Francisco Dagohoy, takes place.
- **1898** — The short-lived Federal State of the Visayas is established during the Philippine Revolution.
- **1898** — The Cantonal Republic of Negros briefly exists as an independent state.
- **May 23, 2005** — Executive Order No. 429 attempts to transfer Palawan to Western Visayas, but is later held in abeyance.
- **May 29, 2015** — The Negros Island Region is created by Executive Order No. 183.
- **August 9, 2017** — The Negros Island Region is revoked by Executive Order No. 38 due to lack of funds.
- **June 13, 2024** — The Negros Island Region is re-established, including Siquijor.

## Body

### Etymology and Origins
The exact origin of the name "Visayas" is unknown. Pre-colonial hypotheses link it to the Srivijaya empire or a Bisaya group in Borneo, but linguistic and historical evidence for these is considered paltry or speculative. The first documented use is possibly by a 12th-century Chinese official. The Spanish initially called the inhabitants "Pintados" (the painted ones) for their tattoos, but "Bisaya" was likely an existing endonym. Genetic studies suggest Austronesian peoples from Taiwan populated the Visayas, though local myths like the *Maragtas* epic describe ancestors from Borneo.

### Colonial History and Resistance
Spanish colonization began in 1565, with the Visayas and its kingdoms converting to Christianity. The Spanish established *encomiendas* (colonial land grants) on islands like Panay and Cebu. By the 18th and 19th centuries, colonial pressures sparked revolts, such as those led by Francisco Dagohoy in Bohol. During the Philippine Revolution, a short-lived Federal State of the Visayas was formed. After the Spanish-American War, the Cantonal Republic of Negros briefly existed before American takeover.

### Geography and Administration
The Visayas has a total land area of 71,503 km² and consists of several major islands, including Panay, Negros, Cebu, Bohol, Leyte, and Samar. Administratively, it is divided into four regions: Western Visayas, Negros Island Region, Central Visayas, and Eastern Visayas. The Negros Island Region has been created, revoked, and re-established multiple times due to political and funding issues. Scholars debate whether the provinces of Palawan and Masbate are also part of the Visayas.

### Culture and Demographics
Visayan culture is characterized by hospitality, strong religious traditions, and vibrant festivals like the Sinulog (Cebu) and Ati-Atihan (Aklan). The primary languages are Visayan languages, with Cebuano being the most widely spoken (approx. 20 million speakers). Other major languages include Hiligaynon and Waray. There is ongoing contention over the term "Bisaya," which is often used interchangeably with "Cebuano" but can refer to different groups depending on the region.

## Terms
- **Visayas**: One of the three principal geographical divisions of the Philippines, located in the central archipelago.
- **Pintados**: The general term used by the Spanish for Visayan peoples, referring to their practice of full-body tattooing (*batok*).
- **Encomienda**: A Spanish colonial system where conquistadors were granted land and the right to tribute from the native inhabitants.
- **Endonym**: A name used by a group of people for themselves, as opposed to an exonym given by outsiders.
- **Austronesian**: A large language family to which the languages of the Visayas and the Philippines belong.
- **Maragtas**: A historical document (1907) containing a Visayan folk tradition about ten Bornean datus settling in Panay.
- **Negros Island Region (NIR)**: An administrative region created and revoked multiple times, most recently re-established in 2024.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Etymology of "Visayas"**: The origin of the name is unknown, with competing theories linking it to Srivijaya, Borneo, or Chinese records, all of which are considered speculative.
- **Geographical Boundaries**: Scholars debate whether the provinces of Palawan and Masbate are part of the Visayas, challenging the traditional three-division model of the Philippines.
- **Linguistic Identity**: The term "Bisaya" is contested; it is often equated with Cebuano but is used to refer to different Visayan language groups (e.g., Hiligaynon, Waray) in various regions, causing ambiguity.

# Penang

## Overview
Penang is a Malaysian state on the northwest coast of Peninsular Malaysia, divided by the Strait of Malacca into Penang Island (home to the capital, George Town) and the mainland territory of Seberang Perai. It is one of Malaysia's most developed and densely populated states, with a diverse, multicultural population and a robust economy driven by electronics manufacturing and services. Its history as a British free port and strategic trading hub has shaped its unique cultural and economic landscape.

## Body

### History and Establishment
Penang's modern history began in 1786 when Francis Light, acting for the British East India Company (EIC), acquired Penang Island from the Sultan of Kedah. The island was renamed Prince of Wales Island and the capital, George Town, was established. In 1800, the mainland hinterland of Province Wellesley (Seberang Perai) was added. Penang became a key British free port and, in 1826, the capital of the Straits Settlements. It was a vital entrepôt and later a major tin exporter and financial centre. During World War II, Japan occupied Penang from 1941 until its liberation in 1945. Post-war, Penang initially resisted merger into the Malayan Union but eventually joined the Federation of Malaya, gaining independence in 1957.

### Governance and Politics
Penang is one of four Malaysian states without a hereditary monarchy. Its head of state is a Governor, appointed by the King of Malaysia, while the head of government is the Chief Minister. The state has a unicameral 40-seat Legislative Assembly. Penang's political landscape is distinct due to its ethnic Chinese plurality and commercial base; it is the only state where the Chief Minister has been continuously ethnic Chinese since independence. The state government has limited powers compared to the federal government, primarily over land, agriculture, and local administration. A 2025 sovereignty dispute with Kedah concerns the historical annual lease payment for Penang.

### Economy
Penang is an economic powerhouse with Malaysia's highest GDP per capita. Its economy transitioned from entrepôt trade to electronics manufacturing after the federal government revoked George Town's free port status in 1969. The establishment of the Bayan Lepas Free Industrial Zone in 1972 was instrumental in this shift. Today, Penang is Malaysia's top destination for foreign direct investment (FDI) and its largest exporter, with a major role in the global semiconductor supply chain. The services sector, including tourism, medical tourism, and global business services, is also significant. The state has a low unemployment rate and high average wages.

### Demographics and Culture
As of 2020, Penang had a population of 1.74 million, making it Malaysia's most densely populated state. The population is ethnically diverse, with Chinese and Bumiputeras (including Malays) each at about 41%, and Indians at 9%. Malay is the official language, but Penang Hokkien serves as a common lingua franca. The state is religiously diverse, with Muslims at 45% and Buddhists at 38%. This multiculturalism is reflected in its festivals, cuisine, and architecture. George Town's historic core, a UNESCO World Heritage Site, is celebrated for its unique architectural and cultural townscape.

### Infrastructure and Geography
Penang consists of Penang Island (295 km²) and Seberang Perai (748 km²), connected by two bridges. The state has a tropical rainforest climate. Its infrastructure includes the Penang International Airport (Malaysia's second busiest) and the Port of Penang. Land reclamation projects, such as the planned Silicon Island, address land scarcity. The state government manages water supply, with the Muda River as the primary source, and has the lowest domestic water tariff in Malaysia. A new Mutiara LRT line, linking George Town and Seberang Perai, is under construction.

## Terms
- **Bumiputera**: A Malay term meaning "sons of the soil," referring to Malays and other indigenous groups in Malaysia.
- **Entrepôt**: A port or trading post where goods are imported, stored, and re-exported.
- **Free port**: A port open to all merchants for trade without payment of customs duties.
- **Straits Settlements**: A British crown colony comprising Penang, Malacca, and Singapore.
- **Suzerainty**: A relationship in which a region or people is a tributary to a more powerful entity, which controls its foreign affairs while allowing internal autonomy.
- **Tertiary sector**: The service sector of the economy, including finance, tourism, and healthcare.
- **Vernacular school**: A school where the medium of instruction is a native language other than Malay, such as Mandarin or Tamil.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Sovereignty dispute**: In 2025, Kedah's Menteri Besar announced intentions to sue for a revised annual lease payment for Penang, arguing the historical amount is no longer equitable. Penang's state government rejects this, citing its sovereignty under the Malaysian Constitution.
- **Economic decline**: Persistent brain drain and federal policies prioritizing Kuala Lumpur have contributed to Penang losing its leading position in the national economy by the 21st century, though recent growth has been strong.
- **Urban planning challenges**: Issues like incoherent planning, traffic management, and the dilapidation of George Town's heritage area following the repeal of the Rent Control Act in 2001 have been sources of societal dissatisfaction.

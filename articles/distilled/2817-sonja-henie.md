# Sonja Henie

## Overview
Sonja Henie (1912–1969) was a Norwegian figure skater and film star who dominated women's singles skating in the late 1920s and 1930s. She became one of the most famous athletes and entertainers of her era, winning an unprecedented three Olympic gold medals and ten consecutive World Championships before transitioning to a highly successful Hollywood career. Her innovative style and celebrity status permanently transformed figure skating into a popular spectacle and a legitimate Olympic sport.

## Timeline
- **8 April 1912** — Born in Kristiania (now Oslo), Norway.
- **1924** — Places eighth at the Winter Olympics at age 11.
- **1927** — Wins first of ten consecutive World Championships at age 14.
- **1928** — Wins first Olympic gold medal in women's singles.
- **1931** — Wins first of six consecutive European Championships.
- **1932** — Defends Olympic title, winning second gold medal.
- **1936** — Wins third Olympic gold medal and final World title.
- **1936** — Turns professional; signs with Twentieth Century Fox.
- **1937** — First major film hit, *Thin Ice*, released.
- **1956** — Retires from professional ice shows.
- **12 October 1969** — Dies of chronic lymphocytic leukemia at age 57.

## Body

### Competitive Career
Henie's competitive career was marked by unprecedented dominance. After placing last at the 1924 Olympics, she won her first World title in 1927 in a controversial 3–2 decision over Herma Szabo. She then won three consecutive Olympic gold medals (1928, 1932, 1936), becoming one of the youngest champions and one of only two skaters (with Gillis Grafström) to win three Olympic singles titles. Her ten consecutive World titles (1927–1936) and six consecutive European titles (1931–1936) remain unmatched records for consecutive wins. Her final Olympic victory in 1936 was highly contested, with silver medalist Cecilia Colledge trailing by only a few points after the school figures section. The subsequent draw for the free skate, which gave Henie the advantageous last position, was criticized as unfair.

### Professional and Film Career
After 1936, Henie turned professional, opening opportunities for skaters to earn a living. She signed with Twentieth Century Fox, becoming one of Hollywood's highest-paid stars. Her films, such as *Thin Ice* (1937) and *Sun Valley Serenade* (1941), were box-office hits that blended musical comedy with elaborate skating numbers. She demanded and received total control over her skating sequences. Beyond film, she toured North America with her own professional ice shows, the "Hollywood Ice Revue," produced with Arthur Wirtz. These lavish musical extravaganzas, staged at venues like Rockefeller Center's Center Theatre, helped establish ice shows as a major form of entertainment in the United States.

### Influence and Legacy
Henie is credited with revolutionizing figure skating's aesthetics and presentation. She was the first to use dance choreography, adopt the short skirt, and wear white boots, which emphasized leg lines and became the standard for female skaters. Her incorporation of balletic elements, like using toepicks for poses, and her glamorous, sequined costumes shifted the sport's image toward a "spectacle of the skater's body." Historian Ellyn Kestnbaum argues Henie's films and shows were the first mass-media exposure to skating for many, permanently linking the sport with the image of the glamorous movie star and influencing generations of performers' costumes.

### Personal Life and Controversies
Henie's fame moved in elite social circles, leading to controversy over her association with Adolf Hitler. She was photographed giving a Nazi salute at the 1936 Olympics and later lunched with Hitler, drawing criticism in the Norwegian press. She was married three times, to Dan Topping, Winthrop Gardiner Jr., and finally to Norwegian shipping magnate Niels Onstad. After retiring in 1956, she and Onstad settled in Oslo, amassing an art collection that formed the basis of the Henie Onstad Art Centre. She was diagnosed with chronic lymphocytic leukemia in the mid-1960s and died in 1969 while being flown home from Paris.

## Terms
*   **Ordinals:** A judging system where judges rank skaters from first to last; the skater with the most first-place ordinals wins.
*   **School figures:** Compulsory patterns traced on the ice that were a major component of competition scoring in Henie's era.
*   **Amateurism:** The strict rule in Henie's time that athletes could not accept payment for their sport, which Henie's family circumvented via "expense money."
*   **Hollywood Ice Revue:** The name of Sonja Henie's professional touring ice shows produced with Arthur Wirtz.
*   **Chronic lymphocytic leukemia:** The type of cancer Henie was diagnosed with in the mid-1960s, which caused her death.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **1927 World Championship Controversy:** The result, a 3–2 decision for Henie over Herma Szabo, was controversial due to the nationalities of the judges, with three of the five first-place ordinals for Henie coming from Norwegian judges.
*   **1936 Olympic Fairness:** The fairness of Henie's third Olympic gold medal is debated. Her rival, Cecilia Colledge, was disadvantaged by drawing an early position in the free skate, while Henie skated last. The draw process itself came under suspicion.
*   **Nazi Associations:** Henie's pre- and post-war associations with Hitler and other high-ranking Nazi officials remain a subject of historical scrutiny and controversy, despite her later claims of political naivety.

# Justinian I

## Overview
Justinian I (r. 527–565) was a Byzantine emperor whose ambitious reign aimed to restore the Roman Empire's former glory through military conquest, legal reform, and monumental building. His legacy is defined by the partial recovery of Western territories, the codification of Roman law, and the construction of Hagia Sophia, though his campaigns strained the empire's resources and were followed by significant territorial losses.

## Timeline

- **533–534** — Vandal Kingdom in North Africa conquered
- **535–540** — First phase of Gothic War in Italy
- **541–543** — Plague of Justinian devastates the empire
- **540–562** — Renewed war with the Sassanid Empire
- **541–554** — Second phase of Gothic War; Italy secured
- **552** — Byzantine province of Spania established in Hispania
- **565** — Justinian dies; succeeded by Justin II

## Body

### Early Life and Accession
Justinian was born c. 482 in Tauresium (modern North Macedonia) to a Latin-speaking peasant family. Adopted by his uncle Justin, a commander of the Excubitors, he was educated in Constantinople. Justinian became Justin's close confidant and was crowned co-emperor on 1 April 527, becoming sole ruler after Justin's death on 1 August 527. He married Theodora, a former actress and prostitute, around 525; she became a highly influential political partner.

### Legislative Activities
Justinian's most enduring legacy is the *Corpus Juris Civilis*, a comprehensive revision of Roman law overseen by his quaestor Tribonian. It comprised the *Codex Justinianeus* (imperial constitutions), the *Digesta* (older legal texts), the *Institutiones* (a law textbook), and the *Novellae* (new laws in Greek). This codification preserved Roman law, forming the basis for later Byzantine law and influencing modern civil law systems. His legislation also restricted divorce, protected women and slaves, and persecuted heretics, pagans, and Jews.

### Military Campaigns
Justinian pursued *renovatio imperii* (restoration of the Empire). His general Belisarius conquered the Vandal Kingdom in North Africa (533–534). The Ostrogothic Kingdom in Italy was reconquered after two costly phases (535–540, 541–554), led by Belisarius and Narses. A small province, Spania, was established in southern Hispania. These campaigns re-established Roman control over the western Mediterranean, increasing annual revenue by over a million *solidi*. However, they were protracted and expensive, with Italy costing about 300,000 pounds of gold. Simultaneously, he fought the Sassanid Empire, concluding an "Eternal Peace" in 532 and a fifty years' peace in 562.

### Religious Policy
Justinian sought religious unity under the Chalcedonian Church, which defined Christ as having two natures. He persecuted Miaphysites (who believed in one united nature), pagans, Jews, and heretics. His attempts to compromise with Miaphysites, such as condemning the "Three Chapters," satisfied no one and caused schisms. He closed the Neoplatonic Academy in Athens in 529 and promoted the cult of Archangel Michael.

### Building and Culture
After the Nika riots of 532 destroyed much of Constantinople, Justinian launched a massive building program. His masterpiece was the Hagia Sophia, rebuilt with a revolutionary dome. He also constructed churches, fortifications, cisterns (like the Basilica Cistern), and the city of Justiniana Prima. His reign saw a flourishing of Byzantine culture, with historians like Procopius and poets like Romanus the Melodist.

### Economy and Administration
Justinian inherited a surplus treasury. He reformed tax collection to fund his wars, increasing annual revenue from 5,000,000 to 6,000,000 *solidi* by 550. He established a silk monopoly and attempted new trade routes. His reign was marked by administrative simplification but also by the devastating Plague of Justinian (541–543), which reduced the population and strained resources.

## Terms
- **Corpus Juris Civilis**: The comprehensive codification of Roman law under Justinian, forming the basis of modern civil law.
- **Solidus**: The standard gold coin of the late Roman and Byzantine Empire.
- **Miaphysitism**: A Christian doctrine holding that Christ has one united divine-human nature, rejected by the Chalcedonian Church.
- **Nika Riots**: A 532 revolt in Constantinople against Justinian, brutally suppressed, which led to the rebuilding of Hagia Sophia.
- **Renovatio Imperii**: Latin for "restoration of the Empire," Justinian's ambition to reconquer former Western Roman territories.
- **Praetorian Prefect**: A high-ranking imperial official, often overseeing finance or a major region.
- **Excubitors**: An elite imperial guard unit in the late Roman Empire.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Birthplace Discrepancy**: Procopius states Justinian built Justiniana Prima near his birthplace, but the identified sites of Tauresium and Justiniana Prima are far apart, causing scholarly debate.
- **Plague's Impact**: The scale of mortality from the Plague of Justinian is disputed; recent evidence questions the traditional estimate of tens of millions dead.
- **Long-term Consequences**: Historians debate whether Justinian's conquests were a strategic overextension that weakened the empire or a profitable expansion that paid for itself through taxation of wealthy provinces like North Africa and Italy.
- **Procopius's Bias**: The historian Procopius wrote both a panegyric (*Buildings*) and a hostile *Secret History*, making his accounts a complex and sometimes contradictory source.

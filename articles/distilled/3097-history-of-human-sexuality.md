# History of human sexuality

## Overview
The history of human sexuality encompasses the evolving social norms, regulations, and scientific understandings of sexual behavior across cultures and eras. It matters because these patterns have profoundly shaped social structures, religious doctrines, legal systems, and individual identities, from prehistoric times to the modern sexual revolution and the impact of diseases like AIDS.

## Timeline
- **c. 3000 BC** — Earliest evidence of attitudes towards sex in ancient Indian texts (Vedas).
- **c. 8th century AD** — *Tale of Genji* written in Japan, treating eroticism as a central aesthetic value.
- **3rd-5th century AD** — Vatsyayana compiles the *Kama Sutra* in India.
- **1861** — Johann Bachofen publishes *Mother Right*, theorizing a matriarchal stage in human sexual history.
- **1960s** — Introduction of the birth control pill, a key factor in the Western sexual revolution.
- **1974** — U.S. Supreme Court legalizes the pill for unmarried people (*Eisenstadt v. Baird*).
- **1980s** — AIDS epidemic emerges, profoundly changing sexual behavior and education globally.
- **1988** — Indian government outlaws the Devadasi temple prostitute system.

## Body

### Study and Sources
The academic study of sexuality's history was influenced by Johann Bachofen's 1861 theory of a progression from a sex-positive "aphroditic" stage to matriarchy, and finally to patriarchy. Modern explanations are based in evolutionary biology and human behavioral ecology. Primary sources are often scarce due to historical self-censorship and euphemism, but include legislation, religious texts, literature, medical texts, and linguistic studies.

### Sex in Various Cultures
Sexual norms varied greatly. In pre-colonial Native America, "two spirit" individuals (formerly called berdache) held cross-gender roles and could marry, but this faded under colonial pressure. Ancient Indian literature, like the *Kama Sutra*, treated sex as a science and art for the educated classes. Chinese culture enforced female virginity but had a double standard for men, and its literature openly discussed diverse sexual behaviors. In Japan, sexuality was historically integrated into cultured life without pejorative judgment, though social control was maintained through ostracism. The geisha was a trained artist for non-sexual companionship, not a prostitute. Ancient Greece institutionalized pederasty and saw the phallus as a fertility symbol. Ancient Rome emphasized male self-control (*virtus*) and female integrity (*pudicitia*), with same-sex acts acceptable for a citizen in the dominant role. French Polynesian cultures historically had more permissive attitudes towards childhood sexuality and premarital sex.

### Same-Sex Relations
Attitudes toward same-sex relationships have ranged from institutional integration to severe repression. A 1973 compilation of pre-industrial cultures found strong disapproval in 41% of societies, acceptance in 21%, and no concept of it in 12%. Abrahamic religions established sodomy as a sin, a condemnation predating Christianity. Scholars debate whether historical figures like Socrates experienced homosexuality as an exclusive identity, with some arguing this is an anachronistic modern construction.

### Religion and Sex
Abrahamic religions (Judaism, Christianity, Islam) traditionally endorse heterosexual, procreative sex within marriage, labeling other acts as "sodomy." Judaism views marital sex as holy but forbids adultery, incest, and male homosexuality. Christianity re-emphasized exclusive, indissoluble marriage and, through figures like Augustine, linked sexual lust to original sin. Islam permits sex only within marriage and prescribes severe punishments for *zina* (extramarital sex), though strict evidentiary requirements apply. In contrast, Hinduism historically accepted sex as an art and spiritual practice, as detailed in texts like the *Kama Sutra*.

### Technology, Prostitution, and Disease
The 20th-century birth control pill and legal abortion gave women greater control over reproduction. Digital technology has expanded sexual exploration for youth. Prostitution, the "world's oldest profession," has existed in all forms, with social acceptance varying by time and place. Male prostitution has a long history, and transgender individuals are disproportionately represented in sex work today due to discrimination. Sexually transmitted diseases have always been present; the AIDS epidemic since the 1980s has radically impacted global health, life expectancy, and sexual education.

## Terms
- **Berdache/Two Spirit**: A historical term (now considered outdated) for a Native American individual who fulfilled a cross-gender social and spiritual role.
- **Hetaera**: In ancient Greece, a high-class courtesan or companion, distinct from a wife.
- **Pederasty**: A socially acknowledged relationship between an adult male and a younger adolescent male, common in ancient Greece.
- **Pudicitia**: The Roman virtue of sexual integrity and modesty expected of female citizens.
- **Sodomy**: A legal and religious term for non-procreative sexual acts, especially anal sex, historically criminalized in many cultures.
- **Zina**: In Islamic law, extramarital sexual intercourse, which is forbidden and punishable.
- **Kama Sutra**: An ancient Indian Sanskrit text on sexuality, eroticism, and emotional fulfillment in life.
- **Geisha**: A traditional Japanese female entertainer trained in arts like music and conversation, not a prostitute.
- **Devadasi**: A historical system in South India of women dedicated to temple service, which included ritual prostitution and was outlawed in 1988.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Anachronism in Identity**: Do modern labels like "gay" or "homosexual" accurately describe historical figures and practices, or do they impose a contemporary identity framework on the past?
- **Bachofen's Theory**: To what extent was early human society matriarchal, as theorized by Bachofen, and how did the transition to patriarchy occur?
- **Impact of Technology**: How will digital technologies like dating apps and pornography continue to reshape sexual norms, relationships, and health outcomes for future generations?

# San Antonio

## Overview
San Antonio is a major city in south-central Texas, United States, and the state's second-most populous city. Its history as a Spanish colonial outpost and its role in the Texas Revolution, particularly the Battle of the Alamo, are foundational to its identity. Today, it is a significant economic, cultural, and military hub with a large Hispanic population.

## Timeline
- **1691** — Spanish explorers name the area "San Antonio" on the feast day of St. Anthony of Padua.
- **1718** — The Misión de San Antonio de Valero (The Alamo) and Presidio San Antonio de Béxar are founded.
- **1731** — Canary Island colonists arrive, forming the nucleus of the first chartered civil settlement in Texas.
- **1835** — Texian forces capture San Antonio in the Battle of Bexar during the Texas Revolution.
- **1836** — The Battle of the Alamo occurs; all defenders are killed.
- **1845** — The United States annexes Texas, leading to the Mexican–American War.
- **1877** — The first railroad reaches San Antonio, connecting it to major markets.
- **2015** — The San Antonio Missions are designated a UNESCO World Heritage site.
- **2017** — San Antonio is designated a UNESCO "City of Creativity for Gastronomy."

## Body

### Early History and Settlement
The area was inhabited by the Payaya people, who called it Yanaguana ("refreshing waters"). Spanish explorers named it San Antonio in 1691. A formal mission and presidio were established in 1718 to counter French expansion and prevent illegal trade. The families who settled around these structures formed Villa de Béjar. In 1731, colonists from the Canary Islands arrived, creating the first organized civil government in Texas, the villa of San Fernando de Béxar.

### Revolution and Statehood
San Antonio grew to be the largest Spanish settlement in Texas and its capital. In 1835, during the Texas Revolution, Texian forces captured the city. In early 1836, a volunteer force occupied the Alamo mission. The subsequent Battle of the Alamo (February 23–March 6, 1836) resulted in the death of all Texian defenders, who became martyrs for Texas independence. The cry "Remember the Alamo" rallied Texian forces to eventual victory. Texas was annexed by the U.S. in 1845, leading to the Mexican–American War, which devastated the city's population.

### Growth and Modern Era
After the Civil War, San Antonio prospered as a cattle industry center. The arrival of the railroad in 1877 spurred development. The city's population grew steadily, nearly doubling between 1970 and 2005. It is a major military hub, hosting several U.S. Armed Forces facilities, and has a diversified economy. The city's five 18th-century Spanish missions, including The Alamo, were named a UNESCO World Heritage site in 2015.

## Terms
- **Presidio**: A fortified military outpost established by the Spanish in colonial frontier regions.
- **Mission**: A religious and social center established by Spanish colonists to convert and educate Indigenous peoples.
- **Tejano**: A Texan of Hispanic descent, especially one whose family lived in the region before the U.S. annexation.
- **Texian**: A term used for settlers in Mexican Texas, primarily of American origin, who fought for independence.
- **Edwards Aquifer**: A significant underground water source that supplies drinking water to San Antonio.
- **Council-manager system**: A form of local government where an elected council sets policy and a hired city manager oversees daily operations.
- **Extraterritorial jurisdiction (ETJ)**: The authority a city exercises over adjacent unincorporated land for planning and subdivision rules.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The city's aggressive annexation policy is controversial, as residents in unincorporated areas may face higher taxes without immediate improvements in services.
- San Antonio's low population density and lack of a major intra-city rail system contribute to its ranking as a car-dependent city with low walkability.
- The city has historically lacked top-tier professional sports franchises beyond the NBA's Spurs, with ongoing efforts to attract an NFL team.

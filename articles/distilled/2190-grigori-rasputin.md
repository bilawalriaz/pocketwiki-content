# Grigori Rasputin

## Overview
Grigori Rasputin was a Russian peasant who became a mystic and faith healer, gaining immense influence over Tsar Nicholas II and his family, particularly through his perceived ability to treat the hemophilia of their son, Alexei. His scandalous reputation and the perception of his undue political influence discredited the Tsarist government, contributing to the conditions that led to the Russian Revolution and the end of the Romanov dynasty.

## Timeline
- **21 January 1869** — Born in Pokrovskoye, Siberia.
- **1897** — Undergoes a religious conversion during a pilgrimage to Verkhoturye.
- **c. 1903-1905** — Travels to Saint Petersburg, gaining aristocratic patrons.
- **1 November 1905** — First meets Tsar Nicholas II and Empress Alexandra.
- **Late 1906** — Begins acting as a faith healer for Tsarevich Alexei's hemophilia.
- **12 July 1914** — Survives an assassination attempt by Khioniya Guseva.
- **1915** — Influence peaks as Nicholas II leaves to command the army in WWI.
- **30 December 1916** — Assassinated by a group of nobles in Saint Petersburg.
- **March 1917** — Body exhumed and burned after the Tsar's abdication.

## Body

### Early Life and Conversion
Rasputin was born a peasant in Siberia and remained illiterate into early adulthood. In 1897, he experienced a religious conversion after a pilgrimage to the St. Nicholas Monastery at Verkhoturye, where he met the starets (elder) Makary. He became a strannik (holy wanderer), spending years traveling to holy sites. By the early 1900s, he had a small following in his village, which sparked rumors of heresy, though investigations failed to confirm his membership in the Khlyst sect.

### Rise to Prominence
Word of Rasputin's charisma reached Saint Petersburg around 1903-1905. He was introduced to influential church figures, including Archimandrite Theofan, who invited him into elite social circles. The aristocracy, interested in the occult, was captivated by this native Russian "holy man." In 1905, he was introduced to the Tsar and Tsarina by the "Black Princesses," Militsa and Anastasia of Montenegro.

### Healer to the Tsarevich
Rasputin's core influence stemmed from his role as a healer for Alexei, who suffered from hemophilia. The exact timing is debated, but by 1906-1907, the imperial family believed he could stop the boy's bleeding. A pivotal moment occurred in 1912, when Rasputin's telegram telling the Tsarina to keep doctors away from a delirious Alexei preceded the boy's recovery. Historians speculate his success may have been due to calming the patient, reducing stress, or unknowingly advising against the use of aspirin, which worsens bleeding.

### Controversy and Scandal
Rasputin's position was marked by scandal. He was accused of rape, heresy, and drunkenness, and rumors of an affair with the Tsarina circulated, fueled by pornographic cartoons and his own release of intimate letters. His access to the nursery, where he was seen with the grand duchesses in their nightgowns, horrified court staff and relatives. Despite investigations and opposition from figures like Prime Minister Stolypin, the Tsar and Tsarina defended him, with Alexandra dismissing accusations as slander.

### Assassination and Aftermath
By late 1916, Rasputin's influence was blamed for Russia's military failures and political decay. A group of nobles, including Prince Felix Yusupov and Vladimir Purishkevich, plotted his murder. On 30 December 1916, they lured him to the Yusupov Palace, where, according to Yusupov's dramatic account, he survived poisoned cakes and wine before being shot. His body was dumped in the Neva River. An autopsy found death by a single bullet to the head, with no evidence of poisoning or drowning. His body was exhumed and burned in 1917 to prevent his grave from becoming a rallying point.

## Terms
- **Starets**: An elder, typically a monk, revered for spiritual wisdom and guidance.
- **Strannik**: A holy wanderer or pilgrim in Russian Orthodox tradition.
- **Haemophilia**: A genetic disorder that impairs the body's ability to make blood clots, leading to excessive bleeding.
- **Tsesarevich**: The title of the heir apparent to the throne of the Russian Empire.
- **Okhrana**: The Tsar's secret police, responsible for political security.
- **Duma**: The legislative assembly or parliament of the Russian Empire.
- **Lampadnik**: A ceremonial court position Rasputin held, responsible for keeping lamps lit before icons.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Healing Mechanism**: Historians debate whether Rasputin's apparent success with Alexei was due to supernatural power, hypnotism, the calming effect of his presence, or practical advice like avoiding aspirin.
- **Political Influence**: The extent of Rasputin's direct political power is debated; some see him as a puppet of the Tsarina, while others argue he actively influenced ministerial appointments.
- **Assassination Details**: The precise sequence of events during his murder is uncertain, with Yusupov's sensational account being the most famous but not necessarily the most accurate. An autopsy contradicted claims of poisoning and drowning.
- **Relationship with the Imperial Family**: The nature of his relationship with the grand duchesses remains a subject of scandalous rumor, though all official accounts and investigations describe it as innocent.

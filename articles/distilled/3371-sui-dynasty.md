# Sui dynasty

## Overview
The Sui dynasty (581–618 AD) was a short-lived but pivotal Chinese imperial dynasty that reunified China after centuries of division. Founded by Yang Jian (Emperor Wen), it ended the Northern and Southern dynasties period and laid the institutional and infrastructural foundations for the subsequent Tang dynasty's golden age. Often compared to the earlier Qin dynasty, the Sui unified the realm, implemented sweeping reforms, and collapsed after a brief period due to overambitious projects and wars.

## Timeline
- **577** — Northern Zhou, led by Xianbei rulers, conquers Northern Qi, reunifying northern China.
- **581** — Yang Jian usurps the throne from the Northern Zhou, founding the Sui dynasty as Emperor Wen.
- **589** — Sui forces conquer the Chen dynasty, reunifying China proper.
- **605** — Emperor Yang moves the capital to the newly rebuilt city of Luoyang.
- **602–605** — Sui military campaigns in Vietnam (Linyi-Champa Campaign) retake the Hanoi area and push south.
- **609** — Emperor Yang's first major expedition against Goguryeo begins.
- **612–614** — Series of massive, failed Sui invasions against Goguryeo in Korea.
- **618** — Emperor Yang is assassinated by minister Yuwen Huaji; the dynasty collapses.
- **618** — Li Yuan (Emperor Gaozu) establishes the Tang dynasty in the north.

## Body

### Foundation and Reunification
The Sui was founded by Yang Jian, a general from the northwest military aristocracy who had become regent to the child emperor of the Northern Zhou. In 581, he usurped the throne, establishing the Sui dynasty and adopting a new character for its name. Emperor Wen emphasized Han cultural identity, purged rival princes, and initiated reforms to strengthen the state. In 589, a massive Sui army conquered the Chen dynasty in the south, reunifying China. The capital was initially Daxing (modern Xi'an), but was later moved to Luoyang in 605.

### Reforms and Prosperity
The Sui implemented major centralizing reforms. The equal-field system aimed to reduce inequality and boost agricultural productivity. The Five Departments and Six Boards system reformed administration. Coinage was standardized. The state encouraged Buddhism, with Emperor Wen presenting himself as a Cakravartin (Buddhist righteous king) to legitimize rule. These policies, along with infrastructure improvements like granaries, led to a vast agricultural surplus and rapid population growth, reaching a historical peak not surpassed for over a century.

### Mega-Projects and Their Cost
The Sui undertook enormous construction projects. The Grand Canal was initiated by Emperor Wen and greatly expanded by Emperor Yang, linking the political capitals in the north and west with the economic centers in the south and east. While it later facilitated trade and cultural exchange, its construction forcibly conscripted millions of workers at a heavy human cost. The Great Wall was also extended and consolidated. These projects, managed by a centralized bureaucracy, severely strained the state's resources and the populace.

### Military Campaigns and Collapse
The dynasty's decline was driven by costly wars and overextension. Emperor Yang launched a series of massive, failed invasions against Goguryeo in Korea, which incurred a devastating financial and manpower deficit. Combined with the heavy taxation and compulsory labor for construction projects, this led to widespread popular revolts. The dynasty disintegrated, and in 618, Emperor Yang was assassinated by his own minister. In the ensuing chaos, the aristocrat Li Yuan rose to power, founding the Tang dynasty.

### Culture and Religion
The Sui was a cultural transition period. The state promoted Taoism, with different schools favored by successive emperors. Buddhism flourished, with the Tiantai school founded by Zhiyi. Confucianism was also supported, with philosopher Wang Tong active during this era. Poetry continued, linking the Six Dynasties period to the Tang.

## Terms
- **Equal-field system**: A land distribution system aiming to reduce economic inequality and improve agricultural productivity by allocating land to farmers.
- **Five Departments and Six Boards**: An administrative system that preceded the Tang's Three Departments and Six Ministries, centralizing state governance.
- **Grand Canal**: A massive inland waterway system initiated by the Sui to link the Yellow River valley with the Yangtze region, improving grain transport and later trade.
- **Cakravartin king**: A Buddhist concept of a righteous monarch who uses military force to defend the faith, adopted by Emperor Wen for legitimacy.
- **Goguryeo**: One of the Three Kingdoms of Korea, which was the target of several large-scale, failed Sui military invasions.
- **Northern and Southern dynasties**: The period of political division in China (420–589 AD) that preceded the Sui reunification.
- **Xianbei**: A nomadic people who ruled the Northern Zhou dynasty before the Sui takeover; the Sui emperors had intermarried with them but later emphasized Han identity.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The exact motivations and long-term impact of the Grand Canal's initial construction are debated, with its primary purposes being grain shipment to the capital and military logistics.
- The role of Buddhism as a unifying cultural force during the transition from division to Sui reunification is a subject of historical analysis.
- The extent to which Emperor Yang's policies (e.g., promoting Confucian education over nomadic support) directly caused the loss of crucial alliances and contributed to the dynasty's fall is discussed.

# Taipei

## Overview
Taipei is the capital and a special municipality of Taiwan, serving as the island's political, economic, and cultural center. Located in the Taipei Basin in northern Taiwan, it is the core of the Taipei–Keelung metropolitan area, home to roughly one-third of Taiwan's population. Its history as a regional capital began in the Qing dynasty, and it has been the seat of the Republic of China (ROC) central government since 1949.

## Timeline
- **1709** — Han Chinese colonists begin settling in the Taipei Basin.
- **1875** — Northern Taiwan is incorporated into the new Taipeh Prefecture under the Qing dynasty.
- **1894** — Taipeh is formally made the provincial capital.
- **1895** — Japan annexes Taiwan; Taipei (renamed Taihoku) becomes the capital of the Japanese colonial government.
- **1945** — Japan surrenders; ROC control of Taiwan begins.
- **1949** — The Kuomintang (KMT) government relocates to Taiwan, declaring Taipei the provisional ROC capital.
- **1967** — Taipei is upgraded to a special municipality.
- **1990** — Wild Lily student rallies in Taipei catalyze Taiwan's transition to multi-party democracy.
- **2004** — Taipei 101, then the world's tallest building, opens.

## Body

### History and Political Status
The Taipei Basin was originally inhabited by the Ketagalan people. Han Chinese settlement increased in the early 18th century under the Qing dynasty. Taipei became the provincial capital in 1894. After the First Sino-Japanese War, Japan ceded Taiwan in 1895, and Taipei (as Taihoku) remained the colonial capital, undergoing modern urban planning. Following Japan's 1945 surrender, the ROC took control. After the KMT's defeat in the Chinese Civil War, it moved the ROC government to Taipei in 1949. The city has been the national capital since. In English, "Taipei" often refers to the ROC government, and "Chinese Taipei" is used in international contexts due to Taiwan's ambiguous political status.

### Geography and Climate
Taipei City is an enclave within New Taipei City, situated on the Taipei Basin. It is bordered by the Tamsui and Xindian rivers. The terrain is low-lying in the center, rising to the north into the Yangmingshan National Park, which includes the dormant Qixing Mountain (1,120 m). The city has a humid subtropical climate with hot, humid summers and mild, foggy winters. It is affected by the Pacific typhoon season (June–October). Air quality is generally good compared to other Asian cities, though smog is common after dry periods.

### Economy and Demographics
Taipei is Taiwan's business, financial, and technology hub, part of the "Taiwan Economic Miracle." Its economy is driven by information and communications technology, biotechnology, and financial services. Major global and local corporations are headquartered there. The city's GDP per capita is among the highest in East Asia. The population of the municipality is approximately 2.5 million, while the greater metropolitan area has over 7 million residents. The population is aging, and growth has slowed, though it remains one of the world's most densely populated urban areas.

### Culture and Landmarks
Taipei is a center for tourism and culture. Key landmarks include the National Palace Museum (housing ancient Chinese artifacts), the Chiang Kai-shek Memorial Hall, and the Taipei 101 skyscraper. The city is known for its vibrant night markets, such as Shilin, and shopping districts like Ximending. It has numerous temples reflecting Chinese folk religion, Taoism, and Buddhism, including the historic Lungshan Temple. Major annual events include the Taipei Lantern Festival and New Year's Eve celebrations at Taipei 101.

### Government and Transportation
Taipei is a special municipality governed by a mayor elected by direct popular vote. The city is divided into 12 administrative districts. It has an extensive public transportation system, with the Taipei Metro (MRT) as its backbone. The EasyCard contactless smartcard is used for transit and retail. Taipei Main Station is a hub for metro, bus, conventional rail, and high-speed rail. The city is served by two airports: Taoyuan International for most international flights and Songshan for domestic and select regional flights.

## Terms
- **Special Municipality**: A top-level administrative division in Taiwan, directly under the central government.
- **Taipei Basin**: An ancient lakebed in northern Taiwan where the city is primarily located.
- **Ketagalan**: A Plains Indigenous people who were the original inhabitants of the Taipei Basin region.
- **Kuomintang (KMT)**: The Chinese Nationalist Party that ruled Taiwan and relocated the ROC government to Taipei in 1949.
- **Taipei Metro (MRT)**: The rapid transit system serving the Taipei metropolitan area.
- **EasyCard**: A contactless smartcard used for fare payment on public transportation and at retail outlets in Taipei.
- **Chinese Taipei**: The name used for Taiwan in many international organizations and events.
- **GaWC**: The Globalization and World Cities Research Network, which classifies cities by their global connectivity; Taipei is rated an "Alpha − City."

## Debates and Open Questions
- The political status of Taiwan and its relationship with the People's Republic of China remains internationally ambiguous, leading to the use of terms like "Chinese Taipei."
- The source notes that the name "Taipei" can refer either to the municipality alone or the entire metropolitan area, creating potential ambiguity.

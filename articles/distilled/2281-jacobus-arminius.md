# Jacobus Arminius

## Overview
Jacobus Arminius (1560–1609) was a Dutch Reformed theologian whose challenges to the dominant Calvinist doctrines of his time, particularly on predestination, laid the foundation for Arminianism. His teachings sparked major theological controversy, leading to the formal condemnation of his views at the Synod of Dort and the creation of the five points of Calvinism in response.

## Timeline
- **1560** — Born in Oudewater, Netherlands.
- **1575** — Mother killed in Spanish massacre; adopted by Theodorus Aemilius.
- **1576–1582** — Studies at Leiden University.
- **1582–1583** — Studies under Theodore Beza in Geneva and at Basel.
- **1587** — Begins pastoring in Amsterdam.
- **1590** — Marries Lijsbet Reael.
- **1603** — Appointed professor of theology at Leiden University.
- **1604** — Public debate with colleague Franciscus Gomarus begins.
- **1608** — Speaks before the States of Holland to explain his views.
- **1609** — Dies in Leiden; his views are later systematized by the Remonstrants.

## Body

### Early Life and Education
Orphaned young, Arminius was adopted and later mentored by mathematician Rudolph Snellius, who enabled his studies at Leiden University. There, he was influenced by teachers with diverse Reformed perspectives, including those critical of high Calvinism. He later studied under Calvin's successor, Theodore Beza, in Geneva, though he initially faced criticism for using Ramist philosophical methods. Despite early tensions, he earned commendations for his learning and piety from both Beza and the faculty at Basel.

### Ministry in Amsterdam
Arminius served as a pastor in Amsterdam from 1587. He was initially tasked with refuting the anti-Calvinist teachings of Dirck Coornhert. However, as Arminius studied the issue of predestination more deeply, he became conflicted, eventually developing views inconsistent with the strict Calvinism of Beza. Controversy arose from his sermons on Romans, where he taught that conviction of sin was possible for the unregenerate, leading to accusations of Pelagianism. Despite interventions by civic authorities to maintain peace, theological disputes with colleagues like Petrus Plancius continued.

### Professor at Leiden and Conflict with Gomarus
In 1603, Arminius became a theology professor at Leiden. His public debates with his supralapsarian colleague, Franciscus Gomarus, escalated the controversy. Arminius advocated for revising the Belgic Confession and Heidelberg Catechism, arguing that strict Calvinist predestination was not biblically sound. The conflict moved beyond the university, involving church classes and foreign theologians. In 1608, both men presented their views to the States of Holland, with Arminius seeking a national synod to settle the matter. The States attempted to mediate, but the polemical tone of the debate, especially from Gomarus, deepened the rift.

### Theology and Legacy
Arminius taught a "prevenient" (or preventing) grace, conferred by the Holy Spirit upon all people, which makes belief and salvation possible. He emphasized that God's election was conditional upon foreseen faith, not an unconditional decree. After his death, his followers, known as Remonstrants, formalized their disagreements in five articles. The Synod of Dort (1618–19) condemned these views and defined the five points of Calvinism in opposition. Despite persecution, Arminianism survived and was later championed by figures like John Wesley, becoming a dominant theological system, especially in Methodism and the United States.

## Terms
- **Arminianism**: A theological system based on the teachings of Jacobus Arminius, emphasizing conditional election, prevenient grace, and human free will in salvation.
- **Predestination**: The doctrine of God's eternal decree determining the salvation or damnation of individuals.
- **Supralapsarianism**: The Calvinist view that God's decree of election and reprobation logically preceded His decree to permit the Fall.
- **Infralapsarianism**: The Calvinist view that God's decree of election and reprobation logically followed His decree to permit the Fall.
- **Prevenient Grace**: A concept taught by Arminius that God's grace is given to all people, enabling them to freely accept or reject salvation.
- **Remonstrants**: The followers of Arminius who formally protested the strict Calvinist doctrines in the 1610 Remonstrantiæ.
- **Belgic Confession**: A foundational doctrinal statement of the Reformed churches, which Arminius sought to revise.
- **Synod of Dort**: A national synod of the Dutch Reformed Church (1618–19) that condemned Arminianism and formulated the five points of Calvinism.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The precise nature and extent of Arminius's early Calvinism and when his views began to diverge is a subject of scholarly study.
- The political dimensions of the controversy, involving figures like Johan van Oldenbarnevelt, influenced its course and the state's attempts to mediate.
- The relationship between Arminius's theology and later Wesleyan Arminianism is debated, as John Wesley developed his views independently but acknowledged general agreement.

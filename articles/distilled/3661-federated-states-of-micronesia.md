# Federated States of Micronesia

## Overview
The Federated States of Micronesia (FSM) is a sovereign island nation in the western Pacific Ocean, comprising four states across the Caroline Islands. It is significant as a former UN Trust Territory that gained independence through a unique Compact of Free Association with the United States, which remains responsible for its defense and provides major financial aid. The nation is characterized by its vast ocean territory, small land area, and rich cultural heritage.

- **c. AD 500–1500** — The Saudeleur dynasty rules from Nan Madol on Pohnpei.

- **1899** — Spain sells the archipelago to Germany following the Spanish–American War.

- **1947** — The islands become the U.S.-administered Trust Territory of the Pacific Islands (TTPI) under a UN mandate.
- **1979** — Four TTPI districts ratify a constitution to form the Federated States of Micronesia.
- **1986** — The FSM gains independence under a Compact of Free Association with the United States.
- **1991** — The FSM is admitted to the United Nations.
- **2004** — The Compact of Free Association with the U.S. is renewed.
- **2022** — The FSM agrees to remain in the Pacific Islands Forum after a brief withdrawal announcement.

## Body

### History and Independence
The ancestors of today's Micronesians settled the islands over four thousand years ago. European contact began in the 16th century, leading to Spanish colonial rule. In 1899, Spain sold the islands to Germany, which administered them until World War I, when Japan seized control. After World War II, the islands were placed under a U.S. UN trusteeship. On May 10, 1979, four of the trust's districts formed the Federated States of Micronesia. Full independence was achieved on November 3, 1986, under a Compact of Free Association with the United States, which formally ended the trusteeship in 1990.

### Government and Politics
The FSM is governed by a 1979 constitution that establishes a federal republic with a separation of powers. The unicameral Congress has 14 members: four senators (one per state) and ten representing population districts. Congress elects the President and Vice President from among the four state senators. The U.S. is responsible for the FSM's defense under the Compact of Free Association, which also allows FSM citizens to work in the U.S. and provides economic aid. The FSM has diplomatic relations with 56 countries and is a UN member.

### Geography and Climate
The FSM consists of 607 islands spanning 2,900 km, with a total land area of 702 km² but a vast exclusive economic zone of nearly 3 million km². The four states—Yap, Chuuk, Pohnpei, and Kosrae—are each centered on volcanic islands and include numerous atolls. The climate is tropical rainforest, warm and humid year-round with high rainfall, especially on windward slopes. Mount Nahnalaud on Pohnpei is one of the wettest places on Earth. Weather patterns are influenced by the El Niño-Southern Oscillation.

### Economy and Society
The economy relies on subsistence farming, fishing, and significant financial assistance from the U.S. The population is predominantly Micronesian, with Chuukese and Pohnpeian as the largest groups. English is the official language, but numerous Austronesian languages are spoken locally. The society is over 97% Christian, with a mix of Catholic and Protestant denominations stemming from colonial history. Baseball is a popular sport.

## Terms
- **Compact of Free Association**: An agreement between the FSM and the U.S. granting the U.S. responsibility for FSM defense and providing economic aid, while allowing FSM citizens to live and work in the U.S.
- **Exclusive Economic Zone (EEZ)**: A maritime zone where a nation has special rights to explore and use marine resources, extending 200 nautical miles from its coast.
- **Trust Territory of the Pacific Islands (TTPI)**: A UN trust territory administered by the United States from 1947 to 1994, which included the FSM.
- **Nan Madol**: A UNESCO World Heritage Site on Pohnpei, a ceremonial center of artificial islands and canals built by the Saudeleur dynasty.
- **Saudeleur dynasty**: A centralized political and religious system that ruled Pohnpei from about AD 500 until 1500.
- **Operation Hailstone**: A major 1944 U.S. naval attack on the Japanese base at Truk Lagoon (now Chuuk Lagoon) during World War II.
- **Rai stones**: Large stone disks used as traditional currency on Yap, valued for their size and history.
- **Achromatopsia**: A rare, extreme form of color blindness, known locally as *maskun*, notably prevalent on Pingelap atoll.

## Debates and Open Questions
The source notes a historical curiosity: some remote islands like Kapingamarangi were not specifically named in the 1899 German-Spanish treaty, a fact unnoticed until the late 1940s. While Spain acknowledged this in 1949, it has made no modern claims. The FSM's heavy dependence on U.S. aid is cited as a major concern for its long-term economic growth.

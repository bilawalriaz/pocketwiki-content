# Karakum Desert

## Overview
The Karakum Desert, meaning "Black Sand" in Turkmen, is the 12th largest desert in the world, covering about 70% of Turkmenistan. It is a significant Central Asian landscape defined by its arid climate, sparse population, and important natural resources, including oil, gas, and a major irrigation canal.

## Body

### Geography and Hydrography
The Karakum is a vast east-west swath in Turkmenistan, bordered by the Caspian Sea to the west and the Kyzylkum Desert to the northeast. It is divided into three regions: the elevated northern Trans-Unguz Karakum, the low-lying Central Karakum, and the southeastern Karakum, which contains salt marshes. Since the 1980s, the smaller Aralkum Desert has occupied much of the former Aral Sea bed, a loss primarily caused by Soviet-era irrigation projects. The desert is crossed by the Karakum Canal, the world's second-largest irrigation canal, which diverts water from the Amu Darya river. To the south, the Murghab and Tejen rivers flow from the Hindu Kush mountains into the desert, providing irrigation water.

### Environment and Wildlife
The environment is extremely arid, with annual rainfall between 70 and 150 mm. The sands in the Aral region are composed of evaporites and alkaline mineral deposits washed from irrigated fields. Wildlife is not highly diverse and includes insects, arachnids, reptiles, and birds. Mammals include the tolai hare, goitered gazelle, and corsac fox.

### Geology and Resources
The desert contains significant oil and natural gas deposits. Its most famous geological feature is the Darvaza gas crater, a natural gas field that collapsed into a cavern and has been burning since 1971. Locally called the "Door to Hell," it is a major tourist attraction.

### Human Activity and Projects
The oases of Mary and Tejen are noted for cotton cultivation. The Trans-Caspian Railway crosses the desert. A major ongoing project is the construction of Golden Age Lake (Altyn Asyr köli) in the Karashor Depression. This artificial lake is planned to be filled by a network of canals that will pump runoff water from irrigated fields. The project is estimated to cost up to $4.5 billion and take 15 years to fill.

## Terms
- **Endorheic**: A closed drainage basin that retains water and allows no outflow to other external bodies of water.
- **Evaporites**: Minerals formed by the evaporation of water, often leaving saline deposits.
- **Pluvial period**: A geological period of increased rainfall.
- **Irrigation canal**: An artificial waterway constructed to carry water from a river or other source to land for crop cultivation.
- **Collector canal**: A large canal designed to gather and transport drainage or runoff water from agricultural areas.

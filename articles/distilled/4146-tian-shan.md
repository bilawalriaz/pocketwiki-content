# Tian Shan

## Overview
The Tian Shan is a vast mountain system in Central Asia, sacred in Tengrism and critical to regional geography and ecology. It is a major source of rivers, a cradle for important crops like apples and tulips, and a key site for studying climate change due to its rapidly receding glaciers.

## Timeline
- **c. 3000 BC** — Tulips originate in the Tian Shan Mountains.
- **Last Glacial Maximum** — The range was covered by a vast plateau glacier; the snowline was ~1200m lower than today.
- **950 CE** — Satuq Bughra Khan converts to Islam, beginning its spread among Turkic peoples of the region.
- **1850s** — Russian explorer Peter Semenov becomes one of the first Europeans to extensively document the range.
- **1300s** — The strain of *Y. pestis* causing the Black Death may have originated in the Tian Shan.
- **1961–present** — Tian Shan glaciers have lost 27% of their ice mass, nearly four times the global average.
- **2013** — The eastern Tian Shan in China's Xinjiang is listed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
- **2016** — The western Tian Shan in Kyrgyzstan, Kazakhstan, and Uzbekistan is listed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

## Body

### Geography and Geology
The Tian Shan stretches approximately 2,900 km east from Tashkent, Uzbekistan, straddling Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan, Uzbekistan, and China's Xinjiang. It is part of the Himalayan orogenic belt, formed by the collision of the Indian and Eurasian tectonic plates. Its highest peak is Jengish Chokusu (7,439 m) in Kyrgyzstan, and its lowest point is the Turpan Depression (154 m below sea level). The range is separated from the Tibetan Plateau by the Taklamakan Desert and Tarim Basin. Major rivers like the Syr Darya, Ili, and Tarim originate here. Continuous permafrost typically forms above 3,500–3,700 m.

### Named Ranges and Structure
The Tian Shan comprises numerous sub-ranges. In China, it begins with the Qarliq Tagh and Barkol Mountains, followed by the Bogda Shan. The Borohoro Mountains separate Dzungaria from the Ili River basin. In Kyrgyzstan, the main range continues as the Narat Range to the Central Tian Shan, home to the highest peaks. West of this, the range splits around Issyk Kul Lake, with the Terskey Alatau to the south and the Kyungey Ala-Too to the north. The Kyrgyz Ala-Too continues west, and south of the Fergana Valley, ranges like the Fergana and Turkestan curve toward the Pamirs.

### Ice Age and Climate
During glacial periods, the Tian Shan plateau was covered by a connected ice-stream network and a plateau glacier. The only major remnant is the 61 km long South Inylschek glacier. The glacial area covered about 118,000 km². The snowline was about 1200 m lower than today, indicating a temperature depression of 7.2 to 8.4 °C during the Last Glacial Maximum. The range has an alpine climate (Köppen ETH). Its glaciers are now rapidly receding, losing 27% of their ice since 1961, with half projected to disappear by 2050.

### Ecology and Agriculture
The Tian Shan hosts important forests of Schrenk's spruce above 2,000 m and unique natural forests of wild walnuts and apples on lower slopes. It is the origin place for tulips and the ancestors of many crops, including apricots, pears, and sweet apples. The evolution of the sweet apple was driven by natural selection, with bears likely acting as key seed dispersers. The region's ecological diversity was shaped by its geological history and the warm influence of the Indian Ocean monsoon, which limited past glaciation. The strain of *Y. pestis* that caused the Black Death may have originated here.

### Religion and History
The Tian Shan is sacred in Tengrism, a Central Asian shamanistic religion. Its second-highest peak, Khan Tengri, means "Lord of the Spirits," the religion's supreme deity. A major historical turning point was the conversion of Satuq Bughra Khan to Islam around 950 CE, which began the spread of Islam among the region's Turkic peoples.

## Terms
- **Tengrism**: A Central Asian shamanistic religion where the sky deity Tengri is supreme.
- **Orogenic belt**: A zone of crustal deformation resulting from the collision of tectonic plates, forming mountain ranges.
- **Permafrost**: Ground that remains frozen for two or more consecutive years.
- **Schrenk's spruce (*Picea schrenkiana*)**: A species of spruce tree native to the Tian Shan mountains.
- **Silk Road**: An ancient network of trade routes connecting East and West Asia.
- **Köppen climate classification ETH**: A climate type denoting an ice cap climate with all months below 0 °C.
- **Self-incompatible**: A genetic mechanism in plants that prevents self-pollination, promoting cross-pollination.
- **Cyanoglycosides**: Toxic compounds found in apple seeds that deter seed predators.

## Debates and Open questions
- The precise origin of the Black Death pandemic is debated, with one hypothesis placing its source in the Tian Shan region.
- The exact historical reference for the name "Tian Shan" is uncertain; it may relate to the Xiongnu word *Qilian* for "sky" or "heaven," but this is not definitively proven.

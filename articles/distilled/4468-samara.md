# Samara

## Overview
Samara is a major Russian city and administrative center of Samara Oblast, located at the confluence of the Volga and Samara rivers. With a population over 1.14 million, it is a significant industrial, cultural, and historical hub. Its strategic location on the Volga has shaped its role as a commercial, military, and aerospace center throughout its history.

## Timeline
- **1586** — Officially founded with the construction of a fortress at the river confluence.
- **1780** — Became an *uyezd* (county) town of Simbirsk Governorate.
- **1 January 1851** — Became the center of the newly formed Samara Governorate.
- **1935** — Renamed Kuybyshev in honor of Bolshevik leader Valerian Kuybyshev.
- **October 1941** — Selected as the alternative Soviet capital during World War II; key government and cultural institutions evacuated there.
- **1960** — Became the Soviet Union's missile shield center.
- **25 January 1991** — Reverted to its historical name, Samara.
- **2007** — Hosted the EU-Russia Summit.
- **2018** — Hosted matches for the FIFA World Cup.
- **2 July 2020** — Awarded the title "City of Labor Valor."

## Body

### Etymology and Early History
The city is named after the Samara River, with the name likely meaning "summer water" in an ancient Indo-Iranian language. The region is associated with the Neolithic Samara culture and the Kurgan hypothesis for the Proto-Indo-European homeland. Before 1586, the Samara Bend was a pirate haven. The official fortress founded in 1586 protected Russia's eastern frontier. By the 14th century, it appeared on Italian maps as a Volga port, growing into a center for trade and diplomacy with the East.

### Soviet Period and World War II
Renamed Kuybyshev in 1935, the city became a critical wartime industrial base. In 1941, it was designated the alternative Soviet capital should Moscow fall, hosting evacuated government bodies and cultural institutions. A special Revolution Day parade was held there on November 7, 1941. The city's factories supplied aircraft, firearms, and ammunition. After the war, its defense industry expanded, leading to its status as a closed city. In 1960, it became the center of the Soviet missile shield, and its plants produced the Vostok launch vehicle for the first crewed spaceflight.

### Post-Soviet Development and Urban Layout
The city regained its name, Samara, in 1991. It remains a leading industrial center, particularly in oil, petrochemicals, and aerospace. Its urban development began with the 1586 fortress, expanding along the Volga banks. Early plans in the 18th and 19th centuries imposed a rectangular grid. The 1937 "Greater Kuybyshev" plan aimed for major industrial expansion, but World War II accelerated growth as defense factories were evacuated to the city. Post-war plans in 1949 developed new industrial and residential districts like Bezymyanka, creating a two-center structure that lasted into the late 20th century.

### Economy, Culture, and Infrastructure
Samara is a top Russian city for industrial production, known for aerospace (Progress Rocket Space Centre), engines, aircraft, and food products like Zhiguli beer. Culturally, it hosts opera, ballet, drama theaters, and museums, including the Alabin Museum of Local History (founded 1886). The city is a major transport hub with a river port, rail links, and Kurumoch International Airport. Public transport includes the Samara Metro (opened 1987) and an extensive tram network (since 1915). It hosted FIFA World Cup matches in 2018 at the Samara Arena.

## Terms
*   **Uyezd:** A historical administrative division of Russia, equivalent to a county.
*   **Pale of Settlement:** The region within the Russian Empire where permanent residency by Jews was allowed.
*   **Closed city:** A city with restricted access due to the presence of sensitive military or industrial facilities.
*   **Samara Bend:** The large peninsula formed by a loop in the Volga River near the city.
*   **Kurgan hypothesis:** A theory proposing the Pontic–Caspian steppe as the original homeland (*urheimat*) of the Proto-Indo-European languages.
*   **Samara culture:** A Neolithic archaeological culture of the 5th millennium BC in the middle Volga region.
*   **Samara Urban Okrug:** The municipal division of the city, equivalent to its administrative status.

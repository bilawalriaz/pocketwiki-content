# Ceramic art

## Overview
Ceramic art is the creation of objects from inorganic, non-metallic materials, primarily clay, shaped and hardened by heat. It encompasses a vast range of forms, from utilitarian pottery to fine art sculpture, and serves as a key archaeological record for many cultures. Its history spans from prehistoric vessels to modern studio practice, reflecting technological innovation and cultural exchange.

## Timeline
- **c. 20,000 BCE** — Oldest known pottery vessels found in East Asia (Xianrendong Cave, China).
- **c. 500 BCE–200 CE** — Nok culture figurines created in West Africa.
- **c. 575 BCE** — Ishtar Gate of Babylon constructed with glazed bricks.
- **100–200 CE** — Early true porcelain produced in China's Eastern Han period.
- **8th–18th centuries** — Islamic potters develop tin-opacified glazing and elaborate painted wares.
- **16th–17th centuries** — Iznik pottery produced in Ottoman Turkey, heavily used in architecture.
- **1710** — First European hard-paste porcelain recipe devised at Meissen, Germany.
- **Late 18th century** — Josiah Wedgwood industrializes pottery production and perfects transfer-printing in England.
- **1920s–present** — Modern studio pottery movement emerges, emphasizing individual artistry.

## Body

### Materials and Types
Ceramics are classified by the clay and firing conditions used. **Earthenware** is fired at lower temperatures, remains porous, and includes common **terracotta**. **Stoneware** is fired to higher temperatures, becoming vitreous (glass-like) and non-porous. **Porcelain**, first developed in China, is fired at very high temperatures (1,200–1,400 °C), resulting in a hard, white, translucent, and resonant material. **Bone china**, an English invention, is a type of porcelain containing bone ash, known for its strength and translucency.

### Surface Treatments
Decoration is applied through various methods. **China painting** involves applying pigments to glazed porcelain, often requiring multiple firings. **Slipware** uses a liquid clay suspension (**slip**) for decoration, applied before the first firing. Techniques include **sgraffito** (scratching through a layer of colored slip) and **terra sigillata** (a refined slip for burnishing). **Burnishing** polishes the clay surface with a hard object to create a smooth, sometimes water-resistant finish.

### Forms and Functions
Ceramic objects serve diverse purposes. **Studio pottery** refers to unique or small-run pieces made by individual artists. **Tiles** are used for architectural decoration and flooring. **Figurines** are small sculptural representations. **Tableware** includes functional dishes and vessels. **Terracotta** also refers to sculptural artworks, such as the Terracotta Army of China.

### History and Regional Developments
Ceramic art has a long, independent history across the globe. The oldest vessels are from East Asia. In the ancient Mediterranean, Greek and Etruscan cultures are renowned for painted pottery, while Roman production was vast but less artistically refined. Islamic potters innovated with glazes and decoration, influencing European styles like **tin-glazed pottery** (e.g., maiolica, Delftware). The European discovery of porcelain secrets in the 18th century led to major factories at Meissen, Sèvres, and in England. In the Americas, sophisticated traditions developed independently, such as the Nazca and Moche cultures in Peru and Pueblo pottery in North America. The 20th-century studio pottery movement, with figures like Bernard Leach, emphasized handcraft and artistic expression.

## Terms
- **Ceramic**: Art made from inorganic, non-metallic materials shaped and hardened by heat.
- **Clay**: A natural earthy material that becomes hard when fired.
- **Earthenware**: Pottery fired at relatively low temperatures, remaining porous.
- **Stoneware**: A vitreous or semi-vitreous ceramic, fired at high temperatures, making it non-porous.
- **Porcelain**: A hard, white, translucent ceramic made from kaolin and fired at very high temperatures.
- **Glaze**: A glassy coating applied to ceramics for decoration and to make them impermeable.
- **Slip**: A liquid suspension of clay in water, used for decoration or joining pieces.
- **Kiln**: A furnace for firing pottery to high temperatures.
- **Studio pottery**: Pottery made by an artist or artisan, typically in a small studio, emphasizing individual creation.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The precise purpose and cultural significance of early figurines, such as the Venus of Dolní Věstonice and Nok figures, remain unclear.
- The exact date and location of the invention of true porcelain in China is debated, with archaeological finds continually pushing dates earlier.
- The term "porcelain" lacks a universal definition and has been applied inconsistently to various materials.

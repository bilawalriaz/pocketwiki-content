# Scythians

## Overview
The Scythians were an ancient Eastern Iranic nomadic people who dominated the Pontic Steppe (modern Ukraine and southern Russia) from the 7th to 3rd centuries BC. They were renowned for their mastery of mounted warfare, particularly archery, and their distinctive "Animal Style" art. Their history illustrates the dynamic interplay between nomadic and settled civilizations, influencing military technology, trade, and culture across Eurasia.

## Timeline
- **9th–8th centuries BC** — Scythians migrate from Central Asia to the Pontic Steppe.
- **8th century BC** — Displace Cimmerians as dominant power on the western Eurasian Steppe.
- **7th century BC** — Cross the Caucasus; raid West Asia alongside Cimmerians.
- **c. 679 BC** — King Išpakāya killed by Neo-Assyrian king Esarhaddon.
- **c. 650–625 BC** — Reign of Madyes; Scythian hegemony peaks in West Asia.
- **625 BC** — Median king Cyaxares assassinates Scythian leaders, ending their West Asian dominance.
- **c. 513 BC** — Persian king Darius I campaigns against the Scythians in Europe.
- **4th century BC** — "Golden Age" of Scythian culture and wealth; reign of King Ateas.
- **339 BC** — King Ateas killed in battle against Philip II of Macedon.
- **Early 3rd century BC** — Sarmatian invasions collapse the Pontic Scythian kingdom.
- **1st century BC** — Scythians retreat to "Little Scythia" in Crimea and Dobruja.
- **c. 250 AD** — Scythians cease to exist as an independent group, assimilated by other populations.

## Body

### Origins and Migration
The Scythians originated in Central Asia and Siberia, part of a broader westward migration of Iranic nomads. They arrived in the Ciscaucasian Steppe by the 8th century BC, displacing the Cimmerians. Their early culture, the Chernogorovka-Novocherkassk complex, shows links to earlier Bronze Age traditions.

### West Asian Period (7th–6th centuries BC)
Scythians crossed the Caucasus into West Asia, where they interacted with the Neo-Assyrian Empire. King Bartatua became an Assyrian vassal, and his son Madyes expanded Scythian power, conquering Urartu and defeating Cimmerians. This period introduced Scythians to ironworking, scale armor, and West Asian artistic influences. Their dominance ended when the Median king Cyaxares assassinated their leaders around 625 BC.

### Pontic Kingdom and Greek Relations
After retreating from West Asia, the Scythians consolidated power in the Pontic Steppe. They established a kingdom that traded grain, slaves, and furs with Greek colonies on the Black Sea coast. This trade brought Greek luxury goods and artistic influences, leading to the Hellenization of the Scythian aristocracy. The kingdom reached its zenith in the 4th century BC under King Ateas.

### Decline and Legacy
The Scythian kingdom declined after Ateas's death in 339 BC, facing military defeats, economic crises, and Sarmatian invasions. By the 3rd century BC, they were pushed to fortified settlements in Crimea and Dobruja ("Little Scythia"). They became sedentary farmers and were eventually assimilated by Sarmatians, Goths, and other groups. Their name was later used generically for various steppe nomads.

## Terms
- **Scythians proper**: The Iranic people who dominated the Pontic and Crimean Steppes between the Danube and Don rivers from the 7th to 3rd centuries BC.
- **Kurgan**: A type of barrow grave tomb, ranging from simple to elaborate royal tombs containing the "Scythian triad."
- **Scythian triad**: A set of distinctive artifacts—weapons, horse harnesses, and "Animal Style" art—used to identify Scythian culture.
- **Animal Style**: An artistic tradition featuring stylized depictions of animals, often in dynamic poses, characteristic of steppe nomads.
- **Gōrytos**: A combined quiver-bowcase used to carry a Scythian composite bow and arrows.
- **Skythikon**: A potent poison made from decomposing snakes, human blood, and dung, used to coat arrowheads.
- **Sarmatians**: An Iranic nomadic people who migrated from the east, eventually conquering and assimilating the Scythians.
- **Little Scythia**: The fortified territories in Crimea and Dobruja where Scythians retreated after the collapse of their Pontic kingdom.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Dynastic Continuity**: Scholars debate whether the Scythians were ruled by a single dynasty from their West Asian period onward or by multiple dynasties (e.g., those of Bartatua, Spargapeithes, and Ariapeithes).
- **Darius I's Campaign**: The outcome of the Persian king Darius I's 513 BC campaign against the Scythians is unclear; Persian sources claim victory, while Greek accounts (via Herodotus) describe a Scythian victory through evasion.
- **Terminology**: Modern scholars debate the use of "Scythian" broadly for all Iron Age steppe nomads versus narrowly for the Pontic Scythians proper, with the latter preferred by many Scythologists.
- **Origins of Griffin Legend**: One hypothesis suggests the griffin myth originated from Scythians encountering *Protoceratops* fossils, though this is contested by scholars who trace the imagery to earlier West Asian traditions.

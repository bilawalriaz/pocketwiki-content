# Sikhism

## Overview
Sikhism is a monotheistic Indian religion founded in the Punjab region in the late 15th century CE by Guru Nanak. It emphasizes devotion to one formless God (Waheguru), equality, selfless service (seva), and justice. With 25–30 million followers, it is one of the youngest major world religions.

## Timeline
- **c. 1469–1539** — Life of Guru Nanak, founder of Sikhism
- **1526** — Guru Nanak establishes community at Kartarpur
- **1606** — Martyrdom of Guru Arjan by Mughal emperor Jahangir
- **1675** — Execution of Guru Tegh Bahadur by Aurangzeb
- **1699** — Guru Gobind Singh founds the Khalsa; names Guru Granth Sahib as eternal guru
- **1708** — Death of Guru Gobind Singh; line of human gurus ends
- **1839** — Death of Maharaja Ranjit Singh; Sikh Empire declines
- **1873** — Singh Sabha Movement begins to revitalize Sikh identity
- **1947** — Partition of India; mass migration and violence affecting Sikhs
- **1984** — Operation Blue Star and anti-Sikh riots following Indira Gandhi's assassination

## Body

### Origins and the Ten Gurus
Sikhism originated with Guru Nanak (1469–1539), born in present-day Pakistan. He taught devotion to one God, social equality, and rejection of ritualism. His message was passed through nine successive gurus. The tenth guru, Guru Gobind Singh (1666–1708), ended the line of human gurus by installing the Guru Granth Sahib as the eternal spiritual authority.

### Core Beliefs and Practices
Central to Sikh belief is Ik Onkar—the unity of God. Sikhs practice simran (remembrance of God), kirtan (devotional singing), and sevā (selfless service). The five Ks—kesh (uncut hair), kara (steel bracelet), kirpan (ceremonial sword), kachera (cotton undergarment), and kangha (wooden comb)—are worn by initiated Sikhs (Khalsa) as articles of faith.

### Scripture
The Guru Granth Sahib is the primary scripture, compiled initially by Guru Arjan in 1604 (as the Adi Granth) and finalized by Guru Gobind Singh in 1678. It contains hymns from seven Sikh gurus and includes compositions from Hindu bhagats and Muslim saints. Written in Sant Bhasha and Gurmukhi script, it is treated as a living guru.

### Historical Persecution and Resistance
Two Sikh gurus were executed by Mughal rulers for refusing conversion: Guru Arjan (1606) and Guru Tegh Bahadur (1675). These martyrdoms galvanized Sikh resistance. In response to persecution, Guru Gobind Singh founded the Khalsa in 1699, creating a community combining spiritual devotion with martial readiness—the sant-sipahi ideal.

### Miri and Piri
Guru Hargobind introduced the doctrine of Miri and Piri, representing temporal (political) and spiritual authority respectively. This dual concept became foundational, affirming that spiritual insight must engage with worldly justice and governance.

### Modern History
After the fall of the Sikh Empire in 1849, Sikh institutions declined under British rule. The Singh Sabha Movement (1873) revitalized Sikh identity, promoting orthodoxy and opposing syncretic practices. Partition in 1947 caused massive displacement and violence. In 1984, Operation Blue Star damaged the Golden Temple complex, triggering anti-Sikh riots after Indira Gandhi's assassination.

### Sects and Diversity
Sikhism includes various sects such as Udasi, Nirmala, Khalsa, and Ahmadi-inspired groups. Some, like the Akali Nihangs, maintain distinct practices including ritual cannabis use. Caste distinctions persist despite Sikh teachings against them, particularly affecting Dalit Sikhs.

## Terms
- **Ik Onkar**: The Sikh concept of one supreme God.
- **Gurmukhi**: Script standardized by Guru Angad for writing Sikh scriptures.
- **Khalsa**: Initiated Sikh community founded by Guru Gobind Singh in 1699.
- **Miri Piri**: Doctrine combining temporal and spiritual authority.
- **Sant-sipahi**: Ideal of soldier-saint balancing devotion and defense.
- **Seva**: Selfless service performed without expectation of reward.
- **Simran**: Meditative remembrance of God’s name.
- **Amritdhari**: Baptized Sikh who has undergone initiation ceremony.
- **Gurbani**: Hymns and teachings found in the Guru Granth Sahib.
- **Langar**: Community kitchen serving free vegetarian meals to all visitors.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Dasam Granth and Sarbloh Granth**: Authenticity disputed; attributed to Guru Gobind Singh by some, written later by unknown authors according to scholars.
- **Term "Sikhism"**: Some prefer "Sikhi," arguing "Sikhism" was coined by British colonizers and implies rigid doctrine contrary to Sikh philosophy.
- **Dietary practices**: Disagreement exists over meat consumption; some follow strict vegetarianism while others eat jhatka meat.
- **Cannabis use**: Akali Nihangs ritually consume cannabis (shaheedi degh), conflicting with broader prohibitions on intoxicants.
- **Caste system**: Despite official rejection, caste-based discrimination persists among rural Sikhs.
- **Khalistan movement**: Advocacy for independent Sikh state remains controversial and politically sensitive.

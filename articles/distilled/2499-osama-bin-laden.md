# Osama bin Laden

## Overview
Osama bin Laden was the founder and first general emir of al-Qaeda, a Salafi jihadist organization. He orchestrated the September 11, 2001 attacks on the United States, which directly killed 2,977 people and triggered the U.S.-led global war on terror. His life and actions were central to the rise of transnational jihadist terrorism in the late 20th and early 21st centuries.

## Timeline
- **10 March 1957** — Born in Saudi Arabia.
- **1979** — Leaves Saudi Arabia to aid Afghan mujahideen fighting the Soviet occupation.
- **1984** — Co-founds Maktab al-Khidamat (MaK) to recruit and fund foreign fighters.
- **1988** — Founds al-Qaeda.
- **1991** — Exiled from Saudi Arabia for criticizing the royal family.
- **1992** — Moves to Sudan, establishing a base for operations.
- **1996** — Expelled from Sudan; returns to Afghanistan and allies with the Taliban.
- **7 August 1998** — Al-Qaeda bombs U.S. embassies in Kenya and Tanzania.
- **11 September 2001** — Al-Qaeda attacks the World Trade Center and Pentagon, killing 2,977.
- **2 May 2011** — Killed by U.S. Navy SEALs in Abbottabad, Pakistan.

## Body

### Early Life and Radicalization
Born into a wealthy Saudi family, bin Laden studied economics and business administration but became deeply interested in Islam and jihad. He left college in 1979 to join the Afghan mujahideen fighting the Soviet Union. In 1984, he co-founded Maktab al-Khidamat (MaK) to recruit and fund foreign fighters, using his personal inheritance. After the Soviet withdrawal from Afghanistan in 1989, he commanded a mujahideen force in the ensuing civil war. His public criticism of the Saudi royal family for allowing U.S. troops on Saudi soil during the Gulf War led to his exile in 1991.

### Building Al-Qaeda and Early Attacks
Bin Laden relocated to Sudan in 1991, where he expanded al-Qaeda's network and financed jihadist conflicts in Algeria, Bosnia, and Afghanistan. Al-Qaeda's first major attack on the U.S. was the 1993 World Trade Center bombing. Expelled from Sudan in 1996, he returned to Afghanistan, allied with the Taliban, and declared holy war against the United States in fatwas issued in 1996 and 1998. The 1998 U.S. embassy bombings in East Africa marked a significant escalation, leading to his indictment in the U.S. and official designation as a terrorist.

### The September 11 Attacks and Aftermath
The 9/11 attacks, planned primarily by bin Laden and Khalid Sheikh Mohammed, involved 19 hijackers crashing four airliners into U.S. landmarks. The attacks killed 2,977 people, injured 25,000, and led to the collapse of the World Trade Center. The U.S. launched the global war on terror, invading Afghanistan to depose the Taliban and hunt bin Laden. He escaped the Battle of Tora Bora in late 2001 and spent the next decade in hiding, primarily in Pakistan, while al-Qaeda continued attacks worldwide, including in Bali (2002), Istanbul (2003), and London (2005).

### Death and Legacy
In 2010, U.S. intelligence identified bin Laden's compound in Abbottabad, Pakistan. On 2 May 2011, U.S. Navy SEALs raided the compound and killed him. His death was a major symbolic victory for the U.S., but al-Qaeda continued under new leadership. Bin Laden is overwhelmingly viewed in the West as a terrorist and mass murderer, while his popularity among Muslims peaked during the Iraq War before declining. His actions fundamentally shaped global counter-terrorism policies and conflicts.

## Terms
- **Salafi jihadist**: An adherent of a fundamentalist Sunni Islamic ideology that advocates violent struggle to establish a global caliphate.
- **Mujahideen**: Muslim fighters engaged in jihad, or struggle in the name of Islam.
- **Fatwa**: A non-binding ruling on a point of Islamic law given by a qualified scholar.
- **Caliphate**: A form of Islamic government led by a caliph, a political and religious successor to the prophet Muhammad.
- **Al-Qaeda**: The militant Islamist organization founded by Osama bin Laden in 1988.
- **Taliban**: A fundamentalist Islamist movement and former de facto ruling government of Afghanistan.
- **War on terror**: The international military campaign led by the United States following the 9/11 attacks.
- **Enhanced interrogation techniques**: A euphemism for torture methods used by the CIA on suspected terrorists.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The extent of Saudi government involvement in funding or supporting the 9/11 attacks remains disputed, with some U.S. investigators alleging ties while the 9/11 Commission found no evidence of foreign government funding.
- The precise knowledge and role of Pakistan's Inter-Services Intelligence (ISI) in harboring bin Laden in Abbottabad is debated, with conflicting reports from U.S., Pakistani, and journalistic sources.
- The long-term strategic impact of bin Laden's strategy to "bleed" the U.S. into economic collapse through prolonged wars is a subject of analysis, particularly regarding the 20-year war in Afghanistan.

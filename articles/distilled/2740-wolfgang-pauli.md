# Wolfgang Pauli

## Overview
Wolfgang Pauli (1900–1958) was an Austrian theoretical physicist and a foundational figure in quantum mechanics. He is most famous for the Pauli exclusion principle, which explains the structure of matter, and for proposing the neutrino to solve a crisis in nuclear physics. His work was critical to the development of modern physics, and he was known as the "conscience of physics" for his rigorous and often critical intellect.

## Timeline
- **25 April 1900** — Born in Vienna, Austria.
- **1918** — Published first paper on Einstein's general relativity.
- **1921** — Received Ph.D. from LMU Munich; completed a 237-page review of relativity theory.
- **1924** — Proposed the Pauli exclusion principle.
- **1926** — Derived the hydrogen spectrum using Heisenberg's matrix mechanics; developed theory of paramagnetism.
- **1928** — Appointed Professor of Theoretical Physics at ETH Zurich.
- **4 December 1930** — Proposed the existence of the neutrino in a letter to a conference.
- **1934** — Married Franziska "Franca" Bertram.
- **1940** — Re-derived the spin-statistics theorem; moved to the Institute for Advanced Study in the US.
- **1945** — Awarded the Nobel Prize in Physics for the exclusion principle.
- **1956** — Neutrino experimentally confirmed by Reines and Cowan.
- **15 December 1958** — Died in Zurich, Switzerland.

## Body

**Early Life and Education**
Pauli was born in Vienna to a prominent family and was raised Roman Catholic. He showed early brilliance, publishing his first paper on general relativity at age 18. After earning his Ph.D. in 1921, he wrote a monumental review of relativity theory that became a standard reference, praised by Einstein. He worked with leading physicists like Max Born and Niels Bohr before becoming a lecturer in Hamburg.

**Major Scientific Contributions**
Pauli's most important work was the **exclusion principle** (1924), which states that no two electrons in an atom can have the same set of four quantum numbers. This explained the shell structure of atoms and the periodic table. He later introduced the **Pauli matrices** to describe electron spin, a fundamental property of matter. In 1930, to solve a problem in beta decay where energy appeared not to be conserved, he hypothesized a neutral, nearly massless particle. This particle, named the **neutrino** by Enrico Fermi, was confirmed in 1956. Pauli also made key contributions to quantum field theory, including the **spin-statistics theorem** and regularization techniques.

**Later Career and Personal Life**
Pauli spent most of his career at ETH Zurich, interrupted by a move to the US during World War II. He was a perfectionist and a devastating critic, earning the nickname "conscience of physics." He famously dismissed unclear theories as "not even wrong." He had a deep interest in psychoanalysis, collaborating with Carl Jung on the relationship between physics and psychology, a concept known as the **Pauli-Jung Conjecture**. He died of pancreatic cancer in 1958, in room 137—a number linked to his lifelong fascination with the fine-structure constant.

## Terms
- **Pauli Exclusion Principle**: A quantum mechanical principle stating that no two identical fermions (e.g., electrons) can occupy the same quantum state simultaneously.
- **Neutrino**: A nearly massless, neutral elementary particle proposed by Pauli to explain missing energy in beta decay.
- **Quantum Number**: A numerical value that describes a specific property of an electron in an atom (e.g., energy, angular momentum).
- **Spin**: An intrinsic form of angular momentum carried by elementary particles, a quantum property with no classical analogue.
- **Pauli Matrices**: A set of three 2×2 complex matrices used in quantum mechanics to represent the spin operators for a spin-1/2 particle.
- **Spin-Statistics Theorem**: A fundamental theorem linking a particle's spin to its statistical behavior: half-integer spin particles are fermions, integer spin particles are bosons.
- **Aufbau Principle**: A rule for building up the electron configuration of an atom by adding electrons to orbitals in order of increasing energy.
- **Fine-Structure Constant**: A dimensionless fundamental constant (approximately 1/137) characterizing the strength of the electromagnetic interaction.

## Debates and Open Questions
- Pauli was a vocal critic of the **modern synthesis of evolutionary biology**, arguing against a purely gene-based view of inheritance. His arguments are sometimes cited in support of **epigenetic inheritance**.
- His collaboration with Carl Jung on **synchronicity** and a "psychophysically neutral reality" remains a controversial and non-mainstream philosophical interpretation of quantum physics.
- The **Pauli effect**—the anecdotal tendency for equipment to fail in his presence—was taken seriously enough by some colleagues to be investigated, linking to his interest in parapsychology.

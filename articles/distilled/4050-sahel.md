# Sahel

## Overview
The Sahel is a semi-arid biogeographical transition zone in Africa, stretching between the Sahara Desert to the north and the wetter Sudanian savannas to the south. It is defined by its hot, dry climate, vulnerability to drought and desertification, and a rapidly growing population facing severe challenges of food insecurity, political instability, and terrorism. The region's history, culture, and current geopolitical significance are shaped by this harsh environment and its position as a crossroads of trade, migration, and conflict.

## Timeline
- **c. 4000 BC** — Climate change causes the Sahara and Sahel to dry rapidly, forcing migrations.
- **750 AD onwards** — Sahelian kingdoms (e.g., Alodia) emerge, founding cities like Timbuktu and Gao.
- **Late 19th C.** — Western Sahel falls to France, becoming part of French West Africa.
- **1960** — French territories in the Sahel gain independence.
- **1968–1974** — A major drought leads to large-scale famine and international aid.
- **2010** — Severe famine and record-breaking heatwaves strike the Sahel.
- **2011** — The Libyan Crisis exacerbates regional instability and terrorism.
- **2020s** — A wave of military coups occurs; the Sahel becomes the global epicenter of terrorism.

## Body

### Geography and Climate
The Sahel spans 5,900 km from the Atlantic to the Red Sea, covering about 3 million km². It is a flat, hot semi-arid region with a short rainy season and a long dry season. Annual rainfall varies from 100–200 mm in the north to 700–1,000 mm in the south. The climate is characterized by intense heat, low humidity, and high sunshine duration. The region has experienced severe droughts, most notably from 1968–1974 and in 2010, which have caused famines and accelerated desertification.

### Flora, Fauna, and Environment
The landscape is dominated by grassland and savanna, with drought-tolerant acacia trees as the most prominent flora. Historically home to large grazing mammals, many species are now vulnerable or extinct due to over-hunting and competition with livestock. The Sahel faces critical environmental issues, including desertification from over-farming and over-grazing, and frequent major dust storms. Initiatives like the Great Green Wall aim to combat desertification through reforestation.

### Culture and Society
Traditionally, many Sahelian peoples have been semi-nomadic, practicing transhumance—moving livestock between northern and southern grazing areas. The region is home to diverse ethnic groups, including the Fulani, Tuareg, Hausa, and Arabs. Culturally, the Sahel is identified as a likely origin point for key elements of the blues music tradition, brought to North America by enslaved people. French is widely spoken as a legacy of colonialism, though its official status is being challenged in several countries.

### History and Geopolitics
Early agriculture in the region declined around 4000 BC due to drying. Larger kingdoms emerged from 750 AD. The western Sahel was colonized by France in the late 19th century and gained independence in 1960. Since the 2010s, the Sahel has been destabilized by terrorism, coups, and insurgencies, earning it the label "Coup Belt." By 2021, it accounted for 35% of global terrorism deaths. Political instability has led to shifting alliances, with some governments moving away from Western partners toward Russia and China.

## Terms
- **Sahel**: The semi-arid transition zone between the Sahara Desert and the Sudanian savannas.
- **Transhumance**: A traditional pastoral practice of moving livestock seasonally between different grazing areas.
- **Desertification**: The process by which fertile land becomes desert, typically as a result of drought, deforestation, or inappropriate agriculture.
- **Griot**: A West African historian, storyteller, praise singer, or musician.
- **Blue note**: A musical note sung or played at a slightly lower pitch than the standard major scale for expressive purposes, a hallmark of blues music.
- **Françafrique**: A term for the relationship between France and its former African colonies, often implying neocolonial influence.
- **G5 Sahel**: A former alliance of five Sahel countries (Burkina Faso, Chad, Mali, Mauritania, Niger) for security cooperation.

## Debates and Open questions
- The exact origins and influences on the blues music tradition are debated, with scholars pointing to the Sahel's musical culture as a key source.
- The causes of increased rainfall in the Sahel since the 1990s are debated, with possible links to global warming and changes in Atlantic and Mediterranean sea temperatures.
- The effectiveness of international interventions and the future geopolitical alignment of Sahel states (e.g., toward Russia or China) amid ongoing instability are major open questions.

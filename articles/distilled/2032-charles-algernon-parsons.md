# Charles Algernon Parsons

## Overview
Sir Charles Algernon Parsons (1854–1931) was an Anglo-Irish engineer whose invention of the practical steam turbine in 1884 revolutionized marine propulsion and electrical power generation. His work enabled faster ships and cheap, plentiful electricity, fundamentally shaping modern naval and electrical engineering.

## Body

### Early Life and Education
Parsons was born into an aristocratic Anglo-Irish family and educated at home by private tutors. He studied mathematics at Trinity College Dublin and St. John's College, Cambridge. After graduating, he took the unusual step for an earl's son of becoming an engineering apprentice at W. G. Armstrong, later working on rocket-powered torpedoes at Kitsons in Leeds.

### Invention of the Steam Turbine
In 1884, while head of electrical development at Clarke, Chapman and Co., Parsons designed a practical compound steam turbine. Unlike the contemporary impulse turbine of Gustaf de Laval, which was limited by high centrifugal forces, Parsons's design split the steam pressure drop into small expansions over many turbine stages. This allowed for moderate speeds and avoided the damaging cutting action of high-velocity steam, making the turbine a viable prime mover for generating electricity.

### Founding of Parsons and Company
Parsons founded C. A. Parsons and Company in 1889 to manufacture his turbo generators. That same year, he established the Newcastle and District Electric Lighting Company, which opened the world's first turbo generator power station in 1890. Although his first turbine was only 1.6% efficient, rapid improvements led to his first megawatt turbine in 1899.

### Marine Applications and *Turbinia*
Parsons applied his turbine to marine propulsion, founding the Parsons Marine Steam Turbine Company. In 1894, he built *Turbinia*. Its unannounced, high-speed demonstration at the 1897 Diamond Jubilee Fleet Review—reaching 34 knots compared to the Royal Navy's 27 knots—proved the technology's potential. This led to the adoption of turbines in destroyers, passenger ships, and the battleship HMS *Dreadnought*.

### Honours and Legacy
Parsons received numerous awards, including the Rumford Medal (1902), Copley Medal (1928), and Bessemer Gold Medal (1929). He was knighted in 1911 and elected to the Order of Merit in 1927. His company, later part of Siemens, survived him, as did the Grubb Parsons telescope company (until 1985). His wife and daughter co-founded the Women's Engineering Society in 1919.

## Terms
- **Compound steam turbine:** A turbine that splits the total pressure drop of the steam into small expansions across multiple stages in series, reducing steam velocity and centrifugal forces.
- **Impulse turbine:** A turbine design (like de Laval's) where steam expands completely in a single nozzle, creating high-velocity jets that strike blades, subjecting the mechanism to high stress.
- **Turbo generator:** An electricity generator driven directly by a steam turbine.
- **Prime mover:** A machine that converts a source of energy (like steam) into mechanical motion to drive other machinery, such as a generator.
- **Knot (kn):** A unit of speed equal to one nautical mile per hour, used in maritime and aviation contexts.
- **Neuritis:** Inflammation of a nerve or nerves, given as the cause of Parsons's death.

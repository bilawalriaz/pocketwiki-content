# Sichuan

## Overview
Sichuan is a major inland province in Southwestern China, historically known as Ba-Shu. It is a cradle of indigenous civilization, a strategic heartland for numerous dynasties, and today is China's most populous western province and a significant economic powerhouse. Its unique geography, from fertile basins to the Tibetan Plateau, has shaped its distinct culture, cuisine, and role as a refuge throughout Chinese history.

## Timeline
- **c. 2700–1750 BC** — Baodun culture flourishes in the Chengdu Plain.
- **316 BC** — Qin armies conquer the kingdoms of Shu and Ba.
- **221–263 AD** — Liu Bei establishes the Shu Han kingdom during the Three Kingdoms era.
- **755–763 AD** — Emperor Xuanzong of Tang flees to Sichuan during the An Lushan Rebellion.
- **1023 AD** — The world's first true paper money (*jiaozi*) is issued in Chengdu.
- **1273 AD** — The Mongol Yuan dynasty breaks through Song defenses in Sichuan.
- **Mid-17th century** — Zhang Xianzhong's rebellion devastates the province.
- **1786 AD** — A landslide dam failure on the Dadu River causes a flood killing 100,000 people.
- **1938–1945** — Chongqing serves as the wartime capital of the Republic of China.
- **1949–1952** — Sichuan is divided into four administrative areas after Communist victory.
- **1959–1961** — The province suffers gravely during the Great Chinese Famine.
- **1997** — Chongqing is separated from Sichuan to become a direct-controlled municipality.
- **2008** — A magnitude 7.9/8.0 earthquake strikes near Chengdu, killing over 87,000.

## Body

### History and Administration
Sichuan's recorded history begins with the indigenous Ba and Shu kingdoms, which were conquered by the Qin dynasty in 316 BC. The Qin introduced advanced agriculture, including the Dujiangyan irrigation system. The region's defensible basin geography made it a frequent refuge and base for rival states, most notably Liu Bei's Shu Han during the Three Kingdoms (221–263). It was a cultural and economic center during the Tang and Song dynasties, but suffered massive population loss during the Mongol conquests and the 17th-century rebellion of Zhang Xianzhong. The modern province's borders were largely set in the early 18th century. After 1949, it was reorganized multiple times, most significantly when Chongqing was separated in 1997 to spearhead western development.

### Geography and Climate
The province has two distinct parts: the fertile, humid subtropical Sichuan Basin in the east and the mountainous, high-altitude eastern Tibetan Plateau in the west. The Yangtze River and its tributaries, including the Min, Jialing, and Tuo, flow through the basin. The western mountains, part of the Hengduan range, contain the province's highest point, Gongga Shan (7,556 m). The region is geologically active, with the Longmenshan Fault causing the devastating 2008 earthquake. Climate varies from cloudy, mild winters in the basin to frigid, alpine conditions in the west.

### Economy
Sichuan is China's 5th-largest provincial economy and the largest in Western China. Historically called the "Province of Abundance," it is a major agricultural base for grain, pork, and citrus. It is rich in mineral resources, holding China's largest reserves of natural gas, vanadium, titanium, and lithium. The province is a key industrial center for heavy industry, electronics, and automobile manufacturing, with major development zones in Chengdu and Mianyang. It is also a significant tourism destination, home to UNESCO sites like Jiuzhaigou Valley and the Giant Panda Sanctuaries.

### Culture and Demographics
The majority Han population speaks Sichuanese Mandarin, a divergent dialect of Mandarin Chinese. The province is also home to significant Tibetan, Yi, and Qiang ethnic minorities in its western regions. Sichuan cuisine is world-famous for its bold flavors, liberal use of chili peppers and the numbing Sichuan pepper, and iconic dishes like hot pot, Kung Pao chicken, and mapo tofu. The region is a major habitat for the giant panda, with large reserves and breeding centers like the Chengdu Research Base.

## Terms
- **Ba-Shu**: The historical name for the region, referring to the ancient Ba and Shu kingdoms that once occupied the Sichuan Basin.
- **Sichuan Basin**: A lowland region in eastern Sichuan, fertile and densely populated, surrounded by mountains.
- **Hengduan Mountains**: A series of north-south mountain ranges in western Sichuan, forming the eastern edge of the Tibetan Plateau.
- **Dujiangyan**: An ancient irrigation system engineered by Li Bing of the Qin dynasty to control the Min River, still in use today.
- **Sichuanese Mandarin**: The primary Chinese dialect spoken in the province, distinct from Standard Mandarin in phonology and vocabulary.
- **Sichuan pepper**: A spice native to the region, known for its citrusy flavor and a unique numbing sensation (*málà*).
- **Giant panda**: A bear native to south-central China, with the majority of its wild population living in Sichuan's bamboo forests.
- **Third Front campaign**: A Chinese government initiative (1960s-70s) to develop industrial capacity in interior provinces like Sichuan for national defense.

## Debates and open questions
- The etymology of "Sichuan" is commonly misinterpreted as "four rivers." Historical geographer Tan Qixiang argues it is a contraction of "Four Plain Circuits," referring to a Song dynasty administrative division.
- The origin of the Minjiang dialects is debated; some linguists consider them a direct descendant of the extinct Old Sichuanese (Ba-Shu Chinese), while others see them as a branch of Southwestern Mandarin.
- The population collapse in Sichuan during the Mongol conquest (from 2.64 million families in 1162 to 120,000 in 1282) is attributed to multiple factors, including war deaths, forced deportations, evacuations, and possible census inaccuracies.

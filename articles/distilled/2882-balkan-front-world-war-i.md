# Balkan Front (World War I)

## Overview
The Balkan Front was a major theatre of World War I, primarily involving Serbia and Montenegro against the Central Powers (Austria-Hungary, Germany, Bulgaria, and the Ottoman Empire). It was significant for the initial resistance of Serbia, the complex political situation in Greece, and the final Allied offensive in 1918 that broke Bulgaria and contributed to the collapse of the Central Powers.

## Timeline
- **1914** — Three failed Austro-Hungarian offensives against Serbia.
- **Oct 1915** — Bulgaria declares war on Serbia, joining the Central Powers.
- **Late 1915** — Serbia and Montenegro conquered; Serbian army retreats through Albania to Corfu.
- **1915-1918** — Macedonian front established near Salonika; protracted trench warfare.
- **1916** — Italy occupies southern Albania and Northern Epirus.
- **1917** — Greece joins the Allied Powers after the National Schism.
- **Sep 1918** — Vardar Offensive breaks Bulgarian lines.
- **Sep 29, 1918** — Bulgaria signs an armistice.
- **Nov 1918** — Liberation of Serbia, Albania, and Montenegro; Allied forces halt at Hungarian border.

## Body

### Serbian–Montenegrin Campaign and Italian Involvement
The war began with Serbian resistance against Austro-Hungarian invasions, aided by a Russian offensive in the north. In 1915, a combined Austro-Hungarian, German, and Bulgarian attack from north and east forced a Serbian retreat through Albania. The remnant Serbian army was evacuated by Allied ships to Corfu and later reformed in Salonika. Italy, which had occupied Vlorë in Albania in 1914, expanded its control during the war, eventually proclaiming central and southern Albania a protectorate in 1917. Italy also sought to seize Dalmatia from Austria-Hungary, a goal outlined in the 1915 Treaty of London.

### Bulgaria's Entry and the Macedonian Front
Bulgaria joined the Central Powers in 1915, motivated by a desire to reclaim territories from Serbia, Greece, and Romania lost in the Balkan Wars. Germany promised Bulgaria Serbian Macedonia and a loan. Bulgarian forces achieved early victories, occupying southern Serbia and parts of Greek Macedonia. The Allied presence in Greece, centered on the Macedonian front at Salonika, stabilized into trench warfare. The front remained largely static until 1918.

### The National Schism in Greece
Greece was politically divided between Prime Minister Venizelos, who favored the Allies, and King Constantine, who advocated neutrality. After the Allied landing at Salonika, Venizelos established a rival government in northern Greece. Allied pressure, including a blockade and the capture of Thessaly in 1917, led to King Constantine's abdication. Greece then joined the Allies and declared war on the Central Powers in July 1917.

### The 1918 Vardar Offensive and Collapse
In September 1918, a multinational Allied army under French General Franchet d'Espèrey launched the Vardar Offensive from Greece. The offensive broke through Bulgarian lines, forcing Bulgaria to sue for peace on September 29. Allied forces then advanced north, liberating Serbia and threatening Austria-Hungary, which accepted an armistice on November 4, 1918. The offensive halted at the Hungarian border in November.

## Terms
*   **Central Powers:** The alliance of Germany, Austria-Hungary, the Ottoman Empire, and Bulgaria during World War I.
*   **Allies (Entente):** The coalition opposing the Central Powers, including Serbia, France, the United Kingdom, Russia, Italy, and later Greece.
*   **Macedonian Front:** The line of conflict in the Balkans, centered around Salonika (Thessaloniki), Greece.
*   **National Schism:** The political division in Greece between pro-Allied and pro-neutral factions during World War I.
*   **Vardar Offensive:** The September 1918 Allied offensive that broke the Bulgarian army and led to the collapse of the Central Powers' southern flank.
*   **Army of the Orient:** The multinational Allied force stationed on the Macedonian front.
*   **Treaty of London (1915):** A secret pact promising Italy territorial gains, including parts of Dalmatia, in exchange for joining the Allies.
*   **Treaty of Neuilly (1919):** The post-war treaty that imposed territorial losses and reparations on Bulgaria.

## Debates and Open Questions
The historian John Keegan argued that the installation of a "violently nationalist" government in Athens after the war complicated the Allied effort to resettle Europe, as it mobilized Greece for the "Great Idea" of recovering a Greek empire in the east.

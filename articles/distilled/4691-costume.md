# Costume

## Overview
Costume is the distinctive style of dress and/or makeup that reflects culture, including class, gender, occupation, ethnicity, nationality, activity, or epoch. While the term historically described appropriate clothing for specific activities (e.g., riding costume), its modern usage is largely limited to unusual, out-of-date, or identity-changing attire, such as theatrical, Halloween, and mascot costumes. Costume is a key element in performance, cultural expression, and social ritual.

## Timeline
- **8th century** — Pope Gregory VIII designates November 1 as All Saints Day, incorporating Samhain's costuming tradition into Christian holidays.
- **1879** — Artist Hans Makart designs costumes for a Viennese parade celebrating the Austro-Hungarian Emperor's wedding anniversary, creating a regular event.
- **Late 19th century** — "Costumiers," often women, run businesses making complicated or intimate female costumes, including millinery and corsetry.
- **Early 20th century** — Ready-to-wear apparel begins to replace handmade clothing, altering the costume industry.
- **2022** — United States households spend an average of $100 preparing for Halloween, with $34 going to costume-related spending.

## Body

### Etymology and General Usage
The term "costume" derives from the Italian and French words for fashion or custom, ultimately tracing to the Latin *consuetudo* ("custom"). From the eighteenth century onward, it came to mean clothing exclusively. Its general usage for everyday attire has been largely replaced by terms like "dress" or "attire," with "costume" now denoting clothing for performance, disguise, or historical reenactment.

### National and Theatrical Costume
National costume expresses local identity and cultural attributes, often serving as a source of pride (e.g., the Scottish kilt, Japanese kimono). In Bhutan, traditional dress like the men's *Gho* and women's *Kira* has been prescribed for centuries. Theatrical costume is clothing worn to portray a character in performance. It communicates context like historical period, location, and character traits, sometimes through stylized exaggeration, as in Commedia dell'arte.

### Costume Construction and Jobs
A costume technician translates a designer's two-dimensional sketch into a garment. Key construction techniques include **draping** (manipulating fabric on a form to create a pattern) and **flat drafting** (drawing patterns from measurements). The industry includes specialized roles: the **costume designer** creates the concept, the **costume technician** constructs the garment, the **wardrobe supervisor** maintains costumes during a run, and the **milliner** makes hats.

### Religious Festivals and Parades
Costumes are central to holidays derived from religious festivals. Halloween costumes evolved from Celtic Samhain disguises to ward off spirits, later incorporated into Christian All Hallows Eve. Other examples include Mardi Gras jesters, Christmas Santa Claus suits, and Purim costumes in Judaism, celebrating a reversal of destiny. Parades and processions, like Chinese New Year Lion Dances or historical pageants, also provide occasions for costume.

### Sporting Events, Parties, and Play
Costumes are worn at public events like fun runs and private masquerade balls. **Mascots**—costumed representatives of teams or businesses—appear at sporting events and festivals. For children, costumes facilitate role-play and exploration of identities, from superheroes to professions. **Cosplay** (from "costume play") is a performance art where participants wear detailed costumes to represent specific characters, often at conventions.

### Design and Industry
Costume design is the art of envisioning clothing for a character or performer to denote status, provide protection, or create visual interest. Professional costume companies design, create, rent, and sell costumes for theater, film, and trade, with some maintaining large rental warehouses. Organizations like the Costume Designers Guild and the National Costumers Association support the industry.

## Terms
- **Costume**: Distinctive dress reflecting culture, epoch, or identity, now often for performance or disguise.
- **Costumier**: A maker of costumes, especially complex or intimate women's clothing.
- **Draping**: A pattern-making technique of manipulating fabric directly on a dress form.
- **Flat drafting**: Creating a pattern by drawing on paper based on measurements.
- **Milliner**: A hatmaker.
- **Mascot**: A costumed character representing a team, brand, or organization.
- **Cosplay**: A performance art involving wearing costumes to represent specific characters.
- **Fursuit**: An animal costume worn by members of the furry fandom.

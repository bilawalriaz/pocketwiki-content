# Autism

## Overview
Autism spectrum disorder (ASD) is a neurodevelopmental condition characterized by persistent differences in social communication and interaction, alongside restricted, repetitive patterns of behavior, interests, or sensory processing. These traits emerge in early childhood and persist throughout life, varying widely in presentation and support needs—from minimal assistance to 24-hour care. Autism is highly heritable, with genetics explaining most risk, while environmental factors play a smaller, primarily prenatal role. There is no cure; management focuses on therapies to build skills and reduce barriers, and medications for co-occurring conditions like anxiety or epilepsy. The neurodiversity movement challenges the medical model, framing autism as a natural human variation requiring accommodation rather than cure, a perspective that remains debated among stakeholders.

## Timeline
- **1911** — Paul Eugen Bleuler coins *Autismus* to describe social withdrawal in schizophrenia.
- **1926** — Grunya Sukhareva publishes detailed observations of six boys with traits closely matching modern ASD criteria, distinguishing them from schizophrenia.
- **1940s** — Leo Kanner and Hans Asperger independently describe "early infantile autism" and "autistic psychopathy" in children.
- **1980** — DSM-III establishes separate diagnoses for autism and schizophrenia; "infantile autism" appears as a pervasive developmental disorder.
- **1994** — DSM-IV adds Asperger syndrome and PDD-NOS; U.S. special-education autism classification introduced.
- **1998** — Andrew Wakefield publishes fraudulent study linking MMR vaccine to autism; later retracted, Wakefield struck off medical register.
- **2012** — "Double empathy problem" theory proposed, reframing social difficulties as mutual misunderstanding between autistic and non-autistic people.
- **2013** — DSM-5 unifies previous sub-diagnoses (classic autism, Asperger, PDD-NOS) into single "autism spectrum disorder" (ASD) with dimensional severity ratings.
- **2018/2022** — ICD-11 released (2018) and comes into effect (2022), classifying ASD based on co-occurring intellectual disability and language impairment levels.
- **2022** — DSM-5-TR published, retaining DSM-5 ASD framework. CDC reports U.S. prevalence at 1 in 31 children (2022 data).

## Body

### Signs and Characteristics
Autism is defined by two core domains: **social communication/interaction difficulties** and **restricted, repetitive behaviors (RRBs)**. Presentation is highly variable ("spiky profile"), with strengths in some domains alongside significant disabilities in others.

**Social Communication:** Difficulties span three areas: *social-emotional reciprocity* (e.g., reduced shared attention, atypical conversation flow, *monotropism*—intense focus on specific interests), *nonverbal communication* (e.g., atypical eye contact, prosody, gesture integration), and *relationship development* (e.g., difficulty inferring subtext, higher bullying victimization). The *double empathy problem* (2012) posits these are bidirectional mismatches rather than unilateral deficits. Many autistic people *mask* (consciously suppress traits), which correlates with poorer mental health and delayed diagnosis.

**Restricted/Repetitive Behaviors (RRBs):** DSM-5-TR requires at least two of: *stereotyped movements* (stimming: rocking, hand-flapping, echolalia), *insistence on sameness* (distress at minor routine changes), *highly restricted, fixated interests* (intense focus providing fulfillment/expertise), and *sensory reactivity* (hyper- or hypo-reactivity to input, experienced by ~90%). Stimming serves self-regulation; suppression increases stress.

**Associated Features:** *Self-injury* occurs ~3x more often (head-banging, biting), linked to communication frustration, sensory regulation, or basal ganglia connectivity. *Elopement* (wandering) affects ~50% of families; drowning is a leading cause of death. *Autistic burnout* (prolonged exhaustion from masking/demands), *inertia* (difficulty initiating/switching/stopping tasks), *meltdowns* (outward overwhelm), and *shutdowns* (inward withdrawal) are widely reported non-diagnostic experiences. *Cognitive profile* is often uneven; *savant skills* (prodigious memory, splinter skills) are more common. *Motor difficulties* (60–80%), *atypical eating* (70% of children), and *sleep problems* (~66%) are prevalent.

### Causes
**Genetics:** Autism is highly heritable (twin studies). Risk is polygenic (hundreds of common variants with tiny effects) and/or driven by rare *de novo* mutations (deletions/duplications). ~25% have *syndromic autism* (e.g., fragile X, Down syndrome). Genes converge on synaptic function, chromatin remodeling, and excitatory/inhibitory neurotransmission balance. Older paternal age increases *de novo* mutation risk. Recurrence in siblings: 7–20%.

**Prenatal/Perinatal Factors:** Advanced parental age, maternal gestational diabetes/infection/inflammation, certain medication exposures (e.g., valproate), and significant air pollution show associative links. No single factor is conclusive.

**Disproven Hypotheses:** *Refrigerator mother* (cold parenting) caused stigma but lacks evidence. *Vaccines* (MMR, thimerosal): Wakefield’s 1998 study was fraudulent; a 2014 meta-analysis of 1.25M children found no link. Rates rose after thimerosal removal.

**Neurocognitive Theories:** *Intense World Theory* (hyper-reactive neural microcircuits); *Enhanced Perceptual Functioning* (superior low-level perception as root); *Weak Central Coherence* (detail-over-context bias); *Empathizing–Systemizing* (cognitive style bias toward rule-based systems).

### Diagnosis and Classification
**DSM-5-TR (2022):** Single ASD diagnosis. Two core domains: (A) Social Communication, (B) RRBs. Severity levels (1–3) rated *per domain* based on support needed. Onset in early development (may manifest later when demands exceed capacity).

**ICD-11 (2022):** Code 6A02. Classifies primarily by co-occurring *intellectual disability* (present/absent) and *functional language impairment* (absent/mild/moderate/severe).

**Screening/Assessment:** Professional evaluation required. Criteria mandate "clinically significant impairment" beyond cultural expectations. Diagnosis often missed in girls/women due to camouflaging and different behavioral presentation (male:female diagnosis ratio ~3:1). Adult diagnosis increasing since 1990s; late diagnosis often follows anxiety/depression treatment and brings relief/access to accommodations.

### Management
**Non-Pharmacological:** *Applied Behavior Analysis (ABA)* uses positive reinforcement to teach skills/reduce harmful behaviors. Early intensive ABA shows gains in language/IQ, but faces criticism: ethical concerns (compliance focus, prompt dependency, masking enforcement), adverse event underreporting, and conflicts of interest. *Developmental/Naturalistic* models (e.g., DSP, ESDM) use play-based, child-led approaches. *Speech/OT/CBT/social skills training* target specific domains. *Mindfulness* shows promise for distress/cognition. *Parent-mediated* and *teacher-implemented* interventions extend gains.

**Inclusion:** *Universal Design for Learning (UDL)* and *SPACE framework* (Sensory, Predictability, Acceptance, Communication, Empathy) address environmental barriers in school/work. Workplace accommodations (flexible hours, noise reduction, clear instructions) improve employment outcomes (currently very low).

**Pharmacological:** No drug treats core features. >50% of U.S. autistic children prescribed psychoactives. *Risperidone/aripiprazole* (FDA-approved for irritability/aggression) carry significant side effects (metabolic, sedation); UK NHS restricts use. *Stimulants* (methylphenidate) less effective for ADHD in autism vs. non-autism, more side effects. *SSRIs/antipsychotics* show limited efficacy for RRBs.

**Alternative/Complementary:** *Chelation, bleach (MMS), CEASE therapy, hyperbaric oxygen* are dangerous/ineffective. *Gluten/casein-free diet* lacks reliable evidence; casein-free diets risk low bone density.

**Emerging:** *Music therapy* (severity/functioning/QoL), *animal-assisted therapy* (social communication/irritability), *CBT/mindfulness for adults*.

### Prognosis
Symptoms relatively stable; ~90% of children diagnosed <6 retain diagnosis at 1-year follow-up. Apparent "improvement" may reflect masking. Long-term: ~85% need support for independent living. Predictors of independence: spoken language <6, IQ >50, marketable skills. Mortality ~2x general population (higher with co-occurring neuro/psych conditions). **Suicidality:** Autistic people ~3x more likely to attempt suicide; 15–25% of adults have attempted. Risk factors: masking, victimization (bullying/assault), unemployment, depression (4x risk), gender diversity. SBQ-ASC (2023) is only validated screening tool.

### Epidemiology
**Prevalence:** WHO: ~1 in 100 children (2012–2021), rising. CDC (US): 1 in 31 (2022 data), up from 1 in 150 (2000). Rise attributed to broader criteria, awareness, service access, earlier diagnosis. Adult prevalence similar if assessed.

**Sex Ratio:** Males diagnosed ~3x more. Females under-identified due to camouflaging, different phenotype, diagnostic bias.

**Co-occurring Conditions:** ADHD (25–32%), Intellectual Disability (30–40%, decreasing proportion as diagnosis expands), Epilepsy (~10%, higher with ID), Anxiety (17–23%), Depression (9–13%), OCD (7–10%), GI disorders (very common, linked to irritability/sleep), Sleep disorders (~66%), Motor dyspraxia/hypermobility/EDS (high rates), Gender dysphoria (elevated).

**Sexuality/Gender:** Autistic people more likely LGBTQ+; ~70% non-heterosexual in one 2018 study vs. 30% controls. Higher rates of asexuality, transgender/gender-nonconforming identity.

### History
**Etymology:** Bleuler (1911): *autos* (self) + *-ismos* (state), describing schizophrenia withdrawal. Sukhareva (1926): First detailed clinical picture of ASD in children, distinguishing from schizophrenia decades before DSM-III (1980). Kanner (1943) and Asperger (1944) established modern clinical foundations.

**Diagnostic Evolution:** DSM-III (1980) separated autism from schizophrenia. DSM-IV (1994) broadened spectrum (Asperger, PDD-NOS). DSM-5 (2013) unified into ASD. ICD-11 (2022) aligned dimensionally but retains intellectual/language specifiers.

### Society and Culture
**Neurodiversity Movement:** Frames autism as natural variation, not pathology. Advocates: acceptance, accommodation, identity-first language ("autistic person"), therapies targeting coping/harm reduction not normalization. Controversial: critics argue it centers lower-support-needs voices, potentially neglecting those with high support needs who may view autism as disability. Clinical guidelines (UK, US, Australia) increasingly adopt neurodiversity-affirming language.

**Legal/Discrimination:** Legal barriers exist (e.g., Sweden driving/military, US military disqualification, UK firearms discrimination ruling). Discrimination in hiring, education, healthcare common; often rapid upon perception of difference; can lead to violence/exclusion.

**Driving:** Only ~20% of US autistic adults licensed by 21 (vs. >60% non-autistic). Difficulties: slower hazard perception, motor coordination, sensory overload, executive function. Licensed autistic drivers have similar crash rates but fewer speeding/suspension violations.

**Family/Culture:** Parental grief/stigma common; cultural values shape reaction. Autistic parents may understand children better. Public figures (Greta Thunberg, Bill Gates) disclose diagnoses, reframing narrative. Events: World Autism Awareness Day, Autistic Pride Day.

## Terms
- ****Neurodevelopmental disorder**** — A condition originating in the developmental period involving impairments in brain function affecting emotion, learning, self-control, or memory.
- ****Spectrum**** — A classification model indicating high variability in trait presentation and support needs, not a simple linear scale from mild to severe.
- ****Stimming**** — Self-stimulatory behavior (e.g., rocking, hand-flapping, echolalia) serving self-regulation or sensory modulation.
- ****Masking (Camouflaging)**** — Conscious or unconscious suppression of autistic traits to appear non-autistic; linked to burnout, mental health decline, and delayed diagnosis.
- ****Double empathy problem**** — Theory that social communication breakdowns between autistic and non-autistic people are bidirectional, not solely an autistic deficit.
- ****Monotropism**** — Cognitive style characterized by intense, narrow focus on a limited number of interests at a time.
- ****Alexithymia**** — Difficulty identifying and describing one's own emotions; co-occurs in ~50% of autistic people vs. ~5% non-autistic.
- ****Syndromic autism**** — Autism occurring as part of a known genetic syndrome (e.g., fragile X, Down syndrome); ~25% of cases.
- ****Idiopathic autism**** — Autism without a known single-gene or chromosomal cause; typically polygenic.
- ****Diagnostic overshadowing**** — Attributing symptoms of a co-occurring condition (e.g., anxiety, GI pain) to autism itself, delaying appropriate treatment.

## Debates and open questions
*   **Neurodiversity vs. Medical Model:** Whether autism should be framed primarily as a difference to accommodate or a disorder to treat; tension between advocates for high-support-needs individuals and neurodiversity proponents.
*   **ABA Ethics:** Whether ABA’s focus on normalization/compliance constitutes ethical violation or necessary skill-building; lack of adverse event monitoring in research.
*   **Diagnostic Boundaries:** Whether current criteria (DSM-5-TR/ICD-11) capture the full phenotype; proposals for biological markers, prototype descriptions, or splitting autism into distinct etiologies.
*   **Female Phenotype:** Extent to which diagnostic criteria and tools are biased toward male presentation, leading to systematic underdiagnosis in girls/women.
*   **Suicidality Mechanisms:** Relative contributions of masking, victimization, co-occurring mental illness, and autism-specific stressors to extremely elevated suicide risk; lack of validated interventions.
*   **Etiological Heterogeneity:** Whether "autism" represents a single natural category or a final common pathway for many distinct genetic/environmental etiologies (the "fractionable autism triad" hypothesis).
*   **Adult Outcomes:** Paucity of longitudinal data on aging, late-diagnosed adults, and efficacy of adult-specific interventions.
*   **Environmental Risk:** Which specific prenatal/perinatal environmental factors (beyond valproate, infection, pollution) have causal roles, and their interaction with genetic susceptibility.

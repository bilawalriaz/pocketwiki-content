# Vilnius

## Overview
Vilnius is the capital and largest city of Lithuania, serving as the nation's political, economic, and cultural heart. Its Old Town is one of Europe's largest and best-preserved, designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1994. The city's history as a multicultural capital, particularly its significant Jewish heritage, has shaped its identity, and it has evolved into a modern European financial and technology hub.

## Timeline
- **c. 1323** — First mentioned as the capital of Lithuania in letters by Grand Duke Gediminas.
- **1387** — Granted city rights after the Christianization of Lithuania.
- **1579** — Vilnius University established by Grand Duke Stephen Báthory.
- **1795** — Becomes part of the Russian Empire following the Third Partition of Poland.
- **1920** — Occupied by Poland after a conflict with Lithuania.
- **1939** — Annexed by Lithuania, then by the Soviet Union in 1940.
- **1990** — Lithuania declares independence from the Soviet Union.
- **1991** — Soviet troops attack the TV Tower, killing 14 civilians on January 13.
- **1994** — Old Town designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
- **2009** — Named a European Capital of Culture.
- **2023** — Hosts the NATO Summit.

## Body

### History and Identity
Vilnius has been Lithuania's capital since at least 1323. It flourished as the capital of the Grand Duchy of Lithuania, becoming a multicultural center compared to Babylon. Before World War II, it was a major Jewish center, dubbed "the Jerusalem of the North" by Napoleon. The city's demographics shifted significantly after World War II due to population exchanges; as of 2021, about two-thirds of residents are Lithuanians. The city was the site of key events in Lithuania's 1990 independence from the Soviet Union.

### Architecture and Cityscape
The Old Town is renowned for its Vilnian Baroque architecture, influenced by Italian and Swiss-Italian artists. The city is the easternmost Baroque city and the largest such city north of the Alps. The historic center is built around three castles and the former city wall. A modern Central Business District has developed in the Šnipiškės neighborhood since the 2000s. Vilnius is noted for its extensive green space, covering 68.8% of its area.

### Culture and Institutions
Vilnius is a major cultural center with numerous museums, galleries, and theaters. The first Eastern European printing house was established here in 1520. The city has a rich musical heritage, from the 17th-century opera to modern jazz and choral festivals. It is home to the Lithuanian National Opera and Ballet Theatre and the Lithuanian National Philharmonic Society. The annual Kaziukas Fair and Vilnius Culture Night are significant events.

### Economy and Science
As Lithuania's economic center, Vilnius has a per-capita GDP over €40,000. It is a leading European fintech hub and a global center for laser technology, with Lithuanian companies holding over 50% of the world market share in ultrashort pulse lasers. The city hosts major research institutions like the Centre for Physical Sciences and Technology and the Vilnius University Laser Research Centre.

### Education and Religion
Vilnius University, founded in 1579, is the largest and oldest university in Lithuania. The city is the seat of the Roman Catholic Archdiocese of Vilnius and has a significant history of religious diversity, including a major Jewish community centered on the Vilna Gaon. Key pilgrimage sites include the Chapel of the Gate of Dawn and the Sanctuary of the Divine Mercy.

## Terms
- **Vilnian Baroque**: An architectural style named after the city, characterized by its development in the Grand Duchy of Lithuania, influenced by Italian and Swiss-Italian artists.
- **Grand Duchy of Lithuania**: A historical state that existed from the 13th to the 18th century, of which Vilnius was the capital.
- **Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth**: A dual state formed in 1569 by the Union of Lublin, uniting the Kingdom of Poland and the Grand Duchy of Lithuania.
- **UNESCO World Heritage Site**: A landmark or area with legal protection by an international convention administered by the United Nations Educational, Scientific and Cultural Organization.
- **Fintech**: Technology-driven innovation in financial services.
- **Vilna Gaon**: The honorific for Rabbi Eliyahu Kremer, a major 18th-century scholar of Judaism whose writings influence Orthodox Jews.
- **January Events**: The violent crackdown by Soviet troops in Vilnius on January 13, 1991, which killed 14 civilians during Lithuania's independence movement.
- **Kaziukas Fair**: An annual folk arts and crafts fair held in Vilnius around the feast of Saint Casimir.

## Debates and Open Questions
The historical ethnic composition of Vilnius, particularly during the 19th and early 20th centuries, is a subject of scholarly debate, with different censuses (e.g., Russian 1897, German 1916, Polish 1931) providing varying estimates for Polish, Lithuanian, and Jewish populations. The city's identity has been shaped by these demographic shifts and competing national narratives.

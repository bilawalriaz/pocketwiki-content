# History of Morocco

## Overview
Morocco's history spans from prehistoric human habitation to the modern kingdom, shaped by indigenous Berber cultures, successive Islamic dynasties, and European colonialism. Its strategic location at the crossroads of Africa, Europe, and the Middle East has made it a cultural and political crossroads, with a continuous thread of Berber identity underlying waves of external influence. The modern state, ruled by the Alawi dynasty since the 17th century, emerged from a French protectorate in 1956 and continues to navigate regional tensions and internal reforms.

- **c. 300,000 years ago** — Oldest known *Homo sapiens* remains found at Jebel Irhoud.
- **8th–6th centuries BCE** — Phoenician colonization of the Moroccan coast begins.
- **5th century BCE** — Carthage extends hegemony over coastal areas.
- **33 BCE–40 CE** — Mauretania becomes a client state, then a province, of the Roman Empire.
- **Early 8th century CE** — Muslim conquest of the Maghreb brings Islam and Arabic to the region.
- **740–743 CE** — Berber Revolt breaks Morocco from Umayyad Caliphate control.
- **789–974 CE** — Idrisid dynasty founds the first Moroccan state, centered in Fez.
- **c. 1060–1147 CE** — Almoravid dynasty unifies Morocco and intervenes in Al-Andalus.
- **c. 1121–1269 CE** — Almohad Caliphate dominates North Africa and Iberia.
- **1912** — Treaty of Fez establishes a French protectorate over Morocco.
- **1956** — Morocco gains independence from France and Spain.
- **1999–present** — Reign of King Mohammed VI, marked by reforms and the Western Sahara conflict.

## Body

### Prehistoric and Ancient Foundations
Archaeological evidence shows Morocco was inhabited by hominids at least 400,000 years ago. Early *Homo sapiens* remains at Jebel Irhoud, dated to at least 300,000 years ago, are among the oldest known. The region saw successive cultures, from Mesolithic hunter-gatherers to Neolithic herders who domesticated cattle. Phoenician traders established coastal depots from the 8th century BCE, followed by Carthagenean hegemony. After Carthage's decline, the indigenous Berber kingdom of Mauretania emerged, later becoming a client and then a province of the Roman Empire, administered as Mauretania Tingitana with its capital at Volubilis.

### Islamic Conquest and Early Dynasties
The Muslim conquest in the early 8th century CE introduced Islam and Arabic. The Berber Revolt of 740–743 broke the region from the Umayyad Caliphate, leading to fragmentation into small states like the Barghawata and Emirate of Sijilmasa. The Idrisid dynasty, founded by Idris I in 789, established the first Moroccan state centered in Fez, which became a major religious and commercial center. Successive Berber-led dynasties—the Almoravids, Almohads, Marinids, and Wattasids—alternately unified Morocco and expanded into Al-Andalus (Islamic Iberia), but also faced internal strife and external threats from Christian kingdoms.

### European Encroachment and the Protectorate
From the 15th century, Portuguese and Spanish incursions targeted Morocco's coast. The Alawi dynasty, which has ruled since 1666, initially maintained sovereignty but faced growing European pressure. France established a protectorate in 1912 via the Treaty of Fez, with Spain receiving a northern zone. The protectorate period saw economic exploitation, the rise of Moroccan nationalism, and resistance, including the Rif War (1921–1926). Sultan Mohammed V became a symbol of independence, and after his exile and return, negotiations led to Moroccan independence in 1956.

### Independence and Modern Morocco
Post-independence, Morocco became a constitutional monarchy under Mohammed V and later his son, Hassan II. Hassan II's reign (1961–1999) was marked by political repression ("years of lead"), the 1973 "Moroccanization" of the economy, and the unresolved Western Sahara conflict, which began after Spain ceded the territory in 1976. A ceasefire with the Polisario Front has held since 1991, but a promised referendum on self-determination has never occurred. King Mohammed VI (since 1999) has enacted political and social reforms, including a new constitution recognizing Amazigh as an official language, while managing regional tensions, including a diplomatic break with Algeria in 2021.

## Terms
- **Berbers (Amazigh):** Indigenous people of North Africa, whose languages and cultures predate Arab and Islamic influence.
- **Idrisid dynasty:** Considered the founders of the first Moroccan state, ruling from 789 to 974.
- **Almoravid dynasty:** A Sanhaja Berber dynasty (c. 1060–1147) that unified Morocco and intervened in Iberia.
- **Almohad Caliphate:** A Masmuda Berber-led empire (c. 1121–1269) that dominated North Africa and Iberia.
- **Marinid dynasty:** A Zenata Berber dynasty (c. 1244–1465) that succeeded the Almohads and made Fez a cultural center.
- **Alawi dynasty:** The current ruling dynasty of Morocco, in power since 1666.
- **Protectorate:** A form of colonial administration where a foreign power controls foreign affairs and key institutions, while a local ruler remains a figurehead.
- **Polisario Front:** A Sahrawi nationalist movement seeking independence for Western Sahara, backed by Algeria.
- **Amazigh:** The self-designation of the Berber people; recognized as an official language of Morocco in 2011.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Western Sahara's status:** The territory remains disputed between Morocco, which administers most of it, and the Polisario Front, which seeks a referendum on independence. The UN-led peace process has been stalled for decades.
- **Nature of pre-Islamic society:** The extent of Romanization and the persistence of Berber cultural and political autonomy in the hinterland during ancient periods are subjects of scholarly discussion.
- **Impact of colonialism:** Historians debate the long-term economic and social effects of the French protectorate, particularly regarding infrastructure development versus resource extraction and cultural disruption.

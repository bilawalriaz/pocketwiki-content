# Dissolution of the Soviet Union

## Overview
The dissolution of the Soviet Union was the formal end of the USSR as a sovereign state on December 26, 1991. It concluded the Cold War and resulted from a combination of chronic economic stagnation, the unsustainable burden of the arms race, intense ethnic nationalism, and the destabilizing effects of Mikhail Gorbachev's reforms (glasnost and perestroika). The collapse led to the independence of 15 republics and a severe economic crisis across the post-Soviet space.

## Timeline
- **11 March 1985** — Mikhail Gorbachev is elected General Secretary of the Communist Party.
- **16 November 1988** — Estonia becomes the first union republic to declare state sovereignty.
- **23 August 1989** — The Baltic Way, a human chain of ~2 million people, protests Soviet rule across Estonia, Latvia, and Lithuania.
- **11 March 1990** — Lithuania becomes the first republic to declare independence.
- **12 June 1991** — Boris Yeltsin is elected President of the Russian SFSR.
- **19–21 August 1991** — A failed coup by communist hardliners against Gorbachev accelerates the republics' declarations of independence.
- **8 December 1991** — The Belavezha Accords are signed by Russia, Ukraine, and Belarus, declaring the Soviet Union no longer exists.
- **21 December 1991** — The Alma-Ata Protocol is signed, formally establishing the Commonwealth of Independent States (CIS).
- **25 December 1991** — Gorbachev resigns; the Soviet flag is lowered from the Kremlin for the last time.
- **26 December 1991** — The Soviet of the Republics formally dissolves the Soviet Union.

## Body

### Causes and Gorbachev's Reforms
The collapse resulted from long-term economic stagnation, the financial strain of the arms race and foreign conflicts, and resurgent ethnic nationalism. Gorbachev's reforms, intended to revive the system, inadvertently accelerated its end. *Glasnost* (openness) allowed public criticism and exposed historical crimes, while *perestroika* (restructuring) attempted economic and political changes that destabilized the central government. His abandonment of the Brezhnev Doctrine, which had enforced Soviet control over Eastern Europe, led to the peaceful revolutions of 1989 that toppled communist regimes in the Warsaw Pact.

### Rise of Nationalism and Sovereignty Movements
Gorbachev's reforms unleashed powerful nationalist movements across the 15 union republics. The Baltic states led the way, with Estonia declaring sovereignty in 1988 and Lithuania declaring full independence in March 1990. Violent ethnic conflicts erupted in the Caucasus, notably between Armenia and Azerbaijan over Nagorno-Karabakh, and in Georgia. Mass protests and the formation of popular fronts (e.g., in Estonia, Latvia, Lithuania, Moldova, and Ukraine) challenged Moscow's authority. By 1990, competitive elections in several republics brought reformers and nationalists to power, leading to a "war of laws" where local governments rejected Soviet legislation.

### The Final Collapse (1991)
A failed coup by communist hardliners in August 1991 fatally weakened the central government. In its aftermath, republics rushed to declare independence. On December 8, the leaders of Russia, Ukraine, and Belarus signed the Belavezha Accords, proclaiming the USSR's dissolution and creating the CIS. The Alma-Ata Protocol on December 21 brought in most other republics. On December 25, Gorbachev resigned, and the Soviet flag was lowered. The next day, the upper chamber of the Supreme Soviet formally dissolved the union. Russia, as the largest republic, became the USSR's recognized successor state, inheriting its UN Security Council seat.

### Aftermath and Consequences
The dissolution caused a catastrophic economic decline across the post-Soviet states, with GDP falling an average of 51% and life expectancy dropping sharply, particularly in Russia. Poverty and inequality surged. The transition was marked by "shock therapy" economic reforms and numerous post-Soviet conflicts over territory and ethnic minorities. The CIS was formed as a loose successor community, though some states, like the Baltic nations, moved decisively toward the West. The event also had global repercussions, ending the Cold War and impacting countries like Cuba, North Korea, and Afghanistan that had relied on Soviet support.

## Terms
- **Glasnost**: Gorbachev's policy of "openness" that increased transparency and freedom of speech, allowing public criticism of the government and historical crimes.
- **Perestroika**: Gorbachev's policy of "restructuring" aimed at reforming the Soviet political and economic system.
- **Sovereignty**: The principle that a republic's laws take precedence over those of the Soviet Union, a key step toward independence.
- **Belavezha Accords**: The agreement signed on December 8, 1991, by Russia, Ukraine, and Belarus that declared the Soviet Union dissolved and established the CIS.
- **Commonwealth of Independent States (CIS)**: A loose association of post-Soviet states formed to replace the Soviet Union.
- **Union Republics**: The 15 constituent republics of the USSR, each designated as the homeland of a major ethnic group.
- **Popular Fronts**: Pro-independence political movements that emerged in several republics (e.g., Estonia, Latvia, Lithuania) to challenge Soviet rule.
- **War of Laws**: The conflict in 1990-91 where republics passed laws contradicting Soviet legislation, asserting local control over their economies and resources.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Intentionalist vs. Structuralist Explanations**: Historians debate whether the collapse was primarily due to the policies and decisions of individuals like Gorbachev and Yeltsin (intentionalist) or was the inevitable result of deep-seated structural problems within the Soviet system (structuralist).
- **Role of Chernobyl**: Gorbachev himself later suggested the 1986 Chernobyl disaster, more than his reforms, was a real cause of the collapse by exposing systemic failures.
- **Legality of Dissolution**: The legal basis for the dissolution is debated, particularly the ratification of the Belavezha Accords by the Russian parliament, which some argued was unconstitutional under both Soviet and Russian law.
- **Impact on Global Order**: Scholars continue to analyze whether the collapse allowed neoliberal capitalism to become the dominant global system and contributed to rising global inequality.

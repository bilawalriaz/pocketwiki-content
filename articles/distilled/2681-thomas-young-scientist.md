# Thomas Young (scientist)

## Overview
Thomas Young (1773–1829) was a British polymath whose work fundamentally shaped modern science. He is best known for establishing the wave theory of light, pioneering the study of elasticity, and making crucial contributions to the decipherment of Egyptian hieroglyphs. His interdisciplinary genius influenced fields from physics to linguistics, though his complex writing style sometimes hindered the communication of his ideas.

## Timeline
- **1773** — Born in Milverton, Somerset, to a Quaker family.
- **1796** — Earned a doctorate in medicine from the University of Göttingen.
- **1801** — Appointed professor of natural philosophy at the Royal Institution.
- **1801** — First described astigmatism and proposed a theory of colour vision.
- **1803** — Presented his interference experiment to the Royal Society, supporting the wave theory of light.
- **1804** — Developed the theory of capillary action based on surface tension.
- **1807** — Described the concept of Young's modulus in his *Course of Lectures*.
- **1813** — Introduced the term "Indo-European languages."
- **1814** — Completed translation of the demotic text of the Rosetta Stone.
- **1822** — Jean-François Champollion published his hieroglyphic decipherment, sparking a priority dispute with Young.
- **1829** — Died in London of atherosclerosis.

## Body

### Wave Theory of Light
Young's most important achievement, in his own view, was re-establishing Christiaan Huygens' wave theory of light against Isaac Newton's dominant corpuscular theory. Through experiments with interference—most famously a double-slit experiment using sunlight—he demonstrated that light behaves as a wave. He used these experiments to calculate the wavelengths of different colours, arriving at values close to modern measurements. His work was later extended and solidified by Augustin-Jean Fresnel.

### Elasticity and Mechanics
In 1807, Young described the property of elasticity now known as Young's modulus (E), which relates stress (force per unit area) to strain (fractional deformation) in a material. This was a revolutionary concept because, unlike Hooke's law, it provided a material constant independent of geometry, allowing engineers to predict deformation without physical testing for every new component. The concept was not entirely new, with precursors in work by Leonhard Euler (1727) and Giordano Riccati (1782).

### Vision and Colour
Young is considered the founder of physiological optics. He explained how the eye focuses by changing the curvature of its lens and was the first to describe astigmatism. He proposed the trichromatic theory of colour vision, hypothesizing that the retina contains three types of nerve fibres sensitive to different colours. This theory, later developed by Hermann von Helmholtz, foreshadowed the modern understanding of cone cells.

### Egyptian Hieroglyphs
Starting in 1813, Young made significant advances in deciphering ancient Egyptian writing. He completely translated the demotic (a later Egyptian script) text of the Rosetta Stone and correctly identified six hieroglyphic signs as having phonetic values. However, he did not deduce the full grammar. His work was foundational, but Jean-François Champollion later published a more complete decipherment system in 1822, leading to a bitter priority dispute. Scholars now generally credit Young with pioneering the decipherment of demotic script.

### Other Contributions
Young's work spanned many fields. He developed the Young–Laplace equation for capillary action, defined the modern concept of "energy," and created a rule for paediatric drug dosage. In linguistics, he introduced the term "Indo-European languages" and proposed a universal phonetic alphabet. He also developed a method for tuning musical instruments known as Young temperament.

## Terms
*   **Wave theory of light:** The theory that light propagates as a wave, as opposed to a stream of particles (corpuscles).
*   **Interference:** A phenomenon in which two waves superpose to form a resultant wave of greater, lower, or the same amplitude, crucial evidence for wave behaviour.
*   **Young's modulus (E):** A measure of the stiffness of a solid material, defined as the ratio of tensile stress to tensile strain.
*   **Stress:** The internal force per unit area within materials that arises from externally applied forces.
*   **Strain:** The deformation or displacement of a material that results from an applied stress.
*   **Trichromatic theory:** The theory that colour perception is based on three types of photoreceptors (cones) in the eye.
*   **Demotic script:** A simplified Egyptian script used for everyday documents, derived from hieratic.
*   **Rosetta Stone:** A granodiorite stele inscribed with a decree in three scripts (hieroglyphic, demotic, and Greek), key to deciphering hieroglyphs.
*   **Capillary action:** The ability of a liquid to flow in narrow spaces without the assistance of, or even in opposition to, external forces like gravity.
*   **Indo-European languages:** A language family spoken by more than half the world's population, a term coined by Young in 1813.

## Debates and open questions
The primary historical debate concerns the priority of deciphering Egyptian hieroglyphs between Thomas Young and Jean-François Champollion. While Champollion produced the first comprehensive system, Young's work on the demotic script and his identification of phonetic hieroglyphs were essential precursors. The dispute was fueled by nationalistic rivalry between Britain and France. Modern scholarship generally acknowledges Young's crucial foundational role while crediting Champollion with the final breakthrough.

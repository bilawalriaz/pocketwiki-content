# Berlin Wall

## Overview
The Berlin Wall was a guarded concrete barrier that encircled West Berlin from 1961 to 1989, physically separating it from East Berlin and the German Democratic Republic (GDR). Its construction was a direct response to a massive "brain drain" of East Germans fleeing to the West, and it became the most prominent symbol of the Cold War's Iron Curtain. Its fall in 1989 paved the way for German reunification.

## Timeline
- **13 August 1961** — East German troops begin closing the border and erecting barbed wire, marking the start of construction.
- **15 June 1961** — GDR leader Walter Ulbricht publicly states, "No one has the intention of erecting a wall!"
- **24 August 1961** — Günter Litfin becomes the first person shot and killed trying to cross the Wall.
- **17 August 1962** — Peter Fechter, an 18-year-old, is shot and bleeds to death in the death strip in full view of Western media.
- **26 June 1963** — U.S. President John F. Kennedy delivers his "Ich bin ein Berliner" speech in West Berlin.
- **12 June 1987** — U.S. President Ronald Reagan challenges Soviet leader Mikhail Gorbachev to "tear down this Wall!"
- **9 November 1989** — Following a miscommunicated press conference, East Germans flood the border crossings; guards open the gates.
- **22 December 1989** — The Brandenburg Gate section of the Wall reopens.
- **3 October 1990** — German reunification is formally completed.

## Body

### Background and Cause
After World War II, Germany was divided into occupation zones, leading to the creation of the capitalist Federal Republic of Germany (West Germany) in 1949 and the communist German Democratic Republic (East Germany) in 1949. The Berlin Blockade (1948-49) and the subsequent airlift highlighted early tensions. A major crisis for the GDR was "Republikflucht" (flight from the Republic): by 1961, 3.5 million East Germans (20% of the population) had fled west, primarily through Berlin. This exodus, disproportionately of young, educated professionals, created a severe "brain drain" that threatened the GDR's economic viability and political credibility.

### Construction and Purpose
Despite public denials, the GDR, with Soviet approval, moved to seal the border. On 13 August 1961, East German troops began erecting barbed wire and fences along the 156 km border around West Berlin. The barrier was built on East German territory and evolved into a complex system of walls, watchtowers, and a "death strip" with anti-vehicle defenses. Its primary purpose was to stop the emigration of its citizens. The GDR officially called it the "Anti-Fascist Protection Rampart," while West Berliners termed it the "Wall of Shame."

### Life and Escape Under the Wall
The Wall split families and made West Berlin an isolated exclave. While it successfully halted the mass exodus, it became a global symbol of communist oppression, especially after border guards shot and killed would-be escapeers. Over the Wall's existence, an estimated 5,000 people successfully defected using methods like tunnels, hot air balloons, and zip lines. At least 140 people died in escape attempts. The U.S. and its allies maintained a military presence in West Berlin and used formal crossings like Checkpoint Charlie, while travel for ordinary East Germans was severely restricted.

### Fall and Reunification
The Wall's fall was triggered by a chain of events: reforms in the Soviet Union, the opening of Hungary's border with Austria in 1989, and mass protests within East Germany. On 9 November 1989, after a confused press conference by official Günter Schabowski suggested new travel rules were effective "immediately," thousands of East Berliners gathered at the crossings. Overwhelmed guards eventually opened the gates. The event led to the collapse of the GDR and formal German reunification on 3 October 1990.

## Terms
- **Iron Curtain**: The political, military, and ideological barrier erected by the Soviet Union after WWII to seal off itself and its dependent eastern and central European allies from open contact with the West.
- **Republikflucht**: The German term for the flight of citizens from East Germany to the West before the Wall's construction.
- **Brain Drain**: The emigration of highly trained or intelligent people from a particular country, a major problem for the GDR.
- **Death Strip**: The area between the inner and outer walls of the Berlin Wall, designed with obstacles and clear lines of fire to stop escapees.
- **Checkpoint Charlie**: The most famous crossing point between East and West Berlin, used by Allied personnel and foreigners.
- **Grenzmauer 75**: The fourth and final generation of the Berlin Wall, constructed from 1975, consisting of reinforced concrete sections.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Death Toll**: The exact number of people killed at the Berlin Wall is disputed, with estimates ranging from at least 140 to over 200.
- **Responsibility for Construction**: While the initiative is often attributed to the GDR, historical records suggest Soviet leader Nikita Khrushchev played a key role, though he was initially wary.
- **Cultural Legacy**: Polls taken decades after reunification show a persistent minority in both former East and West Germany who viewed the Wall's existence favorably or supported its rebuilding, indicating a lasting "wall in the head" (*Mauer im Kopf*).

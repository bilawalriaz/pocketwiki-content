# Jonah Lomu

## Overview
Jonah Lomu (1975–2015) was a New Zealand rugby union player widely regarded as the sport's first global superstar. His unprecedented combination of size, speed, and power revolutionized the wing position and had a massive impact on rugby's popularity as it turned professional in the mid-1990s.

- **1994** — Becomes the youngest ever All Black (19 years, 45 days) on debut.

- **Dec 1995** — Diagnosed with nephrotic syndrome, a serious kidney disorder.
- **1996** — Wins the inaugural Super 12 Final with the Auckland Blues; wins first Tri-Nations.
- **1998** — Wins a gold medal in Sevens Rugby at the Commonwealth Games.

- **2000** — Scores the winning try in the "match of the century" against Australia.

- **2005** — Returns to professional rugby, playing for Cardiff Blues and North Harbour.
- **2007** — Retires from professional rugby; inducted into the International Rugby Hall of Fame.
- **18 November 2015** — Dies in Auckland from a heart attack linked to his kidney disease.

## Body

### Early Life and Career Beginnings
Born in Pukekohe to Tongan parents, Lomu grew up between Tonga and South Auckland. He attended Wesley College, where he excelled in athletics. He began his rugby career as a forward (flanker) before switching to the wing, a move he called "the best move he could have made." He debuted for New Zealand's under-19 team in 1993 and gained early fame at the 1994 Hong Kong Sevens. In 1994, he became the youngest All Black test player, debuting against France.

### 1995 World Cup and Global Stardom
Lomu's performance at the 1995 Rugby World Cup made him a global icon. Despite having only two test caps, he scored seven tries in five matches. His four-try demolition of England in the semi-final, including a famous try where he ran over defender Mike Catt, cemented his reputation. His display coincided with rugby union turning fully professional, and he became the game's biggest drawcard. He was named the BBC Overseas Sports Personality of the Year in 1995.

### International Career and Health Struggles
Lomu continued to be a prolific try-scorer for the All Blacks through the late 1990s and early 2000s, scoring key tries in Tri-Nations matches. His career was significantly impacted by his 1995 diagnosis of nephrotic syndrome, a genetic kidney disorder. He missed the 1997 Tri-Nations due to the illness. His international career effectively ended in 2002 as his kidney function deteriorated, requiring dialysis. He finished with 63 caps and 37 tries for New Zealand.

### Comeback, Retirement, and Legacy
After a kidney transplant in 2004, Lomu made a professional comeback in 2005, playing for Cardiff Blues in Wales and North Harbour in New Zealand. He failed to regain an All Blacks contract and retired in 2007. He was inducted into the IRB Hall of Fame in 2011. Lomu died unexpectedly in 2015 from a heart attack linked to his kidney disease. He is remembered as a transformative figure who changed the physical profile of rugby wingers and was a cultural icon for the Pasifika community.

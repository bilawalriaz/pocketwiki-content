# Tajikistan

## Overview
Tajikistan is a landlocked, mountainous country in Central Asia, home to over 11 million people. It is the only Central Asian state with a Persian-speaking (Iranic) majority, distinguishing it from its Turkic neighbors. Its modern history is defined by its time as a Soviet republic, a devastating post-independence civil war, and its current status as an authoritarian state with an economy heavily reliant on remittances and natural resources.

## Body

### History and Independence
The territory has been home to ancient cultures and was ruled by a succession of empires, including the Achaemenids, Kushans, and Samanids, who revived Persian culture. It was conquered by the Mongols and later became part of the Khanate of Bukhara before being annexed by the Russian Empire in the 19th century. After the Russian Revolution, the Tajik ASSR was created within Uzbekistan in 1924 and became a separate Soviet republic in 1929. Soviet rule involved secularization, collectivization, and the deliberate separation of the Tajik language from broader Persian. Tajikistan declared independence in 1991, which was immediately followed by a civil war (1992-1997) fought along regional and political lines. The war ended with a UN-brokered peace deal, and President Emomali Rahmon, who came to power during the conflict, has maintained an authoritarian rule since.

### Government and Politics
Tajikistan is a presidential republic with four provinces. The government is characterized as an authoritarian regime led by President Emomali Rahmon since 1994. Elections are regularly criticized by international observers (e.g., OSCE) as unfair and lacking democratic standards. The state is constitutionally secular but regulates Islam, the religion of 97.5% of the population. The government has conducted campaigns against perceived Islamic extremism, including restrictions on facial hair and hijabs. Freedom of the press is restricted, and public criticism of the regime is not tolerated.

### Economy and Infrastructure
Tajikistan has a developing, transitional economy that is highly dependent on remittances from migrant workers abroad (primarily in Russia) and the production of aluminium and cotton. Poverty has declined significantly since 2010, but the economy remains vulnerable to external shocks. The government is pursuing major infrastructure projects, particularly in hydropower (e.g., the Rogun Dam) and transportation. As a landlocked country, it is investing in roads, railways, and airports to improve regional connectivity, with significant investment from China under the Belt and Road Initiative. Drug trafficking from Afghanistan remains a major illegal economic activity and source of corruption.

### Demographics and Culture
Tajiks are the ethnic majority, with minorities of Uzbeks and Russians. The state language is Tajik (a variety of Persian), and Russian is the inter-ethnic language. The culture blends Persian and Central Asian traditions. Key cultural elements include the ancient Nowruz festival, traditional music (Shashmaqom, Falak), and distinctive embroidery (Chakan, Suzani). The national sport is gushtigiri (wrestling), and football is also popular. Tajikistan has achieved some success in Olympic sports, particularly in judo and boxing.

## Terms
- **Samanid Empire (819–999):** A Persianate Muslim dynasty that ruled Central Asia, reviving Persian culture and Islam after the Arab conquests.
- **Basmachi:** A term for the resistance movement by Muslim guerrillas against Bolshevik rule in Central Asia in the 1920s and early 1930s.
- **Tajik ASSR:** The Tajik Autonomous Soviet Socialist Republic, created in 1924 within Uzbekistan before becoming a full union republic in 1929.
- **United Tajik Opposition:** The coalition of liberal, democratic, and Islamist groups that fought the government in the 1992–1997 civil war.
- **Remittance-dependent economy:** An economy where a significant portion of GDP comes from money sent home by citizens working abroad.
- **Rogun Dam:** A major hydroelectric dam under construction on the Vakhsh River, intended to be the world's tallest upon completion.
- **Shashmaqom:** A classical Persianate musical tradition of Tajikistan and Uzbekistan, inscribed on the UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage list.
- **Chakan embroidery:** A traditional Tajik needlework art form, also recognized by UNESCO as intangible cultural heritage.

## Debates and open questions
- The **unemployment rate** is a subject of debate, with a large discrepancy between official registered figures and higher international estimates, reflecting a vast informal economy.
- The **political stability** of the country is often characterized as "profoundly averse to risk" due to the trauma of the civil war, which may hinder genuine political reform.
- The **long-term economic vulnerability** of Tajikistan is a key concern, as its growth is heavily tied to remittance flows from Russia and global commodity prices, with diversification remaining a major challenge.

# Yokohama

## Overview
Yokohama is Japan's second-largest city by population and its most populous municipality, serving as the capital of Kanagawa Prefecture. It transformed from a small fishing village into a major cosmopolitan port city after Japan ended its policy of national seclusion in the mid-19th century. Today, it is a key economic, cultural, and commercial hub within the Greater Tokyo Area.

## Body

### History and Development
Before Western contact, Yokohama was a small fishing village. The 1853–54 arrival of Commodore Perry forced Japan to open ports. Although Kanagawa-juku was initially chosen, the Tokugawa shogunate built port facilities in Yokohama instead, officially opening it in 1859. The city rapidly became a base for foreign trade, with foreigners settling in the Kannai district and later the Yamate district (The Bluff). Yokohama was the site of many Japanese "firsts" during the Meiji era, including the first foreign trading port, Chinatown (1859), English-language newspaper (1861), and railway station (1872). After the Meiji Restoration of 1868, the port developed for silk trade, and Western technological transfer continued. The city was officially incorporated in 1889.

The early 20th century saw rapid industrial growth, with factories built on reclaimed land north toward Kawasaki, forming the Keihin Industrial Area. This growth brought both affluence and the formation of large slums. Yokohama was devastated by the Great Kantō earthquake on September 1, 1923, which killed over 30,000 people in the city. It was rebuilt, only to be largely destroyed again by U.S. air raids during World War II, most notably the Great Yokohama Air Raid on May 29, 1945. Post-war, Yokohama served as a major U.S. transshipment base. The city was designated by government ordinance in 1956. Major modern developments include the opening of the first subway line in 1972 and the start of the Minato Mirai 21 project in 1983, which featured landmarks like the Cosmo Clock 21 Ferris wheel and the Landmark Tower.

### Geography and Climate
Yokohama has a total area of 437.38 km² and is located on Tokyo Bay in the Kantō region. Its growth has been limited by surrounding hills, leading to significant land reclamation from the sea. The city is highly prone to natural phenomena like earthquakes and tropical cyclones due to its location on the Pacific Ring of Fire. It has a humid subtropical climate with hot, humid summers and chilly winters. The coldest recorded temperature was −8.2 °C in 1927, and the hottest was 38.1 °C in 2025.

### Demographics and Administration
As of June 2024, Yokohama's population is 3,772,726, making it Japan's second-most populated city. It is a designated city with 18 wards, with its government seat in Naka Ward. A significant portion of the population commutes to work outside the city, primarily to Tokyo. The foreign national population is 121,042 (3.2%), with the largest groups from China, South Korea, and Vietnam.

### Economy and Transport
Yokohama's 2020 gross regional product was 14.06 trillion yen. Major industries include wholesale/retail, healthcare, and professional services. The city is home to headquarters of companies like Isuzu and Nissan. It is a major port, ranked 31st globally by cargo volume. Transport is served by the Tōkaidō Shinkansen, multiple railway lines, and subway systems. It has no airport but is served by Tokyo's Haneda and Narita airports.

## Terms
- **Seclusion (Sakoku):** Japan's national policy of isolation from foreign trade and influence, ended in the mid-19th century.
- **Treaty Port:** A port opened to foreign trade by treaty, as Yokohama was in 1859.
- **Extraterritoriality:** The state of being exempted from the jurisdiction of local law, enjoyed by foreign residents in Yokohama's early settlement.
- **Keihin Industrial Zone:** A major industrial area encompassing parts of Tokyo, Yokohama, and Kawasaki.
- **Designated City:** A Japanese city status granting it prefectural-level administrative powers.
- **Minato Mirai 21:** A major urban development project on reclaimed land in Yokohama, started in 1983.
- **Great Kantō Earthquake:** A catastrophic earthquake on September 1, 1923, that devastated the Kantō region, including Yokohama.
- **Humid Subtropical Climate (Köppen: Cfa):** A climate type characterized by hot, humid summers and cool winters.

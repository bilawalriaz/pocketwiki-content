# Yi Sun-sin

## Overview
Yi Sun-sin (1545–1598) was a Korean admiral and military general who is celebrated for his undefeated naval record against the Japanese during the Imjin War (1592–1598). His strategic genius, innovations like the turtle ship, and unwavering integrity made him a pivotal figure in defending Joseon Korea and are central to his legacy as one of history's greatest naval commanders.

## Timeline
- **April 28, 1545** — Born in Hanseong (present-day Seoul).
- **1576** — Passed the military examination.
- **1583** — Defeated Jurchen marauders and captured their chief, Mu Pai Nai.
- **March 13, 1591** — Assumed command of the Jeolla Province Left Naval District.
- **June 14, 1592** — Won the Battle of Okpo, his first major naval victory.
- **August 13, 1592** — Achieved a decisive victory at the Battle of Hansan Island.
- **1593** — Appointed Naval Commander of the Three Provinces.
- **1597** — Dismissed, arrested, and tortured; later demoted to common soldier.
- **August 28, 1597** — The Joseon fleet is decimated at the Battle of Chilchonryang under Admiral Wŏn Kyun.
- **October 1597** — Won the Battle of Myeongnyang with only 13 ships against over 133 Japanese vessels.
- **December 16, 1598** — Killed in action at the Battle of Noryang, the final major battle of the war.

## Body

### Early Life and Military Career
Yi Sun-sin was born into a yangban (aristocratic) family, but his grandfather and father had failed to establish themselves in government. He relied on his neighbor, the scholar-official Yu Sŏngnyong, for career advancement. After passing the military examination in 1576, he served on the northern frontier against Jurchen marauders, where his strategic skills quickly earned him recognition. However, jealousy from superiors led to false accusations, imprisonment, and torture. He was eventually rehabilitated and, in 1591, was appointed Commander of the Jeolla Province Left Naval District, where he began a crucial naval buildup.

### The Imjin War and Naval Victories
When Japan invaded Korea in 1592, Yi, despite having no prior naval command experience, led the Joseon navy to a string of rapid victories. In the first campaign, he won the Battle of Okpo without losing a ship. His forces used innovative tactics, such as the "crane wing" formation, and deployed the newly built turtle ship (*geobukseon*)—a warship with an iron-spiked, covered deck to prevent boarding and a dragon figurehead that emitted smoke. His most famous victory was the Battle of Hansan Island in 1592, which crippled Japanese naval operations. In 1593, he was promoted to Naval Commander of the Three Provinces.

### Dismissal, Defeat, and Reinstatement
In 1597, court enemies manipulated King Seonjo into believing false intelligence. When Yi refused to follow a dangerous order based on this, he was arrested, tortured, and demoted to a common soldier. His successor, Admiral Wŏn Kyun, led the Joseon fleet to catastrophic defeat at the Battle of Chilchonryang, losing almost all ships. Yi was hastily reinstated to command the 13 surviving warships. In the subsequent Battle of Myeongnyang, he masterfully used the treacherous currents of the Myeongnyang Strait to defeat a Japanese fleet of over 300 ships, a victory considered one of history's greatest naval upsets.

### Final Battle and Death
Yi died from a gunshot wound during the Battle of Noryang on December 16, 1598, the last major engagement of the war. As he was struck, he reportedly ordered his men not to announce his death and to continue the fight. His body was returned to his hometown of Asan for burial.

### Legacy and Evaluation
Posthumously honored with the title Chungmugong (Duke of Loyalty and Warfare), Yi is revered in Korea as its greatest national hero. His wartime journals, the *Nanjung Ilgi*, are a UNESCO Memory of the World document. Military historians often compare him to Horatio Nelson, and he was highly esteemed by Japanese admirals like Tōgō Heihachirō. His legacy is celebrated in South Korea through statues, awards, and cultural depictions, while North Korea also honors him as a patriot, though with less emphasis on his aristocratic background.

## Terms
*   **Imjin War (1592–1598):** The Japanese invasions of Korea, also known as the Japanese invasions of Korea (1592–1598).
*   **Joseon:** The Korean dynasty that ruled from 1392 to 1897.
*   **Yangban:** The aristocratic ruling class or gentry of dynastic Korea.
*   **Panokseon:** A traditional Korean warship with a multi-decked structure.
*   **Geobukseon (Turtle Ship):** An armored warship designed by Yi Sun-sin, featuring a covered, spiked deck and a dragon figurehead.
*   **Nanjung Ilgi:** Yi Sun-sin's personal war diary, a UNESCO-listed historical document.
*   **Chungmugong:** Yi Sun-sin's posthumous title, meaning "Duke of Loyalty and Warfare."
*   **Samdo Sugun Tongjesa:** The title "Naval Commander of the Three Provinces," held by Yi.

## Debates and Open Questions
The exact number of naval engagements Yi Sun-sin fought is a subject of historical debate, though it is generally accepted he fought in at least 23 battles, winning all of them. The precise design and number of turtle ships used in specific battles are also topics of scholarly discussion.

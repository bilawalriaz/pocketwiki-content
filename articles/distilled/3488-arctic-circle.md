# Arctic Circle

## Overview
The Arctic Circle is the northernmost of Earth's five major circles of latitude, located at approximately 66°34′ N. It marks the southernmost latitude where the Sun can remain continuously above or below the horizon for 24 hours, creating the phenomena of midnight sun and polar night. This line defines the southern boundary of the Arctic region, which is divided among eight nations and is a zone of significant geopolitical and environmental interest.

## Body

### Astronomical Definition and Phenomena
The Arctic Circle is the southernmost latitude in the Northern Hemisphere where the Sun's center can stay continuously above or below the horizon for 24 hours. At the December solstice, locations north of the Circle experience polar night (no sunrise), while at the June solstice, they experience midnight sun (no sunset). The duration of these events increases with proximity to the North Pole. Due to atmospheric refraction and the Sun's disk shape, the midnight sun is visible about 90 km south of the Circle on the summer solstice, and the Sun can be seen about 90 km north of the Circle on the winter solstice.

### Location and Drift
The Circle's exact position depends on Earth's axial tilt, which varies by about 2° over a 41,000-year cycle due to lunar tidal forces. Currently, the Arctic Circle is drifting toward the North Pole, and the Antarctic Circle toward the South Pole, at a rate of approximately 14.5 meters per year. The Circle is roughly 16,000 km in circumference, and the area north of it covers about 20 million km², or 4% of Earth's surface.

### Human Geography and Jurisdiction
The largest communities north of the Arctic Circle are in Russia (Murmansk, Norilsk), Norway (Tromsø), and Sweden (Kiruna). The region's land and non-international waters are under the jurisdiction of eight states. Under international law like UNCLOS, the North Pole and surrounding high seas are not owned by any country, but the "Arctic Five" (Canada, Denmark, Norway, Russia, USA) have specific rights over their continental shelves and exclusive economic zones (EEZs).

### Climate
The climate is generally cold, but coastal areas of Norway are milder due to the Gulf Stream, keeping ports ice-free. Interior regions experience extreme temperature swings, with summer highs in Norilsk, Russia, reaching 30°C and winter lows falling below -50°C.

## Terms
- **Arctic Circle**: The parallel of latitude at approximately 66°34′ N, marking the southern limit of the area where the Sun does not set on the summer solstice or rise on the winter solstice.
- **Polar Night**: The period when the Sun does not rise above the horizon for 24 hours or more, occurring north of the Arctic Circle in winter.
- **Midnight Sun**: The phenomenon of the Sun remaining visible at local midnight, occurring north of the Arctic Circle in summer.
- **Axial Tilt (Obliquity)**: The angle between Earth's rotational axis and its orbital plane, which determines the position of the polar circles.
- **Exclusive Economic Zone (EEZ)**: A maritime zone under UNCLOS where a state has special rights regarding exploration and use of marine resources.
- **Gulf Stream**: A warm Atlantic ocean current that moderates the climate of coastal Northern Europe.
- **Atmospheric Refraction**: The bending of light as it passes through Earth's atmosphere, which affects the apparent position of the Sun near the horizon.

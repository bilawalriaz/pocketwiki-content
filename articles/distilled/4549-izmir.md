# İzmir

## Overview
İzmir (historically Smyrna) is Turkey's third-largest city and a major Aegean port with over 3,000 years of recorded urban history. Its strategic location has made it a perennial center of trade, cultural exchange, and conflict, shaping its identity as a cosmopolitan Mediterranean metropolis.

## Timeline
- **c. 7th–4th millennium BC** — Neolithic and Chalcolithic settlements at Yeşilova Höyük.
- **c. 3rd millennium BC** — Bayraklı settlement ("Old Smyrna") established.
- **c. 610–600 BC** — Lydians conquer and burn Old Smyrna.
- **545 BC** — Persians destroy Old Smyrna.
- **c. 340 BC** — Alexander the Great refounds Smyrna at Mount Pagos (Kadifekale).
- **133 BC** — Smyrna bequeathed to the Roman Republic.
- **1076** — Seljuk Turks under Çaka Bey capture Smyrna.
- **1415** — Ottomans capture Smyrna for the second time.
- **15 May 1919** — Greek Army lands in Smyrna.
- **9 September 1922** — Turkish Army retakes Smyrna, ending the Greco-Turkish War.
- **13–22 September 1922** — Great Fire of Smyrna destroys Greek and Armenian quarters.
- **1923** — Population exchange between Greece and Turkey.

## Body

### Names and Etymology
The modern Turkish name *İzmir* derives from the Greek *Smyrna* (Smýrni/Smýrnē). The name's origin is debated, possibly linked to an Amazon named Smyrna or the myrrh-producing shrub. In English, "Smyrna" was used until the 1930s, when Turkey promoted the Turkish name internationally.

### Ancient and Classical History
Archaeological evidence shows continuous settlement from the Neolithic period. "Old Smyrna" at Bayraklı flourished as an Ionian city-state from c. 1000 BC, peaking between 650 and 545 BC. It was destroyed by the Persians in 545 BC. Alexander the Great refounded the city on the slopes of Mount Pagos around 340 BC. Under Roman rule after 133 BC, Smyrna prospered as a major city and was one of the Seven Churches of Asia. It was devastated by an earthquake in 178 AD but rebuilt with imperial aid.

### Medieval and Ottoman Period
Smyrna was first captured by Turkic forces in 1076. After periods of Crusader and Genoese control, it was taken by the Beylik of Aydın in the 14th century. The Ottomans gained final control in 1426. The city grew into a major international port from the late 16th century, attracting European merchants and developing a large, diverse population including Greeks, Armenians, Jews, and Levantines. By the 19th century, it was a cosmopolitan hub with railways and a significant non-Muslim majority.

### Contemporary Period and the Great Fire
Following World War I, Greek forces occupied Smyrna in 1919. The Turkish Army recaptured the city on 9 September 1922. A catastrophic fire from 13–22 September 1922 destroyed the Greek and Armenian quarters, causing massive casualties and displacement. The 1923 population exchange between Greece and Turkey resulted in the expulsion of the remaining Greek population. The city was rebuilt as part of the new Turkish Republic.

### Demographics and Climate
As of 2024, İzmir Province has a population of about 4.5 million. The city has a hot-summer Mediterranean climate with hot, dry summers and cool, rainy winters. It is home to Turkey's second-largest Jewish community and a notable Afro-Turk population.

### Economy and Transport
İzmir is a leading Turkish port for exports and has a diversified economy in industry, trade, and services. It is served by Adnan Menderes International Airport and an integrated public transport network including metro, commuter rail (İZBAN), trams, buses, and ferries.

## Terms
*   **Smyrna:** The historical Greek name for İzmir, used internationally until the 1930s.
*   **Old Smyrna:** The Archaic Period city located at the Bayraklı hill, distinct from the later city founded by Alexander the Great.
*   **Ionian League:** A confederation of Greek city-states on the western coast of Anatolia, of which Smyrna was a member.
*   **Vilayet:** An administrative division of the Ottoman Empire, equivalent to a province.
*   **Capitulations:** Privileged trading conditions granted to foreign nationals in the Ottoman Empire.
*   **Population Exchange (1923):** A compulsory exchange of populations between Greece and Turkey, formalized by the Treaty of Lausanne.
*   **İZBAN:** The commuter rail system serving the İzmir metropolitan area.
*   **Kemeraltı:** The historic bazaar zone of İzmir, established by the Ottomans.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   The precise origin of the city's name is uncertain, with competing theories linking it to an Amazon, the myrrh plant, or pre-Greek roots.
*   The extent of Mycenaean influence at Old Smyrna during the late Bronze Age requires further archaeological investigation.
*   The death toll and responsibility for the 1922 Great Fire of Smyrna remain subjects of historical dispute between Greek and Turkish narratives.

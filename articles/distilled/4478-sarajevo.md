# Sarajevo

## Overview
Sarajevo is the capital and largest city of Bosnia and Herzegovina, situated in the heart of the Balkans. Known as the "Jerusalem of Europe" for its long history of religious and cultural diversity, it is a major political, economic, and cultural center. The city has gained international renown through pivotal historical events, from the assassination that sparked World War I to hosting the 1984 Winter Olympics and enduring the longest siege of a capital city in modern history.

## Body

### Etymology and Early History
The name Sarajevo derives from the Turkish *saray* ("palace"), with the suffix "-evo" likely of Slavic origin. The city was founded in the 1460s by the Ottoman governor Isa-Beg Ishaković, who built a palace, mosque, and marketplace, transforming a village cluster into a major urban center. It grew rapidly, becoming the largest Ottoman city in the Balkans after Istanbul by the 16th century. The city's multicultural fabric was established early, with Sephardic Jews arriving after expulsion from Spain in the late 15th century.

### Austro-Hungarian and Yugoslav Periods
Austria-Hungary occupied Bosnia in 1878, leading to rapid industrialization and modernization, including the introduction of trams and Western architecture. This era ended with the 1914 assassination of Archduke Franz Ferdinand, which precipitated World War I. After the war, Sarajevo became part of the Kingdom of Yugoslavia. Following World War II, it became the capital of the Socialist Republic of Bosnia and Herzegovina within Yugoslavia, experiencing massive growth and industrial development. This period culminated in the prosperous 1984 Winter Olympics.

### The Bosnian War and Siege
Following Bosnia's independence declaration in 1992, Bosnian Serb forces besieged Sarajevo for 1,425 days. The siege, the longest of a capital in modern warfare, resulted in 11,541 deaths and 56,000 injuries. The city was heavily damaged by constant bombardment. The Dayton Agreement in 1995 ended the war but partitioned the metropolitan area, leading to a mass exodus of Serbs and creating a more ethnically homogeneous city.

### Modern Sarajevo
Post-war reconstruction has transformed Sarajevo into the fastest-growing city in Bosnia and Herzegovina. It has regained international recognition, being named a UNESCO Creative City of Film in 2019 and a top travel destination by National Geographic in 2024. The economy is driven by manufacturing, administration, and a booming tourism sector that leverages its historical sites and 1984 Olympic legacy.

## Terms
- **Butmir culture:** A Neolithic archaeological culture discovered in the Sarajevo suburb of Ilidža, known for its unique pottery.
- **Daesitiates:** An ancient Illyrian tribe in the Sarajevo region, the last in Bosnia to resist Roman occupation.
- **Vrhbosna:** A medieval Bosnian province in the area of modern Sarajevo; its exact location is debated.
- **Baščaršija:** The old bazaar and historical commercial center of Sarajevo, established during the Ottoman era.
- **Sarajevo Roses:** Concrete scars from mortar explosions during the siege, filled with red resin as memorials.
- **Dayton Agreement:** The 1995 peace accord that ended the Bosnian War and established the current political structure of Bosnia and Herzegovina.
- **Sevdalinka:** A traditional genre of Bosnian folk music, for which Sarajevo was a major center.
- **New Primitives:** A post-punk urban subculture that emerged in Sarajevo in the early 1980s.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The exact location and importance of the medieval settlement of Vrhbosna in relation to modern Sarajevo is debated by scholars.
- The origin of the "-evo" suffix in the city's name is disputed, with theories pointing to either Slavic or Turkish linguistic roots.
- The impact of administrative pressures on ethnic self-identification in census data is analyzed, suggesting a potential discrepancy between official figures and personal identification.

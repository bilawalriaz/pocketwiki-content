# Dresden

## Overview
Dresden is the capital of the German state of Saxony and a major cultural and economic center. Historically the residence of Saxon electors and kings, it was renowned for its Baroque architecture and art collections. The city was largely destroyed in the 1945 bombing during World War II and was subsequently rebuilt, first under East German rule and then after German reunification in 1990. Today, it is a hub for high-tech industries, education, and tourism.

## Timeline
- **1206** — First documentary evidence of Dresden.
- **1485** — Treaty of Leipzig divides Wettin lands; Dresden becomes capital of the Duchy of Saxony.
- **1547** — Duke Moritz becomes Elector; Dresden becomes capital of the Electorate of Saxony.
- **1697** — Elector Frederick Augustus I becomes King Augustus II the Strong of Poland, initiating a cultural golden age.
- **1756–1763** — Dresden suffers heavy destruction during the Seven Years' War.
- **1806** — Dresden becomes capital of the Kingdom of Saxony under Napoleon.
- **1849** — May Uprising in Dresden is suppressed by Prussian forces.
- **1945 (Feb 13–15)** — Allied bombing destroys the city center, killing approximately 22,500–25,000 people.
- **1990** — German reunification; Dresden becomes capital of the re-established Free State of Saxony.
- **1994–2005** — Reconstruction of the Frauenkirche.
- **2004** — Dresden Elbe Valley declared a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
- **2009** — UNESCO revokes the World Heritage status due to the construction of the Waldschlösschen Bridge.

## Body

### History
Dresden's history begins with its first mention in 1206 as a German trading settlement. It became the capital of the Duchy of Saxony in 1485 and later the Electorate. Under Augustus II the Strong (reigned 1697–1733), the city flourished as a European center for art and technology, with the construction of landmarks like the Zwinger and Frauenkirche. It became the capital of the Kingdom of Saxony in 1806.

The city was a center of the 1848–49 revolutions. After German unification in 1871, it industrialized. During World War II, the February 1945 bombing by Allied forces killed tens of thousands and destroyed the historic center. Post-war, Dresden became part of East Germany (GDR), where reconstruction favored socialist modern architecture, though some landmarks were restored. After reunification in 1990, extensive reconstruction, including the Frauenkirche, revived the historic cityscape.

### Geography and Climate
Dresden lies on both banks of the Elbe River, mostly in the Dresden Basin, at an altitude of about 113 meters. It is the fourth-largest German city by area. The city is notably green, with 62% green areas and forests, including the Dresden Heath. It has an oceanic climate with continental influences; summers average 19.0 °C in July, and winters are slightly colder than the German average. Flood protection is critical due to its riverside location.

### Demographics and Governance
Dresden's population peaked at 649,252 in 1933, dropped to 368,519 in 1945, and was 557,075 as of 2019. The city is governed by a directly elected mayor and a city council. As the state capital, it houses the Saxon state parliament and ministries.

### Economy
Dresden's economy is dominated by high-tech sectors, often called "Silicon Saxony," including semiconductor manufacturing (e.g., GlobalFoundries, Infineon), pharmaceuticals (e.g., GlaxoSmithKline), and mechanical/electrical engineering. Tourism is also significant. The unemployment rate was 5.3% in December 2019.

### Culture and Education
Dresden is a major cultural center with the Dresden State Art Collections, the Semperoper (home to the Sächsische Staatskapelle Dresden, founded 1548), and the Dresdner Kreuzchor boys' choir. The Striezelmarkt is one of Germany's largest Christmas markets. Education is led by the Dresden University of Technology (TU Dresden), founded in 1828. The city hosts numerous research institutes, including three Max Planck Institutes and the largest Fraunhofer Society location worldwide.

## Terms
- **Electorate of Saxony:** A state of the Holy Roman Empire whose ruler (Elector) had the right to vote for the Emperor. Dresden was its capital from 1547.
- **Wettin dynasty:** The ruling family of Saxony and later Poland, who made Dresden their capital.
- **Baroque and Rococo:** Ornate artistic and architectural styles prominent in 18th-century Dresden.
- **Silicon Saxony:** The term for the high-tech semiconductor industry cluster centered in Dresden.
- **TU Dresden:** Dresden University of Technology, one of Germany's largest and most prestigious technical universities.
- **Frauenkirche:** A Lutheran church in Dresden, originally built in the 18th century, destroyed in 1945, and rebuilt 1994–2005.
- **Zwinger:** A Baroque palace complex in Dresden, built in the early 18th century.
- **Semperoper:** The opera house of the Saxon State Opera, originally built in the 19th century and rebuilt after WWII.
- **Dresdner Kreuzchor:** A historic boys' choir founded in the 13th century.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The February 1945 bombing of Dresden remains controversial. While the Allies described it as a legitimate military operation, researchers have argued the attacks were disproportionate and primarily killed civilians.
- The construction of the Waldschlösschen Bridge led to the revocation of the Dresden Elbe Valley's UNESCO World Heritage status in 2009, a decision contested by the city.
- The sale of the city's publicly subsidized housing organization to a US investment firm in 2006 sparked debate over the loss of public control over the housing market.

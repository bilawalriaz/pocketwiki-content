# Racism

## Overview
Racism is the belief that humans can be divided into distinct biological groups called "races" possessing inherent, inheritable behavioral traits and capacities, and that these groups can be ranked hierarchically. While often treated as a timeless phenomenon, scholars identify racism as a relatively modern ideology that crystallized during the European age of imperialism and the Atlantic slave trade to justify economic exploitation and political domination. It operates at individual, institutional, and structural levels, manifesting as overt supremacism, covert implicit biases, "color-blind" policies that ignore disparate impacts, and cultural assumptions of superiority. Scientific consensus rejects race as a valid biological taxonomy for humans—genetic variation is clinal and does not map onto discrete racial categories—yet the social construct of race produces material inequalities in health, wealth, and justice. International law, anchored by the UN Convention on the Elimination of All Forms of Racial Discrimination (1966), condemns racial differentiation as scientifically false and morally condemnable, though enforcement remains uneven.

## Timeline
- **1775** — Johann Blumenbach divides humanity into five racial groups by skin color, positing non-white "degeneration."
- **1853–55** — Arthur de Gobineau publishes *An Essay on the Inequality of the Human Races*, founding essentialist scientific racism.
- **1869** — Francis Galton coins "eugenics," applying statistics to "inheritance of intelligence."
- **1899** — Rudyard Kipling publishes "The White Man's Burden"; Joseph Conrad publishes *Heart of Darkness* criticizing colonial brutality.
- **1919** — Racial equality proposal supported by majority but not adopted at Paris Peace Conference (League of Nations Covenant).
- **1933** — Nazi Party seizes power in Germany; implements racial hierarchy (Aryan "master race") and anti-Jewish laws.
- **1948** — UN adopts Universal Declaration of Human Rights; South African National Party institutes apartheid.
- **1950** — UNESCO *The Race Question* statement condemns scientific racism; urges replacing "race" with "ethnic groups."
- **1966** — UN International Convention on the Elimination of All Forms of Racial Discrimination adopted.
- **1964/1965** — US Civil Rights Act and Voting Rights Act outlaw legal segregation; Immigration Act ends racial quotas.
- **1994** — Apartheid ends in South Africa.
- **2001** — EU Charter of Fundamental Rights explicitly bans racism.

## Body

### Etymology, Definition, and Usage
The revised Oxford English Dictionary cites the term "racism" in a quote from 1903, noting "racialism" was cited in 1902. The word came into widespread usage in the Western world in the 1930s, when it was used to describe the social and political ideology of Nazism. The root "race" entered English via Middle French; ultimate origin is debated (possibly Arabic *ra's* or Hebrew *rosh*, "head/origin"). Early 19th-century scientists generally accepted racial taxonomy and hierarchy, guiding "scientific racism"—a misnomer due to absent empirical support. Modern biology and genetics reject race as a meaningful genetic classification; human genome research shows greater variation within populations than between them. Sociologists define race as a **social construct**: while based on observable physical traits, the meanings and hierarchies attached to them are culturally produced. Garner (2009: p. 11) identifies three common elements across definitions of racism: first, a historical, hierarchical power relationship between groups; second, a set of ideas (an ideology) about racial differences; and, third, discriminatory actions (practices).

### Legal Frameworks
The UN does not define "racism" but defines "racial discrimination" (1965 UN International Convention on the Elimination of All Forms of Racial Discrimination) as any distinction based on race, colour, descent, or national/ethnic origin that impairs equal enjoyment of human rights. The 1948 Universal Declaration of Human Rights asserts rights "without distinction of any kind." UNESCO’s 1978 Declaration affirms all humans belong to a single species, born equal in dignity. British law defines "racial group" inclusively (race, colour, nationality, ethnic/national origin). Norway removed "race" from anti-discrimination law (banning discrimination based on ethnicity, national origin, descent, skin color) deeming the term unethical.

### Social and Behavioral Sciences
Sociology focuses heavily on "white racism" in majority-white societies, where aggregate advantages are termed **white privilege**. W. E. B. Du Bois (first African American Harvard PhD) declared "the problem of the twentieth century is the problem of the color line." Wellman (1993) defines racism as culturally sanctioned beliefs defending white advantages. Outcomes are measured via inequality in income, wealth, education, and health. **Racial identity** and **racial centrality** (salience of racial identity) mediate perception of discrimination and emotional distress (Sellers & Shelton, 2003). Since the civil rights era, racism has shifted from **overt** to **covert/aversive** forms. **Implicit bias** (subconscious associations) influences behavior without conscious awareness; Eberhardt (2004) showed "blackness" primes crime associations, affecting visual processing and decisions. Stereotypes (e.g., "Nigerian Prince" scammers) perpetuate subconscious racism.

### Humanities and Discourse
Discourse analysis (Van Dijk, 1992) examines how perpetrators and victims describe racism; descriptions implicating white elites are often marked with skepticism (quotation marks, distancing). Literature reflects power dynamics: the "white savior narrative" centers white heroes in Black stories (*Uncle Tom’s Cabin*, *To Kill a Mockingbird*). African American literature includes the "literature of white estrangement," challenging white supremacy by analyzing whiteness.

### Aspects of Racism
*   **Aversive Racism** (Kovel): Implicit avoidance of interracial interaction despite professed egalitarianism; affects hiring, legal decisions, helping behavior.
*   **Color Blindness**: Disregarding race in policy (e.g., opposing affirmative action). Critics (Bonilla-Silva) argue it naturalizes racial inequality by making whiteness the norm and minimizing racism.
*   **Cultural Racism**: Belief in inherent superiority of one culture’s products/traditions over others; overlaps with xenophobia and communalism (South Asia).
*   **Economic Racism**: Historical disparity perpetuated via education deficits and unconscious bias; examples include color film tuned for white skin, biased facial recognition.
*   **Institutional/Structural Racism** (Carmichael, late 1960s): Collective organizational failure to serve people due to colour/culture/origin; protects dominant culture interests via power structures, not just individual prejudice.
*   **Othering**: Constructing a group as fundamentally "not the norm" (exotic, underdeveloped) to define the self as standard; colonial Europe feminized/weakened the "Orient" to masculinize/strengthen the West.
*   **Racial Segregation**: Spatial/social separation in daily life (housing, schools, facilities); outlawed but persists via norms (Schelling’s models).
*   **Reverse Racism**: Concept describing discrimination against dominant groups. Many scholars reject it: minority groups lack systemic power for "prejudice plus power" racism (substantive equality view).
*   **Supremacism**: Ideology justifying colonialism, slavery, genocide. Examples: "White Man’s Burden," US Declaration of Independence ("merciless Indian savages"), Jefferson’s *Notes on Virginia* (Black inferiority), Baum’s call for Native annihilation. Non-white supremacisms (Black, Arab, East Asian) also exist.
*   **Symbolic/Modern Racism**: Outward unprejudiced behavior masking inward bias; resistance to structural change via ostensibly non-racial reasons; microaggressions.
*   **Subconscious Biases**: Implicit racial biases in self-identified non-racists; impact similar to racism but less pronounced, not deliberate.

### International Law
1919: Racial equality clause supported by majority but not adopted at Paris Peace Conference. 1943: Japan/ally Greater East Asia Conference declares abolition of racial discrimination. 1945: UN Charter promotes rights "without distinction as to race." 1950: UNESCO *The Race Question* (Montagu, Lévi-Strauss, Myrdal, Huxley) condemns scientific racism, urges "ethnic groups" over "race"; influences *Brown v. Board* (1954). 1950: European Convention on Human Rights adopted. 1966: UN Convention on Elimination of Racial Discrimination adopted, defining racial discrimination broadly. 2001: EU Charter of Fundamental Rights (Art. 21) bans discrimination on race, ethnic origin, religion, etc.

### Ideology: Scientific Racism and Nationalism
19th-century **scientific racism** classified humanity biologically. Blumenbach (1775): five races via skin color. **Polygenism** (Meiners): separate creation; "beautiful White" vs. "ugly Black" races. Retzius showed all humans are mixed; Steensby (1907) conjectured Australian Aboriginals as "purest." Post-WWII/Holocaust, racist ideologies discredited ethically/politically/scientifically, yet persist. Du Bois noted culture (shared history, laws, religion) matters more than "race." Late 19th-century nationalists fused "race," ethnicity, and "survival of the fittest" into ethnic nationalism. **Nordicism** equated "Germanic" with racial superiority. Culture became seen as physical manifestation of racial essence. Modern definition: racism *creates* races through social division ("racialisation"), independent of somatic differences.

### Ethnicity and Ethnic Conflicts
Racism often conflated with xenophobia/ethnocentrism. Ethno-national conflict usually centers on land/resources. Notions of race/racism intensify conflicts: when adversary is "inferior other," violence is more ruthless (Richter on Pontiac’s Rebellion: emergence of pan-"Indian" vs. pan-"White" identities). Davidson argues racism surfaced in 19th century to justify American slavery. Racism drove transatlantic slave trade, US segregation, South African apartheid, genocides (Armenian, Holocaust, Rwandan, Croatian Serbs), and colonial projects. Indigenous peoples remain targets.

### Ethnic and Racial Nationalism
Post-Napoleonic "nationalities question" reshaped Europe. French Revolution invented *levée en masse* (mass conscription) and patriotic sentiment. 1848 Revolutions, Italian/German unification followed. Ottoman/Austro-Hungarian collapse created Balkan nation-states with minorities. **Ethnic nationalism** (hereditary nation membership) arose from Romantic nationalism (Herder, Fichte, Hegel, Michelet) vs. liberal nationalism (Renan: "daily plebiscite"; Mill). In Germany, it blended with scientific racism: **Pan-Germanism** (Alldeutscher Verband, 1891) promoted German imperialism, "racial hygiene," anti-Jewish intermarriage bans. **Völkisch movement** combined Pan-Germanism with racial antisemitism; Thule Society founded DAP (1918), precursor to Nazi Party. **Master race** biology (Aryan/Nordic) justified ethnic cleansing for "racial purity." In France, Dreyfus Affair (late 19th c.) shifted nationalism rightward: Barrès founded ethnic nationalist discourse; Maurras (Action Française) theorized "anti-France" (Protestants, Jews, Freemasons, foreigners as "internal foreigners").

### History: Ethnocentrism to Modern Racism
**Aristotle**: Greeks free by nature, "barbarians" slaves by nature (soul, not body, determinative). Puzzo: Racism requires correlation of physical traits with moral qualities + division into superior/inferior stocks—absent pre-16th century; ancients practiced ethnocentrism, not racism.
**Early Antisemitism**: Debated if religious repression or ancient racism.
**Medieval Arab Writers** (Al-Muqaddasi, Al-Jahiz, Ibn Khaldun): Lewis argues ethnocentric prejudice developed via conquests, slave trade, Aristotelian slavery ideas (applied to Zanj/Turks), Judeo-Christian divisions. Anti-Black prejudice by 8th century; some authors urged respect. 14th century: Al-Abshibi, Ibn Khaldun dehumanized sub-Saharan Africans. Hannoum: French Orientalists racialized translations.
**Limpieza de Sangre** (Spain, post-Reconquista 1492): "Cleanliness of blood" doctrine blocked New Christian (converted Jew/Muslim) mobility; origin of "blue blood" (visible veins = no "dark-skinned enemy" contamination). Inquisition targeted secret practice. Legislation persisted in military contexts into 19th century; exported to Americas (caste system). Valladolid debate (1550–51): Sepúlveda (Indians as natural slaves) vs. Las Casas (Indians as free men). Spanish colonies developed complex racial caste system for social control; prejudice by perceived European ancestry persists.
**Racism as Modern Phenomenon** (Foucault): Early modern "discourse of race struggle" (Britain → France: Boulainvilliers). "Race" ≈ "nation/people." Boulainvilliers: French aristocrats = Frankish invaders; Third Estate = vanquished Gallo-Romans. Early modern racism *opposed* nation-state. Montlosier: Nordic aristocracy vs. plebeian Gauls.
**19th Century**: Racism intertwined with nationalism → ethnic nationalist discourse (pan-Germanism, pan-Slavism, etc.). Foucault: medieval "race struggle" discourse split: (1) racists/eugenicists → "state racism" (Nazism); (2) Marxists → "class struggle." Arendt: 19th-c. popular racism legitimized imperialism (Herero/Nama genocide 1904–08, Armenian genocide 1915–17). Kipling’s "White Man’s Burden" (1899) illustrates cultural superiority. Hamitic theory: North Africans (Ethiopians, Somalis, Berbers, Egyptians) as "Caucasoid," superior to sub-Saharan Africans; failures blamed on "interbreeding with Negroes." Meinhof: Bantu = Hamitic + Negro; Hottentots = Hamitic + Bushmen. US: American Colonization Society (1817) to remove Black Americans (Clay: "unconquerable prejudice"). Whitecapping (Indiana, late 19th c.) drove Black laborers off land. Racist election posters (1860s). Galton (1873): proposed Chinese settlement of Africa to "supplant the inferior Negro race."
**20th Century**: **Nazism** (1933–45): Germans = Aryan *Herrenvolk*. Hitler admired US race laws (citizenship, anti-miscegenation → Nuremberg Laws). "Mississippi must be the Volga." Racial hierarchy: Germans/Germanics top; Slavs (Poles, Serbs, Russians) = *Untermenschen*; Jews bottom (inhuman). Holocaust: ~6M Jews, 2.5M Poles, 0.5M Serbs, 0.2–0.5M Romani killed. *Generalplan Ost*: expel/enslave/exterminate Slavs for *Lebensraum*. 2.8M Soviet POWs killed (Jun 1941–Jan 1942). Ustaše (Croatia): Serb genocide, racial laws. **US**: White supremacy until Civil Rights Act (1964). 1790 Naturalization Act: whites only. *US v. Bhagat Singh Thind* (1923): high-caste Hindus not "white." Luce-Celler Act (1946): 100 Indians/year. 1965 Immigration Act opened demographics. **South Africa**: Apartheid (1948–94): legislative racial hierarchy, pass laws, segregated services. Sharpeville (1960), Soweto (1976). **Other**: Durban riots (1949), Burma expulsion of Indians (1962), Zanzibar Revolution (1964), Uganda Asian expulsion (1972).
**Contemporary**: Congo Civil War (1998–2003): Pygmies hunted/eaten as "subhuman." Botswana San/Bushmen treatment condemned by UN (2008). Zimbabwe (Mugabe) accused of anti-white racism (2008). China: anti-African student riots (Nanjing 1988–89); Lou Jing debate (2009). Mauritania expelled 70k Black Mauritanians (late 1980s). Sudan/Darfur: enslavement, sexual abuse, racial character. Niger deportation of Arabs (2006). Jakarta riots anti-Chinese (1998); PNG anti-Chinese riots (2009). Fiji anti-Indo-Fijian violence (2000). Malaysia: Bumiputera policies codify racial preference. Myanmar: Rohingya persecution driven by "racist hatred" (HRW). US: *de facto* segregation persists (loans, policing). Europe: Pew 2016 high anti-Romani views (Italy 82%, Greece 67%). Harvard survey: Czechia, Lithuania, Belarus, Ukraine highest anti-Black bias. Cambridge 2023: 88% Black Britons report workplace discrimination; 79% police stop-and-search bias; 80% education barrier.

### Scientific Racism
Modern biological race concept developed in 19th century. **Scientific racism** uses science to justify racist beliefs (early 18th c., peak mid-19th c. New Imperialism). Overcame Church resistance (monogenism). Combined with **unilineal social progress** (European superiority) and **social Darwinism** ("survival of fittest," Spencer 1864). Darwin (*Descent of Man*, 1871) opposed rigid racial differences: one species, common descent, mental similarities. Late 19th c.: fused with **eugenics** ("degeneration," "blood heredity"). Disciplines: physical anthropology, anthropometry, craniometry, phrenology, physiognomy (now discredited). **Neo-Darwinian synthesis** (1930s) → gene-centered view (1960s). **Human Genome Project**: no clear genetic basis for racial groups; no genes exclusive to one population.

### Heredity and Eugenics
Galton (1869): first eugenics theory, statistics on "inheritance of intelligence" → intelligence testing. Zola (*Les Rougon-Macquart*, 1871–): heredity determines behavior (high-born = politics/medicine; low-born = alcoholism/prostitution/homicide). 1930s: scientists debunked Nazi racial theories (minority view until mid-20th c.). 1950 UNESCO *The Race Question*: 1930s debunking project abandoned due to appeasement; resumed post-Holocaust. Boasian anthropology (US) shifted consensus. 1950 statement signed by renowned scholars denounced scientific racism.

### Polygenism and Racial Typologies
Gobineau (1853–55): *Essay on Inequality of Human Races*—essentialist race, biological legitimacy. Vacher de Lapouge (1899): *The Aryan and his Social Role*—hierarchical typology: *Homo europaeus* (dolichocephalic, Teutonic/Protestant) vs. *Homo alpinus* (brachycephalic, "Jew" archetype) vs. *Homo mediterraneus*. Fused race/class: upper class = *europaeus*, lower = *alpinus*. "Selectionism": annihilate trade unionists (degenerate), create fixed labor types. Ripley (1899): *Races of Europe* (same classification, influential in US). Chamberlain (British→German), Madison Grant (*Passing of the Great Race*, 1916): statistics for 1924 Immigration Act (restricted Jews, Slavs, Southern Europeans—blocked escape from Nazis).

### Human Zoos
"People Shows" linked popular and scientific racism. Joice Heth (P.T. Barnum, 1836), Saartjie Baartman ("Hottentot Venus," England). New Imperialism period → WWII. Carl Hagenbeck: exhibited "savages" beside animals. Ota Benga (Congolese pygmy, Bronx Zoo, 1906, Madison Grant): "missing link" to orangutans → racism tied to Darwinism/social Darwinism. 1931 Paris Colonial Exhibition (Kanaks). 1958 Brussels World Fair: "Congolese village."

### Theories on Origins of Racism
**Evolutionary Psychology** (Tooby, Cosmides): Race used as proxy for coalition membership (ancestral humans rarely met other races). Kurzban experiment: memory confusion protocol; subjects misattributed statements to same-race speakers (race as visual guide to "side"); effect vanished when clothing color signaled sides. **Cooperation Simulation** (Hammond, Axelrod): Ethnocentric strategies (favor own color)

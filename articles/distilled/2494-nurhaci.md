# Nurhaci

## Overview
Nurhaci (1559–1626) was the founding khan of the Jurchen-led Later Jin dynasty, which his son Hong Taiji renamed the Qing dynasty. He unified the Jurchen tribes, created the Eight Banners military system, and launched the conquest of Ming China's northeast, laying the groundwork for the eventual Qing takeover in 1644. His reign marks the transition from fragmented Jurchen clans to a centralized Manchu state.

## Timeline
- **1559** — Born into the Aisin Gioro clan of the Jianzhou Jurchens.
- **1582** — Grandfather and father killed in a Ming-led attack; begins revenge campaign.
- **1583–1588** — Subdues core Jianzhou Jurchen tribes and towns.
- **1593** — Defeats a nine-tribe coalition at the Battle of Gure.
- **1599** — Orders creation of the Manchu script from Mongolian; begins conquest of the Hulun tribes.
- **1616** — Declares himself Khan, founds the Later Jin dynasty.
- **1618** — Issues the Seven Grievances; attacks Ming, capturing Fushun.
- **1619** — Defeats a major Ming army at the Battle of Sarhū.
- **1621** — Conquers Shenyang and Liaoyang, making Shenyang the capital in 1625.
- **1626** — Defeated at the Battle of Ningyuan; dies of wounds.

## Body

**Early Life and Rise to Power**
Nurhaci grew up in the Ming general Li Chengliang's household, learning Chinese and reading military classics. After his father and grandfather were killed in 1582, he began expanding power from thirteen inherited suits of armor. He subdued rival Jurchens while maintaining a facade of loyalty to the Ming, receiving titles like "dragon-tiger general" in 1595.

**Unifying the Jurchens and Founding the State**
Nurhaci systematically conquered rival tribes, including the Hada (1603), Hoifa (1607), Ula (1613), and Yehe (1619). In 1599, he commissioned the Manchu script. In 1616, he proclaimed the Later Jin dynasty, appointing five chief councilors. He used the Eight Banners system to organize his military and society, with banners controlled by his sons and brothers.

**Invasion of the Ming Dynasty**
In 1618, Nurhaci issued the Seven Grievances and attacked Ming territory. He captured Fushun through a negotiated surrender, then defeated Ming relief forces. Key victories included the battles of Sarhū (1619), Kaiyuan, and the conquest of Shenyang and Liaoyang (1621). He treated Han subjects variably, initially offering concessions but later enacting discriminatory policies after a 1623 revolt. He died in 1626 after a defeat at Ningyuan by Ming general Yuan Chonghuan.

**Relationship with Joseon**
Nurhaci sought diplomatic engagement with Joseon, even offering troops during the Imjin War (1592), which were rejected. Border conflicts over ginseng gathering continued. After the Ming defeat at Sarhū (1619), Joseon General Gang Hong-rip surrendered to Nurhaci. Nurhaci demanded Joseon hand over Ming general Mao Wenlong, who harassed his forces from Ka Island, but was refused. He avoided invading Joseon to prevent a two-front war.

**Succession and Legacy**
Nurhaci died without a clear heir, encouraging rule by his senior sons (beiles). His consort Abahai was forced to commit suicide. Hong Taiji eventually secured the succession by exchanging banner control. Nurhaci's unification of the Jurchens and creation of the Eight Banners were his most lasting contributions, forming the military backbone of the Qing Empire.

## Terms
- **Eight Banners**: A socio-military system created by Nurhaci to organize Jurchen society, with each banner under the control of a royal family member.
- **Jurchen**: A Tungusic people from Manchuria, ancestors of the Manchu, who were divided into various tribes before unification.
- **Later Jin**: The dynasty founded by Nurhaci in 1616, named after the earlier Jurchen Jin dynasty; renamed Qing by his son.
- **Seven Grievances**: A 1618 document listing Nurhaci's complaints against the Ming, used to justify rebellion.
- **Beile**: A Manchu noble title, often used for princes or high-ranking leaders.
- **Khan**: A title for a ruler or leader, used by Nurhaci after 1616.
- **Manchu script**: A writing system created in 1599 by adapting the Mongolian vertical script for the Jurchen language.
- **Ginseng**: A valuable root harvested in Manchuria, a key trade good and source of conflict with Ming and Joseon.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Succession mystery**: It is debated whether Nurhaci intended his young son Dorgon to succeed him, with Daišan as regent, or if he truly favored collegial rule among his senior sons.
- **Treatment of Han subjects**: Scholars analyze the shift in Nurhaci's policies from initial concessions to discrimination after the 1623 revolt, reflecting challenges in governing a multi-ethnic state.
- **Name origins**: The meaning of "Nurhaci" is uncertain, with suggestions ranging from "wild boar skin" to "brave person."
- **Historical sources**: Primary sources like the Manchu Veritable Records are propagandistic, while Korean records (e.g., Veritable Records of the Joseon Dynasty) provide contemporary accounts but may have their own biases.

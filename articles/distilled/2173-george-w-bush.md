# George W. Bush

## Overview
George W. Bush served as the 43rd President of the United States from 2001 to 2009. His presidency was decisively shaped by the September 11, 2001 terrorist attacks, leading to the wars in Afghanistan and Iraq, the creation of the Department of Homeland Security, and a significant expansion of executive power. Domestically, he enacted major tax cuts, education reform, and a prescription drug benefit for Medicare, but his tenure ended amid the 2008 financial crisis and Great Recession. He is the son of the 41st president, George H. W. Bush.

## Timeline
- **July 6, 1946** — Born in New Haven, Connecticut.
- **1994** — Elected Governor of Texas.
- **November 7, 2000** — Wins the presidential election after a contested recount in Florida, decided by the Supreme Court in *Bush v. Gore*.
- **September 11, 2001** — Terrorist attacks occur, reshaping his presidency and leading to the "War on Terror."
- **October 7, 2001** — U.S. invasion of Afghanistan begins.
- **March 20, 2003** — U.S. invasion of Iraq begins.
- **November 2, 2004** — Re-elected president, defeating John Kerry.
- **August 29, 2005** — Hurricane Katrina devastates the Gulf Coast, leading to widespread criticism of the federal response.
- **September 15, 2008** — Lehman Brothers collapses, triggering the global financial crisis.
- **January 20, 2009** — Leaves office; Barack Obama is inaugurated as his successor.

## Body

### Early Life and Career
Born into the prominent Bush family, George W. Bush graduated from Yale University (1968) and Harvard Business School (1975). He served in the Texas Air National Guard from 1968 to 1974. After Harvard, he worked in the oil industry, founding Arbusto Energy, which later merged into Harken Energy. In 1989, he led a group to purchase the Texas Rangers baseball team, serving as managing general partner until 1998.

### Governor of Texas (1995–2000)
Bush was elected Governor of Texas in 1994, defeating incumbent Ann Richards. As governor, he focused on tort reform, education funding, and criminal justice reform. He signed legislation allowing concealed handgun permits and presided over 152 executions. In 1999, he signed a law requiring electric retailers to buy renewable energy, helping Texas become the leading producer of wind-powered electricity in the U.S. He was re-elected in 1998 with a record 68% of the vote.

### Presidential Elections
In the 2000 election, Bush defeated Al Gore in the Electoral College (271-266) after a controversial recount in Florida was halted by the Supreme Court's *Bush v. Gore* decision, despite Gore winning the popular vote. In 2004, he won re-election against John Kerry, securing 50.7% of the popular vote.

### Domestic Policy
Bush enacted a $1.35 trillion tax cut program in 2001 and another in 2003. His signature education law, the No Child Left Behind Act (2002), aimed to improve student performance through standardized testing. He signed the Medicare Modernization Act (2003), creating Medicare Part D for prescription drugs. His administration's response to Hurricane Katrina in 2005 was widely criticized. The 2008 financial crisis led to the Troubled Asset Relief Program (TARP) to stabilize the economy. He also signed the Genetic Information Nondiscrimination Act (2008).

### Foreign Policy and the War on Terror
The September 11 attacks defined Bush's foreign policy. He ordered the invasion of Afghanistan (2001) to overthrow the Taliban and destroy al-Qaeda. He signed the USA PATRIOT Act, expanding surveillance powers. In 2003, he ordered the invasion of Iraq, citing claims of weapons of mass destruction and ties to al-Qaeda. No such weapons were found. He authorized "enhanced interrogation techniques," later deemed torture by critics. He launched the President's Emergency Plan for AIDS Relief (PEPFAR) in 2003, which has been credited with saving millions of lives. He withdrew from the Anti-Ballistic Missile Treaty (2002) and opposed the Kyoto Protocol on climate change.

### Second Term and Legacy
Bush's second term saw failed attempts to reform Social Security and immigration. He appointed Chief Justice John Roberts and Justice Samuel Alito to the Supreme Court. His approval ratings, which peaked at 90% after 9/11, fell to 19% by 2008. Historians generally rank him as a below-average president, though his public image has improved since leaving office.

## Terms
- **No Child Left Behind Act (NCLBA):** A 2002 federal education law that increased accountability for public schools through standardized testing.
- **USA PATRIOT Act:** A 2001 law that expanded law enforcement's surveillance and investigative powers to combat terrorism.
- **PEPFAR:** The President's Emergency Plan for AIDS Relief, a 2003 initiative to combat the global HIV/AIDS epidemic.
- **TARP:** The Troubled Asset Relief Program, a 2008 program to purchase toxic assets from banks to stabilize the financial system.
- **Bush Doctrine:** The administration's policy of preemptive war against perceived threats, used to justify the invasion of Iraq.
- **Enhanced Interrogation Techniques:** Controversial methods, including waterboarding, authorized by the Bush administration for use on terror suspects.
- **Axis of Evil:** A term used by Bush in 2002 to describe governments he accused of sponsoring terrorism and seeking weapons of mass destruction (Iraq, Iran, North Korea).

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Justification for the Iraq War:** The failure to find weapons of mass destruction led to intense criticism that the administration manipulated or exaggerated intelligence.
- **Response to Hurricane Katrina:** The administration's handling of the disaster is widely viewed as a major failure of leadership and emergency management.
- **Economic Policy:** The long-term impact of the Bush tax cuts and increased spending on the national debt and economic inequality is debated.
- **Civil Liberties:** The expansion of surveillance via the PATRIOT Act and the use of enhanced interrogation techniques raised significant legal and ethical questions about executive power and human rights.
- **Historical Ranking:** While scholars generally rank him in the lower tier of presidents, his public approval has risen post-presidency, and supporters credit his counterterrorism policies and PEPFAR with saving lives.

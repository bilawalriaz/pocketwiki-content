# Aden

## Overview
Aden is an ancient port city in southern Yemen, strategically located on the Gulf of Aden near the Red Sea approach. Its position has made it a vital maritime hub connecting Africa, Asia, and the Middle East for millennia. The city has served as a capital for South Yemen (1967–1990) and has been Yemen's temporary capital since the 2014 civil war, highlighting its enduring political and economic significance.

## Timeline
- **c. 3000 BC** — Mentioned in ancient trade routes; known to Greeks as "Arabia Emporion."
- **8th–7th centuries BC** — Home to the ancient Kingdom of Awsan.
- **275 AD** — Himyarite Kingdom overthrows Sheba and takes control of Aden.
- **571 AD** — Sasanian Empire takes control of the city.
- **1513** — Portuguese naval siege fails.
- **1538** — Ottoman Empire takes control.
- **1839** — British East India Company conquers Aden.
- **1937** — Aden becomes a British Crown Colony, separate from India.
- **1963** — Colony incorporated into the Federation of South Arabia.
- **1967** — British forces evacuate; Aden becomes capital of South Yemen.
- **1990** — Unification of North and South Yemen; Aden loses capital status.
- **2015** — President Hadi declares Aden the temporary capital amid civil war.

## Body

### Antiquity and Medieval History
Aden's importance stems from its natural harbor and location on Indian Ocean trade routes. It was known to ancient Greeks and Romans, though the 1st-century *Periplus of the Erythraean Sea* described it as a simple village. The Himyarite Kingdom developed major infrastructure, including vast water cisterns. After the introduction of Islam in the 7th century, Aden stagnated until the 9th century. It flourished under successive dynasties like the Rasulids (13th–15th centuries), who built schools and codified trade laws. The city was a cosmopolitan port visited by scholars like Ibn Battuta.

### Colonial Era (1839–1967)
British interest began in the late 18th century, driven by strategic concerns after Napoleon's invasion of Egypt and the need for a coaling station for steamships traveling to India. The British East India Company formally conquered Aden in 1839. It was declared a free trade port in 1850 and governed as part of British India until 1937, when it became a Crown Colony. The port's importance grew with the opening of the Suez Canal in 1869. In 1963, the Colony was incorporated into the Federation of South Arabia, sparking an insurgency (the Aden Emergency) by nationalist groups. British forces evacuated in 1967.

### Post-Independence and Civil War
After British withdrawal, Aden became the capital of the People's Democratic Republic of Yemen (South Yemen), a Marxist-Leninist state allied with the Soviet Union. The economy suffered from the 1967–1975 closure of the Suez Canal. Internal power struggles culminated in a bloody 1986 civil war within the ruling Socialist Party. Following Yemeni unification in 1990, Aden lost its capital status. The city became a battleground in the 1994 civil war and later faced terrorist attacks by al-Qaeda. Since 2014, it has been the temporary capital of the internationally recognized government during the Yemeni civil war, enduring significant destruction and political fragmentation, including a 2018 coup by the secessionist Southern Transitional Council.

### Economy and Infrastructure
Aden's economy has historically relied on its port and free zone (established 1991). Key industries included an oil refinery (operational from 1954) and salt production. The city's infrastructure, including Aden International Airport (established 1927), was developed during the British period. The ongoing civil war has severely damaged infrastructure, causing chronic electricity shortages reliant on diesel generators, though a new solar power plant opened in 2024.

## Terms
- **Entrepôt**: A port or trading post where goods are imported, stored, and re-exported.
- **Tombolo**: A deposition landform in which an island is attached to the mainland by a narrow piece of land such as a spit or bar.
- **Aden Protectorate**: The British-administered hinterland of Aden, consisting of various sultanates and sheikhdoms.
- **Federation of South Arabia (FSA)**: A political entity formed by the British in 1963 to unite states of the Aden Protectorate, with Aden as a member state.
- **Aden Emergency (1963–1967)**: The period of insurgency against British rule in Aden and the FSA.
- **Southern Transitional Council (STC)**: A secessionist political body in southern Yemen that seized control of Aden in 2018.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The exact antiquity of Aden is debated; local legends claim it is as old as human history, while historical records confirm its role in ancient trade.
- The primary cause of the 1986 South Yemen civil war is attributed to regional and factional rivalries within the Socialist Party, but the precise triggers remain complex.
- The political future of Aden is uncertain, caught between the goals of the internationally recognized government, the STC's secessionist aims, and the broader Yemeni civil war.

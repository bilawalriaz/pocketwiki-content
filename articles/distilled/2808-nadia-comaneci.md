# Nadia Comăneci

## Overview
Nadia Comăneci is a Romanian retired gymnast, widely regarded as one of the greatest athletes of the 20th century. She achieved global fame for becoming the first gymnast to be awarded a perfect score of 10.0 at the Olympic Games, a feat that revolutionized the sport and brought unprecedented popularity to gymnastics in the mid-1970s.

## Timeline
- **November 12, 1961** — Born in Onești, Romania.
- **c. 1968** — Begins training with coach Béla Károlyi.
- **1970** — Becomes the youngest gymnast to win the Romanian Nationals at age 9.
- **1975** — Wins all-around and multiple event golds at the European Championships.
- **July 18, 1976** — Scores the first perfect 10.0 in Olympic history at the Montreal Games.
- **1976** — Wins three gold medals at the Montreal Olympics, becoming the youngest Olympic all-around champion at 14.
- **1977** — Defends European all-around title; Romanian team controversially walks out of competition.
- **1978** — Wins world title on balance beam at the World Championships.
- **1979** — Becomes first gymnast to win three consecutive European all-around titles.
- **1980** — Wins two gold medals at the Moscow Olympics, including defending her balance beam title.
- **1981** — Coaches Béla and Márta Károlyi defect during a U.S. tour; Comăneci's life in Romania becomes heavily restricted.
- **1984** — Officially retires from gymnastics.
- **November 27, 1989** — Defects from Romania, shortly before its revolution.
- **1996** — Marries American gymnast Bart Conner in Bucharest.

## Body

### Early Life and Training
Nadia Comăneci was born in Onești, Romania. Her mother enrolled her in gymnastics as a child due to her high energy. At age six, she was selected by coach Béla Károlyi for his experimental school after he spotted her doing cartwheels. She began competing in 1970 and quickly rose through the ranks, winning her first national title at age nine and her first international all-around title at age ten.

### Rise to Olympic Glory (1975–1976)
Comăneci's first major international success came at the 1975 European Championships. Her breakthrough occurred at the 1976 Summer Olympics in Montreal. On July 18, she scored the first perfect 10.0 in Olympic gymnastics history on the uneven bars. The scoreboard, not designed for such a score, displayed "1.00." She earned six more perfect 10s at the Games, winning gold in the all-around, balance beam, and uneven bars. At 14, she became the youngest Olympic gymnastics all-around champion, a record that stands due to subsequent age-eligibility rule changes.

### Career Challenges and Later Competition (1977–1984)
Following the 1977 European Championships, where the Romanian team walked out over scoring disputes, Comăneci was separated from the Károlyis and moved to Bucharest. She struggled with physical changes and unhappiness, which affected her performance. She regained form to win a third consecutive European all-around title in 1979 and helped Romania win its first team gold at the 1979 World Championships despite competing with a severe hand infection. At the 1980 Moscow Olympics, she won two more gold medals (balance beam, floor exercise) and two silvers. Her coach's public protests over scoring at these Games led to increased surveillance of Comăneci by the Romanian government. She was allowed to observe (not compete at) the 1984 Los Angeles Olympics before retiring in 1984.

### Defection and Personal Life
After the 1981 defection of her coaches, Comăneci was closely monitored by the Securitate secret police and restricted from travel. On November 27, 1989, she defected with a group, traveling through Hungary and Austria before settling in the United States. She eventually moved to Oklahoma, where she worked with and married fellow Olympic gold medalist Bart Conner. They married in Bucharest in 1996 after the fall of the Communist regime, in a wedding televised live in Romania. She became a U.S. citizen in 2001.

### Legacy and Post-Competitive Life
Comăneci is celebrated for her clean technique, innovative skills, and stoic demeanor. She has two skills named after her in the Code of Points. Post-retirement, she has held leadership roles in gymnastics organizations, co-owns a gymnastics academy and media company with her husband, and is active in charity work, including founding a children's clinic in Bucharest. She remains a prominent figure in the Olympic movement, participating in torch relays and commentary.

## Terms
*   **Perfect 10.0:** The highest possible score in gymnastics under the scoring system used during Comăneci's career, signifying a routine with no deductions.
*   **Code of Points:** The official set of rules and scoring guidelines used by the International Gymnastics Federation (FIG) to judge competitions.
*   **Securitate:** The secret police of Communist Romania, known for surveillance and repression of citizens.
*   **Defection:** The act of abandoning one's country or cause, especially to seek refuge in another; Comăneci fled Romania in 1989.
*   **All-around:** A competition where gymnasts perform on all apparatus, with the winner having the highest combined score.
*   **Uneven bars:** A women's artistic gymnastics apparatus consisting of two parallel bars set at different heights.
*   **Balance beam:** A women's artistic gymnastics apparatus consisting of a narrow beam on which gymnasts perform routines.

## Debates and open questions
*   The scoring controversies at the 1977 European Championships and the 1980 Moscow Olympics, where Comăneci and her coach publicly disputed judging decisions.
*   The impact of the Romanian government's strict control and surveillance on Comăneci's mental health and career trajectory after 1976.
*   The debate over whether the age-eligibility rule change in gymnastics (raising the minimum age to 16) was a direct response to her record-setting performance at age 14.

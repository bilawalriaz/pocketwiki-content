# Zhengzhou

## Overview
Zhengzhou is the capital and largest city of Henan Province, China, and a major national political, economic, and transport hub. Situated in the historically significant Zhongyuan region, it is considered a cradle of Chinese civilization with over 4,000 years of continuous habitation. Its strategic location at the junction of key railways and its rapid modern economic growth have transformed it into one of China's most important inland cities.

## Timeline
- **c. 1600–1046 BC** — Site of the Shang dynasty capital Áo or Bó.
- **587 CE** — First becomes the seat of a prefectural administration (Guanzhou) in the Sui dynasty.
- **1903** — Beijing–Hankou Railway arrives, beginning its rise as a rail hub.
- **1923** — Workers' strike begins, commemorated by the Erqi Memorial Tower.
- **1938** — Nationalist forces breach Yellow River dikes at Huayuankou to hinder Japanese advance, causing massive flooding.
- **1954** — Henan Provincial Government relocates from Kaifeng, making Zhengzhou the provincial capital.
- **1990** — Zhengzhou Commodity Exchange, China's first futures exchange, is approved.
- **2013** — Zhengzhou Metro begins operation.
- **2017** — Officially designated as a National Central City.
- **2021** — Record-breaking floods cause over 300 deaths and widespread displacement.

## Body

### History and Etymology
The area's history begins with the early Shang dynasty urban center, with archaeological evidence of settlements around 1600 BC. After the Shang, the Zhou-era city-state of Guan was founded, giving the city its early name, Guancheng. The name "Zhengzhou" dates to the Sui dynasty (AD 582), and the city became a major terminus for the New Bian Canal, supplying imperial capitals. Its modern importance surged with the arrival of railways in the early 20th century, making it a critical junction. In 1954, it became the provincial capital. Rapid economic and population growth followed China's reforms in the 1990s.

### Geography and Climate
Zhengzhou is located in north-central Henan on the southern bank of the Yellow River, where its valley widens into the North China Plain. The city's terrain transitions from mountains in the west to fertile plains in the east. It has a monsoon-influenced humid subtropical climate with four distinct seasons, hot summers, and cool, dry winters. Annual mean temperature is 15.4 °C (59.7 °F), with annual precipitation around 630 mm (25 in).

### Economy
Zhengzhou is a major economic center for inland China. Its GDP was ¥1.02 trillion in 2018 and ¥1.269 trillion in 2021. Key sectors include manufacturing (textiles, automobiles, electronics, with Foxconn's "iPhone City" being the world's largest smartphone production site), agriculture (wheat, cotton, and local specialties), and services. The Zhengzhou Commodity Exchange is a major global player in agricultural futures. The Zhengdong New Area, designed by Kisho Kurokawa, serves as the financial and modern business core.

### Transportation
Zhengzhou is a premier national transport hub. It is the junction of the Longhai and Beijing–Guangzhou railways and a major node on China's high-speed rail network, offering fast connections to Beijing (2.5 hours), Shanghai (4 hours), and other megacities. The Zhengzhou Metro system, opened in 2013, had 13 lines and 450 km of track as of 2026. Zhengzhou Xinzheng International Airport is the busiest in central China. The city also has one of the nation's highest highway densities.

### Culture and Education
Zhengzhou is a major research city, ranking among the world's top 60 per the Nature Index. It hosts numerous universities, including Zhengzhou University. The city is home to two UNESCO World Heritage Sites: the Shaolin Monastery and Pagoda Forest, and the Dengfeng Observatory. The Henan Museum holds over 130,000 cultural objects. Modern landmarks include the 388-meter Zhongyuan Tower and the "Big Corn" skyscraper.

## Terms
*   **Zhongyuan:** The Central Plains region of China, historically considered a cradle of Chinese civilization.
*   **National Central City:** A designation by the Chinese central government for cities with significant national political, economic, and transport influence.
*   **Prefecture (zhou):** A historical administrative division in China, larger than a county.
*   **Futures Exchange:** A regulated financial market where contracts for the future delivery of commodities or assets are traded.
*   **New Bian Canal:** A historical waterway connecting the Yellow River to the capital region, crucial for grain transport.
*   **Köppen climate classification Cwa:** A climate type denoting a monsoon-influenced humid subtropical climate with dry winters.
*   **Zhengdong New Area:** A major modern economic development zone in eastern Zhengzhou, designed as a financial and business center.
*   **Nature Index:** A database that tracks research output and author affiliations, used to rank cities and institutions in scientific research.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   The exact location of the early Shang capitals (Áo, Bó, or Yan) in the Zhengzhou area is debated among archaeologists.
*   The source notes that Zhengzhou's administrative subdivisions are "likely to undergo significant changes in the near future due to increasingly rapid urban expansion," indicating ongoing planning and potential reorganization.

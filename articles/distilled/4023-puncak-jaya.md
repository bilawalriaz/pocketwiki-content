# Puncak Jaya

## Overview
Puncak Jaya, also known as Carstensz Pyramid, is the highest mountain peak on an island, located in the Sudirman Range of Central Papua, Indonesia. With an elevation of 4,884 m (16,024 ft), it is the highest peak in Indonesia and Oceania, and is often listed as one of the Seven Summits. Its significance is heightened by its equatorial location, the presence of rapidly retreating glaciers, and its status as a technically demanding climb.

## Timeline
- **1623** — Dutch explorer Jan Carstenszoon first sights the glaciers on the peak.
- **1909** — Hendrik Albert Lorentz reaches the snowfield of nearby Puncak Trikora.
- **1919** — Predecessor of Lorentz National Park, encompassing the Carstensz Range, is established.
- **1936** — Dutch Carstensz expedition climbs East Carstensz and Ngga Pulu, but fails to climb Carstensz Pyramid.
- **1962** — The Carstensz Pyramid summit is first climbed by an expedition led by Heinrich Harrer.
- **1963** — Indonesia renames the peak Puncak Sukarno after President Sukarno.
- **1995-2005** — The mountain is closed to tourists and climbers.
- **2000s** — The Meren Glacier melts away.
- **2010** — An expedition finds glaciers are disappearing at a rate of seven metres thickness per year.
- **2018** — Glaciers are predicted to vanish in the 2020s.

## Body

### Geology and Glaciers
Puncak Jaya is the highest point on the Central Range, formed in the late Miocene by the collision of the Australian and Pacific plates. It is composed of middle Miocene limestones. While its peak is ice-free, several glaciers exist on its slopes, including the Carstensz Glacier and Northwall Firns. These are among the world's few equatorial glaciers. Analysis shows significant retreat since the 1850s, indicating regional warming of about 0.6 °C per century between 1850 and 1972. Satellite imagery since the 1970s indicates rapid retreat, with the Meren Glacier disappearing between 1994 and 2000. The remaining glaciers are projected to vanish in the 2020s.

### Climbing and Access
Puncak Jaya is considered one of the more technically demanding climbs among the Seven Summits, despite its lower elevation. The standard route involves a difficult multi-day trek through dense rainforest to base camp, followed by a technical rock climb up the north face and summit ridge. Access requires a government permit and is facilitated through adventure tourism agencies. The area is highly remote with no nearby rescue facilities; injuries often require evacuation by foot. Climbing is complicated by common porter strikes and cold, freezing temperatures near the summit.

### History and Naming
The highlands were inhabited before European contact, and the peak is known as Nemangkawi in the indigenous Amungme language. Dutch explorer Jan Carstenszoon named it "Carstensz Pyramid" in 1623, but his report of equatorial snow was ridiculed. The first successful ascent of the Carstensz Pyramid summit occurred in 1962. After Indonesia took control in 1963, the peak was renamed Puncak Sukarno, later changed to Puncak Jaya (meaning 'Victorious Peak'). The name Carstensz Pyramid remains in common use among mountaineers.

## Terms
- **Topographic isolation**: The distance to the nearest point of equal elevation, used to rank mountain peaks.
- **Seven Summits**: The highest mountains on each of the seven continents.
- **Ice cap climate (Köppen: EF)**: A climate where the average temperature of every month is below 0 °C (32 °F).
- **Little Ice Age**: A period of regional cooling, particularly in the Northern Hemisphere, from about the 14th to the 19th century.
- **Orogeny**: The process of mountain formation through large-scale structural deformation of the Earth's lithosphere.
- **Tyrolean traverse**: A technique for crossing a gap or crevasse using a rope anchored on both sides.
- **Hypoxemia**: A condition of abnormally low oxygen levels in the blood.
- **De-Sukarnoization**: The political process of removing the influence and naming of Indonesia's first president, Sukarno.

## Debates and open questions
- **Highest peak in Oceania**: Puncak Jaya is considered the highest peak in Oceania when New Guinea is included as part of the Australian continent biogeographically. However, its location in Indonesian-administered Western New Guinea also places it within Southeast Asia, where it is not the highest peak.
- **Highest summit in 1936**: Due to extensive glacial retreat, the now-subsidiary peak of Ngga Pulu was estimated to be the highest summit in 1936, reaching over 4,900 m (16,100 ft).

# Masjid al-Haram

## Overview
Masjid al-Haram, the Great Mosque of Mecca, is the holiest and largest mosque in Islam, enclosing the Kaaba, the direction of prayer for Muslims worldwide. It is the central site for the Hajj pilgrimage, a mandatory duty for able-bodied Muslims, and the lesser ʿUmrah pilgrimage. Its history of continuous expansion reflects the growth of the Muslim community and the political control of its various rulers.

## Timeline
- **c. 3000 BC (Islamic tradition)** — Abraham and Ishmael build the Kaaba, establishing the sanctuary.
- **630 CE** — Muhammad conquers Mecca, purges the Kaaba of idols, and establishes monotheistic worship.
- **637 CE** — Caliph Umar expands the mosque, increasing its area to 3,600 m².
- **692 CE** — Umayyad Caliph Abd al-Malik ibn Marwan orders the first major renovation.
- **779–786 CE** — Abbasid Caliphs al-Mahdi and al-Hadi expand the mosque to 28,000 m².
- **1570** — Ottoman Sultan Selim II commissions architect Mimar Sinan for a major renovation.
- **1955–1976** — First Saudi expansion adds minarets and incorporates the Mas'a gallery.
- **20 November 1979** — Extremist insurgents seize the mosque in a violent siege.
- **1986–1994** — Second Saudi expansion under King Fahd adds a new wing and outdoor prayer area.
- **2008–present** — Third Saudi expansion (King Abdullah Project) aims to increase capacity to over 2.5 million worshippers.
- **11 September 2015** — A construction crane collapses onto the mosque, killing at least 111 people.
- **5 March 2020** — The mosque begins nighttime closures and suspends ʿUmrah due to the COVID-19 pandemic.

## Body
### Origins and Early History
Islamic tradition holds that the prophet Abraham and his son Ishmael built the Kaaba, which became the focal point of a sacred sanctuary. The site was a center of polytheistic worship in pre-Islamic Arabia, with the Kaaba housing numerous idols. In 630 CE, Muhammad's conquest of Mecca ended this practice, purifying the sanctuary for monotheistic worship and establishing it as the direction of prayer for Muslims.

### Expansions and Renovations
The mosque has been repeatedly expanded to accommodate growing numbers of worshippers. Early caliphs Umar and Uthman enlarged the structure. Major renovations occurred under the Umayyad and Abbasid caliphates, with the Abbasid expansion under al-Mahdi and al-Hadi (779–786) setting its size until the modern era. The Ottoman period saw significant architectural renovations, notably by Mimar Sinan in 1570, which introduced domes and columns that are the oldest surviving parts of the current building.

### The Saudi Era and Modern Developments
The Saudi dynasty has overseen three massive expansions. The first (1955–1976) added minarets and enclosed the Mas'a gallery. The second (1986–1994) under King Fahd added a new wing and modern amenities. The ongoing third expansion, initiated by King Abdullah in 2008, aims to dramatically increase capacity to over 2.5 million worshippers. This era has also seen controversy, including the 1979 seizure of the mosque by insurgents and the demolition of historical sites to make way for new construction. The mosque's operations were significantly impacted by the COVID-19 pandemic in 2020.

### Pilgrimage and Sacred Sites
The mosque is the setting for the Hajj, one of Islam's Five Pillars, and the ʿUmrah pilgrimage. Key sites within the complex include the Kaaba itself, the Black Stone set in its eastern corner, the semi-circular Hatim wall, and the Maqam Ibrahim stone. The hills of Safa and Marwa, connected by the Mas'a walkway, are integral to the saʿī ritual. The Zamzam Well, located east of the Kaaba, is a traditional water source for pilgrims.

## Terms
- **Kaaba**: The cuboid-shaped building at the center of the mosque, the most sacred site in Islam and the direction of prayer.
- **Hajj**: The annual Islamic pilgrimage to Mecca, a mandatory religious duty for Muslims who are physically and financially able.
- **ʿUmrah**: A lesser pilgrimage to Mecca that can be undertaken at any time of year.
- **Tawaf**: The ritual of circumambulating the Kaaba seven times during Hajj and ʿUmrah.
- **Qibla**: The direction of the Kaaba, towards which Muslims pray.
- **Mataf**: The open, circular area surrounding the Kaaba where tawaf is performed.
- **Mas'a**: The enclosed walkway between the hills of Safa and Marwa, used for the saʿī ritual.
- **Saʿī**: The ritual of walking or running seven times between the hills of Safa and Marwa, commemorating Hagar's search for water.
- **Zamzam Well**: An ancient well located east of the Kaaba, a traditional source of water for pilgrims.
- **Custodian of the Two Holy Mosques**: A title held by the King of Saudi Arabia, referring to his role as guardian of the mosques in Mecca and Medina.

## Debates and Open Questions
Scholarly debate exists regarding the mosque's early history. Some archaeological evidence, such as a stone inscription from the Taif region, suggests the construction of the Masjid al-Haram dates to 78 AH, which challenges traditional Islamic historiography. Additionally, interpretations of Quranic verses, such as the reference to "Masjid al-Haram" in Surah Al-Isra, lead to questions about whether the term always referred to the mosque in Mecca. The significance of early mosque structures facing different directions remains a subject of debate between revisionist historians and conservatives. Furthermore, the expansion projects have sparked controversy over the destruction of early Islamic heritage sites in Mecca.

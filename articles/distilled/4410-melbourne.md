# Melbourne

## Overview
Melbourne is the capital and most populous city of the Australian state of Victoria and the second-most populous city in Australia. Founded in 1835, it grew rapidly during the 1850s gold rush to become a major global metropolis. Today, it is a leading financial, cultural, and sporting centre in the Asia-Pacific region, known for its diverse population, extensive tram network, and high liveability rankings.

## Timeline
- **1803** — First, short-lived British penal settlement established at Port Phillip.
- **1835** — Free settlers from Van Diemen's Land found Melbourne.
- **1837** — Settlement named Melbourne after the British Prime Minister, William Lamb, 2nd Viscount Melbourne.
- **1847** — Declared a city by Queen Victoria.
- **1851** — Port Phillip District separates to become the Colony of Victoria, with Melbourne as its capital.
- **1851–1860s** — Victorian gold rush triggers explosive population and economic growth.
- **1880s** — "Marvellous Melbourne" land boom; city becomes one of the world's wealthiest.
- **1890s** — Financial crisis and depression halt construction and growth.
- **1901** — Melbourne becomes the interim seat of the new Commonwealth of Australia.
- **1927** — Federal parliament moves to Canberra.
- **1956** — Hosts the Summer Olympics, the first held outside Europe or the US.
- **2020–2022** — Experiences the world's longest cumulative COVID-19 lockdown (262 days).

## Body

### Indigenous History and Colonisation
The Melbourne area has been home to Aboriginal Victorians for over 40,000 years. The traditional custodians are the Boonwurrung, Woiwurrung, and Wurundjeri peoples of the Kulin nation. The area was known as Narrm or Naarm. A brief British penal settlement in 1803 was abandoned. In 1835, free settlers from Van Diemen's Land (Tasmania) founded the city. A disputed "treaty" between settler John Batman and Wurundjeri elders was annulled by colonial authorities. The settlement was officially named Melbourne in 1837. Between 1836 and 1842, Aboriginal groups were largely dispossessed of their land.

### Gold Rush and Boom Eras
The discovery of gold in Victoria in 1851 sparked a massive gold rush, with Melbourne as the colony's main port. The city's population nearly doubled within months and it overtook Sydney as Australia's most populous city by 1865. The wealth funded grand civic buildings and infrastructure. The 1880s "land boom" saw Melbourne reputedly become the world's richest city, a period dubbed "Marvellous Melbourne." This bubble burst in the early 1890s, causing a severe depression and halting construction for years.

### Federation and Modern Growth
Melbourne served as the interim capital of the newly federated Australia from 1901 until the federal parliament moved to Canberra in 1927. Post-WWII immigration, primarily from Southern Europe, boosted growth. The city lost its status as Australia's primary financial centre to Sydney in the late 1970s but has since experienced significant renewal. Major projects like Federation Square and the Crown Casino complex, along with a construction boom in the 2010s, have reshaped the city. Melbourne was the world's most liveable city for much of the 2010s.

### Urban Structure and Transport
Melbourne's urban area is built around the Hoddle Grid, a central business district (CBD) layout from 1837. The city is characterised by low-density suburban sprawl, though inner-city density has increased. It has the world's largest tram network and an extensive metropolitan rail system. Melbourne Airport is the nation's second-busiest, and the Port of Melbourne is its busiest seaport. The city has a high dependency on private vehicles, supported by a large freeway network.

### Culture and Economy
Often called Australia's cultural capital, Melbourne is renowned for its music, theatre, visual arts, and street art. It is a UNESCO City of Literature and hosts major festivals and sporting events, including the Australian Open, the Melbourne Cup horse race, and the Australian Grand Prix. The economy is highly diversified, with strengths in finance, manufacturing, research, IT, education, and tourism. It is a major financial centre and headquarters to several of Australia's largest corporations.

## Terms
- **Hoddle Grid**: The original grid layout of Melbourne's central business district, designed by Robert Hoddle in 1837.
- **Kulin nation**: A coalition of Aboriginal Australian peoples who are the traditional custodians of the land encompassing Melbourne.
- **Land boom**: A period of extraordinary economic growth and speculative investment in real estate, particularly in the 1880s.
- **Marvellous Melbourne**: A phrase coined in 1885 to describe the opulence and energy of the city during its 1880s land boom.
- **Free Tram Zone**: An area in the Melbourne CBD and Docklands where tram travel is free.
- **Metro Tunnel**: A major underground rail project (opened 2025-2026) designed to increase capacity through the CBD.
- **Suburban Rail Loop**: A proposed 90-kilometre underground orbital rail line through Melbourne's middle suburbs.
- **Level Crossing Removal Project**: A state government initiative to remove dangerous and congested railway level crossings across Melbourne.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The nature of John Batman's 1835 "treaty" with Wurundjeri elders is heavily disputed; it may have been a gift exchange or a *tanderrum* ceremony allowing temporary access, not a permanent land sale.
- The boundaries between the traditional lands of the Wurundjeri and Bunurong peoples, agreed in 2021, remain contested by some individuals on both sides.
- The exact meaning and application of the Indigenous names Narrm and Naarm are debated; sources differ on whether they refer specifically to the city area or the wider Port Phillip Bay region.

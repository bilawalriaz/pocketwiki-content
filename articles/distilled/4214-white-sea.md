# White Sea

## Overview
The White Sea is a southern inlet of the Barents Sea on Russia's northwest coast, historically vital for trade and now part of Russia's internal waters. It is one of only four seas named after a color, alongside the Black, Red, and Yellow seas.

## Timeline
- **11th century** — Residents of Novgorod first knew of the White Sea.
- **Late 14th century** — Settlement at Kholmogory on the Northern Dvina grows.
- **1492** — First international seaport established at Kholmogory with a fleet sailing to Denmark.
- **1553** — English ship *Edward Bonaventure* arrives, initiating direct trade with England.
- **1555** — Queen Mary issues a charter for the Muscovy Company to trade via the White Sea.
- **1584** — Ivan IV establishes New Kholmogory (later Arkhangelsk) as a new port.
- **1596** — New Kholmogory begins to be known as Arkhangelsk.
- **1703** — Foundation of Saint Petersburg reduces the White Sea's trade importance.
- **1916** — Port of Murmansk is officially founded.
- **1920s** — Most northern Russian sea shipments divert to the ice-free port of Murmansk.

## Body

### Geography and Topography
The White Sea is a water-filled depression on the Baltic Shield continental shelf. It connects to the Barents Sea via a narrow, shallow strait called the "gorlo" (throat). The sea has four main bays: Kandalaksha Gulf (the deepest at 340 m), Onega Bay, Dvina Bay (home to the port of Arkhangelsk), and Mezen Bay. The seabed is uneven, with silt and sand in the central part and Dvina Bay, and sand and stones in the north. The Solovetsky Islands are the main island group.

### Hydrography and Climate
Rivers, notably the Northern Dvina, Mezen, and Onega, discharge about 215 km³ of fresh water annually, significantly influencing salinity and water exchange with the Barents Sea. Tides are semidiurnal, with amplitudes up to 10 meters in Mezen Bay. The climate is polar to subarctic, with cold, cloudy summers (average July temperatures 8–10 °C) and cold winters (average February temperatures around −15 °C). The sea freezes from October–November to April–May, with ice thickness usually about 40 cm.

### History and Trade
From the 15th to early 18th centuries, the White Sea was Russia's major trade route. The port of Kholmogory, and later Arkhangelsk, handled shipments of fur and fish. English and Dutch ships were frequent visitors. The sea's role as a primary trade route diminished after the founding of Saint Petersburg (1703) and later with the development of the ice-free port of Murmansk in the 1920s. The White Sea–Baltic Canal now connects it to the Baltic Sea.

### Fauna and Economy
The sea hosts over 700 invertebrate species, about 60 fish species, and marine mammals like beluga whales. The fishing industry targets species such as herring, cod, and salmon. The sea is a key traffic center for northwestern Russia, with major ports including Arkhangelsk, Belomorsk, and Kandalaksha. Navigation is maintained year-round with icebreakers.

## Terms
- **Gorlo**: The narrow, shallow strait connecting the White Sea to the Barents Sea.
- **Baltic Shield**: The continental shelf block containing the White Sea's water-filled depression.
- **Semidiurnal tides**: Tides that occur twice a day.
- **Salinity**: The salt concentration in water, measured in parts per thousand (‰).
- **Pomors**: "Seaside settlers" who conducted maritime trade from Kholmogory.
- **Muscovy Company**: An English chartered company authorized to trade with Russia via the White Sea route.

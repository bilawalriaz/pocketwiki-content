# Margaret I of Denmark

## Overview
Margaret I (1353–1412) was a Danish princess who became queen regnant of Denmark, Norway, and Sweden. She is most famous for founding the Kalmar Union in 1397, which united the three Scandinavian kingdoms under a single monarch for over a century. Her reign is noted for its political skill, administrative reforms, and a foreign policy focused on strengthening the Nordic realm against external threats, particularly the Hanseatic League.

## Timeline
- **March 1353** — Born at Søborg Castle, Denmark.
- **9 April 1363** — Marries King Haakon VI of Norway and Sweden.
- **1370** — Son Olaf is born.
- **1375** — Procures the election of her infant son Olaf as King of Denmark.
- **1380** — Olaf succeeds his father as King of Norway; Margaret rules in his name.
- **1387** — Olaf dies; Margaret is chosen Regent of Norway and Denmark.
- **1388** — Swedish nobles elect her "Sovereign Lady and Ruler."
- **24 February 1389** — Decisive victory over King Albert of Sweden at the Battle of Falköping.
- **1395** — Compact of Lindholm releases Albert; Hanseatic League holds Stockholm.
- **1396** — Stockholm is surrendered to Margaret.
- **1397** — Treaty of Kalmar is published; her grandnephew Erik is crowned king of the united realms.
- **28 October 1412** — Dies suddenly in Flensburg Harbor.

## Body

### Early Life and Marriage
Margaret was the youngest child of King Valdemar IV of Denmark. In 1363, at age ten, she married Haakon VI, a union that was part of a complex Nordic power struggle. After the deaths of her husband (1380) and her only son, Olaf (1387), Margaret, who had already ruled in their names, was elected regent of Denmark and Norway. She had already demonstrated her diplomatic skill by recovering the duchy of Schleswig from the Holstein-Rendsburg Counts via the Compact of Nyborg (1386).

### Rise to Power and the Union of Kalmar
With her position in Denmark and Norway secure, Margaret turned to Sweden, where nobles were rebelling against King Albert. After being elected their ruler in 1388, she defeated Albert in battle in 1389, though he held out in Stockholm until 1395. In 1389, she adopted her grandnephew Bogislav, who became Erik of Pomerania, and made him king of Norway. In 1397, she formalized the Kalmar Union, crowning Erik as king of all three kingdoms. The treaty proposed an "everlasting union" for mutual defense, while assuring each realm would be governed by its own laws and customs. Margaret remained the effective ruler until her death.

### Governance and Policy
Margaret was a pragmatic and centralized ruler. She recovered vast amounts of alienated crown lands (a process called *reduktion*) and reformed the currency. She ruled through a loyal bureaucracy of civil and ecclesiastical officials, often placing Danes in key positions in Sweden and Norway, which some historians criticize as favoritism. Her foreign policy was circumspect and neutral, avoiding entanglement in European wars like the Hundred Years' War. She focused on recovering lost territories (like Gotland) and securing advantageous marriages, such as Erik's 1406 marriage to England's Princess Philippa, which formed a defensive alliance.

### Death and Legacy
Margaret died suddenly in 1412 while at war with Holstein over Schleswig. Her sarcophagus is in Roskilde Cathedral. She is remembered as a highly capable leader, sometimes called the "Semiramis of the North" or "Lady King." Her political genius is widely acknowledged, though historians debate her motives—whether she was an idealist uniting Scandinavia against German influence or a Machiavellian power-seeker. The Kalmar Union she founded endured, in various forms, until the 16th century.

## Terms
*   **Queen Regnant:** A female monarch reigning in her own right, not merely as a consort.
*   **Kalmar Union:** A personal union that joined the three kingdoms of Denmark, Norway, and Sweden (including Finland) under a single monarch from 1397 to 1523.
*   **Hanseatic League:** A powerful commercial and defensive confederation of merchant guilds and market towns in Northern Europe, which dominated Baltic trade.
*   **Reduktion:** The policy of reclaiming crown lands that had been alienated or granted away to nobles or the church.
*   **Danehof:** A medieval Danish assembly or court, which fell into disuse under Margaret's centralized rule.
*   **Riksråd:** The council of the realm in Scandinavian kingdoms; Margaret minimized its influence.
*   **Pax Dei (Peace of God):** A movement to limit warfare and protect non-combatants; Margaret extended its principles to include "kvindefred" (women's peace).
*   **Compact of Nyborg (1386):** An agreement where the Holstein-Rendsburg Counts returned Schleswig to the Danish Crown as a fief under strict conditions.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Motives:** Historians debate whether Margaret's primary goal was an idealistic union of Scandinavia to counter German power or a pragmatic consolidation of her own dynastic power and control.
*   **Favoritism:** Some Norwegian and Swedish historians criticize her for favoring Denmark and being autocratic, while others note she was generally respected and is celebrated as a unifying figure.
*   **The Act of Union:** The formal Act of Union resulting from the 1397 Kalmar congress was never completed. Scholars continue to debate the reasons for this omission.
*   **Cause of Death:** The historical record is silent on her cause of death. Theories include plague, shock, or even poisoning by her successor, Erik.

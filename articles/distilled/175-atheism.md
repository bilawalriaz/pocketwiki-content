# Atheism

## Overview
Atheism is the absence of belief in deities, ranging from a passive lack of belief (implicit/negative atheism) to an active assertion that no gods exist (explicit/positive atheism). It contrasts with theism (belief in at least one deity) and is distinct from, though often overlapping with, agnosticism (the view that the existence of deities is unknown or unknowable). Historically rooted in ancient Indian and Greek philosophy, atheism resurged in Europe during the Enlightenment and expanded globally in the 20th century through movements like Marxism and secular humanism. Estimates of the non-theistic population range from 500 million to 1.1 billion. Atheists generally argue that the burden of proof lies with theists, citing a lack of empirical evidence, the problem of evil, and the success of naturalistic explanations for phenomena traditionally attributed to the divine. They advocate for secular ethics, scientific autonomy, and the separation of religion from state power.

## Timeline
- **c. 6th century BCE** — Chārvāka (Lokāyata) materialist school originates in India, explicitly rejecting deities and Vedic authority.
- **c. 5th century BCE** — Greek term *átheos* ("godless") shifts from censure ("impious") to denote active denial of the gods; Diagoras of Melos condemned in Athens.
- **c. 300 BCE** — Epicurus develops materialistic philosophy; gods exist but are indifferent to humans, removing fear of divine wrath.
- **381 CE** — Christianity becomes Roman state religion under Theodosius I; heresy becomes punishable, suppressing open atheism in West.
- **9th–11th century** — Islamic Golden Age produces skeptical rationalists (al-Warraq, al-Rawandi) and explicit atheist al-Maʿarri.
- **1566** — Term "atheist" (from French *athée*) appears in English.
- **1674** — Matthias Knutzen publishes first known explicit atheist writings in German.
- **1770** — Baron d'Holbach publishes *The System of Nature*, a major Enlightenment atheist work.
- **1842** — George Holyoake last imprisoned in UK for atheism; coins term "secularism".
- **1917–1991** — Soviet Union enforces state atheism: bans religious instruction, establishes League of Militant Atheists.
- **1948** — *McCollum v. Board of Education* (US) strikes down religious education in public schools.
- **1963** — *Murray v. Curlett* (US) bans compulsory prayer in public schools.
- **2000s** — "New Atheism" movement (Harris, Dawkins, Hitchens, Dennett) advocates active countering of religious influence.

## Body

### Definition and Classification
Writers contest the boundaries of atheism: which supernatural entities count as "gods," whether atheism is a positive philosophical stance or a mere absence of belief, and whether it requires conscious rejection. The prevailing norm defines atheism as an explicit stance against theism.
**Implicit vs. Explicit:** *Implicit atheism* is the absence of theistic belief without conscious rejection (e.g., newborns, those unexposed to theism). *Explicit atheism* is the conscious rejection of belief. Baron d'Holbach (1772) and George H. Smith argued all children are born atheists. Ernest Nagel contested counting mere absence as "philosophical atheism"; Graham Oppy classifies those lacking the concept of a god (e.g., infants) as "innocents."
**Negative vs. Positive:** Antony Flew and Michael Martin distinguish *negative (weak/soft) atheism* (all non-theism short of positive affirmation) from *positive (strong/hard) atheism* (explicit affirmation that gods do not exist). Martin argues agnosticism entails negative atheism, though many agnostics reject this. J.J.C. Smart suggests some self-described agnostics are actually atheists deterred by extreme philosophical skepticism. Richard Dawkins proposes a spectrum of theistic probability instead of discrete categories.
**Theistic Innatism:** Before the 18th century, Western thought often held that belief in God is innate (*theistic innatism*), rendering true atheism impossible or a form of denial. Sam Harris argues "atheism" should not exist as a label, akin to "non-astrologer."

### Etymology
The Greek adjective *átheos* (ἄθεος, *a-* "without" + *theos* "god") originally meant "ungodly" or "impious" (censure). By the 5th century BCE, it indicated active godlessness ("denying the gods"). The abstract noun *atheotēs* meant "atheism." Cicero transliterated *átheos* into Latin. Early Christians and Hellenists used it pejoratively against each other. The English "atheist" (from French *athée*) appears by 1566; "atheism" (from French *athéisme*) by 1587. Initially describing disbelief in the monotheistic Abrahamic god, the term expanded in the 20th century to cover disbelief in all deities.

### Arguments for Atheism
**Epistemological:** David Hume’s skepticism holds certainty impossible; unobservable metaphysical concepts (like God) should be rejected as "sophistry and illusion." Michael Martin argues atheism is a justified true belief via "mid-level principles" of ordinary and scientific reasoning. *Theological noncognitivism* (A.J. Ayer, Theodore Drange) claims "God exists" is cognitively meaningless, not a proposition, placing it outside standard atheism/agnosticism.
**Ontological:** Most atheists lean toward *ontological monism* (one fundamental substance). *Philosophical materialism* and *physicalism* posit only matter/physical entities exist, excluding non-material deities. *Naturalism* asserts everything is natural; science explains the world via physical laws. A PhilPapers survey cites 56.5% of philosophers lean toward physicalism, 49.8% toward naturalism. Graham Oppy distinguishes *direct arguments* (theism fails on its own terms, e.g., problem of evil) from *indirect arguments* (arguing for naturalism, which is inconsistent with theism).
**Logical (Problem of Evil):** Deductive arguments assert logical incompatibility between divine attributes (omnipotence, omniscience, omnibenevolence) and the existence of evil/suffering. Epicurus originated the trilemma; David Hume (1779) formalized it: God is either impotent, malevolent, or not God. Vasubandhu (4th/5th century) outlined similar Buddhist arguments.
**Secular Accounts of Religion:** Ludwig Feuerbach and Sigmund Freud view God as a human invention fulfilling psychological needs. Karl Marx and Friedrich Engels call religion a social function ("opium of the people") used by the powerful to oppress. Mikhail Bakunin argues the idea of God negates human liberty; if God existed, "it would be necessary to abolish him."

### Atheism and Ethics
**Secular Ethics:** Phil Zuckerman correlates societal well-being (lower murder rates, less prejudice, authoritarianism) with irreligion in developed democracies. Joseph Baker and Buster Smith note atheists often view their position as more moral than religion. Critics (e.g., Blaise Pascal) claim atheism leads to moral relativism or meaninglessness ("no atheists in foxholes"), though counter-examples exist. Plato’s *Euthyphro dilemma* questions if morality derives from God (arbitrary) or is independent (God unnecessary). Susan Neiman and Julian Baggini argue ethical behavior based solely on divine mandate is blind obedience; atheism provides a superior basis for evaluating moral imperatives independently.
**Criticism of Religion:** "New Atheists" (Hitchens, Dennett, Harris, Dawkins) and predecessors (Russell, Ingersoll, Voltaire) cite harmful doctrines and historical events (Crusades, inquisitions, terrorism). Sam Harris links divine authority to authoritarianism; studies correlate religious fundamentalism with prejudice. Believers counter that atheist regimes (Soviet Union) committed mass murder. Harris and Dawkins reply Stalin/Mao acted from dogmatic ideology, not *in the name of* atheism.
**Religions and Spirituality:** Atheism is not mutually exclusive with all religion. Non-theistic traditions exist in Hinduism, Jainism, Buddhism, and modern movements (humanistic Judaism, Christian atheism, Neopaganism).

### History
**Early Indian Religions:** Atheistic schools appear in the Vedic period. *Samkhya* (oldest orthodox Hindu school) and early *Mimamsa* reject God. *Chārvāka* (c. 6th century BCE) is thoroughly materialistic/anti-theistic, classified as heterodox for rejecting Vedas. Knowledge of it is fragmentary, preserved largely in rival schools' refutations. Jainism and Buddhism also reject a personal creator.
**Classical Antiquity:** Roots in pre-Socratic philosophy (Democritus, Atomists), though modern atheism was rare. Anaxagoras condemned for denying divinity of celestial bodies. Diagoras of Melos (late 5th century BCE) sentenced to death for mocking Mysteries; modern scholars debate if he was a modern-sense atheist or rejected gods via problem of evil (Tim Whitmarsh). Protagoras was agnostic. Socrates executed for "not believing in gods of the state," though he denied atheism charges. David Sedley concludes 5th-century Athens had an "atheist underground." Hellenistic period: Epicurus (c. 300 BCE) taught gods exist but are indifferent; universe governed by chance; goal is *ataraxia* (peace of mind) via eliminating fear of divine wrath. Euhemerus claimed gods were deified rulers. Theodorus Cyrenaicus and Strato of Lampsacus reputedly denied gods. Sextus Empiricus (c. 200 CE) compiled arguments against gods, recommending suspension of judgment. Early Christians called "atheists" for rejecting Graeco-Roman gods; after Theodosius I (381), heresy punishable.
**Early Middle Ages to Renaissance:** Islamic Golden Age (9th–11th century) produced skeptical rationalists (al-Warraq, al-Rawandi, al-Razi) and explicit atheist al-Maʿarri (religion a "fable"). Work survives mainly via Muslim apologists' refutations. In Europe, atheism rare; Medieval Inquisition suppressed heterodoxy. William of Ockham limited human knowledge of divine essence. Renaissance expanded free inquiry (Leonardo da Vinci); critics included Machiavelli, Montaigne, Rabelais.
**Early Modern Period:** Reformation attacked Catholic authority, inspiring attacks on Protestant authority (Geoffrey Blainey). Deism rises. Etienne Dolet executed 1546. Baruch Spinoza called first well-known "semi-atheist" in Christian lands; natural laws explain universe. 17th/18th century: Criticism frequent in France/England. Thomas Hobbes espoused materialism. Matthias Knutzen (1674) first explicit atheist writer; followed by Kazimierz Łyszczyński (treatise on non-existence of God) and Jean Meslier (1720s). Baron d'Holbach (Enlightenment) publishes *The System of Nature* (1770). First English defense of atheism (William Hammon, Mathew Turner) late 18th century. Voltaire, David Hume (empiricist skepticism), Immanuel Kant (limits of metaphysical knowledge) undermine natural theology. French Revolution: Civil Constitution of the Clergy, Jacobin Cult of Supreme Being (deistic). 19th century: Ludwig Feuerbach (God as human invention) influences Marx and Nietzsche. George Holyoake (1842) last UK imprisonment for atheism; coins "secularism."
**20th Century:** Atheism recognized in Marxism, logical positivism, existentialism, humanism, feminism. Bertrand Russell, John Dewey (naturalism), J.N. Findlay, J.J.C. Smart (analytical philosophy) reject God. State atheism in Soviet Union (Lenin, Stalin: legislative acts, League of Militant Atheists; Stalin softened during WWII) and Communist China (Mao). 1966 *Time* "Is God Dead?" cites Death of God theology. Periyar E.V. Ramasamy fights caste/religion in India. US: Vashti McCollum (1948), Madalyn Murray O'Hair (1963) win Supreme Court cases on school religion. Freedom from Religion Foundation founded 1976.
**21st Century:** "New Atheism" (Harris, Dennett, Dawkins, Hitchens, Stenger) advocates active countering of religion. Cites 9/11 and creationism pushes as catalysts. Global Atheist Conventions held in Melbourne (2010, 2012); 2018 cancelled for insufficient interest.

### Demographics
Quantification is difficult due to varying definitions of "atheism" vs. "non-religious" vs. non-theistic spirituality.
**Global:** 2010 *Encyclopædia Britannica* survey: non-religious 9.6%, atheists 2.0% (excluding atheistic Buddhists). Average annual change 2000–2010: −0.17% (decline attributed to lower birth rates in irreligious countries). Win-Gallup: "convinced atheists" 13% (2012), 11% (2015), 9% (2017). Top 2012 countries: China (47%), Japan (31%), Czech Republic (30%), France (29%). NORC 2012: East Germany highest, Czech Republic second. Atheism correlates with societal/individual security.
**Europe:** 2010 Eurobarometer: "don't believe in spirit/God/life force" high in France (40%), Czech Republic (37%), Sweden (34%); low in Poland (5%), Romania (1%). EU average 20%. 2012 Eurobarometer: 16% non-believers/agnostics, 7% atheists. Pew 2012: 18% religiously unaffiliated; majority only in Czech Republic (75%) and Estonia (60%). Pew 2018: "Do not believe in God" ranges from Portugal (13%) to Sweden (60%).
**Asia:** Religiously unaffiliated majority in North Korea (71%), Japan (57%), Hong Kong (56%), China (52%).
**Australasia:** Australia 2021 Census: 38% "no religion." New Zealand 2018: 48.2% "no religion" (up from 30% in 1991).
**United States:** World Values Survey 2014: 4.4% self-ID atheist, 11.1% "no" to belief in God. Pew 2014: 3.1% atheist (up from 1.6% in 2007); 13.6% of unaffiliated. GSS 1991–2014: atheists ~2–3%, agnostics ~4–5% (flat). American Family Survey 2017: 34% unaffiliated (5% atheist). Pew 2023: 28% unaffiliated.
**Arab World:** Profile rising; organizing in cafés/social media despite crackdowns. Gallup 2012: 5% Saudis "convinced atheists." Very few youth have atheist acquaintances (<1% in Morocco, Egypt, Saudi Arabia, Jordan; 3–7% in UAE, Bahrain, Kuwait, Palestine). UAE exception: 51% report traces of atheism locally.
**Attitudes:** Atheists held in poor regard globally; implicitly viewed as prone to immorality. Pew 2016: 15% French, 45% Americans, 99% Indonesians say belief in God necessary for morality. US "feeling thermometer": atheists and Muslims tie for lowest rating. Mortality salience increases negative perception among religious students (death anxiety hypothesis).
**Wealth, Education, Reasoning:** Positive correlations between atheism and education, wealth, IQ. Pew 2024: US atheists 77% white (vs 62% general). 2008 study: intelligence negatively related to religious belief in Europe/US. 137-country sample: national IQ vs. disbelief correlation 0.60. Nigel Barber: atheism blossoms with economic security (social democracies). Relationship not large; hypotheses: mediation by nonconformity (religion as conformist choice) or analytical reasoning (disbelief requires deliberative override of intuitive belief). 2017 study: atheists higher reasoning capacity (unrelated to demographics). 2015/2016 studies: atheists score higher on Cognitive Reflection Test (analytic thinking); meta-analysis confirms 18.7% higher score, negative correlation with religiosity. Effect found in academic philosophers controlling for confounds. Some studies show culture/gender dependence (men higher on both atheism and analytic thinking tests).

## Terms
- ****Atheism (broad)**** — Absence of belief in the existence of deities.
- ****Atheism (narrow)**** — The position that there are no deities.
- ****Theism**** — Belief that at least one deity exists.
- ****Implicit atheism**** — Absence of theistic belief without a conscious rejection of it (e.g., newborns).
- ****Explicit atheism**** — Conscious rejection of theistic belief.
- ****Negative (weak/soft) atheism**** — All forms of non-theism short of positively affirming gods do not exist.
- ****Positive (strong/hard) atheism**** — Explicit affirmation that gods do not exist.
- ****Agnosticism**** — View that the existence of deities is unknown or unknowable; often contrasted with or seen as entailing negative atheism.
- ****Theological noncognitivism**** — Position that "God exists" expresses no coherent proposition (cognitively meaningless).
- ****Naturalism**** — View that everything is fundamentally natural; no supernatural phenomena exist; science explains the world via physical laws.
- ****Physicalism**** — Ontological position that only physical entities exist.
- ****Problem of evil**** — Argument that an omnipotent, omniscient, omnibenevolent God is logically incompatible with the existence of evil and suffering.
- ****Secularism**** — Principle of separation of government/institutions from religion; coined by George Holyoake (1842).
- ****New Atheism**** — Early-21st-century movement advocating that religion should be actively countered, criticized, and exposed by rational argument.
- ****State atheism**** — Official government promotion of atheism and suppression of religion (e.g., Soviet Union, Communist China).

## Debates and open questions
*   **Definition boundaries:** Whether atheism requires conscious rejection (explicit) or includes mere absence of belief (implicit); whether "god" refers only to personal creators or all supernatural/transcendental concepts.
*   **Atheism vs. Agnosticism:** Whether agnosticism is a distinct middle ground, a form of negative atheism, or a separate epistemological category (theological noncognitivism).
*   **Burden of proof:** Whether theists must prove God exists or atheists must prove God does not; whether "absence of evidence is evidence of absence."
*   **Morality's foundation:** Whether objective morality requires a divine lawmaker (Euthyphro dilemma) or can be grounded in secular reasoning/human well-being.
*   **Causality of demographics:** Whether correlations between atheism and intelligence/education/wealth reflect analytical reasoning overriding intuition, nonconformity traits, cultural factors, or economic security reducing existential anxiety.
*   **Historical responsibility:** Whether 20th-century atrocities by officially atheist regimes (USSR, China) were caused by atheism itself or by dogmatic totalitarian ideologies that happened to be atheistic.
*   **Global trajectory:** Whether atheism is declining as a global percentage due to demographic shifts (lower birth rates in secular nations) despite rising absolute numbers or visibility in specific regions.

# Brasília

## Overview
Brasília is the planned capital city of Brazil, inaugurated in 1960 to replace Rio de Janeiro. It was designed as a modernist utopia to foster national integration and development by moving the capital to a central location. The city is renowned for its unique airplane-shaped urban plan and modernist architecture, and it serves as the seat of all three branches of Brazil's federal government.

## Timeline
- **1821** — José Bonifácio de Andrada e Silva suggests the name "Brasília" for a future capital.
- **1891** — Brazil's new constitution mandates moving the capital from Rio de Janeiro to the country's center.
- **1956** — President Juscelino Kubitschek initiates planning and construction of the new capital.
- **1957** — Lúcio Costa wins the international design contest for the city's plan.
- **21 April 1960** — Brasília is officially inaugurated as Brazil's capital.
- **1987** — Brasília is inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
- **2014** — Brasília hosts matches for the FIFA World Cup.
- **2017** — UNESCO names Brasília a "City of Design."

## Body

### Planning and Design
The idea of a central capital dates back to 1827, but serious planning began after the 1891 constitution. President Kubitschek fulfilled his campaign pledge by launching the project in 1956. Architect Lúcio Costa's winning plan, shaped like an airplane or bird, featured a cross-axial design. The "Monumental Axis" (east-west) houses government buildings, while the "Residential Axis" (north-south) contains housing in self-contained "superblocks" (superquadras). The design prioritized automobile traffic and modernist principles, influenced by Le Corbusier. Chief architect Oscar Niemeyer designed the iconic public buildings, while landscape architect Roberto Burle Marx created the gardens. The city was built in just 41 months.

### Government and Legal Status
Brasília has a unique legal status as an administrative region, not a municipality. The Federal District, which contains the city, is a *sui generis* (unique) entity with the combined powers of a state and a municipality. It is governed by a governor and a Legislative Chamber, but has no mayor or city council. The city hosts all federal government branches, 124 foreign embassies, and is the site of significant international declarations.

### Geography and Climate
Located in the Central-West region on the Brazilian Highlands at an elevation of 1,000 meters, Brasília has a tropical savanna climate with distinct rainy (October-April) and dry (May-September) seasons. The artificial Paranoá Lake was created to increase water availability and humidity. The city experiences low humidity in the dry season and has recorded temperatures from 1.6°C to 36.4°C.

### Demographics and Economy
Brasília's population grew rapidly from its founding, far exceeding initial estimates. The 2022 census recorded 2.8 million residents in the metropolitan area, with a mixed-race majority (48.7%). The economy is dominated by public administration and services, reflecting its role as a governmental center. It has the highest GDP per capita among major Latin American cities. Industries include construction, food processing, and software, with a focus on non-polluting sectors to preserve the UNESCO site.

### Culture and Architecture
The city is a cultural hub with a cosmopolitan character, hosting major festivals, concerts, and film events. Its modernist architecture, primarily by Oscar Niemeyer, is a defining feature. Key structures include the National Congress, the Cathedral of Brasília (a hyperboloid structure), the Palácio do Planalto (presidential workplace), and the Palácio da Alvorada (presidential residence). The city's design integrates art into its urban landscape.

### Transportation
Brasília's international airport is the third-busiest in Brazil. The city's road network was designed for cars, but a metro system and extensive bus network serve the population, especially connecting the central Plano Piloto to satellite cities. The JK Bridge, an award-winning structure, crosses Lake Paranoá.

## Terms
- **Plano Piloto**: The original planned city area of Brasília, shaped like an airplane, which houses the federal government and is a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
- **Superquadra (superblock)**: A residential unit in Brasília's plan, designed as a self-contained neighborhood with uniform apartment buildings and integrated green space.
- **Monumental Axis**: The east-west "fuselage" of the airplane-shaped city, containing government ministries, monuments, and the Praça dos Três Poderes.
- **Sui generis**: A Latin term meaning "of its own kind," used to describe the Federal District's unique legal status as neither a state nor a municipality.
- **Modernist architecture**: An architectural style emphasizing function, simplicity, and new materials, prominently featured in Brasília's public buildings.
- **Satellite cities**: Urban areas surrounding the central Plano Piloto, where much of Brasília's population resides, often developed informally.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Urban Planning Critique**: Critics argue Costa's plan created a "modernist platonic fantasy" that prioritized political aspirations and automobiles over human needs, leading to social segregation and long commutes.
- **Social Segregation**: The superquadra design, while intended to integrate classes, has been accused of fostering spatial segregation and alienation.
- **Growth vs. Plan**: The city's rapid, unplanned growth in satellite cities contrasted sharply with the controlled utopian vision of the original Plano Piloto, creating a "fantasy island" effect of inequality.
- **Symbolism**: The airplane-shaped plan is interpreted variously as an homage to aviation, a symbol of modernity, and a Cold War-era statement aligning Brazil with the victorious Allies.

# Julio Iglesias

## Overview
Julio Iglesias (born 23 September 1943) is a Spanish singer and songwriter recognized as the most commercially successful Spanish singer in history, with over 300 million records sold worldwide in 14 languages. A former professional footballer whose career ended after a 1963 car accident, he pivoted to music while rehabilitating, winning the Benidorm Song Festival in 1968. He achieved early dominance in Europe and Latin America before breaking into the English-speaking market in the 1980s with the multi-platinum album *1100 Bel Air Place*. His career spans over five decades, 5,000 concerts, and a rare crossover appeal that made him the best-selling male Latin artist of all time.

## Timeline
- **23 Sep 1943** — Born in Madrid to Dr. Julio Iglesias Sr. and María del Rosario de la Cueva y Perignat.
- **1963** — Near-fatal car accident ends professional football career (goalkeeper for Real Madrid Castilla); begins learning guitar during two-year rehabilitation.
- **1968** — Wins Benidorm Song Festival with "La vida sigue igual"; signs with Discos Columbia; releases debut album *Yo Canto*.
- **1970** — Represents Spain at Eurovision Song Contest with "Gwendolyne" (4th place); scores European #1 hit with "Un canto a Galicia".
- **1979** — Moves to Miami; signs with CBS International; begins recording in English, French, Italian, Portuguese, and German.
- **1981** — Releases *De niña a mujer*; scores first UK #1 with cover of "Begin the Beguine".
- **1984** — Releases *1100 Bel Air Place*; sells over 3 million US copies; "To All the Girls I've Loved Before" (duet with Willie Nelson) hits US Country #1.
- **1988** — Wins Grammy Award for Best Latin Pop Album (*Un hombre solo*).
- **2001** — Recognized as Person of the Year by Latin Recording Academy.
- **23 Apr 2013** — Inducted into Latin Songwriters Hall of Fame.
- **1 Apr 2013** — Honored in Beijing as Most Popular International Artist in China; receives Guinness World Record for Best-selling Male Latin Artist.
- **May 2015** — Awarded Honorary Doctorate by Berklee College of Music.

## Body

### Early Life and Career Pivot
Iglesias was born into a prominent Madrid family; his father was a leading gynecologist and his maternal grandparents included a native of Guayama, Puerto Rico. He claims Jewish ancestry on his maternal side, noting the surname "de la Cueva" (of the cave) refers to Jews in hiding. He balanced law studies at CEU San Pablo University with a professional football career as a goalkeeper for Real Madrid Castilla. A 1963 automobile accident shattered his lower spine, leaving him unable to walk for two years and permanently weakening his legs. During hospitalization, a nurse gave him a guitar to recover hand dexterity, leading to the discovery of his musical talent. He later completed his law degree at Complutense University of Madrid in 2001.

### European and Latin American Breakthrough (1968–1978)
Iglesias won the 1968 Benidorm Song Festival with "La vida sigue igual," which spawned a film of the same name. His debut album *Yo Canto* peaked at No. 3 in Spain. At Eurovision 1970, "Gwendolyne" placed fourth, but the follow-up "Un canto a Galicia"—sung in Galician to honor his father—became a trans-European #1 hit, selling one million copies in Germany alone. He cultivated the Italian market with "Se mi lasci non vale" (1975) and released successful albums including *Soy* (1973), *A flor de piel* (1974), and *El amor* (1975), establishing a multilingual repertoire spanning Spanish, Galician, French, and Italian.

### English-Language Crossover and Global Stardom (1979–1990s)
Relocating to Miami in 1979 and signing with CBS International catalyzed his global expansion. The 1981 album *De niña a mujer* yielded his first English hit, a cover of "Begin the Beguine," which reached #1 in the UK. The 1984 album *1100 Bel Air Place* established him in the US, selling over three million copies. It featured "To All the Girls I've Loved Before" (duet with Willie Nelson), a US Country #1 and Billboard Hot 100 Top Five hit, and "All of You" (duet with Diana Ross), a Top Twenty Pop hit boosted by a popular music video. The album also included a cover of "The Air That I Breathe" featuring The Beach Boys. He won the 1988 Grammy for Best Latin Pop Album (*Un hombre solo*) and recorded a crossover duet with Stevie Wonder ("My Love") the same year. High-profile collaborations continued with Frank Sinatra ("Summer Wind," 1993) and Dolly Parton, Sting, and Art Garfunkel on *Crazy* (1994).

### Later Career and Business Ventures (2000s–Present)
Iglesias maintained commercial momentum with *Divorcio* (2003), which sold 350,000 copies in Spain on its first day and topped charts in five countries. A subsequent ten-month world tour saw more than half the shows sell out within days. He released *Romantic Classics* (2006), interpreting standards by Foreigner, George Michael, and Richard Marx, recording versions in Filipino and Indonesian for Asian markets; it became his highest Billboard debut (US #31). He invested heavily in the Dominican Republic, acquiring hotel complexes and co-owning Punta Cana International Airport with investors including Oscar de la Renta, becoming a Dominican citizen in 2005. He performed in Equatorial Guinea (2012) and was featured on the *Tinker Tailor Soldier Spy* soundtrack (2011) with a live "La Mer" from 1976.

### Personal Life and Legal Matters
Iglesias married Filipina television host Isabel Preysler in 1971; they had three children—Chábeli, Julio Jr., and Enrique (who became a global superstar)—before divorcing in 1979 (annulled 1980). He later married Dutch model Miranda Rijnsburger in 2010; they have five children. His father died in 2005 at age 90; a week prior, it was revealed his father's 42-year-old wife was pregnant with their second child. A high-profile paternity suit filed by Javier Sánchez (claiming conception in 1975) resulted in a 2019 Valencia court ruling in Sánchez's favor based on resemblance and maternal testimony, despite rejected DNA evidence (sourced from a bottle used by Julio Jr.). The Provincial Court of Valencia reversed this in 2021, and the Supreme Court dismissed the claim in April 2021. In January 2026, two former employees accused him of sexual assault; he denied the allegations, and Spanish prosecutors dropped charges days later citing lack of jurisdiction (alleged incidents occurred in the Dominican Republic and Bahamas).

### Honours and Recognition
Iglesias holds the Gold Medal for Merit in the Fine Arts (Spain) and the Gold Medal of the Community of Madrid. France awarded him the Knight of the Legion of Honour and the Medal of the City of Paris. UNICEF named him Special Ambassador for the Performing Arts (1989). He received a Hollywood Walk of Fame star (1985), the Latin Recording Academy Person of the Year (2001), International Latin Music Hall of Fame induction (2002), and the Lo Nuestro Excellence Award (1995).

## Terms
- **Benidorm Song Festival**: Annual Spanish song contest; Iglesias's 1968 victory with "La vida sigue igual" launched his professional career.
- **Eurovision Song Contest**: International song competition; Iglesias represented Spain in 1970 with "Gwendolyne," finishing fourth.
- **Crossover**: Industry term for an artist successfully reaching a new language or demographic market; Iglesias achieved this in 1984 with *1100 Bel Air Place*.
- **Adult Contemporary (AC)**: Radio format/chart for melodic, mainstream music; Iglesias's "All of You" reached No. 2 on this chart.
- **Latin Songwriters Hall of Fame**: Institution honoring Latin music composers; Iglesias inducted in 2013 as a composer/lyricist.
- **Punta Cana International Airport**: Privately owned airport in the Dominican Republic; Iglesias co-owns it with investors including Oscar de la Renta.
- **Guinness World Records**: Reference book certifying world records; certified Iglesias as Best-selling Male Latin Artist (2013).
- **Lo Nuestro Awards**: Major US Latin music awards; Iglesias won Excellence Award (1995) among others.
- **Real Madrid Castilla**: Reserve team of Real Madrid CF; Iglesias played goalkeeper here in the Segunda División before his accident.
- **Annulment (Catholic Church)**: Declaration that a marriage was canonically invalid; Iglesias and Preysler obtained one in 1980.

## Debates and open questions
- **Paternity of Javier Sánchez**: A decades-long legal battle (1992–2021) centered on whether Iglesias fathered Sánchez during a 1975 encounter. A 2019 trial court ruled yes based on physical resemblance and the mother's testimony, rejecting indirect DNA evidence. Appellate and Supreme Courts ultimately ruled paternity could not be proven.
- **2026 Sexual Assault Allegations**: Two former employees filed criminal complaints in January 2026 alleging assault, harassment, and forced medical testing. Iglesias denied the claims. Spanish prosecutors dropped the case citing lack of jurisdiction over acts alleged to have occurred in the Dominican Republic and Bahamas; the legal resolution in those jurisdictions remains unreported in the source.
- **Jewish Ancestry Claim**: Iglesias states he is Jewish "from the waist up" via his maternal line, citing the surname "de la Cueva" as evidence of *converso* heritage. This genealogical claim is presented as personal belief rather than documented fact.

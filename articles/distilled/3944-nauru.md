# Nauru

## Overview
Nauru is a tiny Pacific island nation whose modern history is defined by the discovery and rapid depletion of phosphate rock. This single resource brought immense wealth but also severe environmental damage and economic collapse, creating a classic "resource curse." Today, Nauru's economy and politics are heavily influenced by its relationship with Australia, particularly through hosting an offshore immigration detention centre.

## Timeline
- **c. 1000 BCE** — First settled by Micronesians.
- **1888** — Annexed by the German Empire, ending a local civil war.
- **1900** — Phosphate discovered; mining begins in 1906.
- **1914** — Occupied by Australia during World War I.
- **1942-1945** — Occupied by Japan during World War II.
- **1968** — Gains independence from Australia.
- **1989** — Sues Australia over environmental damage from mining; settles out of court.
- **2001** — Begins hosting Australian immigration detention centre (the "Pacific Solution").
- **2024** — Severs ties with Taiwan, re-establishing diplomatic relations with China.

## Body

### Colonial Era and Phosphate Mining
First settled by Micronesians, Nauru was annexed by Germany in 1888. Phosphate was discovered in 1900, leading to commercial mining from 1906. After World War I, it became a League of Nations mandate administered by Australia, New Zealand, and the UK, with mining controlled by the British Phosphate Commission. Japan occupied the island during World War II (1942-1945). Post-war, it became a UN trust territory, still administered by Australia.

### Independence and the Resource Curse
Nauru became self-governing in 1966 and fully independent in 1968. The new government purchased the mining assets, and income from phosphate made Nauruans among the world's richest people. However, by the 1990s, viable phosphate reserves were exhausted. Mismanagement of the Nauru Phosphate Royalties Trust, which shrank from A$1.3 billion in 1991 to A$138 million in 2002, led to severe financial crisis. The National Bank of Nauru became insolvent in 2006.

### Politics and Foreign Relations
Nauru is a parliamentary republic where the president is elected by the 19-member parliament. It has no formal political parties; candidates run as independents. Land tenure is unique: all Nauruans have rights to all land, which is owned by individuals and family groups. Foreign policy is often pragmatic; Nauru has switched diplomatic recognition between China and Taiwan for financial aid. It has also recognised breakaway regions like Abkhazia and South Ossetia, reportedly receiving aid from Russia.

### Economy and the Detention Centre
The economy remains dependent on phosphate mining (now by state-owned RONPhos) and Australian aid. A major source of income since 2001 has been the Nauru Regional Processing Centre, an Australian immigration detention facility. Its operations accounted for 35% of GDP in 2017. Nauru has no personal income tax and the government employs 95% of the workforce. In 2025, it announced a "golden passport" scheme to fund climate relocation.

### Geography and Environment
Nauru is a 21 km² coral island with a central plateau. Phosphate mining has devastated about 80% of the land, leaving a barren landscape of limestone pinnacles and contaminating groundwater. The island has no rivers; water is gathered from rain or imported. The climate is hot and humid, with variable rainfall influenced by El Niño.

### Society and Health
The population is about 12,500. The official languages are Nauruan and English. Christianity is the main religion. Nauru faces severe public health challenges: it has the world's highest rates of obesity (71.7% in 2012), type 2 diabetes (over 40% of the population), and tobacco smoking (48.3% in 2022). Life expectancy was 60.6 years for males in 2009.

## Terms
- **Resource curse**: The paradox where countries with abundant natural resources often experience less economic growth, less democracy, and worse development outcomes than countries with fewer resources.
- **Phosphate rock**: A sedimentary rock containing high concentrations of phosphate minerals, used primarily as agricultural fertilizer. Nauru's deposits were near the surface.
- **League of Nations mandate**: A legal status for certain territories administered by member states after World War I, under the supervision of the League.
- **Trust territory**: A non-self-governing territory placed under an administrative authority by the United Nations Trusteeship Council.
- **Client state**: A state that is economically, politically, or militarily dependent on a more powerful state.
- **Matrilineal descent**: A kinship system where lineage and inheritance are traced through the mother's line.
- **Unicameral parliament**: A legislative body with a single chamber or house.
- **Golden passport**: A citizenship-by-investment program where citizenship is granted in exchange for a significant financial contribution.

## Debates and Open questions
- The long-term viability of Nauru's economy post-phosphate is uncertain, with deep-sea mining proposed as a potential but controversial alternative.
- The environmental and human impact of the Australian immigration detention centre remains a point of international criticism and domestic economic dependence.
- The effectiveness of the "golden passport" scheme for funding climate adaptation and population relocation is untested.

# Kingdom of Sicily

## Overview
The Kingdom of Sicily was a major Mediterranean state that existed from 1130 to 1816, encompassing the island of Sicily and, for long periods, the southern Italian Peninsula. Founded by the Norman Roger II, it was a crossroads of Latin, Greek, and Islamic cultures and a significant political and economic power. Its history is marked by dynastic changes, conflicts with the Papacy, and a gradual shift from a centralized Norman-Hohenstaufen state to a divided and eventually Spanish-dominated kingdom, culminating in its merger into the Kingdom of the Two Sicilies.

## Timeline
- **1071** — County of Sicily founded during the Norman conquest of southern Italy.
- **1130** — Roger II proclaimed King of Sicily, creating the Kingdom.
- **1194** — Kingdom falls to the House of Hohenstaufen after a succession crisis.
- **1266** — Charles of Anjou conquers Sicily, ending Hohenstaufen rule.
- **1282** — Sicilian Vespers revolt expels Angevin rule from the island.
- **1302** — Peace of Caltabellotta formally divides the kingdom into an island and a mainland part.
- **1412** — Compromise of Caspe permanently adds Sicily to the Crown of Aragon.
- **1713** — House of Savoy gains Sicily after the War of the Spanish Succession.
- **1720** — Austria exchanges Sardinia for Sicily with Savoy.
- **1735** — Bourbon rule begins with Charles VII of Sicily.
- **1816** — Kingdom of Sicily is merged with the Kingdom of Naples to form the Kingdom of the Two Sicilies.

## Body

### Norman and Hohenstaufen Foundations
The kingdom was established by Roger II in 1130, uniting Norman territories including Sicily, Malta, and parts of southern Italy. Roger II centralized government through laws like the Assizes of Ariano (1140) and made Sicily a leading maritime power. His successors, William I and William II, maintained the kingdom's strength, though its North African possessions were lost. A succession crisis after William II's death led to the Hohenstaufen dynasty taking the throne in 1194. Under Frederick II, the kingdom reached its administrative peak with the Constitutions of Melfi (1231), which created a centralized absolute monarchy and remained the basis of Sicilian law until 1819.

### Division and Foreign Rule
The Sicilian Vespers revolt of 1282, a popular uprising against Angevin rule, led to the permanent division of the kingdom. The island became the Kingdom of Sicily (or Trinacria) under the Crown of Aragon, while the mainland territories became the Kingdom of Naples under the Angevins. Sicily was integrated into the Crown of Aragon in 1412 and later into the Spanish monarchy. Spanish rule strengthened the Catholic Church via the Inquisition but led to economic decline through high taxes and monopolies. After the War of the Spanish Succession, Sicily was briefly held by Savoy (1713-1720) before being transferred to Austria.

### Bourbon Rule and Unification
From 1735, Sicily was ruled by a cadet branch of the Spanish Bourbons. King Charles VII initiated reforms and economic growth. Later, under Ferdinand IV/III, the kingdom was influenced by Britain, especially during the Napoleonic Wars when the British fleet protected the island. British influence led to a liberal constitution in 1812 and the end of feudalism. After Napoleon's defeat, Ferdinand I abolished Sicilian autonomy in 1816, merging it with Naples to form the Kingdom of the Two Sicilies. This sparked revolts in 1820 and 1848-49, which were suppressed, contributing to the instability that led to Italian unification in 1861.

### Society, Economy, and Religion
The kingdom's society evolved from a multicultural Norman realm where Latin, Greek, Muslim, and Jewish communities coexisted, to a more homogenized Catholic society under later rulers. The economy was initially prosperous, based on agriculture (especially wheat) and maritime trade with cities like Genoa and Pisa. However, feudalization under the Angevins and inefficient Spanish trade policies caused long-term decline. The kingdom had a parliament from 1097, which persisted until 1812.

## Terms
- **Assizes of Ariano (1140):** Laws enacted by Roger II to centralize government and regulate feudal relations.
- **Constitutions of Melfi (1231):** A legal code issued by Frederick II that established a centralized, absolute monarchy and reduced feudal power.
- **Sicilian Vespers (1282):** A successful revolt against Angevin rule in Sicily, leading to the island's separation from the mainland.
- **Kingdom of Trinacria:** Name used for the Kingdom of Sicily on the island from 1282 to 1442.
- **Compromise of Caspe (1412):** Agreement that permanently united the Kingdom of Sicily with the Crown of Aragon.
- **Kingdom of the Two Sicilies:** State formed in 1816 by merging the Kingdom of Sicily and the Kingdom of Naples.
- **Salma:** A unit of dry measure for grain in the kingdom, equivalent to about 275 or 300 litres depending on the region.
- **Tari:** The basic gold coin used in the Norman and later periods.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Timing of Economic Decline:** Scholars debate when Sicily's economic decline began. Some argue it started in the late 13th century, while others, like Clifford Backman, contend it began later, during the reign of Frederick III. Stephen Epstein suggests a 15th-century revival occurred.
- **Impact of Foreign Rule:** The long-term effects of Aragonese, Spanish, and Bourbon rule on Sicilian society and economy are debated, particularly regarding the extent of exploitation versus development.
- **Role of the Mafia:** The origins of the Sicilian Mafia are traced to brigand groups formed in the 18th and 19th centuries in response to feudal oppression and state weakness, but its exact evolution is a subject of historical study.

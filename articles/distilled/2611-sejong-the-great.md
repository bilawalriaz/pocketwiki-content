# Sejong the Great

## Overview
Sejong the Great (1397–1450) was the fourth monarch of the Joseon dynasty in Korea. He is widely regarded as the greatest king in Korean history, primarily for creating Hangul, the native Korean alphabet, and for presiding over a golden age of science, technology, and culture. His reign solidified Confucian governance and expanded Korea's borders and influence.

## Timeline
- **May 15, 1397** — Born as Yi To, third son of the future King Taejong.
- **June 1418** — Deposed as heir, Grand Prince Yangnyŏng, and made crown prince.
- **August 1418** — Ascends the throne after Taejong's abdication.
- **1419** — Launches the Ōei Invasion against Japanese pirates on Tsushima Island.
- **1420** — Reestablishes the Hall of Worthies, a royal research institute.
- **1433** — Creates the first native Korean calendar, Ch'ilchŏngsan.
- **1434** — Compiles the 56-volume medical text *Hyangyakchipsŏngbang*.
- **1443** — Reveals the newly created Hangul alphabet to his court.
- **1444** — Completes the massive 365-volume medical text *Ŭibangyuch'wi*.
- **1446** — Promulgates Hangul with the publication of *Hunminjeongeum*.
- **1450** — Dies at age 52; succeeded by his son, King Munjong.

## Body

### Rise to Power
Sejong was born the third son of Grand Prince Chŏngan (later King Taejong). He was considered more gifted than his elder brother, the heir apparent Grand Prince Yangnyŏng, who had behavioral issues. After a series of scandals and a failed test of leadership by Taejong, Yangnyŏng was deposed in June 1418. Sejong was named crown prince and became king two months later when Taejong abdicated. Taejong remained influential as king emeritus until his death in 1422.

### Governance and Domestic Policy
Sejong governed through Confucian principles, emphasizing deliberation and debate. He established the Hall of Worthies as a think tank to advise on policy and conduct research. He reformed the tax system, lowering the land tax rate from 10% to 5% through the *kongpŏp* system, which improved peasant welfare without harming state revenue due to increased agricultural productivity. He revised legal codes, improved prison conditions, and attempted to elevate marginalized social groups like the *ch'ŏnmin*. While promoting Confucianism, he privately practiced Buddhism, creating tension with his court.

### Science, Technology, and Culture
Sejong's reign was a "golden age" for Korean science. He oversaw the creation of the first native calendar (*Ch'ilchŏngsan*), the rain gauge (*ch'ŭgugi*), and advanced astronomical instruments. He promoted mathematics and medicine, commissioning major texts like *Ŭibangyuch'wi*. Innovations in movable type printing, agriculture (*Nongsa chiksŏl*), and cartography flourished. Culturally, he reformed court music, sponsored art, and composed music himself.

### Creation of Hangul
Before Hangul, Korea used complex Chinese characters (*Hanja*), limiting literacy to the elite. Sejong, with a personal interest in linguistics, developed Hangul to promote universal literacy. The alphabet was revealed in 1443 and published in 1446 with the *Hunminjeongeum*. It faced immediate backlash from Confucian scholars who saw it as a departure from Chinese civilization. Despite this, Sejong promoted its use, though it was not officially adopted by the government until 1894.

### Foreign Policy
Sejong expanded Korea's northern borders to their approximate current extent through military campaigns and assimilation policies toward Jurchen tribes. He managed relations with Ming China by balancing Confucian deference (*sadae*) with assertions of Korean autonomy. With Japan, he used a combination of punitive action (the 1419 Ōei Invasion) and managed trade to reduce piracy and increase peaceful contact, culminating in the 1443 Treaty of Kyehae.

### Later Life and Death
Sejong suffered from recurring health issues, likely diabetes or ankylosing spondylitis, which worsened in his later years. Beginning in 1445, he delegated daily governance to Crown Prince Munjong. He died in 1450 at age 52, the first Joseon king to die in office.

## Terms
- **Hangul**: The native alphabet of the Korean language, created by Sejong in 1443 to promote literacy.
- **Hall of Worthies**: A royal research institute and think tank established by Sejong in 1420.
- **Ch'ilchŏngsan**: The first indigenous Korean calendar, developed in 1444.
- **Ŭibangyuch'wi**: A massive 365-volume medical encyclopedia completed in 1445.
- **Kongpŏp**: A reformed tax system implemented by Sejong that lowered the land tax rate.
- **Sadae**: The Confucian policy of deference to Ming China, which Sejong balanced with Korean autonomy.
- **Hyanghwa**: A status for foreigners allowing them to reside in Korea with certain exemptions, encouraging assimilation.
- **Hunminjeongeum**: The document published in 1446 that introduced and explained the Hangul alphabet.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Extent of Sejong's Role in Hangul**: While contemporary evidence credits Sejong, some scholars debate whether he was the sole author or primarily a patron, given his busy schedule.
- **Health Diagnosis**: Historians and medical researchers debate whether Sejong's symptoms indicate type 1 or type 2 diabetes, or ankylosing spondylitis.
- **Effectiveness of Northern Policies**: Historians have mixed evaluations of Sejong's efforts to pacify the northern frontier, noting that Korean settlers often struggled and the region remained problematic.
- **North Korean View**: North Korean texts reportedly evaluate Sejong skeptically as a feudal oppressor, downplaying his achievements and attributing Hangul's development to the masses.

# Arequipa

## Overview
Arequipa is Peru's second most populous city and a major economic, cultural, and political center in the south. Known as the "Legal Capital of Peru," it serves as the seat of the Constitutional Court and has historically acted as a counterweight to Lima's centralized power. Its historic center, built from white volcanic stone (*sillar*), is a UNESCO World Heritage Site, and the city is a key industrial and commercial hub with strong regional identity.

## Timeline
- **15 August 1540** — City founded as "Villa Hermosa de Nuestra Señora de la Asunta de Arequipa."
- **25 September 1541** — Elevated to city status by royal decree of Charles V.
- **1612** — Diocese of Arequipa established by papal bull.
- **1784** — Major earthquake destroys much of the city, leading to reconstruction in *sillar*.
- **1805** — Granted the title "Most Faithful" by Charles IV for loyalty to the Spanish Crown.
- **13 January 1835** — President Luis José de Orbegoso installs his government in Arequipa.
- **1868** — Earthquake causes enormous damage; city described as "probably no other inland city of South America was so well built before the earthquake."
- **31 August 1882** — President Lizardo Montero declares Arequipa the capital of Peru during the War of the Pacific.
- **10 January 1966** — Arequipa Industrial Park established by law.
- **2000** — UNESCO inscribes 332 hectares of the historic center as a World Heritage Site.
- **23 June 2001** — 8.4-magnitude earthquake damages historic buildings.
- **2019** — Arequipa included in UNESCO Creative Cities Network for gastronomy.

## Body

### Geography and Climate
Arequipa lies in the Andes at 2,328 m above sea level, in the Chili River valley. The valley is bordered by mountains to the north and east and coastal hills to the south and west, with the volcanoes Misti, Chachani, and Pichu Pichu visible from the city. It has a temperate arid climate (Köppen BWk) with an average annual temperature of 14.5 °C and low precipitation (75-100 mm annually). The city experiences high solar radiation and is described as a "cultural and natural oasis" in an arid landscape.

### History and Political Role
The site was inhabited by nomadic groups and later settled agricultural communities before Inca expansion. The Inca established *mitma* (relocated) groups for control but did not found a city in the Hispanic sense. The Spanish foundation in 1540 created a planned grid city. During the viceregal period, Arequipa gained importance through trade and loyalty to the Crown, earning titles like "Most Faithful."

After independence, Arequipa became a center of political opposition and liberalism, frequently challenging Lima's centralism. It was the scene of numerous uprisings, earning the nickname "Lion of the South." The city twice served as the seat of the national government (1835 and 1882). Historian Jorge Basadre described it as "the pistol aimed at the heart of Lima." In 1978, it was chosen as the seat of the Constitutional Court, a role it holds today.

### Economy
Arequipa is Peru's second most industrialized city. The Department of Arequipa had a nominal GDP of S/31.458 billion in 2024. Industrial activity includes manufacturing, textiles from camelid wool, and mining (copper and molybdenum concentrates). The city is a commercial hub with links to Chile, Bolivia, and Brazil, connected by the Southern Railway. Tourism is significant; in 2022, it received over 1.5 million visitors. The labor market is the largest in Peru's interior, with major activity in commerce, services, and manufacturing.

### Architecture and Urban Form
The historic center is characterized by its use of *sillar*, a white volcanic stone that became the primary building material after the 1582 earthquake, providing seismic resistance. The architecture blends Spanish and Indigenous features, forming the "Arequipan School." Key monuments include the Cathedral, the Monastery of Santa Catalina, and numerous colonial *casonas* (mansions). The city's layout follows a foundational grid from 1540. Urban expansion accelerated in the 20th century, especially after earthquakes in 1958 and 1960, leading to peripheral growth and the "tertiarization" of the city center.

### Culture and Identity
Arequipa has a strong regional identity, sometimes called a "Spanish island." The local Spanish dialect features *voseo* (use of "vos" instead of "tú") and Quechua-origin suffixes. A distinctive rural dialect is known as "Loncco speech." The city is renowned for its gastronomy, with over 500 documented dishes, and was named a UNESCO Creative City for Gastronomy in 2019. Cultural life includes numerous museums, theatres, and a vibrant literary tradition that produced Nobel laureate Mario Vargas Llosa.

## Terms
- **Sillar**: A white or pink volcanic stone used extensively in Arequipa's architecture for its seismic-resistant properties.
- **Voseo**: A linguistic feature where the pronoun "vos" is used instead of "tú" for the second-person singular, common in Arequipan Spanish.
- **Loncco**: A term referring to the rural farmers and their distinctive dialect and cultural traditions around Arequipa.
- **Fidelismo**: The historical loyalty of Arequipa to the Spanish Crown during the colonial and early independence periods.
- **Mitma (Mitmaes)**: Inca policy of forcibly relocating populations to new areas for administrative control and cultural integration.
- **Picantería**: A traditional Arequipan eatery, recognized as Cultural Heritage of the Nation, serving local cuisine and reflecting social traditions.
- **Arequipan School**: An architectural style blending Spanish and Indigenous features, prominent in the city's viceregal and republican religious and civil buildings.
- **Cabildo**: The municipal council or town hall governing a Spanish colonial city.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Etymology of "Arequipa"**: Multiple hypotheses exist, including derivations from Puquina, Quechua ("Yes, stay here"), or Aymara ("behind the peak"), with no definitive consensus.
- **Inca Founding**: Chronicles conflict on whether the Inca founded a town in the Chili valley; archaeological evidence suggests only the establishment of *mitma* groups, not a formal town.
- **First Declaration of Independence**: Historians debate whether the first act of Peruvian independence was signed in Supe, Ica, or elsewhere, with most agreeing on the cabildo of Supe in April 1820.
- **Political Role**: Scholars analyze whether Arequipa's numerous 19th-century uprisings were driven by principled defense of law and regionalism or by the interests of local elites.

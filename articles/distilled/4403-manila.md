# Manila

## Overview
Manila, the capital of the Philippines, is a historic port city on Manila Bay. It was a crucial hub in the first global trade network via the Manila–Acapulco galleon and has been the nation's political, economic, and cultural center for centuries, despite facing significant destruction in World War II and ongoing challenges like extreme population density and natural hazards.

## Timeline
- **c. 3000 BC** — Earliest evidence of human life (Angono Petroglyphs) near present-day Manila.
- **By 1258** — A Tagalog-fortified polity called Maynila exists on the site.
- **June 24, 1571** — Spanish conquistador Miguel López de Legazpi establishes the city and begins building Intramuros.
- **1571–1898** — Manila serves as the capital of the Spanish East Indies and a key node in the galleon trade.
- **1762–1764** — British forces occupy Manila during the Seven Years' War.
- **July 31, 1901** — Under American rule, Manila becomes the first chartered city in the Philippines.
- **1905** — Daniel Burnham's master plan for the city is commissioned.
- **January 2, 1942** — Japanese forces occupy Manila.
- **February 3–March 3, 1945** — The Battle of Manila results in massive civilian casualties and citywide destruction.
- **June 18, 1949** — Manila becomes an autonomous city via its Revised Charter.
- **November 7, 1975** — Metropolitan Manila is created as an integrated regional unit.
- **February 22–25, 1986** — The People Power Revolution, centered on EDSA, leads to the ouster of Ferdinand Marcos.

## Body

### Etymology and Early History
The name "Manila" derives from the Filipino term *Maynilà*, likely meaning "where indigo plants are abundant" (*may nilà*) or "where nilad plants are abundant" (*may nilad*). The earliest evidence of human life nearby is the Angono Petroglyphs (c. 3000 BC). By the 13th century, Manila was a fortified settlement and trading port. It was influenced by the Hindu Brunei empire and later converted to Islam through ties with the Sultanate of Brunei.

### Spanish Colonial Era (1571–1898)
After defeating the last indigenous ruler, Rajah Sulayman, in 1571, the Spanish established Manila as the capital of the Spanish East Indies. They built the walled city of Intramuros and developed the Manila–Acapulco galleon trade, which connected Asia with the Americas and Europe, making Manila one of the world's first global cities. The city's wealth and diverse population attracted people from across Asia, Europe, and the Americas. Direct Spanish rule began after Mexico's independence in 1821, leading to economic growth and the rise of an educated class (*ilustrados*) that fueled the Philippine Revolution.

### American Period and World War II
Following the 1898 Battle of Manila, the United States took control. Manila was chartered in 1901, and American city planner Daniel Burnham designed a modern capital. During World War II, the city was occupied by Japan from 1942. The 1945 Battle of Manila was one of the war's bloodiest, killing about 100,000 civilians and leaving the city in ruins.

### Postwar to Contemporary Era
Reconstruction followed the war, and in 1948, the national government seat moved to Quezon City. Manila experienced a "Golden Age" in the 1950s. In 1975, President Ferdinand Marcos created Metropolitan Manila and later reinstated Manila as the capital. The city was a focal point of protests during the Martial Law era, culminating in the 1986 People Power Revolution that ousted Marcos. In recent decades, Manila has faced challenges like traffic congestion, pollution, and natural disaster risks, while also undergoing urban renewal and maintaining its role as a major economic and cultural center.

## Terms
- **Galleon Trade:** The system of Spanish sailing ships that traded goods between Manila and Acapulco, Mexico, from 1565 to 1815.
- **Intramuros:** The historic "Walled City" of Manila, built by the Spanish in the 16th century as the seat of government and religion.
- **Ilustrados:** The educated, reform-minded class of Filipinos during the Spanish colonial period who advocated for change.
- **Barangay:** The smallest administrative division in the Philippines, a native term for a village or district.
- **Highly Urbanized City (HUC):** A city classification in the Philippines with a large population and high income, independent from provinces.
- **Earthquake Baroque:** A Spanish colonial architectural style featuring thick walls and buttresses to withstand earthquakes.
- **Jeepney:** A popular, brightly colored public utility vehicle in the Philippines, adapted from U.S. Army jeeps.
- **Metro Manila:** The officially defined metropolitan area and capital region of the Philippines, comprising Manila and surrounding cities.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The exact etymology of "Manila" is debated, with competing theories favoring "may nilà" (indigo) or "may nilad" (a mangrove plant).
- The demographic composition of historical Manila is complex, with studies showing varying estimates of European, Chinese, and mixed ancestry in the population.
- The future of Manila Bay is a subject of concern, with debates over the environmental and social impacts of large-scale reclamation projects versus the need for urban development and flood protection.

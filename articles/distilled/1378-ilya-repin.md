# Ilya Repin

## Overview

Ilya Yefimovich Repin (1844–1930) was a Ukrainian-born Russian painter who became one of the most renowned artists in Russia in the 19th century. He is best known for large-scale historical and genre paintings such as *Barge Haulers on the Volga* (1873), *Religious Procession in Kursk Province* (1880–1883), *Ivan the Terrible and His Son Ivan* (1885), and *Reply of the Zaporozhian Cossacks* (1880–1891). He was also celebrated for revealing portraits of leading Russian literary and artistic figures, especially Leo Tolstoy, with whom he had a long friendship. Repin was the first Russian artist to achieve European fame using specifically Russian themes, and his work made him the leader of a new movement of critical realism in Russian art.

## Body

### Early life and training

Repin was born on 5 August [O.S. 24 July] 1844 in Chuguev, a provincial town in the Kharkov Governorate of the Russian Empire. His father, Yefim Vasilyevich Repin (1804–1894), was a military settler who served in an Uhlan regiment, fought in the Russo-Persian War of 1826–1828, the Russo-Turkish War of 1828–1829, and the Hungarian campaign of 1849, then became an itinerant horse merchant. Although some sources claimed Cossack or Ukrainian ancestry, Repin had none; his ancestors were ethnic Russians who served in the streltsy (early Russian military corps) and were sent to Chuguev to assist local Cossacks. He identified as a Russian born in Little Russia, the name applied to Ukraine at the time, yet felt affinity with both Cossacks and Ukrainians.

At sixteen, Repin's skill was recognized and he joined an artel, a cooperative of artists, traveling around Voronezh province to paint icons and wall paintings. In October 1863 he competed for admission to the Imperial Academy of Arts in Saint Petersburg, failed, but persevered, rented a small room, and took courses. In January 1864 he succeeded and attended classes without fee. At the academy he met Ivan Kramskoi, who became his professor and mentor. In 1869 he won a second-class gold medal for *Job and His Brothers*.

### First success and Paris

In 1870 Repin traveled to the Volga River to sketch barge haulers. His drawings won him a commission from Grand Duke Vladimir Alexandrovich for a large-scale painting, *Barge Haulers on the Volga*, completed in 1873. The following year he was awarded a first-class gold medal for *The Resurrection of the Daughter of Jairus*. In May 1872 he married Vera Alexeievna Shevtsova; the marriage lasted fifteen years and produced four children, but was difficult due to Repin's affairs.

*Barge Haulers on the Volga*, shown at the Vienna International Exposition, brought him international attention and a grant from the Academy of Fine Arts. He spent two years in Paris, where he saw the First Impressionist Exhibition in 1874. He admired some impressionist techniques, especially depictions of light and color, but felt their work lacked moral or social purpose. He spent two months at Veules-les-Roses in Normandy painting landscapes in the open air. His major Russian work created in Paris was *Sadko* (1876), a mystical allegory of an undersea kingdom, which won him a place in the Russian Academy of Fine Arts.

### Moscow, the Wanderers, and major works

Repin returned to Russia in 1876 and moved to Moscow, where he became involved with the "Wanderers," an artistic movement founded in 1863 that was resolutely realistic, patriotic, and politically engaged, determined to break with classical models and create a specifically Russian art. He produced major historical works including *Religious Procession in Kursk Governorate* (1883), notable for its extraordinary crowd of realistic figures, and *Ivan the Terrible and His Son Ivan* (1885), depicting the tsar just after killing his son with his sceptre in a demented rage. The painting caused a scandal; some critics saw it as veiled criticism of Tsar Alexander III, who had brutally suppressed opposition after a failed assassination attempt. It was vandalised twice and removed from view at the tsar's request, though the tsar later reconsidered and it was put back on view.

*They Did Not Expect Him* (1884–1888) depicts a former narodnik, or revolutionary, emaciated from prison and exile, returning unexpectedly to his family, the story told through family expressions and small details.

### Repin and Tolstoy

In 1880, Leo Tolstoy came to Repin's studio to introduce himself, beginning a thirty-year friendship until Tolstoy's death in 1910. Repin regularly visited Tolstoy at his Moscow residence and his country estate at Yasnaya Polyana, painting a series of portraits of Tolstoy in peasant dress, working and reading under a tree. His last trip to see Tolstoy was in 1907. Repin's portraits of Tolstoy in country dress were widely exhibited and helped build Tolstoy's legendary image.

### Portraits of composers and the Cossacks

Repin painted portraits of major Russian composers, including Modest Mussorgsky, whom he painted in four sittings beginning four days before the composer's death from alcoholism; the proceeds from the painting's sale erected a monument to Mussorgsky. His portrait of Mikhail Glinka was painted after Glinka's death, based on drawings and recollections of others. Other subjects included Alexander Glazunov and Anton Rubinstein.

In 1888 Repin traveled to southern Russia and the Caucasus to study descendants of the Zaporozhian Cossacks. This gave him material for his most famous historical work, *Reply of the Zaporozhian Cossacks*, depicting an apocryphal 1678 event when Cossacks supposedly drafted an insulting letter to the Turkish sultan, addressing him as "The Grand Imbecile." Repin worked on it periodically between 1880 and 1891. Most models were faculty members from the Academy of Arts of various nationalities. The finished work was so popular he painted a second version.

### Finland, revolution, and later years

In 1890 Repin met Natalia Nordman (1863–1914), a writer and feminist who became his common-law wife. In 1899 they built "The Penates" ('Penaty' in Russian), a country house in Kuokkala, Viipuri Province, Finland, about an hour by train from St. Petersburg. After he resigned from the Academy of Fine Arts in 1907, it became his full-time home and studio. He hosted vegetarian breakfasts adapted from Tolstoy and elaborate Wednesday receptions for figures including Chaliapin, Gorky, and Mayakovsky.

The repression of popular demonstrations in 1905 disillusioned Repin; he called it "the year of disaster and shame," resigned his teaching post, and joined the Constitutional Democratic Party. He was offered the rank of Councillor of State and invited to take a seat in the Duma, the national assembly. In 1908 he publicly denounced capital punishment in Russia.

After the October 1917 Bolshevik Revolution, Finland declared independence and the border closed. Repin refused to return to Russia, turning to Finnish clients and painting a group portrait of notable Finnish leaders including Eliel Saarinen, Jean Sibelius, and Carl Gustav Mannerheim. He welcomed the February Revolution but was hostile to the Bolsheviks and appalled by their violence and terror. He even objected to the Soviet spelling reform that changed his name from "Рѣпинъ" to "Репин," as the elimination of the letter "ѣ" led many to misspell his name as Ryopin. Stalin sent a delegation including former student Isaak Brodsky to persuade him to return, but Repin refused. His final work, *The Hopak Dance*, begun in 1926, portrayed his admiration of Ukraine; he painted it in oil on linoleum because he could not get a large enough canvas. Repin died in 1930 and was buried at The Penates. After the Winter War in 1939, Kuokkala was annexed by the Soviet Union, and in 1948 renamed Repino in his honor. The Penates became a museum in 1940 and is now a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

### Style and legacy

Repin produced more than three hundred portraits, preceding each with six or seven sketches. He painted most notable political figures, writers, and composers of his time; one exception was Dostoevsky, whose mysticism he did not appreciate. For some paintings he made one hundred or more preliminary sketches. His method was the reverse of impressionism: he produced works slowly and carefully, was never satisfied, and often painted multiple versions years apart. His style owed something to Édouard Manet and Diego Velázquez. His 1873 *Barge Haulers on the Volga*, radically different from previous Russian paintings, made him the leader of critical realism in Russian art, praised by contemporaries like Vladimir Stasov and Fyodor Dostoyevsky.

## Terms

- **Wanderers**: An artistic movement founded in 1863, resolutely realistic, patriotic, and politically engaged, determined to create a specifically Russian art.
- **Artel**: A cooperative of artists; Repin joined one at age sixteen to paint icons and wall paintings.
- **Narodnik**: A revolutionary; the returning figure in *They Did Not Expect Him*.
- **Streltsy**: Early Russian military corps; Repin's ancestors served in them.
- **Little Russia**: The name applied to Ukraine at the time of Repin's birth.
- **Critical realism**: The artistic movement Repin led, choosing nature and character over academic formalism.
- **Duma**: The national assembly; Repin was invited to take a seat in it.
- **The Penates ('Penaty')**: Repin's country house in Kuokkala, Finland, now a museum and UNESCO World Heritage Site.

## Debates and open questions

- Some sources referred to Repin as having Cossack or Ukrainian ancestry, but he had none; his ancestors were ethnic Russians sent to Chuguev to assist local Cossacks. Despite this, he felt affinity with both Cossacks and Ukrainians.
- *Ivan the Terrible and His Son Ivan* was controversial: some critics saw it as veiled criticism of Tsar Alexander III, while the more aesthetic faction of the Wanderers considered it overly sensationalist. It was vandalised twice and removed from view at the tsar's request before being restored.
- *Reply of the Zaporozhian Cossacks* depicts an apocryphal event in 1678, when Cossacks supposedly drafted an insulting letter to the Turkish sultan; the historical accuracy of the event is not established in the source.

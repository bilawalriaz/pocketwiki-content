# Vienna

## Overview
Vienna is the capital and most populous city of Austria, serving as the country's cultural, economic, and political center. With a history spanning from a Roman military camp to a major European capital, it is renowned for its musical heritage, imperial architecture, and role as a host for international organizations.

## Timeline
- **1st century AD** — Romans found the military camp Vindobona.
- **1155** — Vienna becomes the seat of the Babenberg dynasty.
- **1221** — Vienna is granted city rights.
- **1529 & 1683** — Vienna successfully defends against Ottoman sieges.
- **1804** — Vienna becomes the capital of the Austrian Empire.
- **1814–15** — The Congress of Vienna is held in the city.
- **1918** — Vienna becomes the capital of the Republic of German-Austria.
- **1919–1934** — The period of "Red Vienna" sees major socialist municipal reforms.
- **1938** — Adolf Hitler announces the Anschluss (annexation of Austria) in Vienna.
- **1945** — Vienna is liberated by Soviet forces and divided into four occupation zones.
- **1955** — The Austrian State Treaty ends four-power occupation.
- **2001** — Vienna's historic center is designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

## Body

### Historical Development
The city originated as the Roman legionary camp Vindobona in the 1st century. After a period of Slavic and Avar influence, it became the permanent seat of the Babenberg dynasty in 1155 and was granted city rights in 1221. The Habsburgs later established Vienna as the seat of Holy Roman Emperors in the 16th century, a role it held until 1806. Following the Napoleonic Wars, it became the capital of the Austrian Empire and later the Austro-Hungarian Empire. The 20th century brought major upheavals, including the brief socialist experiment of "Red Vienna" (1919–1934), the trauma of the Nazi Anschluss and World War II, and a decade of four-power occupation after the war. Sovereignty was restored with the Austrian State Treaty in 1955.

### Culture and Society
Vienna is a global city of music, having been home to composers from Mozart and Beethoven to Mahler and Strauss. It was also a cradle of modernism, hosting figures like Sigmund Freud and the Vienna Secession art movement. The city's demographic history reflects its imperial past and 20th-century crises; its population peaked at over two million in 1910, declined after the World Wars and the Holocaust, and has grown again through migration. As of 2021, 34.1% of residents had no religious affiliation, 31.8% were Catholic, and 14.8% were Muslim.

### Geography and Infrastructure
Vienna lies on the Danube River at the eastern edge of the Alps. Its climate is humid subtropical. The city is divided into 23 districts and is known for its extensive public transport network, including a U-Bahn (metro), trams, and buses. Major green spaces include the Prater park, the Donauinsel (Danube Island), and the grounds of Schönbrunn Palace. The Danube has been extensively regulated for flood protection, notably through the creation of the New Danube and Donauinsel in the 1970s–80s.

### Economy and International Role
Vienna generates 25.1% of Austria's GDP and is a hub for international organizations, hosting the UN, OPEC, and the OSCE. It is a major center for conferences and tourism, with 17.3 million overnight stays in 2023. The city's economy is strong in services, life sciences, and information technology.

## Terms
- **Anschluss**: The annexation of Austria into Nazi Germany in 1938.
- **Babenbergs**: The first ruling dynasty of Austria, who made Vienna their seat in 1155.
- **Gemeindebauten**: Public housing complexes, many built during the "Red Vienna" period.
- **Heuriger**: A tavern where local winemakers sell their new wine.
- **Habsburgs**: The dynasty that ruled Austria and the Holy Roman Empire for centuries.
- **Red Vienna (Das rote Wien)**: The period from 1919 to 1934 when Vienna was governed by socialist politicians implementing ambitious social reforms.
- **Ringstrasse**: A grand boulevard developed in the late 19th century, replacing the old city walls.
- **Wiener Schnitzel**: A traditional Viennese dish of a breaded and fried veal cutlet.
- **Wienerlied**: A traditional song genre from Vienna, sung in the local dialect.

## Debates and Open Questions
The source notes that the etymology of the name "Vienna" (from the River Wien) is distinct from the Roman name "Vindobona" and is of Celtic origin, possibly meaning "forest river." The exact derivation of the Hungarian name "Bécs" is debated, with theories linking it to words for "kiln" or "fortified place." The population figures for Vienna's Jewish community vary between sources, with estimates of 8,000 or 15,000 members.

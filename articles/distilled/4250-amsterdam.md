# Amsterdam

## Overview
Amsterdam is the capital and largest city of the Netherlands, founded at the mouth of the Amstel river. It grew from a small fishing village into a major global port and financial center during the 17th-century Dutch Golden Age. The city is renowned for its historic canal ring, a UNESCO World Heritage Site, its cultural institutions, and a long tradition of tolerance and liberalism.

## Timeline
- **c. 1275** — First documented mention of Amsterdam ("apud Amestelledamme").
- **1345** — The Mirakel van Amsterdam makes the city a major pilgrimage site.
- **1602** — The Amsterdam Stock Exchange, the world's first, is founded by the Dutch East India Company (VOC).
- **1609** — The Bank of Amsterdam begins operations.
- **1613** — Construction of the concentric canal ring (Grachtengordel) begins.
- **1750** — Amsterdam is the fourth-largest city in Western Europe.
- **1815** — The United Kingdom of the Netherlands is established, marking a turning point after decline.
- **1940-1945** — Nazi occupation; over 100,000 Dutch Jews are deported and murdered.
- **1945** — Amsterdam is liberated by Canadian forces on 5 May.
- **1977** — The first metro line opens.
- **2010** — The Grachtengordel is added to the UNESCO World Heritage List.

## Body

### Founding and Early Growth
Amsterdam's origins are linked to a dam built on the Amstel river between 1264 and 1275. The All Saint's Flood of 1170, which turned the IJ into a wide estuary, made the Amstel mouth attractive for permanent settlement. The city's first documented mention is a 1275 toll privilege from Count Floris V. It received city rights in 1300 or 1306. Growth accelerated from the 15th century, fueled by Baltic trade in grain and timber and innovations in the herring fishery, which established the foundations for its later transcontinental trade system.

### Golden Age and Decline
During the 17th-century Dutch Golden Age, Amsterdam became the wealthiest city in the Western world and a leading financial and trade hub. Its merchants dominated the VOC and Dutch West India Company, creating a global trading network. The city was also involved in the Atlantic slave trade until 1814. Prosperity declined in the 18th and early 19th centuries due to wars. A second Golden Age began in the late 19th century with industrialization and major infrastructure projects like the Amsterdam–Rhine Canal.

### 20th Century to Present
The 20th century saw expansion, food shortages during World War I, and severe trauma during the Nazi occupation, including the deportation of most of its Jewish population. Post-war reconstruction involved building new suburbs and, later, controversial urban renewal that sparked the Nieuwmarkt riots. Since the 2010s, the city has faced challenges from mass tourism, rising real estate prices, and gentrification. Immigration has made it one of the world's most multicultural cities, with about 180 nationalities represented.

### Geography and Climate
Amsterdam is located in North Holland, with its center at about −2 m (−6.6 ft) below sea level. The city is defined by its water, with over 100 km of canals, most notably the three main concentric canals: Herengracht, Keizersgracht, and Prinsengracht. It has an oceanic climate, with moderately warm summers and cool, damp winters.

### Economy and Transport
Amsterdam is the financial and business capital of the Netherlands, home to the Amsterdam Stock Exchange (AEX) and headquarters of major corporations. The Port of Amsterdam is the fourth-largest in Europe. Schiphol Airport is the Netherlands' busiest and one of Europe's largest. The city is a major tourist destination and is famous for its cycling culture, with more bicycles than residents.

### Culture and Society
Amsterdam's cultural life is anchored by world-class museums like the Rijksmuseum and Van Gogh Museum, the Concertgebouw concert hall, and a vibrant performing arts scene. The city is known for its nightlife, festivals like King's Day, and the historic De Wallen red-light district. It has a long history of religious tolerance, hosting diverse communities since the 17th century. Today, it is a secular city where Christianity and Islam are the largest religious groups.

## Terms
- **Grachtengordel**: The concentric canal ring in Amsterdam's city center, built in the 17th century.
- **Dutch Golden Age**: The 17th century, when the Netherlands was a leading economic, scientific, and artistic power.
- **VOC (Dutch East India Company)**: The world's first publicly traded company, established in 1602 to trade with Asia.
- **Polder**: Low-lying land reclaimed from the sea or a river, protected by dikes.
- **Stadsdelen**: Semi-autonomous boroughs or districts into which Amsterdam is divided for local governance.
- **Kleinkunst**: A Dutch cabaret tradition combining music, storytelling, and comedy.
- **AEX**: The Amsterdam Exchange, part of Euronext and the world's oldest stock exchange.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The exact date of Amsterdam's founding dam is historically estimated between 1264 and 1275, but not precisely known.
- The original intentions behind the 17th-century canal layout are speculated by historians to have been practical and defensive rather than ornamental, as the original plans are lost.
- The city faces ongoing challenges balancing its appeal to tourists with the livability and affordability for its residents, a situation compared to Venice.

# Cambodia

## Overview
Cambodia is a Southeast Asian nation with a rich history centered on the Khmer Empire, which dominated the region from the 9th to 15th centuries. Its modern history is marked by French colonization, the devastating Khmer Rouge regime (1975-1979), and a subsequent period of recovery and political consolidation under long-time Prime Minister Hun Sen. Today, it is a constitutional monarchy facing challenges of corruption, human rights issues, and environmental degradation, while its economy relies on agriculture, textiles, and tourism.

## Timeline
- **c. 802 AD** — Jayavarman II declares himself king, founding the Khmer Empire.
- **15th century** — The Khmer Empire declines; Angkor is abandoned in 1432.
- **1863** — Cambodia becomes a French protectorate.
- **1953** — Cambodia gains independence from France.
- **1970** — A coup led by Lon Nol establishes the U.S.-aligned Khmer Republic.
- **1975** — The Khmer Rouge, led by Pol Pot, takes power.
- **1975-1979** — The Cambodian genocide occurs under the Khmer Rouge.
- **1979** — Vietnam invades Cambodia, ousting the Khmer Rouge.
- **1991** — The Paris Peace Accords are signed, ending long-term conflict.
- **1993** — A UN-backed election restores the monarchy.
- **1997** — Hun Sen consolidates power in a coup d'état.
- **2023** — Hun Manet succeeds his father, Hun Sen, as Prime Minister.

## Body

### Etymology and Prehistory
The name "Cambodia" derives from the Sanskrit *Kambojadeśa*, meaning "land of the Kamboja," linked to the legendary sage Kambu. Archaeological evidence shows human occupation from the Pleistocene era, with hunter-gatherer communities and the earliest earthenware ceramics dating to around 6000 BC. Rice farming penetrated the region in the third millennium BC, and Iron Age settlements emerged by about 500 BC, indicating trade networks and social organization.

### The Khmer Empire and its Decline
The Khmer Empire, which grew out of the earlier Indianized states of Funan and Chenla, was formally established in 802 AD. It became Southeast Asia's largest empire in the 12th century, with its capital at Angkor, a vast urban complex. The empire facilitated the spread of Hinduism and later Buddhism. After a series of wars, Angkor was sacked and abandoned in 1432. Subsequent centuries saw the Khmer monarchy weakened by rivalry and domination from the Siamese (Thai) and Vietnamese.

### Colonialism and Modern Conflict
France established a protectorate in 1863, ruling until Cambodia's independence in 1953. The Vietnam War era led to civil war, culminating in a 1970 coup. The Khmer Rouge regime (1975-1979) carried out a genocide that killed an estimated 1 to 3 million people. The regime was ousted by a Vietnamese invasion, leading to a decade of occupation. Peace was brokered by the 1991 Paris Peace Accords, which established a UN transitional authority and led to a 1993 election restoring the monarchy.

### Politics and Governance
Cambodia is a constitutional monarchy and de jure multi-party state, but the Cambodian People's Party (CPP) has dominated politics since the 1997 coup led by Hun Sen. The government has been criticized for authoritarianism, human rights abuses, and corruption. Hun Sen, one of the world's longest-serving leaders, was succeeded as Prime Minister by his son, Hun Manet, in August 2023, though he remains influential as CPP leader and Senate President.

### Geography and Environment
Cambodia's landscape is dominated by a central plain, the Mekong River, and the Tonle Sap, Southeast Asia's largest lake. The climate is tropical monsoon. The country is highly vulnerable to climate change. Biodiversity is significant but threatened by one of the world's highest deforestation rates; primary forest cover fell from over 70% in 1969 to just 3.1% in 2007. Upstream dams on the Mekong pose a danger to fisheries and food security.

### Economy and Society
Agriculture is the dominant economic sector, but growth is driven by textiles, construction, and tourism. The garment industry accounts for 80% of exports. Tourism, centered on Angkor Wat, is a major source of revenue. The population is predominantly ethnic Khmer (95.8%), with Theravada Buddhism practiced by over 95%. Challenges include widespread corruption, land grabbing, and a legacy of unexploded landmines.

## Terms
- **Khmer Rouge**: The communist regime that ruled Cambodia from 1975 to 1979, responsible for the Cambodian genocide.
- **Angkor Wat**: A vast temple complex in Cambodia, originally Hindu and later Buddhist, built by the Khmer Empire in the 12th century.
- **Tonle Sap**: Southeast Asia's largest freshwater lake, vital for Cambodia's fisheries and agriculture, with a unique seasonal flood pulse.
- **Devaraja**: A "god-king" cult instituted by Khmer rulers, legitimizing their authority as divine.
- **UNTAC**: The United Nations Transitional Authority in Cambodia, which administered the country during the 1992-1993 peace process.
- **CPP**: The Cambodian People's Party, the ruling political party that has dominated Cambodia's government since 1979.
- **Khmer Loeu**: Collective term for the indigenous highland ethnic groups of Cambodia.
- **Cham**: An ethnic minority group in Cambodia, many of whom are Muslim.

## Debates and open questions
- The exact death toll of the Cambodian genocide under the Khmer Rouge remains debated, with estimates ranging from 1 to 3 million.
- The function and precise age of prehistoric "circular earthworks" discovered near Memot are still debated by archaeologists.
- The long-term impact of upstream Mekong River dams on Cambodia's Tonle Sap fisheries and food security is a major ongoing concern.

# Giza

## Overview
Giza is a major Egyptian city and the capital of Giza Governorate, located on the west bank of the Nile opposite Cairo. It is globally renowned as the site of the Giza Plateau, home to the Great Pyramid, the Great Sphinx, and other ancient monuments, making it a critical center for tourism, history, and archaeology. The city is a vital economic, cultural, and educational hub within the Greater Cairo metropolis.

## Timeline
- **c. 3100 BC** — Memphis, near modern Giza, becomes the capital of the unified Egyptian state under Pharaoh Narmer.
- **26th century BC** — Major monuments, including the Great Sphinx and pyramids, are constructed on the Giza Plateau.
- **642 AD** — The city of Giza is founded by the Muslim conquerors of Egypt.
- **1891** — The Giza Zoo, Africa's first, is established.
- **1924** — Cairo University is moved to Giza.
- **2018** — Sphinx International Airport opens.
- **2023** — Giza's population is recorded as 4,458,135.
- **2024** — Tourism revenues in Egypt reach $15.3 billion nationally. |
- **2025** — Giza is visited by 14.7 million tourists; it is listed in the UNESCO Creative Cities Network.

## Body

### Geography and Climate
Giza lies on the Giza Plateau, which overlooks the site of the ancient capital Memphis. The city has a hot desert-like arid climate (Köppen: BWh), with high summer temperatures reaching 40 °C (104 °F) and infrequent rain. Windstorms in spring can bring Saharan dust.

### History and Administration
The area served as a necropolis for pharaohs during the second millennium BC. After various conquests, the city was founded in 642 AD. Today, Giza is administered by an appointed City Head and the Governor of Giza. It is divided into nine urban districts, including El-Haram (The Pyramids district).

### Economy and Tourism
Tourism is a primary economic driver, centered on the Giza Plateau and the Grand Egyptian Museum (GEM). The city is also a hub for filmmaking, with studios like Studio Misr, and hosts the headquarters of several multinational companies and banks. The Giza Zoo, established in 1891, is a major attraction.

### Infrastructure and Education
Giza is served by Cairo International Airport and the newer Sphinx International Airport. It shares a metro system with Cairo and has an extensive road network. The city is a major educational center, home to Cairo University and numerous other institutes, including the Higher Institute of Cinema.

### Culture and Media
Giza's cultural landscape includes the GEM, the Egyptian Agricultural Museum, and the Imhotep Museum at Saqqara. The city is a center for media production, hosting the Egyptian Media Production City (EMPC). It is also known for the Pyramids Sound and Light Show and major concerts held at the Giza Plateau.

## Terms
- **Necropolis**: A large, ancient cemetery, especially one with elaborate tomb monuments.
- **Köppen climate classification**: A system for classifying the world's climates.
- **Pharaoh**: A ruler in ancient Egypt.
- **UNESCO World Heritage Site**: A landmark or area with legal protection for its cultural, historical, or scientific significance.
- **Grand Egyptian Museum (GEM)**: A new, large museum in Giza dedicated to ancient Egyptian civilization.
- **Mastaba**: A type of ancient Egyptian tomb in the form of a flat-roofed, rectangular structure with sloping sides.
- **Greater Cairo metropolis**: The urban area encompassing Cairo, Giza, and other surrounding cities.

## Debates and Open Questions
The exact etymology of the name "Giza" is unknown, with proposed origins from Arameo-Arabic, Ancient Egyptian, or Persian roots. The precise functions and construction methods of the Giza Plateau monuments continue to be subjects of archaeological research.

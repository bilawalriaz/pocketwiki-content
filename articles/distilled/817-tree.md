# Tree

## Overview  
A tree is a perennial plant with an elongated stem (trunk) supporting branches and leaves, evolving this form independently across many plant lineages to compete for sunlight. Though not a taxonomic group, trees are vital ecologically—stabilizing soil, storing carbon, and providing habitat—and economically, supplying timber, food, and medicines. Their growth involves secondary thickening via vascular cambium, and reproduction occurs through seeds or spores depending on the species.

## Timeline  
- **~400 million years ago** — Earliest trees (tree ferns, lycophytes) appear in Carboniferous
- **~385 million years ago** — *Wattieza*, earliest known tree, from Middle Devonian
- **~319 million years ago** — Whole genome duplication may have led to gymnosperms
- **245–66 million years ago** — Conifers flourish during Mesozoic
- **Cretaceous period** — Flowering tree forms evolve
- **66–2 million years ago** — Angiosperm trees displace conifers in Tertiary
- **1.5 million years ago** — Glacial periods cause forest retreat and recolonization
- **2015** — Estimated 3.04 trillion mature trees globally

## Body  

### Definition and Classification  
The term "tree" lacks a universal definition. Broadly, it refers to any plant with an elongated trunk supporting leaves or branches above ground. Narrower definitions require woody tissue formed by secondary growth—annual outward thickening via vascular cambium. Under this stricter view, herbaceous plants like palms and bananas aren't true trees despite their size. Most trees are angiosperms (hardwoods), while others are gymnosperms (softwoods like conifers). Some monocots, such as bamboos and palms, develop pseudo-wood without true secondary growth.

### Structure and Function  
Trees consist of roots, trunk, branches, and leaves, all connected by vascular tissues. Roots anchor the plant and absorb water/nutrients, often forming mutualisms with mycorrhizal fungi that enhance nutrient uptake. The trunk elevates leaves for light capture and transports fluids via xylem (water/minerals upward) and phloem (sugars downward). Secondary growth—driven by vascular cambium—produces wood (xylem) inward and phloem outward, while cork cambium forms protective bark. Annual growth rings reflect seasonal cycles.

### Reproduction and Dispersal  
Most trees reproduce via seeds, though tree ferns use spores. Pollination occurs through wind or animals. Seed dispersal mechanisms include wind (winged seeds), animals (fleshy fruits), gravity, and explosive dehiscence. Some species depend on fire or temperature cues for germination.

### Ecological Role  
Trees stabilize soil, reduce erosion, moderate climate, and sequester carbon. Forests host immense biodiversity, especially tropical rainforests. They create microhabitats for epiphytes, fungi, and fauna. In mangroves, roots slow water flow and trap sediment, aiding coastal protection.

### Human Uses  
Trees provide food (fruits, nuts, sap), fuel (wood, charcoal), construction materials (timber, engineered wood), medicines (quinine, aspirin precursors), and cultural value (bonsai, sacred groves). Urban planting improves air quality and mental well-being.

### Evolutionary History  
Tree forms evolved convergently in unrelated groups. Early trees were spore-bearing (ferns, horsetails). Seed-bearing gymnosperms emerged later, followed by angiosperm dominance in the Cretaceous. Climate shifts during the Quaternary drove forest expansion and contraction.

### Threats and Conservation  
Habitat loss threatens ~20,000 tree species listed on the IUCN Red List, with over 8,000 endangered. Sacred groves and protected areas serve as refuges. Drought and disease also impact individual trees.

### Mythology and Culture  
Trees feature prominently in global mythologies—from Yggdrasil in Norse cosmology to the Tree of Life in Abrahamic traditions. Sacred groves preserve biodiversity and cultural heritage.

## Terms  
- **Secondary growth**: Thickening of stems/roots via lateral meristems (vascular and cork cambium)  
- **Vascular cambium**: Meristematic tissue producing xylem and phloem  
- **Mycorrhiza**: Mutualistic association between fungal hyphae and plant roots  
- **Xylem**: Vascular tissue conducting water and minerals upward  
- **Phloem**: Tissue distributing sugars and other organic compounds  
- **Deciduous**: Shedding leaves seasonally  
- **Evergreen**: Retaining green foliage year-round  
- **Canopy**: Uppermost layer of leaves/branches in a forest  
- **Sapling**: Young tree  
- **Hardwood/Softwood**: Angiosperm vs. gymnosperm wood  

## Debates and Open Questions  
- No universally accepted botanical definition of "tree"; boundaries blur with shrubs and large herbs  
- Whether certain monocots (e.g., palms, bamboos) qualify as trees due to lack of true secondary growth  
- Uncertainty in global tree counts due to sampling bias toward Europe and North America  
- Mechanisms behind tree longevity and resistance to disease/pathogens remain under study  
- Impact of climate change on forest composition and migration patterns is ongoing research

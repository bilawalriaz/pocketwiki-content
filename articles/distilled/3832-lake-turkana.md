# Lake Turkana

## Overview
Lake Turkana is the world's largest permanent desert and alkaline lake, located in Kenya's Rift Valley. It is a critical ecological and archaeological site, home to unique wildlife and pivotal hominin fossils, but is now threatened by the construction of a major dam on its primary water source.

## Timeline
- **c. 34.8 mya** — Oldest volcanic activity in the region (Nabwal Hills).
- **c. 4.18–3.99 mya** — Extensive basalt extrusions form the Gombe Group Basalts.
- **c. 9500 years ago** — Lake level peaks ~100m above current surface; likely connected to the Nile system.
- **c. 300 BCE** — Archaeoastronomical site Namoratunga is dated.
- **6 March 1888** — First recorded European visit by Count Teleki; lake named "Rudolf."
- **1972** — Skull 1470 (Homo rudolfensis) discovered at Koobi Fora.
- **1975** — President Kenyatta renames the lake "Turkana."
- **1984** — "Turkana Boy" (Homo ergaster) skeleton discovered.
- **1994** — Australopithecus anamensis fossils discovered, dating to ~4 million years ago.
- **2018** — Lake Turkana Wind Power Project commissioned.
- **2021** — Kenya Government report estimates lake surface area increased 10% since 2010.

## Body

### Physical Characteristics and Climate
Lake Turkana is a saline, alkaline lake with no outlet; water is lost only by evaporation. It is fed primarily by the Omo River (90% of inflow), with the Turkwel and Kerio rivers also contributing. The climate is hot and very dry, with strong winds and frequent violent storms. The lake's volume and dimensions fluctuate significantly; for example, its level fell by 10 metres between 1975 and 1993. The water is slightly brackish with very high fluoride levels, making it generally unsuitable for drinking or irrigation. The surrounding geology is predominantly volcanic, with Central Island being an active volcano.

### Biology and Ecology
The lake's aquatic biome and surrounding desert shrubland support unique life. It contains about 50 fish species, including 12 endemics like the Rudolf lates. Non-endemic fish are mainly of Nilotic origin, a remnant of a prehistoric connection to the Nile. The lake formerly held Africa's largest Nile crocodile population (estimated at 14,000 in 1968). The region is also home to hundreds of bird species, including flamingos and the African skimmer. On land, dry grasslands support mammals like Grévy's zebra and Grant's gazelle. The Lake Turkana National Parks are a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

### Geology and Anthropology
The lake lies in the East African Rift, a graben formed by tectonic plate separation. Fluctuating lake levels and volcanic ash deposits created ideal conditions for preserving fossils. The Turkana Basin is a major site for paleoanthropology, yielding key hominin fossils that demonstrate human evolution involved multiple lineages. Discoveries include *Australopithecus anamensis* (4 million years ago), *Homo rudolfensis* (2 million years ago), and the "Turkana Boy" (*Homo ergaster*). Evidence of early tool use (Lomekwi 3) and possible inter-group conflict (Nataruk site, 10,000 years ago) have also been found. The region's human populations have adapted subsistence strategies to the lake's changing levels over millennia.

### Threats and Development
The primary modern threat is the Gilgel Gibe III Dam in Ethiopia on the Omo River. Proponents state the dam will cause a temporary water level reduction of less than 50 cm per year during filling. Critics, including the Africa Resources Working Group, warn it could reduce the lake level by up to 10 metres, drastically increasing salinity, destroying fisheries, and threatening the livelihoods of over 500,000 people, potentially leading to conflict. A 2021 Kenyan government report, however, estimated the lake's surface area had increased by 10% between 2010 and 2020. The region also has a new wind power project (310 MW) and faces pressures from infrastructure and a cash-based economy.

## Terms
- **Alkaline lake**: A lake with a pH greater than 8, often high in dissolved salts.
- **Endemic**: A species found only in a specific geographic location.
- **Grabben**: A depressed block of the Earth's crust bordered by parallel faults, forming a valley.
- **Hominin**: A member of the tribe Hominini, which includes modern humans and their immediate ancestors after the split from the chimpanzee lineage.
- **Rift**: A linear zone where the Earth's crust is being pulled apart.
- **Saline**: Containing dissolved salts.
- **Tuff**: Rock formed from consolidated volcanic ash.

## Debates and open questions
- **Impact of the Gilgel Gibe III Dam**: The magnitude of the dam's effect on Lake Turkana's water level and salinity is highly contested, with estimates ranging from negligible to catastrophic.
- **Evolutionary significance**: The region's fossil record continues to inform debates about the number of hominin lineages and the processes of human evolution.
- **Future of subsistence**: The long-term viability of traditional pastoralism and fishing versus a cash-based economy in the face of climate change and development is uncertain.

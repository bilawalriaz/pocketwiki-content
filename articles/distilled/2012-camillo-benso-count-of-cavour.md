# Camillo Benso, Count of Cavour

## Overview
Camillo Benso, Count of Cavour, was the leading statesman of the Italian Risorgimento (the movement for Italian unification). As Prime Minister of the Kingdom of Sardinia, he masterminded the diplomatic and military campaigns that transformed a small Piedmontese state into a nearly unified Kingdom of Italy, making him the central figure in the creation of the modern Italian nation.

## Timeline
- **10 August 1810** — Born in Turin during Napoleonic rule.
- **1832–1848** — Serves as mayor of Grinzane, administering his family estate.
- **1848** — Enters politics after the granting of a constitution in Piedmont.
- **1852** — Becomes Prime Minister of the Kingdom of Sardinia.
- **1855** — Enters the Crimean War on the side of Britain and France.
- **July 1858** — Secretly agrees with Napoleon III to provoke war with Austria.
- **1859** — Fights the Second Italian War of Independence; gains Lombardy.
- **1860** — Cedes Savoy and Nice to France; annexes Tuscany and Emilia.
- **1860** — Manages the aftermath of Garibaldi's conquest of the south.
- **18 March 1861** — Becomes the first Prime Minister of the newly declared Kingdom of Italy.
- **6 June 1861** — Dies in office, three months after Italian unification is declared.

## Body

### Early Life and Economic Reforms
Born into a Piedmontese noble family, Cavour was educated at a military academy but developed liberal and economic ideas through travel and study. He believed economic modernization must precede political change. From his family estate and through founding the newspaper *Il Risorgimento*, he advocated for agricultural innovation, free trade, and, most importantly, a vast expansion of the railway system, which he saw as key to unifying the peninsula.

### Rise to Power in Piedmont
Cavour entered politics after the liberal upheavals of 1848. He quickly rose through the Piedmontese government by uniting moderate centre-left and centre-right politicians. As Prime Minister from 1852, he pursued anticlerical policies, weakening Church power over land and education, and launched the railway expansion that gave Piedmont one-third of Italy's tracks by 1860. His goal was initially to expand Piedmont, not create a unified Italy.

### Diplomacy and the Wars of Unification
Cavour's genius was in diplomacy. He entered the Crimean War (1855) to gain a seat at the peace table. He then secretly allied with France's Napoleon III, agreeing to cede Savoy and Nice in exchange for French military help against Austria. This led to the Second Italian War of Independence (1859), which secured Lombardy. When Garibaldi's volunteer expedition conquered the south, Cavour skillfully managed the situation, annexing the central states and linking them with Garibaldi's conquests to form a united kingdom under King Victor Emmanuel II.

### Prime Minister of Italy and Death
As Italy's first Prime Minister, Cavour faced the "Roman Question"—how to make Rome the capital without conflict with the Pope—and the issue of Venetia, still under Austrian rule. He advocated for a "free church in a free state." Exhausted by the strain of unification, he died of malaria in 1861, just three months into his premiership, before Rome (1870) or Venice (1866) were incorporated.

## Terms
- **Risorgimento**: The 19th-century movement for Italian unification.
- **Connubio**: The political "marriage" or coalition Cavour formed between moderate centre-left and centre-right politicians.
- **Trasformismo**: A political practice of forming flexible, opportunistic coalitions to maintain power, which Cavour employed.
- **Roman Question**: The political problem of reconciling the temporal power of the Pope with making Rome the capital of a unified Italy.
- **Historical Right**: The conservative political faction in the Italian parliament to which Cavour belonged.
- **Expedition of the Thousand**: Giuseppe Garibaldi's 1860 campaign with volunteer redshirts that conquered the Kingdom of the Two Sicilies.
- **Armistice of Villafranca**: The 1859 peace treaty that ended the Second Italian War of Independence, which Cavour initially opposed.

## Debates and Open Questions
- Historians debate whether Cavour was a democratic parliamentarian or an authoritarian manipulator who used bribes, election rigging, and emergency powers.
- His objective is debated: did he plan for a unified Italy from the start, or was his primary goal merely to expand the Kingdom of Sardinia?
- The relationship between Cavour's diplomatic statecraft and Garibaldi's revolutionary action is a central scholarly debate about the nature of Italian unification.

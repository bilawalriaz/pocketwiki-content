# Elam

## Overview
Elam was an ancient civilization centered in modern-day southwestern Iran (Khuzestan and Ilam provinces) and a small part of southern Iraq. It was a major political force in the Ancient Near East for over two millennia, with its culture and language significantly influencing the later Achaemenid Persian Empire. Its history is primarily reconstructed from Mesopotamian sources, and its language, Elamite, is generally considered a language isolate.

## Timeline
- **c. 3200–c. 2700 BC** — Proto-Elamite period; undeciphered Proto-Elamite script used at Susa.
- **c. 2700 BC** — Beginning of the Old Elamite period; earliest historical records.
- **c. 2400–c. 2100 BC** — Awan dynasty rules; conflicts with the Akkadian Empire.
- **2004 BC** — Elamites, allied with Susa, sack the Sumerian city of Ur.
- **c. 1970–c. 1770 BC** — Sukkalmah (Epartid) dynasty; Elam is a major power in Mesopotamian politics.
- **c. 1500–c. 1100 BC** — Middle Elamite period; Anshanite dynasties rule, Susa becomes the capital.
- **1158 BC** — Shutruk-Nakhkhunte defeats the Kassites and sacks Babylon.
- **646 BC** — Assyrian king Ashurbanipal sacks Susa, devastating Elam.
- **640 BC** — Last Elamite king captured; Elam annexed by Assyria.
- **539 BC** — Achaemenid Persian rule begins in Susa.

## Body

### Geography and Identity
Elam encompassed the lowlands of Khuzestan (ancient Susiana) and the highlands of the Zagros Mountains. Ancient sources debated whether "Elam" referred only to the highlands or was synonymous with Susiana. Modern scholarship notes that "Elam" is an exonym (foreign name) derived from Sumerian, not a term used by the indigenous peoples, who identified by regional names like Anshanites or Shimashkians. The name Khuzestan itself derives from Old Persian for Susa/Elam.

### Proto-Elamite and Old Elamite Periods (c. 3200–c. 1500 BC)
The Proto-Elamite culture emerged around Susa, featuring an undeciphered script. The Old Elamite period saw the rise of dynasties from Awan, Shimashki, and the Sukkalmah (Epartid). Elam was a frequent rival and sometimes conqueror of Mesopotamian states, famously sacking Ur in 2004 BC. The Sukkalmah dynasty was a major power, with its rulers addressed as "Father" by Mesopotamian kings. Elam also engaged in maritime trade with the Indus Valley Civilization until c. 1900 BC.

### Middle Elamite Empire (c. 1500–c. 1100 BC)
The Anshanite dynasties (Kidinuids, Igehalkids, Shutrukids) unified the highlands and lowlands, taking the title "king of Anshan and Susa." Susa became the dominant capital, and Elamite language and culture replaced Akkadian influence. The empire reached its peak under the Shutrukid dynasty, whose kings conducted military campaigns into Babylonia, looting treasures like the Stele of Hammurabi. Power waned after defeats by Babylon and Assyria in the late 12th century BC.

### Neo-Elamite Period and Fall (c. 1100–540 BC)
This period saw the migration of Iranian peoples (Medes, Persians) to the plateau. Elam, weakened by internal strife, became entangled in conflicts between Assyria and Babylon. Assyrian kings, particularly Ashurbanipal, invaded repeatedly, culminating in the total destruction of Susa in 646 BC and the annexation of Elam in 640 BC. A fragmented Elamite rule briefly re-emerged but was absorbed by the Median and then the Achaemenid Persian Empires.

### Language and Legacy
Elamite is a language isolate, written first in Linear Elamite and later in Mesopotamian cuneiform. It remained an official language of the Achaemenid Empire. Elamite traditions, art, and the title "king of Anshan" were adopted by the Persians, making Elam a cultural conduit between Mesopotamia and the Iranian plateau. The name survived into the Hellenistic period as Elymais and influenced modern provincial names in Iran.

## Terms
- **Language Isolate**: A language with no demonstrated genetic relationship to any other known language.
- **Cuneiform**: A system of writing using wedge-shaped marks on clay tablets, developed in Mesopotamia.
- **Linear Elamite**: An early, undeciphered writing system used in Elam before the adoption of cuneiform.
- **Sukkalmah**: The title meaning "Great Regent," borne by rulers of the Epartid dynasty.
- **Anshan**: A major highland city and political center of Elam, later adopted as a title by Persian kings.
- **Susa**: The primary lowland capital of Elam, a major archaeological site.
- **Exonym**: A name for a people or place used by outsiders, not by the people themselves.
- **Polytheism**: The belief in or worship of more than one god.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Geographic Definition**: Scholars debate whether "Elam" originally referred only to the highlands or encompassed both highlands and lowlands (Susa).
- **Proto-Elamite Script**: The undeciphered Proto-Elamite script may represent the Elamite language or another, unknown language.
- **Language Relations**: While Elamite is classified as an isolate, a minority hypothesis (Elamo-Dravidian) suggests a possible link to Dravidian languages, a view not widely accepted.
- **Cultural Continuity**: The extent to which Elamite culture was absorbed into versus distinct from the succeeding Achaemenid Persian culture is a subject of study.

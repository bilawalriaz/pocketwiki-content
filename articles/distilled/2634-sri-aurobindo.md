# Sri Aurobindo

## Overview
Sri Aurobindo (1872–1950) was an Indian philosopher, yogi, and nationalist who evolved from a revolutionary activist for Indian independence into a major spiritual teacher. He formulated Integral Yoga, a path aiming not for escape from the world but for the spiritual transformation of human life into a divine existence. His synthesis of Eastern and Western thought, particularly through works like *The Life Divine*, has made him a significant figure in modern philosophy and spirituality.

## Timeline
- **15 August 1872** — Born Aurobindo Ghosh in Calcutta, Bengal Presidency.
- **1879** — Family moves to England for education.
- **1890–1892** — Studies at King's College, Cambridge; passes ICS exam but deliberately fails practical to avoid service.
- **1893** — Returns to India; joins Baroda State Service.
- **1901** — Marries Mrinalini Devi.
- **1906** — Becomes first principal of the National College, Calcutta (now Jadavpur University).
- **1908** — Arrested in the Alipore Bomb Case; imprisoned in solitary confinement.
- **6 May 1909** — Acquitted after the trial.
- **April 1910** — Moves to Pondicherry (then French India) to escape British prosecution, beginning his spiritual retreat.
- **1914** — Starts the philosophical journal *Arya*.
- **24 November 1926** — Sri Aurobindo Ashram is founded; he retires into seclusion, leaving its management to Mirra Alfassa ("The Mother").
- **5 December 1950** — Dies in Pondicherry of uremia.

## Body

### Early Life and Revolutionary Politics
Born into a Bengali family with Brahmo Samaj connections, Aurobindo was educated in England, becoming fluent in numerous languages. After returning to India in 1893, he worked in Baroda while secretly engaging with Indian nationalist movements. He became a prominent leader in the Indian National Congress, advocating for complete independence (*Swaraj*). His involvement with revolutionary groups like the Anushilan Samiti led to his arrest in 1908 in the Alipore Bomb Case. His year in prison was a period of profound spiritual transformation, during which he reported mystical experiences, including a sense of guidance from Swami Vivekananda.

### Spiritual Turn and Integral Yoga
After his acquittal in 1909, Aurobindo withdrew from politics. Facing sedition charges, he moved to the French colony of Pondicherry in 1910. There, he dedicated himself to spiritual practice and writing. In 1926, with his collaborator Mirra Alfassa (known as The Mother), he established the Sri Aurobindo Ashram. His core teaching, Integral Yoga, is detailed in *The Life Divine* and *The Synthesis of Yoga*. It posits that the world is a real manifestation of the Divine (*Brahman*) undergoing evolution. The goal is not liberation from the world but its transformation through the descent of a higher consciousness, the "Supermind," into matter, life, and mind.

### Philosophy and Literary Work
Sri Aurobindo's philosophy synthesizes Indian Vedantic thought with elements of Western evolutionism. He argued that evolution has a purpose: to manifest the Supermind, a "Truth-Consciousness" that bridges the divine and material realms. His major philosophical work, *The Life Divine*, compiles essays from the journal *Arya*. His epic poem *Savitri* (c. 24,000 lines) is a spiritual allegory. He also wrote extensive commentaries on the Vedas, Upanishads, and the Bhagavad Gita. Scholars note his engagement with Western philosophers like Hegel and Teilhard de Chardin, though he stated his philosophy was founded primarily on the Upanishads and direct experience.

### Legacy and Influence
Sri Aurobindo's influence extends to philosophy, spirituality, and the human potential movement. Thinkers like Ken Wilber and organizations such as the California Institute of Integral Studies trace their lineage to his work. His ashram and the township of Auroville, founded by The Mother in 1968, continue his spiritual and educational experiments. He was nominated for the Nobel Prize in Literature (1943) and the Nobel Peace Prize (1950).

## Terms
- **Integral Yoga**: Sri Aurobindo's system of spiritual practice aiming for the total transformation of human consciousness and life into a divine existence, integrating all aspects of being.
- **Supermind**: A central concept in his philosophy; a level of consciousness above the human mind, described as a "Truth-Consciousness" that is the direct knowledge of the Divine and the agent of spiritual evolution.
- **Brahman**: The ultimate, unchanging reality in Indian philosophy; for Aurobindo, it is the Divine that manifests as the universe through *līlā* (divine play).
- **Swaraj**: A Sanskrit term meaning "self-rule," used in the Indian independence movement to denote complete autonomy from British rule.
- **Anushilan Samiti**: A Bengali revolutionary organization focused on armed struggle against British rule, with which Aurobindo was associated.
- **Alipore Bomb Case (1908)**: A famous trial where Aurobindo was charged with treason for his alleged involvement in a bombing; he was acquitted.
- **The Mother (Mirra Alfassa)**: A French spiritual collaborator of Sri Aurobindo, considered his spiritual equal, who managed the Sri Aurobindo Ashram after 1926.
- **Vedanta**: A school of Hindu philosophy based on the Upanishads, which Aurobindo synthesized with Western thought.
- **Upanishads**: Ancient Sanskrit texts forming the philosophical basis of Vedanta, which Aurobindo studied deeply and considered the foundation of his philosophy.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Influence of Western Philosophy**: While Aurobindo credited the Upanishads as his primary source, scholars like S.K. Maitra and R. Puligandla argue his work shows a significant synthesis with Western thought (e.g., Hegel, evolutionary theory). Aurobindo himself acknowledged familiarity but downplayed direct influence.
- **Critique of Shankara**: Aurobindo's philosophy is often contrasted with the Advaita Vedanta of Adi Shankara, which he critiqued as world-negating (*māyāvāda*). Some scholars, like R. Puligandla, suggest this critique may misrepresent Shankara's position in Aurobindo's effort to create an integral, world-affirming system.
- **Nature of the Supermind**: The concept of the Supermind as a future evolutionary stage for humanity is a unique and debated aspect of his metaphysics, bridging the divine and the material in a way distinct from traditional Eastern or Western models.

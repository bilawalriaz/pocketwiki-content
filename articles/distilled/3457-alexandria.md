# Alexandria

## Overview
Alexandria is a major Egyptian city and Mediterranean seaport, founded in 331 BC by Alexander the Great. It became the capital of the Ptolemaic Kingdom and one of the most important intellectual and commercial centers of the ancient world, renowned for its Great Library and Lighthouse. Its strategic location has made it a pivotal hub for trade and cultural exchange for over two millennia, though it faces significant modern threats from sea-level rise.

## Timeline
- **331 BC** — Founded by Alexander the Great on the site of the Egyptian settlement Rhacotis.
- **305 BC** — Ptolemy I Soter declares himself Pharaoh and moves the capital to Alexandria.
- **48-47 BC** — Julius Caesar's siege of Alexandria; the Great Library is damaged.
- **38 AD** — Alexandrian pogroms: violent clashes between Greek and Jewish citizens.
- **365 AD** — A devastating tsunami strikes the city.
- **641 AD** — Alexandria is captured by the Arab general 'Amr ibn al-'As after a 14-month siege.
- **1517** — The city is conquered by the Ottoman Turks.
- **1798** — French troops under Napoleon storm Alexandria.
- **1801** — British forces defeat the French and take control of the city.
- **1820s** — Muhammad Ali begins modern redevelopment, including the Mahmoudiyah Canal.
- **1882** — Alexandria is bombarded and occupied by British naval forces.
- **2002** — The Bibliotheca Alexandrina, a modern library, is inaugurated near the ancient site.

## Body

### Founding and Hellenistic Era
Alexandria was founded in 331 BC by Alexander the Great to be a major Greek city linking Greece to the Nile valley. After Alexander's death in 323 BC, his general Ptolemy I Soter took control, declared himself Pharaoh in 305 BC, and made Alexandria his capital. The city grew rapidly, becoming the largest in the world within a century. It was renowned for the Library of Alexandria, the largest in the ancient world, and the Lighthouse of Alexandria (Pharos), one of the Seven Wonders of the Ancient World. The Ptolemies fostered scholarship while maintaining a distinct population of Greeks, Egyptians, and Jews.

### Roman and Byzantine Period
Alexandria remained a leading city of the Mediterranean under Roman rule, serving as Egypt's capital. It was a major center of early Christianity and hosted the Patriarchate of Alexandria. The city suffered significant damage during the Diaspora revolt of 115 AD and a tsunami in 365 AD. In 619 AD, it fell to the Sassanid Persians before being recovered by the Byzantine Empire in 629 AD.

### Islamic and Medieval Era
In 641 AD, Alexandria was captured by Arab forces under 'Amr ibn al-'As. It continued as a vital trading hub, exporting goods from Arabia, India, and Africa to Europe. Latin merchants established communities there, and the Mamluk sultans controlled the lucrative trade. The city's importance declined after Portuguese navigators discovered a sea route to India in the late 15th century. It was conquered by the Ottoman Turks in 1517.

### Modern Era
Alexandria was stormed by French troops in 1798 and later occupied by the British in 1801. Under Muhammad Ali in the early 19th century, it was rebuilt and modernized, becoming a cosmopolitan center. It was bombarded by the British in 1882. Following the 1956 Suez Crisis and subsequent Arab nationalism, most of its European population left. Today, it is Egypt's second-largest city and a major industrial and commercial port.

### Geography and Climate
Alexandria is located on the Mediterranean coast at the western edge of the Nile Delta. It has a hot steppe/desert climate moderated by the sea, with mild, rainy winters and hot, humid summers. The city is extremely vulnerable to sea-level rise due to its low-lying delta location. Studies predict significant future damage and potential building collapses from rising groundwater.

## Terms
*   **Rhacotis:** The ancient Egyptian settlement upon which Alexandria was founded.
*   **Heptastadion:** A 1,260-meter-long causeway built by Alexander the Great, connecting the mainland to the island of Pharos.
*   **Ptolemaic Kingdom:** The Hellenistic kingdom ruled by the Ptolemaic dynasty, with Alexandria as its capital.
*   **Septuagint:** A Greek version of the Hebrew Bible (Tanakh) produced in Alexandria.
*   **Patriarchate of Alexandria:** An ancient Christian episcopal see, a leading center of early Christianity.
*   **Funduq:** A fortified area within a medieval city dedicated to a foreign trading community, under the authority of a consul.
*   **Bibliotheca Alexandrina:** A major library and cultural center inaugurated in 2002 near the site of the ancient Library of Alexandria.
*   **Catacombs of Kom El Shoqafa:** A multi-level Roman-era necropolis featuring syncretic Romano-Egyptian religious symbols.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   The exact circumstances and timeline of the destruction of the ancient Library of Alexandria remain a lively source of controversy.
*   The precise location and layout of many ancient structures, such as the Musaeum and parts of the Serapeum, are still unknown due to the modern city built over them.
*   Underwater archaeology, pioneered by Franck Goddio, continues to explore the submerged royal quarters and port areas, revealing new information about the ancient city's layout and artifacts.

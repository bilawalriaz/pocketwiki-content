# Jeju Island

## Overview
Jeju Island is South Korea's largest island, a volcanic landmass of significant ecological and cultural importance. It is a UNESCO World Heritage site known for its unique landscape, subtropical climate, and indigenous Jeju people, whose language is critically endangered. The island's economy is heavily reliant on tourism.

## Timeline
- **c. 2 million years ago** — Island formed by submarine volcanic eruptions.
- **c. 1.2 million years ago** — A magma chamber formed under the seafloor and began erupting.
- **c. 700,000 years ago** — The island had formed through volcanic activity, which then paused for about 100,000 years.
- **c. 300,000 years ago** — Volcanic activity restarted along the coastline.
- **c. 100,000 years ago** — Volcanic activity formed Hallasan Mountain.
- **c. 25,000 years ago** — Lateral eruptions around Hallasan created numerous *oreum* (parasitic cones).
- **1642 (approx.)** — First European sighting by the Portuguese, who called it *Ilha de Ladrones*.
- **1648 (latest)** — The name "Quelpart" (from Dutch/French) is attested for the island.
- **1910** — Japanese annexation; island known as *Saishū*.
- **7 July 2000** — Official spelling changed from "Cheju" to "Jeju".

## Body

### Geography and Formation
Jeju is an oval-shaped volcanic island, measuring 73 km east–west and 31 km north–south. It is dominated by Hallasan, a 1,947 m volcano and South Korea's highest peak. The island formed through a series of volcanic eruptions beginning approximately 2 million years ago, with the most recent major activity creating Hallasan about 100,000 years ago. Its landscape consists chiefly of basalt and lava, featuring around 360 *oreum* (small extinct volcanoes). A key ecological feature is the *gotjawal*, a type of forest covering 12% of the island. This forest, growing on porous 'a'a lava, is the primary source of groundwater for the island's population.

### Climate
Jeju has a humid subtropical climate (Köppen *Cfa*) with four distinct seasons. Winters are cool with moderate rainfall, and summers are hot and humid with very high rainfall. The island's climate varies regionally: the northern Jeju City is colder in winter due to continental winds, while the southern Seogwipo is warmer as Hallasan blocks these winds. The highlands around Hallasan have the highest annual precipitation in Korea. In January 2016, a severe cold wave caused major travel disruptions.

### Names and People
The island has been known by many historical names, including Tamna, Samdado ("Island of Three Abundances"), and Quelpart to early Europeans. The indigenous Jeju people have inhabited the island since the early Neolithic period. Their language, the Jeju language, is considered critically endangered by UNESCO. The island is also noted as a region where Korean Shamanism remains particularly intact.

### Biodiversity
Jeju's unique ecosystem supports many endemic species. Numerous plants and animals have scientific names referencing the island, using epithets like *quelpartensis*, *jejuensis*, or *chejuensis*. Examples include the earwig *Anechura quelparta*, the moth *Stegasta jejuensis*, and the mouse *Apodemus chejuensis*.

## Terms
- **Oreum**: A local term for the small extinct volcanoes or parasitic cones found on Jeju Island.
- **Gotjawal**: A unique type of forest that grows on porous 'a'a lava, serving as the island's main groundwater source.
- **'a'a lava**: A type of volcanic rock with a rough, jagged surface, which creates the porous base for *gotjawal* forests.
- **Hallasan**: The central volcano and highest mountain in South Korea, located on Jeju Island.
- **Humid subtropical climate (Cfa)**: A climate classification for regions with hot, humid summers and mild winters, with precipitation year-round.
- **Ramsar Convention**: An international treaty for the conservation and sustainable use of wetlands.
- **Endemic species**: A species found only in a specific geographic location.
- **Neolithic period**: The later part of the Stone Age, characterized by the use of polished stone tools and the development of early agriculture.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The *gotjawal* forest is considered an internationally important wetland by some researchers and a candidate for Ramsar Convention designation, but it has not yet been officially declared a Ramsar site.
- The exact origin of the European name "Quelpart" is debated; it may derive from a Dutch ship or a visual similarity to a type of vessel.

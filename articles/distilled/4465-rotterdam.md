# Rotterdam

## Overview
Rotterdam is the Netherlands' second-largest city by population and its largest by area, serving as a major global logistics hub and Europe's largest seaport. Its modern identity was forged by near-total destruction in World War II, leading to a post-war rebuilding that created a distinctive landscape of modern and experimental architecture. The city is a diverse, multicultural metropolis and a key economic engine for Western Europe.

## Timeline
- **c. 950** — Settlement established at the Rotte stream.
- **1260s** — Dam built on the Rotte, giving the city its name.
- **1340** — City rights granted by Count Willem IV of Holland.
- **1872** — Completion of the Nieuwe Waterweg, spurring major port growth.
- **1940** — German bombing destroys the city centre (14 May).
- **1953** — Lijnbaan, Europe's first car-free shopping street, opens.
- **1960** — Euromast tower erected for the Floriade garden festival.
- **1968** — Rotterdam becomes the first Dutch city with a metro system.
- **1996** — Erasmusbrug (Erasmus Bridge) opens, linking north and south.
- **2001** — Co-European Capital of Culture with Porto.
- **2021** — Hosts the Eurovision Song Contest; Zalmhaven Tower (215m) completed.

## Body

### History and Development
The settlement at the Rotte stream dates to at least 950, with a dam built in the 1260s. City rights were granted in 1340, and a 1350 canal connected it to northern trade routes. Rotterdam became involved in the Atlantic slave trade from the 1600s until abolition in 1814. Its port grew steadily, housing chambers of the VOC and WIC trading companies. The 1872 Nieuwe Waterweg canal triggered explosive growth, evidenced by the 1898 Witte Huis skyscraper, then Europe's tallest office building.

During World War I, Rotterdam was a major spy centre due to Dutch neutrality. In World War II, the German invasion began on 10 May 1940; the city centre was bombed on 14 May, leading to Dutch capitulation. The bombing killed 900 and left 80,000 homeless. Post-war rebuilding from the 1950s-1970s created a modern city, including the 1953 Lijnbaan. From the 1980s, an active architectural policy reshaped the skyline with daring designs.

### Geography and Demographics
Rotterdam is bisected by the Nieuwe Maas river and connected by several tunnels and bridges, including the iconic Erasmusbrug. Much of the city lies below sea level, protected by dikes. It forms the core of the Rijnmond conurbation and the southern wing of the Randstad, a major European megalopolis.

The city is highly diverse, with 52.9% of residents having at least one parent born abroad. It has the Netherlands' highest percentage of residents with a recent migration background from non-industrialized nations. Christianity is the largest religion (36.3%), followed by Islam (13.1%).

### Economy and Port
Rotterdam is Europe's largest seaport, a critical transit point for bulk goods between Europe and overseas. Its economy is anchored by logistics, petrochemicals, and general cargo. The port's access to the Rhine, Meuse, and Scheldt rivers provides a gateway to the industrial Ruhr and Western Europe. Major companies headquartered in Rotterdam include Unilever, Shell (since 2021), and Vitol. The city's unemployment rate (12%) is nearly double the national average.

### Architecture and Culture
The WWII bombing allowed for radical post-war reconstruction, making Rotterdam a global showcase for modern architecture. It is home to firms like OMA (Rem Koolhaas) and MVRDV. Key landmarks include the cube houses (1984), the Markthal (2014), and the Depot Boijmans Van Beuningen (2021), the world's first publicly accessible art storage facility. The city has 38 skyscrapers and 352 high-rises.

Culturally, Rotterdam hosts the International Film Festival Rotterdam (since 1972) and the North Sea Jazz Festival. It was European Capital of Culture in 2001. The city has a vibrant music history, from the 1970 Kralingen Pop Festival to the 1990s gabber scene.

### Sports
Rotterdam calls itself "Sportstad" (City of Sports). It is home to three professional football clubs: Feyenoord (16 national titles, 1970 European Cup winners), Sparta, and Excelsior. The annual Rotterdam Marathon is one of the world's fastest courses. The city also hosts the ABN AMRO World Tennis Tournament and has a strong tradition in swimming, boxing, and rowing.

## Terms
- **Nieuwe Waterweg**: A canal completed in 1872 that connected Rotterdam directly to the North Sea, enabling its growth into a major port.
- **VOC (Vereenigde Oostindische Compagnie)**: The Dutch East India Company, a 17th-century multinational trading company in which Rotterdam held a chamber.
- **WIC (West-Indische Compagnie)**: The Dutch West India Company, a 17th-century trading company involved in the Atlantic slave trade.
- **Randstad**: A megalopolis in the western Netherlands, including Rotterdam, The Hague, Amsterdam, and Utrecht, with a population of 7.1 million.
- **Rijnmond**: The conurbation centered on Rotterdam at the mouth of the Rhine.
- **Gabber**: A style of hardcore electronic music with hard beats, which originated in Rotterdam in the mid-1990s.
- **Opzoomeren**: A local phenomenon since 1994 where residents create facade gardens, with 2,503 participating streets by 2020.
- **Sterker door strijd**: "Stronger through effort," the city's motto presented in 1948 to honor its wartime resilience.

## Debates and Open Questions
The source notes the "healthy competition" and rivalry with Amsterdam, often summarized by sayings like "Amsterdam has it, Rotterdam doesn't need it." This reflects a persistent cultural and economic dialogue between the two major Dutch cities about their respective identities and roles.

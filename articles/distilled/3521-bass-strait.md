# Bass Strait

## Overview
Bass Strait is a shallow, turbulent waterway separating the island state of Tasmania from the Australian mainland (Victoria). Formed around 8,000 years ago by rising sea levels, it isolated Aboriginal Tasmanians from the mainland. The strait is a major shipping route, a source of natural resources, and a site of numerous shipwrecks and disappearances.

## Body

### Formation and Early Human History
Bass Strait was formed approximately 8,000 years ago at the end of the last glacial period. Rising sea levels flooded the prehistoric Bassian Plain, a land bridge that connected southern Victoria to northern Tasmania. This event isolated the Aboriginal Tasmanians, who had arrived around 40,000 years ago. Archaeological evidence suggests at least three waves of Aboriginal colonization. Aboriginal people also lived on Flinders Island until about 4,000 years ago.

### European Discovery and Naming
European detection of the strait began tentatively. Abel Tasman possibly encountered it in 1642 but was driven away by storms. James Cook approached in 1770 but did not enter. The strait's existence was suggested in 1797 by the master of the *Sydney Cove*. Its existence was confirmed in 1798–99 when George Bass and Matthew Flinders circumnavigated Tasmania. Governor Hunter officially named it in 1800. The French later sent an expedition, prompting the British to establish a garrison at Hobart.

### Geography and Maritime Character
Bass Strait is approximately 250 km wide and 500 km long, with an average depth of 60 m. Its central feature is the shallow Bass Basin. The strait is notoriously rough due to strong currents and storms, leading to hundreds of shipwrecks. Despite the danger, it provided a safer, shorter route for ships traveling between Europe/India and Sydney in the 19th century. The seabed is covered by subaqueous dunes and tidal ridges.

### Natural Resources and Infrastructure
The eastern Gippsland Basin contains major oil and gas fields discovered in the 1960s (e.g., Kingfish, Barracouta). The western Otway Basin was developed later. Resources are transported via pipelines to mainland facilities. The strait is crossed by the Basslink HVDC power cable and fibre-optic telecommunications cables. Transport includes air services and the *Spirit of Tasmania* ferries. A tunnel has been proposed.

### Crossings and Popular Culture
The strait is regularly crossed by sailing vessels, including in the Melbourne to Hobart Yacht Race. It has been crossed by windsurfers, dinghies, kayaks, and a paddleboard. The first non-stop kayak crossing was in 2003. The strait is infamous for disappearances, most notably the 1978 UFO incident involving pilot Frederick Valentich. These events have spawned theories, though most losses are attributed to severe weather.

## Terms
- **Bassian Plain:** The prehistoric land bridge that connected Tasmania to mainland Australia before it was flooded.
- **Bass Basin:** A large, shallow depression in the central part of Bass Strait.
- **Bassian Rise & King Island Rise:** Underwater plateaus that form sills separating Bass Basin from the ocean.
- **Gippsland Basin:** An area in eastern Bass Strait containing major oil and gas fields.
- **Latrobe Group:** The Cretaceous-Eocene rock formation from which oil and gas are produced.
- **Basslink:** A high-voltage direct current (HVDC) electrical cable linking Tasmania and Victoria.
- **Roaring Forties:** Strong westerly winds found in the Southern Hemisphere, which affect the strait's weather.

## Debates and Open questions
There is debate over which ocean Bass Strait belongs to. The International Hydrographic Organization (IHO) has different drafts: a 1953 version associates it with the Great Australian Bight, while an unapproved 2002 draft places it in the Pacific Ocean. The Australian Hydrographic Service places it in the Tasman Sea, not the Southern Ocean.

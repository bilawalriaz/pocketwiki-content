# Orange River

## Overview
The Orange River is the longest river in South Africa, stretching 2,432 km (1,511 mi) from the Drakensberg mountains in Lesotho to the Atlantic Ocean. It is a vital economic artery, providing water for irrigation and hydroelectric power across an arid region, and its basin covers about 77% of South Africa's land area. The river also forms significant international borders and is central to the country's history of diamond mining and water management projects.

## Timeline
- **c. 1867** — The Eureka Diamond, South Africa's first, is discovered near Hopetown on the Orange River.
- **1779** — Dutch explorer Robert Jacob Gordon names the river "Orange" in honor of William V of Orange.
- **1970s** — The Orange River Project (ORP) is constructed, including the Gariep and Vanderkloof Dams.
- **1986** — The first phase of the Lesotho Highlands Water Project is completed, delivering water to South Africa's Vaal River system.
- **1985** — A survey records 20,653–26,653 birds at the river's mouth, a recognised Ramsar wetland site.

## Body

### Course and Geography
The Orange River rises at over 3,000 m (9,800 ft) in the Drakensberg mountains of Lesotho, where it is known as the Senqu. It flows westward through South Africa, forming provincial and international borders with Lesotho and Namibia. The river passes through the Gariep and Vanderkloof Dams before meeting its main tributary, the Vaal River. Its final 550 km (340 mi) forms the border with Namibia, flowing through arid regions like the Kalahari and Namaqualand before emptying into the Atlantic Ocean. The river's total catchment area is 973,000 km² (376,000 sq mi).

### Hydrology and Tributaries
Rainfall varies dramatically along the river's course, from about 2,000 mm (79 in) per year at its source to less than 50 mm (2.0 in) at its mouth. This, combined with high evaporation, significantly reduces water volume in the dry season. The upper river is fed by tributaries like the Caledon and Vaal, which drain the high-precipitation Maloti Mountains. The river carries a large sediment load during the wet summer season, posing a long-term threat to engineering projects.

### Economy and Major Projects
The Orange River is crucial for South Africa's economy. The Orange River Project (ORP), built during the apartheid era, aimed to stabilize flow, generate hydroelectric power, and supply water to arid areas like the Eastern Cape. The Gariep Dam is the main storage structure, with water supplied via the Orange-Fish Tunnel. The Lesotho Highlands Water Project supplements the Vaal River system, delivering water through a tunnel under the border. The river also supports commercial diamond mining, irrigation for agriculture (including wine grapes), and recreational rafting.

### Etymology and Names
The river has multiple names reflecting its cultural history. Precolonial inhabitants called it ǂNūǃarib ("Black River") or Kai !Arib ("Great River"), leading to the Afrikaans name Gariep. The Dutch initially called it Groote Rivier ("Great River"). In 1779, Colonel Robert Gordon named it the Orange River after the Dutch ruling House of Orange. In Lesotho, it is known as the Senqu. Since the end of apartheid, "Gariep" has gained favor in official South African correspondence, though "Orange" remains internationally recognized.

### Wildlife
The Orange River has relatively low species diversity and no large animals; hippopotami were hunted to extinction there in the 19th century. Surveys have found 19 fish species in the lower river, including two non-native species. Seven fish species are endemic to the Vaal-Orange system, such as the rock-catfish and smallmouth yellowfish. Rainbow trout are found in the headwaters in Lesotho.

## Terms
- **Catchment**: The area of land where all water drains into a particular river or lake.
- **Alluvial diamonds**: Diamonds found in sediment (sand, gravel) deposited by a river.
- **Ramsar site**: A wetland of international importance designated under the Ramsar Convention.
- **Hydroelectric power**: Electricity generated from the energy of falling or flowing water.
- **Wadi**: A valley or streambed that is dry except during the rainy season.
- **Incised river bed**: A river channel that has cut deeply into the surrounding landscape.
- **Austral summer**: The summer season in the Southern Hemisphere (December–February).
- **Endemic**: Native and restricted to a certain place.

## Debates and Open Questions
The article notes a popular but incorrect belief that the river was named for its orange-colored water, contrasting with the Vaal River's name meaning "pale." There is also an ongoing discussion about renaming the colonial-era "Orange River" to indigenous names like Senqu or iGqili, as the current name is perceived by some to be associated with colonial subjugation.

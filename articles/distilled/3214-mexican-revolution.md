# Mexican Revolution

## Overview
The Mexican Revolution (1910–1920) was a decade-long civil war that overthrew Porfirio Díaz's dictatorship, destroyed the Federal Army, and transformed Mexico's government, society, and culture. It led to a new constitution, the rise of revolutionary generals, and the creation of a dominant political party that ruled for most of the 20th century.

## Timeline
- **1876–1911** — Porfirio Díaz rules Mexico in the Porfiriato.
- **Nov 1910** — Francisco I. Madero issues the Plan de San Luis Potosí, calling for revolt.
- **May 1911** — Díaz resigns and exiles after the Treaty of Ciudad Juárez.
- **Feb 1913** — Military coup overthrows and assassinates President Madero; Victoriano Huerta seizes power.
- **1913–1914** — Constitutionalist Army, led by Venustiano Carranza, fights Huerta.
- **Jul 1914** — Huerta resigns; the Federal Army is disbanded.
- **Oct–Dec 1914** — Convention of Aguascalientes fails; civil war resumes between Constitutionalists and the Convention Army (Villa and Zapata).
- **Apr 1915** — Constitutionalist General Álvaro Obregón defeats Pancho Villa at Celaya.
- **Feb 1917** — The Constitution of Mexico is promulgated.
- **Apr 1919** — Emiliano Zapata is assassinated by agents of President Carranza.
- **1920** — Sonoran generals overthrow and kill Carranza.
- **1929** — Plutarco Elías Calles founds the Partido Nacional Revolucionario, precursor to the PRI.

## Body

### The Porfiriato and the Outbreak of Revolution
Porfirio Díaz's regime (1876–1911) promoted economic growth but concentrated wealth and suppressed opposition. By 1910, labor unrest (e.g., Cananea and Río Blanco strikes) and peasant land loss created discontent. When Díaz jailed electoral opponent Francisco I. Madero and declared himself winner, Madero called for armed uprising. Rebellions overwhelmed the Federal Army, forcing Díaz to resign in May 1911.

### Madero's Presidency and the Huerta Coup
Madero was elected in 1911 but proved politically inexperienced. He demobilized revolutionary forces and retained the old army, leaving his government fragile. He faced rebellion from Emiliano Zapata in Morelos, demanding land reform. In February 1913, General Victoriano Huerta overthrew Madero in a coup; Madero was assassinated. This triggered a bloodier war phase.

### Civil War and Constitutional Victory
Huerta was opposed by Carranza's Constitutionalist Army. After Huerta fled in July 1914, factions met at the Convention of Aguascalientes but failed to agree, leading to civil war between Carranza's forces and an alliance of Pancho Villa and Emiliano Zapata. Carranza's faction, led by Obregón, prevailed by 1915. Carranza then convened a constitutional convention.

### The 1917 Constitution and Revolutionary Consolidation
The 1917 Constitution incorporated radical demands: state power to expropriate land and resources (Article 27), strong labor rights (Article 123), and restrictions on the Catholic Church (Article 3). Carranza became president but did not fully implement reforms. After his overthrow and death in 1920, revolutionary generals held the presidency, consolidating a strong central state and creating the dominant PRI party. President Lázaro Cárdenas (1934–1940) later implemented agrarian reform and nationalized oil, fulfilling key revolutionary goals.

### Zapata and the Revolution in Morelos
Emiliano Zapata led the southern revolution from Morelos. His peasant army focused solely on agrarian reform. After Madero failed to deliver land promises, Zapata rebelled, issuing the Plan of Ayala in 1911. He remained in arms until his assassination in 1919. The agrarian reforms enacted in Morelos proved irreversible.

## Terms
*   **Porfiriato:** Díaz's rule (1876–1911), marked by growth, inequality, and repression.
*   **Hacendado:** Wealthy owner of a large estate (hacienda).
*   **Rurales:** Elite rural police force created by Díaz.
*   **Plan de San Luis Potosí:** Madero's 1910 manifesto calling for uprising.
*   **Constitutionalist Army:** Carranza's faction that fought Huerta and later Villa and Zapata.
*   **Convention of Aguascalientes:** 1914 meeting that failed to unify factions, leading to civil war.
*   **Ejido:** Communal land tenure system established by the 1917 Constitution.
*   **Institutional Revolutionary Party (PRI):** Dominant party founded in 1929 to institutionalize the revolution.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **When did the Revolution end?** Dates range from 1920 to 1940 or 1946.
*   **Was it a true revolution?** Some argue it was a series of regional conflicts, not a unified social revolution.
*   **The role of foreign powers:** U.S. involvement is documented, but influence from Germany and Britain is studied.
*   **Historical memory:** Popular heroes (Villa, Zapata) often differ from the official post-revolutionary narrative.

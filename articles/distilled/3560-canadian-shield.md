# Canadian Shield

## Overview
The Canadian Shield is a vast, ancient geological region forming the core of the North American continent. It is a geologic shield—a large area of exposed Precambrian igneous and high-grade metamorphic rock—characterized by thin soils, numerous lakes, and significant mineral wealth. Its formation and current landscape are the result of billions of years of geological activity and severe glaciation.

## Timeline
- **c. 4.28 Ga** — Estimated age of the Canadian Shield.
- **c. 2.5 Ga** — Formation of the ancient continent Arctica, of which the Shield is a part.
- **c. 2.7 Ga** — Formation of the Sturgeon Lake Caldera complex and many greenstone belts.
- **Over 500 Ma** — Erosion of original tall mountains to the current low-relief topography.
- **Last Ice Ages** — Severe glaciation scrapes the Shield down to bare rock, creating thin soils and a deranged drainage system.

## Body

### Geology and Formation
The Canadian Shield is among the oldest geological areas on Earth, with rock dating from 2.5 to 4.2 billion years. It was originally a region of very tall mountains (about 12,000 m) with intense volcanic activity, but over 500 million years of erosion transformed them into rolling hills. This erosion exposed the roots of these mountains, now visible as greenstone belts—belts of metamorphosed volcanic rock surrounded by granitic rock. The Shield is the largest exposed part of the North American Craton (or Laurentia), the ancient bedrock core of the continent. It contains the Mackenzie dike swarm, the largest known on Earth, and features like the Sudbury Basin, an ancient meteorite impact crater rich in minerals.

### Ecology and Landscape
The Shield's surface is defined by thin soil lying on bedrock with many bare outcrops, a direct result of glaciation scraping the land clean. This poor, often frozen soil and the compacting effects of glaciation lead to poor drainage, creating many marshes, bogs (muskegs), and the region's characteristic multitude of lakes and rivers. The landscape supports vast boreal forests in the south, which transition to taiga and then tundra in the north. These ecosystems are home to mammals like caribou, moose, and bears, as well as numerous fish and bird species.

### Mining and Economics
The Canadian Shield is one of the world's richest areas for mineral ores, including nickel, gold, silver, and copper. Major mining towns like Sudbury, Ontario, extract these resources. The Shield's mineral wealth is often associated with its ancient greenstone belts and unique geological features. For example, the Flin Flon belt is a major copper-zinc-gold district. Recent diamond discoveries in the Northwest Territories are linked to kimberlite pipes that bring diamonds from deep within the craton's mantle to the surface.

## Terms
- **Geologic Shield:** A large area of exposed Precambrian igneous and high-grade metamorphic rocks, forming the ancient core of a continent.
- **Craton:** The stable, ancient interior portion of a continent, typically composed of Precambrian rock.
- **Precambrian:** The vast span of geological time before the Cambrian Period, covering about 88% of Earth's history.
- **Greenstone Belt:** A zone of metamorphosed volcanic rock surrounded by granitic rock, often containing valuable mineral deposits.
- **Deranged Drainage:** A pattern of streams and lakes that is chaotic and irregular, caused by glacial disruption of original watersheds.
- **Post-glacial Rebound:** The rise of land masses after the removal of the huge weight of ice sheets from glaciation.
- **Kimberlite Pipe:** A vertical, carrot-shaped volcanic conduit that can bring diamonds from the mantle to the surface.

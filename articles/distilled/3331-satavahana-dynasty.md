# Satavahana dynasty

## Overview
The Satavahanas were an ancient Indian dynasty that ruled the Deccan region from the late 2nd century BCE to the early 3rd century CE. They played a crucial role in stabilizing the post-Mauryan Deccan, resisting foreign invasions, and fostering trade and cultural exchange between northern and southern India. Their patronage of art, architecture, and literature left a lasting legacy on Indian civilization.

## Timeline
- **Late 2nd century BCE** — Most scholars date the beginning of Satavahana rule.
- **c. 110 BCE** — Earliest dated inscription (Kanaganahalli) of ruler Simuka (Chimuka).
- **c. 1st century BCE** — Satakarni I performs Vedic sacrifices and expands territory.
- **c. 70 BCE–c. 30 CE** — Satakarni II builds gateways at Sanchi Stupa.
- **c. 15–40 CE** — Western Kshatrapa ruler Nahapana expands into former Satavahana territory.
- **c. 60–85 CE** — Gautamiputra Satakarni revives the dynasty and defeats the Western Satraps.
- **c. 85–125 CE** — Vasisthiputra Pulamavi rules a prosperous kingdom and engages in maritime trade.
- **c. 130 CE** — Rudradaman I of the Western Satraps defeats a Satavahana ruler.
- **c. 170–199 CE** — Sri Yajna Satakarni briefly revives Satavahana power.
- **c. 225 CE** — Pulumavi IV, the last main-line king, dies; the empire fragments.

## Body

### Origins and Homeland
The dynasty's origin is debated. The Puranas call them "Andhras," leading some scholars to propose an eastern Deccan (Andhra Pradesh/Telangana) homeland, supported by early coins found at Kotilingala. Others argue for a western Deccan (Maharashtra) origin, citing the concentration of early inscriptions in that region. The dynasty's name, "Satavahana," has multiple proposed etymologies, including a Sanskritized form of an indigenous name or a connection to the solar dynasty.

### Foundation and Early Expansion
The dynasty was founded by Simuka, who overthrew the Kanva dynasty. His successors, Kanha and Satakarni I, expanded the kingdom westward and performed Vedic rituals, establishing Brahmanical authority. Satakarni II further expanded eastward and is credited with embellishing the Sanchi Stupa. The dynasty's early history is reconstructed from Puranic lists, inscriptions, and coins, leading to chronological uncertainties.

### Zenith and Conflict with Western Satraps
The dynasty reached its peak under Gautamiputra Satakarni (c. 60–85 CE), who defeated the Western Satrap ruler Nahapana and restored Satavahana dominance. His son, Pulamavi, maintained this extensive territory and promoted maritime trade. Later, the Satavahanas faced renewed conflict with the Western Satraps under Rudradaman I, who defeated a Satavahana ruler but spared him due to a marriage alliance, leading to a loss of some northern territories.

### Decline and Fragmentation
After a brief revival under Sri Yajna Satakarni, the dynasty weakened due to the rise of feudatories. Following the death of Pulumavi IV (c. 225 CE), the empire fragmented into five smaller kingdoms, including the Abhiras, Andhra Ikshvakus, and Pallavas, marking the end of unified Satavahana rule.

### Administration, Economy, and Religion
The Satavahanas administered a hierarchical system with feudatories like Maharathis and Mahabhojas. They controlled fertile river valleys and mineral resources, boosting agriculture and crafts. Their control of the coastline facilitated trade with the Roman Empire. The rulers patronized both Brahmanism (performing Vedic sacrifices) and Buddhism (donating to monasteries), reflecting a syncretic religious culture.

### Language, Art, and Coinage
The dynasty used Prakrit (a Middle Indo-Aryan language) for inscriptions and courtly literature, though Sanskrit was occasionally used. They were pioneers in issuing portrait coinage, often in lead and copper, with legends in Prakrit and sometimes Dravidian languages. Their artistic patronage resulted in the Amaravati school of sculpture and the earliest surviving paintings at the Ajanta Caves.

## Terms
- **Prakrit**: A broad term for Middle Indo-Aryan languages, used extensively in Satavahana inscriptions and literature.
- **Western Satraps (Kshatrapas)**: Indo-Scythian rulers of western India who were major rivals of the Satavahanas.
- **Varna**: The Hindu social class system; the Satavahanas' varna (Brahmana, Kshatriya, or Shudra) is debated.
- **Ahara**: The largest administrative subdivision of the Satavahana kingdom, often named after its governor.
- **Maharathi**: A hereditary feudal lord under the Satavahanas who could grant villages and maintain relations with the royal family.
- **Amaravati Stupa**: A major Buddhist monument in the Krishna River Valley, built and decorated under Satavahana patronage.
- **Gaha Sattasai**: A collection of Maharashtri Prakrit poems attributed to the Satavahana king Hāla.
- **Naneghat**: A mountain pass with inscriptions recording Vedic sacrifices performed by the Satavahana royal family.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Original Homeland**: Scholars continue to debate whether the Satavahanas originated in the eastern Deccan (Andhra region) or the western Deccan (Maharashtra), based on conflicting Puranic, epigraphic, and numismatic evidence.
- **Chronology**: The exact dates of rulers like Gautamiputra Satakarni are contested, with scholarly estimates varying by several decades.
- **Varna Status**: The dynasty's social status is unclear; Puranic texts call them Shudra, while some inscriptions claim Brahmana status, leading to theories of varna mobility or later reinterpretation.
- **Identity of Defeated Rulers**: It is debated which Satavahana ruler was defeated by the Western Satrap Rudradaman I, with candidates including Gautamiputra, Pulamavi, or a later king.

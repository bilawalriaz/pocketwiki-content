# Margaret Court

## Overview
Margaret Court is an Australian former tennis player who holds the all-time record for Grand Slam titles, with 24 in women's singles and a total of 64 across singles, doubles, and mixed doubles. Her dominance spanned the amateur and Open Eras, and she is celebrated for her athletic achievements, though her later public views on social issues have sparked significant debate.

## Timeline
- **16 July 1942** — Born in Albury, New South Wales.
- **1960** — Wins first Australian Championships singles title at age 17.
- **1962** — Wins three of four Grand Slam singles tournaments.
- **1963** — Becomes first Australian woman to win Wimbledon singles; completes career Grand Slam in singles.
- **1966** — Temporarily retires after a tournament in Munich.
- **1968** — Returns to tennis as the Open Era begins.
- **1970** — Wins all four Grand Slam singles titles (the Grand Slam).
- **1972** — Gives birth to first child; returns to tour later in the year.
- **1973** — Wins three Grand Slam singles titles; loses exhibition match to Bobby Riggs.
- **1977** — Retires permanently after learning she is pregnant with her fourth child.
- **1991** — Ordained as an independent Pentecostal minister.
- **2003** — Melbourne Park's Show Court One renamed Margaret Court Arena.

## Body

### Early Life and Career Beginnings
Born into a modest family in Albury, Court was a multi-sport athlete who discovered tennis at age eight. She was coached by local professional Wally Rutter before moving to Melbourne at 16 to train full-time with former world No. 1 Frank Sedgman. Sedgman's regimen emphasized intense physical fitness, including weightlifting and sandhill running, which became a hallmark of her career.

### Tennis Career and Dominance
Court turned professional and won her first major title at the 1960 Australian Championships. She completed a career Grand Slam in singles by age 21 with her 1963 Wimbledon victory. After a brief hiatus in 1966-67, she returned to dominate the newly formed Open Era, achieving the Grand Slam (winning all four majors in a calendar year) in 1970. Her career was punctuated by breaks for childbirth, but she returned to win three more major singles titles in 1973. She retired in 1977.

Court's records are unparalleled: 24 singles majors, 19 women's doubles titles, and 21 mixed doubles titles. She is one of only three players to achieve a career "boxed set" (winning every type of major title at all four tournaments) and the only one to win a double boxed set. Her powerful serve, net game, and fitness earned her the nickname "The Aussie Amazon."

### Later Life and Ministry
After retiring, Court became involved in Pentecostalism, was ordained as a minister in 1991, and founded Margaret Court Ministries and the Victory Life Centre church in Perth. She has also been active in community outreach through her charity, Margaret Court Community Outreach.

### Personal Views and Controversy
Since the 2010s, Court has been a vocal critic of same-sex marriage and LGBTQ+ rights, leading to public criticism from fellow tennis legends and calls to rename Margaret Court Arena. Her views have been contrasted with her sporting legacy, creating an ongoing public debate about how to honor her athletic achievements while acknowledging her controversial statements.

## Terms
- **Grand Slam**: The four major annual tennis tournaments: Australian Open, French Open, Wimbledon, and US Open.
- **Open Era**: The period in professional tennis beginning in 1968, when professionals were allowed to compete with amateurs in Grand Slam tournaments.
- **Boxed Set**: Winning every possible major title—singles, women's doubles, and mixed doubles—at all four Grand Slam tournaments over a career.
- **Career Grand Slam**: Winning each of the four major tournaments at least once during a career.
- **Serve and Volley**: An aggressive playing style where a player serves and immediately moves to the net to volley.
- **Fed Cup**: The premier international team competition in women's tennis, now called the Billie Jean King Cup.
- **Pentecostalism**: A Christian movement emphasizing direct personal experience of God, often through baptism in the Holy Spirit.

## Debates and Open Questions
The primary debate concerns the reconciliation of Court's historic sporting achievements with her public opposition to LGBTQ+ rights. This has led to ongoing controversy over whether facilities named in her honor, like Margaret Court Arena, should be renamed. There is also discussion about how tennis governing bodies, like Tennis Australia, should acknowledge her records while distancing themselves from her personal views.

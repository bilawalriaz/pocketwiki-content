# Motion

## Overview
Motion is the continuous change in position or configuration of a physical system relative to a reference frame over time. Because no absolute frame of reference exists, all motion is relative, and everything in the universe—from subatomic particles to galaxies—is in constant motion. Physics describes motion through two primary frameworks: classical mechanics (for macroscopic objects at speeds far below light speed) and quantum mechanics (for atomic and subatomic scales), with relativistic mechanics bridging the gap at high velocities. The study of motion without reference to causes is kinematics; the study of forces causing motion is dynamics.

## Timeline
- **July 5, 1687** — Isaac Newton publishes *Philosophiæ Naturalis Principia Mathematica*, establishing three laws of classical mechanics.
- **Early 20th century** — Development of special relativity (Einstein) and general relativity (Einstein, building on Clifford), redefining velocity, gravity, and spacetime.
- **Early 20th century** — Formulation of quantum mechanics, introducing wave–particle duality and the Heisenberg uncertainty principle.
- **1929** — Edwin Hubble demonstrates galactic recession, providing primary evidence for the expansion of spacetime (Hubble's law).
- **2019** — SI base units redefined via the "explicit-constant formulation," fixing the speed of light in vacuum at exactly 299,792,458 m/s.

## Body

### Definition and Relativity of Motion
Motion is mathematically described using vector quantities: **displacement** (change in position with direction and distance), **velocity** (rate of change of displacement), and **acceleration** (rate of change of velocity). An object is at rest only relative to a specific frame of reference; modern physics rejects Isaac Newton's concept of absolute motion because no absolute frame exists. The concept applies broadly to objects, fields, radiation, spacetime curvature, and even abstract configurations like probability densities in quantum mechanics.

### Classical Mechanics
Classical mechanics describes macroscopic objects (projectiles, machinery, astronomical bodies) moving at speeds significantly slower than light. It is founded on **Newton's three laws of motion** (1687):
1.  **Law of inertia**: A body remains at rest or in uniform motion unless acted upon by an external force.
2.  **Force law**: Force ($\vec{F}$) equals the rate of change of momentum ($\Delta m\vec{v}/\Delta t$); for constant mass, $\vec{F} = m\vec{a}$.
3.  **Action–reaction**: For every force exerted by body A on body B, body B exerts an equal and opposite force ($-\vec{F}$) on body A.
These laws unified celestial and terrestrial motion, providing accurate predictions for engineering and orbital mechanics within their domain.

### Relativistic Mechanics
At velocities approaching the speed of light ($c$), classical mechanics fails. **Special relativity** redefines velocity as **rapidity** ($\varphi$), a hyperbolic angle where $\tanh \varphi = v/c$. Acceleration changes rapidity via **Lorentz transformations**. **General relativity** incorporates gravity by describing a curved spacetime using differential geometry (developed by W. K. Clifford and Albert Einstein). In this framework, gravity is not a force but a manifestation of spacetime curvature.

### Quantum Mechanics
Quantum mechanics governs atomic and subatomic particles (electrons, protons, quarks). It introduces **wave–particle duality**: matter and radiation exhibit both wave-like and particle-like behavior. Unlike classical mechanics, the **Heisenberg uncertainty principle** prevents simultaneous exact determination of a particle's position and velocity. Quantum effects also explain macroscopic phenomena like superfluidity, superconductivity, and biological mechanisms (e.g., smell receptors, protein structures).

### Orders of Magnitude: Cosmic to Subatomic Scales
Motion occurs at all scales, often imperceptible to human senses due to lack of a local reference frame (Newton's third law) or minuscule magnitude.
*   **Universe**: Spacetime itself expands, stretching all distances; verified by Hubble's law (galaxies recede from Earth).
*   **Galaxy**: The Milky Way moves at ~600 km/s relative to nearby galaxies (~582 km/s relative to the cosmic microwave background).
*   **Sun/Solar System**: The Sun orbits the Galactic Center at 210–240 km/s; planets co-move.
*   **Earth**: Rotates at 0.465 km/s (equator); orbits the Sun at ~30 km/s.
*   **Continents**: Drift via plate tectonics at ~2.54 cm/year (range: Eurasian Plate ~21 mm/yr to Cocos Plate ~75 mm/yr).
*   **Internal Body**: Blood flows ~0.33 m/s in major vessels; peristalsis moves food at ~3.48 km/hr; lymph moves ~0.0000097 m/s.
*   **Cells**: Cytoplasmic streaming and motor proteins (powered by ATP hydrolysis) move vesicles at ~0.00000152 m/s.
*   **Particles**: All matter above absolute zero exhibits random thermal motion (vibration, collision), perceived as temperature.
*   **Subatomic**: Electrons exist in a probability cloud (not classical orbits); protons/neutrons move within the nucleus due to repulsion and angular momentum.

### Light and the Universal Speed Limit
Light moves in vacuum at exactly **299,792,458 m/s** ($c$), the upper limit for energy, matter, information, and causation. This speed is **invariant**—identical for all observers regardless of their motion—making $c$ a fundamental natural constant. Since the 2019 SI revision, the metre is defined by fixing this exact value. **Apparent superluminal motion** (observed in relativistic jets from black holes or light echoes) results from geometric projection effects and finite light travel time, not actual violation of $c$.

### Classification of Motion
Motion types are categorized by trajectory and force characteristics:
*   **Simple harmonic motion**: Oscillation where restoring force $\propto$ negative displacement (e.g., pendulum).
*   **Linear/Rectilinear motion**: Straight-line path.
*   **Circular/Rotatory motion**: Path around a fixed point or axis.
*   **Curvilinear motion**: Curved path (planar or 3D).
*   **Rolling motion**: Rotation combined with translation (e.g., wheel).
*   **Brownian motion**: Random movement of microscopic particles.
*   **Projectile motion**: Uniform horizontal velocity + vertical accelerated motion (gravity).
*   **Fundamental motions**: Linear, Circular, Oscillation, Wave, Relative, Rotary.

## Terms
- ****Displacement**** — Vector quantity representing the change in position of an object (distance and direction) from a reference point.
- ****Velocity**** — Vector quantity: rate of change of displacement with respect to time (speed with direction).
- ****Acceleration**** — Vector quantity: rate of change of velocity with respect to time.
- ****Kinematics**** — Branch of physics describing motion without reference to its causes (forces).
- ****Dynamics**** — Branch of physics studying forces and their effects on motion.
- ****Reference Frame**** — Coordinate system or perspective relative to which position and motion are measured; no absolute frame exists.
- ****Rapidity**** — In special relativity, the hyperbolic angle $\varphi$ where $\tanh \varphi = v/c$; replaces classical velocity at high speeds.
- ****Lorentz Transformations**** — Mathematical rules in special relativity relating space and time coordinates between inertial frames; govern how acceleration changes rapidity.
- ****Wave–Particle Duality**** — Quantum principle that matter and radiation exhibit both wave-like and particle-like properties simultaneously.
- ****Heisenberg Uncertainty Principle**** — Quantum limit: the position and momentum (velocity) of a subatomic particle cannot both be precisely known simultaneously.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Unification of gravity and quantum mechanics**: General relativity (curved spacetime) and quantum mechanics (probabilistic particle fields) remain mathematically incompatible; a theory of quantum gravity is unresolved.
*   **Nature of dark matter/energy**: Observed cosmic motions (galaxy rotation, universal expansion) imply unseen mass/energy components not explained by the Standard Model.
*   **Measurement problem in quantum mechanics**: The transition from probabilistic wavefunction to definite measured outcome lacks a consensus physical mechanism.
*   **Absolute vs. relational motion**: While Newton's absolute space is rejected, the ontological status of spacetime in general relativity (substantivalism vs. relationalism) remains a philosophical debate.

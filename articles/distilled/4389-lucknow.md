# Lucknow

## Overview
Lucknow is the capital and largest city of Uttar Pradesh, India, and a major cultural, economic, and administrative center in North India. Historically the seat of the Nawabs of Awadh, it is renowned for its syncretic Hindu-Muslim culture (Ganga-Jamuni Tehzeeb), refined cuisine, and architectural heritage. Today, it is an emerging hub for IT, manufacturing, and education, while remaining a key political and military headquarters.

## Timeline
- **6th century BCE** — Part of Kosala, one of the 16 Mahajanapadas.
- **1394–1478** — Part of the Sharqi Sultanate of Jaunpur.
- **c. 1555** — Incorporated into the Mughal Empire by Emperor Humayun.
- **1722** — Saadat Khan establishes the Nawab dynasty of Awadh.
- **1775** — Nawab Asaf-ud-Daula shifts the Awadh capital from Faizabad to Lucknow.
- **1801** — Treaty makes Awadh a vassal of the British East India Company.
- **1856** — British annex Awadh for "maladministration"; Nawab Wajid Ali Shah exiled.
- **1857** — Major center of the Indian Rebellion; site of the Siege of Lucknow.
- **1916** — Lucknow Pact signed at Indian National Congress session; first meeting of Gandhi, Nehru, and Jinnah.
- **1947** — Indian independence; Lucknow becomes capital of Uttar Pradesh.
- **2017** — Lucknow Metro rapid transit system begins operations.

## Body

### Historical Evolution
Lucknow's recorded history begins as part of the ancient Kosala kingdom. Its modern prominence started in 1775 when Nawab Asaf-ud-Daula made it the capital of Awadh. The Nawabs fostered a unique, refined court culture and built iconic monuments like the Bara Imambara. British influence grew after the 1801 treaty, leading to outright annexation in 1856. The city was a pivotal center during the 1857 Rebellion, notably during the Siege of Lucknow. Post-independence, it solidified its role as Uttar Pradesh's political capital.

### Architecture and Culture
Lucknow's architecture blends Mughal, Turkish, and European styles, exemplified by the Bara Imambara, Rumi Darwaza, and La Martiniere College. The city is the heart of Awadhi cuisine, famous for slow-cooked (dum) dishes and kebabs. It is a major center for Shia Islam and Muharram processions. Traditional arts include Kathak dance, Urdu poetry, and the intricate Chikan embroidery, which has Geographical Indication status. The residents' polite, polished speech is a noted cultural trait.

### Economy and Infrastructure
Lucknow's economy is driven by services, IT, manufacturing, and government administration. It hosts major companies like Tata Motors and Hindustan Aeronautics Limited, and is a growing IT hub. The city is the headquarters of the Indian Army's Central Command. Infrastructure includes Chaudhary Charan Singh International Airport, an extensive railway network, and the Lucknow Metro. The Lucknow Outer Ring Road improves regional connectivity.

### Administration and Demographics
The city is administered by the Lucknow Municipal Corporation and the Lucknow Development Authority. It is the seat of the Uttar Pradesh government and a bench of the Allahabad High Court. According to the 2011 census, the city had a population of 2.8 million, with Hindi and Urdu as primary languages. The literacy rate is 84.72%, significantly higher than the state average.

## Terms
- **Nawabs**: Hereditary Muslim rulers of the princely state of Awadh, known for their patronage of arts and culture.
- **Ganga-Jamuni Tehzeeb**: The syncretic Hindu-Muslim culture of the region, named after the confluence of the Ganges and Yamuna rivers.
- **Chikankari**: A traditional and delicate white-on-white embroidery technique from Lucknow, recognized with a Geographical Indication tag.
- **Dum Pukht**: A slow-cooking technique using sealed pots, central to Awadhi cuisine.
- **Kathak**: A classical Indian dance form that originated in the temples of North India and was refined in Lucknow's courts.
- **Marsiya**: A genre of elegiac poetry, primarily in Urdu, commemorating the martyrdom of Imam Husain.
- **Imambara**: A congregation hall for Shia commemoration ceremonies, often an architectural landmark.
- **Mahajanapadas**: The sixteen great kingdoms or republics of ancient India in the late Vedic period.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The exact origin of the city's name is debated, with theories linking it to the Hindu deity Lakshmana, the goddess Lakshmi, or an architect named Lakhna Ahir.
- There is ongoing criticism regarding the lack of a High Court bench in western Uttar Pradesh, which generates over 50% of the state's GDP but must travel to Allahabad or Lucknow for hearings.
- Despite being the capital, Lucknow ranks only 7th in per capita income within Uttar Pradesh, lagging behind cities like Noida and Meerut in industrial growth.

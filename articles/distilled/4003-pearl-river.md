# Pearl River

## Overview
The Pearl River is a major river system in southern China, third-longest in the country and second-largest by volume. It drains a vast basin across several provinces and is economically and geographically significant, flowing through the major city of Guangzhou and forming the Pearl River Delta, a key region separating Macau/Zhuhai from Hong Kong/Shenzhen.

## Timeline
- **c. 3000 BC** — Earliest evidence of rice cultivation in the Pearl River Delta region.
- **214 BC** — Construction of the Lingqu Canal connects the Pearl River system to the Yangtze.
- **7th century AD** — The Grand Canal is extended, linking the Pearl River to northern China.
- **16th century** — Portuguese traders establish a settlement at Macau on the Pearl River Estuary.
- **1839-1842** — The First Opium War is fought, with key battles in the Pearl River Delta.
- **1950s** — Major land reclamation and flood control projects begin in the delta.
- **1979** — Shenzhen is designated a Special Economic Zone, catalyzing delta development.
- **1997** — Hong Kong is returned to China, located on the Pearl River Estuary.
- **1999** — Macau is returned to China, also on the estuary.
- **2010s** — The Hong Kong–Zhuhai–Macau Bridge is constructed across the estuary.

## Body

### Geography and Hydrology
The Pearl River system is formed by the confluence of the Xi (West), Bei (North), and Dong (East) rivers, which all flow into the South China Sea through the Pearl River Delta. Measured from the furthest source of the Xi River, the entire Pearl–Xi–Xun–Qian–Hongshui–Nanpan system is 2,400 km (1,500 mi) long. The Pearl River Basin covers 453,700 km² (175,200 mi²), draining most of Guangdong and Guangxi provinces, parts of Yunnan, Guizhou, Hunan, and Jiangxi, and northern Vietnam. The specific branch named the Pearl River is the widest distributary in the delta but is notably short; its waters are first called the Pearl River just north of Guangzhou. Its estuary, the Bocca Tigris, is dredged for ocean vessels and separates the delta into sections, with the Wanshan Archipelago at its southern tip.

### Name and Cultural Significance
The name "Pearl River" originates from the pearl-colored shells found in its riverbed as it flows through Guangzhou. The river is central to the culture and economy of the region. Numerous brands, such as the Zhujiang Brewery and Pearl River Bridge food manufacturer, are named after it. A 500 kV power line, suspended from three of the world's tallest pylons, crosses the river near the Nansha Bridge.

### Tributaries and Crossings
The Pearl River system has numerous tributaries, including the Bei, Dong, Xi, Yu, Yong, Zuo, You, Xun, Liu, Rong, Hongshui, Beipan, Nanpan, Ba, Gui, and Li rivers. The delta is crossed by many bridges and tunnels, facilitating transport between its major cities and special economic zones.

## Terms
- **Distributary**: A stream that branches off and flows away from a main river channel, common in deltas.
- **Estuary**: The tidal mouth of a large river, where the tide meets the stream.
- **Basin**: The area of land drained by a river and its tributaries.
- **Delta**: A landform created by deposition of sediment carried by a river as its flow leaves its mouth.
- **Liangguang**: A collective term for Guangdong and Guangxi provinces.
- **Bocca Tigris**: The specific name for the estuary of the Pearl River, meaning "Tiger's Mouth."
- **Special Economic Zone (SEZ)**: A designated area in China with more liberal economic regulations to attract foreign investment.

## Debates and Open Questions
The source of the Pearl River is debated. While the Nanpan River is often cited as the source of the longest channel, some definitions place the source in the Hongshui River. The exact length of the system varies depending on which source and channel are measured. The environmental impact of rapid industrialization and urbanization in the Pearl River Delta on water quality and ecosystems is a ongoing concern.

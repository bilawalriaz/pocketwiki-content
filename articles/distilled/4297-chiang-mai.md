# Chiang Mai

## Overview
Chiang Mai is the largest city in northern Thailand and the country's second-largest urban center by population. Founded in 1296 as the capital of the Lan Na kingdom, it is a major cultural, economic, and tourist hub situated in the mountainous Thai highlands. Its historical significance, Buddhist heritage, and modern development initiatives, including a push to become a "smart city," make it a key regional center.

## Timeline
- **1296** — Founded by King Mangrai as the new capital of Lan Na.
- **1556** — Occupied by the Toungoo Empire.
- **1774** — Formally became part of the Thonburi Kingdom after Burmese were driven out.
- **1776–1791** — Abandoned due to Burmese counterattacks.
- **1915** — A sanitary district was created, the precursor to the modern municipality.
- **1935** — Upgraded to a city municipality (*thesaban nakhon*).
- **1983** — Municipal area enlarged to its current 40.2 km².
- **2006** — Site of the Chiang Mai Initiative between ASEAN and China, Japan, South Korea.
- **2017** — Declared a UNESCO Creative City for Crafts and Folk Arts.
- **2023** — Urban population estimated at ~1.2 million; a new city bus system launched.

## Body

### History and Governance
Chiang Mai was founded in 1296 by King Mangrai on a site previously called Wiang Nopburi, succeeding Chiang Rai as the Lan Na capital. Its location on the Ping River and near trade routes ensured its historic importance. The city declined with Lan Na, was occupied by Burma in 1556, and was later incorporated into the Thonburi Kingdom in 1774. After a period of abandonment, it was reoccupied. The modern municipality began as a sanitary district in 1915 and became a city municipality in 1935. The municipal area was last officially enlarged in 1983, but the urban area has since sprawled into six neighboring districts, creating a metropolitan region with over 1 million residents. The city government is led by an elected mayor and a municipal council.

### Geography and Climate
The city municipality covers 40.2 km², while the defined urban area spans 405 km². Chiang Mai sits in a basin in the Thai highlands at an average elevation of 300 meters, west of the Ping River. It is flanked by mountain ranges, including the Thanon Thong Chai range with the prominent peaks of Doi Pui and Doi Suthep. The climate is tropical savanna (Köppen Aw), with warm to hot weather year-round. The city experiences a significant air pollution problem from December to April, with fine-particle dust levels often exceeding safety standards.

### Economy and Tourism
Tourism is a major economic driver, with over 14 million visitors recorded in 2013. The city is renowned for its 117 Buddhist temples (*wat*), including the famous Wat Phra That Doi Suthep, and its cultural festivals like Loi Krathong and Songkran. In 2017, a "smart city" initiative was launched, investing in technology for transportation, agriculture, healthcare, and tourism. This includes bike-sharing, agricultural drones, and augmented reality apps for historical sites. The city is also developing as a medical tourism hub.

### Culture and Society
Chiang Mai is a center of Lanna culture. While Thai is widely spoken, the Northern Thai language (*Kham Mueang*) is also used. The city is known for its distinctive cuisine, including *khan tok* meals, and has been called the "vegan capital of Asia." Major festivals blend Buddhist and local Lanna traditions. Education is served by several universities, with Chiang Mai University being the first government university established outside Bangkok (1964).

## Terms
- **Lan Na**: A historical kingdom in northern Thailand, with Chiang Mai as its capital from 1296.
- **Thai highlands**: The mountainous region in northern Thailand where Chiang Mai is located.
- **Ping River**: A major tributary of the Chao Phraya River, on which Chiang Mai is situated.
- **Thesaban nakhon**: The official designation for a city municipality in Thailand.
- **Khwaeng**: An electoral ward or sub-district within a Thai municipality.
- **Wat**: A Buddhist temple or monastery.
- **Loi Krathong**: A festival where decorated floats are released on water; in Chiang Mai, it coincides with Yi Peng, the sky lantern festival.
- **Songkran**: The traditional Thai New Year festival, celebrated in mid-April.
- **Smart city**: An urban area that uses information and communication technology (ICT) and the Internet of Things (IoT) to optimize city operations and services.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Urban Boundaries**: The official municipal area (40.2 km²) is outdated and does not reflect the actual urban sprawl. A fourth revision of the urban area boundary (405 km²) is under public debate, with proposals to expand into more adjacent subdistricts and forest areas.
- **Population Figures**: Official statistics do not count expatriates, non-permanent residents, or many migrant workers. The real population is estimated to be as high as 1.5 million, significantly higher than the official urban figure of ~1.2 million.
- **Air Pollution**: The annual smoke pollution crisis (December–April) is a major environmental and health issue, with causes including forest fires and agricultural burning. Solutions are actively debated and implemented.
- **Tourism Impact**: While tourism boosts the economy, it strains natural resources, contributes to pollution, and causes traffic congestion, prompting initiatives like non-motorized transport systems.

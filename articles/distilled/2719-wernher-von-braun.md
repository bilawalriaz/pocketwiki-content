# Wernher von Braun

## Overview
Wernher von Braun was a German-American aerospace engineer who became the chief architect of NASA's Saturn V rocket, which propelled the Apollo missions to the Moon. His career spanned from leading Nazi Germany's V-2 rocket program in World War II to becoming a central figure in the U.S. space program, making him a pivotal but controversial figure in the history of rocketry and space exploration.

## Timeline
- **23 March 1912** — Born in Wirsitz, Province of Posen, German Empire.
- **1932** — Graduated with a diploma in mechanical engineering from Technische Hochschule Berlin.
- **27 July 1934** — Received a doctorate in physics from the University of Berlin.
- **3 October 1942** — First successful launch of an A-4 (later V-2) rocket at Peenemünde.
- **20 June 1944** — V-2 rocket MW 18014 becomes the first artificial object to cross the Kármán line into space.
- **2 May 1945** — Surrenders to U.S. forces in Austria.
- **1 October 1945** — Transfer to the United States under Operation Paperclip is publicly announced.
- **31 January 1958** — His team's Juno I rocket launches America's first satellite, Explorer 1.
- **1 July 1960** — Becomes the first director of NASA's Marshall Space Flight Center.
- **16 July 1969** — Saturn V rocket launches the Apollo 11 mission to the Moon.
- **26 May 1972** — Retires from NASA.
- **16 June 1977** — Dies of pancreatic cancer in Alexandria, Virginia.

## Body

### Early Life and German Career
Born into Prussian nobility, von Braun's passion for astronomy and spaceflight was ignited by a telescope and the book *Die Rakete zu den Planetenräumen* by Hermann Oberth. He studied physics and engineering, joining the German Army's rocket program in 1932. He became the technical director at the Peenemünde Army Research Center, where his team developed the A-4 rocket, later renamed the V-2 (*Vergeltungswaffe 2* or "Vengeance Weapon 2"). The V-2 was the world's first long-range guided ballistic missile and the first artificial object to reach space. Its development relied on forced labor from concentration camp prisoners, resulting in more deaths during production than from the weapon itself.

### Nazi Party and SS Membership
Von Braun joined the Nazi Party in 1937 and the Allgemeine-SS in 1940, stating he did so under pressure to continue his rocket work. He was arrested by the Gestapo in 1944 for "defeatist" remarks but was released after intervention by Albert Speer, as he was deemed indispensable to the V-2 program. His exact level of involvement with and knowledge of Nazi atrocities, particularly the use of slave labor at the Mittelwerk factory, remains a subject of historical debate.

### Operation Paperclip and U.S. Army Career
At the end of World War II, von Braun and his team surrendered to the United States. They were brought to America under Operation Paperclip, a secret program to recruit German scientists. Initially working for the U.S. Army at Fort Bliss, Texas, and later Redstone Arsenal in Huntsville, Alabama, von Braun's team refurbished V-2 rockets and developed new missiles, including the Redstone and Jupiter-C. The Jupiter-C was the basis for the Juno I rocket that launched Explorer 1, America's first satellite.

### NASA and the Apollo Program
In 1960, von Braun's team was transferred to the newly formed NASA, where he became director of the Marshall Space Flight Center. He was the chief architect of the Saturn V super heavy-lift launch vehicle, the rocket that successfully carried astronauts to the Moon during the Apollo program. His conservative engineering approach prioritized safety and redundancy. After the Moon landings, he advocated for a human mission to Mars but retired from NASA in 1972 due to budget cuts and a shift in national priorities.

### Popularizer and Later Life
Von Braun was a prolific popularizer of space travel, working with Walt Disney on television films and publishing articles in *Collier's* magazine that envisioned space stations and lunar missions. He became a U.S. citizen in 1955 and later in life underwent a religious conversion to Evangelical Christianity. He died in 1977, having received the National Medal of Science in 1975.

## Terms
- **Operation Paperclip**: A secret U.S. program to recruit scientists from Nazi Germany and other Axis nations after World War II.
- **V-2 (Vergeltungswaffe 2)**: The world's first long-range guided ballistic missile, developed by von Braun's team for Nazi Germany and used as a weapon against Allied cities.
- **Kármán line**: The internationally recognized boundary of space, at an altitude of 100 kilometers (62 miles) above sea level.
- **Saturn V**: The super heavy-lift launch vehicle developed by NASA under von Braun's leadership to carry the Apollo spacecraft to the Moon.
- **Allgemeine-SS**: The general branch of the Nazi Party's Schutzstaffel (SS), distinct from the combat units of the Waffen-SS.
- **Peenemünde Army Research Center**: The German military research facility on the Baltic coast where the V-2 rocket was developed.
- **Mittelwerk**: The underground factory near Nordhausen, Germany, where V-2 rockets were assembled using forced labor from the Mittelbau-Dora concentration camp.
- **Earth orbit rendezvous**: A mission profile where two spacecraft launch separately and dock in Earth orbit before proceeding to the Moon; von Braun initially favored this over the lunar orbit rendezvous method ultimately used for Apollo.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Moral Responsibility**: Historians debate the extent of von Braun's knowledge of and complicity in the use of slave labor and the atrocities committed at the Mittelwerk factory. While he acknowledged awareness of poor conditions, accounts conflict on whether he protested or directly benefited from the system.
- **Engineering Philosophy**: His conservative, safety-first approach is credited with the success of the Saturn V but is also cited as a reason the U.S. lost the early Space Race to the Soviet Union by delaying the first American crewed spaceflight.
- **Legacy**: Von Braun is simultaneously celebrated as the "father of space travel" for his Apollo work and criticized for his membership in the Nazi Party and SS, leading to the renaming of institutions that once bore his name.

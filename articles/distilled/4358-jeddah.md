# Jeddah

## Overview
Jeddah is the largest city in Saudi Arabia's Mecca Province and the country's main commercial center, located on the Red Sea coast. Its global significance stems from its role as the principal gateway for Muslim pilgrims traveling to the holy cities of Mecca and Medina, a function established in the 7th century. The city is a major international hub for trade, diplomacy, and culture, hosting the headquarters of the Organisation of Islamic Cooperation (OIC).

## Timeline
- **c. 115 BC** — Possible founding as a fishing hamlet by the Yemeni Quda'a tribe.
- **647 CE** — Caliph Uthman designates Jeddah as the main port for Mecca.
- **1517** — Ottoman Turks conquer the Mamluk Sultanate, taking control of Jeddah.
- **1813** — Egyptian forces under Muhammad Ali Pasha recapture Jeddah from the First Saudi State.
- **1925** — Jeddah comes under the control of the Al-Saud dynasty.
- **1947** — The remaining walls and gates of the old city are demolished.
- **1982** — A fire destroys some ancient buildings in the old town center, Al-Balad.
- **2014** — The old city of Jeddah (Al-Balad) is granted UNESCO World Heritage status.
- **2021** — Large-scale demolition begins for the Jeddah Central Project under Saudi Vision 2030.

## Body

### Historical Significance and Governance
Jeddah's prominence grew in 647 CE when Caliph Uthman made it the primary port for Hajj pilgrims. It has since been ruled by successive powers, including the Umayyads, Abbasids, Fatimids, Ayyubids, Mamluks, and Ottomans. The Ottomans fortified the city against Portuguese attacks in the 16th century. In the early 20th century, it was part of the Kingdom of Hejaz before being incorporated into the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia in 1925. The city's old quarter, Al-Balad, features traditional coral-stone tower houses and historic mosques.

### Economy and Infrastructure
Jeddah is Saudi Arabia's second-largest city and a primary commercial center. Its economy is anchored by the Jeddah Islamic Port, one of the busiest in the Middle East, and King Abdulaziz International Airport, a major hub for Hajj pilgrims. The city is a focus for capital investment and innovation. Key infrastructure includes the Haramain High Speed Railway connecting it to Mecca and Medina, and the proposed Jeddah Tower, intended to be the world's tallest building.

### Culture and Society
The city's society is shaped by its role as a pilgrimage gateway, hosting over 1,300 mosques and a diverse, multi-ethnic population. Jeddah is known for its open-air art, with numerous public sculptures installed during the 1970s-80s. Its cuisine reflects Hejazi traditions and influences from across the Muslim world. Major cultural events include the Red Sea International Film Festival and the Jeddah Season tourism initiative.

### Urban Challenges
Jeddah faces significant infrastructural challenges, including severe traffic congestion, a sewage system that cannot cope with the population, and recurrent, devastating floods (notably in 2009, 2011, 2015, 2017, and 2022) due to inadequate storm drainage. The city has also been a target for terrorist attacks, most notably the 2004 assault on the U.S. Consulate.

## Terms
- **Hajj**: The annual Islamic pilgrimage to Mecca, a religious duty for Muslims that must be carried out at least once in their lifetime.
- **Hejaz**: A historical region in western Saudi Arabia that includes the holy cities of Mecca and Medina.
- **Al-Balad**: The historic old city center of Jeddah, characterized by traditional architecture and a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
- **Tihamah**: The Red Sea coastal plain of the Hejaz region where Jeddah is located.
- **Mashrabiya**: A type of latticework window, traditionally made of wood, common in Islamic architecture.
- **Sharif**: A title for the ruler of the Hejaz, traditionally a descendant of the Islamic prophet Muhammad.
- **OIC**: The Organisation of Islamic Cooperation, an intergovernmental organization with its administrative center in Jeddah.
- **Saudi Vision 2030**: A strategic framework to reduce Saudi Arabia's dependence on oil, diversify its economy, and develop public service sectors.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The exact founding date of Jeddah is debated; while some sources cite a settlement over 2,500 years old, the traditional account links its founding to the collapse of the Marib Dam in 115 BC.
- The impact and scale of the 2021-2022 Jeddah Central Project demolitions are contested, with reports of forced evictions and inadequate compensation for displaced residents.
- The completion date and final height of the Jeddah Tower remain uncertain after construction was halted in 2018 and later restarted, with completion now expected in 2029.

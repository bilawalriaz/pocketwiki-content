# Mona Lisa

## Overview
The Mona Lisa is a half-length portrait by Leonardo da Vinci, painted in oil on a white poplar panel. Considered an archetypal masterpiece of the Italian Renaissance, it is famed for the subject's enigmatic expression, the atmospheric landscape, and Leonardo's technical innovations like *sfumato*. Its global fame stems from its artistic qualities, its history in the French royal collection, a famous 1911 theft, and its status as a cultural icon.

## Timeline
- **c. 1503–1506** — Believed to have been painted by Leonardo da Vinci, possibly worked on until 1517.
- **1519** — Acquired by King Francis I of France after Leonardo's death.
- **1797** — Goes on permanent display at the Louvre in Paris.
- **1911** — Stolen from the Louvre by Vincenzo Peruggia.
- **1914** — Recovered and returned to the Louvre.
- **1956** — Damaged by a rock thrown by a vandal; bulletproof glass installed thereafter.
- **1962–1963** — Lent to the United States for exhibition in New York and Washington, D.C.
- **2005** — Moved to a new, purpose-built enclosure in the Louvre's Salle des États.

## Body

### Title and Subject
The painting's title, *Mona Lisa*, is based on the presumption it depicts Lisa del Giocondo, wife of Florentine merchant Francesco del Giocondo. The Italian name *La Gioconda* is a pun on her married name, meaning "jocund" or "happy." The identification is supported by a 1550 account from art historian Giorgio Vasari and confirmed by a 2005 discovery of a 1503 marginal note by Agostino Vespucci. However, the sitter's likeness remains uncertain, with alternative subjects proposed, including Isabella d'Este.

### Description and Technique
The portrait shows a woman seated in a three-quarter profile before an imaginary landscape, a novel composition for its time. Leonardo achieved a lifelike quality by avoiding outlines and using *sfumato*, a technique of soft blending that creates ambiguous moods, particularly around the eyes and mouth. The background features a vast landscape with icy mountains and a distant bridge, linked to the figure by a horizon line at eye level. The painting lacks clearly visible eyebrows and eyelashes, which may have been lost over time.

### Creation and Provenance
Leonardo began the portrait in Florence around 1503. He likely took it with him to France in 1516 and may have continued refining it until 1517. After his death in 1519, King Francis I acquired it. The painting remained in French royal collections until the French Revolution, after which it entered the Louvre. Its authenticity is unquestioned, though debates persist about its exact dating and whether Leonardo made multiple versions.

### Theft and Vandalism
The Mona Lisa was not widely known outside art circles until its 1911 theft by Louvre employee Vincenzo Peruggia, an Italian patriot who believed it should be in Italy. The theft generated unprecedented publicity. It was recovered in 1914. Since 1956, the painting has been protected by bulletproof glass after multiple vandalism attempts, including rock-throwing and paint-spraying.

### Conservation and Display
The painting has survived for over 500 years in a remarkable state of preservation, though it has undergone cleanings and treatments. Its poplar panel has warped, requiring stabilizing frames. Since 2005, it has been displayed in a climate-controlled, bulletproof enclosure. As of 2019, about 10.2 million people view it annually at the Louvre.

## Terms
- **Sfumato**: A painting technique of soft blending to create hazy transitions between colors and tones, avoiding sharp outlines.
- **Aerial perspective**: A method of creating the illusion of depth by depicting distant objects as paler and less detailed.
- **Pozzetto armchair**: A type of armchair with a rounded back, in which the subject is seated.
- **Provenance**: The history of ownership of a work of art.
- **Catalogue raisonné**: A comprehensive, annotated listing of all the known works of an artist.
- **Spolvero**: A technique of transferring a drawing to a surface by pouncing charcoal dust through perforations.
- **Golden ratio**: A mathematical ratio (approximately 1.618) often considered aesthetically pleasing; some claim its presence in the painting's composition.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Sitter Identity**: While Lisa del Giocondo is the traditional subject, her likeness is uncertain, and alternative candidates have been proposed.
- **Date and Number of Versions**: Scholars debate the exact years of creation (1503–1517) and whether Leonardo painted one or two versions of the portrait.
- **Landscape Location**: The background landscape has been linked to various Italian regions, including the Montefeltro area and Lecco on Lake Como, but no definitive identification exists.
- **Intentional Use of Golden Ratio**: While features of the golden ratio are identified in the composition, there is little evidence Leonardo intentionally used it.

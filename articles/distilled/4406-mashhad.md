# Mashhad

## Overview
Mashhad is Iran's second-largest city and its holiest, serving as the capital of Razavi Khorasan province. Its global significance stems from the Imam Reza Shrine, the most visited pilgrimage site in Islam, which attracts nearly 30 million pilgrims annually. The city's identity, economy, and political influence are deeply intertwined with this religious center.

## Timeline
- **818 CE** — Imam Reza is buried in the village of Sanabad, which later becomes Mashhad.
- **1220** — Mongol raids destroy many cities in Khorasan, but Mashhad is spared due to its holy sites, leading to population growth.
- **1389** — Tus is sacked and abandoned; its population migrates to Mashhad, which becomes the regional capital.
- **1418** — Goharshad Mosque is built beside the shrine under Timurid patronage.
- **1598** — Shah Abbas I of the Safavid dynasty retakes Mashhad from the Uzbeks, promoting pilgrimage.
- **1736-1796** — Mashhad serves as the capital of Iran under the Afsharid dynasty.
- **1912** — Russian artillery bombs the Imam Reza shrine, causing international outrage.
- **1935** — The Goharshad Mosque rebellion erupts against Reza Shah's modernization policies.
- **1994** — A bomb explodes in the Imam Reza shrine, killing at least 25 people.
- **2009** — President Ahmadinejad declares Mashhad the "spiritual capital of Iran."
- **2017** — Mashhad is named the Cultural Capital of the Islamic World in Asia.

## Body

### Religious Significance and Early History
Mashhad's importance originates from the burial of Ali al-Rida (Imam Reza), the eighth Shia Imam, in 818 CE in the village of Sanabad. The site, meaning "place of martyrdom," grew as pilgrims settled around the shrine. It became a major Shia pilgrimage center, with its religious status bolstered by hadiths (sayings attributed to the Prophet Muhammad) emphasizing the spiritual merit of visiting the shrine. The city also became a leading center of Shia scholarship, with numerous seminaries and libraries.

### Medieval Growth and Political Capital
Mashhad's growth accelerated after Mongol invasions in 1220, as survivors from destroyed cities sought refuge near the protected shrine. The final decline of the neighboring city of Tus in 1389 cemented Mashhad's status as the regional capital. Under the Timurids and Safavids, rulers invested heavily in the shrine complex. Mashhad served as Iran's capital from 1736 to 1796 under the Afsharid dynasty. The city's religious and political importance made it a frequent target, such as the 1912 Russian bombardment of the shrine.

### Modern Era and Contemporary Identity
Under the Pahlavi dynasty, Mashhad saw modernization, including new hospitals, factories, and its first university (1949). However, tensions between the state and clergy erupted in the 1935 Goharshad Mosque rebellion. After the 1979 revolution, the city's conservative religious character was reinforced. The Astan Quds Razavi, the powerful foundation managing the shrine, became a dominant economic and political force. Today, Mashhad's economy is heavily reliant on pilgrimage tourism, and it remains a symbol of Shia identity and a base for conservative clerical influence.

## Terms
*   **Imam Reza Shrine:** The mausoleum of the eighth Shia Imam, Ali al-Rida, in Mashhad. It is the most visited pilgrimage site in Islam and the heart of the city.
*   **Astan Quds Razavi:** The wealthy, tax-exempt foundation that administers the Imam Reza Shrine and its vast endowments. It is a major economic and political player in Mashhad and Iran.
*   **Hawza:** A traditional Islamic seminary for higher religious learning. Mashhad is home to numerous hawzas.
*   **Ziyārah:** The Islamic pilgrimage to a holy site, particularly the visitation of the shrines of Shia Imams.
*   **Waqf:** An inalienable religious endowment under Islamic law, typically involving land or buildings. The Astan Quds Razavi manages extensive waqf properties.
*   **Bonyad:** A large, state-controlled foundation in Iran, often with significant economic and political power. The Astan Quds Razavi is considered the largest active bonyad.
*   **Mashhadi dialect:** A variant of the Persian language spoken in Mashhad, distinguished by its unique tones and stresses.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   The exact ancient origins of the site are debated, with some sources linking it to Alexander the Great and others to the Parthian city of Patigrabanâ.
*   The city's name evolved over time from Sanabad to Mashhad al-Rida, with the shortened "Mashhad" becoming common later.
*   The political and social impact of the Astan Quds Razavi's immense economic power is a subject of ongoing discussion regarding its role in the city's development and governance.

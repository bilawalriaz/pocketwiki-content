# Belarus

## Overview
Belarus is a landlocked Eastern European country with a population of 9.1 million, historically shaped by rule under the Grand Duchy of Lithuania, the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth, the Russian Empire, and the Soviet Union. It gained independence in 1991 and is now a highly centralized authoritarian state under President Alexander Lukashenko, maintaining close ties with Russia while facing international isolation over human rights and its role in the 2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine.

## Timeline
- **c. 5000–2000 BC** — Bandkeramik culture predominates in the region.
- **9th century** — Territory becomes part of Kievan Rus'.
- **1300** — Lands come fully under the Grand Duchy of Lithuania.
- **1569** — Union of Lublin creates the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth.
- **1795** — Third Partition of Poland places Belarus within the Russian Empire.
- **27 July 1990** — Byelorussian SSR declares sovereignty.
- **25 August 1991** — Belarus declares independence from the Soviet Union.
- **1994** — New constitution adopted; Alexander Lukashenko elected president in the country's first and only free post-independence election.
- **2000** — Belarus and Russia sign a treaty forming the Union State.
- **2020** — Disputed presidential election leads to mass protests and international sanctions.
- **2022** — Belarus allows Russian troops to stage part of the invasion of Ukraine from its territory, leading to further sanctions.

## Body

### Etymology and Early History
The name "Belarus" is linked to "White Rus'," with theories suggesting it referred to early Christianized Slavic lands, lands unconquered by Tatars, or the western part of Rus'. The term "Byelorussia" was used officially until 1991. The region was first settled by Baltic tribes in the 3rd century, later taken over by Slavs around the 5th century, and became part of Kievan Rus' in the 9th century.

### Grand Duchy and Polish–Lithuanian Rule
After the Mongol invasion, Belarusian lands joined the Grand Duchy of Lithuania, leading to economic and cultural unification. A personal union with Poland in 1386 eventually formed the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth in 1569, during which Polonization and Catholicism gained influence, though Ruthenian peasants retained their language.

### Russian Empire and Soviet Era
Following the 1795 partitions of Poland, Belarus was incorporated into the Russian Empire, which imposed Russification policies. After World War I and the Russian Revolution, competing states emerged, leading to the creation of the Byelorussian SSR in 1920, a founding Soviet republic in 1922. The territory lost nearly half its land to Poland after the Polish-Soviet War (1918–1921). Modern borders were largely set after the Soviet invasion of eastern Poland in 1939 and finalized after World War II, during which Belarus suffered devastating losses—about a quarter of its population and half its economic resources.

### Independence and the Lukashenko Era
Belarus declared sovereignty in 1990 and independence in 1991. The 1994 constitution established a semi-presidential republic, and Alexander Lukashenko was elected president in the country's only free election. He has since established a highly centralized authoritarian government, eliminating presidential term limits in 2004. Belarus maintains state control over much of the economy and has faced repeated international sanctions for human rights violations, electoral fraud, and its complicity in the 2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine.

### Geography and Demographics
Belarus is a flat, forested country (40% forest cover) with a hemiboreal climate, featuring marshy lands and numerous lakes and rivers. Its population is 84.9% ethnic Belarusian, with significant Russian, Polish, and Ukrainian minorities. The population is declining and aging, with a fertility rate of 1.22. Russian and Belarusian are official languages, though Russian is more commonly spoken at home. Eastern Orthodoxy is the dominant religion.

### Economy and International Relations
The economy is state-dominated, with manufacturing and agriculture (notably potatoes) as key sectors. Belarus is heavily dependent on trade with Russia, which accounts for 66% of its international trade. It is a member of the CIS, CSTO, and EAEU, and maintains a Union State with Russia. Relations with the EU and US are strained due to sanctions over human rights and political repression. Belarus is not a member of the Council of Europe and has no aspirations to join the EU.

## Terms
- **Byelorussian SSR**: The Soviet republic that preceded independent Belarus, a founding member of the USSR in 1922.
- **Union State**: A supranational confederation between Belarus and Russia established by treaties in 1996–99, aiming for closer political and economic integration.
- **Russification**: Policies promoting Russian language and culture, often suppressing local identities, implemented during the Russian Empire and Soviet era.
- **Polonization**: The promotion of Polish language and culture, which influenced Belarusian lands during the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth period.
- **Raion**: An administrative district, the second-level division of Belarus below the oblasts.
- **Oblast**: One of Belarus's six primary administrative regions, named after its capital city.
- **OSCE**: The Organization for Security and Co-operation in Europe, a regional security organization of which Belarus is a member.
- **CSTO**: The Collective Security Treaty Organization, a Russian-led military alliance of which Belarus is a member.
- **EAEU**: The Eurasian Economic Union, an economic bloc including Belarus, Russia, and other post-Soviet states.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The origin of the name "White Rus'" is debated, with multiple theories regarding its etymological roots.
- The legitimacy of Alexander Lukashenko's presidency is contested internationally following the disputed 2020 election, with several countries and the EU refusing to recognize him as the legitimate leader.
- The future of the Union State with Russia remains uncertain due to delays in implementing monetary union and other integration measures.
- The extent of Belarus's complicity in the 2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine and the impact of subsequent international sanctions on its economy and political stability are ongoing issues.

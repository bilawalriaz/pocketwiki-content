# Atacama Desert

## Overview
The Atacama Desert is a hyper-arid plateau in northern Chile, recognized as the driest non-polar desert on Earth. Its extreme conditions, resulting from a unique rain shadow effect, make it a critical natural laboratory for studying extreme environments, including potential analogs for Mars, and a premier location for astronomical observation.

## Timeline
- **c. 33 Ma** — Opening of the Tasmania-Antarctic passage allows cold currents, initiating long-term aridity.
- **c. 7000 BCE** — Chinchorro culture begins settling the coastal areas.
- **1570–1971** — Evidence suggests a period with no significant rainfall.
- **16th–18th C.** — Spanish colonial coastal towns develop as ports for silver exports.
- **19th C.** — Discovery of sodium nitrate deposits leads to conflict, culminating in the War of the Pacific.
- **Early 20th C.** — Invention of synthetic nitrate leads to the abandonment of ~170 mining towns.
- **1991** — Unusual rainfall causes deadly mudflows in Antofagasta and Calama.
- **2003** — Researchers duplicate Viking Mars lander tests on Atacama soil, finding no signs of life.
- **2011** — The Atacama Large Millimeter Array (ALMA) observatory officially opens.
- **2015** — Heavy rainfall triggers floods and mudflows, causing over 100 deaths.
- **2023** — Scientists report a "dark microbiome" of unfamiliar microorganisms in the desert.

## Body

### Geography and Climate
The Atacama stretches along a 1,600 km strip of the Chilean coast, covering 105,000 km². Its defining feature is extreme aridity, with average annual rainfall around 15 mm. This results from a "two-sided rain shadow" created by the high Andes and Chilean Coast Range, which block moisture from both the Pacific and Atlantic oceans. The cool Humboldt Current and South Pacific anticyclone further reinforce dry conditions. Hyper-aridity has persisted since at least the Miocene epoch, making it possibly the oldest desert on Earth.

### Flora and Fauna
Despite the harsh conditions, over 500 plant species have adapted, including herbs, cacti, and the long-lived llareta shrub. In years with sufficient precipitation, the desert experiences a spectacular "desierto florido" (flowering desert). Animal life is limited to specially adapted species. Coastal areas support Humboldt penguins and sea lions, while inland salt flats host flamingos. Mammals include Darwin's leaf-eared mouse, guanacos, and vicuñas. The lomas (hills) sustained by coastal fog provide unique habitats for seasonal plants and animals.

### Human History and Economy
Human presence dates to at least 7000 BCE with the Chinchorro culture, known for mummification. Later, the Atacameño people built fortified towns (pucarás). Spanish colonial rule established coastal ports for silver shipping. The 19th-century discovery of sodium nitrate (saltpeter) made the region a zone of conflict between Chile, Bolivia, and Peru, leading to the War of the Pacific. Chile annexed most of the desert, and a mining boom followed. The industry declined after the invention of synthetic nitrate, leaving behind ghost towns. Today, the economy relies on copper mining, tourism, and astronomical research.

### Scientific Significance
The desert's high altitude, dry air, and lack of light pollution make it one of the world's best sites for astronomy, hosting major observatories like ALMA and the Very Large Telescope. Its Mars-like soils, with minimal microbial life, are used by NASA to test instruments for planetary missions. In 2023, scientists discovered a "dark microbiome" of unfamiliar microorganisms, adding to its importance for astrobiology research.

## Terms
- **Rain shadow:** A dry area on the leeward (downwind) side of a mountain range, where the mountains block the passage of rain-producing weather systems.
- **Hyper-aridity:** A state of extreme dryness with negligible precipitation, defining the core of the Atacama.
- **Lomas:** Hills or small mountains that receive moisture from coastal fog (camanchaca), supporting isolated ecosystems.
- **Pucará:** A fortified town or settlement built by pre-Columbian peoples in the Andes.
- **Sodium nitrate (saltpeter):** A mineral historically mined for fertilizer and explosives, which drove 19th-century economic and political conflict in the region.
- **Salar:** A salt flat, such as the Salar de Atacama, which is a major source of lithium and other minerals.
- **Camanchaca:** A dense coastal fog that provides critical moisture for life in parts of the desert.
- **Desierto florido:** A natural phenomenon where dormant seeds bloom en masse following sufficient rainfall.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The precise southern boundary of the Atacama Desert is debated, with some sources including coastal areas of southern Peru.
- The exact timing and causes of the desert's hyper-aridity are studied, with evidence suggesting it predates the rise of the Andes but was intensified by it.
- The potential for finding signs of past or present life on Mars is actively investigated using the Atacama as a terrestrial analog, with ongoing research into its "dark microbiome."

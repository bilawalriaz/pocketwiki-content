# Mohammad Mosaddegh

## Overview
Mohammad Mosaddegh was an Iranian politician and Prime Minister (1951-1953) who became a symbol of Iranian nationalism for nationalizing the country's oil industry, which had been controlled by the British-led Anglo-Iranian Oil Company (AIOC). His government was overthrown in a 1953 coup d'état orchestrated by the United Kingdom and the United States, an event that profoundly shaped modern Iranian politics and U.S.-Iranian relations.

## Timeline
- **16 June 1882** — Born in Ahmadabad, Iran, to a prominent family.
- **1913** — Receives a Doctorate of Laws from the University of Neuchâtel, Switzerland, the first Iranian to do so from a European university.
- **1925** — Votes against deposing the Qajar dynasty and installing Reza Shah, leading to his temporary retirement from politics.
- **1949** — Founds the National Front (Jebhe Melli) political coalition.
- **28 April 1951** — Elected Prime Minister by the Majlis (Parliament).
- **March 1951** — Nationalizes the Anglo-Iranian Oil Company (AIOC).
- **16 July 1952** — Resigns after the Shah refuses his demand to name the War Minister.
- **21 July 1952** — Reinstated as Prime Minister following mass protests.
- **August 1952** — Granted emergency "dictatorial decree" powers for six months.
- **19 August 1953** — Overthrown in a coup (Operation Ajax) led by the CIA and MI6; General Fazlollah Zahedi installed as Prime Minister.
- **21 December 1953** — Sentenced to three years' solitary confinement.
- **5 March 1967** — Dies under house arrest in Ahmadabad.

## Body

### Early Life and Political Career
Born into a family of high officials, Mosaddegh received a European education, earning a doctorate in law. He began his political career during the Constitutional Revolution and held various ministerial posts, including finance and foreign minister. He was a staunch constitutionalist, famously voting against the 1925 deposition of the Qajar dynasty. After a period of retirement, he re-entered politics in 1944 and founded the National Front in 1949, a coalition dedicated to democracy and ending foreign influence, particularly in Iran's oil industry.

### Prime Minister and the Oil Nationalization
Elected Prime Minister in 1951, Mosaddegh's government introduced social reforms, including unemployment compensation and land reforms. His defining act was the nationalization of the AIOC, which controlled Iranian oil. This triggered the "Abadan Crisis," a severe confrontation with Britain. The AIOC evacuated technicians, and Britain imposed a blockade, crippling Iran's oil production and income. Despite this, Mosaddegh remained popular and, in 1952, secured emergency dictatorial powers to bypass parliamentary gridlock and implement reforms, which included limiting the Shah's budget and powers.

### The 1953 Coup and Aftermath
Following failed negotiations and escalating Cold War fears, the UK and US orchestrated a coup. The CIA, led by Kermit Roosevelt Jr., executed "Operation Ajax," which involved bribing officials, organizing protests, and convincing the Shah to sign decrees dismissing Mosaddegh and appointing General Zahedi. After an initial failed attempt, a second wave of protests and military action on August 19, 1953, succeeded. Mosaddegh was arrested, tried for treason, and spent the rest of his life under house arrest. The Shah's government subsequently signed the Consortium Agreement of 1954, restoring foreign oil interests.

### Legacy
Mosaddegh remains one of Iran's most popular historical figures, a symbol of anti-imperialism and nationalism. His overthrow is widely cited as a root cause of the 1979 Iranian Revolution and enduring Iranian distrust of the United States and United Kingdom. The U.S. government formally acknowledged its role in the coup in 2013.

## Terms
*   **Majlis:** The Iranian parliament.
*   **National Front (Jebhe Melli):** A political coalition founded by Mosaddegh in 1949 to promote democracy and nationalize the oil industry.
*   **Anglo-Iranian Oil Company (AIOC):** The British-owned corporation that controlled Iran's oil industry from 1913 until its nationalization in 1951; later became British Petroleum (BP).
*   **Abadan Crisis:** The economic and political standoff between Iran and Britain following the nationalization of the AIOC, which led to a British blockade and a collapse in Iranian oil revenue.
*   **Operation Ajax:** The codename for the CIA-led covert operation to overthrow Mosaddegh in August 1953.
*   **Farmān:** A royal decree issued by the Shah, used in the coup to formally dismiss Mosaddegh and legitimize the new government.
*   **Consortium Agreement of 1954:** An agreement negotiated after the coup that restored foreign (primarily American and British) control over Iranian oil production.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Motivations for the Coup:** Scholars debate the primary driver: was it British economic interests in oil, or American Cold War fears that Mosaddegh's weakening government would lead to a communist takeover via the Tudeh Party?
*   **Role of the Clergy:** The withdrawal of support from key clerics like Ayatollah Abol-Ghasem Kashani is considered a fatal blow to Mosaddegh's coalition. The reasons for this shift—whether due to fear of communism, opposition to secular reforms, or other factors—are debated.
*   **Electoral Legitimacy:** Accounts differ on the 1952 election. Some historians argue Mosaddegh stopped the vote early to secure a quorum, while others suggest he was responding to British-funded manipulation of the electoral process.
*   **Long-Term Consequences ("Blowback"):** The extent to which the coup directly caused the 1979 Islamic Revolution and subsequent anti-Western sentiment in Iran is a central point of analysis in U.S.-Iranian relations.

# United Arab Emirates

## Overview
The United Arab Emirates (UAE) is a federation of seven hereditary monarchies on the Arabian Peninsula, formed in 1971. It has transformed from a region of tribal sheikhdoms reliant on pearling and trade into a wealthy, modern state with a highly diversified economy and significant global influence, though it faces criticism for its authoritarian governance and human rights record.

## Timeline
- **c. 3000 BCE** — Trade links with Mesopotamia and the Indus Valley flourish, likely motivated by copper trade.
- **630 CE** — The spread of Islam to the region begins following a letter from the Prophet Muhammad.
- **1820** — Britain and local rulers sign a maritime truce, leading to the term "Trucial States."
- **1892** — The Exclusive Agreements make the Trucial States a British protectorate.
- **1958** — First commercial oil discovery offshore at Umm Shaif in Abu Dhabi waters.
- **1960** — First onshore commercial oil discovery at Murban No. 3 well in Abu Dhabi.
- **1962** — First oil exports from the Trucial Coast.
- **1971** — The Trucial States gain independence; six emirates form the UAE. Ras Al Khaimah joins in 1972.
- **1981** — The UAE is a founding member of the Gulf Cooperation Council (GCC).
- **2004** — Sheikh Zayed bin Sultan Al Nahyan, the first president, dies.
- **2020** — The UAE establishes diplomatic relations with Israel via the Abraham Accords.
- **2026** — The UAE quits OPEC and faces retaliatory attacks from Iran during a regional conflict.

## Body

### History and Formation
The region has a long history of settlement and trade, with evidence of habitation over 100,000 years ago. It was part of the Magan civilization and later saw the spread of Islam in the 7th century. From the 16th century, European powers, notably the Portuguese, established a presence. By the 19th century, British efforts to suppress piracy led to treaties with local sheikhdoms, creating the Trucial States, a British protectorate. The pearling industry was a major economic driver until its collapse in the 1930s due to the Great Depression and cultured pearls. The discovery of oil, first commercially in 1958, began the region's modern transformation. Following the British announcement of withdrawal in 1968, the Trucial States negotiated their union, becoming independent as the UAE on 2 December 1971.

### Government and Politics
The UAE is a federal, semi-constitutional monarchy. The highest authority is the Federal Supreme Council, composed of the seven hereditary rulers. It elects a president and vice president from among its members. The federal government has executive, legislative, and judicial branches, but power is concentrated with the rulers. The country is described as an authoritarian state with no democratically elected institutions and severe restrictions on freedoms of speech, assembly, and the press. Human rights organizations report systematic abuses, including the torture and forced disappearance of government critics. The UAE ranks poorly on global freedom and democracy indices.

### Economy and Resources
The UAE has the world's seventh-largest oil and natural gas reserves. However, it has pursued aggressive economic diversification, making its economy the most diversified in the Gulf Cooperation Council. Key sectors now include tourism, finance, and logistics, with Dubai serving as a major international hub. The country has a very high GDP per capita and a unique labor market where expatriates constitute the vast majority of the population. The government implemented a 5% value-added tax (VAT) in 2018. The UAE is a major global wealth magnet and has been criticized as a tax haven.

### Society and Demographics
The population is overwhelmingly composed of expatriates and migrant workers, with Emiratis making up only about 11% of the total. The largest expatriate groups are from South Asia. Arabic is the official language, but English is widely spoken. Islam is the state religion, though the government officially promotes tolerance for other faiths. The country has a very high urbanization rate and a significant gender imbalance in the population. Education and healthcare systems have seen major investment, contributing to high human development indicators.

## Terms
- **Emirate:** A territory ruled by a hereditary monarch styled as an Emir or Sheikh.
- **Trucial States:** The name given to the group of sheikhdoms on the Persian Gulf coast that were British protectorates from 1820 until the formation of the UAE in 1971.
- **Kafala system:** A sponsorship system for migrant workers that ties their legal residency to their employer, often criticized for enabling labor exploitation.
- **Federal Supreme Council:** The highest constitutional authority in the UAE, comprising the seven hereditary rulers of the emirates.
- **OPEC:** The Organization of the Petroleum Exporting Countries, which the UAE was a member of until its exit in 2026.
- **Gulf Cooperation Council (GCC):** A regional intergovernmental political and economic union consisting of all Arab states of the Persian Gulf except Iraq, including the UAE.

## Debates and open questions
- The historical charge of piracy against the Al Qawasim federation by the British is disputed by modern Emirati historians.
- The UAE's border disputes with Iran over the islands of Abu Musa and the Greater and Lesser Tunbs remain unresolved.
- The UAE's role in the ongoing Sudanese Civil War, particularly its alleged support for the Rapid Support Forces (RSF), is a subject of major international scrutiny and legal action.
- The long-term success and social impact of the UAE's economic diversification away from oil dependence is an ongoing development.

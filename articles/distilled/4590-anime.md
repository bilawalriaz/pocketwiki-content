# Anime

## Overview
Anime is animation originating from Japan, characterized by distinctive art styles, production methods, and narrative techniques. It has evolved from early 20th-century experiments into a major global cultural and economic force, influencing media, fashion, and tourism worldwide.

## Timeline
- **c. 1907** — Earliest claimed Japanese animation, *Katsudō Shashin*, is created.
- **1917** — First professional, publicly displayed anime works appear.
- **1933** — First talkie anime short, *Chikara to Onna no Yo no Naka*, is produced.
- **1945** — First feature-length anime film, *Momotaro: Sacred Sailors*, is released.
- **1963** — *Astro Boy* becomes the first major anime television series.
- **1988** — *Akira* is released, helping popularize anime in the West.
- **1995** — *Neon Genesis Evangelion* and *Ghost in the Shell* gain international acclaim.
- **2001** — *Spirited Away* wins the Academy Award for Best Animated Feature.
- **2020** — *Demon Slayer: Mugen Train* becomes the highest-grossing Japanese film.
- **2022** — *Attack on Titan* becomes the first non-English series to win "Most In-Demand TV Series in the World."

## Body

### History and Development
Japanese animation has precursors in traditional art forms like *emakimono* (picture scrolls) and *kamishibai* (paper theater). Modern anime began in the early 20th century, with the first professional works appearing in 1917. The industry developed through government-sponsored propaganda in the 1930s-40s. The modern era was defined by Osamu Tezuka in the 1960s, who established key stylistic conventions and founded Mushi Production, which spawned major studios. The 1970s saw the rise of manga adaptations and the mecha (giant robot) genre. The 1980s bubble economy enabled high-budget films like *Akira*. Since the 1990s, anime has achieved significant international popularity, aided by the internet and streaming services.

### Artistic Style and Production
Anime is characterized by limited animation, detailed backgrounds, and cinematic "camera effects" like panning and zooming. A common visual trait is characters with large, emotive eyes, a style popularized by Tezuka. Production involves storyboarding, voice acting, and cel animation, though digital processes have largely replaced traditional cels since the 1990s. Unlike many Western animations, voice acting is typically recorded after the animation is produced. Anime uses a wide variety of art styles, and character proportions can range from realistic to the exaggerated "chibi" style.

### Industry and Economics
The anime industry comprises over 430 production companies, including major studios like Toei Animation, Studio Ghibli, and MAPPA. It is a significant economic sector, with the global market valued at $26.055 billion in 2021 and projected to reach $62.7 billion by 2032. Revenue comes from streaming, merchandise, and licensing. The industry has faced criticism for overworking and underpaying animators, prompting government and studio-led initiatives for improvement. International distribution, once reliant on licensing and fansubs, is now dominated by legal streaming services like Netflix and Crunchyroll.

### Global Popularity and Cultural Impact
Anime's global popularity surged from the 1990s onward, with series like *Dragon Ball Z* and *Pokémon* becoming international hits. By 2021, anime was the most demanded foreign-language TV genre in the U.S. Streaming platforms have been crucial, with Netflix reporting over 150 million member households watching anime by 2025. Anime has influenced global fashion, inspired Hollywood adaptations (often unsuccessful), and boosted tourism to Japan. It has also entered mainstream discourse, with terms like "otaku" and "anime" added to the Oxford English Dictionary.

### Genres and Formats
Anime is classified by target demographics: *kodomo* (children), *shōnen* (boys), *shōjo* (girls), *seinen* (young men), and *josei* (young women). Genres include science fiction (notably mecha), fantasy, romance, and sports. Adult-oriented anime may contain explicit content, with pornographic works labeled "hentai." Formats include theatrical films, television series, Original Video Animations (OVAs) released directly to home media, and Original Net Animations (ONAs) distributed online. Anime series often use a serialized format with continuous story arcs.

## Terms
- **Anime**: In Japanese, refers to all animation. In English, typically denotes animation produced in Japan.
- **Manga**: Japanese comics or graphic novels, which are a primary source for anime adaptations.
- **Otaku**: A Japanese term for people with obsessive interests, commonly used in English to denote anime and manga fans.
- **Mecha**: A genre of anime featuring giant robots or machines.
- **Shōnen/Shōjo**: Demographic categories for anime/manga aimed at boys and girls, respectively.
- **Seinen/Josei**: Demographic categories for anime/manga aimed at young adult men and women.
- **Hentai**: A term for anime with explicit pornographic content.
- **Cosplay**: The practice of dressing up as characters from anime, manga, or other media.
- **Fansub**: An unofficial, fan-made subtitled translation of an anime series or film.
- **Media Mix**: A Japanese strategy of dispersing a franchise's content across multiple platforms (TV, film, games, merchandise).

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Definition**: A persistent debate exists over whether "anime" should be defined by its Japanese origin or by its distinctive artistic style, which has led to arguments about non-Japanese works like *Avatar: The Last Airbender*.
- **Working Conditions**: The industry faces ongoing criticism regarding the low pay and overwork of animators, with efforts at reform being a current focus.
- **Globalization vs. Authenticity**: As anime becomes a global industry with increased international collaboration and investment, questions arise about how this affects its traditional production methods and cultural identity.
- **Piracy and Distribution**: While legal streaming has reduced fansubbing, the balance between global accessibility, fair compensation for creators, and piracy remains a complex issue.

# Pannonian Avars

## Overview
The Pannonian Avars were a confederation of Eurasian nomads who established the Avar Khaganate in the Carpathian Basin from the late 6th to early 9th centuries. They were a major power in Central, Eastern, and Southeastern Europe, known for their wars with the Byzantine Empire and their role in shaping Slavic migrations. Their elite likely originated from the Rouran Khaganate in present-day Mongolia, and their state collapsed after Frankish conquests in the late 8th century.

## Timeline
- **c. 463** — First clear reference to the "Avars" by Priscus the Rhetor.
- **557** — Avars send their first embassy to Constantinople.
- **567** — Avars ally with Lombards to destroy the Gepid kingdom.
- **582** — Avars capture the key city of Sirmium.
- **626** — Avars and Persians fail to capture Constantinople.
- **631-632** — Bulgar leader Kubrat leads an uprising against Avar authority.
- **670s** — A new wave of settlers (Middle Avar period) arrives in the Carpathian Basin.
- **788** — Frankish campaigns against the Avars begin.
- **791-796** — Charlemagne's campaigns conquer the Avar realm.
- **804** — Bulgaria conquers southeastern Avar lands.
- **c. 822** — Last mention of an Avar prince; Avar identity fades.

## Body

### Origins and Identity
The Pannonian Avars entered history in the mid-6th century, fleeing the rule of the Göktürks on the Pontic-Caspian steppe. Byzantine sources often called them "Pseudo-Avars," a term likely used by the Turks to delegitimize them. The name "Avar" may have been adopted from a prestigious older group. Their core elite likely descended from the Rouran Khaganate, a confederation defeated by the Göktürks. Archaeogenetic studies confirm their elite had primarily Ancient Northeast Asian ancestry, with strong genetic links to modern Mongolic and Tungusic peoples.

### Establishment and Expansion
After initial contact with Byzantium in 557, the Avars expanded westward. They formed a heterogeneous group of about 20,000 horsemen. After being blocked by the Franks, they focused on the Carpathian Basin, destroying the Gepid kingdom in 567 with Lombard help. Under Khagan Bayan I, they established supremacy over Slavic, Germanic, and Bulgar tribes. They captured key Roman cities like Sirmium (582) and raided deep into the Balkans, often when Byzantium was distracted by wars with Persia.

### The Khaganate and Society
The Avar Khaganate was a hierarchical, nomadic empire ruled by a khagan and a warrior aristocracy. The elite were buried with rich grave goods, including gold and symbols of rank. The army was composed of Avar cavalry and subject peoples, including Slavic foot soldiers. Initially separate, Avars and their subjects eventually developed a fused material culture. Archaeological evidence challenges the view of a purely nomadic society, showing signs of pastoralism and agriculture.

### Decline and Collapse
The failed 626 siege of Constantinople marked a turning point. The khaganate faced internal revolts, such as Samo's Slavic realm (631-658) and Bulgar uprisings. A new archaeological culture ("griffin and tendril") in the 8th century may indicate new migrations or internal evolution. Frankish campaigns from 788, led by Charlemagne, conquered the Avar realm by 796. The Franks captured the Avar "Ring" fortress and integrated Avar lands into their empire. The Avar political structure disintegrated, and their identity vanished within a few generations through assimilation with Slavs, Franks, and later Magyars.

## Terms
- **Khagan**: The supreme ruler of a nomadic empire, a title used by the Avars and other steppe peoples.
- **Pannonian Basin**: The central lowland of the Carpathian Basin (modern Hungary and surroundings), the heartland of the Avar Khaganate.
- **Göktürks**: A Turkic people who dominated the Eurasian steppe before the Avars' westward migration.
- **Rouran Khaganate**: A nomadic empire in Inner Asia, considered a likely origin for the Avar elite.
- **Gepids**: A Germanic people who occupied the Carpathian Basin before being destroyed by the Avars and Lombards.
- **Composite bow**: A powerful, layered bow used by steppe horse archers, a key Avar weapon.
- **Griffin and tendril**: A decorative motif on late Avar artifacts, possibly indicating new cultural influences.
- **Endogamy**: The practice of marrying within a specific social group, suggested by genetic studies of the Avar elite.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Origin**: The exact relationship between the Pannonian Avars, the "Avars" mentioned by Priscus, the Rouran, and other steppe groups is debated. The term "Pseudo-Avars" reflects Byzantine and Turkic attempts to delegitimize them.
- **Language**: The Avar language is unknown. Theories propose Turkic, Mongolic, Tungusic, or Iranian origins, with some scholars suggesting a "Para-Mongolic" elite language.
- **Avar-Hungarian Continuity**: Historian Gyula László's theory posits that late Avars survived in large numbers and were assimilated by the arriving Magyars, influencing the Hungarian language. This is contested by mainstream scholarship.
- **Late Avar Culture**: The cause of the "griffin and tendril" archaeological culture in the 8th century is debated—whether it represents new arrivals (e.g., Onogurs) or internal evolution.
- **Assimilation**: The process and timeline of Avar disappearance are unclear. They likely merged with Slavic populations, but the extent of their survival into the Magyar conquest (c. 895) is uncertain.

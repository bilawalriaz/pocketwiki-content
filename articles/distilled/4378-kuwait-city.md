# Kuwait City

## Overview
Kuwait City is the capital and largest city of Kuwait, serving as the nation's political, cultural, and economic center. It is a primate city, meaning it is disproportionately larger and more influential than any other city in the country, housing the headquarters of most corporations and the entire banking system. The city's development from a fishing village to a major metropolis is closely tied to regional trade, the discovery of oil, and geopolitical events in the Persian Gulf.

## Body

### History and Development
Kuwait City began as a small fishing village in the early to mid-1700s. Its transformation into a commercial center was accelerated by regional instability, particularly the Persian siege of Basra (1775–1779), which diverted trade routes and merchant activity to Kuwait. By the late 18th century, it was a principal hub for trade between India, East Africa, and the Red Sea, renowned for its shipbuilding. The discovery of oil in 1937 and its subsequent export from 1946 onward triggered a period of massive growth and modernization known as the "Golden Era" (1946–1982). This era saw the development of a modern standard of living, the establishment of Kuwait University (1966), and the creation of the world's first sovereign wealth fund, the Kuwait Investment Authority.

### Political and Social Shifts
Kuwait was the first Gulf country to establish a constitution and parliament in the early 1960s. The 1960s and 1970s were characterized by a liberal, Western-oriented society with a free press and a thriving cultural scene, including theatre and literature. However, the early 1980s brought economic crisis and social turmoil. The government increasingly allied with Islamists to counter secular democratic opposition, leading to the suspension of parliament in 1986. The 1990 Iraqi invasion and subsequent Gulf War caused immense destruction, including oil well fires that rendered parts of the country uninhabitable and resulted in over 1,000 civilian deaths.

### Geography and Climate
Kuwait City is located on Kuwait Bay, a natural deep-water harbor on the Persian Gulf. The country is generally low-lying and arid, with its highest point at 306 meters above sea level. The climate is an extremely hot desert (Köppen: BWh), with summer temperatures regularly exceeding 45°C (113°F) and sometimes 50°C (122°F). Sandstorms from the shamal wind are common, especially in summer.

### Economy and Infrastructure
Kuwait has a petroleum-based economy, with oil accounting for 43% of GDP and 70% of export earnings. The Kuwaiti dinar is the world's highest-valued currency unit. The city's transport needs are served by Kuwait International Airport, the main hub for state-owned Kuwait Airways and private airlines like Jazeera Airways, and by major ports at Shuwaikh and Ahmadi.

### Culture
Kuwait City is a cultural hub for the Arab world. It is the only Gulf country with a home-grown theatrical tradition, which began in the 1920s. Kuwaiti soap operas are among the most-watched in the region. The city is also known for its museums, including the Kuwait National Museum and the Sheikh Jaber Al-Ahmad Cultural Centre, and for its historical role as a haven for writers and journalists seeking freedom of expression.

## Terms
- **Primate city:** A city that is significantly larger and more dominant than any other in its country, serving as its political, economic, and cultural center.
- **Bani Utub:** A tribal confederation whose settlement in Kuwait in the mid-1700s marked the beginning of its development as a trading center.
- **Golden Era:** The period from 1946 to 1982, characterized by rapid economic prosperity and modernization driven by oil revenues.
- **Sovereign wealth fund:** A state-owned investment fund, with Kuwait's being the first established in the world.
- **Souk Al-Manakh:** An unofficial stock market whose crash in the early 1980s contributed to a major economic crisis.
- **Shamal:** A northwesterly wind that causes frequent sandstorms in the region.
- **Köppen climate classification BWh:** A code for a hot desert climate, characterized by very hot summers and mild winters.

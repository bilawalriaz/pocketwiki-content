# Sitting Bull

## Overview
Sitting Bull (c. 1831–37 – 1890) was a Hunkpapa Lakota leader who became a symbol of Native American resistance to U.S. government policies in the late 19th century. His leadership during the Great Sioux War of 1876, particularly his spiritual role before the Battle of the Little Bighorn, made him a pivotal figure in the Plains Indian Wars. His life story illustrates the conflict between Indigenous sovereignty and U.S. expansion, culminating in his death during the Ghost Dance era.

## Timeline
- **c. 1831–37** — Born in Dakota Territory, named Ȟoká Psíče (Jumping Badger).
- **1846** — At age 14, counts coup on a Crow warrior, receiving his father's name, Sitting Bull.
- **1864** — Leads defense against U.S. forces at the Battle of the Badlands.
- **1866–68** — Fights alongside Red Cloud; refuses to sign the 1868 Treaty of Fort Laramie.
- **1874** — Resists Custer's expedition into the Black Hills.
- **1875** — Performs Sun Dance, has vision of U.S. soldiers falling into camp.
- **June 25, 1876** — Spiritual leader before the Battle of the Little Bighorn; U.S. 7th Cavalry defeated.
- **May 1877** — Leads band into exile in Canada's North-West Territories.
- **July 19, 1881** — Surrenders at Fort Buford, North Dakota.
- **1885** — Tours with Buffalo Bill's Wild West show.
- **December 15, 1890** — Killed by Indian agency police at Standing Rock Agency.

## Body

### Early Life and Warrior Ethos
Born between 1831 and 1837, Sitting Bull earned his name through a brave act at age 14. He first fought U.S. forces in 1864 during the Dakota War's aftermath. His early resistance was part of broader Lakota efforts to protect their lands from military and railroad encroachment.

### Resistance and the Treaty of Fort Laramie
During Red Cloud's War (1866–68), Sitting Bull led attacks on U.S. forts. He famously refused to sign the 1868 Treaty of Fort Laramie, which created the Great Sioux Reservation, stating, "I do not propose to sell any part of my country." While other chiefs like Red Cloud moved to reservations, Sitting Bull maintained independence, making his band a symbol of non-surrender.

### The Great Sioux War and Little Bighorn
Tensions escalated after gold was discovered in the Black Hills (1874), violating the 1868 treaty. In 1875, the U.S. government ordered all Sioux onto reservations, declaring those who refused "hostiles." Sitting Bull's camp grew into a large unity village. Before the Battle of the Little Bighorn (June 25, 1876), he performed a Sun Dance and had a prophetic vision. Though not a direct battlefield commander, his spiritual leadership inspired the coalition that annihilated Custer's battalion. The victory prompted a massive U.S. military response, forcing many Lakota to surrender.

### Exile, Surrender, and Later Years
Refusing to surrender, Sitting Bull led his band to Canada in 1877. After four years, starvation forced their return and surrender in 1881. He was held as a prisoner of war and later lived at Standing Rock Agency. In 1885, he toured with Buffalo Bill's Wild West show, earning money but giving much of it away.

### Ghost Dance and Death
In 1889–90, the Ghost Dance movement, which prophesied a Native American revival, spread to the reservations. U.S. authorities feared Sitting Bull would use his influence to lead an uprising. On December 15, 1890, Indian agency police attempted to arrest him. In the ensuing struggle, he was shot and killed by officers Lieutenant Bull Head and Red Tomahawk.

## Terms
- **Hunkpapa Lakota:** One of the seven council fires of the Lakota (Sioux) people, Sitting Bull's band.
- **Counting coup:** A Plains Indian warrior tradition of touching an enemy in battle without killing them, considered a great honor.
- **Sun Dance:** A sacred annual ceremony involving fasting, prayer, and sacrifice to renew the world and ensure tribal well-being.
- **Ghost Dance:** A religious movement (c. 1889–90) that prophesied the resurrection of Native Americans and the return of the buffalo, alarming U.S. authorities.
- **Indian agency:** A U.S. government administrative office on a reservation, responsible for managing relations with and providing supplies to Native Americans.
- **Treaty of Fort Laramie (1868):** An agreement establishing the Great Sioux Reservation and promising U.S. protection of Lakota lands, including the Black Hills.
- **Strong medicine:** A Lakota concept referring to powerful spiritual power or knowledge possessed by a person.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Leadership Role:** Historians debate the extent of Sitting Bull's formal authority. While some sources from the 1870s called him "Supreme Chief of the whole Sioux Nation," this is contested, as Lakota society was decentralized, with individual bands making their own decisions.
- **Role at Little Bighorn:** His precise military versus spiritual role in the 1876 battle is a point of scholarly discussion; he is generally described as a spiritual leader rather than a tactical commander.
- **Ghost Dance Involvement:** The degree to which Sitting Bull actively supported or merely tolerated the Ghost Dance movement at Standing Rock is unclear, though his presence was seen as a threat by U.S. agents.

# Majapahit

## Overview
Majapahit was a Hindu-Buddhist thalassocratic empire based on Java (modern Indonesia) that, at its 14th-century peak, claimed suzerainty over much of the Indonesian archipelago. It is considered one of the greatest empires in Indonesian and Southeast Asian history and is sometimes seen as a precedent for modern Indonesia's boundaries. Its decline led to the rise of Islamic kingdoms in Java.

## Timeline
- **1293** — Raden Wijaya founds Majapahit after defeating Mongol invaders and the usurper Jayakatwang.
- **1328** — King Jayanegara is murdered; Queen Tribhuwana Wijayatunggadewi ascends the throne.
- **1336** — Gajah Mada becomes prime minister and makes his famous Palapa oath to unify the archipelago.
- **1343** — Majapahit forces conquer the Bali Kingdom.
- **1350** — Hayam Wuruk becomes king, beginning the empire's golden age.
- **1357** — The Battle of Bubat occurs, where the Sundanese royal party is massacred.
- **1365** — The Nagarakretagama is composed, detailing Majapahit's claimed tributaries.
- **1389** — Death of Hayam Wuruk, leading to succession conflicts and decline.
- **1405-06** — The Regreg Civil War is fought between rival claimants.
- **1478** — Traditional date for the fall of Majapahit's core kingdom (though the empire persisted in some form).
- **1527** — The Sultanate of Demak completes the conquest, marking the final collapse.

## Body

### Formation and Early History
Majapahit was established by Raden Wijaya in 1293 following the Mongol invasion of Java. After the Singhasari kingdom was usurped, Raden Wijaya allied with the Mongols to defeat the usurper, then turned on and expelled the Mongol forces. He founded a new capital at a settlement named Majapahit, meaning "bitter maja" after a local fruit. The early reign of his successor, Jayanegara, was marked by rebellions and instability, which were suppressed with the help of the future prime minister Gajah Mada.

### Golden Age
The empire reached its zenith during the reigns of Queen Tribhuwana Wijayatunggadewi and her son Hayam Wuruk (mid-14th century), guided by the ambitious prime minister Gajah Mada. Under their rule, Majapahit expanded militarily, conquering Bali in 1343 and projecting power across the archipelago. The 1365 Nagarakretagama lists 98 tributaries, though the actual extent of direct control is debated. The period was marked by sophisticated court culture, literature, and art. A disastrous diplomatic incident with the Sunda Kingdom in 1357, known as the Battle of Bubat, highlighted the empire's aggressive expansionism.

### Decline and Fall
Following Hayam Wuruk's death in 1389, Majapahit entered a period of decline due to succession wars, most notably the Regreg War (1405-1406). The rise of the Sultanate of Malacca, supported by Ming China, weakened Majapahit's maritime influence. Internal divisions led to a split in the kingdom in 1468. The final blow came from the Sultanate of Demak, a former Majapahit vassal on Java's north coast. Demak forces conquered the weakened Majapahit heartland in 1527, leading to a mass exodus of Hindu-Buddhist courtiers, artisans, and priests to Bali, which became a refuge for Majapahit culture.

### Culture and Society
Majapahit society was a syncretic blend of Hinduism and Buddhism, with the king often seen as an incarnation of deities like Shiva and Buddha. The state religion was likely a syncretic Shiva-Buddha doctrine. Literature flourished, producing works like the Nagarakretagama and the Sutasoma, the latter containing the national motto "Bhinneka Tunggal Ika" (Unity in Diversity). Art and architecture featured a distinctive East Javanese style with stiff, wayang-like figures and the extensive use of red brick. The economy was partly monetized, using imported Chinese copper coins, and thrived on agriculture and control of trade routes for spices.

### Administration and Military
Majapahit employed a bureaucratic structure with the king at the apex, assisted by ministers and a council of elders. Its territory was hierarchically divided into the core Negara Agung, the directly influenced Mancanegara (provinces in Java and Bali), and the tributary Nusantara (outer islands). The military maintained a standing army of 30,000 professional troops and a powerful navy featuring large warships called jongs. Majapahit was an early adopter of gunpowder technology in the region, using cannons known as cetbang.

## Terms
- **Thalassocracy**: A state with its primary power based on the sea and naval dominance.
- **Mandala**: A model of political organization in Southeast Asia where power radiates from a center to tributary polities without direct administrative integration.
- **Nagarakretagama**: A 1365 Old Javanese eulogy that is a primary source for Majapahit's history and claimed extent.
- **Palapa Oath**: The famous vow by prime minister Gajah Mada to not rest until he unified the archipelago under Majapahit.
- **Jong**: The large, multi-decked warship that was the main vessel of the Majapahit navy.
- **Cetbang**: An early type of cannon or hand cannon used by the Majapahit military.
- **Bhayangkara**: The elite royal guard tasked with protecting the king and nobility.
- **Keris**: A distinctive asymmetrical dagger that became widespread across the archipelago during the Majapahit era.
- **Candi bentar**: A split gateway architectural feature that became iconic in Javanese and Balinese architecture.

## Debates and open questions
The true nature and extent of Majapahit's power are heavily debated. The Nagarakretagama's list of 98 tributaries may represent a sphere of influence or geographical knowledge rather than direct political control. Scholars question whether Majapahit was a centralized empire or a network of trade connections and ceremonial overlordship. The exact date of its fall is also contested, with traditional chronograms pointing to 1478, while final conquest by Demak occurred in 1527.

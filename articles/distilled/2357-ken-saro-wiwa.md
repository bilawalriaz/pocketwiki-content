# Ken Saro-Wiwa

## Overview
Ken Saro-Wiwa (1941–1995) was a Nigerian writer and activist who became the international symbol of the struggle for environmental justice and human rights in the Niger Delta. As leader of the Movement for the Survival of the Ogoni People (MOSOP), he led a nonviolent campaign against the environmental devastation caused by oil extraction in Ogoniland. His execution by the Nigerian military government in 1995 sparked global outrage and is considered a foundational event in the international business and human rights movement.

## Timeline
- **10 October 1941** — Born in Bori, near Port Harcourt, Nigeria.
- **1967–1970** — Serves as Civilian Administrator in Bonny during the Nigerian Civil War.
- **1985** — Publishes his best-known novel, *Sozaboy: A Novel in Rotten English*.
- **1990** — Begins devoting most of his time to Ogoni human rights and environmental causes.
- **January 1993** — MOSOP organizes peaceful marches of ~300,000 Ogoni people.
- **May 1994** — Four Ogoni chiefs are murdered; Saro-Wiwa is arrested and accused of inciting the killings.
- **10 November 1995** — Saro-Wiwa and eight other Ogoni leaders (the "Ogoni Nine") are executed by hanging.
- **November 1995** — Nigeria is suspended from the Commonwealth of Nations.
- **June 2009** — Royal Dutch Shell agrees to a $15.5 million out-of-court settlement with victims' families. |
- **12 June 2025** — Saro-Wiwa and the Ogoni Nine are granted a posthumous pardon by President Bola Tinubu.

## Body

### Early Life and Career
Born to a chief in the Nigerian chieftaincy system, Saro-Wiwa was an excellent student who studied English at the University of Ibadan. He worked as a teacher and television producer, creating the wildly popular satirical series *Basi & Company*. During the Nigerian Civil War (1967–1970), he supported the federal government and served as an administrator. His novel *Sozaboy* (1985) used a hybrid language he called "Rotten English" to depict the war's corruption. He was briefly a commissioner in Rivers State but was dismissed in 1973 for supporting Ogoni autonomy.

### Activism and MOSOP
In 1990, Saro-Wiwa became a leading figure in MOSOP, which advocated for the Ogoni people—an ethnic minority whose homeland had suffered extreme environmental damage from oil extraction since the 1950s. MOSOP's Ogoni Bill of Rights demanded autonomy, a fair share of oil proceeds, and environmental remediation, specifically targeting companies like Royal Dutch Shell. In January 1993, MOSOP organized massive peaceful marches, drawing international attention. The Nigerian government responded by militarily occupying the region.

### Arrest, Trial, and Execution
Following the murder of four Ogoni chiefs in May 1994, Saro-Wiwa was arrested and accused of masterminding the killings, despite being denied entry to Ogoniland that day. He was tried by a special military tribunal under General Sani Abacha's dictatorship. The trial was widely condemned as rigged; lawyers resigned in protest, and witnesses later recanted, admitting they were bribed by the government and Shell. In November 1995, Saro-Wiwa and eight other MOSOP leaders were hanged. His last words were: "Lord take my soul, but the struggle continues."

### International Aftermath and Legacy
The executions provoked immediate international condemnation. The UN General Assembly passed a condemning resolution, the EU imposed an arms embargo, and the US recalled its ambassador. Nigeria was suspended from the Commonwealth. The event is credited with launching the international business and human rights movement. Beginning in 1996, lawsuits were filed against Shell under the Alien Tort Statute, leading to a $15.5 million settlement in 2009. Saro-Wiwa's legacy endures through memorials, awards, and cultural references, and he received a posthumous pardon in 2025.

## Terms
- **Ogoni Bill of Rights**: A document crafted by MOSOP outlining demands for Ogoni autonomy, resource sharing, and environmental cleanup.
- **MOSOP**: Movement for the Survival of the Ogoni People, the organization Saro-Wiwa led in nonviolent protest.
- **Rotten English**: A hybrid language of pidgin, standard, and broken English that Saro-Wiwa used in his novel *Sozaboy*.
- **Alien Tort Statute**: A 1789 US law allowing non-citizens to sue in US courts for international human rights violations.
- **Commonwealth of Nations**: A political association of 56 member states, mostly former territories of the British Empire.
- **Ogoni Nine**: The collective name for Saro-Wiwa and the eight other MOSOP leaders executed in 1995.
- **Right Livelihood Award**: An international award often called the "Alternative Nobel Prize," given to Saro-Wiwa in 1995 for his courage.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The precise degree of Royal Dutch Shell's complicity in the Nigerian government's crackdown and the execution of the Ogoni Nine remains a subject of legal and historical debate.
- The motivations behind the Nigerian military government's decision to proceed with the executions despite intense international pressure are analyzed as a test of the Abacha regime's resolve.
- The effectiveness of the posthumous pardon granted in 2025 in achieving reconciliation and justice for the Ogoni people is an ongoing question.

# Accra

## Overview
Accra is the capital and largest city of Ghana, located on the southern coast at the Gulf of Guinea. It serves as the country's economic and administrative hub, having transitioned from a colonial capital to a modern metropolis with growing international influence. Its history, architecture, and urban challenges reflect its evolution from a collection of settlements around European forts into a major African city.

## Timeline
- **15th century** — Kingdom of Accra rules the area from its capital at Ayawaso.
- **1677** — Akwamu Kingdom sacks Ayawaso, beheading the Accra king; survivors retreat to "Small Accra" near Fort Crèvecœur.
- **1877** — Accra replaces Cape Coast as the capital of the British Gold Coast.
- **1957** — Kwame Nkrumah becomes Ghana's first Prime Minister, initiating development plans for Accra.
- **2021** — Census records the Accra Metropolitan District population as 284,124 and the Greater Accra Region as 5.46 million.
- **2022** — UNESCO names Accra World Book Capital for 2023.

## Body

### Etymology and Early History
The name "Accra" derives from the Akan word *Nkran* ("ants"), referring to soldier ants and connoting the military prowess of the Ga people. The local Ga name is *Ga* or *Gaga*. By the 15th century, the Kingdom of Accra was a regional power. European trade began with the construction of forts by the Portuguese, Dutch, British, and Swedes in the 16th and 17th centuries. In 1677, the Akwamu Kingdom conquered Accra, sacking its capital and forcing survivors into smaller settlements near the European forts.

### Colonial Capital and Growth
Britain gradually acquired all European interests in the Gold Coast, culminating in 1871. After the second Anglo-Asante War, Accra was made the colonial capital in 1877 due to its drier climate. This decision spurred expansion, with new neighborhoods like Victoriaborg (for Europeans) and Adabraka (for natives) being established. Infrastructure projects in the 1920s, such as a bridge over the Korle Lagoon and the Korle-Bu hospital, further developed the city. After World War II, Accra became a center for nationalist movements leading to Ghana's independence in 1957.

### Modern Administration and Demographics
The administration of Accra operates at two levels, with strategic initiatives coordinated between district authorities. As of the 2021 census, the core Accra Metropolitan District (20.4 km²) had a population of 284,124, while the larger Greater Accra Region (3,245 km²) had 5.46 million inhabitants. The city's population is young, with 56% under 24 years of age, and has a slight female majority (51%).

### Economy and Infrastructure
Accra is Ghana's economic hub, with an economy estimated at US$3 billion in 2008. Its financial sector includes the Ghana Stock Exchange, numerous banks, and insurance companies. The tertiary service sector is the largest employer. The city is a transportation hub, served by Kotoka International Airport and connected by rail to Tema, Sekondi-Takoradi, and Kumasi. The Port of Tema, opened in 1961, handles Ghana's deepwater shipping.

### Environment and Challenges
As a coastal city, Accra is vulnerable to climate change and sea-level rise. It faces significant environmental challenges, including air pollution (with PM2.5 levels exceeding health standards), plastic pollution, and inadequate drainage infrastructure. The Agbogbloshie suburb is known for e-waste recycling, and the Kantamanto Market handles large volumes of used clothing, both contributing to pollution. Urban green space is decreasing due to population growth and construction.

### Culture and Education
Accra is Ghana's tourist hub, with hotels, museums, and the Accra International Conference Centre. It is home to the University of Ghana and numerous other educational institutions, including Achimota School (founded 1924) and several international schools. Pre-school and primary enrollment rates are high (95-98%). In 2022, UNESCO named Accra the World Book Capital for 2023.

## Terms
- **Gold Coast**: The name for the British colony that became Ghana.
- **Akwamu Kingdom**: A powerful Akan state that conquered the Accra region in the 17th century.
- **Ga people**: The indigenous ethnic group of the Accra Plains.
- **Tro tro**: A common form of public transport in Ghana, consisting of minibuses or vans.
- **Korle Lagoon**: A lagoon in Accra that was a geographical feature in the city's early expansion.
- **Greater Accra Metropolitan Area (GAMA)**: The larger urban area anchored by Accra, with a population of about 4 million.
- **Gamma − city**: A designation by the Globalization and World Cities Research Network indicating a growing level of international influence.

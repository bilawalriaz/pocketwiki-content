# William Ewart Gladstone

## Overview
William Ewart Gladstone (1809–1898) was a dominant British statesman who served as Prime Minister four times, the most of any UK premier. His career spanned nearly the entire Victorian era, and he evolved from a High Tory into the leading figure of the Liberal Party, championing "Gladstonian liberalism" focused on free trade, retrenchment, and individual liberty. His legacy is defined by major reforms in Ireland, finance, and the franchise, as well as his powerful oratory and moralistic political style.

## Timeline
- **1809** — Born in Liverpool to Sir John Gladstone, a wealthy merchant and slave owner.
- **1832** — Elected MP for Newark as a High Tory.
- **1841–1846** — Served as President of the Board of Trade under Prime Minister Robert Peel.
- **1852–1855** — First term as Chancellor of the Exchequer under Lord Aberdeen.
- **1859–1866** — Second term as Chancellor of the Exchequer, implementing free-trade budgets.
- **1868–1874** — First term as Prime Minister; passed reforms including disestablishment of the Church of Ireland.
- **1876** — Published *Bulgarian Horrors*, launching a campaign against Ottoman atrocities.
- **1879–1880** — Led the Midlothian campaign, considered the first modern political campaign.
- **1880–1885** — Second term as Prime Minister; passed the Third Reform Act and faced crises in Egypt and Sudan.
- **1886** — Third term as Prime Minister; proposed Irish Home Rule, causing a party split and his government's fall.
- **1892–1894** — Fourth term as Prime Minister; the Government of Ireland Bill passed the Commons but was rejected by the Lords.
- **1898** — Died at Hawarden Castle, aged 88.

## Body

### Early Life and Political Entry
Gladstone was born in Liverpool and educated at Eton and Oxford, where he developed a reputation as a Tory orator. He entered Parliament in 1832 as a High Tory, a group that formed the Conservative Party under Robert Peel. He served in Peel's governments, but in 1846 joined the breakaway Peelite faction, which later merged into the Liberal Party. His early career was marked by a strong religious conscience and a defense of his father's interests as a slave owner, though he later supported gradual emancipation.

### Chancellor of the Exchequer
Gladstone served as Chancellor four times, most influentially under Lord Palmerston (1859–1866). His budgets aimed to simplify tariffs, reduce indirect taxes, and balance the books through "peace and retrenchment." He championed free trade, notably via the Cobden–Chevalier Treaty with France, and fought to abolish the paper duty, which he saw as a tax on knowledge. His financial policies aimed to let money "fructify in the pockets of the people" and earned him immense popularity with the working class.

### First and Second Premierships (1868–1885)
As Prime Minister, Gladstone enacted "Gladstonian liberalism," which minimized public expenditure and promoted individual liberty. His first ministry (1868–1874) disestablished the Church of Ireland, introduced secret voting, and reformed the army and civil service. After an electoral defeat, he returned in 1880 following a moral campaign against Ottoman atrocities in Bulgaria. His second ministry saw the passage of the Third Reform Act, which greatly expanded the electorate, but was marred by crises in Egypt (leading to British occupation) and the failed relief of General Gordon in Khartoum, which forced his resignation.

### Irish Home Rule and Final Years
Gladstone's political career was ultimately defined by his campaign for Irish Home Rule. Convinced of its justice, he introduced Home Rule bills in 1886 and 1893. The first proposal split the Liberal Party, with Liberal Unionists defecting to ally with the Conservatives. The second bill passed the Commons but was overwhelmingly rejected by the House of Lords. This defeat led to his resignation in 1894 as the oldest ever Prime Minister. He left Parliament in 1895 and died in 1898.

## Terms
*   **Gladstonian liberalism:** A political doctrine promoting equality of opportunity, the repeal of taxes and protectionist trade barriers, and minimal state interference.
*   **Peelites:** A faction of the Conservative Party that followed Robert Peel after the repeal of the Corn Laws in 1846, later merging into the Liberal Party.
*   **Home Rule:** The proposal for a devolved Irish parliament with limited powers, separate from the Westminster Parliament.
*   **Disestablishment:** The withdrawal of a state church from its official status, as applied to the Church of Ireland in 1869.
*   **Third Reform Act (1884):** Legislation that extended the voting franchise to agricultural labourers, nearly doubling the electorate.
*   **Midlothian campaign (1879–80):** A series of public speeches by Gladstone attacking the Conservative government's foreign policy, considered the first modern political campaign.
*   **Bulgarian Horrors:** The term Gladstone used to describe Ottoman repression of Bulgarian rebels in 1876, which he used to launch a moral crusade.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   Historians debate the wisdom of Gladstone's foreign policy during his second ministry, with some describing it as "muddle and incompetence," particularly regarding the occupation of Egypt.
*   The motivations behind the British occupation of Egypt are contested, with explanations ranging from protecting the Suez Canal and British investments to internal Egyptian dynamics.
*   Gladstone's legacy is interpreted differently across the political spectrum: later Conservatives like Margaret Thatcher claimed him as an economic liberal, while his support for Irish Home Rule remains a central, divisive issue in his political narrative.

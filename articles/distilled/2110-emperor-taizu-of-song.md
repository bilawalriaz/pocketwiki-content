# Emperor Taizu of Song

## Overview
Emperor Taizu of Song (927–976), born Zhao Kuangyin, was the founding emperor of the Song dynasty. A former military general, he seized power in a coup and reunified most of China after decades of fragmentation. His reign established a civilian-led government and a long period of internal stability, shaping the dynasty's political and cultural foundations.

## Timeline
- **927** — Born in Luoyang to a military family.
- **949** — Joined the army of Later Han general Guo Wei.
- **951** — Promoted to palace guard commander after Guo Wei founded the Later Zhou dynasty.
- **954** — Distinguished himself at the Battle of Gaoping, securing a key victory.
- **959** — Became a military governor (*jiedushi*), consolidating major military power.
- **960** — Staged the Chenqiao Mutiny, forcing the abdication of the Later Zhou child emperor.
- **960–976** — Reigned as Emperor Taizu of the Song dynasty.
- **960s–970s** — Conquered Southern Han, Later Shu, Southern Tang, and Jingnan, reunifying China proper.
- **976** — Died; succeeded by his brother, Zhao Kuangyi (Emperor Taizong).

## Body

### Rise to Power
Zhao Kuangyin was a skilled military officer who rose through the ranks of the Later Zhou dynasty. His reputation was cemented at the Battle of Gaoping (954), where he led a counterattack that repelled an alliance of the Northern Han and Liao dynasties. He subsequently gained control of the palace guards and built a network of loyal officials and generals. After the death of the Later Zhou emperor Chai Rong in 959, Zhao Kuangyin was positioned to exploit the instability.

In 960, amid rumors of an invasion, Zhao Kuangyin was sent to lead the army. At Chenqiao, his troops mutinied and proclaimed him emperor. He reluctantly accepted, entering the capital and taking the throne with minimal resistance. He founded the Song dynasty, naming it after the prefecture where his army was stationed.

### Reunification and Governance
As emperor, Taizu shifted the reunification strategy from attacking the north first to conquering the weaker southern states. He successfully absorbed Later Shu, Southern Han, Southern Tang, and Jingnan. His only major setback was a failed campaign against the Northern Han, which was protected by the Liao dynasty.

To prevent future military coups, Taizu centralized power. He famously convinced his top generals to retire in exchange for wealth and estates, a move that ended the era of warlordism. He expanded the civil service examination system, making it the primary path to officialdom and reducing military influence in administration. He also established academies that promoted intellectual freedom, contributing to cultural and scientific growth.

### Death and Succession
Taizu died in 976 at age 49. Despite having two adult sons, he was succeeded by his younger brother, Zhao Kuangyi (Emperor Taizong). Traditional accounts cite a deathbed promise made to their mother as the reason. Folklore, however, suggests he may have been murdered by his brother in a plot known as "shadows by the candle and sounds from an axe." After Emperor Taizong, the succession passed to his own line, but in 1161, Emperor Gaozong restored the line of succession to Taizu's descendants.

## Terms
- **Jiedushi**: A military governor or regional commander, a powerful position that often led to warlordism during the Five Dynasties period.
- **Coup d'état**: A sudden and illegal seizure of power from a government, in this case, a military mutiny.
- **Mandate of Heaven**: The ancient Chinese belief that heaven granted the right to rule to a just leader.
- **Civil service examination system**: A bureaucratic recruitment process based on merit through competitive exams, which Taizu expanded.
- **Northern Han**: One of the Ten Kingdoms, located in the north and supported by the Liao dynasty.
- **Liao dynasty**: A Khitan-led empire that was a major rival to the Song.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **The "Taizu's Oath"**: Many sources record an oath by Taizu forbidding his successors from killing scholar-officials. However, this story might be a later historical construct.
- **Succession Dispute**: The exact circumstances of Taizu's death and the succession of his brother over his sons remain a subject of historical debate, with theories ranging from a legitimate promise to fratricide.
- **Military Style**: Whether Emperor Taizu actually invented the Taizu Changquan (Long Fist) martial arts style is unknown, with the tradition being part of later lore.

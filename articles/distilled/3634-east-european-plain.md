# East European Plain

## Overview
The East European Plain is a vast, low-lying interior plain that forms the largest mountain-free landscape in Europe. Stretching from the Baltic Sea to the Ural Mountains, it encompasses the majority of European Russia, Belarus, Ukraine, and parts of several other nations. Its flat terrain and major river basins have historically facilitated movement, settlement, and agriculture across the continent.

## Timeline
- **c. 3000 BC** — The plain is part of the historical region known as the Sarmatic Plain.
- **18th-19th c.** — The plain is a core territory of the Russian Empire and later the Soviet Union.
- **1991** — Following the dissolution of the USSR, the plain is divided among multiple independent nations.

## Body

### Geography and Extent
The East European Plain extends east from the North European Plain, covering approximately 4,000,000 km² (2,000,000 sq mi) with an average elevation of about 170 m (560 ft). Its highest point, at 480 m (1,574.8 ft), is in the Bugulma-Belebey Upland near the Ural Mountains. The plain is bounded by the Baltic, White, Barents, and Kara Seas to the north; the Ural Mountains and Turan Depression to the east; the Caspian Sea, Caucasus, and Crimean Mountains to the south; and the Carpathians, Sudetenland, and Baltic Sea to the west.

### Regional Subdivisions and Landforms
The plain is not uniform but consists of a series of plateaus, uplands, and lowlands. Key subdivisions include the Central Russian Upland, the Volga Upland, and the Valdai Hills in Russia; the Belarusian Ridge; and the Volhynian-Podolian Upland in Ukraine. Major lowland basins include the Dnieper Lowland, the Oka–Don Lowland, and the vast Caspian Depression. Other significant features are the North Russian Plain, the Baltic Uplands, and the Kuma–Manych Depression.

### Hydrography
The plain is drained by several of Europe's largest rivers. The Volga, the longest river in Europe, flows through the Volga Basin. Other major rivers include the Danube, Dnieper, Don, Ural, Vistula, and Pechora. These river systems have been crucial for transportation, irrigation, and the development of civilizations throughout the region.

## Terms
- **Upland:** An area of relatively high ground, often a plateau or a broad, elevated region.
- **Lowland:** A broad area of relatively flat land with a low elevation.
- **Basin:** A large depression or hollow in the Earth's surface, typically containing a major river system.
- **Depression:** A low area in the ground, often a large-scale geological basin like the Caspian Depression.
- **Ridge:** A long, narrow elevation or mountain range.
- **Uval:** A Russian geological term for an elongated elevation with a flat, slightly convex or wavy top and gentle slopes.

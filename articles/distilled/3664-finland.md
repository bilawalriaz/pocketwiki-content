# Finland

## Overview
Finland is a Nordic country in Northern Europe with a population of 5.7 million. Its history is defined by centuries of Swedish rule, followed by autonomy within the Russian Empire, independence in 1917, and a post-WWII transformation into a high-tech welfare state. Today, it is known for its high standard of living, strong education system, and recent shift from neutrality to NATO membership.

## Timeline
- **c. 8500 BC** — Area settled after the last Ice Age.
- **12th–13th centuries** — Northern Crusades lead to gradual incorporation into the Kingdom of Sweden.
- **1809** — Finnish War results in Finland becoming an autonomous Grand Duchy within the Russian Empire.
- **1906** — Finland becomes the first European country to grant universal suffrage.
- **1917** — Finland declares independence from Russia.
- **1918** — Civil war between the "Whites" and "Reds"; Whites are victorious.
- **1939–1940** — Winter War against the Soviet Union; Finland cedes territory.
- **1941–1944** — Continuation War, allied with Germany against the USSR.
- **1944–1945** — Lapland War against retreating German forces.
- **1995** — Finland joins the European Union.
- **2023** — Finland becomes a member of NATO.

## Body

### Prehistory and Early History
The area was settled around 8500 BC. The Stone Age saw the arrival of the Comb Ceramic culture (c. 5200 BC) and later the Corded Ware culture (c. 3000–2500 BC), which may have introduced agriculture. Linguistic evidence suggests the area was first inhabited by speakers of Paleo-European languages before the migration of Finno-Ugric peoples. The Bronze and Iron Ages brought increased trade and contact with Baltic and Scandinavian cultures.

### Swedish Era and Grand Duchy
From the late 13th century, Finland was gradually integrated into the Kingdom of Sweden following a series of crusades. Swedish became the language of administration, while Finnish remained the language of the peasantry. The Protestant Reformation led to a gradual conversion to Lutheranism. This era ended with the Finnish War of 1809, after which Finland became an autonomous Grand Duchy under the Russian Empire. The 19th century saw the rise of Finnish nationalism (the Fennoman movement) and the establishment of a distinct Finnish national identity.

### Independence and World War II
Following the Russian Revolution, Finland declared independence in 1917, which was recognized by Soviet Russia in early 1918. A brief but bitter civil war ensued, won by the anticommunist "Whites." Finland became a republic in 1919. During World War II, Finland fought three separate wars: the Winter War (1939–40) and Continuation War (1941–44) against the Soviet Union, and the Lapland War (1944–45) against Nazi Germany. Despite losing 12% of its territory and paying war reparations, Finland retained its independence and democratic system.

### Post-War Development and Modern Era
After WWII, Finland transformed from an agrarian society to an industrialized one, aided by trade with both the West and the Soviet Union. During the Cold War, it maintained a policy of official neutrality, a stance sometimes called "Finlandisation." Following the collapse of the Soviet Union, Finland integrated more closely with the West, joining the EU in 1995 and the Eurozone in 1999. The Russian invasion of Ukraine in 2022 prompted a decisive shift in public opinion and policy, leading to Finland's accession to NATO in 2023.

## Terms
*   **Fennoman movement:** A 19th-century Finnish nationalist movement that championed the official status of the Finnish language and a distinct national identity.
*   **Grand Duchy:** An autonomous territory within the Russian Empire that Finland was from 1809 to 1917.
*   **Universal suffrage:** The right of all adult citizens to vote; Finland granted this in 1906, the first in Europe.
*   **Winter War:** The 1939–1940 conflict between Finland and the Soviet Union, ending with Finnish territorial concessions.
*   **Continuation War:** The 1941–1944 conflict where Finland allied with Germany to regain territory lost in the Winter War.
*   **Lapland War:** The 1944–1945 conflict where Finland fought retreating German forces in its north.
*   **Finlandisation:** A term coined during the Cold War to describe Finland's policy of avoiding actions that might antagonize the Soviet Union.
*   **Nordic welfare model:** A comprehensive welfare system with universal services and high taxation, which Finland adopted post-WWII.
*   **D'Hondt method:** A proportional representation system used to elect Finland's parliament.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   The exact origins and etymology of the name "Suomi" (Finland) are uncertain and debated.
*   The nature and impact of the "Finlandisation" policy during the Cold War remain subjects of analysis.
*   The long-term demographic challenges posed by Finland's low fertility rate (1.26 children per woman) and aging population are a significant concern.

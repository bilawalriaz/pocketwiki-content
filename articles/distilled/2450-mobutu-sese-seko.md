# Mobutu Sese Seko

## Overview
Mobutu Sese Seko was the dictator of Zaire (now the Democratic Republic of the Congo) from 1965 to 1997. He first came to power through a military coup in 1965, following his role in the earlier ousting of the democratically elected Prime Minister Patrice Lumumba. His rule is defined by extreme corruption, a totalitarian personality cult, and the economic ruin of a resource-rich nation, making him a symbol of Cold War-era kleptocracy.

## Timeline
- **14 October 1930** — Born Joseph-Désiré Mobutu in Lisala, Belgian Congo.
- **14 September 1960** — Leads a bloodless coup, "neutralizing" both President Kasa-Vubu and Prime Minister Lumumba.
- **17 January 1961** — Lumumba is executed after being transferred to Katanga by Mobutu's government.
- **24 November 1965** — Seizes power in a second, decisive coup, becoming head of state.
- **1967** — Establishes the Popular Movement of the Revolution (MPR) as the sole legal party.
- **1 June 1966** — Begins "authenticity" campaign, renaming colonial cities (e.g., Léopoldville to Kinshasa).
- **October 1971** — Renames the country the Republic of Zaire.
- **1972** — Renames himself Mobutu Sese Seko.
- **1973–1974** — Launches "Zairianization," nationalizing foreign businesses and handing them to associates.
- **30 October 1974** — Hosts the "Rumble in the Jungle" boxing match in Kinshasa.
- **May 1990** — Under pressure, agrees to end the MPR's political monopoly.
- **16 May 1997** — Rebel forces under Laurent-Désiré Kabila proclaim victory.
- **7 September 1997** — Dies in exile in Rabat, Morocco.

## Body

### Rise to Power
Mobutu, a member of the Ngbandi ethnic group, was educated by Belgian colonial authorities and served in the Force Publique. He became a journalist and was recruited as an informer for both Belgian intelligence and the CIA. He joined Patrice Lumumba's independence movement, becoming his aide. During the 1960 Congo Crisis, Mobutu was made Army Chief of Staff. Amid a power struggle between President Kasa-Vubu and Prime Lumumba, and under pressure from Western powers opposed to Lumumba's Soviet ties, Mobutu launched a coup on 14 September 1960. Lumumba was later captured and executed in 1961. Mobutu consolidated military control and, after a period of political instability, seized full power in a second coup on 24 November 1965.

### Consolidation and Rule
Mobutu established a totalitarian one-party state under the MPR. He promoted an ideology of "authenticity," purging colonial cultural influences by renaming places, banning Western attire, and changing his own name. His regime was characterized by a pervasive personality cult, with titles like "Father of the Nation." He maintained control through a corrupt patronage network, rewarding loyalty with state funds and positions, while brutally suppressing opposition through public executions and torture. His rule is the archetypal example of kleptocracy; he amassed a personal fortune estimated between $50 million and $5 billion while the national economy collapsed into hyperinflation, debt, and infrastructure decay.

### Foreign Policy and Cold War
Mobutu was a staunch anti-communist, which secured him strong military, diplomatic, and economic support from the United States, France, and Belgium throughout the Cold War. He also built ties with apartheid South Africa and Israel. His relationship with the Soviet Union was frosty, and he turned to China in the 1970s as a counterbalance. This Western support allowed him to survive multiple crises, including the Shaba invasions (1977–78), where French and Belgian troops intervened to repel rebels. After the Cold War ended, Western support waned, and pressure mounted for democratization.

### Downfall and Death
By 1990, economic crisis and domestic unrest forced Mobutu into a coalition government, though he retained real power. His downfall was triggered by the aftermath of the 1994 Rwandan genocide. He welcomed Hutu extremists into eastern Zaire, destabilizing the region. In 1996, a rebellion led by Laurent-Désiré Kabila, backed by Rwanda and Uganda, began advancing west. Mobutu, ill with prostate cancer, was unable to rally resistance. Kabila's forces took Kinshasa on 17 May 1997, renaming the country the Democratic Republic of the Congo. Mobutu fled into exile and died in Morocco three months later.

## Terms
*   **Kleptocracy:** A government with corrupt leaders who use their power to exploit the people and natural resources of their own nation for personal gain.
*   **Authenticité:** Mobutu's cultural policy aimed at purging Belgian colonial influences and promoting a "pure" African identity, involving name changes and dress codes.
*   **Popular Movement of the Revolution (MPR):** The sole legal political party in Zaire from 1967 to 1990, which was defined as the "single institution" of the state.
*   **Binza Group:** A powerful political faction of high-ranking officials, supported by the CIA, that influenced state policy in the late 1960s.
*   **Zairianization:** A 1973–74 policy of nationalizing foreign-owned businesses and transferring them to Zairian (often Mobutu's associates), leading to economic mismanagement.
*   **Shaba I & II:** Invasions of Zaire's Shaba province by Katangan rebels in 1977 and 1978, repelled with French and Belgian military intervention.
*   **Force Publique:** The colonial army of the Belgian Congo, which Mobutu served in and later transformed into the national army.
*   **Abacost:** A Mao-style tunic that became Mobutu's signature attire, mandated as part of the "authenticity" campaign.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Extent of Western Complicity:** Historians debate the precise level of U.S. and Belgian involvement in the 1961 execution of Patrice Lumumba, with evidence suggesting they were aware of and facilitated the transfer that led to his death.
*   **Economic Responsibility:** While Mobutu's corruption is undisputed, scholars analyze the relative impact of his personal looting versus broader structural factors like the collapse of global copper prices in the 1970s on Zaire's economic ruin.
*   **Legacy of "Authenticity":** The long-term cultural and political impact of Mobutu's authenticity campaign is debated—whether it was a genuine decolonization effort or merely a tool for personal aggrandizement and control.

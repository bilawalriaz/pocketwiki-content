# Tigranes the Great

## Overview
Tigranes II (140–55 BC), known as Tigranes the Great, was an Armenian king of the Artaxiad dynasty who ruled from 95 to 55 BC. He transformed Armenia into the most powerful state east of the Roman Republic, creating a vast empire that stretched from the Caspian Sea to the Mediterranean. His reign represents the peak of Armenian political and military power in antiquity, though his conflicts with Rome ultimately led to Armenia's reduction to a client state.

## Timeline
- **c. 120 BC** — Parthian king Mithridates II invades Armenia; Tigranes is taken as a hostage.
- **c. 96/95 BC** — Tigranes is released and appointed king of Armenia by Mithridates II.
- **95–85 BC** — Tigranes expands eastward, annexing Parthian client kingdoms like Adiabene and Media Atropatene.
- **83 BC** — Tigranes is offered and accepts the crown of Syria, ending Seleucid rule.
- **88–85 BC** — During the First Mithridatic War, Tigranes allies with Mithridates VI of Pontus but avoids direct conflict with Rome.
- **69 BC** — Tigranes is decisively defeated by Roman general Lucullus at the Battle of Tigranocerta.
- **68 BC** — Tigranes and Mithridates are defeated again by Lucullus at Artaxata.
- **66 BC** — Tigranes surrenders to Pompey, retaining Armenia as a Roman buffer state.
- **55/54 BC** — Tigranes dies at age 85, having ruled as a Roman client king.

## Body

### Rise to Power and Empire Building
Tigranes began his reign as a Parthian vassal, having spent his youth as a hostage in the Parthian court. He consolidated power within Armenia's feudalistic structure before embarking on rapid expansion. By marrying Cleopatra, daughter of Mithridates VI of Pontus, he formed a strategic alliance. Tigranes exploited Roman distraction during the Social War and Parthian civil strife to annex territories including Cappadocia, Syria, Phoenicia, and Cilicia. At its height, his empire covered 900,000 km² with an estimated population of ten million. He adopted the Achaemenid title "King of Kings" and founded the Hellenistic capital Tigranocerta, forcibly relocating populations to populate it.

### Conflict with Rome
Direct conflict with Rome began when Tigranes sheltered his ally Mithridates VI after the latter's defeats. Roman general Lucullus invaded Armenia, achieving surprise victories at Tigranocerta (69 BC) and Artaxata (68 BC). However, Roman troops eventually mutinied due to harsh campaigning. In 66 BC, Pompey advanced with Tigranes' own son, Tigranes the Younger, who had defected to the Parthians. The elderly Tigranes surrendered, was allowed to keep Armenia's heartland as a buffer state, and paid 6,000 talents of silver. He ruled thereafter as a Roman client king.

### Culture and Legacy
Tigranes blended Iranian and Hellenistic influences, being a Zoroastrian who adopted the Greek title "Philhellene" (friend of the Greeks). His court featured Greek rhetoricians and philosophers, and his capital's architecture mixed Greek and Iranian styles. His coinage, minted in Antioch, is considered among the finest of Armenian monarchs, depicting him with an Armenian tiara featuring Iranian symbols like the star of divinity and birds of prey. Historians debate his legacy: some, like René Grousset, praise him for ensuring Armenian survival, while others, like Robert H. Hewsen, criticize his short-lived empire and forced relocations. He remains a potent symbol in modern Armenian nationalism, inspiring maximalist territorial claims.

## Terms
- **Artaxiad dynasty**: The ruling dynasty of Armenia to which Tigranes belonged.
- **Nakharars**: The feudalistic Armenian nobility who held significant regional power before Tigranes centralized authority.
- **Hellenistic**: Relating to Greek culture and influence that spread after Alexander the Great's conquests.
- **Client king**: A ruler who maintains autonomy under the suzerainty of a more powerful state, in this case Rome.
- **Zoroastrianism**: The ancient Iranian religion followed by Tigranes and many Armenians.
- **Philhellene**: A title meaning "friend of the Greeks," adopted by Tigranes to signal cultural alignment.
- **Tigranocerta**: Tigranes' new capital city, built with a blend of Greek and Iranian architecture.
- **King of Kings**: An ancient title of supreme authority, used by Tigranes and Parthian rulers.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Motivations for expansion**: Soviet Armenian historiography interpreted Tigranes' expansion as economically driven to satisfy the ruling class, while other scholars emphasize political and cultural factors.
- **Parentage of children**: Historian Gagik Sargsyan argues that only Artavasdes II and one daughter were born to Cleopatra of Pontus, with Tigranes' other children born earlier to a different mother.
- **Historical significance**: Scholars disagree on whether Tigranes' empire was a fleeting achievement or crucially shaped Armenian national identity for centuries.
- **Treatment of subjects**: His practice of forcibly relocating conquered populations is criticized as reprehensible, though it was common in ancient state-building.

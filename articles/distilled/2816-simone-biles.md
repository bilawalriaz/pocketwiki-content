# Simone Biles

## Overview
Simone Biles is an American artistic gymnast widely regarded as the greatest of all time. Her record-breaking 11 Olympic and 30 World Championship medals make her the most decorated gymnast in history. Her career is defined not only by unprecedented athletic dominance but also by her public advocacy for mental health and her resilience in overcoming personal and systemic challenges.

## Timeline
- **March 14, 1997** — Born in Columbus, Ohio.
- **2003** — Formally adopted by her maternal grandfather and his wife.
- **2011** — Begins elite gymnastics career at age 14.
- **2013** — Wins first World all-around title; becomes first African American woman to do so.
- **2016** — Wins four gold medals at Rio Olympics, becoming the most decorated American gymnast in a single Games.
- **2018** — Returns to competition; reveals she was sexually assaulted by Larry Nassar.
- **2019** — Wins a record fifth World all-around title and becomes most decorated female gymnast in World history.
- **2021** — Withdraws from most events at Tokyo Olympics due to "the twisties," sparking global conversation on mental health.
- **2022** — Awarded the Presidential Medal of Freedom.
- **2023** — Wins record eighth U.S. all-around title and sixth World all-around title.
- **2024** — Wins three gold medals at Paris Olympics, becoming the third woman to win two Olympic all-around titles.

## Body

### Early Life and Career Beginnings
Born in 1997, Biles and her siblings entered foster care due to their mother's inability to care for them. In 2003, she was adopted by her maternal grandfather, Ron Biles, and his wife, Nellie. She began gymnastics at age six and started elite training at 14 in 2011. After a poor performance at the 2013 U.S. Classic, she began working with a sports psychologist, a move she credits with enabling her subsequent dominance.

### Rise to Dominance (2013–2016)
Biles's senior career began in 2013, and she quickly established herself as the sport's new standard. She won her first World all-around title that year, becoming the seventh American and first African American woman to do so. She successfully defended the title in 2014 and 2015, becoming the first woman to win three consecutive World all-around championships. At the 2016 Rio Olympics, she led the "Final Five" team to gold and won four individual gold medals, setting an American record for a single Games.

### Hiatus, Return, and the Larry Nassar Scandal (2017–2019)
After a competitive hiatus in 2017, Biles returned in 2018 with upgraded skills and a new coach. In January 2018, she publicly revealed she was sexually assaulted by former USA Gymnastics physician Larry Nassar. She competed at the 2018 World Championships with a kidney stone, winning the all-around and becoming the most decorated gymnast in World history. In 2019, she further cemented her legacy by winning a record fifth World all-around title and introducing two new eponymous skills of the highest difficulty.

### The Tokyo Olympics and Mental Health Advocacy (2020–2021)
At the 2020 Tokyo Olympics (held in 2021), Biles experienced "the twisties"—a temporary loss of air awareness—and withdrew from most competitions to protect her safety. She returned to win a bronze medal on the balance beam, which she called her most meaningful medal. Her decision to prioritize mental health sparked widespread discussion and was both criticized and praised, but it fundamentally shifted conversations about athlete well-being.

### Historic Comeback and Continued Excellence (2023–2024)
Biles returned to competition in 2023, winning a record eighth U.S. all-around title and a sixth World all-around title, surpassing Vitaly Scherbo as the most decorated gymnast of all time. At the 2024 Paris Olympics, she won three gold medals, including the all-around, becoming the third woman in history to win two Olympic all-around titles. She also won gold on vault and with the team, solidifying her status as the most decorated Olympic gymnast in U.S. history.

## Terms
*   **Artistic Gymnastics:** A discipline of gymnastics involving performances on apparatus like the vault, uneven bars, balance beam, and floor exercise.
*   **The Twisties:** A psychological phenomenon where a gymnast loses spatial awareness and proprioception (the sense of body position) while performing twisting elements in the air.
*   **Eponymous Skill:** A gymnastics skill named after the first gymnast to successfully perform it in a major international competition.
*   **Code of Points:** The official scoring system used by the International Gymnastics Federation (FIG) to judge gymnastics competitions.
*   **All-Around:** A competition where gymnasts perform on all four apparatus (for women), and the winner is determined by the combined score.
*   **Yurchenko:** A type of vault where the gymnast performs a round-off onto the springboard and a back handspring onto the vaulting table.
*   **Presidential Medal of Freedom:** The highest civilian honor in the United States, awarded for exceptional contributions to national interests, world peace, or cultural endeavors.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Mental Health in Elite Sports:** Biles's withdrawal in Tokyo ignited a debate about the pressure on elite athletes and the legitimacy of prioritizing mental health over competition. While widely praised for starting a conversation, she also faced criticism from some who viewed it as quitting.
*   **Systemic Abuse in Gymnastics:** Biles's testimony against Larry Nassar and USA Gymnastics highlighted systemic failures that enabled abuse. The long-term impact on the sport's governance and athlete safety remains an ongoing issue.
*   **Legacy and "GOAT" Status:** While her medal count and skill difficulty are objectively the highest, debates continue about comparing athletes across different eras and scoring systems. Her decision to compete in 2024 after the Tokyo withdrawals added a new layer to discussions about her career narrative.

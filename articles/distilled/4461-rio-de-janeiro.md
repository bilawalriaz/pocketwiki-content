# Rio de Janeiro

## Overview
Rio de Janeiro is Brazil's second-largest city and a former national capital, renowned for its dramatic natural setting, vibrant culture, and complex social history. It served as the capital of the Portuguese Empire, the Empire of Brazil, and the Republic of Brazil until 1960, making it a unique case of a colonizing country's capital shifting to a colony. Today, it is a major economic, cultural, and tourist hub, though it grapples with significant social inequality and environmental challenges.

- **2 Oct 2009** — International Olympic Committee selects Rio to host the 2016 Summer Olympics.
- **2016** — Rio hosts the Summer Olympics and Paralympics.

## Body

### Historical Development
The region was inhabited by Tupi, Puri, Botocudo, and Maxakalí peoples before European contact. The Portuguese founded the city in 1565 after expelling French colonists from Guanabara Bay. Its importance grew in the late 17th century as a port for gold and diamonds from Minas Gerais, leading to the transfer of the colonial capital from Salvador in 1763. In 1808, the Portuguese royal court's flight to Rio made it the only European capital outside Europe, spurring major cultural and educational development. Rio remained the capital through Brazilian independence (1822), the Empire, and the early Republic until the planned city of Brasília was inaugurated in 1960. The former Federal District became the state of Guanabara, which was later merged with the state of Rio de Janeiro in 1975.

### Economy and Infrastructure
Rio de Janeiro has the second-largest municipal GDP in Brazil. Its economy is dominated by the services sector (65.5%), followed by commerce and industry. The city is the headquarters for major corporations in oil (Petrobras), mining (Vale), and media (Grupo Globo). Historically, its role as the federal capital attracted state-owned companies and federal agencies, many of which remain. The Port of Rio de Janeiro is a key cargo and cruise hub. The city's transportation network includes a metro, suburban rail, light rail, BRT corridors, and extensive bus and ferry systems, though high fares and disjointed systems contribute to inequality. It is served by Galeão International Airport and Santos Dumont Airport.

### Society and Culture
Rio's population is ethnically diverse, with significant White, Mixed, and Black communities, shaped by indigenous, Portuguese, African, and later European and Asian immigration. The city is a cultural icon, famous for Carnival, samba, bossa nova, and landmarks like Christ the Redeemer and Sugarloaf Mountain. It hosted the 2016 Olympics and Paralympics, the 2014 FIFA World Cup final, and numerous other global events. Social issues are pronounced, with stark segregation between wealthy areas and favelas (slums), where over 1.3 million people live. State-sanctioned violence is a critical concern, with high rates of police killings.

### Geography and Environment
Founded at the entrance to Guanabara Bay, Rio's landscape is defined by mountains, forests, and beaches. It contains significant urban green spaces, including the Tijuca National Park, the world's first urban forest. The climate is tropical wet and dry, with hot, stormy summers and warm, mild winters. The city faces serious environmental pollution, particularly in Guanabara and Sepetiba Bays, from untreated sewage and industrial waste. Air pollution from vehicles also exceeds WHO recommendations.

## Terms
- **Favela:** An informal settlement or slum, often on steep hillsides, characterized by poor housing and lack of formal services.
- **Carioca:** A demonym for a resident of Rio de Janeiro.
- **Bossa Nova:** A style of Brazilian music developed in Rio in the late 1950s, blending samba and jazz.
- **Samba:** A Brazilian musical genre and dance style with African roots, central to Carnival.
- **Carnival:** An annual pre-Lenten festival in Rio featuring parades, music, and street parties, especially samba school competitions.
- **BRT (Bus Rapid Transit):** A high-capacity bus-based public transport system with dedicated lanes.
- **Guanabara Bay:** The large ocean bay at the mouth of which Rio de Janeiro was founded.
- **Tenentism:** A 1920s Brazilian political movement of young army officers ("tenentes") that challenged the Old Republic, culminating in the 1930 Revolution.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The city's crime rate is often perceived as high, but the source notes it has a lower incidence than most Brazilian state capitals, highlighting a discrepancy between perception and data.
- The environmental health of Guanabara Bay remains a major concern, with ongoing pollution from sewage and industry, despite decontamination efforts.
- The social and economic impacts of hosting mega-events like the Olympics are subjects of ongoing analysis, particularly regarding their legacy for infrastructure and inequality.

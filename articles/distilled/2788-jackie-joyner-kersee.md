# Jackie Joyner-Kersee

## Overview
Jacqueline Joyner-Kersee is a retired American track and field athlete, widely regarded as one of the greatest female athletes of all time. She excelled in the heptathlon (a seven-event track and field competition) and the long jump, winning multiple Olympic and World Championship medals and setting enduring world records. Her career spanned the 1980s and 1990s, and she later became a prominent philanthropist and advocate for social causes.

## Timeline
- **March 3, 1962** — Born in East St. Louis, Illinois.
- **1980** — Qualifies for the long jump finals at the U.S. Olympic Trials as a high school senior.
- **1984** — Wins silver medal in heptathlon at the Los Angeles Olympics.
- **1986** — Becomes the first woman to score over 7,000 points in a heptathlon at the Goodwill Games.
- **1988** — Sets the heptathlon world record (7,291 points) and wins gold in both heptathlon and long jump at the Seoul Olympics.
- **1991** — Wins long jump at World Championships but withdraws from heptathlon due to injury.
- **1992** — Wins gold in heptathlon and bronze in long jump at the Barcelona Olympics.
- **1996** — Wins bronze in long jump at the Atlanta Olympics after withdrawing from heptathlon due to injury.
- **2000** — Fails to qualify for the Olympic team at the U.S. Trials.

## Body

### Early Life and College Career
Born into poverty in East St. Louis, Joyner-Kersee discovered running at age 9. She was inspired to pursue multi-disciplinary events after seeing a film about Babe Didrikson Zaharias. At UCLA, she was a standout in both track and field and basketball, earning a bachelor's degree in history in 1986. Her coach, Bob Kersee, helped her through the death of her mother; they later married in 1986.

### Olympic and World Championship Success
Joyner-Kersee's competitive peak began with a silver medal in heptathlon at the 1984 Los Angeles Olympics. Her defining moment came at the 1988 Seoul Olympics, where she won gold in both the heptathlon and long jump. In the heptathlon, she set a world record of 7,291 points that still stands. She continued to win medals at the 1992 Barcelona Olympics (heptathlon gold, long jump bronze) and the 1996 Atlanta Olympics (long jump bronze), despite battling injuries and severe asthma throughout her career.

### Post-Athletic Career and Legacy
After retiring from competition, Joyner-Kersee focused on philanthropy, founding the Jackie Joyner-Kersee Foundation to support youth in her hometown. She co-founded Athletes for Hope and collaborated on initiatives to provide internet access to low-income communities. She holds the world record in heptathlon and the top six all-time results in the event. Her long jump personal best of 7.49 m is second on the all-time list.

## Terms
- **Heptathlon:** A combined-event contest in women's track and field consisting of seven events over two days.
- **Long jump:** A track and field event where an athlete attempts to jump as far as possible from a running start.
- **World record:** The best performance ever officially ratified in a specific sport or event.
- **Olympic Trials:** The qualifying competitions held to select athletes for an Olympic team.
- **Broderick Award (Honda Sports Award):** An annual award given to the top female collegiate athlete in a given sport.
- **James E. Sullivan Award:** An annual award given by the AAU to the top amateur athlete in the United States.
- **Athletes for Hope:** A charitable organization co-founded by Joyner-Kersee to connect athletes with charitable causes.

## Debates and Open Questions
Joyner-Kersee's career was marred by allegations of performance-enhancing drug (PED) use. Brazilian runner Joaquim Cruz made accusations in 1988, and coach Darrell Robinson accused her husband in 1989. Doping insider Victor Conte later claimed he witnessed an official notify Bob Kersee of a positive test at the 1988 Seoul Games. Joyner-Kersee has consistently maintained that she competed without PEDs. The source presents these as allegations and does not confirm them.

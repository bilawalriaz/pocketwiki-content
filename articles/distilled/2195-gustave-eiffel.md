# Gustave Eiffel

## Overview
Alexandre Gustave Eiffel (1832–1923) was a French civil engineer whose innovative iron and steel structures, most famously the Eiffel Tower, defined the architectural possibilities of the Industrial Revolution. His career bridged practical engineering and scientific research, leaving a legacy in both landmark construction and the foundational science of aerodynamics.

## Timeline
- **15 Dec 1832** — Born Alexandre Bonickhausen dit Eiffel in France.
- **1855** — Graduates from École Centrale des Arts et Manufactures.
- **1861** — Completes first major project: railway bridge over the Garonne at Bordeaux.
- **1866** — Establishes his own engineering workshops in Levallois-Perret.
- **1868** — Forms partnership with Théophile Seyrig, creating Eiffel et Cie.
- **1877** — Opens the Maria Pia Bridge over the Douro in Portugal.
- **1881** — Begins work on the internal structure for the Statue of Liberty.
- **1887** — Signs contract for the Eiffel Tower; construction begins.
- **1889** — Eiffel Tower completed for the Paris Exposition Universelle.
- **1893** — Convicted (later acquitted) in the Panama Canal scandal; retires from engineering business.
- **1909** — Opens his first wind tunnel at the foot of the Eiffel Tower.
- **27 Dec 1923** — Dies in Paris.

## Body

### Early Career and Bridge Engineering
After graduating from the prestigious École Centrale, Eiffel gained early experience in railway engineering. His breakthrough was managing the construction of a railway bridge over the Garonne at Bordeaux (completed 1861), which used innovative compressed-air caissons. In 1866, he founded his own company, which became renowned for complex iron structures. His work on the Maria Pia Bridge over the Douro in Portugal (1877) and the Garabit Viaduct in France (1884) established his reputation for designing long-span bridges that combined engineering efficiency with aesthetic form.

### The Statue of Liberty and the Eiffel Tower
In 1881, Eiffel was hired by Auguste Bartholdi to engineer the internal support structure for the Statue of Liberty, devising a four-legged pylon to support the copper skin. His most famous work, the Eiffel Tower, was designed by his engineers Maurice Koechlin and Émile Nouguier, with architectural details by Stephen Sauvestre, for the 1889 Paris Exposition. Despite fierce opposition from a "Committee of Three Hundred" artists and intellectuals who called it a "giddy, ridiculous tower," the project proceeded. The tower was an engineering marvel, requiring 18,038 parts and extreme precision in manufacturing. It was completed in just over two years and became an enduring symbol of modern engineering.

### The Panama Scandal and Retirement
In 1887, Eiffel became involved in designing locks for the French Panama Canal project. When the French Panama Canal Company collapsed in 1888 amid financial scandal, Eiffel was implicated. He was initially convicted of misuse of funds in 1893 but was acquitted on appeal. The scandal severely damaged his reputation, leading him to resign from his company and retire from large-scale construction.

### Later Scientific Work
After retiring from engineering, Eiffel focused on meteorology and aerodynamics, fields inspired by his experience with wind forces on structures. He conducted early air resistance experiments by dropping objects from the Eiffel Tower and built a wind tunnel at its base in 1909. His research established that air resistance is proportional to the square of airspeed and clarified that lift on an airfoil is caused by reduced pressure above the wing. This work provided crucial data for early aviation pioneers and earned him the Samuel P. Langley Medal in 1913.

## Terms
- **Compressed-air caissons**: Watertight chambers used in underwater construction, pressurized to keep water out while workers excavate foundations.
- **Girder**: A large iron or steel beam used as a main horizontal support in a structure.
- **Viaduct**: A long bridge-like structure, typically a series of arches or piers, carrying a road or railway across a valley or low ground.
- **Cantilever**: A projecting beam or girder fixed at only one end, used in bridge and tower construction.
- **Airfoil**: A surface, such as an aircraft wing, designed to provide lift when air flows over it.
- **Modulus of elasticity**: A measure of a material's stiffness, defining its resistance to being deformed elastically when a stress is applied.

## Debates and Open Questions
The primary controversy during Eiffel's lifetime was the intense artistic and public opposition to the Eiffel Tower, which critics viewed as an industrial blight on Paris's historic skyline. His legacy also involves ongoing scholarly debate about the attribution of designs, such as the tower, which originated with his engineers Koechlin and Nouguier before his involvement. The Panama Canal scandal remains a point of historical discussion regarding the extent of his culpability versus that of the project's directors.

# Omsk

## Overview
Omsk is a major city in southwestern Siberia, Russia, and the administrative center of Omsk Oblast. It is a critical transport hub on the Trans-Siberian Railway and the Irtysh River, with a population of over one million. Historically, it served as a key administrative and military center for Imperial Russia and was briefly the capital of the anti-Bolshevik White movement during the Russian Civil War.

## Body

### Imperial Foundation and Growth
The Omsk fortress was established in 1716 to secure a trade route to China along the Irtysh River. Its construction was ordered by Siberian Governor Prince M.P. Gagarin. The fortress was rebuilt several times due to design flaws. Omsk gained town status in 1782 and became the administrative capital of Western Siberia in 1822 and later of the Steppes region in 1882. Its military importance waned as the frontier receded, leading to its role as a center for Siberian exile; writer Fyodor Dostoyevsky served a sentence there from 1850 to 1854. The construction of the Trans-Siberian Railway in the 1890s revitalized the city, making it a major logistics and trade hub.

### Civil War and Soviet Era
After the October Revolution, Omsk was seized by anti-Bolshevik White forces. In 1918, it was proclaimed the capital of the "Provisional All-Russian Government" under Admiral Kolchak, which safeguarded the former empire's gold reserves. The Red Army captured the city in 1919. In the Soviet period, administrative functions were transferred to Novosibirsk, stunting Omsk's growth. World War II provided a new impetus, as Omsk's remote location and infrastructure made it a haven for evacuated industry. It became a leader in Soviet military production, including tank design and manufacturing. In the 1950s, a large oil refinery complex was built, further expanding the city.

### Post-Soviet Period
Following the Soviet Union's dissolution, Omsk faced economic instability and political conflict between city and regional authorities. This "information war" between governors and mayors defined local politics for over a decade. Despite this, infrastructure improvements occurred, including park construction and historic center renovation. The city's economy is now dominated by the Gazprom Neft oil refinery, its largest employer. A metro system has been under construction since the late 1980s, with a bridge over the Irtysh opened for cars but the metro line still incomplete.

## Terms
- **Katorga**: A system of penal servitude in the Russian Empire, often involving exile to Siberia.
- **Trans-Siberian Railway**: A network of railways connecting Moscow with the Russian Far East, crucial for Siberian development.
- **White movement**: The loose coalition of anti-Bolshevik forces during the Russian Civil War (1917-1922).
- **Humid continental climate (Köppen Dfb)**: A climate with large seasonal temperature differences, warm to hot summers, and cold winters.
- **City of oblast significance**: An administrative unit in Russia with a status equal to that of districts, directly subordinate to the oblast.
- **Omsk Urban Okrug**: The municipal division of Omsk, a type of urban municipality in Russia.
- **Marshrutka**: A shared taxi service, common in post-Soviet states, operating on fixed routes.

## Debates and Open Questions
The source highlights a persistent and defining political conflict in Omsk: the long-standing "information war" and power struggle between the city's mayors and the governor of Omsk Oblast. This feud, which involved media battles and public confrontations, is presented as a major obstacle to smooth governance and development, though its precise origins and ongoing dynamics are described as complex and sometimes reduced to "political games."

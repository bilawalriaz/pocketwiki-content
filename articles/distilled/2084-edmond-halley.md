# Edmond Halley

## Overview
Edmond Halley (1656–1742) was a foundational English astronomer, mathematician, and physicist whose work bridged theoretical science and practical application. As the second Astronomer Royal, he made seminal contributions to astronomy, including predicting the return of a comet, and pioneered work in fields as diverse as demography, geomagnetism, and actuarial science. His career exemplifies the Enlightenment shift towards empirical investigation and the use of mathematics to explain natural phenomena.

## Timeline
- **1656** — Born in Haggerston, Middlesex.
- **1676–77** — Catalogues southern stars from Saint Helena; observes a transit of Mercury.
- **1682** — Observes the comet later named for him.
- **1687** — Helps fund the publication of Isaac Newton's *Principia Mathematica*.
- **1693** — Publishes life annuities analysis, founding actuarial science.
- **1698–1700** — Commands scientific voyages to study terrestrial magnetism.
- **1705** — Predicts the return of Halley's Comet in 1758.
- **1718** — Discovers the proper motion of "fixed" stars.
- **1720** — Becomes Astronomer Royal.
- **1742** — Dies in Greenwich, aged 85.

## Body

### Early Life and Education
Halley showed an early aptitude for mathematics and astronomy. He studied at St Paul's School and entered The Queen's College, Oxford, in 1673. While an undergraduate, he published papers on the Solar System and corresponded with John Flamsteed, the first Astronomer Royal, about errors in planetary tables. This association led to his first publication and his expedition to Saint Helena.

### Astronomical Work and the *Principia*
In 1676, Halley sailed to Saint Helena to catalogue the southern celestial hemisphere, creating the first telescopic catalogue of southern stars. He observed a transit of Mercury and realised that a transit of Venus could be used to calculate astronomical distances. Upon his return, he was elected a Fellow of the Royal Society. In 1684, he visited Isaac Newton to discuss planetary orbits, discovering Newton had already solved the problem. Halley recognised the importance of Newton's work and arranged and funded its publication as *Philosophiæ Naturalis Principia Mathematica* in 1687. Using Newton's law of universal gravitation, Halley later computed the orbit of the comet observed in 1682, predicting its return in 1758—a prediction confirmed after his death, leading to the comet being named in his honour.

### Scientific Voyages and Diverse Contributions
Beginning in 1698, Halley commanded naval voyages to investigate terrestrial magnetism, producing the first chart of magnetic variation (isogonic lines). His work on trade winds and monsoons (1686) identified solar heating as their cause. In 1691, he built a diving bell and demonstrated its use. He also proposed a hollow Earth model to explain magnetic anomalies. In 1693, his analysis of mortality data from Breslau created a life table, foundational for actuarial science and demography. In 1694, he presented a scientific hypothesis for the biblical Flood involving a comet.

### Later Career and Academic Life
In 1703, Halley became Savilian Professor of Geometry at Oxford. He published a translation of Apollonius's *Conics* and, in 1705, his *Synopsis of the Astronomy of Comets*, which predicted the comet's return. In 1718, he discovered the proper motion of stars by comparing his measurements with ancient records. He succeeded Flamsteed as Astronomer Royal in 1720, a post he held until his death. In 1720, he and William Stukeley made an early, though inaccurate, attempt to scientifically date Stonehenge.

## Terms
- **Astronomer Royal**: A senior post in the Royal Observatory, Greenwich, responsible for astronomical observation and timekeeping.
- **Transit**: The passage of a smaller celestial body (like Venus or Mercury) across the face of a larger one (the Sun).
- **Proper motion**: The apparent angular motion of a star across the sky relative to distant background stars.
- **Isogonic lines**: Lines on a map connecting points of equal magnetic declination (variation of the compass).
- **Actuarial science**: The discipline that uses mathematics and statistics to assess risk in insurance and finance.
- **Life table**: A statistical model showing the probability of death at each age for a given population.
- **Hollow Earth**: A historical hypothesis proposing that the Earth consists of concentric spherical shells.
- **Barotrauma**: Physical damage to body tissues caused by a difference in pressure between an air space inside or beside the body and the surrounding fluid.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Spelling and Pronunciation of Name**: Historical records show varied spellings (Hailey, Hayley, etc.), and modern pronunciation is debated, with "HAL-ee" common for the comet and "HAY-lee" for the surname.
- **Priority for Venus Transit Method**: While Halley is credited with proposing the method to measure the Sun's distance using a Venus transit, the idea was previously described by James Gregory, a fact Halley did not acknowledge.
- **Accuracy of Early Work**: His calculations for the orbit of Kirch's Comet (1680–81) were inaccurate, estimating a 575-year period versus the actual ~10,000 years, though his later work on his own comet was precise.
- **Religious Controversy**: His scientific explanations for biblical events (like the Flood) and his questioning of the Earth's age as per the Bible drew opposition from church authorities, affecting his career prospects.

# Harun al-Rashid

## Overview
Hārūn al-Rashīd (c. 763/766–809) was the fifth Abbasid caliph, whose reign (786–809) is traditionally seen as the zenith of the Abbasid Empire and the beginning of the Islamic Golden Age. He presided over a flourishing Baghdad, established legendary diplomatic ties with Charlemagne, and his court became the setting for many tales in *One Thousand and One Nights*. His death triggered a succession war between his sons, leading to the caliphate's gradual political disintegration.

## Timeline
- **c. 763/766** — Born in Rey, Abbasid Caliphate.
- **780, 782** — Leads nominal campaigns against the Byzantine Empire.
- **786** — Becomes caliph after his brother al-Hadi's death.
- **789** — Death of his influential mother, al-Khayzuran.
- **796** — Moves the court and government to Raqqa.
- **799** — Receives a Frankish mission from Charlemagne.
- **802** — Sends an elaborate embassy and gifts to Charlemagne.
- **802–803** — Fierce campaigns against Byzantine Emperor Nikephoros I.
- **803** — Falls from favor with the Barmakid family; they are imprisoned.
- **809** — Dies in Tus while marching to suppress a revolt in Khorasan.

## Body

### Rise to Power and Early Reign
Hārūn was the son of Caliph al-Mahdi and the influential al-Khayzuran. His military successes against the Byzantines earned him the epithet "al-Rashīd" (the Rightly-Guided) and the position of crown prince. Upon his brother al-Hadi's sudden death in 786, al-Khayzuran orchestrated Hārūn's swift accession. He began his reign by releasing political prisoners and appointing capable ministers, notably the Barmakid Yahya ibn Khalid as vizier, which stabilized the empire.

### Administration and Court
Hārūn's reign saw Baghdad flourish as a world center of knowledge and trade. In 796, he relocated his court to Raqqa, likely for strategic reasons including proximity to the Byzantine frontier and control over Syria. His administration maintained order, enforced market regulations, and collected substantial tribute. The Barmakid family, of Iranian origin, managed the empire's administration for seventeen years until their dramatic fall from grace around 803, possibly due to overreach and disrespect toward the caliph.

### Diplomacy and Foreign Policy
Hārūn maintained a famous diplomatic exchange with Charlemagne, driven by mutual interest in containing the Umayyad emirate of Córdoba and shared enmity toward the Byzantines. He sent unprecedented gifts, including an elephant and a water clock. Against the Byzantines, he conducted punitive campaigns, forcing Emperor Nikephoros I into a humiliating treaty after the latter refused to pay tribute. He also established an alliance with the Chinese Tang dynasty against the Tibetans.

### Succession and Legacy
Hārūn divided the succession between his sons: al-Amin (son of the Arab Zubaida) as primary heir, and al-Ma'mun (son of a Persian concubine) as secondary heir. This division created fatal tensions. He died in 809 while marching to suppress a revolt in Khorasan, leading to a civil war (809–813) between his sons. Al-Amin succeeded him but was later overthrown by al-Ma'mun. All subsequent Abbasid caliphs were his descendants.

## Terms
- **Caliph**: The successor to the Prophet Muhammad as the political and religious leader of the Muslim community.
- **Abbasid**: The third caliphate (750–1258), succeeding the Umayyads, ruling from Baghdad and presiding over the Islamic Golden Age.
- **Barmakids**: An influential Iranian family of former Buddhist priests who served as powerful viziers (chief ministers) under early Abbasid caliphs.
- **Vizier**: The chief minister or executive officer of an Islamic state, holding full administrative powers.
- **Laqab**: An honorific epithet or title, such as "al-Rashīd" (the Rightly-Guided).
- **Bay'ah**: The oath of allegiance sworn to a caliph or ruler.
- **Qadi**: A judge in an Islamic court system.
- **Dirham**: A silver coin used as standard currency in the Abbasid Caliphate.
- **Dinar**: A gold coin, often the Byzantine solidus, used in trade.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The exact date of Hārūn's birth is debated, with sources giving dates from 763 to 766.
- The reasons for the dramatic fall of the Barmakid family in 803 are varied; historical accounts cite specific incidents like entering the caliph's presence without permission, but the underlying cause may have been their growing power and perceived disrespect.
- The precise cause of his brother al-Hadi's death is uncertain, with accounts suggesting either an abdominal ulcer or assassination possibly prompted by his mother, al-Khayzuran.
- Scholars debate whether Hārūn's division of the succession between his sons was a deliberate policy error that directly caused the subsequent civil war and the caliphate's political disintegration.

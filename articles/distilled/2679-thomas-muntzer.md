# Thomas Müntzer

## Overview
Thomas Müntzer (c. 1489–1525) was a radical German reformer and theologian of the early Reformation. He is a pivotal figure because his theology, which emphasized direct spiritual revelation and an imminent apocalypse, led him to break with Martin Luther and become a leader of the 1525 German Peasants' War, making him a key example of the link between religious radicalism and social revolution.

## Timeline
- **c. 1489** — Born in Stolberg, Germany.
- **1514** — Becomes a Catholic priest in Brunswick; begins questioning Church practices.
- **1517** — Meets Martin Luther in Wittenberg.
- **1520** — Preaches in Zwickau, aligning with radical weavers and mystics; dismissed.
- **1521** — Expelled from Prague after writing the *Prague Manifesto*.
- **1523** — Becomes preacher in Allstedt; delivers the radical *Sermon Before the Princes*.
- **1524** — Flees to Mühlhausen; publishes anti-Lutheran pamphlets.
- **Early 1525** — Returns to Mühlhausen; forms the "Eternal League of God" militia.
- **May 1525** — Leads rebel forces at the Battle of Frankenhausen; defeated and captured.
- **27 May 1525** — Executed in Mühlhausen after torture and confession.

## Body

### Early Life and Reformation Contacts
Müntzer received a lengthy education, studying at the Universities of Leipzig and Frankfurt an der Oder. He became a priest in 1514 and soon began criticizing Catholic practices like the sale of indulgences. By 1517, he was in Wittenberg, engaging with Luther's ideas and other reformers. He studied mystics like Johannes Tauler and developed a theology focused on inner spiritual experience over scripture alone, which set him on a path diverging from Luther's more institutional approach.

### Break with Luther and Radical Ministry
Müntzer's break with Luther became public during his time in Zwickau (1520) and Allstedt (1523). In Zwickau, he allied with radical weavers who believed in revelation through dreams. In Allstedt, his popular German-language services and apocalyptic preaching alarmed both Luther and the authorities. His *Sermon Before the Princes* (1524) warned rulers to support the Reformation or face God's wrath. Luther responded by calling for his banishment, cementing their public rift.

### Leadership in the Peasants' War
After fleeing to Mühlhausen in 1524, Müntzer allied with radical preacher Heinrich Pfeiffer. He helped organize an armed militia, the "Eternal League of God," to fight feudal lords. Viewing the 1525 peasant uprising as a divine apocalyptic event, he became a military leader. His forces were decisively defeated at the Battle of Frankenhausen. He was captured, tortured, and executed, becoming a martyr for his cause.

### Theology and Legacy
Müntzer's theology was the engine of his revolutionary actions. He taught that true faith came from personal suffering and direct spiritual revelation (through dreams and visions), not from the Bible alone. He believed the "Elect" (true believers) were tasked with actively helping God usher in a new era by overthrowing corrupt authorities. His famous statement "omnia sunt communia" ("all things are to be held in common") from his confession has been interpreted as an early communist ideal, though scholars debate its context. His legacy influenced later Anabaptist movements and was claimed by 19th-century revolutionaries like Friedrich Engels as a precursor to modern social revolution.

## Terms
*   **Reformation:** The 16th-century religious movement to reform the Catholic Church, which led to Protestantism.
*   **Spiritualism:** A belief that divine truth is revealed directly to the individual through the Holy Spirit, often via dreams or visions, rather than through scripture or church hierarchy.
*   **Apocalypticism:** The belief in an imminent end to the world and a final divine judgment, often with the expectation of a transformative new age.
*   **The Elect:** In Müntzer's theology, the true, spiritually enlightened believers chosen by God to carry out His will.
*   **Utraquism:** A Hussite practice of administering communion in "both kinds" (bread and wine) to the laity, a reform Müntzer supported.
*   **Eternal League of God:** The armed militia Müntzer organized in Mühlhausen in 1525 to fight feudal authorities.
*   **German Peasants' War:** A widespread popular revolt in 1524–1525 by German peasants and plebeians against feudal oppression, which Müntzer helped lead in central Germany.
*   **Omnia sunt communia:** Latin for "all things are to be held in common," a phrase from Müntzer's confession that has been interpreted as a call for communal ownership.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **The "Communist" Interpretation:** Scholars debate whether Müntzer's statement "omnia sunt communia" represents a genuine program for economic communism or was a coerced confession reflecting his captors' fears. Most modern analyses see his primary goal as political revolution, not wealth redistribution.
*   **Theology vs. Social Revolution:** Historians agree his actions were driven by theology, but the precise point at which his apocalyptic beliefs necessitated armed social revolution is debated. Was it inevitable, or did it coincide with the peasants' uprising by circumstance?
*   **Relationship with Luther:** The nature of their early relationship and the exact timing of their definitive break are complex. Müntzer initially saw himself as a fellow reformer with Luther before rejecting his compromises with princely authority.

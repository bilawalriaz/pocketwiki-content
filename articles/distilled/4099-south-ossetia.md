# South Ossetia

## Overview
South Ossetia is a partially recognised state in the South Caucasus, formally the State of Alania since 2017. Its status is central to the Georgian–Ossetian conflict and Georgia–Russia relations. Internationally, it is regarded by Georgia and most UN members as sovereign Georgian territory under Russian occupation. Four UN members—Russia, Venezuela, Nicaragua, and Syria—recognise its independence.

## Body

### History and Conflict
The Ossetians descend from the Alans, a nomadic Iranian tribe. After the 13th-century Mongol invasions, Alans migrated south across the Caucasus into the Kingdom of Georgia. Conflict with Georgians began in the 13th century, with further migration in the 17th century. The territory was annexed by the Russian Empire in 1801.

Following the Russian Revolution, the area became part of the Democratic Republic of Georgia. Conflict erupted in 1918 between landless Ossetian peasants and the Georgian government, leading to uprisings in 1919–1920. After the Red Army's 1921 invasion of Georgia, the Soviet Georgian government created the South Ossetian Autonomous Oblast in 1922 under pressure from the Caucasian Bureau of the Communist Party.

Tensions rose with nationalism in 1989. The South Ossetian council declared sovereignty in 1990, which the Georgian parliament revoked, abolishing autonomy. This led to the 1991–1992 South Ossetia War, marked by atrocities and displacing about 100,000 ethnic Ossetians and 23,000 ethnic Georgians. A 1992 ceasefire left the conflict frozen.

Tensions escalated in 2004 after President Mikheil Saakashvili moved to restore territorial integrity. Georgia closed the Ergneti black market, a smuggling hub, breaking South Ossetian trust and sparking violence. The conflict culminated in the 2008 Russo-Georgian War. Following a Georgian military operation into Tskhinvali on 8 August, Russia launched a full-scale invasion. A ceasefire was negotiated on 12 August, and Russia recognised South Ossetia's independence on 26 August. The war displaced 192,000 people and was followed by ethnic cleansing against Georgians.

### Status and Politics
South Ossetia is a partially recognised state. Georgia and most of the international community, including the EU, NATO, and OSCE, do not recognise its independence and consider it occupied by Russia. Georgia refers to it as the Tskhinvali region. It is a post-Soviet "frozen conflict" zone.

The de facto government is a presidential republic. Since 2008, it has expressed a consistent intention to join Russia. The 2015 "alliance and integration" treaty included provisions to incorporate its military into Russia's armed forces. A 2017 referendum gave equal legal status to the names "Republic of South Ossetia" and "State of Alania." In 2022, President Anatoly Bibilov announced plans for an annexation referendum, but his successor, Alan Gagloev, suspended the process.

### Economy and Demographics
South Ossetia's economy is primarily agricultural and critically dependent on Russian financial assistance, which constituted 83% of its budget by 2021. The 2008 war devastated the economy, halting exports to Georgia and ending its role as a trade conduit. The Roki Tunnel, the only direct route through the Caucasus Mountains between Russia and South Ossetia, is a vital strategic asset.

Before the 1990s conflict, the population was roughly two-thirds Ossetian and 25–30% Georgian. The 1991–1992 war caused mass displacement. The 2015 census by South Ossetian authorities reported 53,532 people, including 89.9% Ossetians and 7.4% Georgians, though Georgian authorities question these figures. The 2022 population estimate was 56,520. Eastern Orthodoxy is the major religion.

## Terms
- **Frozen conflict**: A situation where active military conflict has ended but no comprehensive peace treaty has been reached.
- **Autonomous Oblast**: A Soviet administrative division created for ethnic minorities.
- **Russo-Georgian War**: The five-day 2008 war between Georgia and Russia, with Ossetian and Abkhaz forces, over South Ossetia and Abkhazia.
- **Ethnic cleansing**: The systematic forced removal of an ethnic or religious group from a territory.
- **Partially recognised state**: A polity that meets statehood criteria but is not recognised by all or most UN members.
- **Roki Tunnel**: The only direct road tunnel through the Greater Caucasus, connecting North Ossetia (Russia) to South Ossetia.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Historical presence**: Georgian historiography holds mass Ossetian migration began in the 17th century, while some Ossetian historians claim ancient roots. This dispute is a cause of conflict.
- **Origin of the 2008 war**: An EU fact-finding mission concluded "open hostilities began with a large-scale Georgian military operation," but noted it followed months of mutual provocations. Russia and Georgia blame each other.
- **Population figures**: The accuracy of the 2015 South Ossetian census is questioned by Georgian authorities, with independent estimates suggesting a lower population.
- **Annexation by Russia**: South Ossetian authorities have repeatedly expressed a desire to join Russia, but the process has been postponed multiple times, and political will remains debated.

# Balearic Islands

## Overview
The Balearic Islands are a Spanish archipelago in the western Mediterranean Sea, forming an autonomous community and province. They are a major tourist destination known for their Mediterranean climate, distinct culture, and a history shaped by successive conquests and rule by various Mediterranean powers.

## Timeline
- **c. 2500–2300 BC** — Earliest known habitation by people associated with the Bell Beaker culture.
- **123 BC** — Conquered by Rome under Q. Caecilius Metellus Balearicus.
- **461–468 AD** — Conquered by the Vandals.
- **533/534 AD** — Reclaimed for the Roman (Byzantine) Empire by Belisarius.
- **707** — Submitted to an Umayyad fleet, beginning a period of nominal Islamic rule.
- **903** — Captured by the Emirate of Córdoba.
- **1015** — Seized by the Taifa of Dénia; an independent Taifa of Mallorca is established in 1050.
- **1115** — Sacked by a Pisan-led crusade.
- **1229–1235** — Conquered by King James I of Aragon (Reconquista).
- **1713** — Menorca ceded to Great Britain by the Treaty of Utrecht.
- **1802** — Menorca returned to Spain by the Treaty of Amiens.
- **1983** — Statute of Autonomy granted to the Balearic Islands.

## Body

### Geography and Climate
The archipelago consists of two main groups: the Gymnesian Islands (Mallorca and Menorca) and the Pityusic Islands (Ibiza and Formentera). The province has Spain's longest coastline at 1,428 km. The islands lie on a raised platform called the Balearic Promontory. They have a predominantly hot-summer Mediterranean climate, with some semi-arid zones on Ibiza and Formentera.

### Ancient History and Etymology
The earliest inhabitants were associated with the Bell Beaker culture. Classical sources give two names: *Gymnesiai* (Greek) and *Baleares* (Latin). Theories for *Gymnesiai* include the inhabitants' nudity or their light equipment. The name *Baleares* is linked to their skill as slingers (from Greek *ballein*, "to launch"), though Strabo suggests a Phoenician origin. The islanders were famed mercenaries, using slings with great accuracy from childhood. They were conquered by Rome in 123 BC and later became a separate province, *Hispania Balearica*.

### Medieval Period
After Roman rule, the islands were conquered by Vandals (c. 461–468) and later reclaimed by the Byzantine Empire (533/534). From 707, they were under nominal Islamic suzerainty, becoming a prosperous, de facto independent haven. A Pisan-led crusade sacked Palma in 1115, ending their sea power. The islands were then taken by the Almoravids and later the Almohads. King James I of Aragon conquered them between 1229 and 1235, incorporating them into the Crown of Aragon. A separate Kingdom of Mallorca was created in 1276 but was reabsorbed by Aragon in 1349.

### Early Modern and Modern History
The islands became part of the unified Spanish monarchy after 1469. They were frequently raided by Ottoman and Barbary pirates. Menorca was ceded to Britain in 1713, returned to Spain in 1802, and was occupied by France during the Seven Years' War. During the Spanish Civil War (1936–1939), Menorca and Formentera remained loyal to the Republic, while Mallorca and Ibiza supported the Nationalists. The islands received a Statute of Autonomy in 1983, designating them a "nationality" of Spain in 2007.

### Culture and Demographics
The official languages are Catalan (with local dialects like Mallorquí) and Spanish. The cuisine features dishes like *sobrassada* and Menorcan lobster stew (*caldereta*). The population was 1,183,415 in 2021, with significant foreign communities, notably Moroccans, Germans, and Italians. Roman Catholicism is the predominant religion.

### Administration and Economy
The autonomous community is administered through four insular councils, one for each major island. The economy is heavily reliant on tourism. The GDP was €32.5 billion in 2018, with a per capita GDP close to the EU average.

## Terms
- **Gymnesian Islands:** The eastern group of the Balearics, comprising Mallorca and Menorca.
- **Pityusic Islands:** The western group of the Balearics, comprising Ibiza and Formentera.
- **Talayot:** A type of megalithic structure built by the ancient inhabitants of the Gymnesian Islands.
- **Slinger:** A soldier who fights with a sling; the Balearic islanders were renowned mercenaries for this skill.
- **Taifa:** An independent Muslim-ruled principality in the Iberian Peninsula, formed after the collapse of the Caliphate of Córdoba.
- **Statute of Autonomy:** A legal document granting self-governing powers to an autonomous community of Spain.
- **Consell Insular:** The island-level governing council in the Balearic administration.
- **Sobrassada:** A cured pork sausage from the Balearic Islands.
- **Caldereta:** A traditional Menorcan lobster stew.

## Debates and open questions
The etymology of the ancient names *Gymnesiai* and *Baleares* is debated, with classical sources offering different explanations (e.g., nudity vs. light equipment for *Gymnesiai*; Greek vs. Phoenician origin for *Baleares*).

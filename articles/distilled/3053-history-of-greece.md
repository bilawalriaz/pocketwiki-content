# History of Greece

## Overview
The history of Greece spans from prehistoric habitation to the modern nation-state, encompassing the development of foundational Western civilization through its classical period, subsequent foreign rule, and eventual independence. It is a story of cultural influence, political fragmentation, and resilience, marked by periods of great achievement and foreign domination.

## Timeline
- **c. 7000 BC** — Neolithic Revolution reaches Greece; agricultural societies established.
- **c. 3200–1100 BC** — Bronze Age: Minoan, Cycladic, and Mycenaean civilizations flourish.
- **c. 1100–800 BC** — Greek Dark Ages follow the collapse of Mycenaean civilization.
- **800–490 BC** — Archaic period: rise of city-states (poleis), Greek alphabet adopted.
- **499–449 BC** — Persian Wars: Greek city-states repel invasions from the Persian Empire.
- **431–404 BC** — Peloponnesian War: conflict between Athens and Sparta ends in Spartan victory.
- **336–323 BC** — Reign of Alexander the Great, who creates a vast empire spreading Greek culture.
- **146 BC** — Roman conquest of Greece begins.
- **AD 324** — Constantinople becomes capital of the Eastern Roman (Byzantine) Empire.
- **1204** — Fourth Crusade sacks Constantinople; Latin Empire established.
- **1453** — Fall of Constantinople to the Ottoman Empire.
- **1821–1829** — Greek War of Independence leads to the establishment of the modern Greek state.
- **1974** — Fall of the military junta; establishment of the current Third Hellenic Republic.

## Body

### Prehistory and Bronze Age Civilizations
Greece was first inhabited by early hominins, with the earliest undisputed traces dating back 700,000 years. The Neolithic Revolution (c. 7000 BC) brought agriculture from Anatolia. The Bronze Age saw the rise of the Minoan civilization on Crete (c. 3000–1400 BC), a mercantile society with an undeciphered script (Linear A), and the Mycenaean civilization on the mainland (c. 1600–1100 BC), a warrior culture that used an early form of Greek (Linear B). The Mycenaean collapse c. 1100 BC led to the Greek Dark Ages.

### Ancient Greece: From Dark Ages to Hellenistic Kingdoms
The Dark Ages (1100–800 BC) saw a decline in population and literacy, followed by a renaissance. The Archaic period (800–490 BC) saw the adoption of the Phoenician alphabet and the formation of independent city-states (poleis). The Classical period (490–323 BC) was defined by the Persian Wars, which secured Greek autonomy, and the subsequent rivalry between Athens and Sparta, culminating in the Peloponnesian War. Athenian democracy, philosophy, and art flourished. Macedonian hegemony under Philip II and Alexander the Great (336–323 BC) unified Greece and spread Hellenistic culture across a vast empire. After Alexander's death, his empire fragmented into kingdoms, and Greek political independence ended with the Roman conquest in 146 BC.

### Roman, Byzantine, and Frankish Rule
Roman rule (146 BC–AD 324) saw Greek culture flourish within the empire. The division of the Roman Empire placed Greece in the Eastern, later Byzantine, Empire, with Constantinople as its capital. The Byzantine period (324–1204) was marked by economic prosperity, artistic revival, and defense against invasions. The Fourth Crusade (1204) shattered Byzantine control, leading to Latin and Frankish states. The Byzantine Empire was restored in 1261 but gradually weakened.

### Ottoman Rule and the Birth of Modern Greece
The Ottoman conquest, completed by the mid-16th century, lasted until the Greek War of Independence (1821–1829). With Great Power support, Greece achieved independence, becoming a monarchy. The 19th and early 20th centuries focused on territorial expansion and modernization. The Balkan Wars (1912–13) nearly doubled Greece's size. The 20th century brought national schism, the catastrophic Greco-Turkish War (1919–22), a population exchange, and a devastating Axis occupation in World War II.

### Contemporary Greece
Post-WWII, a civil war (1944–49) ended with a Western-aligned victory. Greece experienced rapid economic growth (the "Greek economic miracle") but also a military dictatorship (1967–74). Democracy was restored in 1974. Greece joined the EU in 1981 and the Eurozone in 2001. A severe sovereign debt crisis beginning in 2009 led to austerity measures and political upheaval. The economy has since shown signs of recovery.

## Terms
*   **Polis:** The fundamental political and social unit of ancient Greece, a self-governing "city-state."
*   **Hellenistic:** The period following the death of Alexander the Great, characterized by the spread of Greek culture across the Eastern Mediterranean and Near East.
*   **Byzantine Empire:** The Eastern Roman Empire, with its capital at Constantinople, that lasted from the 4th century AD to 1453.
*   **Millet System:** The Ottoman administrative system that organized non-Muslim religious communities (like the Orthodox Greeks) into semi-autonomous groups.
*   **Megali Idea:** The 19th-century Greek nationalist vision of uniting all ethnic Greek populations of the Ottoman Empire into a single state.
*   **Metapolitefsi:** The transition from military dictatorship to parliamentary democracy in Greece in 1974.
*   **Sovereign Debt Crisis:** The severe financial crisis that began in 2009, involving a collapse in government revenue and a massive increase in public debt, requiring international bailouts.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   The cause of the Late Bronze Age collapse (c. 1100 BC) is debated, with theories including invasions by "Sea Peoples" or the hypothesized Dorian invasion.
*   The extent and nature of the "Dorian invasion" that traditionally followed the Mycenaean collapse are unsubstantiated by archaeological evidence.
*   The responsibility for the 1922 Great Fire of Smyrna is a point of contention between Greek and Turkish historical narratives.
*   The severity and political motivations behind the austerity measures imposed during the Greek government-debt crisis remain highly controversial.

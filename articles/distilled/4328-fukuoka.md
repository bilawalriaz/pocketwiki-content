# Fukuoka

## Overview
Fukuoka is a major port city on Japan's Kyushu island, historically serving as the country's primary gateway to the Asian mainland. This strategic location fostered centuries of international trade and cultural exchange, giving the city a distinctive identity. Today, it is Japan's fastest-growing major city and a key economic and cultural hub for the region.

## Timeline
- **57 A.D.** — An envoy from the region visits the Han dynasty Chinese court, receiving a gold seal.
- **c. 7th century** — Fukuoka (as Dazaifu) becomes a critical administrative and trade center with Tang China.
- **1241** — The origin of the Hakata Gion Yamakasa festival, commemorating a priest who prayed against plague.
- **1274 & 1281** — Mongol invasions of Kyushu are thwarted, partly by severe storms later called "kamikaze."
- **1600** — Daimyō Kuroda Nagamasa names his new castle town "Fukuoka."
- **1889** — The modern city is formed by merging the merchant port of Hakata and the samurai district of Fukuoka.
- **1945** — The city is firebombed, destroying 21.5% of its urban area.
- **1972** — Fukuoka is designated a government-ordinance city.
- **1975** — The Sanyō Shinkansen high-speed rail is extended to Hakata Station.
- **1981** — The Fukuoka City Subway begins operations.
- **2005** — The Nanakuma Line subway opens; Fukuoka surpasses Kyoto in population.
- **2014** — The city is selected as a National Strategic Zone for global startups.

## Body

### Historical Development
Human settlement dates to the Jomon period, but the area rose to prominence during the Yayoi period as a hub for rice cultivation and continental trade. Its proximity to Korea and China made it Japan's primary port for centuries. Interaction with China is recorded as early as 57 A.D. By the Asuka period, the area (often called Dazaifu) was a major administrative center. After the Tang dynasty's collapse, trade declined, but the city's importance grew through Buddhist temple construction and festivals. The Mongol invasions of 1274 and 1281, defended by a 20 km stone wall built along Hakata Bay, were pivotal events. Following the medieval period, the modern city was formed in 1889 by merging the merchant district of Hakata with the samurai district of Fukuoka, with the name "Fukuoka" ultimately chosen.

### Modern Growth and Economy
Fukuoka saw rapid growth in the 20th century, especially after WWII. It is now the economic center of Kyushu, with a service-sector-focused economy. The city is Japan's largest startup hub, offering special visas and tax incentives. Its GDP is significant, and it hosts the headquarters of major companies like Kyushu Electric Power and JR Kyushu. The port of Hakata and Fukuoka Airport make it a key transportation hub. The city has one of Japan's six stock exchanges and is noted for being one of its most affordable major cities.

### Culture and Tourism
Fukuoka has a vibrant culture, highlighted by major festivals like the Hakata Gion Yamakasa (July) and Hakata Dontaku (May). It is famous for local foods such as Hakata (tonkotsu) ramen, mentaiko, and yatai street stalls. The city hosts over 2 million foreign visitors annually, primarily from South Korea, Taiwan, and China. Key attractions include Fukuoka Castle ruins in Maizuru Park, numerous historic temples, and modern sites like Canal City and Fukuoka Tower. The Itoshima area west of the city is a popular coastal destination.

### Geography and Climate
Fukuoka is bordered by mountains, surrounds Hakata Bay, and opens to the Genkai Sea. It is 1,100 km from Tokyo and about 180 km from Busan, South Korea. The city has a humid subtropical climate with hot, humid summers and mild winters. Annual precipitation averages about 1,600 mm, with a rainy season in June and July. The city experiences occasional earthquakes, most notably a magnitude 7 (lower-6 intensity) quake in 2005 along the Kego fault.

## Terms
*   **Daimyō:** A powerful feudal lord in pre-modern Japan.
*   **Kamikaze:** Literally "divine wind," originally referring to the typhoons that thwarted Mongol invasions; later used for WWII suicide attacks.
*   **Kōrokan:** The government guest house for foreign traders in ancient Japan, located in Fukuoka.
*   **Yatai:** Open-air food stalls, a famous part of Fukuoka's nightlife and food culture.
*   **Hakata-ben:** The distinctive local dialect of the Fukuoka region.
*   **Mentaiko:** Spicy marinated cod roe, a famous Fukuoka specialty.
*   **Tonkotsu ramen:** A pork-bone broth ramen style originating in Fukuoka.
*   **Shinkansen:** Japan's high-speed "bullet train" network.
*   **Matsuri:** A Japanese festival.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   The exact role of the Fukuoka/Dazaifu area in the founding of Japan is debated among scholars, with some claiming it was the first point of entry for outsiders and the Imperial Family.
*   The probability of a major earthquake along the Kego fault, which runs under the city, has been revised upward following the discovery of its extension into the Genkai Sea after the 2005 quake.

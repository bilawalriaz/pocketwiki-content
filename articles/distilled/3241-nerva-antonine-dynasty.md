# Nerva–Antonine dynasty

## Overview
The Nerva–Antonine dynasty was a period of Roman imperial rule from 96 to 192 AD, encompassing seven emperors. It is historically significant for its system of adoptive succession, which produced the "Five Good Emperors" and is often viewed as a high point of stability and good governance before the Empire's later decline.

## Timeline
- **96 AD** — Nerva becomes emperor, initiating the dynasty.
- **98 AD** — Trajan succeeds Nerva, beginning major military expansion.
- **117 AD** — Hadrian succeeds Trajan, focusing on consolidation and defense.
- **138 AD** — Antoninus Pius succeeds Hadrian, beginning a long, peaceful reign.
- **161 AD** — Marcus Aurelius and Lucius Verus become co-emperors after Antoninus Pius's death.
- **169 AD** — Lucius Verus dies, leaving Marcus Aurelius as sole emperor.
- **180 AD** — Commodus, biological son of Marcus Aurelius, succeeds him.
- **192 AD** — Commodus is murdered, ending the dynasty and leading to the Year of the Five Emperors.

## Body

### Adoptive Succession and the "Five Good Emperors"
The dynasty's first six successions were notable because the reigning emperor, lacking a male heir, adopted his chosen successor. Under Roman law, adoption created a legal bond as strong as kinship. This practice, while not new (e.g., Augustus adopted Tiberius), was applied consistently. The first five emperors—Nerva, Trajan, Hadrian, Antoninus Pius, and Marcus Aurelius—are collectively known as the "Five Good Emperors." This label, popularized by Niccolò Machiavelli and Edward Gibbon, suggests that rulers who succeeded by adoption governed more wisely and virtuously than those who inherited the throne by birth, earning loyalty through good governance rather than force.

### The Antonine Emperors
The "Antonine" phase of the dynasty began with Hadrian's death in 138. Hadrian named Antoninus Pius his heir on the condition that Pius adopt both Marcus Aurelius and Lucius Verus. Antoninus Pius's reign was peaceful and characterized by adherence to tradition and cooperation with the Senate. Upon his death in 161, Marcus Aurelius and Lucius Verus became co-emperors. After Verus's death in 169, Marcus Aurelius ruled alone until 180, remembered as a thoughtful administrator and leader.

### Decline and End
The dynasty's adoptive system ended when Marcus Aurelius was succeeded by his biological son, Commodus. This return to hereditary succession is traditionally seen as an unfortunate choice and the beginning of the Empire's decline. Commodus's reign ended with his murder in 192, plunging Rome into the brief civil war known as the Year of the Five Emperors and paving the way for the Severan dynasty.

## Terms
- **Adoptive Succession:** The practice of an emperor legally adopting a chosen successor, creating a bond equivalent to blood kinship under Roman law.
- **Five Good Emperors:** A term for the first five rulers of the dynasty (Nerva to Marcus Aurelius), associated with wise and virtuous governance.
- **Heir Presumptive:** A person who is expected to inherit a title but whose claim can be displaced by the birth of a closer relative.
- **Year of the Five Emperors:** The period of civil war in 193 AD following the dynasty's end, with five claimants to the throne.
- **Severan Dynasty:** The imperial dynasty that succeeded the Nerva–Antonines, established after the Year of the Five Emperors.

## Debates and open questions
The source material presents a clear scholarly consensus on the "Five Good Emperors" as a period of exceptional governance, a view originating with Machiavelli and Gibbon. The primary open question implied is whether the adoptive system itself was the cause of this stability, or if it was coincidental to the personal virtues of those specific rulers. The text also notes that family connections often persisted through the adoptions, complicating a purely merit-based interpretation.

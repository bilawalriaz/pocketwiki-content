# Colombo

## Overview
Colombo is Sri Lanka's commercial capital and most populous city, serving as the nation's primary economic engine and a historic port on the Indian Ocean. Its identity has been shaped by successive waves of colonial rule—Portuguese, Dutch, and British—followed by independence, creating a multicultural urban center that remains the country's financial and diplomatic hub.

## Timeline
- **c. 8th century AD** — Arab traders settle in Colombo due to its strategic port.
- **1505** — Portuguese explorers arrive; name the city "Colombo."
- **1517** — Portuguese begin constructing a fort in Colombo.
- **1656** — Dutch capture Colombo after a siege, ousting the Portuguese.
- **1796** — British capture Colombo from the Dutch.
- **1815** — British make Colombo the capital of the crown colony of Ceylon after the Kandyan Kingdom is ceded.
- **1865** — Colombo Municipal Council is constituted.
- **1948** — Ceylon gains independence; Colombo remains the capital.
- **1980s** — Administrative functions begin moving to Sri Jayawardenepura Kotte.
- **1997** — World Trade Centre twin towers are completed.

## Body

### Etymology and Early History
The name "Colombo" was introduced by the Portuguese in 1505. It is believed to derive from the Sinhala name *Kolon thota* ("port on the river Kelani") or *Kola-amba-thota* ("harbour with leafy mango trees"). The word *kolamba* (fort or harbour) may originate from the indigenous Vedda language. Arab traders had established a presence by the 8th century, controlling trade between Sinhalese kingdoms and the outside world.

### Colonial Eras
The Portuguese established a trading post and fort in 1517, eventually gaining control of the coastal area by 1593 with Colombo as their capital. The Dutch, allied with the Kingdom of Kandy, captured Colombo in 1656 and made it the capital of their maritime provinces. The British took Colombo in 1796 and, after unifying the island in 1815, made it the capital of British Ceylon. They developed the modern city around the fort, establishing municipal governance in 1865.

### Post-Independence and Modern Status
Colombo remained the national capital until the 1980s, when administrative functions were relocated to Sri Jayawardenepura Kotte. Colombo was designated the commercial capital. Despite this, most government institutions, including the President's House and Supreme Court, remain in Colombo, as do nearly all foreign diplomatic missions.

### Geography and Climate
The city is located on the west coast, bordered by the Kelani River to the north and featuring the 65-hectare Beira Lake. It has a tropical rainforest climate (Af), hot year-round with average highs of 31°C (87.8°F) from March to April. Annual rainfall averages 2,500 mm (98 in), with heavy monsoon rains from April to June and September to November.

### Economy and Infrastructure
The Colombo Metropolitan area contributes about 40% of Sri Lanka's GDP. It is the headquarters for most major Sri Lankan corporations. The Port of Colombo is one of the busiest in the world, handling 3.75 million TEUs in 2008. The city has an extensive bus network, a central railway hub, and several expressways. Ratmalana Airport, the city's first international airport (1935), now primarily serves domestic flights.

### Demographics and Culture
Colombo is multi-ethnic and multi-religious, with Sinhalese, Sri Lankan Moor, and Sri Lankan Tamil communities, among others. Major cultural festivals include Vesak (Buddha's birth, enlightenment, and death), Eid Ul Fitr, Christmas, and the Sinhalese and Tamil New Year. The city is home to prominent cricket stadiums, performing arts centers, and the National Museum, established in 1877.

## Terms
- **Charter city**: A city with a specific legal charter defining its governance structure, in this case a mayor-council system.
- **Metropolitan area**: The city and its surrounding suburbs forming a continuous urban region.
- **TEU (Twenty-foot Equivalent Unit)**: A standard unit of cargo capacity for container ships and ports.
- **Tropical rainforest climate (Af)**: A climate classification characterized by high temperatures and significant rainfall throughout the year.
- **Vesak**: A Buddhist festival commemorating the birth, enlightenment, and death of the Buddha.
- **Burgher**: A community in Sri Lanka descended from European colonists, primarily Portuguese and Dutch.
- **Conurbation**: A large urban area formed by the merging of several cities and towns.

## Debates and Open Questions
The exact etymology of "Colombo" is debated, with multiple theories linking it to Sinhala or Vedda words for "port" or "harbour." The completion of the administrative capital's move from Colombo to Sri Jayawardenepura Kotte remains unfinished, leaving Colombo's precise role as a "capital" subject to functional interpretation.

# Ludhiana

## Overview
Ludhiana is the most populous city in the Indian state of Punjab, serving as a major industrial and commercial hub often called "India's Manchester." Its modern history is defined by its founding in the late 15th century, its transformation under British rule, and its post-Partition boom as a manufacturing center for textiles, hosiery, and machinery.

## Timeline

- **1526** — Becomes part of the Mughal Lahore Subah after the establishment of the Mughal Empire.
- **1849** — Annexed by the British East India Company following the First Anglo-Sikh War.
- **1867** — Ludhiana Municipality is established.
- **1947** — Partition of India leads to major demographic shift; refugee influx accelerates industrial growth.
- **1962** — Punjab Agricultural University is established.
- **2009 & 2013** — World Bank ranks Ludhiana as the city in India with the best business environment.
- **2021** — Selected for the Government of India's Smart Cities Mission.

## Body

### Founding and Early History
The modern city was established in 1480 during the Lodi dynasty's rule. According to tradition, Sultan Sikandar Lodi sent commanders to restore authority in Punjab; Nihad Khan founded Ludhiana at the village of Mir Hota. The name evolved from "Lodi-ana," meaning "town of the Lodis." The Lodhi Fort (Purana Qila) is the only surviving monument from this period. The area was part of the Mughal Lahore Subah and developed as a trading center on routes between Delhi and Lahore.

### British Rule and Industrialization
After the Treaty of Amritsar, Ludhiana became a British military cantonment. Following the annexation of Punjab in 1849, railways and irrigation projects transformed it into a commercial center. Industries in hosiery, textiles, and machinery emerged in the late 19th century. The city was an active center during the Indian independence movement, notably contributing to the Ghadar movement.

### Post-Partition Growth
The 1947 Partition caused a massive demographic change, with Hindu and Sikh refugees from West Punjab settling in the city. These refugees, including skilled artisans and entrepreneurs, established small-scale manufacturing units. This influx accelerated the growth of industries like bicycles, hosiery, sewing machines, and auto components, cementing Ludhiana's status as an industrial powerhouse.

### Geography and Climate
Ludhiana is located on the old bank of the Sutlej River, whose course has shifted 13 km south. It has a humid subtropical climate with three seasons. The city faces severe air pollution, with particulate matter levels significantly exceeding WHO standards, and industrial water pollution is a concern along the Budha Dariya.

### Demographics and Language
As of the 2011 census, the population was 1,618,879. Hinduism is the predominant religion (66%), followed by Sikhism (29%). Punjabi is the main mother tongue (67%), with Hindi (29%) widely used due to in-migration from other states.

### Economy
Ludhiana is a major industrial center, ranked by the World Bank as India's best city for business in 2009 and 2013. It is Asia's largest hub for bicycle manufacturing, producing over 50% of India's bicycles annually. The city also produces 60% of India's tractor parts and a large share of auto components, hosiery, and winter clothing. A growing IT sector is also present.

### Transportation
Ludhiana is a key railway junction on the Delhi-Amritsar main line. It is connected by major national highways (NH 44, NH 5). The city is served by the older Ludhiana Airport and the newer Halwara International Airport, inaugurated in 2026. Local transport relies on auto-rickshaws, cycle rickshaws, and taxis, as city bus and BRTS projects have been scrapped.

### Education
The city is home to numerous schools and major institutions, including Punjab Agricultural University (one of Asia's largest), Guru Angad Dev Veterinary and Animal Sciences University, Christian Medical College (the first medical school for women in Asia, founded 1894), and several engineering colleges.

## Terms
- **Misl:** A sovereign state of the Sikh Confederacy in the 18th century.
- **Cis-Sutlej states:** Princely states located south of the Sutlej River, which came under British paramountcy.
- **Ghadar movement:** An early 20th-century revolutionary movement to overthrow British rule in India, organized by Punjabi immigrants in North America.
- **Smart Cities Mission:** A Government of India urban renewal and retrofitting program.
- **Hosiery:** Knitted fabric or clothing, especially underwear and socks; a key industry in Ludhiana.
- **Gatka:** A traditional Sikh martial art practiced in the region.

# Antoni Gaudí

## Overview

Antoni Gaudí i Cornet (1852–1926) was a Catalan architect and designer, widely regarded as the greatest exponent of Catalan Modernisme. His work fused architecture, nature, and religion, producing a sui generis style characterized by organic forms, structural innovation, and meticulous craftsmanship. Most of his buildings are in Barcelona, with his magnum opus, the Sagrada Família, being the most-visited monument in Spain.

## Body

### Early Life and Education

Gaudí was born on 25 June 1852 in Reus, or possibly Riudoms, to Francesc Gaudí i Serra and Antònia Cornet i Bertran. The youngest of five children, he suffered from poor health, including rheumatism, which contributed to his reserved character and early adoption of vegetarianism. Gaudí's family originated in the Auvergne region in southern France, with one ancestor, Joan Gaudí, a hawker who moved to Catalonia in the 17th century.

He studied at the Piarists school in Reus and later moved to Barcelona to study architecture at the Llotja School and the Barcelona Higher School of Architecture, graduating in 1878. His grades were average, and his director, Elies Rogent, famously remarked, "We have given this academic title either to a fool or a genius."

### Professional Development

Gaudí's early works included lampposts for Plaça Reial and the Cooperativa Obrera Mataronense. His breakthrough came in 1878 when Eusebi Güell, impressed by Gaudí's showcase at the Paris World's Fair, became his main patron. This led to commissions like the Palau Güell, Park Güell, and the crypt of the Colònia Güell.

In 1883, Gaudí took over the recently initiated Sagrada Família project, completely redesigning it in his distinctive style. From 1915 until his death, he devoted himself almost exclusively to this project. His work was influenced by neo-Gothic art, Oriental techniques, and natural forms. He rarely drew detailed plans, preferring three-dimensional scale models and on-site improvisation.

### Style and Innovation

Gaudí's style evolved through several phases: Orientalist, Neo-Gothic, Naturalist, and finally Organic Synthesis. He was deeply inspired by nature, studying natural forms and incorporating ruled geometrical forms like hyperbolic paraboloids, hyperboloids, and helicoids into his designs. The catenary arch became a signature element, allowing him to create strong, self-supporting structures without buttresses.

He was a master craftsman who integrated ceramics, stained glass, wrought ironwork, and carpentry into his designs. He introduced techniques like trencadís, using waste ceramic pieces for mosaics. His work on the Sagrada Família exemplified his vision of an interior resembling a forest, with tree-like columns branching into hyperboloid vaults.

### Personal Life and Legacy

Gaudí remained single, dedicating his life to architecture and his Catholic faith. His Catholic faith intensified throughout his life, and religious imagery appears in many of his works. This earned him the nickname "God's Architect." Gaudí was a proponent of Catalan culture but was not politically active. He refused suggestions by politicians such as Francesc Cambó and Enric Prat de la Riba that he run for deputy. His Catalan identity was less political and more geared towards art, history, culture, and language.

He was struck by a tram on 7 June 1926 and died on 10 June 1926 at age 73. His legacy includes seven of his works declared UNESCO World Heritage Sites between 1984 and 2005. His cause for canonisation was opened in the Archdiocese of Barcelona in 2003. Pope Francis authorised Gaudí's declaration as Venerable in April 2025.

## Terms

- **Catalan Modernisme**: A cultural movement in Catalonia, peaking in the late 19th and early 20th centuries, characterized by organic forms, intricate craftsmanship, and a rejection of industrial aesthetics.
- **Trencadís**: A mosaic technique using broken ceramic pieces, often waste material, to create decorative surfaces.
- **Catenary Arch**: A curve formed by a hanging chain, used by Gaudí for its structural efficiency in distributing weight evenly.
- **Ruled Surfaces**: Geometric forms generated by moving a straight line (generatrix) along a path, such as hyperboloids and helicoids.
- **Renaixença**: A 19th-century Catalan cultural revival movement emphasizing the restoration of Catalan language, arts, and identity.
- **Noucentisme**: An early 20th-century Catalan artistic movement that replaced Modernisme, favoring classical order and rationality.
- **Organic Synthesis**: Gaudí's mature style, integrating natural forms with structural innovation to create harmonious, self-supporting architectural systems.
- **Magnum Opus**: Gaudí's masterpiece, the Sagrada Família, a basilica and expiatory church in Barcelona.
- **Venerable**: A title in the Catholic Church given to individuals whose heroic virtues have been recognized by the Pope.
- **Phalanstère**: A utopian community concept popularized by Charles Fourier, which Gaudí and fellow students once planned to implement at Poblet Monastery.

## Debates and Open Questions

- **Birthplace**: Gaudí's exact birthplace is uncertain, with documents citing both Reus and Riudoms. His later claim of being born in Riudoms may have stemmed from displeasure with Reus.
- **Religious Canonization**: While Gaudí's cause for canonization has progressed (declared Venerable in 2025), beatification and sainthood remain pending, requiring documented miracles attributed to his intercession.
- **Architectural Influence**: Gaudí's relationship with 20th-century movements like Bauhaus is debated; while some see his work as antithetical to modernist minimalism, others recognize his influence on architects like Le Corbusier and Santiago Calatrava.
- **Completion of Sagrada Família**: The church's completion, expected around 2026, raises questions about whether the final result will align with Gaudí's original vision, given the loss of his plans and models during the Spanish Civil War.

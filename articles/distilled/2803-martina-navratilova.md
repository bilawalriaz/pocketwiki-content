# Martina Navratilova

## Overview
Martina Navratilova is a Czech-American former professional tennis player widely regarded as one of the greatest athletes in the sport's history. She dominated women's tennis in the 1980s, setting enduring records for titles and weeks ranked No. 1, and was a pioneering activist for gay rights.

## Timeline

- **1975** — Defects from Czechoslovakia; granted U.S. residency.
- **1978** — Wins first Grand Slam singles title at Wimbledon.
- **1981** — Becomes a U.S. citizen; comes out as bisexual.
- **1983** — Achieves a season record of 86–1 (98.9% win rate).
- **1984** — Holds all four Grand Slam singles titles simultaneously.
- **1990** — Wins record ninth Wimbledon singles title.
- **2000** — Inducted into International Tennis Hall of Fame.
- **2006** — Wins final Grand Slam title (mixed doubles, US Open) at age 49.
- **2014** — Marries Julia Lemigova.
- **2023** — Diagnosed with throat and breast cancer; declared cancer-free.

## Body

### Early Life and Career
Born in Prague, Navratilova was raised by her mother and stepfather, Miroslav Navrátil, who became her first coach. She won the Czechoslovak national championship at age 15 in 1972. After turning professional, she reached two Grand Slam singles finals in 1975. Following the US Open that year, she sought political asylum in the United States, leading to the revocation of her Czechoslovak citizenship.

### Rise to Dominance
Navratilova won her first Grand Slam singles title at Wimbledon in 1978. Her rivalry with Chris Evert defined the era, with Navratilova eventually leading their head-to-head 43–37. After adopting a rigorous fitness regimen in 1981, she became the sport's most dominant player. In 1983, she posted a career-best 86–1 record. In 1984, she held all four major singles titles at once, though this is not universally considered a "Grand Slam" as they were not won in a single calendar year.

### Peak and Rivalries
From 1982 to 1986, Navratilova was virtually unbeatable, losing only six singles matches over three years. She won a record six consecutive major singles titles across 1983–84. Her rivalry with Steffi Graf began in the mid-1980s; Navratilova won their first five meetings but Graf eventually surpassed her. Navratilova's final Grand Slam singles title was her record ninth Wimbledon crown in 1990.

### Later Career and Retirement
Navratilova retired from full-time singles competition in 1994 but returned to focus on doubles. She won her last major title, the mixed doubles at the 2006 US Open, at age 49. She retired with Open Era records of 167 singles titles and 177 doubles titles, and a total of 59 Grand Slam titles (18 singles, 31 women's doubles, 10 mixed doubles).

### Personal Life and Activism
Navratilova became a U.S. citizen in 1981 and reacquired Czech citizenship in 2008. She came out as gay in 1981 and has been a prominent activist for LGBTQ+ rights. She has also been a vocal critic of communism and has engaged in public debates on transgender athletes in women's sports. She has faced health challenges, including breast cancer diagnoses in 2010 and 2023.

## Terms
- **Grand Slam**: Winning all four major tennis tournaments (Australian Open, French Open, Wimbledon, US Open) in a single calendar year.
- **Career Grand Slam**: Winning each of the four majors at least once over a player's career.
- **Boxed Set**: A career Grand Slam in singles, women's doubles, and mixed doubles.
- **Open Era**: The period in professional tennis beginning in 1968, when professionals were allowed to compete in Grand Slam tournaments.
- **WTA**: Women's Tennis Association, the principal organizing body of women's professional tennis.
- **Asylum**: Protection granted by a nation to someone who has left their home country as a political refugee.
- **Ductal carcinoma in situ**: A non-invasive condition where abnormal cells are found in the lining of a breast milk duct.

## Debates and Open Questions
- Whether Navratilova's 1984 holding of all four major titles constitutes a true "Grand Slam," as the titles were not won in a single calendar year.
- The appropriate criteria for transgender women's inclusion in elite women's sports, a debate in which Navratilova has been a prominent and controversial participant.
- The statistical impact of the few singles matches she played in her forties on her overall career records.

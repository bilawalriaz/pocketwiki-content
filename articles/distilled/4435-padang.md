# Padang

## Overview
Padang is the capital and largest city of West Sumatra province, Indonesia, and a major cultural and economic hub on Sumatra's west coast. It is renowned for its Minangkabau culture, distinctive cuisine, and historical role as a trading port, having been shaped by pre-colonial kingdoms, European colonialism, and significant natural disasters.

## Timeline
- **16th-17th centuries** — Padang is a trade center for pepper and gold, controlled by the Pagaruyung Kingdom and Aceh Sultanate.
- **1663** — Comes under Dutch authority; a trading post is built in 1680.
- **1781-1784 & 1795-1819** — Under British rule during the Fourth Anglo-Dutch War and Napoleonic Wars.
- **1797** — A major tsunami (5–10m flow depth) follows an estimated 8.5–8.7 Mw earthquake.
- **1819** — City is transferred back to the Netherlands.
- **1833** — Another tsunami (3–4m flow depth) follows an estimated 8.6–8.9 Mw earthquake.
- **1837** — Becomes the seat of government for the West Coast of Sumatra.
- **1906** — Achieves city status (*gemeente*), one of the first two in Sumatra.
- **1942** — Abandoned by the Dutch; occupied by the Japanese.
- **2009** — A 7.9 magnitude earthquake causes over 1,100 fatalities, with 313 in Padang.

## Body

### History and Colonial Era
Padang has been a trade center since the 16th century, initially under the Pagaruyung Kingdom and Aceh Sultanate, trading pepper and gold with European and Asian powers. The Dutch established control in 1663, building a trading post in 1680. The city changed hands between Dutch and British rule several times before being permanently returned to the Netherlands in 1819. After gold mines were exhausted circa 1780, trade shifted to coffee, salts, and textiles. Padang became a major port city and, in 1906, one of the first populated places in Sumatra to gain city status. The city's history is also marked by devastating tsunamis in 1797 and 1833, both triggered by major offshore earthquakes. During World War II, the Japanese occupied the city in 1942.

### Geography and Climate
Located on Sumatra's west coast, Padang covers 694.96 km², with over 60% being hilly protection forest and only about 205 km² considered urban. It has a 68 km coastline and includes 19 small islands. The city has a tropical rainforest climate, making it one of Indonesia's wettest cities, averaging roughly 4,300 mm of rain annually. Temperatures are stable, averaging 26°C. Padang has 21 rivers, and in 1980, two-thirds of the city flooded due to inadequate drainage.

### Governance and Culture
Since 2007, the city government has implemented religiously motivated policies, including requiring female municipal employees and students to wear hijab. Padang is the heart of Minangkabau culture, and its cuisine, known as Padang food, is famous nationwide. Dishes are typically cooked once daily and displayed for customers to choose from. Signature dishes include *rendang* (spicy meat stew), *soto Padang* (beef soup), and *sate* (beef satay).

### Tourism and Landmarks
Padang is a transit point for surfers and tourists visiting the Mentawai Islands and highland areas. Key attractions include Padang Beach (Taplau) for sunsets, Air Manis Beach with the legendary Malin Kundang Stone, and Siti Nurbaya Park. The city's old district near Muaro Harbor features historic Dutch and Chinese architecture, including the former Bank Indonesia building. Notable landmarks are the Adityawarman Museum, the modern Grand Mosque of West Sumatra, and the historic Ganting Grand Mosque.

### Infrastructure
The city is served by Minangkabau International Airport, which replaced the former Tabing Airport. Teluk Bayur harbour, built in 1888, is the largest on Sumatra's west coast and a key gateway to the Mentawai Islands. Public transport includes the TransPadang bus service. A toll road connecting Padang to Sicincin is under construction as part of a larger route to Pekanbaru. Railroad lines connect Padang to several regional cities.

## Terms
- **Gemeente**: A Dutch term for a municipality or city administrative area.
- **Minangkabau**: The ethnic group native to West Sumatra, whose culture and cuisine are central to Padang's identity.
- **Rendang**: A spicy, slow-cooked meat stew, considered a signature dish of Padang cuisine.
- **Rumah Gadang**: The traditional large communal house of the Minangkabau people.
- **Zakat**: An obligatory alms or tax in Islam, which municipal employees in Padang are required to pay.
- **Adipura**: An Indonesian award for the cleanest and greenest cities.
- **TransPadang**: A bus rapid transit system in the city, modeled after Jakarta's TransJakarta.

## Debates and Open Questions
The source notes that the development of the Ombilin coal field with Padang as its outlet port from 1950 was seen by some observers as reflecting the "economic and political colonisation of Indonesia," suggesting a debate about the legacy of colonial-era infrastructure.

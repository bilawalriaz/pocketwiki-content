# Madrid

## Overview
Madrid is the capital and most populous city of Spain, serving as the country's political, economic, and cultural center. It is the second-largest city in the European Union by population and a major global financial hub. Its history as the permanent seat of the Spanish court since 1561 has shaped its development into a modern, service-based economy and a center for arts, education, and international organizations.

## Timeline
- **Late 9th century** — A walled fortress is built by the Emirate of Córdoba on the site of modern Madrid.
- **1083/1085** — Madrid is conquered by Christians under Alfonso VI of León and Castile.
- **1561** — Philip II establishes Madrid as the permanent seat of the court of the Hispanic Monarchy.
- **1706** — Madrid changes hands during the War of the Spanish Succession.

- **2024** — Formula One announces Madrid will host the 2026 Spanish Grand Prix.

## Body

### History and Etymology
The site of Madrid has been occupied since prehistoric times, with remains of Celtic, Roman, and Visigothic settlements. The first historical record of a settlement dates to the Muslim era, when a fortress was built in the second half of the 9th century. The name "Madrid" has debated origins, with theories pointing to Celtic, Arabic, or Latin roots. The city was conquered by Christians in 1083 or 1085 and consolidated as a town of the Crown of Castile in the Late Middle Ages. Its status was transformed in 1561 when Philip II made it the permanent capital, leading to rapid population growth. The city played a key role in the War of the Spanish Succession and the Peninsular War. It was a Republican stronghold during the Spanish Civil War (1936–1939) and experienced significant growth and industrialization during the 20th century, particularly under the Franco regime. After the transition to democracy, Madrid consolidated its position as a major European economic and cultural center.

### Geography and Climate
Madrid is located in the center of the Iberian Peninsula on the Meseta Central, at an average altitude of 650 meters (2,130 ft), making it one of Europe's highest capitals. It has a cold semi-arid climate transitioning to a Mediterranean climate, with hot, sunny summers and cool winters. It is among the sunniest and least rainy capitals in Europe. The city has an extensive network of parks and forests, including the large Monte de El Pardo and the popular El Retiro Park. Water supply is managed by the Canal de Isabel II, with most water coming from dams on the Lozoya River.

### Demographics and Government
Madrid's population has been stable at around 3 million since the 1970s, with a significant foreign-born population, primarily from Latin America. The city is governed by the City Council (Ayuntamiento), led by a mayor. It is also the capital of Spain and the Community of Madrid, housing the national government, parliament, and regional institutions. The city is divided into 21 districts.

### Economy
Madrid's economy is dominated by the service sector, particularly finance, business services, and tourism. It is a major European financial center, home to the Bank of Spain and the Madrid Stock Exchange. The city hosts the headquarters of many large Spanish and multinational companies. Industry, though less dominant, remains significant, especially in high-technology sectors. Madrid is a leading global tourist destination, with major attractions including the "Golden Triangle of Art" museums. The city faces challenges such as income inequality, high rental prices, and gentrification.

### Culture and Landmarks
Madrid is a major cultural hub, renowned for its world-class museums like the Prado, Reina Sofía, and Thyssen-Bornemisza. Its architecture spans from medieval remnants to modern skyscrapers. The city is a center for Spanish literature and language, hosting the Royal Spanish Academy. It has a vibrant nightlife and is famous for its cuisine, including dishes like *cocido madrileño*. Major festivals include the San Isidro festivities and one of the world's largest LGBT Pride celebrations. Madrid is also a major sports city, home to top football clubs Real Madrid and Atlético Madrid.

### Transport
Madrid is a central transport node for Spain, with a radial network of highways and a high-speed rail (AVE) system connecting it to major cities. Its public transport system includes the Madrid Metro (one of Europe's longest), commuter rail (Cercanías), and an extensive bus network. Madrid-Barajas Airport is one of Europe's busiest. The city has implemented low-emission zones to reduce pollution.

## Terms
*   **Autovía:** A type of high-capacity, dual-carriageway highway in Spain.
*   **Cercanías:** The commuter rail service serving Madrid and its metropolitan area.
*   **Chulapo/a:** A traditional Madrilenian style of dress and associated cultural identity.
*   **Golden Triangle of Art:** The area along the Paseo del Prado containing the Prado, Reina Sofía, and Thyssen-Bornemisza museums.
*   **Meseta Central:** The high plateau in the center of the Iberian Peninsula where Madrid is located.
*   **Movida (La Movida):** A cultural and artistic movement that emerged in Madrid after the end of the Francoist dictatorship.
*   **Villa de realengo:** A historical designation for a town directly linked to the Spanish Crown.
*   **Viajes de agua:** Historical water channels or qanats used to supply water to Madrid before modern infrastructure.

## Debates and open questions
*   The etymology of the name "Madrid" is unknown, with competing theories from Celtic, Arabic, and Latin origins.
*   The social composition of Real Madrid's fan base is debated, with claims it is primarily middle-class versus suggestions of a large working-class following.
*   Bullfighting remains a controversial tradition in Madrid, with significant public debate surrounding it.

# Paraguayan War

## Overview
The Paraguayan War (1864–1870), also called the War of the Triple Alliance, was Latin America's deadliest inter-state conflict. Fought between Paraguay and the Triple Alliance of Argentina, Brazil, and Uruguay, it resulted in the near-total destruction of the Paraguayan state, military, and a large portion of its population. The conflict reshaped the regional balance of power with profound, long-lasting consequences.

- **1 May 1865** — Brazil, Argentina, and Uruguay sign the secret Treaty of the Triple Alliance.
- **11 Jun 1865** — Brazilian fleet destroys the Paraguayan Navy at the Battle of Riachuelo.
- **24 May 1866** — Battle of Tuyutí, one of Latin America's bloodiest, results in heavy Paraguayan losses.

- **25 Jul 1868** — The Paraguayan fortress of Humaitá falls after a long siege.
- **1 Jan 1869** — Allied forces occupy the Paraguayan capital, Asunción.
- **1 Mar 1870** — Paraguayan President Francisco Solano López is killed at the Battle of Cerro Corá.

## Body

### Origins and Outbreak
The war's roots lay in unresolved colonial-era territorial disputes and the struggle for regional hegemony between Brazil and Argentina. Paraguay, under President Francisco Solano López, had developed a strong military to counter its larger neighbors. The immediate trigger was the Uruguayan War (1864), where Brazil intervened against the Blanco government, a Paraguayan ally. When Brazil invaded Uruguay in October 1864, Paraguay protested. In November, Paraguay seized a Brazilian ship, leading to a declaration of war. After Argentina refused Paraguay a right of way to attack Brazil, Paraguay declared war on Argentina in March 1865. Brazil, Argentina, and Uruguay then formed the Triple Alliance, with the secret aim of removing López from power.

### Paraguayan Offensive and Allied Counterattack
Paraguay initially took the initiative, invading the Brazilian province of Mato Grosso in December 1864 and later the Argentine province of Corrientes and southern Brazil. However, the Brazilian naval victory at Riachuelo in June 1865 crippled Paraguay's ability to sustain its offensive. By late 1865, the Triple Alliance had regrouped and invaded Paraguay. The war devolved into a series of bloody battles, including Tuyutí (May 1866), where Paraguay suffered over 12,000 casualties. A major Paraguayan defensive victory at Curupayty (September 1866) halted the Allied advance for nearly a year.

### Siege of Humaitá and Fall of Asunción
In late 1866, the Marquess of Caxias took command of the Allied forces, reorganizing the army and resuming the offensive in July 1867. His strategy involved outflanking Paraguay's strong defensive line anchored on the fortress of Humaitá. After Brazilian ironclads successfully passed the fortress's guns in February 1868, Humaitá was isolated and fell in July. Caxias then executed a flanking maneuver through the Gran Chaco, appearing behind Paraguayan lines. In December 1868, Allied forces won decisive victories at Avay and Lomas Valentinas, destroying the last major Paraguayan armies. Asunción was occupied on 1 January 1869.

### Guerrilla War and End of Conflict
With his army shattered and the capital taken, López fled north and organized a guerrilla resistance. This final phase, known as the Campaign of the Hills, was marked by extreme brutality and the conscription of slaves and children. The resistance ended only with López's death at the Battle of Cerro Corá on 1 March 1870. Allied forces, primarily Brazilian, occupied Paraguay until 1876.

### Consequences and Legacy
The war devastated Paraguay. It lost about 30% of its territory to Brazil and Argentina and suffered catastrophic population losses, with modern estimates suggesting 60–70% of its pre-war population perished, mostly from disease and hunger. The country was occupied and saddled with war debt until 1943. For Brazil, the war increased its regional influence but also created a massive public debt and strengthened the military's role in politics, contributing to the fall of the Empire in 1889. Argentina consolidated its national government and gained territory. The conflict's interpretation remains controversial, with some viewing it as a case of genocide against the Paraguayan people.

## Terms
- **Triple Alliance**: The secret treaty signed on 1 May 1865 between Brazil, Argentina, and Uruguay to wage war against Paraguay until its government was overthrown.
- **Guerrilla warfare**: Irregular, non-conventional fighting tactics used by Paraguayan forces after the defeat of its main armies.
- **Ironclad**: A type of warship clad in iron or steel armor, which gave the Brazilian navy a decisive technological advantage.
- **Humaitá**: A heavily fortified Paraguayan position on the Paraguay River, whose siege and capture was a turning point in the war.
- **Caudillo**: A military and political leader with strong personal power, a common figure in 19th-century Latin America.
- **Gran Chaco**: A vast, sparsely populated region west of the Paraguay River, a major area of territorial dispute after the war.
- **Vivandeiras**: Women, often Afro-Brazilian, who accompanied Brazilian armies to perform logistical tasks like cooking and laundry.
- **Zuavos**: Black-only military units created in the Brazilian army at the start of the war.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Causes of the War**: Historians debate whether the war was primarily caused by López's expansionist ambitions, colonial border disputes, Brazilian and Argentine interference in Uruguay, or a combination.
- **Casualty Figures**: The exact death toll is fiercely disputed. Estimates for Paraguay's pre-war population range from 300,000 to 1.3 million, leading to widely varying casualty estimates from 7% to over 60% of the population.
- **Genocide Claim**: Some revisionist historians and a 2022 Mercosur Parliament sub-commission argue the mass death constitutes genocide, a characterization contested by other scholars.
- **British Influence**: A popular theory claims British economic interests instigated the war. Most historians find little evidence, noting the British government opposed the conflict.

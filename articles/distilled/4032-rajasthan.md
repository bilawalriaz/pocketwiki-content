# Rajasthan

## Overview
Rajasthan, meaning "Land of Kings," is India's largest state by area, located in the northwest. It is defined by the Thar Desert and the Aravalli Range, and its history is dominated by the resistance of Rajput kingdoms to successive foreign invasions, from Arab and Turkic forces to the Mughal Empire. The modern state was formed in 1949 by merging former princely states into the Indian Union.

## Timeline
- **c. 3000–1500 BCE** — Settlement at Balathal contemporary with the Harappan civilisation.
- **405–35 BCE** — Western Kshatrapas (Saka rulers) establish the Saka era in the region.
- **8th–11th century** — Gurjara-Pratihara Empire acts as a barrier to Arab invaders.
- **1192** — Muhammad Ghori defeats Prithviraj Chauhan at the Second Battle of Tarain.
- **1526–1707** — Period of Mughal dominance; Rajput states like Mewar resist under rulers like Maharana Pratap.
- **18th century** — Decline of Mughal power leads to Maratha influence in Rajputana.
- **Early 19th century** — Rajput kingdoms sign treaties accepting British suzerainty.
- **30 March 1949** — State of Rajasthan formed by merging Rajputana Agency states into the Indian Union.
- **1973** — Ranthambore National Park becomes part of Project Tiger.
- **2015** — Jaipur Metro becomes operational.
- **2019** — Rajasthan achieves 100% electricity connection for its population.

## Body

### Geography and Climate
Rajasthan is dominated by two major features: the Thar Desert in the northwest and the Aravalli Range, which runs southwest to northeast for over 850 km. The Aravallis divide the state, leaving the northwest arid and the southeast more fertile and hilly. The Thar Desert, extending into Pakistan, is a rain shadow region with extreme temperatures. Major rivers include the Chambal and Banas in the east, and the saline Luni in the west.

### Ancient and Medieval History
The region has roots in the Vedic and Indus Valley civilisations, with sites like Kalibangan. Rajput clans entered India in the first millennium CE, establishing kingdoms. The Gurjara-Pratihara Empire (8th–11th century) resisted Arab invasions. From the 12th century, Rajput states, notably Mewar, fought against Turkic and Mughal invasions. Leaders like Rana Sanga and Maharana Pratap became symbols of resistance. After Mughal decline, Maratha influence grew until British paramountcy was established in the 19th century.

### Formation and Administration
The modern state was created on 30 March 1949, merging nineteen princely states, three chiefships, and the British district of Ajmer-Merwara. It is governed by a parliamentary system with a bicameral legislature. The state is divided into 7 divisions and 41 districts, administered by the Indian Administrative Service. The High Court has its principal seat in Jodhpur.

### Economy
Rajasthan's economy is primarily agricultural, producing wheat, barley, pulses, and oilseeds. It is India's largest wool producer and a major source of minerals, including salt, copper, zinc, and marble. The state is a leader in renewable energy, particularly solar power, with the Bhadla Solar Park being a world-leading photovoltaic cluster. Tourism is a significant and growing industry.

### Demographics and Culture
As of the 2011 Census, Rajasthan had a population of over 68 million, with a sex ratio of 928 women per 1000 men. Hindi is the official language, but Rajasthani languages like Marwari, Mewari, and Dhundari are widely spoken. The population is predominantly Hindu (88.49%). Culture is marked by distinct cuisine (e.g., dal-baati-churma), folk music and dance (e.g., Ghoomar, Kalbelia), and vibrant arts like block printing and blue pottery.

## Terms
- **Rajputana:** The historical name for the region, used by the British, comprising the Rajput princely states.
- **Thar Desert:** Also known as the Great Indian Desert, a large arid region in northwestern India and southeastern Pakistan.
- **Aravalli Range:** One of the oldest mountain ranges in India, running through Rajasthan and acting as a climatic divide.
- **Gurjara-Pratihara:** A medieval Indian dynasty that ruled much of northern India and resisted Arab invasions.
- **Mewar:** A historic Rajput kingdom in southern Rajasthan, known for its resistance to Mughal rule.
- **Princely State:** A territory in British India under the nominal control of an Indian ruler, not directly administered by the British.
- **Project Tiger:** A conservation programme launched in 1973 to protect Bengal tigers in India.
- **Dal-baati-churma:** A traditional Rajasthani meal consisting of lentils, baked wheat balls, and a sweet crumbled wheat dish.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The exact population percentages of various communities (e.g., Brahmins, Jats, Gurjars, Meenas) are debated, with different reports providing varying figures.
- The literacy rate, while improving significantly, remains below the national average, and the low female literacy rate is a persistent issue.
- The historical extent and influence of certain ancient kingdoms, like the Matsya kingdom of the Vedic period, are subjects of scholarly interpretation.

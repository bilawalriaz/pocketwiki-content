# Montevideo

## Overview
Montevideo is the capital and largest city of Uruguay, situated on the northeastern bank of the Río de la Plata. It serves as the country's political, economic, and cultural hub, hosting the administrative headquarters of major Latin American trade blocs like Mercosur. The city is renowned for its high quality of life, historic architecture, and vibrant cultural scene, including being the birthplace of tango.

## Body

### Foundation and Early History
The site was first fortified by the Portuguese in 1723, but Spanish forces under Bruno Mauricio de Zabala expelled them in 1724 as part of the Spanish-Portuguese dispute over the platine region. The city was populated by families from Buenos Aires and the Canary Islands. A 1724 census recorded over 100 families of Galician and Canary Islands origin, more than 1000 indigenous people (mostly Guaraní), and some enslaved people of Bantu origin. Montevideo became the main port of the Viceroyalty of the Río de la Plata after 1776, leading to commercial rivalry with Buenos Aires.

### 19th Century: Conflict and Growth
The city was briefly occupied by British forces in 1807 before being recaptured by Spanish criollos. It became a stronghold during the May Revolution of 1810 and subsequent independence movements. After Uruguay's independence in 1828, Montevideo was named the capital. The 1830s–1850s were dominated by civil war between the Blanco and Colorado parties, culminating in the eight-year siege (1843–1851) where the city was supplied by sea with British and French support. Post-war, the city expanded with new infrastructure, including the first public sanitation (1854–1861), the Teatro Solís (1856), and the first railway line (1869).

### 20th Century to Present
Early 20th-century European immigration (notably Spanish and Italian) fueled rapid growth; by 1908, 30% of the population was foreign-born. The city hosted the 1930 FIFA World Cup at the Estadio Centenario. During World War II, the German cruiser *Admiral Graf Spee* was scuttled in Montevideo's port (1939). The mid-20th century brought economic stagnation and political violence, including the Tupamaros guerrilla movement and the 1973–1985 dictatorship. Democracy returned in 1985, and the city has since seen economic modernization, including the construction of the World Trade Center (1998) and the Telecommunications Tower (2000). It consistently ranks as the top city for quality of life in Latin America.

### Geography and Climate
Montevideo lies on the Río de la Plata, with the Bay of Montevideo forming a natural harbor. The city has an average elevation of 43 meters, with the highest point being the Cerro de Montevideo (134 m). It has a humid subtropical climate (Köppen Cfa) with four distinct seasons: cool winters, warm summers, mild autumns, and volatile springs. Annual rainfall is about 950 mm. Snowfall is extremely rare, with only four recorded instances, the last in 1930.

### Administration and Demographics
The city is divided into 8 political municipalities and 62 barrios (neighborhoods). As of the 2023 census, the city proper had a population of 1,287,452, making up 36.8% of Uruguay's total. The population is predominantly White (87.1%), with significant Black (10.7%) and Mixed (4.6%) communities. Christianity is the largest religion, with Catholicism as the main denomination.

### Economy and Infrastructure
Montevideo is Uruguay's economic center, home to most major businesses and state-owned companies. The Port of Montevideo is a major South American port. The city has a strong banking sector, historically dubbed "the Switzerland of America." Key infrastructure includes the Carrasco International Airport and the Tres Cruces bus terminal. The Rambla, a 27-km coastal avenue, is a central recreational space.

### Culture and Landmarks
The city is a UNESCO Creative City for Literature. Its architecture ranges from colonial buildings in Ciudad Vieja to modern skyscrapers. Major landmarks include the Palacio Salvo, the Estadio Centenario, and the Rambla. Cultural life is vibrant, with tango, candombe, and murga as key musical forms. The Montevideo Carnival is a major annual festival. The city has numerous museums, theaters, and parks like Parque Batlle and Parque Prado.

## Terms
- **Barrio**: A neighborhood or ward; Montevideo is divided into 62 barrios.
- **Blancos and Colorados**: The two traditional political parties of Uruguay, originating from the 19th-century civil wars.
- **Candombe**: A style of music and dance of African origin, central to Montevideo's culture.
- **Ciudad Vieja**: The historic old city center, originally surrounded by a wall.
- **Guerra Grande**: The Great War (1839–1851), a civil war in Uruguay with international involvement.
- **Mercosur**: A South American trade bloc; its administrative headquarters are in Montevideo.
- **Rambla**: A coastal avenue or promenade; Montevideo's Rambla is a defining urban feature.
- **Tango**: A dance and musical genre that originated in the Río de la Plata region, including Montevideo.

## Debates and open questions
- The etymology of "Montevideo" is debated, with several competing theories (e.g., "Monte vide eu," "Montem vídeo," "Monte Vidi") and no conclusive evidence for any single origin.
- The exact location and identity of the "five mounts" preceding the Cerro de Montevideo in the "Monte-VI-D-E-O" theory remain unconfirmed.

# Punjab

## Overview
Punjab is a historical region in South Asia, straddling modern-day Pakistan and northwestern India. Defined by its five major rivers, it has been a cradle of ancient civilizations, a crossroads of empires, and a vital agricultural heartland. Its modern boundaries are a product of historical partitions, most notably the 1947 division of British India, which created distinct political and demographic landscapes on either side of the new border.

## Timeline
- **c. 3000 BCE** — Indus Valley Civilization flourishes in the region.
- **1500–500 BCE** — Indo-Aryan migrations occur; Vedic period begins.
- **326 BCE** — Alexander the Great invades, fighting King Porus at the Battle of the Hydaspes.
- **c. 320 BCE** — Chandragupta Maurya establishes the Mauryan Empire, incorporating Punjab.
- **c. 820–1022 CE** — Hindu Shahi dynasty rules western Punjab.
- **1186 CE** — Ghurid conquest of Lahore ends Ghaznavid rule.
- **1320 CE** — Tughlaq dynasty founded in Delhi, controlling Punjab.
- **1799–1849** — Sikh Empire, founded by Ranjit Singh, rules the Punjab.
- **1849** — British annex Punjab after the Anglo-Sikh Wars.
- **1919** — Jallianwala massacre in Amritsar fuels Indian independence movement.
- **1947** — Partition of British India divides Punjab into East (India) and West (Pakistan).
- **1966** — Indian Punjab is reorganized, creating the states of Punjab, Haryana, and Himachal Pradesh.

## Body

### Etymology and Geography
The name "Punjab" derives from Persian words meaning "Land of Five Waters," referring to the Jhelum, Chenab, Ravi, Sutlej, and Beas rivers. The region's geographical definition has shifted over time. Under British rule (1849–1947), the Punjab Province was a vast territory encompassing modern Indian states of Punjab, Haryana, Himachal Pradesh, Chandigarh, Delhi, and Pakistani regions including Punjab, Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, and Islamabad. The 1947 Partition created West and East Punjab, with the latter further divided in 1966.

### Ancient and Medieval History
Early urban societies like the Indus Valley Civilization gave way to Indo-Aryan migrations and the rise of kingdoms chronicled in epics like the Mahabharata. The region saw invasions by Alexander the Great (4th century BCE) and later became part of the Mauryan Empire. The medieval period saw rule by the Hindu Shahis, Ghaznavids, and Ghurids, followed by the Delhi Sultanate's Tughlaq and Sayyid dynasties. The Langah Sultanate also held power in Multan.

### Early Modern to Colonial Period
The Mughal Empire controlled Punjab from the 16th century until its decline in the 18th century, leading to the rise of the Sikh Confederacy and later the Sikh Empire under Maharajah Ranjit Singh (1799–1849). The British annexed the region after the Anglo-Sikh Wars. Under British rule, Punjab became a key colonial asset, with Lahore as a cultural center and Rawalpindi a military hub. The region was a significant source of manpower for World War I and a center of anti-colonial activity, including the 1919 Jallianwala massacre.

### Partition and Modern Demographics
The 1947 Partition caused massive communal violence and migration, resulting in a sharp demographic shift. West Punjab became almost exclusively Muslim, while East Punjab (now Indian Punjab) saw its Hindu and Sikh populations become majorities. Today, Pakistani Punjab is predominantly Sunni Muslim, while Indian Punjab is majority Sikh (57.7%) with a significant Hindu minority (38.5%). The Punjabi language is written in Gurmukhi script in India and Shahmukhi script in Pakistan.

### Economy
Punjab is an agricultural powerhouse, known as the "breadbasket" of both India and Pakistan. The Green Revolution (mid-1960s to mid-1970s) significantly boosted its output of wheat, rice, cotton, and sugarcane. Pakistani Punjab contributes over 50% of Pakistan's GDP, while Indian Punjab is one of India's wealthier states. Agriculture remains a primary employer in both regions.

## Terms
*   **Punjab:** A historical region in South Asia, meaning "Land of Five Waters."
*   **Indus Valley Civilization:** An ancient Bronze Age civilization (c. 3300–1300 BCE) in the northwestern regions of South Asia.
*   **Indo-Aryan migrations:** The movement of Indo-Iranian-speaking peoples into the Indian subcontinent, beginning c. 1500 BCE.
*   **Mahajanapadas:** Sixteen great kingdoms and oligarchies of ancient India, prominent from the sixth to fourth centuries BCE.
*   **Mughal Empire:** A Muslim empire in the Indian subcontinent (1526–1857).
*   **Sikh Empire:** A Sikh sovereign state (1799–1849) founded by Maharajah Ranjit Singh.
*   **Partition of 1947:** The division of British India into the independent dominions of India and Pakistan, which split Punjab.
*   **Green Revolution:** A period of technology transfer and agricultural transformation (mid-1960s to mid-1970s) that increased crop yields.
*   **Gurmukhi:** The script used to write the Punjabi language in India.
*   **Shahmukhi:** The script used to write the Punjabi language in Pakistan.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   The precise historical and ethnographic boundaries of the "Punjab region" are imprecise and have changed over time.
*   The exact site of the Battle of the Hydaspes (326 BCE) between Alexander the Great and Porus remains unknown.
*   The early life and exact accession date of Chandragupta Maurya are unclear, with conflicting accounts from Greek and Indian sources.
*   The demographic impact and scale of casualties during the 1947 Partition are subjects of ongoing historical study.

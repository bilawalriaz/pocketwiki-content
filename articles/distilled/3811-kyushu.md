# Kyushu

## Overview
Kyushu is Japan's third-largest and most southerly main island, historically serving as the nation's gateway to the Asian continent. Its name derives from its nine ancient provinces, and it is characterized by a unique cultural identity with strong historical ties to the Ryukyu Islands (Okinawa). Today, it is a major economic region with a population of over 12 million, known for its volcanic geography, distinct cuisine, and significant industrial output.

## Timeline
- **c. 3000 BC** — Theory of an independent, southern-influenced dynasty and culture on Kyushu.
- **8th century** — Dazaifu established as a special administrative center in the Taihō Code reforms.
- **1993** — Yakushima registered as a World Natural Heritage site.
- **2000** — Gusuku Sites and Related Properties of the Kingdom of Ryukyu registered as a World Heritage site.
- **2015** — Sites of Japan's Meiji Industrial Revolution registered as a World Heritage site.
- **2017** — Sacred Island of Okinoshima and Associated Sites registered as a World Heritage site.
- **2018** — Hidden Christian Sites in the Nagasaki Region registered as a World Heritage site.
- **2021** — Amami-Ōshima, Tokunoshima, northern Okinawa, and Iriomote Islands registered as World Natural Heritage sites.
- **September 2022** — Shin-Kanmon Tunnel Shinkansen route to Nagasaki opens.

## Body

### Geography and Climate
Kyushu is a mountainous island with significant tectonic activity, hosting Japan's largest active volcano, Mount Aso (1,591 m), and numerous hot springs, most famously in Beppu. Its highest point is Mount Kujū at 1,791 m. The island is separated from Honshu by the Kanmon Straits. The climate varies, with subtropical conditions in the south, particularly in Miyazaki and Kagoshima Prefectures.

### Population and Politics
The population is concentrated in the northwest, in cities like Fukuoka and Kitakyushu, with a corridor extending southwest. The eastern seaboard is sparsely populated. Politically, the region is a stronghold of the Liberal Democratic Party. While the population of the Ryukyu Islands (Okinawa and Kagoshima) has declined since 2000, the overall regional population decline is mild due to relatively high birth rates among Ryukyuans.

### Economy
Kyushu's economy accounts for about 10% of Japan's total output, making it the country's fourth-largest economic region. It has a balanced industrial structure, with major agricultural output (20% of national total) and key manufacturing sectors in automobiles and semiconductors. The region has strong economic ties to Asia, with ports like Hakata and Kitakyushu serving as major logistics nodes. It is also noted for porcelain production (Arita, Imari, Satsuma, Karatsu) and hosts the Tanegashima Space Center.

### Transportation
Kyushu is linked to Honshu by the Kanmon Railway Tunnel and the Shin-Kanmon Tunnel for the Shinkansen, as well as the Kanmon Bridge and roadway tunnel. The Kyushu Shinkansen connects major cities like Fukuoka and Kagoshima. Fukuoka Airport is the main international and domestic hub, supplemented by several smaller regional airports. Ferry services connect the island to Honshu and Shikoku.

### Culture and Society
Kyushu maintains deep cultural and economic ties with Okinawa, evident in shared musical scales, cuisine, and language. The traditional gottan instrument reflects this hybrid identity, resembling the Okinawan sanshin. People from Kyushu are stereotyped as conservative and strong-willed ("Kyushu danji"). Cuisine varies by region but is characterized by fresh ingredients and distinct seasonings like sweet soy sauce and barley miso.

## Terms
- **Saikaidō**: The historical regional name for Kyushu and its surrounding islands, meaning "West Sea Circuit."
- **Dazaifu**: A special administrative center established in the 8th century to govern the Kyushu region.
- **Kanmon Straits**: The body of water separating Kyushu from Honshu.
- **Ryukyu Islands**: An archipelago including Okinawa, with which Kyushu has ancient cultural and economic ties.
- **Gottan**: A traditional Kyushu stringed instrument, considered a midpoint between the shamisen (mainland) and sanshin (Okinawa).
- **Kyushu danji**: A cultural stereotype describing men from Kyushu as wild, strong, and conservative.
- **Hakata-ori**: A traditional woven textile from Fukuoka.
- **Satsuma Kiriko**: A type of cut glass from Kagoshima.
- **Tanegashima Space Center**: Japan's largest rocket-launch complex, located on Tanegashima island.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The theory that ancient Kyushu was home to an independent dynasty with a culture distinct from Honshu is presented as a theory, not established fact.
- The precise nature and extent of cultural exchange between Kyushu and the Ryukyu Islands throughout history is a subject of ongoing scholarly interest, as evidenced by the hybrid characteristics of instruments like the gottan.

# Kingdom of Castile

## Overview
The Kingdom of Castile was a medieval Iberian polity that originated as a frontier county of the Kingdom of León. It evolved into a major independent kingdom, played a central role in the Reconquista (the Christian reconquest of the Iberian Peninsula from Muslim rule), and through unions with León and later Aragon, formed the core of what would become Spain. Its history is defined by cycles of separation and union with León, extensive southern conquests, and its role as a conduit for cultural exchange between Christian Europe and the Islamic world.

## Timeline
- **c. 800** — First reference to the name "Castile" in a document from the Kingdom of Asturias.
- **850** — Rodrigo becomes the first count of a wider, united Castile under the Kings of Asturias.
- **931** — Count Fernán González reunifies Castile and achieves autonomous status from the Kingdom of León.
- **1035** — Following the death of Sancho III of Navarre, Ferdinand I becomes Count of Castile and later King of León and Castile.
- **1065** — Death of Ferdinand I; Castile is separated from León and becomes an independent kingdom under Sancho II.
- **1072** — Assassination of Sancho II leads to the second union of Castile and León under Alfonso VI.
- **1085** — Castile conquers the major cultural center of Toledo from Muslim rule.
- **1230** — Ferdinand III definitively unites the kingdoms of Castile and León, forming the Crown of Castile.
- **1469** — Marriage of Ferdinand II of Aragon and Isabella I of Castile begins the union of the two crowns.
- **1492** — The Catholic Monarchs conquer Granada, ending Muslim rule in Iberia and completing the Reconquista.

## Body

### Origins and Autonomy (9th–11th Centuries)
Castile began as a march, or frontier territory, on the eastern edge of the Kingdom of Asturias (later León), protected by castles (*castra*). Its name reflects this defensive origin. The County of Castile was repopulated by settlers from Cantabria, Asturias, and the Basque region. In 931, Count Fernán González reunified the county and secured its autonomy from León, establishing a hereditary rule that allowed the county to grow in power.

### Expansion and Union with León (11th–12th Centuries)
The 11th century saw Castile become a kingdom and engage in a complex, fluctuating union with León. After a brief separation in 1065, the two kingdoms were reunited in 1072 under Alfonso VI. This period also marked Castile's role as a cultural bridge. The 1085 conquest of Toledo, a major center of learning, facilitated the "School of Toledo," a translation movement that transmitted Greek and Arabic scientific and philosophical works to Latin Europe, sparking intellectual advances.

### Definitive Union and the Crown of Castile (13th Century)
The rivalry between Castile and León ended in 1230 when Ferdinand III inherited both kingdoms, creating a permanent union. This merged realm, known as the Crown of Castile, incorporated Castile, León, and territories conquered from Muslim taifas (principalities), such as Córdoba, Seville, and Murcia. This consolidation created a powerful Christian state dominating central and southern Iberia.

### The Catholic Monarchs and the Foundation of Spain (15th–16th Centuries)
The House of Trastámara ruled Castile from 1369. The 1469 marriage of Isabella I of Castile and Ferdinand II of Aragon united the two most powerful Iberian kingdoms in a personal union. As the Catholic Monarchs, they centralized power, completed the Reconquista with the conquest of Granada in 1492, and initiated overseas expansion. Their union laid the foundation for the political entity of Spain, which was fully realized under their grandson, Charles I.

## Terms
- **Reconquista**: The centuries-long period of Christian reconquest of the Iberian Peninsula from Muslim rule.
- **County of Castile**: The 9th-century frontier lordship that was the precursor to the Kingdom of Castile.
- **Crown of Castile**: The composite monarchy formed by the permanent union of the kingdoms of Castile and León, along with their conquered territories.
- **Taifa**: An independent Muslim-ruled principality in the Iberian Peninsula, especially after the collapse of central caliphal authority.
- **Cortes**: The medieval parliaments or representative assemblies of the kingdoms of Castile and León.
- **Mozarabic rite**: The traditional Christian liturgy of the Iberian Peninsula, replaced by the Roman rite in Castile in 1080.
- **School of Toledo**: A 12th-century translation center where Arabic and Greek texts were translated into Latin, transmitting classical knowledge to Europe.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The extent of translation activity and the preservation of Greek knowledge during the early Umayyad rule in Al-Andalus is debated, with a minority view suggesting it was more widespread than commonly thought.
- The precise origins and early settlement patterns of the County of Castile involve complex migrations from various northern Iberian regions.

# Second Sino-Japanese War

## Overview
The Second Sino-Japanese War (1937–1945) was a major conflict between the Republic of China and the Empire of Japan, often considered the start of World War II in Asia. It was the largest Asian war of the 20th century, resulting in immense Chinese civilian casualties and reshaping East Asian geopolitics, ultimately leading to the Chinese Civil War's resumption and the creation of the United Nations.

## Timeline
- **18 Sep 1931** — Japanese staged the Mukden Incident, invading Manchuria and establishing the puppet state of Manchukuo.
- **7 Jul 1937** — Marco Polo Bridge Incident near Beijing escalates into full-scale war.
- **Aug–Nov 1937** — Protracted Battle of Shanghai.
- **Dec 1937** — Japanese capture Nanjing, perpetrating the Nanjing Massacre.
- **Oct 1938** — Japanese capture Wuhan; Nationalist government relocates to Chongqing.
- **21 Aug 1937** — Sino-Soviet Non-Aggression Pact signed, leading to Soviet aid.
- **Apr 1941** — Soviet-Japanese Neutrality Pact halts Soviet aid to China.
- **Dec 1941** — Japan attacks Pearl Harbor; U.S. enters the war and increases aid to China.
- **1942–1945** — China fights alongside Allies in the Burma campaign.
- **1944** — Japan launches Operation Ichi-Go, its largest campaign in China.
- **2 Sep 1945** — Japan formally surrenders aboard the USS Missouri.

## Background and Pre-War Events
The war's roots lie in Japanese expansionism following its victory in the First Sino-Japanese War (1894–95). Japan gained Taiwan, influence in Korea, and rights in Manchuria. China's internal weakness during the Warlord Era (post-1916) and the ongoing Chinese Civil War (since 1927) between Chiang Kai-shek's Nationalists (KMT) and Mao Zedong's Communists (CCP) created a power vacuum. Japan exploited this, staging the 1931 Mukden Incident to invade Manchuria. From 1931–1937, sporadic clashes occurred while Chiang pursued a policy of "first internal pacification, then external resistance" against the Communists. The 1936 Xi'an Incident, where Chiang was kidnapped by his own generals, forced him to form a United Front with the CCP against Japan.

## Course of the War
Full-scale war began after the Marco Polo Bridge Incident (7 July 1937). Japan quickly captured Beijing and Tianjin. The Battle of Shanghai (Aug–Nov 1937) was a costly three-month stalemate before Japan took the city. The subsequent fall of Nanjing (Dec 1937) led to the Nanjing Massacre, where Japanese troops killed an estimated 200,000–300,000 civilians. The Nationalist government retreated to Chongqing. By 1938, after the Battle of Wuhan, the war reached a stalemate. Japan controlled major cities and coastal areas, while the Nationalists held the interior and the Communists waged guerrilla warfare from base areas like Shaanxi.

The war became part of WWII after Japan's 1941 attack on Pearl Harbor. The U.S. became China's main supporter via Lend-Lease, though supply lines like the Burma Road were tenuous. The Second United Front between KMT and CCP collapsed in 1941, with both sides focusing more on fighting each other. Japan implemented the brutal "Three Alls Policy" (kill all, loot all, burn all) in occupied areas, killing millions of civilians. In 1944, Japan's Operation Ichi-Go seized territory and airfields but failed to break Chinese resistance. China launched final offensives in 1945 before Japan's surrender following the atomic bombings and Soviet invasion of Manchuria.

## Tactics and Warfare
China employed "magnetic warfare" to lure Japanese into ambushes and used "dare to die" suicide squads against tanks. The CCP specialized in guerrilla warfare and sabotage. Japan frequently used chemical weapons (e.g., mustard gas) and biological warfare (e.g., Unit 731 spreading plague), violating international law. Aerial bombardment of Chinese cities like Chongqing was widespread.

## Casualties and Aftermath
The war caused catastrophic losses. Chinese deaths are estimated between 10–20 million, mostly civilians from massacre, famine, and disease. Japanese military deaths in China ranged from 400,000 to over 1 million. The war ended with Japan's surrender on 2 September 1945. China emerged as one of the "Big Four" Allied powers and a permanent UN Security Council member, regaining Taiwan. However, the weakened Nationalist government soon lost the resumed Chinese Civil War to the Communists, who proclaimed the People's Republic of China in 1949. The Nationalists retreated to Taiwan.

## Terms
- **United Front**: An alliance between the KMT and CCP to resist Japan, formed in 1937 after the Xi'an Incident.
- **Manchukuo**: The Japanese puppet state established in Manchuria in 1932 under the last Qing emperor, Puyi.
- **Three Alls Policy**: A Japanese scorched-earth strategy ("kill all, loot all, burn all") used in occupied China to suppress guerrilla activity.
- **Operation Ichi-Go**: Japan's largest 1944 campaign in China, aimed at securing railway lines and destroying Allied airfields.
- **Comfort Women**: Women, mostly Korean, forced into sexual slavery by the Japanese military.
- **Lend-Lease**: U.S. program providing military aid to Allied nations, including China after 1941.
- **Guerrilla Warfare**: Irregular warfare tactics, like sabotage and ambushes, used effectively by CCP forces behind Japanese lines.
- **Nanjing Massacre**: The mass murder and rape of civilians by Japanese troops after capturing Nanjing in December 1937.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **War's Start Date**: China officially recognizes 1931 (the "Fourteen Years' War") to emphasize early resistance, while the traditional view uses 1937.
- **Casualty Figures**: Estimates vary widely, especially for Chinese civilian deaths (10–20 million) and the exact number killed in the Nanjing Massacre.
- **Historical Revisionism**: Controversy persists in Japan over textbook depictions of war crimes like the Nanjing Massacre and comfort women, affecting Sino-Japanese relations.
- **CCP vs. KMT Contribution**: Debates continue over the relative military roles of the Nationalists (who fought major battles) and the Communists (who focused on guerrilla warfare and political expansion).

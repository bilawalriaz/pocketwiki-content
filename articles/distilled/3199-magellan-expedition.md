# Magellan expedition

## Overview
The Magellan-Elcano expedition (1519-1522) was the first voyage to circumnavigate the globe. Planned by Portuguese explorer Ferdinand Magellan and completed under Spanish navigator Juan Sebastián Elcano, its primary goal was to find a western maritime route to the Spice Islands (Moluccas). The expedition proved the Earth was round, revealed the vastness of the Pacific Ocean, and demonstrated that a westward sea route to Asia was possible, though it proved too long for commercial use.

## Timeline
- **22 March 1518** — King Charles I of Spain names Magellan and Rui Faleiro captains general.
- **20 September 1519** — The five-ship fleet departs Sanlúcar de Barrameda, Spain.
- **1 November 1520** — The fleet enters the strait later named the Strait of Magellan.
- **28 November 1520** — The fleet exits the strait into the Pacific Ocean, which Magellan names "Mar Pacifico."
- **6 March 1521** — The fleet reaches Guam in the Mariana Islands.
- **16 March 1521** — The fleet first sights the Philippines, landing on Homonhon.
- **31 March 1521** — The first Catholic Mass in the Philippines is held on Limasawa.
- **7 April 1521** — Rajah Humabon of Cebu and others are baptized.
- **27 April 1521** — Magellan is killed in the Battle of Mactan against Lapu-Lapu.
- **1 May 1521** — Most of the remaining Spanish officers are killed in a feast in Cebu.
- **8 November 1521** — The fleet reaches the Moluccas, anchoring at Tidore.
- **21 December 1521** — The *Victoria*, commanded by Elcano, departs the Moluccas for Spain via the Indian Ocean.
- **6 September 1522** — The *Victoria* arrives in Sanlúcar de Barrameda, completing the circumnavigation.

## Body

### Background and Funding
Following Columbus's voyages, Spain sought a western route to Asia after Portugal secured eastern routes via the 1494 Treaty of Tordesillas. Ferdinand Magellan, a Portuguese sailor with experience in the East, proposed a westward voyage to the Moluccas to King Charles I of Spain. The king funded the expedition, hoping to secure a spice trade route. The fleet, called the Armada del Maluco, consisted of five ships and about 270 men, with a diverse crew including Spaniards, Portuguese, and others.

### The Atlantic and South America
The fleet crossed the Atlantic and reached Brazil in December 1519. After sailing south along the coast, they mistakenly identified the Río de la Plata as a potential strait. Continuing south, they overwintered at Port St. Julian, where a major mutiny led by Juan de Cartagena was suppressed; Cartagena and another conspirator were marooned. The *Santiago* was lost in a storm while scouting. In October 1520, the fleet discovered the strait connecting the Atlantic and Pacific, which Magellan named the "All Saints' Channel." During the passage, the *San Antonio* deserted and returned to Spain.

### The Pacific and the Philippines
Magellan underestimated the Pacific's size, leading to a grueling 98-day crossing marked by starvation and scurvy. The fleet reached Guam in March 1521, then sailed to the Philippines. Magellan formed alliances with local leaders, and the first Catholic Mass was held. After converting many in Cebu, Magellan was killed leading a small force against Lapu-Lapu at Mactan. In a subsequent massacre in Cebu, most of the remaining officers were killed. The fleet, now reduced to two ships, eventually reached the Moluccas in November 1521.

### The Return and Completion
In the Moluccas, the crew loaded cloves. The *Trinidad* was damaged and attempted to return east, but was captured by the Portuguese. The *Victoria*, under Juan Sebastián Elcano, sailed west across the Indian Ocean, rounded the Cape of Good Hope, and returned to Spain on 6 September 1522. Only 18 of the original crew completed the circumnavigation on the *Victoria*. The expedition proved the Earth was round but revealed the Pacific was far larger than anticipated, making the western route commercially impractical.

## Terms
- **Circumnavigation**: The act of sailing or traveling all the way around an island, a continent, or the entire planet.
- **Treaty of Tordesillas (1494)**: An agreement between Spain and Portugal dividing newly discovered lands outside Europe between them along a meridian.
- **Moluccas**: A group of islands in eastern Indonesia, historically known as the Spice Islands for their valuable spice production.
- **Scurvy**: A disease caused by a deficiency of vitamin C, common on long sea voyages due to lack of fresh fruits and vegetables.
- **Carrack**: A large, multi-decked sailing ship used by European powers from the 15th to 17th centuries.
- **Strait of Magellan**: A navigable sea route at the southern tip of South America, connecting the Atlantic and Pacific Oceans.
- **Mar Pacifico**: Spanish for "Peaceful Sea," the name Magellan gave to the Pacific Ocean.
- **Adelantado**: A Spanish title granted to a governor or military commander of a frontier territory.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The exact reasons for the desertion of the *San Antonio* are disputed; its officers claimed they arrived early at a rendezvous, while others suggest a mutiny led by pilot Estêvão Gomes.
- The identity and fate of some crew members, such as the Sicilian master Salomon Antón executed for sodomy, are recorded with varying details in different sources.
- The precise location of the first Catholic Mass in the Philippines is debated, with Limasawa traditionally cited but some historians proposing other islands.

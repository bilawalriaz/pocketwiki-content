# Crimean Khanate

## Overview
The Crimean Khanate was a Crimean Tatar state that existed from 1441 to 1783 on the Crimean Peninsula and adjacent steppes. It was a successor state to the Golden Horde, a major power in Eastern Europe and the Black Sea region, and its history is defined by its complex relationships with the Ottoman Empire, Poland-Lithuania, and the rising Russian Empire. Its economy was heavily reliant on the slave trade, and its eventual annexation by Russia marked a major shift in the regional balance of power.

## Timeline
- **1441** — Hacı I Giray establishes the Crimean Khanate.
- **1475** — Ottoman Empire conquers Genoese colonies and Theodoro, making the Khanate a protectorate.
- **1502** — Meñli I Giray defeats the last khan of the Great Horde, ending its claims on Crimea.
- **1571** — Devlet I Giray raids and burns Moscow.
- **1572** — Crimean forces suffer a catastrophic defeat at the Battle of Molodi, losing access to the Volga.
- **1699** — Treaty of Karlowitz ends the era of major Crimean slave raids into Russia and Ukraine.
- **1736-1739** — Russo-Turkish War; Russian forces under Münnich penetrate and devastate the Crimean Peninsula.
- **1774** — Treaty of Küçük Kaynarca makes the Khanate independent from the Ottoman Empire.
- **1783** — Russian Empire annexes the Crimean Khanate, violating the 1774 treaty.

## Body

### Establishment and Ottoman Suzerainty
The Khanate originated in the early 15th century when clans of the Golden Horde settled in Crimea and invited the Genghisid prince Hacı I Giray to rule. After a war for independence from the Horde (1420–1441), he established the state. In 1475, the Ottoman Empire conquered the Genoese and Greek coastal cities, making the Khanate an Ottoman protectorate. The Ottoman Sultan gained veto power over the selection of Crimean khans, but the khans retained significant autonomy in the steppes, minting coins and conducting foreign policy. The Crimean cavalry became vital to Ottoman military campaigns.

### Economy and the Slave Trade
The slave trade was the backbone of the Crimean economy. The Khanate conducted frequent raids, known as *sefers* (official campaigns) or *çapuls* (private raids), into Poland-Lithuania, Muscovy, and the Danubian principalities to capture slaves. These were exported primarily to the Ottoman Empire through ports like Caffa. Historians estimate that between 1500 and 1700, about 2 million slaves were exported from Russia and Poland-Lithuania. The raids caused immense suffering and deep-seated hatred for the Khanate among its northern neighbors.

### Decline and Russian Annexation
The Khanate's decline was linked to the weakening of the Ottoman Empire and the growing power of Russia. After the Treaty of Karlowitz (1699) outlawed major raids, the Khanate lost a key source of income and military prestige. Internal conflicts weakened the Giray dynasty's support among the noble clans. Following the Russo-Turkish War (1768–1774), the Treaty of Küçük Kaynarca made the Khanate independent from the Ottomans but under Russian influence. In 1783, Empress Catherine II intervened in a civil war and annexed the entire territory, violating the treaty. The last khan, Şahin Giray, was later executed by the Ottomans.

### Government and Society
The Khanate was ruled by the Giray dynasty, who traced their lineage to Genghis Khan. The khan governed with the participation of the *Qaraçı Beys*, leaders of the major noble clans. Internally, territory was divided among beys, with peasants working the land in common and paying a tax of one-tenth of produce. The legal system blended Tatar, Islamic, and Ottoman law. Non-Muslim minorities, including Greeks, Armenians, and Jews, lived in separate communities under the *millet* system, with their own courts but subject to extra taxes.

## Terms
- **Giray dynasty**: The ruling family of the Crimean Khanate, claiming descent from Genghis Khan.
- **Qaraçı Beys**: Leaders of the major noble clans (e.g., Şirin, Barın) who shared power with the khan.
- **Sefer**: An officially declared military campaign led by the khan.
- **Çapul**: A raid undertaken by groups of noblemen, sometimes illegally.
- **Millet system**: An Ottoman system granting non-Muslim religious communities autonomy in their own legal and religious affairs.
- **Vakif**: Charitable endowments, often of land, whose revenues supported religious and social institutions.
- **Kalga**: The second-highest official in the Khanate, usually the khan's heir, administering the eastern peninsula.
- **Nureddin**: The third-highest official, administering the western peninsula.

## Debates and Open questions
The exact nature of the Khanate's relationship with the Ottoman Empire is debated. While it was a protectorate after 1475, the khans retained significant sovereignty in the steppes, leading to questions about whether it was a true vassal or a semi-independent ally. The scale and impact of the slave trade are also subjects of historical analysis, with estimates of the number of captives varying and ongoing discussion about its long-term demographic and social effects on the region.

# Woody Guthrie

## Overview
Woodrow Wilson "Woody" Guthrie (1912–1967) was a seminal American folk singer and songwriter whose work championed socialism, anti-fascism, and the struggles of the working class. He is a foundational figure in American folk music, influencing generations of artists from Bob Dylan to Bruce Springsteen, and his song "This Land Is Your Land" remains a cultural anthem.

- **1929** — Moves to Pampa, Texas, after his mother is institutionalized.
- **c. 1935** — Migrates to California during the Dust Bowl.
- **1940** — Writes "This Land Is Your Land" in New York City.
- **1941** — Writes 26 songs for the Bonneville Power Administration documentary project.

- **1943** — Publishes autobiography *Bound for Glory*.
- **1952** — Diagnosed with Huntington's disease.
- **October 3, 1967** — Dies of complications from Huntington's disease in New York.

## Body

### Early Life and the Dust Bowl
Born to middle-class parents in Oklahoma, Guthrie's early life was marked by family tragedy, including his mother's institutionalization for Huntington's disease. He left school and, after his mother's death, moved to Pampa, Texas, in 1929. The onset of the Dust Bowl in the 1930s prompted Guthrie to leave his wife and children to join the mass migration of "Okies" to California seeking work.

### Rise to Fame and Political Awakening
In Los Angeles, Guthrie gained fame on radio station KFVD. He was introduced to socialist and communist circles by newscaster Ed Robbin, becoming a "fellow traveler" who wrote a column for the communist newspaper *People's World*. After being fired from KFVD due to his political leanings following the Molotov–Ribbentrop Pact, he moved to New York City in 1940. There, he recorded his seminal album *Dust Bowl Ballads* and wrote "This Land Is Your Land" as a response to Irving Berlin's "God Bless America."

### New York, the Almanac Singers, and World War II
In New York, Guthrie was embraced by the folk community and collaborated with Pete Seeger. He became a core member of the Almanac Singers, a folk-protest group that initially wrote "peace" songs during the Nazi-Soviet Pact but shifted to anti-fascist material after Hitler's invasion of the USSR. During World War II, Guthrie served in the U.S. Merchant Marine, surviving a torpedo attack, before being drafted into the Army. He later married his second wife, Marjorie Mazia, and they lived in Brooklyn, where he was highly productive, writing his autobiography *Bound for Glory* and numerous songs.

### Later Life and Legacy
Guthrie's health declined in the late 1940s due to Huntington's disease, a genetic disorder inherited from his mother. His erratic behavior led to divorce from Marjorie, though she later cared for him. He was hospitalized from 1956 until his death in 1967. His work experienced a major revival during the American folk revival of the late 1950s and 1960s, inspiring artists like Bob Dylan, who visited him in the hospital. His unrecorded lyrics have been set to music by artists like Billy Bragg and Wilco, and his archives are housed at the Woody Guthrie Center in Tulsa, Oklahoma.

## Terms
*   **Dust Bowl:** A period of severe dust storms in the 1930s that damaged the ecology and agriculture of the American and Canadian prairies, causing mass migration.
*   **Fellow Traveler:** A person who sympathizes with the platform of a political party (often the Communist Party) but is not a formal member.
*   **Almanac Singers:** A folk music group co-founded by Guthrie and Pete Seeger in the 1940s, known for its protest songs and communal living.
*   **Huntington's Disease:** A fatal, inherited neurodegenerative disorder that causes the progressive breakdown of nerve cells in the brain, leading to physical and mental deterioration.
*   **Hootenanny:** A term for a folk music gathering or jam session, popularized by Guthrie and Seeger.
*   **Molotov–Ribbentrop Pact:** A 1939 non-aggression treaty between Nazi Germany and the Soviet Union, which influenced the political stance of Guthrie and the Almanac Singers.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Communist Party Membership:** The extent of Guthrie's formal involvement with the Communist Party is debated. While he was closely associated and wrote for its publications, some sources claim he was a member, while others, including his own statements, suggest he was not.
*   **Political Evolution:** Scholars analyze how Guthrie's political views shifted, particularly his initial anti-war stance during the Nazi-Sovist Pact and his subsequent fervent anti-fascism after the German invasion of the USSR.

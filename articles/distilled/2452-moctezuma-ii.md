# Moctezuma II

## Overview
Moctezuma Xocoyotzin (c. 1466–1520), known as Moctezuma II, was the ninth emperor of the Aztec Empire. His reign (c. 1502–1520) marked the empire's greatest territorial extent and its first contact with Europeans, culminating in the Spanish conquest. His leadership and the subsequent collapse of the empire under his successors remain a pivotal and debated chapter in Mesoamerican history.

## Timeline
- **c. 1466** — Born, son of Emperor Axayácatl.
- **c. 1502/1503** — Crowned as *tlatoani* (ruler) of the Aztec Empire.
- **1505** — Severe drought and famine strike central Mexico.
- **1506** — Conquest of Zozollan; construction of the Chapultepec aqueduct begins.
- **c. 1507** — Major military campaigns in Oaxaca and Guerrero; conquest of Tototepec.
- **1508** — Rebellion in Atlixco is suppressed.
- **1510** — Widespread rebellions erupt across the empire.
- **1516** — Death of Nezahualpilli, ruler of Texcoco, triggers a succession crisis.
- **1518** — First reports of Spanish ships on the eastern coast.
- **1519** — Hernán Cortés and Spanish forces arrive; Moctezuma meets them in November and is later taken prisoner.
- **29 June 1520** — Moctezuma is killed during the early stages of the Spanish conquest.
- **1521** — The Aztec Empire falls to Spanish and Tlaxcalan forces.

## Body

### Reign and Internal Policy
Moctezuma centralized the empire by creating new provincial divisions and sending bureaucrats with military garrisons to enforce laws and collect taxes. His early reign was marked by a major drought (1505) and famine, which he addressed by increasing tributes and freeing enslaved children. He implemented elitist policies, widening the social divide between nobles (*pipiltin*) and commoners (*macehualtin*) by prohibiting commoners from serving in his palace or high government positions. These policies aimed to resolve conflicts between the nobility, merchants, and warrior class by consolidating power in his person.

### Construction and Expansion
Moctezuma built a vast palace (*tecpan*) in Tenochtitlan, featuring a large courtyard, guest rooms for allied rulers, and a meditation room painted black. He also established the *Totocalli* (House of Birds), a prestigious zoo with diverse animals. His military campaigns focused more on suppressing rebellions than territorial expansion, though he did conquer areas in Oaxaca, Guerrero, and the Chichimec region. He achieved approximately 43 victories in around 73 engagements, making him one of the most militarily active Aztec rulers.

### War with Tlaxcala and Huejotzingo
Major conflicts erupted with the rival states of Tlaxcala and Huejotzingo. A significant battle at Atlixco (c. 1503 or 1507) resulted in a humiliating defeat for the Aztecs, with heavy casualties on both sides. Subsequent wars weakened Huejotzingo, allowing Tlaxcala to become the empire's primary rival. These prolonged conflicts drained resources and created alliances that would later aid the Spanish conquest.

### Texcoco Crisis
The death of Texcoco's ruler Nezahualpilli in 1516 sparked a succession crisis. Moctezuma supported his nephew Cacamatzin, leading to a rebellion by another son, Ixtlilxochitl. The conflict was eventually settled by dividing the province, but the crisis resurfaced during the Spanish conquest when Moctezuma ordered Cacamatzin's arrest to prevent an attack on the Spaniards.

### Contact with the Spanish
In 1519, Moctezuma met Hernán Cortés and his forces, exchanging gifts. He was soon taken prisoner by the Spanish, possibly as a pretext after an attack by an Aztec tribute collector. Despite his captivity, he continued to wield some influence, protecting the Spaniards from internal threats. His death in June 1520 is disputed: Spanish accounts claim he was stoned by his own people, while indigenous sources state he was killed by the Spanish.

## Terms
*   **Tlatoani**: The Nahuatl term for the ruler or emperor of an Aztec city-state, meaning "speaker."
*   **Altepetl**: The basic political unit in Mesoamerica, often translated as "city-state."
*   **Pipiltin**: The hereditary nobility class in Aztec society.
*   **Macehualtin**: The commoner class in Aztec society.
*   **Calmecac**: Educational institutions for the Aztec nobility, focusing on religion, warfare, and leadership.
*   **Flower War**: Ritualized battles arranged between states for the purpose of capturing prisoners for sacrifice, not territorial gain.
*   **Cihuacoatl**: A high-ranking official in the Aztec government, akin to a prime minister or chief advisor.
*   **Tecpan**: The official palace or administrative center of an Aztec ruler.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Cause of Death**: Accounts differ on whether Moctezuma was killed by Spanish soldiers or by his own people during a siege.
*   **Role in the Conquest**: Scholars debate whether Moctezuma was a weak-willed leader who facilitated the Spanish conquest or a capable ruler facing an unprecedented crisis. Some sources portray him as a tyrant seeking absolute control.
*   **Interpretation of Speeches**: The meaning of Moctezuma's reported speech to Cortés—whether a genuine submission or a polite assertion of dominance—is contested, as it may reflect post-conquest mythmaking or cultural misinterpretation.
*   **Omens and Prophecy**: The significance of pre-conquest omens and the belief that the Aztecs saw the Spanish as gods (possibly linked to the Quetzalcoatl legend) is debated, with some historians viewing it as a post-conquest rationalization for defeat.

# Linus Torvalds

## Overview
Linus Torvalds is a Finnish software engineer best known as the creator and lead developer of the Linux kernel, the core of the Linux operating system, and the Git version control system. His work has been foundational to the open-source software movement, enabling the development of widely used operating systems and collaborative software projects.

## Timeline
- **28 December 1969** — Born in Helsinki, Finland.
- **1981** — Begins programming with a VIC-20 computer at age 11.
- **1988–1996** — Attends University of Helsinki, earning a master's degree in computer science.
- **5 January 1991** — Purchases an Intel 80386-based PC, enabling work on Linux.
- **Late 1991** — First Linux prototypes are publicly released online.
- **14 March 1994** — Linux kernel version 1.0 is released.
- **1997–2003** — Works at Transmeta in California.
- **3 April 2005** — Begins development of Git.
- **2012** — Receives the Millennium Technology Prize for creating Linux.
- **16 September 2018** — Apologizes for past behavior and announces a temporary break from kernel maintenance.

## Body

### Early Life and Education
Torvalds was born into a Swedish-speaking family in Finland. His interest in computers began in 1981 with a VIC-20, and he progressed to writing machine code and modifying his Sinclair QL's operating system. He studied computer science at the University of Helsinki, with his studies interrupted by mandatory military service in the Finnish Navy. His first exposure to Unix came in 1990 via a DEC MicroVAX, and he was inspired by Andrew Tanenbaum's book on MINIX.

### Creation of Linux
In January 1991, Torvalds purchased an Intel 80386 PC and began developing a kernel, inspired by MINIX. He released the first prototypes publicly in late 1991. A pivotal moment came in autumn 1991 when he attended a speech by Richard Stallman, leading him to switch the kernel's license to the GNU General Public License version 2 (GPLv2). This decision was crucial for Linux's adoption within the free software ecosystem. The kernel's name "Linux" was assigned by a friend, Ari Lemmke, who administered the FTP server hosting the code.

### Career and Authority
After working at Transmeta, Torvalds joined the Open Source Development Labs (later the Linux Foundation), which sponsors his full-time work on the kernel. While his direct code contributions have decreased over time, he retains final authority over which code is merged into the official kernel. He also created Git in 2005 after a dispute over version control software, and later developed Subsurface, a scuba dive logging application.

### Personal Philosophy and Conduct
Torvalds is a vocal advocate for open-source development but pragmatically uses the "best tool for the job." He is known for his blunt communication style on the Linux kernel mailing list, which has drawn criticism. In 2018, he took a temporary break from maintaining the kernel to reflect on his behavior, returning after releasing Linux 4.19. In 2024, he defended the exclusion of some Russian developers from kernel maintenance, citing compliance with government sanctions.

## Terms
- **Linux kernel**: The core component of the Linux operating system, created and maintained by Torvalds.
- **Open source**: A software development model where source code is made publicly available for anyone to inspect, modify, and distribute.
- **GPLv2**: The GNU General Public License version 2, a widely used free software license that guarantees end users the freedom to run, study, share, and modify the software.
- **Git**: A distributed version control system designed to handle everything from small to very large projects with speed and efficiency, created by Torvalds.
- **MINIX**: An educational, stripped-down Unix-like operating system created by Andrew Tanenbaum, which inspired Torvalds.
- **Kernel**: The core program of an operating system that manages hardware and software resources.
- **Version control**: A system that records changes to files over time, allowing recall of specific versions later.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Torvalds's leadership style**: His blunt and sometimes abrasive communication on public mailing lists has been a recurring point of criticism and debate regarding community governance and inclusivity.
- **The 2018 Code of Conduct**: The sudden replacement of the kernel's "Code of Conflict" with a new Code of Conduct sparked significant debate within the developer community about its implications for free speech and project management.
- **The 2024 maintainer exclusion**: The removal of some developers based on nationality or email address raised questions about the political neutrality of the project and the legal pressures facing open-source foundations.

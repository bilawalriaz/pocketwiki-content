# Indore

## Overview
Indore is the largest city and commercial capital of Madhya Pradesh, India. It evolved from a 16th-century trading hub into a major industrial, educational, and financial center, known for its cleanliness and rapid modern development.

## Timeline
- **465 CE** — Gupta inscription mentions Indore as "Indrapura" with a sun temple.
- **1715** — Marathas invade the region, demanding chauth (tax) from Mughal administrators.
- **1724** — Peshwa Baji Rao I campaigns in Malwa; Malhar Rao Holkar is left to oversee Indore.
- **1733** — Malhar Rao Holkar is formally granted the pargana (administrative unit) by the Peshwa.
- **1766** — Ahilyabai Holkar moves the state headquarters to Indore.
- **1818** — Holkars are defeated by the British; Indore becomes a princely state capital.
- **1870** — First municipality is constituted in Indore.
- **1956** — Indore is incorporated into the newly formed state of Madhya Pradesh.
- **2015** — Indore is declared a municipal corporation.
- **2025** — Estimated metropolitan population reaches 5.6 million.

## Body

### Etymology and Early History
The city's name is believed to derive from the Indreshwar Mahadev Temple, dedicated to Shiva. Gupta-era inscriptions from 465 CE refer to the area as "Indrapura," noting a sun temple endowed by King Skandagupta. During the Mughal period, the area was divided between the Ujjain and Mandu administrative units.

### Maratha and Holkar Rule
Maratha incursions began in 1715, with Peshwa Baji Rao I consolidating control by 1724. He left Malhar Rao Holkar in charge, and the Holkar dynasty became the region's rulers. Ahilyabai Holkar, who ruled from 1767, is remembered for a period of stability and temple building. The British defeated the Holkars in 1818, making Indore a princely state under British paramountcy. The city's modern infrastructure, including railways (1875) and electricity (1906), developed during the late Holkar period.

### Post-Independence and Modern Development
After Indian independence in 1947, Indore acceded to the Indian Union and became part of Madhya Pradesh in 1956. It has since transformed into the state's financial capital and a major industrial hub, particularly for pharmaceuticals and automotive manufacturing. The city is a leading educational center, hosting an IIT and an IIM, and is consistently ranked as India's cleanest city.

### Economy and Infrastructure
Indore's economy is driven by manufacturing, IT, and services. Major industrial areas include Pithampur and the Indore Special Economic Zone. The city has multiple IT parks and hosts companies like TCS and Infosys. Transport is served by Devi Ahilyabai Holkar International Airport, a major railway junction, and an extensive road network including National Highways 52 and 47. Public transport includes a city bus system, a BRTS (iBUS), and a developing metro.

### Demographics and Culture
As of the 2011 census, Indore city had a population of 1,994,397. Hindi is the primary language, with significant Marathi and Urdu-speaking communities. The city is noted for its vibrant street food culture, especially at Chappan Dukan and Sarafa Bazaar, and is considered one of India's most vegetarian cities. Cultural landmarks include the historic Rajwada and Lalbagh Palaces.

## Terms
- **Chauth**: A tax (25% of revenue) levied by Maratha rulers on conquered territories.
- **Pargana**: A historical administrative unit or district in India.
- **Princely State**: A territory under a native ruler, with varying degrees of autonomy, under British paramountcy.
- **BRTS**: Bus Rapid Transit System, a high-capacity bus-based public transport system.
- **Smart Cities Mission**: A flagship urban renewal program of the Government of India.
- **Swachh Survekshan**: An annual survey of cleanliness, hygiene, and sanitation in cities and towns across India.
- **Ramsar Site**: A wetland site designated to be of international importance under the Ramsar Convention.

## Debates and Open questions
- The exact origin of the city's name is debated, with theories linking it to the Indreshwar Temple or the older name "Indur."
- The implementation of a Police Commissionerate system in Indore has been proposed multiple times but remains unimplemented due to administrative tussles.
- The city's claim to being India's "most vegetarian city" is noted as debatable, though surveys indicate a high percentage of vegetarians.

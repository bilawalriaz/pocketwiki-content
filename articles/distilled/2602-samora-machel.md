# Samora Machel

## Overview
Samora Machel was a Mozambican revolutionary and the first President of Mozambique, serving from independence in 1975 until his death in a 1986 plane crash. A Marxist-Leninist, he led the country through its transition from Portuguese colonial rule, the establishment of a one-party socialist state, and a devastating civil war backed by apartheid South Africa and Rhodesia. His leadership was central to Mozambique's independence struggle and its early post-colonial identity.

- **Oct 1966** — Becomes head of FRELIMO's army after the death of its first commander.
- **1970** — Elected President of FRELIMO.
- **Apr 25, 1974** — Carnation Revolution in Portugal overthrows the colonial government.
- **Jun 25, 1975** — Proclaims independence and becomes the first President of Mozambique.

- **Oct 19, 1986** — Dies in a plane crash at Mbuzini, South Africa.

## Body

### Early Life and Independence Struggle
Born to a farming family under discriminatory Portuguese colonial rule, Machel trained as a nurse. He joined the anti-colonial movement after witnessing racial inequality and land appropriation. He fled to Tanzania in 1963 to join FRELIMO (Mozambique Liberation Front), where he received military training. He rose rapidly through the ranks, becoming a key commander in the war for independence that began in 1964. After FRELIMO's founder was assassinated in 1969, Machel was elected party president in 1970, solidifying a Marxist-Leninist ideological direction.

### Independence and Socialist State
Following the 1974 Portuguese coup, Machel skillfully navigated negotiations, insisting on full independence and dealing directly with the military faction in Lisbon. Mozambique became independent on June 25, 1975, with Machel as president. His government immediately nationalized land, health, and education institutions, and later rented housing, transforming urban demographics. In 1977, FRELIMO was reorganized as a Leninist vanguard party, establishing a one-party state. The regime introduced "reeducation centers" for political opponents and social deviants, which were marked by harsh conditions and deaths, though some emphasis on rehabilitation also existed.

### Civil War and Regional Conflict
Mozambique's strategic support for liberation movements, like Zimbabwe's ZANU, and its implementation of UN sanctions against white-minority Rhodesia, provoked retaliation. Rhodesia, and later apartheid South Africa, backed the rebel movement RENAMO, plunging Mozambique into a brutal civil war from 1977. Despite a 1984 non-aggression pact with South Africa (the Nkomati Accord), support for RENAMO continued. The war devastated the country's economy and infrastructure, forcing FRELIMO to adopt severe authoritarian measures, including the extension of the death penalty for economic crimes.

### Death and Legacy
Machel died on October 19, 1986, when his plane crashed in South Africa after a summit in Zambia. The official investigation attributed the crash to pilot error, but Mozambique and the Soviet Union disputed this, suggesting sabotage by a decoy navigation beacon. Theories involving South African or Soviet involvement persist. Machel is buried at Heroes' Square in Maputo. His legacy is complex: he is celebrated as a liberation hero and anti-apartheid figure, but also associated with the repressive aspects of the one-party state and the civil war's devastation. His widow, Graça Machel, later married Nelson Mandela.

## Terms
*   **FRELIMO:** Mozambique Liberation Front, the nationalist movement and political party that led the independence struggle and governed Mozambique after 1975.
*   **Marxism-Leninism:** A political ideology combining Karl Marx's theories of class struggle and socialism with Vladimir Lenin's theories on revolutionary vanguardism and the one-party state.
*   **RENAMO:** Mozambican National Resistance, the rebel movement that fought a civil war against the FRELIMO government from 1977, backed by Rhodesia and later apartheid South Africa.
*   **Nkomati Accord:** A 1984 non-aggression pact between Mozambique and South Africa, in which each promised to stop supporting rebel groups in the other's territory.
*   **Front Line States:** A coalition of African countries bordering South Africa that opposed apartheid and minority rule in southern Africa.
*   **Reeducation Centers:** Institutions established by the FRELIMO government to detain and "rehabilitate" political opponents, petty criminals, and those deemed anti-social.
*   **Carnation Revolution:** The largely bloodless 1974 military coup in Portugal that overthrew the authoritarian Estado Novo regime and led to the decolonization of its African territories.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Cause of the 1986 Plane Crash:** The official cause is pilot error, but alternative theories suggest sabotage by a false navigation beacon (possibly by South Africa) or a conspiracy involving the Soviet Union, which had grown wary of Machel's perceived shift toward the West.
*   **Machel's Authoritarian Legacy:** Scholars debate the extent to which Machel's "salvationist stance" and the creation of reeducation centers were a necessary response to instability versus a deliberate turn toward repression that violated his own promises of amnesty.
*   **Effectiveness of the Nkomati Accord:** The accord is widely seen as a failure, as South Africa continued to support RENAMO while Mozambique only partially expelled ANC members, raising questions about its strategic purpose and Machel's diplomatic leverage.

# Susan B. Anthony

## Overview
Susan B. Anthony (1820–1906) was a central figure in the American women's suffrage movement. As a tireless organizer and activist, she co-founded key suffrage organizations and was instrumental in the decades-long campaign that culminated in the 19th Amendment, which granted women the right to vote.

## Timeline
- **1820** — Born in Adams, Massachusetts.
- **1851** — Met Elizabeth Cady Stanton, beginning a lifelong partnership.
- **1852** — Co-founded the Women's State Temperance Society after being barred from speaking.
- **1863** — Co-founded the Women's Loyal National League, leading a massive petition drive for abolition.
- **1866** — Co-founded the American Equal Rights Association (AERA) for universal suffrage.
- **1868** — Co-founded the National Woman Suffrage Association (NWSA) after the AERA split.
- **1872** — Arrested and tried for voting illegally in Rochester, New York.
- **1878** — Arranged for the "Susan B. Anthony Amendment" to be introduced in Congress.
- **1890** — Merged the NWSA with the rival AWSA to form the National American Woman Suffrage Association (NAWSA).
- **1906** — Died in Rochester, New York.
- **1920** — The 19th Amendment, based on her proposed amendment, was ratified.

## Body

### Early Life and Influences
Born into a Quaker family committed to social reform, Anthony was influenced by her father's abolitionism and her family's belief in gender equality. After the Panic of 1837 disrupted her education, she worked as a teacher, where she was distressed by the unequal pay for women. In 1845, her family moved to Rochester, New York, becoming active in abolitionist circles and associating with reformers like Frederick Douglass.

### Partnership with Elizabeth Cady Stanton
In 1851, Anthony met Elizabeth Cady Stanton, forming a pivotal partnership. Their skills were complementary: Stanton was the intellectual and writer, while Anthony was the organizer and speaker. This collaboration made the New York women's rights movement highly effective. Their work began with temperance, but after Anthony was denied the right to speak at a conference, they founded the Women's State Temperance Society.

### Anti-Slavery and Civil War Activism
Anthony was a dedicated abolitionist, serving as a New York state agent for the American Anti-Slavery Society and aiding the Underground Railroad. During the Civil War, she and Stanton founded the Women's Loyal National League. Its petition drive, collecting nearly 400,000 signatures, was the largest in U.S. history at the time and significantly aided the passage of the 13th Amendment abolishing slavery.

### Post-War Campaigns and the Split in the Movement
After the war, Anthony and Stanton formed the AERA to campaign for equal rights for all, including women and African Americans. A major split occurred over the proposed 15th Amendment, which would enfranchise Black men but not women. Anthony and Stanton opposed it, arguing it created an "aristocracy of sex." This led to the 1869 formation of two rival groups: the NWSA (Anthony and Stanton) and the AWSA (Lucy Stone). The two merged in 1890 to form NAWSA, with Anthony as its effective leader.

### The Voting Trial and Constitutional Amendment
In 1872, Anthony was arrested for voting in a presidential election. Her trial, *United States v. Susan B. Anthony*, became a national cause. She was found guilty and fined $100, which she refused to pay. The trial highlighted the lack of legal suffrage for women. In 1878, she and Stanton arranged for a suffrage amendment to be introduced in Congress. It later became known as the Susan B. Anthony Amendment and was ratified as the 19th Amendment in 1920.

### International Work and Later Years
Anthony was a key figure in creating the International Council of Women (1888) and the International Woman Suffrage Alliance (1902). She co-authored the six-volume *History of Woman Suffrage* with Stanton and Matilda Joslyn Gage. In her later years, she remained the leader of NAWSA, training younger activists like Carrie Chapman Catt. Her 80th birthday was celebrated at the White House. She died in 1906, having never seen the national suffrage victory she worked for.

## Terms
- **Suffrage**: The right to vote in political elections.
- **Abolitionist**: A person who favored the abolition of slavery.
- **Temperance**: The movement advocating for moderation or complete abstinence from alcohol.
- **Feme covert/feme sole**: Legal terms for a married woman (covert) and an unmarried woman (sole), with the latter having more legal rights to sign contracts.
- **National Woman Suffrage Association (NWSA)**: The organization founded by Anthony and Stanton that focused on a federal suffrage amendment.
- **American Woman Suffrage Association (AWSA)**: The rival organization, led by Lucy Stone, that pursued a state-by-state strategy.
- **National American Woman Suffrage Association (NAWSA)**: The merged organization formed in 1890, which became the primary suffrage group.
- **19th Amendment**: The U.S. Constitution amendment ratified in 1920 that prohibits denying the right to vote based on sex.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **The Fifteenth Amendment Split**: The strategic disagreement over whether to support the 15th Amendment without a suffrage provision for women caused a bitter, decades-long rift in the women's movement.
- **Views on Abortion**: A posthumous dispute exists over Anthony's stance on abortion. Historians like Ann D. Gordon state she never voiced an opinion on the topic, contradicting later claims by some anti-abortion groups.
- **Relationship with Stanton**: While their partnership was foundational, their interests diverged later in life. Anthony became more conservative, forming alliances with groups like the WCTU, while Stanton grew more radical, as seen in her controversial work *The Woman's Bible*.

# Timbuktu

## Overview
Timbuktu is an ancient city in Mali, situated on the southern edge of the Sahara Desert. It rose to global prominence as a major medieval center of Islamic scholarship and trans-Saharan trade, particularly in gold and salt. Its golden age under the Mali and Songhai Empires established it as a legendary city of learning and wealth, a reputation that later fueled European myths of mystery and riches.

## Timeline
- **c. 5th century BC** — Iron Age settlement established near the modern city.
- **Early 12th century** — Timbuktu becomes a permanent settlement.
- **c. 1325** — Visit of Mansa Musa boosts the city's fame and trade.
- **Early 14th century** — Timbuktu becomes part of the Mali Empire.
- **1468** — The Songhai Empire absorbs Timbuktu.
- **1591** — Moroccan army defeats the Songhai and makes Timbuktu their capital.
- **1893** — French colonial forces take control of Timbuktu.
- **1960** — Mali gains independence; Timbuktu is part of the new republic.
- **1988** — Timbuktu's historic center is designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
- **2012** — Islamist rebels capture the city, destroying shrines and manuscripts.
- **2013** — French and Malian forces retake Timbuktu.
- **2023** — JNIM militants impose a blockade on the city.

## Body

### Rise and Golden Age
Timbuktu began as a seasonal settlement and was permanent by the early 12th century. Its strategic location at the edge of the Sahara made it a hub for trans-Saharan trade routes, dealing in salt, gold, and ivory. The city's fame and wealth soared after a visit by Mansa Musa around 1325. It became a major center of Islamic learning, attracting scholars from across the Muslim world. The Sankoré Madrasah and other institutions supported thousands of students, and an extensive book trade flourished. This scholarly golden age occurred under the Mali Empire and continued under the Songhai Empire.

### Decline and Colonial Era
Timbuktu's prominence waned after the Moroccan invasion of 1591. The new rulers executed or exiled many scholars, and competition from trans-Atlantic trade routes diminished its economic importance. The city was ruled by various groups until the French colonized the region in 1893. French control lasted until Mali's independence in 1960.

### Modern Challenges and Heritage
In recent decades, Timbuktu has faced severe threats. Islamist groups, including al-Qaeda allies, have attacked the city, destroying cultural sites and manuscripts. A 2023 blockade by JNIM has caused food shortages and displacement. Despite these challenges, efforts by local communities and international organizations aim to preserve its unique heritage, including hundreds of thousands of ancient manuscripts. The city's population has declined significantly from its medieval peak.

## Terms
- **Madrasah**: An Islamic educational institution, often centered around a mosque.
- **Trans-Saharan trade**: The historical network of trade routes crossing the Sahara Desert, linking sub-Saharan Africa with North Africa, the Mediterranean, and the Middle East.
- **Sankoré Madrasah**: A famous Islamic university and mosque in Timbuktu, central to the city's scholarly tradition.
- **Ahmed Baba Institute**: A major research library and manuscript collection in Timbuktu, named after a 16th-century scholar.
- **World Heritage Site**: A location designated by UNESCO as having special cultural or physical significance, warranting protection.
- **Harmattan**: A dry, dusty trade wind that blows from the Sahara towards the Gulf of Guinea, limiting visibility.
- **Azalai**: Traditional salt caravans that transported slabs of rock salt from Taoudenni to Timbuktu.
- **Koyra Chiini**: A Songhay language that is the primary language spoken in Timbuktu.

## Debates and open questions
- The exact etymology of the name "Timbuktu" is unclear, with multiple theories proposing Songhay, Berber, or other linguistic origins.
- Archaeological research has not yet found remains from the 11th or 12th century within the modern city limits, complicating the understanding of its early foundation.
- The current status and location of many of Timbuktu's ancient manuscripts remain uncertain following the 2012–2013 attacks and ongoing instability.

# Hussite Wars

## Overview
The Hussite Wars (1419–c. 1434) were a series of religious civil wars in the Kingdom of Bohemia, pitting Hussite reformers against the Catholic Church and Holy Roman Empire. Sparked by the execution of reformer Jan Hus, the conflict saw innovative Hussite armies repeatedly defeat multiple papal crusades. It ended with a compromise allowing Hussites to practice a variant rite, marking a significant early challenge to papal authority and a precursor to the Protestant Reformation.

## Timeline
- **1415** — Jan Hus executed for heresy at the Council of Constance.
- **1419** — First Defenestration of Prague triggers open revolt; King Wenceslaus IV dies.
- **1420** — First Anti-Hussite crusade fails; Hussites win at Battle of Vítkov Hill.
- **1421** — Hussites adopt the Four Articles of Prague; Jan Žižka begins using wagon forts.
- **1422** — Second crusade fails; Hussites win at Battle of Německý Brod.
- **1424** — Jan Žižka dies; Prokop the Great becomes Taborite leader.
- **1426** — Hussites defeat invaders at Battle of Aussig.
- **1427** — Fourth crusade fails; Hussites win at Battle of Tachov.
- **1431** — Fifth crusade fails; Hussites win at Battle of Domažlice.
- **1434** — Moderate Utraquists defeat radical Taborites at Battle of Lipany.
- **1436** — The Compacts of Basel are signed, granting Hussites limited religious freedom.

## Body

### Origins and Outbreak
The wars began after proto-Protestant reformer Jan Hus was executed in 1415 for criticizing church corruption. His death outraged the Bohemian nobility and populace. Tensions escalated after King Wenceslaus IV died in 1419, leading to the First Defenestration of Prague, where Hussites threw Catholic officials from a window. This sparked open revolt and the expulsion of Catholic Germans from Bohemian cities.

### Hussite Factions and Military Innovation
The Hussites split into two main groups: the moderate **Utraquists** (or Calixtines), who demanded communion in both kinds (bread and wine), and the radical **Taborites**, who sought a more thorough reformation. Under military leader Jan Žižka, the Hussites developed revolutionary tactics. They used **wagon forts** (mobile fortifications) and early handheld firearms like the *píšťala* (a precursor to the pistol) and *houfnice* (a cannon, origin of "howitzer"). These weapons, effective against armored cavalry and requiring less training, allowed peasant militias to defeat professional crusader armies.

### Crusades and Internal Strife
The Catholic Church and Holy Roman Emperor Sigismund launched five major crusades against the Hussites, all of which failed. The Hussites won key battles at Vítkov Hill (1420), Německý Brod (1422), and Domažlice (1431). However, the Hussite movement was plagued by internal conflict. After Žižka's death in 1424, civil war broke out between the Utraquists and Taborites. The Taborites were decisively defeated by the Utraquist-Catholic alliance at the Battle of Lipany in 1434, effectively ending the radical faction.

### Resolution and Aftermath
Following the Taborite defeat, moderate Hussites negotiated with the Council of Basel. The resulting **Compacts of Basel** (1436) allowed Hussites to practice their rite, including communion in both kinds, in exchange for acknowledging the Pope's authority. The wars left Bohemia devastated, with its population falling from an estimated 2.8–3.37 million around 1400 to 1.5–1.85 million by 1526. The Utraquist church remained established in Bohemia until 1620, and Hussite traditions influenced later Protestant movements.

## Terms
- **Hussites**: Followers of Jan Hus, advocating for church reform in Bohemia.
- **Utraquists (Calixtines)**: Moderate Hussites who insisted on receiving communion in both kinds (*sub utraque specie*).
- **Taborites**: Radical Hussites based in the town of Tábor, seeking a more thorough reformation.
- **Wagon Forts**: Mobile defensive fortifications used by Hussites to counter cavalry charges.
- **Píšťala**: A Czech term for an early handheld firearm, origin of the English word "pistol."
- **Houfnice**: A Hussite cannon, origin of the English word "howitzer."
- **Four Articles of Prague**: The core Hussite demands, including free preaching of the word and communion in both kinds.
- **Compacts of Basel**: The 1436 agreement granting Hussites limited religious freedom within the Catholic Church.
- **Defenestration**: The act of throwing someone out of a window, a key event sparking the wars.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The exact cause of King Wenceslaus IV's death in 1419 is debated; it may have been natural or caused by shock from the Defenestration.
- The degree to which Hussite military innovations directly influenced later European warfare is a subject of scholarly discussion.
- The long-term impact of the Compacts is debated, as they were eventually repealed and Hussite traditions were absorbed into broader Protestantism.

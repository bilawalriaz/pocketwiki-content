# Kashmir

## Overview
Kashmir is a mountainous region in the northern Indian subcontinent, historically a cultural crossroads. Its modern significance stems from a territorial dispute following the 1947 partition of British India, which has led to its administration being divided among India, Pakistan, and China, making it one of the world's most enduring geopolitical conflicts.

## Timeline
- **1339** — End of Hindu Lohara dynasty rule; beginning of Muslim sultanates.
- **1586** — Kashmir Valley incorporated into the Mughal Empire.
- **1752** — Region annexed by the Afghan Durrani Empire.
- **1819** — Sikh Empire under Ranjit Singh conquers the Kashmir Valley.
- **1846** — Treaty of Amritsar: British sell Kashmir to Gulab Singh, creating the Princely State of Jammu and Kashmir.
- **1947** — Partition of India; Hari Singh hesitates to accede to either India or Pakistan.
- **1947-48** — First Kashmir War; UN-mediated ceasefire establishes a Line of Control.
- **1962** — Sino-Indian War; China gains control of Aksai Chin.
- **1965** — Second Indo-Pakistani War over Kashmir ends in stalemate.
- **2019** — India revokes the special autonomy of Jammu and Kashmir, bifurcating it into two union territories.

## Body

### Etymology and Terminology
The name "Kashmir" is derived from Sanskrit, with popular etymologies linking it to the sage Kashyapa or meaning "land desiccated from water." The term originally referred only to the Kashmir Valley but now encompasses the former princely state. Administrative terminology is politically charged: India refers to Pakistani-administered areas as "Pakistan-occupied Kashmir" (POK), while Pakistan calls Indian-administered areas "Indian-occupied Kashmir" (IOK). Neutral sources often use "Pakistan-administered Kashmir" and "India-administered Kashmir."

### Historical Rule and the Princely State
After centuries under Hindu dynasties, the Kashmir Valley was ruled by Muslim sultanates, then the Mughal and Afghan empires. Sikh rule (1819-1846) was oppressive, marked by high taxes and anti-Muslim laws. In 1846, following the First Anglo-Sikh War, the British purchased the region and installed Gulab Singh as ruler, creating the multi-ethnic Princely State of Jammu and Kashmir. The state combined a Muslim-majority valley with Hindu-majority Jammu and Buddhist-majority Ladakh. Under Dogra (Hindu) rule, the Muslim peasantry faced heavy taxation and discrimination.

### Partition and Conflict
At the 1947 partition, the Muslim-majority princely state was expected to join Pakistan. Its Hindu ruler, Hari Singh, hesitated, leading to a Pakistani-backed tribal invasion. He acceded to India in exchange for military assistance, triggering the first Indo-Pakistani war. A UN-brokered ceasefire in 1948 established a Line of Control (LoC) but left the promised plebiscite unheld. The region has since been the cause of two more wars (1965, 1999) and remains divided.

### Current Status and Administration
The former princely state is now administered by three countries. India controls about half the area, administering the union territories of Jammu and Kashmir and Ladakh. Pakistan controls a third, divided into Azad Kashmir and Gilgit-Baltistan. China controls the remaining portion, including Aksai Chin and the Trans-Karakoram Tract. The Siachen Glacier area is also contested between India and Pakistan. Neither India nor Pakistan formally recognizes the other's claims.

### Geography and Climate
Kashmir is predominantly mountainous, traversed by the Himalayas, Karakoram, and Hindu Kush ranges. It is drained by the Indus, Jhelum, and Chenab rivers. The topography creates diverse climates, from subtropical in Jammu to alpine and arid Tibetan climates in Ladakh and Gilgit-Baltistan. The Pir Panjal Range blocks the Indian monsoon from the main Kashmir Valley.

### Demographics and Economy
Historically, the Kashmir Valley has been overwhelmingly Muslim (over 90% in early 20th-century censuses), while Jammu had a Hindu majority. The Kashmiri Pandits, a Hindu minority in the valley, underwent a mass exodus in the 1990s due to insurgency. The economy is agriculture-based, with rice as a staple. Kashmir is famed for its handicrafts, including Pashmina shawls, silk carpets, and saffron cultivation. The 2005 earthquake severely impacted the economy.

## Terms
- **Princely State:** A semi-autonomous region under the suzerainty of the British Crown, ruled by a local monarch.
- **Line of Control (LoC):** The de facto border dividing Indian- and Pakistani-administered Kashmir, established after the 1948 ceasefire.
- **Suzerainty:** A relationship where a state controls its internal affairs but its foreign policy is guided by another power.
- **Plebiscite:** A direct vote by the electorate on a particular proposal, in this context, a UN-demanded referendum on Kashmir's accession.
- **Dogra:** The Hindu Rajput dynasty that ruled the Princely State of Jammu and Kashmir from 1846 to 1947.
- **Kashmiri Pandits:** Kashmiri Hindus, historically a Brahmin community and minority in the Kashmir Valley.
- **Aksai Chin:** A high-altitude desert plateau administered by China but claimed by India.
- **Trans-Karakoram Tract:** A portion of the former princely state administered by China and ceded by Pakistan in 1963, a transfer not recognized by India.

## Debates and Open Questions
The core dispute is the unresolved status of Kashmir: should it be part of India, Pakistan, or an independent entity? The UN-mandated plebiscite was never held, with both nations blaming each other for failing to meet preconditions. Scholarly debate continues on the legality of the 1947 accession, the causes of the 1990s insurgency and Pandit exodus, and the human rights record of all administering powers. The 2019 revocation of Jammu and Kashmir's autonomy by India remains a major point of contention.

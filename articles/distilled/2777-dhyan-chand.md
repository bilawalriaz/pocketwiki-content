# Dhyan Chand

## Overview
Major Dhyan Chand (1905–1979) is widely regarded as the greatest field hockey player in history. His extraordinary ball control and goal-scoring prowess led India to three consecutive Olympic gold medals (1928, 1932, 1936) during an era of Indian dominance in the sport. His legacy is commemorated through India's highest sporting honors and national celebrations.

## Timeline
- **29 August 1905** — Born in Allahabad, India.
- **29 August 1922** — Enlisted in the British Indian Army.
- **1928** — Won first Olympic gold medal at Amsterdam; top scorer with 14 goals.
- **1932** — Won second Olympic gold medal at Los Angeles; India set a world record with a 24–1 final win.
- **1933** — Led Jhansi Heroes to victory in the prestigious Beighton Cup.
- **1936** — Captained India to third Olympic gold medal at Berlin; defeated Germany 8–1 in the final.
- **1947** — Led Indian team on an undefeated tour of East Africa.
- **1956** — Awarded the Padma Bhushan; retired from the Indian Army.
- **3 December 1979** — Died in Delhi.
- **1995** — 29 August designated as India's National Sports Day.
- **2002** — National Stadium in Delhi renamed Dhyan Chand National Stadium.

## Body

### Early Life and Army Career
Dhyan Chand was born into a Bais Rajput family in Allahabad. His father, a soldier in the British Indian Army, played hockey, which influenced Chand's early exposure to the sport. Due to frequent army transfers, Chand's formal education was limited, though he later graduated from Victoria College, Gwalior. He enlisted in the army on his 17th birthday in 1922, where his hockey talent was first showcased in regimental tournaments.

### Olympic Dominance (1928–1936)
Chand's international career began with a successful Indian Army tour of New Zealand in 1926. He was selected for the 1928 Amsterdam Olympics, where he scored 14 goals as India won its first hockey gold. His performance was so exceptional that Dutch authorities broke his stick to check for a magnet. He repeated this success at the 1932 Los Angeles Olympics, where India set a world record with a 24–1 victory over the USA in the final. As captain at the 1936 Berlin Olympics, he led India to a third gold, defeating Germany 8–1 in the final. He scored 37 goals across his three Olympic campaigns.

### Later Career and Legacy
After the 1936 Olympics, Chand continued to play for his regiment and led successful tours, including an undefeated East African tour in 1947. He retired from the army in 1956 and was awarded the Padma Bhushan. He later served as Chief Hockey Coach at the National Institute of Sports. His birthday is celebrated as National Sports Day, and India's highest sporting honor, the Major Dhyan Chand Khel Ratna Award, is named after him. The BBC called him "hockey's equivalent of Muhammad Ali."

## Terms
*   **Sepoy:** A private in the British Indian Army.
*   **Lance Naik/Naik:** Ranks in the British Indian Army, equivalent to a lance corporal and corporal.
*   **Viceroy's Commissioned Officer (VCO):** A class of officer in the British Indian Army, equivalent to a modern junior commissioned officer.
*   **Beighton Cup:** A prestigious field hockey tournament in India.
*   **Padma Bhushan:** India's third-highest civilian honor.
*   **Major Dhyan Chand Khel Ratna Award:** India's highest sporting honor for lifetime achievement.
*   **National Sports Day:** An annual celebration in India on 29 August, Dhyan Chand's birthday.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Goal Count in 1936 Final:** There is a discrepancy between newspaper reports and Chand's own autobiography regarding the number of goals he scored in the 1936 Olympic final. Newspapers claimed he scored six goals, while Chand stated he scored three. The International Hockey Federation records attribute three goals to him.
*   **Bharat Ratna Nomination:** Chand was a nominee for India's highest civilian award, the Bharat Ratna, in 2014. The award was given to others, and an RTI filing suggested the Prime Minister's Office ignored a sports ministry recommendation, leading to disappointment among his family.

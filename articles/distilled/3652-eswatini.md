# Eswatini

## Overview
Eswatini, formerly Swaziland, is a small, landlocked absolute monarchy in Southern Africa, bordered by South Africa and Mozambique. It is Africa's last absolute monarchy, ruled by King Mswati III since 1986, and faces significant challenges including a severe HIV/AIDS epidemic, economic dependence on South Africa, and ongoing political tensions over democratic reforms.

## Timeline
- **c. 18th century** — Swazi kingdom established under Ngwane III.
- **1881** — British convention recognizes Swazi independence during the Scramble for Africa.
- **1903** — Swaziland becomes a British High Commission Territory after the Second Boer War.
- **6 September 1968** — Swaziland regains full independence from Britain.
- **12 April 1973** — King Sobhuza II suspends the constitution and rules by decree.
- **1986** — Mswati III becomes king.
- **2005** — New constitution adopted.
- **19 April 2018** — Country officially renamed Kingdom of Eswatini.
- **June 2021** — Major pro-democracy protests and civil unrest occur.

## Body

### History and Formation
The Swazi people trace their origins to the Embo-Dlamini branch of the Nguni, who migrated south during the Bantu expansion. They settled in the region by the 18th century, with the kingdom formally established under King Ngwane III. The country's name derives from King Mswati II (reigned c. 1840s-1860s), who greatly expanded and unified the territory. European colonial powers drew its modern boundaries in 1881. After a period under the South African Republic's administration, it became a British protectorate in 1903. Independence was achieved in 1968 under a Westminster-style constitution.

### Political System
Eswatini is an absolute monarchy where the king (ngwenyama) holds supreme executive, legislative, and judicial power. The 2005 constitution provides for a bicameral Parliament (Libandla), but political parties are banned from contesting elections. The king appoints the prime minister, a majority of senators, and a minority of legislators. Elections are held every five years using the non-partisan *tinkhundla* (constituency) system. The judiciary is dual, comprising both a Western-style court system and traditional Swazi courts.

### Economy
The economy is lower-middle income and closely tied to South Africa, which provides over 90% of imports and buys about 70% of exports. The lilangeni currency is pegged to the South African rand. Key sectors are manufacturing (37% of GDP, especially textiles and sugar processing) and services (50%). Agriculture employs about 75% of the population in subsistence farming but is low-productivity. The country's major exports are sugar and soft drink concentrate, which are vulnerable to climate change and shifting global trade preferences. A large portion of wealth is held by the state and the king.

### Society and Demographics
The population is predominantly ethnic Swazi, speaking siSwati and English. Christianity is the major religion (83%), often blended with traditional beliefs. Eswatini faces a severe health crisis, with the world's highest HIV prevalence rate among 15-49 year-olds (27.1% as of 2019). Life expectancy is low (58 years as of 2018), and the population is very young, with a median age of 22.

### Culture and Environment
Traditional culture centers on the homestead, polygamous family structure, and the central role of the king. Key cultural events are the *Incwala* (first fruits/kingship ceremony) and the annual *Umhlanga* Reed Dance. Geographically, Eswatini is divided into four climatic regions from the mountainous Highveld to the hot Lowveld. It has rich biodiversity with several formal protected areas, though land degradation is a major threat.

## Terms
- **Ngwenyama**: The title for the king of Eswatini, meaning "lion."
- **Ndlovukati**: The title for the queen mother, a spiritual and national figurehead.
- **Tinkhundla**: The administrative constituencies and electoral system used in Eswatini.
- **Libandla**: The bicameral Parliament of Eswatini, consisting of the Senate and House of Assembly.
- **Incwala**: The most important annual Swazi cultural ceremony, a "kingship ceremony" involving the first fruits.
- **Umhlanga**: The annual Reed Dance ceremony where unmarried girls present reeds to the queen mother.
- **Lilangeni**: The official currency of Eswatini, pegged to the South African rand.
- **Swazi Nation Land (SNL)**: Land held in trust by the king for the Swazi people, where most subsistence farming occurs.

## Debates and open questions
The primary debate concerns the nature of Eswatini's governance. Pro-democracy activists and international observers criticize the absolute monarchy for prohibiting political parties, suppressing dissent, and failing to implement meaningful reforms. The government maintains that the *tinkhundla* system and traditional structures are the appropriate form of Swazi democracy. The 2021 civil unrest highlighted deep public frustration over political repression, economic inequality, and the king's lavish spending amid widespread poverty. The long-term stability of the monarchy in the face of these pressures remains an open question.

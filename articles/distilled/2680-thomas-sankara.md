# Thomas Sankara

## Overview
Thomas Sankara was a Burkinabé revolutionary and Marxist leader who served as the first President of Burkina Faso from 1983 until his assassination in 1987. He is remembered for launching an ambitious series of social, ecological, and economic reforms aimed at creating a self-reliant, anti-imperialist state, making him an iconic figure in African revolutionary history.

## Body

### Early Life and Military Career
Thomas Sankara was born in 1949 in Yako, French Upper Volta. He attended primary school in Bobo-Dioulasso and later the lycée Ouezzin Coulibaly. Despite his Catholic parents' wishes for him to become a priest, he chose a military career, entering the Kadiogo military academy in 1966. His studies in Madagascar (1970-1973) exposed him to revolutionary ideas and agricultural science. He fought in a border war with Mali in 1974, which he later renounced as "useless and unjust." In 1976, he became commander of the Commando Training Centre in Pô and joined a secret revolutionary organization called the ROC.

### Rise to Power and Revolutionary Government
Sankara served as Minister of Information in 1981-82, promoting investigative journalism before resigning in protest. He was appointed Prime Minister in January 1983 but was dismissed and arrested in May. A coup led by his ally Blaise Compaoré on 4 August 1983 brought him to the presidency at age 33. He launched the "Democratic and Popular Revolution," defining its ideology as anti-imperialist. His government, the Council of the Revolution (CNR), implemented sweeping reforms. He renamed the country Burkina Faso in 1984 and personally wrote its national anthem.

### Domestic Reforms
Sankara's policies prioritized self-sufficiency. He launched mass vaccination programs, reducing infant mortality from 20.8% to 14.5%. His government built schools, health centres, and infrastructure, including over 700 km of rail. To combat desertification, a campaign planted over 10 million trees. Agriculture was revolutionized through land nationalization, irrigation projects, and village cereal banks, leading to a 75% rise in cereal production and food self-sufficiency by 1987. Social reforms included banning female genital mutilation, forced marriages, and polygamy, and appointing women to high government positions. He established Popular Revolutionary Tribunals to prosecute corruption and Committees for the Defense of the Revolution (CDRs) to promote popular mobilization.

### Foreign Policy and Conflict
Sankara's foreign policy was anti-imperialist. He rejected loans from the IMF and criticized France and the United States. He fostered ties with Cuba, sending youth there for training. His rhetoric and support for revolutionary movements isolated him from many West African leaders allied with France. In 1985, a border dispute with Mali escalated into the Agacher Strip War, which ended with a ceasefire and an ICJ ruling that split the territory. The conflict damaged Burkina Faso's military credibility and moderated its foreign policy stance.

### Assassination and Legacy
On 15 October 1987, Sankara and twelve officials were assassinated in a coup led by Blaise Compaoré, who reversed most of Sankara's policies and ruled for 27 years. Sankara's body was buried in an unmarked grave. In 2021, Compaoré was charged with his murder, and in 2022, he was found guilty and sentenced to life in prison in absentia. Sankara's remains were exhumed in 2015 and reinterred in a mausoleum in 2025. He is often called "Africa's Che Guevara" and remains a symbol of anti-imperialist revolution and integrity.

## Terms
- **Pan-Africanist**: An advocate for the political, social, and economic unity of all people of African descent.
- **Marxist**: A follower of the political and economic theories of Karl Marx, advocating for class struggle and a classless society.
- **Autarky**: A policy of national economic self-sufficiency, minimizing imports.
- **Committees for the Defense of the Revolution (CDRs)**: Mass organizations inspired by Cuba, intended to serve as a foundation for society and promote popular mobilization and vigilance.
- **Popular Revolutionary Tribunals**: Courts established to try former officials and others for corruption, tax evasion, or counter-revolutionary activity in public proceedings.
- **Agacher Strip**: A territory disputed between Burkina Faso and Mali, the subject of a 1985 war.
- **Council of the Revolution (CNR)**: The collective leadership body that governed Burkina Faso under Sankara.
- **Anti-imperialism**: Opposition to imperialism, particularly the economic and political influence of Western powers.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Human Rights Record**: Sankara's government faced criticism from organizations like Amnesty International for arbitrary detentions and the procedures of the Popular Revolutionary Tribunals, which did not meet international legal standards.
- **Cause of the Agacher Strip War**: There is debate over whether Sankara intentionally provoked the conflict with Mali to rally domestic support, as alleged by some sources, or if it was primarily a Malian aggression aimed at triggering a coup in Burkina Faso.
- **Role of Foreign Powers**: The exact extent of foreign involvement, particularly by France and Libya, in Sankara's rise and assassination remains a subject of investigation and speculation.

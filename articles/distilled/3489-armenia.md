# Armenia

## Overview
Armenia is a landlocked country in the Armenian highlands of West Asia, part of the Caucasus region. It is one of the world's oldest civilizations, with a history spanning millennia, and was the first state to adopt Christianity as its official religion in AD 301. Its strategic location has made it a crossroads of empires, leading to periods of sovereignty, foreign domination, and a modern struggle for independence and security, particularly following the Armenian genocide and the Nagorno-Karabakh conflict.

## Body

### Ancient and Medieval History
The Armenian highlands have been inhabited since the Stone Age. The first Armenian state, Urartu, was established in 860 BC. By the 6th century BC, it was replaced by the Satrapy of Armenia. The Kingdom of Armenia reached its peak under Tigranes the Great in the 1st century BC. In AD 301, Armenia became the first nation to adopt Christianity as its state religion. The ancient kingdom was later split between the Byzantine and Sasanian Empires. After periods of Arab and Seljuk rule, the Bagratuni dynasty restored an Armenian kingdom in the 9th century, which fell in 1045. The Armenian Kingdom of Cilicia, a Christian state allied with the Crusaders, existed on the Mediterranean coast from the 11th to 14th centuries.

### Early Modern Era and Genocide
From the 16th to 19th centuries, the Armenian homeland was divided between the Ottoman and Persian empires. By the 19th century, Eastern Armenia was conquered by the Russian Empire, while Western Armenia remained under Ottoman rule. During World War I, the Ottoman government systematically exterminated up to 1.5 million Armenians in the Armenian genocide. Following the Russian Revolution, the First Republic of Armenia declared independence in 1918 but was short-lived, being incorporated into the Soviet Union by 1920.

### Soviet and Post-Soviet Armenia
As the Armenian Soviet Socialist Republic, Armenia experienced stability, cultural revival, and later, Stalinist repressions. After World War II, a period of liberalization occurred. In the late 1980s, the Karabakh movement, advocating for the unification of the Nagorno-Karabakh Autonomous Oblast with Armenia, sparked ethnic violence and a devastating earthquake in 1988. Armenia declared independence in 1991. The post-Soviet years were marked by the First Nagorno-Karabakh War (1992–1994), which ended with a ceasefire and Armenian control over the region, and a severe economic blockade by Azerbaijan and Turkey. The 2018 Velvet Revolution led to a change in government. The 2020 Second Nagorno-Karabakh War resulted in significant territorial losses for Armenian forces. In September 2023, a major Azerbaijani offensive led to the dissolution of the self-declared Republic of Artsakh and the exodus of its Armenian population.

### Government and Foreign Relations
Armenia is a unitary, multi-party, democratic parliamentary republic. It is a member of numerous European organizations, including the Council of Europe, and is a candidate for EU membership. Historically, Armenia maintained close security ties with Russia through the CSTO, but relations have deteriorated since 2023. Its borders with Turkey and Azerbaijan remain closed due to the unresolved Nagorno-Karabakh conflict and Turkey's refusal to acknowledge the Armenian genocide.

### Economy and Demographics
Armenia's economy is primarily based on industrial output and mineral extraction, with a growing IT sector. It faces challenges from blockades and relies on investment from the large global Armenian diaspora, estimated at 8 million. The population of Armenia is approximately 3 million, with ethnic Armenians comprising 98.1% of the inhabitants. The Armenian Apostolic Church is the predominant religion.

## Terms
*   **Urartu:** The first Armenian state, established in the Armenian highlands in 860 BC.
*   **Armenian Apostolic Church:** The world's oldest national church and the primary religious establishment in Armenia, part of Oriental Orthodoxy.
*   **Armenian genocide:** The systematic extermination of up to 1.5 million Armenians by the Ottoman government during World War I.
*   **First Republic of Armenia:** The short-lived independent Armenian state that existed from 1918 to 1920.
*   **Nagorno-Karabakh:** A region in the South Caucasus, historically populated by Armenians, that was the focus of a long-running conflict between Armenia and Azerbaijan.
*   **Velvet Revolution:** The 2018 series of anti-government protests in Armenia that led to a change in government.
*   **CSTO (Collective Security Treaty Organization):** A Russia-led military alliance of which Armenia was a member, but from which it has effectively frozen participation.
*   **Diaspora:** The worldwide dispersion of Armenians from their historic homeland, with communities significantly larger than the population of Armenia itself.

## Debates and open questions
*   The exact origins of the name "Armenia" are uncertain and debated, with theories linking it to the legendary patriarch Hayk, the Hittite vassal state Ḫayaša-Azzi, or the Urartian region of Arme.
*   The total death toll of the Armenian genocide is debated, with estimates ranging from 600,000 to 1.5 million, though the event is recognized as genocide by the vast majority of Western historians.
*   The future status of Armenia's foreign policy is a major open question, as it seeks closer ties with the West (including EU candidacy) while managing deteriorated relations with its traditional security ally, Russia.

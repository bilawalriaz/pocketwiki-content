# Pol Pot

## Overview
Pol Pot was the leader of the Khmer Rouge, the Maoist revolutionary group that ruled Cambodia as Democratic Kampuchea from 1975 to 1979. His radical attempt to create an agrarian communist society led to the Cambodian genocide, resulting in the deaths of an estimated 1.5–2 million people, approximately one-quarter of the country's population. His regime is widely condemned as one of the most brutal in modern history.

- **Dec 1978** — Vietnam invades Cambodia.
- **7 Jan 1979** — Vietnamese forces capture Phnom Penh; Pol Pot and the Khmer Rouge flee.
- **15 Apr 1998** — Pol Pot dies under house arrest in the Khmer Rouge jungle stronghold.

## Body

### Early Life and Radicalization
Born to a prosperous farmer, Pol Pot (born Saloth Sâr) received an elite education in Cambodia and Paris. In Paris, he joined the French Communist Party and became involved in clandestine Marxist circles. Returning to Cambodia in 1953, he joined the anti-colonial Khmer Viet Minh. After the group's retreat to North Vietnam in 1954, he worked as a teacher while secretly building a communist movement. In 1963, he became leader of the Communist Party of Kampuchea (CPK) and fled to the jungle to avoid government repression.

### Rise to Power and Civil War
From jungle bases, Pol Pot relaunched the war against the Cambodian government in 1968. After a 1970 coup by Lon Nol, the Khmer Rouge allied with the deposed Prince Sihanouk, which greatly boosted recruitment. The conflict became part of the wider Vietnam War, with massive U.S. bombing of Cambodia fueling Khmer Rouge support. By 1975, the Khmer Rouge controlled the country and captured Phnom Penh on April 17.

### Rule of Democratic Kampuchea (1975–1979)
Pol Pot sought to create a purely agrarian, classless society through "Year Zero," a policy of completely destroying existing culture and traditions. Cities were emptied, and the population was forced into rural labor camps. Money, religion, and private property were abolished. The regime carried out mass executions, torture, and forced labor, leading to widespread death from violence, starvation, and disease. Internal purges within the CPK intensified, with thousands sent to the S-21 prison for execution. The regime's radical policies and paranoia culminated in the Cambodian genocide.

### Downfall and Exile
Tensions with Vietnam escalated into border wars. In December 1978, Vietnam invaded Cambodia, toppling the Khmer Rouge in January 1979. Pol Pot and the surviving Khmer Rouge retreated to jungle bases near the Thai border, where they waged a guerrilla war against the Vietnamese-backed government for the next decade. Supported by China, the U.S., and Thailand as a bulwark against Vietnamese influence, the Khmer Rouge retained Cambodia's UN seat until 1991.

### Final Years and Death
Following the end of the Cold War and UN-brokered peace talks, the Khmer Rouge fractured. Pol Pot's health declined, and in 1997 he was placed under house arrest by his own commanders after ordering the assassination of defense minister Son Sen. He was sentenced to life imprisonment by a Khmer Rouge tribunal. Pol Pot died of a heart attack on April 15, 1998.

## Terms
*   **Khmer Rouge:** The communist movement led by Pol Pot, whose name means "Red Cambodians" in French.
*   **Democratic Kampuchea:** The official name of Cambodia under Khmer Rouge rule (1975–1979).
*   **Year Zero:** The Khmer Rouge concept of completely restarting society by destroying all previous culture, history, and institutions.
*   **Angkar:** The secretive "Organization" that was the supreme power in Democratic Kampuchea, later revealed to be the CPK.
*   **S-21 (Tuol Sleng):** A former high school converted into a notorious security prison where thousands were tortured and executed.
*   **New People/Enemy People:** The term used for urban dwellers and former government officials, who were forcibly evacuated to the countryside and treated as enemies.
*   **Base People:** The rural peasants, who were considered the core of the revolution and given preferential treatment.
*   **Autarky:** A policy of complete national self-sufficiency, which the Khmer Rouge pursued by cutting off most foreign relations and trade.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Death Toll:** Estimates of deaths under the Khmer Rouge range from 1.5 to 2 million, but figures as low as 1 million and as high as 3 million have been cited. The exact proportion of deaths from execution versus starvation and disease is debated.
*   **Ideological Deviation:** Scholars debate whether Pol Pot's ideology was a genuine, if extreme, form of Maoism or a unique, xenophobic Khmer nationalism with only a "thin Marxist veneer."
*   **International Complicity:** The extent to which foreign powers, particularly China and the United States, supported the Khmer Rouge during and after its rule for strategic reasons remains a significant historical and ethical question.
*   **Personal Responsibility:** While Pol Pot was the undisputed leader, the degree to which he directly ordered specific atrocities versus the autonomous violence of local cadres is analyzed by historians.

# Carl Gustav Jacob Jacobi

## Overview
Carl Gustav Jacob Jacobi (1804–1851) was a German mathematician whose work fundamentally shaped the fields of elliptic functions, number theory, and determinants. His theories provided essential tools for mathematical physics, particularly in solving problems of classical mechanics and planetary motion, and his algebraic methods influenced generations of mathematicians.

## Body

### Early Life and Education
Jacobi was born to Ashkenazi Jewish parents and was initially homeschooled by his uncle. He entered the Potsdam Gymnasium in 1816, where his advanced preparation allowed him to skip to the senior year. At Berlin University from 1821, he studied both philology and mathematics but ultimately chose mathematics by 1823. He earned his doctorate in 1825 and began lecturing at the University of Berlin.

### Academic Career
In 1826, Jacobi moved to Königsberg University, where he rose from private lecturer to a tenured professor by 1829. He held this position until 1843, when overwork caused a health breakdown. After recovering in Italy, he lived in Berlin as a royal pensioner. His political involvement in the 1848 Revolution briefly threatened his pension, but it was restored due to his scientific fame.

### Mathematical Contributions
Jacobi's most celebrated work was his theory of elliptic functions, detailed in his 1829 *Fundamenta nova*. He developed the elliptic theta function and proved that a single-valued, multiply periodic function cannot have more than two periods with a non-real ratio. His method of "inverting" known results, such as elliptic integrals, opened new research fields.

He applied elliptic functions to number theory, providing new proofs for Fermat's two-square and Lagrange's four-square theorems. He also advanced the theory of determinants, inventing the Jacobian determinant (a matrix of partial derivatives) and reintroducing the standard ∂ notation for partial derivatives in 1841.

In mechanics, he contributed to the Hamilton–Jacobi theory and introduced the Jacobi integral (1836) for celestial mechanics. His work on symmetric polynomials (now called Schur polynomials) and the Jacobi identity for Lie brackets further cemented his legacy across multiple mathematical disciplines.

## Terms
- **Elliptic function**: A complex function that is doubly periodic, meaning it repeats its values in two independent directions in the complex plane.
- **Theta function**: A special function of several complex variables, crucial in the theory of elliptic functions and mathematical physics.
- **Jacobian determinant**: The determinant of a matrix of all first-order partial derivatives of a vector-valued function, used in changing variables in multiple integrals.
- **Multiply periodic function**: A function that repeats its values after shifts by two or more linearly independent periods.
- **Jacobi symbol**: A generalization of the Legendre symbol used in number theory to simplify calculations involving quadratic residues.
- **Jacobi identity**: An identity satisfied by the Lie bracket in Lie algebras, analogous to the associative property.
- **Hamilton–Jacobi theory**: A reformulation of classical mechanics that is useful for solving problems of motion, especially in celestial mechanics.
- **Quadratic reciprocity**: A fundamental theorem in number theory that describes the relationship between the solvability of two related quadratic equations.

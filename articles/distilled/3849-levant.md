# Levant

## Overview
The Levant is a historical and cultural subregion of West Asia along the Eastern Mediterranean, central to the study of ancient civilizations and modern geopolitics. Its definition has evolved from a broad term for "the East" to a specific archaeological and cultural zone, making it a key concept for understanding the region's layered history and diverse populations.

## Timeline
- **13th–14th c.** — Italian term *levante* used for Eastern Mediterranean commerce.
- **1497** — Term "Levant" first appears in English.
- **1581** — England founds the Levant Company to trade with the Ottoman Empire.
- **1920–1946** — French mandate over Syria and Lebanon is called the "Levant States."
- **2013** — *The Oxford Handbook of the Archaeology of the Levant* defines its modern archaeological boundaries.

## Body

### Etymology and Historical Usage
The term derives from the French *levant* ("rising"), referring to the sunrise in the east. It entered English in the 1497 and was popularized by 16th-century English merchants. Historically, its meaning shifted: it first denoted all lands east of Italy, then narrowed to the Ottoman provinces of Syria-Palestine and Egypt, and later became associated with the French mandate states after World War I.

### Geographical and Archaeological Definition
In modern academic use, particularly archaeology, the Levant refers to a specific cultural area. Its core includes Syria, Lebanon, Jordan, Palestine, Israel, and south Cilicia (Turkey), with Cyprus often included due to close historical ties. Archaeologists define its boundaries as the Taurus Mountains to the north, the eastern deserts to the east, Wadi al-Arish in Sinai to the south, and the Mediterranean Sea to the west. It is commonly divided into the Northern and Southern Levant by the Litani River.

### Demographics and Culture
The region is home to Levantine Arabs, Jews, Kurds, Assyrians, Armenians, and others, with a total population of approximately 57.6 million. The majority are Muslims, primarily Sunni, but it is also the birthplace of Christianity and home to many Christian denominations, as well as Jews, Druze, and Yazidis. The dominant language is Levantine Arabic, with other languages including Hebrew, Greek, Turkish, and Aramaic. Genetic studies show modern Levantines derive ancestry from ancient Semitic-speaking peoples of the Bronze and Iron Ages.

## Terms
- **Levant**: A subregion of West Asia along the Eastern Mediterranean, used historically and in archaeology to denote a specific cultural area.
- **Levantine**: An adjective describing the culture, cuisine, or people of the Levant.
- **Levant Company**: An English trading company founded in 1581 to commerce with the Ottoman Empire.
- **Levant States**: The term for the French mandate over Syria and Lebanon (1920–1946).
- **Southern Levant**: The area south of the Litani River, including modern Israel, Palestine, and Jordan.
- **Northern Levant**: The area north of the Litani River, primarily modern Syria and Lebanon.
- **Mashriq**: An Arabic term meaning "eastern place," broadly equivalent to the Levant.
- **Ash-Shām**: An Arabic term for the region, sometimes translated as "the Levant."

## Debates and Open questions
The precise modern boundaries of the Levant are debated, with some including Cyprus, Egypt, Iraq, and parts of Turkey. The term itself is considered archaic by some dictionaries for contemporary use, though it remains standard in archaeology and is being reclaimed in political and social sciences. There is also disagreement on whether "Levant" is an accurate translation of the Arabic *ash-Shām* as used by groups like ISIL.

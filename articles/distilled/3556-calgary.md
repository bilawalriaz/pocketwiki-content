# Calgary

## Overview
Calgary is the largest city in Alberta and the third-largest in Canada, located at the confluence of the Bow and Elbow Rivers in the foothills of the Canadian Rockies. Its economy, historically rooted in agriculture and railways, became dominated by the oil and gas industry after the 1914 Turner Valley discovery, though it has since diversified. The city is known for hosting the 1988 Winter Olympics and the annual Calgary Stampede.

## Body

### Early History and Incorporation
The area has been inhabited for at least 11,000 years by pre-Clovis people and later by First Nations including the Blackfoot Confederacy. The first recorded European visitor was David Thompson in 1787. The North-West Mounted Police established Fort Calgary in 1875, officially named in 1876. The arrival of the Canadian Pacific Railway in 1883 catalyzed growth, leading to the town's incorporation in 1884. Early years were marked by political turmoil involving Magistrate Jeremiah Travis and a devastating fire in 1886, after which a bylaw required downtown buildings to be constructed from local sandstone. Calgary became a city in 1894.

### Economic Development and the Oil Age
Calgary's early economy was based on agriculture and railways. The 1914 Turner Valley oil discovery began the city's transformation into an energy hub, though the industry faced booms and busts. The 1947 Leduc discovery solidified Calgary's role as the centre of Canada's oil and gas industry, attracting corporate head offices. The city experienced massive growth during the 1970s energy crisis, followed by a severe recession in the 1980s due to the National Energy Program and falling oil prices. The economy has since diversified into sectors like technology, logistics, and tourism, though energy remains the largest GDP contributor.

### The 1988 Olympics and Modern Growth
Calgary hosted the 1988 Winter Olympics, the first in Canada. The Games provided a major economic boost during a recession and left a legacy of world-class sporting facilities like the Olympic Oval and Canada Olympic Park. The city continued to grow rapidly, with population surges in the 2000s and again in the 2020s, driven by inter-provincial migration attracted by relative housing affordability. Recent challenges include the 2013 flood, a downtown office vacancy crisis, and critical failures in the water feeder main system in 2024 and 2025.

## Terms
- **Chinook**: A warm, dry wind that can cause rapid temperature swings in Calgary's winter.
- **CTrain**: Calgary's light rail transit (LRT) system, which is fare-free in the downtown core.
- **+15**: An extensive network of elevated, enclosed pedestrian skyways connecting downtown buildings.
- **Palliser's Triangle**: A semi-arid steppe region in the Canadian Prairies, within which Calgary is located.
- **Treaty 7**: An 1877 agreement between the Crown and several First Nations, including the Blackfoot, covering southern Alberta.
- **Sandstone City**: A nickname for Calgary after an 1886 fire bylaw mandated sandstone construction for major downtown buildings.
- **Bearspaw South Feeder Main**: Calgary's largest water pipe, whose catastrophic failures in 2024 and 2025 caused major water crises.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Indigenous Naming**: There have been ongoing efforts and debates regarding the use and official recognition of Indigenous names for the city, such as Mohkínstsis (Blackfoot) and Wîchîspa Oyade (Stoney Nakoda), including a 2017 application to rename Calgary.
- **Downtown Revitalization**: The city struggles with high downtown office vacancy rates (20-30%) and is implementing plans for office-to-residential conversions to address the issue.
- **Infrastructure Maintenance**: The 2024 and 2025 water main failures, attributed to deferred maintenance, have raised critical questions about the management and funding of the city's aging infrastructure.

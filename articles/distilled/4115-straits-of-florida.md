# Straits of Florida

## Overview
The Straits of Florida is a narrow waterway separating the Florida Keys from Cuba, connecting the Gulf of Mexico to the Atlantic Ocean. It is a critical maritime passage known for carrying the powerful Florida Current, a major component of the Gulf Stream system, and is a region of significant ecological and economic interest, including debates over offshore oil exploration.

## Timeline
- **1947–1962** — Five oil wells drilled in state waters south of the Florida Keys; all were dry holes.
- **1960–1961** — Gulf Oil drills three dry wells in federal waters south of the Florida Keys.
- **1977** — The Cuba–United States Maritime Boundary Agreement establishes the EEZ boundary halfway between the nations.
- **2004** — Spanish company Repsol drills in deep Cuban waters, finds a noncommercial oil deposit.
- **2008** — Cuba signs an oil exploration agreement with Petrobras; a claim of active Chinese drilling is later debunked.
- **2009** — Cuba signs an exploration agreement with Russian company Zarubezhneft.
- **2009** — A joint venture for Bahamian drilling is announced, but applications are later put on hold.
- **2011** — Petrobras withdraws from its 2008 agreement with Cuba due to poor prospects.
- **2013** — Diana Nyad completes a 106-mile swim from Cuba to Florida without a shark cage.

## Body

### Oil and Gas Exploration
Exploration for oil and gas has occurred on both the U.S. and Cuban sides of the strait, with mixed results. Early U.S. drilling in state and federal waters from 1947 to 1961 yielded only dry holes. The maritime boundary between the U.S. and Cuba was formally defined in 1977. Cuba has three producing offshore fields near its north coast, and the U.S. Geological Survey estimates the North Cuba Basin holds significant undiscovered resources. Exploration by foreign companies like Repsol, Petrobras, and Zarubezhneft has occurred in Cuban waters, though Petrobras withdrew in 2011. The issue of offshore drilling became a contentious topic in the 2008 U.S. elections. Meanwhile, drilling applications in Bahamian waters are on hold pending boundary negotiations with neighboring countries.

### Ocean Currents and Climate
The strait is the conduit for the Florida Current, the initial segment of the Gulf Stream. This immense current transports vast volumes of water, heat, and carbon, profoundly influencing weather and climate from Florida to Europe. Its strength can be influenced by climate variability in the tropical Pacific. Despite sub-seasonal fluctuations caused by wind and eddies, long-term observations show the current's transport has been remarkably stable over decades. The consistent energy flow has also led to studies of the Gulf Stream as a potential renewable energy source. Localized features, such as long-lasting cyclonic eddies, can redistribute nutrients and affect marine ecosystems and navigation.

### Ecology and Human Activity
The strait supports diverse marine life, including commercially important species like the Caribbean Spiny Lobster. Unique organisms such as the lettuce sea slug, which performs photosynthesis using ingested chloroplasts, are also found there. Corals like the boulder brain coral provide foundational habitats. Human interaction includes notable feats like the marathon swim across the strait, completed by Diana Nyad in 2013.

## Terms
- **Strait:** A narrow passage of water connecting two larger bodies of water.
- **Exclusive Economic Zone (EEZ):** A maritime zone where a sovereign state has special rights regarding the exploration and use of marine resources.
- **Florida Current:** A warm ocean current flowing northward through the Straits of Florida, forming the beginning of the Gulf Stream.
- **Gulf Stream:** A powerful, warm, and swift Atlantic ocean current that originates in the Gulf of Mexico and influences climate across the North Atlantic.
- **Mesoscale Eddy:** A large, rotating mass of water that breaks off from a main current, affecting local ocean conditions and ecosystems.
- **Primary Production:** The synthesis of organic compounds from carbon dioxide by marine plants and algae, forming the base of the ocean food web.

## Debates and Open Questions
The primary debate concerns the environmental and economic risks versus the potential benefits of offshore oil and gas exploration in the strait. This was a "hotly contested topic" in the 2008 U.S. elections. The exact boundaries of maritime zones between Cuba, the Bahamas, the U.S., and the Turks and Caicos Islands remain a subject of negotiation, impacting future exploration rights.

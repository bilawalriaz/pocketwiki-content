# Polish–Lithuanian–Teutonic War

## Overview
The Polish–Lithuanian–Teutonic War (1409–1411) was a major conflict between the Teutonic Knights and the allied Kingdom of Poland and Grand Duchy of Lithuania. Sparked by a Samogitian uprising, the war culminated in the decisive Battle of Grunwald, one of medieval Europe's largest battles. The Teutonic defeat, though not leading to massive territorial loss, crippled the Order financially and politically, shifting the balance of power in Central Europe and cementing the rise of the Polish–Lithuanian union as the dominant regional force.

## Timeline
- **1230s** — Teutonic Knights begin the Prussian Crusade, conquering pagan Prussians by the 1280s.
- **1385** — Union of Kreva: Lithuanian Grand Duke Jogaila marries Polish Queen Jadwiga, creating a personal union and converting Lithuania to Christianity.
- **1404** — Peace of Raciąż places Samogitia under Teutonic control.
- **May 1409** — Samogitian uprising against Teutonic rule begins.
- **6 August 1409** — Teutonic Grand Master Ulrich von Jungingen declares war on Poland and Lithuania.
- **8 October 1409** — A nine-month truce is signed, mediated by Wenceslaus IV of Bohemia.
- **24–30 June 1410** — Polish–Lithuanian forces unite and cross the Vistula River at Czerwinsk.
- **15 July 1410** — **Battle of Grunwald:** Decisive defeat of the Teutonic Knights.
- **26 July – 19 September 1410** — Unsuccessful siege of the Teutonic capital, Marienburg (Malbork).
- **1 February 1411** — Peace of Thorn signed, imposing heavy reparations on the Knights.
- **1422** — Treaty of Melno permanently resolves territorial disputes between the parties.

## Body

### Background and Causes
The conflict stemmed from long-standing territorial and economic disputes. The Teutonic Knights, a crusading military order, had conquered pagan Prussia and for a century raided Lithuania, particularly the region of Samogitia, which separated their Prussian and Livonian territories. The 1385 Union of Kreva, which united Poland and Lithuania under a Christian king, removed the religious justification for the Knights' campaigns. However, the Order contested the sincerity of the conversion and maintained control over Samogitia. Poland also had claims to Dobrzyń Land and Danzig (Gdańsk), while the Knights controlled vital river mouths for trade.

### The War and Battle of Grunwald
The war began with a Teutonic invasion of Poland in August 1409, following a Samogitian uprising. After a truce, both sides prepared for a major campaign. In 1410, the Polish and Lithuanian armies executed a complex joint march into Prussia, aiming for the Teutonic capital at Marienburg. The Teutonic forces, expecting separate invasions, were outmaneuvered. The two armies met at the Battle of Grunwald on 15 July 1410. The Teutonic Knights were decisively defeated; most of their leadership, including Grand Master von Jungingen, were killed. The allied army then delayed its advance, allowing a defense of Marienburg to be organized. The subsequent siege failed, and the Polish–Lithuanian forces eventually withdrew.

### Aftermath and Consequences
The Peace of Thorn (1411) saw the Knights cede Dobrzyń Land to Poland and renounce claims to Samogitia for the lifetimes of the Polish and Lithuanian rulers. However, the most significant consequence was the crippling financial burden of war reparations. To pay the indemnity, the Knights borrowed heavily, confiscated church treasures, and raised taxes, causing economic decline and internal unrest. The defeat also left them with few forces and difficulty recruiting Christian volunteers, forcing reliance on expensive mercenaries. This decline contributed to the formation of the Prussian Confederation in 1441 and later conflicts.

## Terms
- **Teutonic Knights:** A German crusading military order that established a monastic state in Prussia after conquering pagan Baltic peoples.
- **Personal Union:** A system where two or more states share the same monarch but remain politically separate, as between Poland and Lithuania after 1385.
- **Samogitia:** A region in modern Lithuania, strategically important as it separated the Teutonic Knights' territories in Prussia and Livonia.
- **Battle of Grunwald (1410):** The decisive engagement of the war, resulting in a catastrophic defeat for the Teutonic Knights.
- **Peace of Thorn (1411):** The treaty ending the war, which imposed heavy financial reparations on the defeated Teutonic Order.
- **Prussian Confederation:** An alliance of Prussian cities and nobility formed in 1441 to resist Teutonic rule, leading to later wars.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The reason for the Lithuanian light cavalry's retreat during the Battle of Grunwald is debated: scholars question whether it was a genuine defeat or a preconceived tactical maneuver.
- The delay of the Polish–Lithuanian forces after the battle, which allowed the defense of Marienburg to be organized, has been speculated by some historians to be an intentional strategy to preserve a balance of power, but a lack of primary sources prevents a definitive explanation.

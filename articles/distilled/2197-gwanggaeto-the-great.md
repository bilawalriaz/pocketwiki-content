# Gwanggaeto the Great

## Overview
Gwanggaeto the Great (r. 391–412) was the 19th monarch of the Korean kingdom of Goguryeo. His reign marked the beginning of Goguryeo's golden age, transforming it into a powerful empire and a dominant force in East Asia through extensive military campaigns that expanded its territory across Manchuria, the Korean Peninsula, and beyond.

## Timeline
- **374** — Gwanggaeto is born.
- **391** — Succeeds his father, King Gogukyang, and adopts the imperial title "Taewang" (Supreme King).
- **392** — Launches a major campaign against Baekje, capturing 10 walled cities.
- **396** — Defeats Baekje, capturing its capital Wiryeseong (present-day Seoul).
- **395** — Invades the Khitan Baili clan to the west, destroying 3 tribes.
- **398** — Conquers the Sushen people to the northeast.
- **400** — Defeats a Baekje-Gaya-Wa coalition to aid Silla, making it a protectorate.
- **402** — Conquers a fortress from the Xianbei state of Later Yan.
- **404** — Defeats an invasion by the Wa (Japanese archipelago).
- **405-406** — Repels Later Yan attacks and conquers the Liaodong Peninsula.
- **410** — Attacks Eastern Buyeo to the northeast.
- **412** — Dies of an unknown illness; succeeded by his son Jangsu.

## Body
### Background and Rise to Power
At Gwanggaeto's birth in 374, Goguryeo was weakened after a major defeat by the rival kingdom of Baekje in 371. His grandfather, King Gogukwon, was slain and the capital Pyongyang sacked. Goguryeo's subsequent kings, Sosurim and Gogukyang, focused on internal reforms, including spreading Buddhism and instituting military changes, which laid the groundwork for future expansion. Gwanggaeto succeeded his father in 391, immediately asserting Goguryeo's imperial status by adopting the era name Yeongnak and the title "Taewang," declaring equality with Chinese emperors.

### Campaigns Against Baekje and Southern Korea
Gwanggaeto's primary southern rival was Baekje. He launched repeated campaigns, culminating in the 396 capture of its capital, Wiryeseong, on the Han River. This victory severely weakened Baekje. In 400, when Silla (another Korean kingdom) requested aid against a Baekje-led coalition, Gwanggaeto sent 50,000 troops, crushed the alliance, and established Silla as a protectorate, effectively subduing the other Korean kingdoms and bringing much of the peninsula under Goguryeo's influence.

### Northern and Western Conquests
Gwanggaeto simultaneously expanded westward and northward. He defeated the Khitan tribes (395) and conquered the Sushen people (398). A major conflict with the Xianbei-led state of Later Yan resulted in Goguryeo's conquest of the Liaodong Peninsula by 406, recovering the ancient domain of Gojoseon. He also repelled attacks from Later Yan and the Wa, and in 410 attacked Eastern Buyeo, further consolidating control over Manchuria and surrounding regions.

### Death and Legacy
Gwanggaeto died in 412 at age 38. His conquests—reportedly 64 walled cities and 1,400 villages—marked the zenith of Goguryeo's power and are considered a high point in Korean history. He is one of only two Korean rulers honored with the title "the Great." His achievements are recorded on the Gwanggaeto Stele, erected in 414 by his son Jangsu, which is the largest engraved stele in the world. He remains a potent symbol of Korean nationalism.

## Terms
- **Goguryeo**: One of the Three Kingdoms of Korea, located in the northern part of the peninsula and Manchuria.
- **Taewang**: An imperial title meaning "Supreme King" or "Emperor," asserting equality with Chinese rulers.
- **Yeongnak**: The era name adopted by Gwanggaeto, meaning "Eternal Rejoicing."
- **Baekje**: A powerful rival Korean kingdom in the southwest, dominant before Gwanggaeto's reign.
- **Silla**: A Korean kingdom in the southeast that became a Goguryeo protectorate.
- **Xianbei**: A proto-Mongol nomadic people who founded the Later Yan state.
- **Wa**: A term for people from the Japanese archipelago, often involved in conflicts on the Korean peninsula.
- **Liaodong Peninsula**: A strategically important region in present-day China, conquered by Gwanggaeto.
- **Gwanggaeto Stele**: A massive stone monument erected in 414 detailing Gwanggaeto's reign and achievements.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The identity of the enemy in Gwanggaeto's 407 campaign of 50,000 troops is debated; it could be Later Yan, Baekje, or the Wa.
- Passages on the Gwanggaeto Stele concerning the Wa are subject to differing interpretations by Korean and Japanese scholars.
- The exact maximum western extent of Goguryeo under Gwanggaeto is uncertain, though archaeological evidence of fortress ruins in Mongolia suggests it may have reached further than traditionally recorded.

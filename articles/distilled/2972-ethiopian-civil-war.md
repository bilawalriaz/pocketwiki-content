# Ethiopian Civil War

## Overview
The Ethiopian Civil War (1974–1991) was a protracted conflict between the Marxist–Leninist military junta known as the Derg and a coalition of anti-government rebel groups. It began with the Derg's overthrow of Emperor Haile Selassie and ended with the junta's collapse, reshaping Ethiopia's political structure and resulting in massive human and economic costs.

## Timeline
- **12 September 1974** — The Derg overthrows Emperor Haile Selassie in a coup d'état.
- **21 March 1975** — The Derg abolishes the monarchy and adopts Marxism–Leninism as state ideology.
- **1976** — The Derg initiates the Qey Shibir (Red Terror) campaign of political repression.
- **13 July 1977** — Somalia invades the Ogaden region, triggering the Ogaden War.
- **Early 1978** — Soviet and Cuban military intervention helps Ethiopia repel Somali forces.
- **1983–1985** — A devastating famine affects millions, killing an estimated 400,000–590,000 people.
- **1987** — The Derg dissolves, establishing the civilian People's Democratic Republic of Ethiopia (PDRE).
- **15–19 February 1989** — Eritrean rebels win the decisive Battle of Shire.
- **28 May 1991** — The EPRDF coalition captures Addis Ababa; President Mengistu Haile Mariam flees.

## Background
By the 1960s, the Ethiopian Empire under Haile Selassie faced widespread discontent due to political stagnation, economic problems, and human rights abuses. A radicalized student movement and a failed 1960 military coup highlighted the regime's fragility. The government's increasing reliance on the military for repression politicized the armed forces. Regional insurgencies, notably the Eritrean War of Independence (since 1961) and the Ogaden insurgency, further destabilized the empire.

## The War
### The Derg's Rise and the Red Terror
The Derg, a committee of low-ranking military officers, seized power in September 1974. It nationalized industries and land, implementing a socialist program. Facing challenges from various opposition groups, the Derg launched the Qey Shibir (Red Terror) in 1976, a campaign of violent repression primarily targeting rival Marxist groups like the EPRP. The campaign, escalated under Mengistu Haile Mariam from 1977, involved brutal tactics and resulted in tens of thousands of deaths, alienating many civilians and driving support toward rebel fronts.

### Regional Conflicts and Foreign Intervention
The Derg fought simultaneous insurgencies. In Eritrea, the Eritrean People's Liberation Front (EPLF) fought for independence. In the south, the Western Somali Liberation Front (WSLF) sought to join a Greater Somalia. In 1977, Somalia invaded the Ogaden to support the WSLF, initially succeeding. This triggered a massive Soviet and Cuban military intervention that repelled Somali forces by early 1978. The Derg then redirected its forces to Eritrea, reversing rebel gains made during the Ogaden diversion.

### Famine, Decline, and Collapse
By the mid-1980s, Derg policies, counterinsurgency campaigns, and a severe 1983–1985 famine caused widespread devastation and economic decline. The famine killed hundreds of thousands and created a large diaspora. Rebel groups, including the Tigrayan People's Liberation Front (TPLF) and EPLF, gained strength and popular support. The Derg attempted to civilianize its rule in 1987, forming the PDRE, but this failed to stem opposition. As Soviet support waned, the EPRDF coalition, led by the TPLF, launched a final offensive, capturing Addis Ababa on 28 May 1991 and ending the war.

## Terms
*   **Derg**: The military junta that ruled Ethiopia from 1974 to 1987, officially the Provisional Military Administrative Council.
*   **Qey Shibir (Red Terror)**: A campaign of violent political repression carried out by the Derg against suspected opponents, primarily in urban areas.
*   **Eritrean People's Liberation Front (EPLF)**: The primary rebel group fighting for Eritrean independence from Ethiopia.
*   **Tigrayan People's Liberation Front (TPLF)**: An ethnic Tigrayan rebel group that became the leading force in the EPRDF coalition.
*   **Ogaden War**: A 1977–1978 conflict where Somalia invaded Ethiopia's Ogaden region to support ethnic Somali insurgents.
*   **Ethiopian People's Revolutionary Democratic Front (EPRDF)**: The coalition of rebel groups, led by the TPLF, that overthrew the Derg regime in 1991.
*   **Neftenya**: A term for Amhara settlers in other regions, often associated with the imperial and Derg-era policies of ethnic domination.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   The exact death toll of the Qey Shibir is debated, with estimates ranging widely from 30,000 to 750,000.
*   The primary causes of the 1983–1985 famine are debated, with arguments focusing on the relative roles of drought versus Derg government policies and counterinsurgency tactics.
*   The legacy of the war and the Derg era remains contentious, with recent events like the Tigray War drawing parallels to past atrocities and highlighting ongoing issues of accountability.

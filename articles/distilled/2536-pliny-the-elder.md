# Pliny the Elder

## Overview
Gaius Plinius Secundus, known as Pliny the Elder (23/24 AD – 79 AD), was a Roman author, naturalist, and naval commander whose encyclopedic work *Naturalis Historia* became a foundational model for later encyclopedias. His life and death, particularly during the eruption of Mount Vesuvius, provide a key window into Roman intellectual and public life in the 1st century AD.

## Timeline
- **c. 23/24 AD** — Born in Como, Gallia Transpadana.
- **c. 46 AD** — Enters the Roman army as a junior officer.
- **c. 47 AD** — Participates in the conquest of the Chauci in Germania Inferior.
- **c. 50 AD** — Serves as military tribune under Pomponius Secundus in Germania Superior.
- **c. 55-58 AD** — Promoted to praefectus alae (cavalry commander) in Germania Inferior.
- **68 AD** — Death of Emperor Nero; end of the Julio-Claudian dynasty.
- **69 AD** — Vespasian becomes emperor after civil war, founding the Flavian dynasty.
- **c. 70-76 AD** — Serves a series of procuratorships in Gaul, Africa, and Hispania.
- **77 AD** — *Naturalis Historia* is dedicated to Emperor Titus and likely published.
- **24 August 79 AD** — Dies at Stabiae while attempting rescue operations during the eruption of Mount Vesuvius.

## Body
### Early Life and Military Career
Pliny was born into an equestrian family in Como. His father took him to Rome for education in law. At about age 23, he began a military career, serving in Germania Inferior and Superior. He participated in campaigns against Germanic tribes and wrote his first work, *De Jaculatione Equestri* (On the Use of the Dart by Cavalry), which is now lost. His military service provided him with firsthand knowledge of geography and natural phenomena, which later informed his writing.

### Literary Work Under Nero and the Flavians
During the reign of Emperor Nero (54-68 AD), Pliny avoided politically sensitive topics, focusing instead on grammar and rhetoric. He wrote *Studiosus* and *Dubii Sermonis*, both lost. After Vespasian became emperor in 69 AD, Pliny was appointed to a series of procuratorships (provincial governorships) across the empire. This period allowed him to gather extensive material for his major works. He completed *Bella Germaniae* (a history of Roman-Germanic wars) and began his encyclopedic project.

### *Naturalis Historia*
Pliny's masterwork, *Naturalis Historia*, is a 37-volume encyclopedia covering the entire field of ancient knowledge, from botany and zoology to art and mining technology. He compiled it from personal observation, his own earlier works, and extracts from hundreds of other authors, which were dictated to scribes. The work was dedicated to Emperor Titus in 77 AD and is the only one of Pliny's seven works to survive. It became a standard reference for centuries and is the sole source for many Roman technological and artistic practices.

### Death at Vesuvius
In 79 AD, while serving as commander of the fleet at Misenum, Pliny observed the eruption of Mount Vesuvius. He set out by ship to investigate and rescue people, including his friend Rectina. He died at Stabiae, likely from toxic gases or a respiratory condition exacerbated by the fumes. His death was famously described in letters by his nephew, Pliny the Younger.

## Terms
- **Equestrian**: The second-highest social class in Rome, below the senatorial class, often involved in business and public service.
- **Procurator**: A governor of an imperial province or an official overseeing financial affairs for the emperor.
- **Praefectus cohortis**: A commander of an infantry cohort, a junior officer rank in the Roman army.
- **Military tribune**: A staff officer position in the Roman army, often a step in the equestrian career path.
- **Praefectus alae**: A commander of a cavalry wing (ala), a senior military position.
- **Encyclopedist**: A person who compiles knowledge from various fields into a comprehensive reference work.
- **Plinian eruption**: A catastrophic volcanic eruption style named after Pliny's detailed description of the 79 AD Vesuvius event.
- **Gallia Transpadana**: A region of northern Italy (beyond the Po River) that was part of Cisalpine Gaul.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Number of Procuratorships**: Scholars debate whether Pliny held two, three, or four procuratorships. The exact sequence and dates of his service in Gallia Narbonensis, Africa, Hispania Tarraconensis, and Gallia Belgica are reconstructed from his writings and are not all definitively attested.
- **Cause of Death**: While Pliny the Younger attributed his uncle's death to toxic volcanic gases, later historians have suggested alternatives like a stroke or heart attack, possibly triggered by the fumes.
- **Composition of *Naturalis Historia***: The exact timeline for the writing of the encyclopedia is unknown. It was likely begun after 70 AD, but the precise order and dates of its individual books are determined by scholarly analysis rather than clear historical record.

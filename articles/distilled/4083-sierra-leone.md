# Sierra Leone

## Overview
Sierra Leone is a West African nation with a complex history shaped by European colonization, the slave trade, and a devastating civil war. Its modern identity is defined by a diverse population, a post-conflict recovery, and significant challenges in governance and development. Understanding its history is key to grasping the roots of its current social and political landscape.

- **1787** — The "Province of Freedom" settlement is founded by the British for the "Black Poor" of London.
- **1792** — Nova Scotian Black Loyalists found Freetown.
- **1808** — The coastal Sierra Leone Colony becomes a British Crown Colony.
- **1896** — The inland Sierra Leone Protectorate is created following the Berlin Conference.
- **1961** — Sierra Leone gains independence from the United Kingdom.
- **1971** — The country becomes a presidential republic under Siaka Stevens.

- **2002** — The civil war ends following international intervention.
- **2014** — A major Ebola virus outbreak occurs in the country.
- **2018** — Julius Maada Bio is elected president.

## Body

### Early History and European Contact
Sierra Leone has been inhabited for at least 2,500 years. Its dense rainforests and the tsetse fly provided natural defenses against large empires like Mali. Islam was introduced by Susu traders in the 18th century. European interaction began in 1462 with Portuguese explorer Pedro de Sintra. European traders established posts primarily for the slave trade, which profoundly shaped the region's socio-economic landscape.

### The Founding of Freetown and the Creole People
The modern state's foundations were laid by British resettlement schemes. In 1787, the "Province of Freedom" was established for London's "Black Poor," including African Americans who had fought for the British in the American Revolutionary War. This initial settlement struggled. In 1792, Nova Scotian Black Loyalists, facing hardship in Canada, founded the permanent settlement of Freetown. Later, Jamaican Maroons and thousands of "Liberated Africans" (freed from illegal slave ships after the 1807 abolition of the slave trade) arrived. These groups intermarried and formed the Creole (Krio) people and the Krio language.

### Colonial Era and the Hut Tax War
In 1808, the coastal area became a British Crown Colony. In 1896, following the Berlin Conference, Britain annexed the interior as a Protectorate. This dual administration created tensions. In 1898, a new "hut tax" imposed by the British sparked the Hut Tax War, a major resistance led by Temne chief Bai Bureh in the north and Mende chiefs in the south. The British suppression of the war ended mass resistance but intermittent unrest continued. The colonial period saw the rise of Freetown as an educational hub, notably with the founding of Fourah Bay College in 1827.

### Independence, Coups, and Civil War
Sierra Leone gained independence in 1961 under Prime Minister Milton Margai. In 1971, under Prime Minister Siaka Stevens, it became a presidential republic. Stevens' rule grew increasingly authoritarian, and in 1978, the APC became the sole legal party. He was succeeded by Joseph Saidu Momoh in 1985. In 1991, a brutal civil war erupted between the government and the RUF rebel group, led by Foday Sankoh and backed by Liberia's Charles Taylor. The conflict, marked by extreme violence and multiple coups, lasted 11 years. International intervention, notably by the United Kingdom and a UN peacekeeping force, was crucial in defeating the RUF and ending the war in 2002.

### Post-War Recovery and Modern Challenges
Since 2002, Sierra Leone has experienced relative stability and returned to multi-party democracy. However, it faces immense challenges. The economy is heavily dependent on mining, particularly diamonds, and agriculture. As of 2019, 59.2% of the population was affected by multidimensional poverty. The country was severely impacted by the 2014 Ebola epidemic. In 2022, violent protests over the cost of living resulted in dozens of deaths. The nation continues to work on strengthening its institutions and improving living standards.

## Terms
- **Creole (Krio):** An ethnic group descended from freed African-American, Afro-Caribbean slaves, and liberated Africans who settled in Freetown. They speak Krio, an English-based creole language that is the national lingua franca.
- **Hut Tax War (1898):** A conflict sparked by the British colonial government's imposition of a tax on dwellings, leading to armed resistance by Temne and Mende chiefs.
- **Liberated Africans:** Thousands of formerly enslaved people rescued by the Royal Navy from illegal slave ships after the 1807 abolition of the slave trade and resettled in Freetown.
- **Paramount Chief:** A traditional leader of a chiefdom, a system of local governance recognized and incorporated into the British colonial administration.
- **Revolutionary United Front (RUF):** A rebel group that fought against the Sierra Leone government from 1991 to 2002, notorious for its brutal tactics and use of "blood diamonds."
- **Blood Diamonds:** Diamonds mined in a war zone and sold to finance an insurgency, notably used by the RUF during Sierra Leone's civil war.
- **Multi-dimensional poverty:** A measure of poverty that considers deprivations in health, education, and living standards, not just income.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The effectiveness of post-war recovery efforts is debated, given persistent high poverty, corruption, and the 2014 Ebola crisis and 2022 protests.
- The historical impact of the dual Colony/Protectorate administration and the Hut Tax War on modern ethnic and regional political divisions is a subject of scholarly analysis.
- The role of "blood diamonds" in fueling the civil war and the success of subsequent international certification schemes to regulate the trade remain significant topics.

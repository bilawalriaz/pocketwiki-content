# Berlin

## Overview
Berlin is the capital and largest city of Germany, a major European hub for culture, politics, media, and science. Its modern identity is shaped by 20th-century history: its role as the German Empire's capital, devastation in World War II, Cold War division, and 1990 reunification. Today, it is a global city known for its vibrant arts scene, innovative economy, and diverse population.

## Timeline
- **1237** — First documented mention of Berlin (or its twin town Cölln).
- **1417–1701** — Capital of the Margraviate of Brandenburg.
- **1701–1918** — Capital of the Kingdom of Prussia.
- **1871–1918** — Capital of the German Empire.
- **1920** — Greater Berlin Act incorporates suburbs, expanding the city's area and population.
- **1933–1945** — Capital of Nazi Germany; site of the 1936 Summer Olympics.
- **1945** — Divided into four Allied occupation sectors after World War II.
- **1948–1949** — Berlin Blockade and the subsequent Berlin Airlift.
- **1961** — Construction of the Berlin Wall begins.
- **1989** — Fall of the Berlin Wall.
- **1990** — German reunification; Berlin becomes the capital of a unified Germany again.
- **1999** — Relocation of the German federal government and parliament to Berlin is mostly completed.

## Body

### History and Etymology
The area has been settled for millennia, with Slavic tribes present before German rule began in the 12th century under the Margraviate of Brandenburg. The name "Berlin" likely derives from a West Slavic root meaning "swamp." The twin towns of Berlin and Cölln, first documented in 1237, grew due to their location on key trade routes. The Hohenzollern dynasty ruled from Berlin from 1415 until 1918, establishing it as the capital of Brandenburg, then Prussia, and finally the German Empire in 1871.

The 20th century defined modern Berlin. It was a center of the Roaring Twenties and the Weimar Republic before becoming the capital of Nazi Germany. The city was heavily bombed in World War II, killing around 125,000 civilians. After the war, it was divided into West Berlin (aligned with the US, UK, and France) and East Berlin (Soviet sector). The Berlin Wall, built in 1961, physically divided the city until its fall in 1989. Reunification in 1990 restored Berlin as the capital of Germany, leading to major redevelopment, including the completion of Berlin Brandenburg Airport in 2020.

### Geography and Climate
Berlin is located in northeastern Germany on the Northern European Plain, with a mainly flat topography. It is built along the Spree River, which flows into the Havel in the west. About one-third of the city's area consists of forests, parks, lakes, and waterways, with the Müggelsee being the largest lake. The highest point is the man-made Arkenberge hills (122 m).

The city has an oceanic climate bordering on a humid continental climate, featuring mild to warm summers and cold winters. Annual precipitation is modest at 570 mm. The urban heat island effect can make temperatures up to 4°C higher in the city than in surrounding areas.

### Demographics and Society
With 3.897 million registered inhabitants (2024), Berlin is the most populous city in the European Union. Its population is highly diverse, shaped by historical immigration waves, including French Huguenots in the 17th century and Turkish "guest workers" in the 1960s/70s. As of 2019, about 777,000 residents held foreign nationality. German is the official language, but Turkish, English, Polish, and Arabic are widely spoken. The city is religiously diverse, with about 37% of residents belonging to a church or religious organization (2011 census), including significant Protestant, Catholic, and Muslim communities.

### Government and Politics
Berlin is one of Germany's 16 states, governed by a Senate led by a Governing Mayor. The state parliament is the Abgeordnetenhaus. The city is subdivided into 12 boroughs, each with its own council. As the national capital, Berlin hosts the offices of the German President, Chancellor, and Parliament (Bundestag), as well as 158 foreign embassies.

### Economy and Infrastructure
Berlin's economy is based on the service sector, with important industries including life sciences, information technology, media, and tourism. In 2025, its GDP was €218.3 billion. The city is a major startup hub and home to companies like Siemens, Deutsche Bahn, and Zalando. Tourism is significant, with 28.7 million overnight hotel stays recorded in 2014.

The city has a complex public transport network managed by BVG and Deutsche Bahn, including the U-Bahn (subway), S-Bahn (suburban rail), trams, and buses. Berlin Brandenburg Airport (BER) is the main international airport. The city is also known for its extensive bicycle lane system.

### Culture and Education
Berlin is a global cultural center, home to 138 museums and over 400 art galleries, including the UNESCO World Heritage Site Museum Island. Its nightlife is internationally renowned. The city hosts the Berlinale film festival and has a vibrant performing arts scene with numerous theaters and orchestras, such as the Berlin Philharmonic.

The Berlin-Brandenburg region is a major center for higher education and research, with four public universities (including Humboldt University and the Free University) and institutions like the Max Planck Society. The Charité is one of Europe's largest university hospitals. Historically, 67 Nobel laureates are affiliated with Berlin-based institutions.

## Terms
*   **Gründerzeit:** A period of rapid industrialization and economic boom in Germany from the 1870s until World War I, which triggered Berlin's population growth.
*   **Hohenzollern:** The German dynasty that ruled Brandenburg, Prussia, and the German Empire from Berlin from 1415 to 1918.
*   **Greater Berlin Act (1920):** The law that incorporated dozens of surrounding cities and villages into Berlin, vastly increasing its area and population.
*   **Berlin Blockade (1948–49):** The Soviet attempt to cut off Allied access to West Berlin, countered by the Berlin Airlift.
*   **Berlin Wall (1961–1989):** The barrier erected by East Germany that physically divided Berlin into East and West during the Cold War.
*   **Bundestag:** The lower house of the German Parliament, which has been seated in the Reichstag building in Berlin since 1999.
*   **S-Bahn:** An urban rail system serving Berlin and its surrounding metropolitan area.
*   **Charité:** A major university hospital in Berlin, one of the largest in Europe, with a history dating back to 1710.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   The etymology of "Berlin" is debated, with a connection to the Old Polabian word for "swamp" being a leading theory.
*   The completion of the government's relocation from Bonn to Berlin remains an ongoing discussion, with some ministries still based in the former capital.
*   The city's rapid growth and popularity have led to debates about housing affordability, with around 20% of residents at risk of poverty and a similar share spending at least 40% of their income on rent (2025 Social Report).

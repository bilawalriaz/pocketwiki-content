# Northern Mariana Islands

## Overview
The Commonwealth of the Northern Mariana Islands (CNMI) is an unincorporated U.S. territory in the western Pacific Ocean, comprising 14 islands north of Guam. It is significant as a former Spanish, German, and Japanese colony that became a U.S. commonwealth in 1986, granting its residents U.S. citizenship. Its history reflects broader patterns of colonialism, strategic conflict in World War II, and the complexities of territorial governance within the United States.

## Timeline
- **c. 1500–1400 BC** — Islands first settled by people from the Philippines.
- **1521** — Ferdinand Magellan claims the archipelago for Spain.
- **1899** — Spain sells the Northern Marianas to Germany.
- **1918** — Japan gains control under a League of Nations mandate.
- **1944** — U.S. forces capture Saipan and Tinian in the Battle of Saipan.
- **1947** — Islands become part of the U.S.-administered UN Trust Territory of the Pacific Islands (TTPI).
- **1975** — Voters approve a covenant to become a U.S. commonwealth.
- **1986** — The CNMI becomes a U.S. territory; residents gain U.S. citizenship.
- **2009** — The CNMI elects its first non-voting delegate to the U.S. House of Representatives.

## Body

### History and Colonial Rule
The Mariana Islands were the first remote Oceanian islands settled by humans, beginning around 1500–1400 BC. Spanish colonization began in 1521, leading to a catastrophic population decline from disease and conflict. In the 17th century, Spain forcibly relocated most Chamorros to Guam, allowing Carolinian immigration to the northern islands. Spain sold the Northern Marianas to Germany in 1899. After World War I, Japan acquired the islands as a mandate, developing a sugar cane economy and a large settler population. During World War II, Japan used the islands as a military base; the 1944 Battle of Saipan was a major U.S. victory. Post-war, the islands were administered by the U.S. as part of the UN Trust Territory of the Pacific Islands.

### Path to Commonwealth Status
After World War II, several referendums on integration with Guam failed when Guam rejected the proposals. In the 1970s, the Northern Marianas chose to seek closer ties with the U.S. rather than independence. A covenant to establish a commonwealth was approved in 1975, and a new government took effect in 1978. The UN trusteeship ended in 1990, and the CNMI came fully under U.S. sovereignty on November 4, 1986, with its residents becoming U.S. citizens.

### Geography and Climate
The CNMI consists of 14 main islands, with a total area of 183.5 square miles. The southern islands are limestone, while the northern islands are volcanic, with active volcanoes on Anatahan, Pagan, and Agrihan. The highest point is Mount Agrihan at 3,166 feet. The climate is tropical rainforest, moderated by trade winds, with a dry season from December to June and a rainy season from July to November that can include typhoons.

### Politics and Government
The CNMI is a presidential representative democracy with a separation of powers. Its government consists of an executive branch led by a governor, a bicameral legislature, and a judicial system. The current governor is David M. Apatang. The territory is divided into four municipalities: Saipan, Tinian, Rota, and the sparsely populated Northern Islands. Under the covenant, only certain U.S. constitutional provisions apply, and federal laws apply only if they are uniform across all states. Residents are exempt from U.S. federal income tax.

### Economy
The economy has historically relied on tourism and garment manufacturing. The garment industry, which exploited lower labor standards, collapsed after WTO quotas were lifted in 2005 and U.S. minimum wage laws were phased in, reaching parity in 2015. Tourism, primarily from Japan, South Korea, and China, is a key sector but has faced declines. Agriculture, including cattle ranching on Tinian, is important locally but represents a small portion of GDP.

### Demographics and Culture
The 2020 population was 47,329, a decline from 2000 due to economic changes. The population is diverse, with Filipino, Chamorro, Chinese, and Carolinian ethnic groups. Official languages are English, Chamorro, and Carolinian. Roman Catholicism is the predominant religion. The culture blends indigenous Chamorro and Carolinian traditions with Spanish, Japanese, and American influences.

## Terms
- **Commonwealth**: A political relationship with the United States where the territory has a high degree of self-governance but is not a sovereign state.
- **Unincorporated territory**: A U.S. territory to which only parts of the U.S. Constitution apply.
- **Covenant**: The 1976 agreement that established the political union between the CNMI and the United States.
- **Trust Territory of the Pacific Islands (TTPI)**: A UN trust territory administered by the U.S. after World War II, which included the CNMI.
- **Chamorro**: The indigenous people of the Mariana Islands.
- **Carolinian (Refaluwasch)**: A Micronesian ethnic group that migrated to the Northern Marianas in the 19th century.
- **Latte stone**: A megalithic pillar used by ancient Chamorros as a foundation for buildings.
- **Non-voting delegate**: A representative from a U.S. territory who can serve in Congress but cannot vote on the House floor.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Political status**: The CNMI's relationship with the U.S. continues to evolve, with ongoing discussions about the scope of federal authority, particularly regarding immigration and labor laws.
- **Economic sustainability**: The territory faces challenges in diversifying its economy beyond tourism and managing the impacts of global economic shifts.
- **Cultural preservation**: Efforts continue to preserve the Chamorro and Carolinian languages and traditions amidst significant demographic and cultural change.

# Leopold I, Holy Roman Emperor

## Overview
Leopold I (1640–1705) was Holy Roman Emperor from 1658 until his death, a pivotal Habsburg ruler whose long reign was defined by major wars against the Ottoman Empire and France. His reign saw the recovery of Hungary from Ottoman control and the beginning of the War of the Spanish Succession, shaping the balance of power in Europe. He was also a noted composer and patron of music.

- **5 May 1705** — Dies in Vienna.

## Body

### Early Life and Accession
Leopold was the second son of Emperor Ferdinand III and was initially educated for a career in the clergy. This plan ended in 1654 when his elder brother died, making Leopold the heir. He was elected king of Hungary (1655), Bohemia (1656), and Croatia (1657) before being elected Holy Roman Emperor in 1658. To secure his election, he promised France not to aid Spain, marking the start of a lifelong rivalry with Louis XIV. His education left him deeply pious, knowledgeable in theology and sciences, but also reserved and socially awkward.

### Wars with France and the Ottoman Empire
Leopold's reign was dominated by two major conflicts. In the east, he fought the Ottoman Empire. After initial wars, the Ottomans besieged Vienna in 1683. The siege was broken by an allied army led by John III Sobieski of Poland. Subsequent Imperial victories, led by commanders like Prince Eugene of Savoy, culminated in the Treaty of Karlowitz (1699), which transferred almost all of Hungary to Habsburg control.

In the west, Leopold fought three wars against France: the Franco-Dutch War (1672–1678), the Nine Years' War (1689–1697), and the War of the Spanish Succession (1701–1714). The last war began when Leopold refused to accept the will of the childless Spanish king Charles II, which left the throne to a French prince. Leopold claimed it for his own son, Charles. The war continued after his death, ending with the Treaty of Rastatt (1714), which did not give Austria the decisive victory it sought.

### Internal Rule and the Empire
Leopold governed his hereditary lands with absolutist tendencies, strengthened by the post-Thirty Years' War decline of Protestant nobility. He faced persistent unrest in Hungary, partly due to his suppression of Protestantism. This contributed to a Hungarian revolt allied with the Ottomans, leading to the 1683 siege. After reconquering Hungary, he granted autonomy to Serbs in his domains via the 1690–1691 Edicts.

Within the Holy Roman Empire, Leopold adapted to the post-Westphalia reality where imperial authority was weakened. He made the Imperial Diet at Regensburg a permanent body, a tool for Habsburg influence. Political changes, like creating a new Elector of Hanover (1692) and granting the Prussian kingship (1700), further diluted imperial authority but were necessary to secure allies for his wars.

### Character and Legacy
Leopold was industrious, deeply religious, and tenacious of his rights, embodying the *pietas Austriaca* (loyal Catholic attitude of the Habsburgs). Physically, he was short with a pronounced Habsburg jaw. Though not a military man by inclination, he was a warrior-king by circumstance. He was also a talented composer and major patron of music, enriching the Viennese court. His reign solidified Habsburg control over Hungary and Bohemia but left the Empire more fragmented.

## Terms
*   **Holy Roman Emperor:** Elected monarch of the Holy Roman Empire, a multi-ethnic complex of territories in Central Europe.
*   **Habsburg:** A European royal dynasty of German origin that provided rulers for the Holy Roman Empire, Spain, and Austria for centuries.
*   **Pietas Austriaca:** The distinctive, staunchly Catholic loyalty and piety of the Habsburg dynasty.
*   **Grand Alliance:** A coalition formed in 1689, including the Holy Roman Empire, England, Spain, and others, to oppose the expansionist policies of France's Louis XIV.
*   **Treaty of Karlowitz (1699):** Ended the Great Turkish War, ceding most of Hungary from Ottoman to Habsburg control.
*   **War of the Spanish Succession (1701–1714):** A major European conflict triggered by the disputed succession to the Spanish throne after the death of the childless Charles II of Spain.
*   **Réunions:** A policy of Louis XIV of France to annex territories on his borders by legal pretext, which alarmed the Holy Roman Empire.
*   **Counter-Reformation:** The Catholic Church's response to the Protestant Reformation, which Leopold fervently supported.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   The article notes that Leopold's disdain for the German language in favor of Italian is considered by some historians to be exaggerated.
*   The impact of his clerical education on his rule is debated; it made him pious and knowledgeable but may have instilled a fatalistic streak that made him reject compromise, particularly on religious issues.
*   The overall assessment of his reign is mixed: he was successful against the Ottomans but his wars with France were costly and inconclusive, and his policies weakened the authority of the emperor within the Holy Roman Empire.

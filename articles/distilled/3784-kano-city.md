# Kano (city)

## Overview
Kano is a major city in northern Nigeria and a historic center of trans-Saharan trade, Islamic scholarship, and Hausa political power. Its continuous settlement for over a millennium, strategic location, and role as a commercial hub have made it one of West Africa's most historically significant urban centers.

## Timeline
- **7th century** — Dala Hill is a site of a hunting/gathering community engaged in ironwork.
- **999** — Bagauda becomes the first king of Kano, beginning the Bagauda dynasty.
- **12th century** — King Ali Yaji converts to Islam, establishing a Sultanate.
- **1463–1499** — Reign of Muhammad Rumfa; Kano's commercial and political peak in the Middle Ages.
- **1805** — Kano is defeated by the Fulani and becomes an Emirate of the Sokoto Caliphate.
- **1903** — British forces capture Kano after the Battle of Kano.
- **1967** — Kano State is created from Northern Nigeria.
- **1980** — Riot led by radical preacher Mohammed Marwa Maitatsine.
- **2001** — Riots over the introduction of Sharia law kill 100 people.
- **2012** — Boko Haram bomb attacks kill up to 162 people.
- **2014** — Emir Ado Bayero dies; Sanusi Lamido Sanusi becomes the new Emir.
- **2019** — Governor Ganduje splits the Kano Emirate into four new emirates.

## Body

### Founding and Early History
Kano's origins trace to a 7th-century community on Dala Hill. According to the Kano Chronicle, the Bagauda dynasty was established in 999. The city's early development involved the construction of defensive walls, begun by King Gijimasu in the 12th century and completed by his son Tsaraki.

### Medieval Islamization and Trade
In the 12th century, King Ali Yaji converted to Islam, transforming Kano into a Sultanate. The city's wealth grew immensely from the trans-Saharan trade. Under King Muhammad Rumfa (1463–1499), Kano reached its medieval zenith, described by Leo Africanus as a center of wealthy merchants and skilled craftsmen. Rumfa expanded the city, built the Kurmi market, and promoted Islamization.

### The Sokoto Caliphate and British Conquest
In 1805, the Fulani jihad led by Usman dan Fodio conquered Kano, making it an emirate within the Sokoto Caliphate. It prospered as a commercial center for textiles, leather, and dyes. In 1903, British forces captured the city, ending its sovereignty. Kano initially became the administrative center of Northern Nigeria before being replaced by other cities.

### Modern Era and Political Tensions
After Nigerian independence, Kano State was created in 1967. The city has faced periodic violence, including the 1980 Maitatsine riot, 2001 Sharia-related riots, and sustained attacks by Boko Haram from 2012 onward. The traditional institution has also been a site of political conflict, exemplified by the 2019 balkanization of the Emirate and the 2020 dethronement of Emir Sanusi Lamido Sanusi.

### Economy and Culture
Kano's economy has been rooted in trade for centuries, producing goods like sandals, hides, and textiles for export. It remains a major commercial hub, though manufacturing is hampered by inconsistent policies and power supply. Culturally, it is known for the annual Durbar festival, a spectacular equestrian parade, and its distinctive Sudano-Sahelian architecture, including the Ancient Kano City Walls.

## Terms
- **Emirate:** A territory or state ruled by an emir, a title for a Muslim ruler or commander.
- **Hausa:** A major ethnic group in West Africa, primarily in northern Nigeria and southern Niger.
- **Sokoto Caliphate:** A 19th-century Islamic empire in West Africa founded by Usman dan Fodio's jihad.
- **Trans-Saharan trade:** The historical trade routes crossing the Sahara Desert, connecting sub-Saharan Africa with North Africa and the Mediterranean.
- **Sudano-Sahelian architecture:** A style of building using mudbrick and other local materials, common in the Sahel region.
- **Durbar:** A traditional festival involving a ceremonial horse parade, often held to mark Islamic holidays.
- **Sharia:** Islamic religious law derived from the Quran and the teachings of the Prophet Muhammad.
- **Kano Chronicle:** A historical manuscript detailing the kings and events of the Kano kingdom.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The ethnic identity of Kano's earliest inhabitants on Dala Hill is uncertain; they may have been Hausa or speakers of Niger–Congo languages.
- The precise role and extent of political interference in the 2014 succession of Emir Sanusi Lamido Sanusi are disputed.
- The long-term impact of the 2019 balkanization of the Kano Emirate on traditional authority and social cohesion remains a subject of scholarly and local debate.

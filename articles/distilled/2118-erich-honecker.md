# Erich Honecker

## Overview
Erich Honecker was the hardline communist leader of East Germany (GDR) from 1971 to 1989. He presided over a period of relative economic stability and international recognition but refused to reform the state, leading to his ouster as popular protests and the collapse of the Eastern Bloc swept through the region in 1989. His leadership is defined by the construction of the Berlin Wall, the "order to fire" policy, and the eventual fall of the regime he led.

## Body

### Early Life and Opposition
Honecker was born into a Protestant family in the Saar region. He joined the communist youth movement as a teenager and later studied at the International Lenin School in Moscow. After the Nazi seizure of power in 1933, he worked for the KPD underground. He was arrested in 1935 and sentenced to ten years in prison for treason. He was freed by Soviet troops in April 1945.

### Rise in the SED
After the war, Honecker joined the Ulbricht Group of returning communists. He co-founded and led the Free German Youth (FDJ) until 1955. As the SED's Security Secretary, he was the prime organizer of the building of the Berlin Wall in 1961 and bore administrative responsibility for the "order to fire" along the border. In 1971, with Soviet support, he replaced Walter Ulbricht as the SED's General Secretary.

### Leadership and Policy
As leader, Honecker promoted "consumer socialism," improving living standards and housing in exchange for political loyalty. His greatest foreign policy successes were normalizing relations with West Germany via the 1972 Basic Treaty and achieving full UN membership for the GDR. He refused to implement Soviet-style reforms (perestroika and glasnost) in the late 1980s, citing support from other hardline leaders like Kim Il Sung and Nicolae Ceaușescu.

### Downfall and Resignation
In 1989, mass protests erupted and tens of thousands of East Germans fled via Hungary and Czechoslovakia. Honecker, sidelined by illness, failed to respond decisively. After the GDR's 40th anniversary celebrations were disrupted by chants for Gorbachev's help, the SED Politburo forced him to resign on 18 October 1989, citing "ill health." The Berlin Wall fell three weeks later.

### Exile, Trial, and Death
After German reunification, Honecker sought asylum in the Chilean embassy in Moscow. He was extradited to Germany in 1992 to stand trial for manslaughter related to deaths at the Berlin Wall. The trial was abandoned in 1993 after a court ruled that continuing it would violate his human dignity, as he was terminally ill with liver cancer. He was released and exiled to Chile, where he died in 1994.

## Terms
- **SED (Socialist Unity Party of Germany):** The communist party that ruled East Germany as a single-party state.
- **National Defence Council:** The supreme command body for East Germany's armed forces, chaired by Honecker.
- **State Council:** The collective head of state of East Germany; Honecker became its chairman in 1976.
- **"Order to fire" (Schießbefehl):** The official policy authorizing border guards to use lethal force against anyone attempting to cross the Inner German border illegally.
- **Basic Treaty (1972):** The agreement that normalized relations between East and West Germany, recognizing each other's existence.
- **Perestroika and Glasnost:** The political and economic liberalization reforms introduced in the Soviet Union by Mikhail Gorbachev in the 1980s.
- **Unrechtsstaat:** A German term meaning "unlawful state," used to describe the legal and moral illegitimacy of the East German government.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Responsibility for the Wall:** Honecker claimed political but not legal or moral guilt for the Berlin Wall and border deaths, arguing the decision was a collective Warsaw Pact action to prevent a third world war. Courts and historians largely reject this, holding him directly responsible.
- **Role of Illness:** Honecker's serious illness in 1989 (a missed cancer diagnosis) coincided with the regime's crisis, raising questions about how his incapacity affected the Politburo's ability to respond to protests.
- **Trial Legality:** The abandonment of his trial on human dignity grounds remains controversial, with victims' groups and some politicians protesting the decision to release a dying man accused of serious crimes.

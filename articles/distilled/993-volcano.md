# Volcano

## Overview
A volcano is a crustal vent or fissure through which magma, ash, and gases escape from a subsurface magma chamber. On Earth, most volcanoes form at tectonic plate boundaries—divergent zones (e.g., mid-ocean ridges) producing gentle eruptions, and convergent zones (e.g., the Pacific Ring of Fire) producing violent eruptions. Additional volcanism occurs at hotspots (mantle plumes) and continental rifts. Volcanoes are classified by activity (active, dormant, extinct) and by form (shield, stratovolcano, cinder cone, etc.), and they profoundly influence climate, hazards, and human civilization.

## Timeline
- **Early 17th century** — Term "volcano" derived from Italian island Vulcano, named for Roman god Vulcan
- **79 AD** — Plinian eruption of Mount Vesuvius destroys Pompeii and Herculanum
- **1815** — Mount Tambora eruption causes "Year Without a Summer"
- **1980** — Mount St. Helens erupts, demonstrating cryptodome dynamics
- **1991** — Mount Pinatubo eruption; successful evacuation saves ~20,000 lives
- **2008** — Chaitén volcano erupts unexpectedly, challenging dormancy assumptions
- **2018** — Submarine volcano near Mayotte generates mysterious global humming sounds

## Body

### Plate Tectonics and Volcanic Settings
Earth’s lithosphere is divided into moving plates driven by mantle convection. Most volcanoes occur at plate boundaries: divergent boundaries (mid-ocean ridges) generate new oceanic crust via decompression melting of rising mantle rock; convergent boundaries (subduction zones) produce viscous, gas-rich magma through flux melting of a mantle wedge above a descending oceanic plate. Hotspots, caused by mantle plumes rising from the core-mantle boundary, create volcanic island chains like Hawaii as plates drift over them. Continental rifting involves upwelling mantle beneath continents, potentially splitting plates and forming features like the East African Rift.

### Volcanic Features and Landforms
Volcanoes require a magma chamber, conduit, and vent. Types include:
- **Shield volcanoes**: Broad, gently sloping, formed by fluid basaltic lava (e.g., Hawaiian Islands).
- **Stratovolcanoes (composite)**: Steep-sided, layered with lava and tephra, explosive due to high-silica magma (e.g., Mount Fuji).
- **Cinder cones**: Small, steep-sided cones from pyroclastic eruptions (e.g., Parícutin).
- **Lava domes**: Formed by slow extrusion of viscous felsic lava (e.g., Mount St. Helens).
- **Fissure vents**: Linear eruptions of basaltic lava, often feeding shield volcanoes.
- **Calderas/supervolcanoes**: Collapse features from massive eruptions (>1,000 km³), capable of global climate effects (e.g., Yellowstone).

### Eruption Styles and Materials
Eruptions vary by magma composition and gas content:
- **Effusive**: Low-viscosity basaltic lava flows (Hawaiian style).
- **Explosive**: High-silica magma traps gas, leading to violent eruptions (Plinian, Vulcanian).
- **Phreatomagmatic**: Interaction of magma with groundwater causes steam-driven explosions.
- **Phreatic**: Superheated groundwater explodes without new magma.

Erupted materials include **lava** (magma at surface), **tephra** (fragmental ejecta; ash <2 mm), and **volcanic gases** (steam, CO₂, SO₂). Lava types range from ultramafic (<45% silica) to felsic (>63% silica), influencing viscosity and explosivity.

### Activity Classification
Volcanoes are categorized as:
- **Active**: Currently erupting or showing unrest (earthquakes, gas emissions).
- **Dormant**: No recent activity but potential for future eruptions.
- **Extinct**: No magma supply; unlikely to erupt again.

Definitions overlap; some "dormant" volcanoes unexpectedly reactivate (e.g., Chaitén in 2008). Monitoring uses seismic data, ground deformation, and gas measurements to forecast eruptions, though warning times vary widely.

### Hazards and Benefits
Volcanic hazards include pyroclastic flows, lahars, ashfall, volcanic winter (global cooling from stratospheric aerosols), and acid rain. Historical impacts include the 1815 Tambora eruption causing crop failures and the 1991 Pinatubo eruption lowering global temperatures. Conversely, volcanic soils are highly fertile, and geothermal energy offers renewable power. Volcanic tourism is a major industry.

### Extraterrestrial Volcanism
Volcanoes exist beyond Earth. Venus shows extensive lava plains and possible recent activity. Mars hosts giant shield volcanoes like Olympus Mons. Jupiter’s moon Io is the most volcanically active body, erupting sulfur and silicates. Moons like Europa, Enceladus, and Triton exhibit cryovolcanism—eruptions of water, ammonia, or methane instead of molten rock.

## Terms
- **Magma**: Molten or semi-molten rock beneath Earth’s surface.
- **Tephra**: Fragmental volcanic material ejected during eruptions; ash is <2 mm.
- **Flux melting**: Process where water lowers the melting point of mantle rock in subduction zones.
- **Decade Volcanoes**: 16 volcanoes selected for intensive study due to hazard potential and proximity to populations.
- **Pyroclastic flow**: Fast-moving, destructive avalanche of hot gas, ash, and rock.
- **VEI (Volcanic Explosivity Index)**: Scale from 0 to 8 measuring eruption magnitude.
- **Cryovolcanism**: Eruption of volatile substances (water, ammonia) rather than molten rock.
- **Magma chamber**: Subsurface reservoir of molten rock feeding volcanic systems.
- **Lahar**: Volcanic mudflow of water and loose volcanic material.
- **Hotspot**: Volcanic region thought to be fed by a mantle plume, independent of plate boundaries.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Mantle plume hypothesis**: While widely accepted for hotspot formation (e.g., Hawaii), some argue alternative mechanisms like shallow mantle processes may explain certain features.
- **Dormant vs. extinct classification**: Definitions remain inconsistent among volcanologists; some advocate abandoning "dormant" due to ambiguity.
- **Supervolcano predictability**: Long repose periods (e.g., Yellowstone’s ~700,000 years) make forecasting difficult, raising concerns about sudden reawakening.
- **Extraterrestrial activity**: Ongoing debate over whether Venus and Mars currently host active volcanoes, based on conflicting satellite and atmospheric data.
- **Climate impact quantification**: Uncertainty remains in modeling how volcanic aerosols affect long-term climate change and extinction events.

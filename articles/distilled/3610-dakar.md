# Dakar

## Overview
Dakar is the capital and largest city of Senegal, situated on the Cap-Vert peninsula, the westernmost point of mainland Africa. Its strategic location has made it a critical hub for trade, colonial administration, and regional politics, evolving from a collection of Lebou villages to a major West African metropolis and financial center.

## Timeline
- **1444** — Portuguese reach the Bay of Dakar.
- **1536** — Portuguese begin using Gorée Island as a base for the slave trade.
- **1677** — France captures Gorée Island from the Dutch.
- **1795** — Lebou people establish an independent theocratic state, the "Lebou Republic," with its capital at Ndakaaru (Dakar).
- **1857** — French establish a military post at Ndakaaru and annex the Lebou Republic.
- **1887** — Dakar is split off from Gorée as a separate French commune.
- **1902** — Dakar replaces Saint-Louis as the capital of French West Africa.
- **1940** — Battle of Dakar: Free French and British forces fail to capture the city from Vichy French control.
- **1944** — Thiaroye mutiny: West African conscripts revolt against poor conditions; French troops open fire.
- **1959-1960** — Dakar is the capital of the short-lived Mali Federation.
- **1960** — Dakar becomes the capital of independent Senegal.
- **2026** — Dakar is scheduled to host the Summer Youth Olympics, the first Olympic event in Africa.

## Body

### Foundation and Colonial Era
The Cap-Vert peninsula was settled by the Lebou people. The Portuguese established a presence on Gorée Island in the 16th century, using it as a base for the Atlantic slave trade until its final abolition in French territories in 1848. France took control of Gorée in 1677 and later annexed the mainland. Dakar grew as a major port and administrative center, replacing Saint-Louis as the capital of French West Africa in 1902. Colonial urbanization was marked by racial segregation, exemplified by the forced relocation of Africans to the Médina quarter after a 1914 plague epidemic, while the Lebou successfully resisted expropriation.

### Independence and Modern Development
Dakar was the capital of the Mali Federation (1959-1960) before becoming the capital of independent Senegal. President Léopold Sédar Senghor envisioned it as a "Sub-Saharan African Athens." The city is a major financial center, hosting the Central Bank of West African States (BCEAO). It has diverse communities, including Lebanese, Moroccan, and French expatriates. Urbanization has sprawled eastward, creating a large conurbation.

### Administration and Geography
Dakar is a commune and a department, divided into four arrondissements and 19 communes d'arrondissement. The Dakar Region encompasses the metropolitan area. The city has a hot semi-arid climate moderated by sea breezes. It is highly vulnerable to future sea level rise due to climate change, with projected damages in the billions of dollars.

### Culture and Landmarks
Dakar's culture emphasizes family and community, with traditional dishes like Cebbu Jën. It is a center for Islamic culture and home to the Layene Brotherhood, a Sufi order. Notable landmarks include the African Renaissance Monument, the Médina quarter, and the UNESCO World Heritage Site of Gorée Island, which preserves the history of the slave trade. The city hosts major festivals like the Dakar Biennale.

### Transport and Economy
Dakar is a transport hub, with the Autonomous Port of Dakar and the terminus of the Dakar-Niger railway. It is the starting point for three Trans-African Highways. Modern infrastructure includes the Train Express Regional (TER) and the Sunu BRT, an electric bus rapid transit network expected to carry 300,000 passengers daily. Blaise Diagne International Airport is the main international gateway.

## Terms
- **Lebou**: An aquacultural subgroup of the Wolof ethnic group, the original settlers of the Cap-Vert peninsula.
- **Gorée Island**: An island off Dakar used by the Portuguese, Dutch, and French as a base for the Atlantic slave trade; now a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
- **Lebou Republic**: An independent, theocratic state established by the Lebou people in 1795, with its capital at Ndakaaru (Dakar).
- **Médina**: A quarter of Dakar created by French colonial authorities in 1914 to relocate the African population after a plague epidemic.
- **CFA franc**: The unified currency of West African states, managed by the Central Bank of West African States (BCEAO) headquartered in Dakar.
- **Layene Brotherhood**: A Sufi tariqa (order) founded in 1883 among the Lebou people in Yoff, Dakar.
- **Commune d'arrondissement**: A local administrative division in Dakar with extensive powers, similar to a London borough.
- **TER (Train Express Regional)**: A modern railway line connecting Dakar to Blaise Diagne International Airport.
- **Sunu BRT**: A 100% electric bus rapid transit network in Dakar.

## Debates and open questions
- The exact death toll of the 1944 Thiaroye mutiny is debated, with official French accounts citing around 35 deaths and army veterans claiming over 300.
- The historical significance and scale of the slave trade on Gorée Island versus the mainland are subjects of ongoing scholarly discussion.

# Florida

## Overview
Florida is a southeastern U.S. state and a peninsula between the Gulf of Mexico and the Atlantic Ocean. It is the third-most populous state, known for its tourism-driven economy, unique subtropical/tropical ecosystems, and a history shaped by Spanish colonization, American expansion, and rapid 20th-century growth.

## Timeline
- **1513** — Juan Ponce de León becomes the first European to land in Florida.
- **1565** — Spain founds St. Augustine, the oldest continuously inhabited European settlement in the U.S.
- **1763** — Spain cedes Florida to Great Britain in the Treaty of Paris.
- **1783** — Spain regains Florida from Britain after the American Revolutionary War.
- **1821** — The U.S. acquires Florida from Spain via the Adams–Onís Treaty.
- **1845** — Florida is admitted as the 27th U.S. state.
- **1861** — Florida secedes from the Union, joining the Confederacy.
- **1868** — Florida is readmitted to the Union after the Civil War.
- **1920s** — A land boom and subsequent hurricanes lead to economic volatility.
- **1945–** — Post-WWII, air conditioning and migration fuel massive population growth.
- **2000** — The presidential election recount in Florida decides the national outcome.

## Body

### History
Native American groups inhabited Florida for at least 14,000 years. Spanish explorer Juan Ponce de León landed in 1513, naming the region *La Pascua Florida*. Spain established St. Augustine in 1565 and controlled the territory, though it was contested by Britain, which held it from 1763 to 1783. The U.S. acquired Florida in 1821. Statehood in 1845 was followed by the Seminole Wars (1816–1858), the longest and most extensive American Indian Wars, which resulted in the forced removal of most Seminoles. Florida seceded in 1861 and was readmitted in 1868.

### Demographics and Culture
Florida's population is diverse, with large Hispanic (especially Cuban and Puerto Rican), African American, and West Indian communities. The state has a high proportion of residents born elsewhere and a significant retiree population. Its culture reflects European, Latino, African, and Indigenous influences. English is the official language, but Spanish is widely spoken.

### Geography and Climate
Florida is the southernmost contiguous state, with the nation's longest coastline. Its climate ranges from humid subtropical in the north to tropical in the south. It is the most hurricane-prone state and hosts unique ecosystems like the Everglades, the largest tropical wilderness in the U.S. The Florida Reef is the only living coral barrier reef in the continental U.S.

### Economy
Florida's economy is the fourth-largest in the U.S., driven by tourism, agriculture (especially citrus), real estate, and transportation. It is a top destination for international visitors, with attractions like Walt Disney World. The state has no personal income tax.

### Government and Politics
Florida's government has three branches. It is a major political swing state, though it has trended Republican in recent elections. The 2000 presidential election recount, decided by the Supreme Court in *Bush v. Gore*, highlighted its pivotal role. The state retains the death penalty.

## Terms
- **Peninsula:** A piece of land almost surrounded by water or projecting out into a body of water.
- **Seminole Wars:** A series of three conflicts (1816–1858) between the U.S. and the Seminole people in Florida, primarily over land and the issue of runaway slaves.
- **Adams–Onís Treaty:** The 1819 agreement by which the U.S. acquired Florida from Spain.
- **Gross State Product (GSP):** The total value of goods and services produced within a state, analogous to GDP for a nation.
- **Everglades:** A vast, slow-moving "river of grass" ecosystem in southern Florida, home to unique wildlife.
- **Florida Reef:** The only living coral barrier reef in the continental United States.
- **Swing State:** A state where the two major political parties have similar levels of support, making it competitive in elections.
- **Bush v. Gore:** The 2000 U.S. Supreme Court case that effectively decided the presidential election by halting a Florida recount.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Historical Interpretation:** Historians debate the primary causes of the Seminole Wars, with traditional views emphasizing Seminole raids and slave havens, while more recent scholarship highlights U.S. settler pressure on Seminole lands.
- **Political Status:** Following the 2022 elections, pundits debate whether Florida has shifted from a perennial swing state to a reliably Republican "red" state.
- **Environmental Threats:** The long-term impact of climate change, including sea-level rise and coral bleaching, on Florida's ecosystems and economy is a critical ongoing concern.

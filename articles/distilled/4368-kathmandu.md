# Kathmandu

## Overview
Kathmandu is the capital and largest city of Nepal, located in the central Kathmandu Valley. It is the nation's primary governmental, financial, industrial, and cultural hub, with a history stretching back to at least the 2nd century AD. The city is a major tourist gateway to the Himalayas and home to several UNESCO World Heritage Sites, reflecting its deep Hindu and Buddhist heritage.

## Timeline
- **c. 2nd century AD** — Earliest archaeological evidence of settlement found in the valley.
- **c. 400 AD** — Licchavi dynasty establishes rule, merging settlements into the city of Kathmandu.
- **1628** — First known Western reference by Portuguese Jesuit Father Joao Cabral.
- **1768** — Gorkha Kingdom conquers the valley, making Kathmandu the capital of modern Nepal.
- **1846** — Kot massacre initiates Rana rule, which lasts until 1951.
- **1951** — End of Rana rule; political modernization begins.
- **1979** — Kathmandu Valley's monument zones inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
- **1995** — Kathmandu Municipality upgraded to a metropolitan city.
- **2001** — Nepalese royal massacre occurs at Narayanhiti Palace.
- **2006** — People's Movement (Jana Andolan II) leads to restoration of parliament.
- **2015** — Major earthquake severely damages historic Durbar Squares.
- **2025** — Gen Z protests lead to the resignation of the prime minister.

## Body

### Etymology and History
The name "Kathmandu" derives from *Kasthamandap*, a 16th-century wooden pavilion in the city. Historically, it was also known as *Kantipur* ("City of Light"). The indigenous Newar people call it *Yẽn*. Archaeological findings date to 185 AD, but the city's recorded history begins with the Licchavi dynasty (c. 400 AD), which unified earlier settlements. The subsequent Malla era (c. 12th–18th centuries) saw intense artistic and architectural development among competing city-states. The Gorkha conquest in 1768 established Kathmandu as the capital of a unified Nepal. The 20th century saw the end of Rana autocracy in 1951, the 2001 royal massacre, and the 2006 revolution that set Nepal on a path to a federal democratic republic.

### Geography and Climate
Kathmandu sits in the northwestern part of the Kathmandu Valley at an average elevation of 1,400 metres (4,600 ft). It is dissected by the Bagmati River and its tributaries. The climate is humid subtropical (Cwa) at lower elevations, with warm days and cool nights. Average annual rainfall is about 1,400 mm, mostly during the monsoon season (June–September). Air pollution is a severe issue, with PM2.5 concentrations far exceeding WHO guidelines.

### Demographics and Society
The city is multi-ethnic, with the native Newar people (24.59%) as the largest group, followed by Bahuns (21.62%) and Chhetri (18.66%). Nepali is the most common mother tongue (62%), followed by Newari (19%). The population is predominantly Hindu, with a significant Vajrayana Buddhist presence. Religious and cultural festivals are central to urban life.

### Economy and Tourism
Kathmandu is Nepal's most important commercial and industrial center, contributing over a third of the national GDP. Key sectors include trade, manufacturing (garments, carpets), and tourism. Tourism is vital, with visitors drawn to the city's cultural heritage and as a base for Himalayan trekking. The Thamel neighborhood is a major tourist hub.

### Architecture and Heritage
The city's architecture reflects a fusion of Hindu and Buddhist traditions from the ancient India-Tibet trade route. The Kathmandu Valley's seven monument zones, including three Durbar Squares (Hanuman Dhoka, Patan, Bhaktapur), Pashupatinath Temple, and the stupas of Swayambhunath and Boudhanath, are UNESCO World Heritage Sites. The 2015 earthquake caused significant damage to many historic structures.

### Government and Infrastructure
Kathmandu is administered by the Kathmandu Metropolitan City (KMC), divided into 32 wards. It is the headquarters of Kathmandu District and hosts the national government. Key infrastructure includes Tribhuvan International Airport and major highways connecting to India and China. The city faces challenges with water supply and waste management.

## Terms
- **Newar**: The indigenous ethnic group of the Kathmandu Valley, with a distinct language (Nepal Bhasa) and culture.
- **Licchavi**: A dynasty that ruled the Kathmandu Valley from c. 400 to 750 AD, marking a classical period of art and architecture.
- **Malla**: A dynasty that ruled the valley from the 12th to 18th centuries, characterized by competitive city-states and flourishing of arts.
- **Durbar Square**: A plaza in front of a royal palace, typically featuring temples and civic buildings; three are preserved in the Kathmandu Valley.
- **Stupa**: A hemispherical Buddhist structure containing relics, used as a place of meditation.
- **Kumari**: A tradition of worshipping a pre-pubescent girl as the incarnation of the divine female energy (devi).
- **Guthi**: A traditional trust or endowment for social, religious, or community purposes.
- **SAARC**: The South Asian Association for Regional Cooperation, headquartered in Kathmandu since 1985.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The exact origins and early history of the valley's settlement are debated, with archaeological evidence dating to the 2nd century AD but legendary accounts describing a much earlier mythical past.
- The traditional selection process for the Kumari is a subject of ongoing cultural and religious discussion, especially after the end of the monarchy.
- The impact of rapid urbanization (4% annual growth as of 2010) on the city's cultural heritage and environment is a major contemporary challenge.

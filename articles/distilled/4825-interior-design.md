# Interior design

## Overview
Interior design is the art and science of enhancing interior spaces to create healthier, more aesthetically pleasing environments. It evolved from instinctive decoration into a multifaceted profession involving conceptual development, space planning, and construction management, distinct from interior decorating.

## Timeline
- **c. 3000 BC** — Ancient Egyptians place "soul houses" in tombs, revealing early interior details.
- **17th-18th C.** — Interior decoration is the concern of homemaker, upholsterer, or craftsman.
- **1851** — Owen Jones decorates the Crystal Palace for the Great Exhibition.
- **1856** — Owen Jones publishes *The Grammar of Ornament*, formulating 37 design principles.
- **1874** — Rhoda and Agnes Garrett become first women to train professionally as home decorators.
- **1897** — Edith Wharton and Ogden Codman publish *The Decoration of Houses*.
- **1899** — Institute of British Decorators is formed.
- **1905** — Elsie de Wolfe designs the Colony Club, gaining recognition.
- **1913** — Elsie de Wolfe publishes *The House in Good Taste*.
- **1925** — Art Deco style is named after the Paris Exposition.
- **1957** — U.S. National Society of Interior Designers is established.
- **1966** — UK Interior Decorators and Designers Association is established.

## Body

### History and Professionalization
Interior design emerged as a distinct profession due to societal development and complex architecture. In ancient India, architects like Vishwakarma also functioned as interior designers. Early evidence includes 5,000-year-old painted walls and Greek/Roman decorative mosaics. By the 17th-18th centuries, decoration was handled by homemaker or craftsman. The mid-to-late 19th century saw expansion as the growing middle class desired domestic trappings of wealth. Large furniture firms began offering full interior design services. This role was later usurped by independent designers, paving the way for professional interior design in the mid-20th century.

### Transition to a Separate Profession
By the early 20th century, amateur advisors and publications challenged retail companies' monopoly. Feminist author Mary Haweis advocated for individualized design. The 1899 formation of the Institute of British Decorators gave impetus to decoration as a separate artistic profession. In America, Candace Wheeler encouraged a new American design style. The 1897 book *The Decoration of Houses* by Edith Wharton and Ogden Codman denounced Victorian style and argued for proper space planning. Pioneering designers like Elsie de Wolfe and Syrie Maugham rejected Victorian heaviness for lighter, more practical designs.

### Establishment and Expansion
The profession became more established after World War II, with increased home spending and the creation of interior design courses and textbooks. Organizations were formed to regulate education and standards, such as the U.S. National Society of Interior Designers (1957) and the UK Interior Decorators and Designers Association (1966). Professional status is achieved through education, self-imposed standards, and gate-keeping organizations.

### Design Principles and Specialties
Interior design uses fundamental principles from visual arts. Color is a powerful tool that affects human behavior and perception of space. Lighting is crucial for enhancing features and setting mood. Specialties include residential design (private homes) and commercial design, which encompasses retail, corporate, healthcare, hospitality, institutional, and exhibition design. Other areas include sustainable design, which prioritizes eco-friendly and energy-efficient principles.

### Education and Profession
Training can be informal (working with a professional) or formal (college, art school, or university degree). Accredited programs provide a standard of excellence. Working conditions vary: designers may be employees, self-employed (32% in 2020), or work on contract. The work can be stressful, involving deadlines, budgets, and client needs.

## Terms
- **Interior Design:** The art and science of enhancing interior spaces for health and aesthetics, involving planning, research, and management.
- **Interior Decorator:** A term, common in the US, for someone who furnishes or adorns a space with decorative elements; decorators do not design layouts or handle technical aspects.
- **Art Deco:** A 1920s-30s style characterized by geometric forms, metallic colors, and new materials like chrome and glass.
- **Space Planning:** The functional layout of spaces within a building, a key emphasis of interior designers.
- **Sustainable Design:** An approach prioritizing eco-friendly, energy-efficient, and functional design.
- **Majlis Painting:** A traditional form of wall decoration in the Asir province of Saudi Arabia, featuring bright geometric patterns.
- **Visual and Spatial Branding:** The use of space to express a corporate brand.

## Debates and Open Questions
The source notes that the profession of interior design is still unregulated in the UK, meaning it is not yet officially a profession there. The distinction between interior design and interior decorating remains a point of clarification, with designers handling technical planning and decorators focusing on adornment.

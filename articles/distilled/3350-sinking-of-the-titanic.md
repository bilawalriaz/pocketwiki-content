# Sinking of the Titanic

## Overview
The RMS Titanic, the largest ocean liner in service, sank on 15 April 1912, four days into its maiden voyage from Southampton to New York. The collision with an iceberg killed up to 1,635 people, a toll compounded by insufficient lifeboats and poor evacuation procedures. It remains one of the deadliest peacetime maritime disasters and spurred major international safety reforms.

## Timeline
- **2 April 1912** — Titanic enters service as the world's largest ship.
- **10 April 1912** — Maiden voyage begins from Southampton; a near-collision with SS *City of New York* causes a brief delay.
- **14 April 1912** — Titanic receives seven ice warnings. At 23:40, lookouts sight an iceberg.
- **15 April 1912, 02:20** — Titanic sinks, breaking apart and plunging to the seabed.
- **15 April 1912, 04:00** — RMS *Carpathia* begins rescuing survivors from lifeboats.
- **18 April 1912** — *Carpathia* arrives in New York with 710 survivors.
- **19 April 1912** — U.S. Senate inquiry begins.
- **2 May 1912** — British inquiry begins.
- **1914** — International Convention for the Safety of Life at Sea (SOLAS) is established.
- **1 September 1985** — The wreck of the Titanic is discovered on the seabed.

## Body

### Background and Voyage
Titanic was the second of three Olympic-class liners built by Harland & Wolff for White Star Line. It was nearly 100 feet longer than previous record holders RMS *Lusitania* and *Mauretania*. The maiden voyage began 10 April 1912 from Southampton, stopping at Cherbourg, France, and Queenstown (now Cobh), Ireland. It carried 892 crew and 1,320 passengers, from millionaires to emigrants. Captain Edward Smith, White Star Line's most senior captain, commanded the ship.

### Ice Warnings and Collision
The North Atlantic had severe ice in April 1912. Titanic received seven ice warnings on 14 April. Despite this, the ship maintained about 22 knots (41 km/h; 25 mph), standard practice then. At 23:40, lookouts Frederick Fleet and Reginald Lee sighted an iceberg. First Officer William Murdoch ordered a hard turn to starboard and engines stopped, but the ship struck the iceberg in a glancing blow. The impact buckled hull plates along a 300-foot starboard section, opening six of sixteen compartments to the sea. Modern analysis suggests damage totaled only about 12–13 square feet (1.1–1.2 m²).

### Flooding and Sinking
Titanic could stay afloat with four forward compartments flooded, but with six breached, it was doomed. Designer Thomas Andrews informed Captain Smith the ship would sink in about two hours. Water flooded at an estimated 7 long tons (7.1 t) per second, overwhelming the pumps. The ship began to list and sink by the bow. At 00:05 on 15 April, Captain Smith ordered lifeboats uncovered and distress signals sent.

### Evacuation and Lifeboats
Titanic carried only 20 lifeboats, enough for about 1,178 people—barely half those aboard. This was not unusual then, as lifeboats were meant to ferry passengers to rescue ships, not hold everyone. The evacuation was poorly organized, with many lifeboats launched partially filled due to lack of training, confusion, and passenger reluctance. Officers Lightoller (port side) and Murdoch (starboard side) interpreted "women and children first" differently: Lightoller allowed only women and children, while Murdoch permitted some men. Many third-class passengers couldn't reach the boat deck due to locked gates, complex routes, and language barriers.

### Final Plunge and Rescue
As the ship's angle increased, panic set in. The band played on the boat deck until near the end. At about 02:15, Titanic's structure failed and the ship broke in two. The bow sank first, followed by the stern at 02:20. Over 1,500 people were left in freezing water (−2 °C / 28 °F), where most died within minutes from cold shock and hypothermia. RMS *Carpathia*, 58 miles away, arrived around 04:00 and rescued all 710 survivors by 09:15. The nearby SS *Californian*, whose radio operator had shut down for the night, saw distress rockets but failed to respond.

### Aftermath and Legacy
The disaster caused global outrage and led to public inquiries in the U.S. and UK. Both found the loss resulted from standard but unsafe practices, including excessive speed in ice-prone waters and insufficient lifeboats. The tragedy prompted the International Ice Patrol and the first SOLAS convention in 1914, which mandated 24-hour radio watches, sufficient lifeboats for all, and regular lifeboat drills. The wreck was discovered in 1985 and is slowly decaying on the seabed.

## Terms
*   **Maiden voyage:** The first journey of a ship carrying passengers.
*   **Watertight compartments:** Hull sections separated by bulkheads to contain flooding.
*   **Bulkhead:** A dividing wall between ship compartments.
*   **Distress signal (CQD/SOS):** International radio signals for emergency help.
*   **SOLAS (Safety of Life at Sea):** An international treaty setting minimum ship safety standards.
*   **Cold shock:** The body's immediate response to sudden cold water immersion, which can cause cardiac arrest.
*   **Hypothermia:** A dangerous drop in body temperature from prolonged cold exposure.
*   **International Ice Patrol:** A service monitoring iceberg dangers in North Atlantic shipping lanes.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **The role of SS *Californian*:** Debate persists over whether it could have saved more lives had its radio operator not shut down or if its crew responded to Titanic's distress rockets.
*   **The ship's break-up:** Survivors initially reported the ship sank intact, but the wreck confirmed it broke in two. Theories differ on whether the break started top-down or bottom-up.
*   **Lifeboat capacity and evacuation:** Scholars debate whether filling lifeboats to capacity was feasible given panic and lack of training, and how many more lives could have been saved with better procedures.
*   **Rivet quality:** Some analysis suggests brittle iron rivets in the bow may have contributed to hull failure upon impact, though this is contested, as sister ship *Olympic* served safely with similar rivets.

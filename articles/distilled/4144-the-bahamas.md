# The Bahamas

## Overview
The Bahamas is an archipelagic nation in the Atlantic Ocean, part of the Lucayan Archipelago, comprising over 700 islands and cays. Its history is defined by indigenous settlement, European colonization, the transatlantic slave trade, and a modern economy heavily reliant on tourism and offshore finance. The country gained independence from the United Kingdom in 1973 and remains a stable parliamentary democracy and Commonwealth realm.

## Timeline
- **c. 500–800 AD** — Lucayan people (Taíno) settle the Bahamas from Hispaniola and Cuba.
- **1492** — Christopher Columbus makes his first New World landfall on San Salvador (Guanahaní).
- **1513** — Spanish enslavement of Lucayans leaves the islands mostly deserted.
- **1648** — English "Eleutheran Adventurers" from Bermuda establish the first permanent European settlement on Eleuthera.
- **1718** — The Bahamas becomes a British crown colony to suppress piracy.
- **c. 1783** — British resettlement of American Loyalists and their enslaved Africans begins, establishing plantations.
- **1807** — The British Empire abolishes the slave trade.
- **1834** — Slavery is abolished throughout the British Empire.
- **1964** — A new constitution grants the Bahamas internal self-governance.
- **1973** — The Bahamas achieves full independence from the United Kingdom.
- **2019** — Hurricane Dorian devastates the northwestern islands at Category 5 strength.

## Body

### Pre-Colonial and Colonial History
The first inhabitants were the Lucayan people, a branch of the Taíno, who migrated from Hispaniola and Cuba between AD 500 and 800. An estimated 30,000 Lucayans lived there when Christopher Columbus arrived in 1492. The Spanish enslaved and transported the Lucayans to Hispaniola, where most died from disease and harsh conditions, leaving the islands depopulated by 1513. English settlers from Bermuda arrived in 1648, establishing the first permanent colony. Piracy flourished until 1718, when Britain made the islands a crown colony and suppressed the "Pirates' republic."

### Slavery, Loyalists, and Emancipation
Following the American Revolutionary War, Britain resettled thousands of American Loyalists in the Bahamas, bringing enslaved Africans who became the demographic majority. The British slave trade was abolished in 1807, and the Royal Navy began intercepting illegal slave ships, resettling liberated Africans in the Bahamas. Slavery itself was abolished in 1834. Notably, from 1818, the Bahamas became a haven for manumission; enslaved people from outside the British West Indies who reached the islands were freed. This included survivors from wrecked American domestic slave ships like the *Comet*, *Encomium*, and *Creole*, creating diplomatic tension with the United States.

### Independence and Modern Politics
Political parties formed in the 1950s along ethnic lines. Internal self-government was granted in 1964, and Sir Lynden Pindling of the Progressive Liberal Party (PLP) became the first black premier in 1967. The Bahamas achieved full independence on 10 July 1973, joining the Commonwealth of Nations. The PLP dominated politics for two decades amid allegations of corruption and drug links, while the economy grew via tourism and finance. The Free National Movement (FNM) won power in 1992, leading to alternating governance between the two parties. The country remains a stable parliamentary constitutional monarchy with King Charles III as head of state.

### Geography and Climate
The Bahamas consists of about 700 islands and 2,400 cays, with a total land area of 10,010 km². The islands are low and flat, with the highest point, Mount Alvernia, at 64 m. The climate is tropical savannah, with a hot, wet season from May to October. The country is highly vulnerable to hurricanes, as demonstrated by the devastation of Hurricane Dorian in 2019. Climate change poses a significant threat due to sea-level rise, as at least 80% of the land is below 10 meters in elevation.

### Economy
The economy is dominated by tourism (about 70% of GDP) and offshore financial services (about 15% of GDP). The Bahamas is a major international financial centre and is considered a tax haven, with no income, corporate, or capital gains taxes. Revenue comes from import tariffs, VAT, and other fees. Agriculture and manufacturing are minor sectors, and about 80% of food is imported. The country has a high GDP per capita but faces risks from its economic dependence and climate vulnerability.

## Terms
*   **Commonwealth realm:** A sovereign state that has the British monarch as its head of state.
*   **Crown colony:** A British colony administered by a governor appointed by the Crown.
*   **Lucayans:** The indigenous Taíno people who inhabited the Bahamas before European contact.
*   **Manumission:** The act of an owner freeing their enslaved people.
*   **Oolite:** A sedimentary rock formed from small, spherical grains of calcium carbonate.
*   **Tax haven:** A jurisdiction with a competitive tax regime that attracts foreign assets, often with low or no taxes on certain income.
*   **Westminster system:** A democratic system of government based on the British Parliament, featuring a prime minister, cabinet, and bicameral legislature.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Columbus's Landfall:** While it is consensus that Columbus first landed in the Bahamas, the specific island is debated. The traditional site is San Salvador Island (formerly Watling's Island), but an alternative theory based on 1986 calculations suggests Samana Cay.
*   **Name Etymology:** The origin of the name "Bahamas" is disputed. It is derived from the Lucayan *Bahama* ("large upper middle island"), but a popular folk etymology links it to the Spanish *baja mar* ("shallow sea"), which scholars argue is incorrect. Other theories connect it to *Guanahaní* or *Bimini*.
*   **Political Future:** There is a growing republican movement in the Bahamas, with recent opinion polls indicating majority support for an elected head of state instead of a monarch.

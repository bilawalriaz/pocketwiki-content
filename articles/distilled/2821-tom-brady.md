# Tom Brady

## Overview
Tom Brady is widely regarded as the greatest quarterback in NFL history. His 23-season career, defined by unprecedented longevity and success, culminated in seven Super Bowl championships—more than any individual player or franchise. His journey from a overlooked draft pick to a record-setting champion illustrates the impact of skill, preparation, and competitive drive on professional sports.

## Timeline
- **August 3, 1977** — Born in San Mateo, California.
- **1995–1999** — Plays college football at the University of Michigan.
- **April 16, 2000** — Selected 199th overall by the New England Patriots in the 6th round of the NFL Draft.
- **September 23, 2001** — Becomes the Patriots' starting quarterback after Drew Bledsoe's injury.
- **February 3, 2002** — Wins first Super Bowl (XXXVI) and is named Super Bowl MVP.
- **February 1, 2015** — Wins fourth Super Bowl (XLIX) with a late-game comeback.
- **May 11, 2015** — Suspended four games for his role in the "Deflategate" scandal.
- **February 5, 2017** — Wins fifth Super Bowl (LI) after leading the largest comeback in Super Bowl history.
- **March 20, 2020** — Signs with the Tampa Bay Buccaneers as a free agent.
- **February 7, 2021** — Wins seventh Super Bowl (LV) with the Buccaneers, becoming the oldest Super Bowl MVP at 43.
- **February 1, 2022** — Announces his first retirement from the NFL.
- **March 13, 2022** — Announces his return to play for the 2022 season.
- **February 1, 2023** — Announces his permanent retirement from the NFL.

## Body

### Early Life and College Career
Born in San Mateo, California, Brady was a fan of the San Francisco 49ers and quarterback Joe Montana. At Junípero Serra High School, he played football, basketball, and baseball. He was drafted by the Montreal Expos in the 18th round of the 1995 MLB draft but chose to attend the University of Michigan. After redshirting and serving as a backup, Brady became the Wolverines' starter in 1998 and 1999, leading them to a 20–5 record and victories in the 1999 Citrus Bowl and 2000 Orange Bowl.

### New England Patriots Dynasty (2000–2019)
Brady was selected 199th overall in the 2000 NFL Draft, a compensatory pick. He became the starter in 2001 after Drew Bledsoe's injury and led the Patriots to their first Super Bowl title. Over 20 seasons, Brady and coach Bill Belichick formed the NFL's most successful quarterback-head coach tandem, leading the Patriots to 17 division titles, 13 AFC Championship Games, and six Super Bowl victories. Key moments include the perfect 16–0 regular season in 2007, a record-setting 21-game winning streak spanning 2003–2004, and dramatic Super Bowl wins in 2015 (XLIX) and 2017 (LI). His tenure was also marked by the "Deflategate" controversy, which resulted in a four-game suspension in 2016.

### Tampa Bay Buccaneers and Final Seasons (2020–2022)
In 2020, Brady left New England for the Tampa Bay Buccaneers. In his first season, he led the team to a Super Bowl LV victory, becoming the only player to win Super Bowls with teams from both conferences. He set single-season records for completions (485) and passing yards (5,316) in 2021. After a brief retirement, he returned for the 2022 season, breaking numerous career records before retiring permanently in February 2023.

### Records and Legacy
Brady holds nearly every major quarterback record, including most career passing yards (89,214), completions (7,753), touchdown passes (649), and wins (251). He is the only player to win seven Super Bowls and the only quarterback to win them in three separate decades. His longevity is highlighted by being the oldest NFL MVP (age 40) and oldest Super Bowl MVP (age 43). He was unanimously named to the NFL 100th Anniversary All-Time Team.

### Post-Retirement Career
In 2024, Brady became the lead color commentator for the NFL on Fox. He also became a part-owner of the Las Vegas Raiders (5% stake) and the Las Vegas Aces (WNBA). He founded the production company 199 Productions and the TB12 health and wellness brand.

## Terms
- **Compensatory Pick:** An extra draft selection awarded to a team that lost more or better compensatory free agents than it signed in the previous year.
- **Pocket Passer:** A quarterback who primarily operates from within the protected "pocket" formed by offensive linemen, relying on accuracy and decision-making over mobility.
- **Tuck Rule:** A former NFL rule (abolished in 2013) stating that a forward passing motion is not complete if the quarterback loses possession while attempting to tuck the ball back to his body.
- **Deflategate:** A 2015 scandal involving allegations that the New England Patriots deliberately underinflated footballs during the AFC Championship Game, leading to Brady's suspension.
- **MVP (Most Valuable Player):** An annual award given to the league's most outstanding player.
- **Super Bowl MVP:** An award given to the most valuable player of the Super Bowl.
- **Pro Bowl:** The NFL's annual all-star game.
- **All-Pro:** A designation for the best players at their position in a given season, as selected by the Associated Press.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Greatest of All Time (GOAT):** While Brady is widely considered the greatest quarterback, debates persist comparing his achievements to other legends like Joe Montana and Peyton Manning, often centering on era differences and team success.
- **Deflategate Veracity:** Significant debate continues over the NFL's handling of the investigation, with later reports suggesting the league may have used flawed data and that the footballs' air pressure could be explained by the Ideal Gas Law.
- **Brady-Belichick Dynasty Credit:** A central debate is the relative contribution of Brady versus coach Bill Belichick to the Patriots' dynasty, a discussion that intensified after Brady won a Super Bowl with a different team.

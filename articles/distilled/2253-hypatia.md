# Hypatia

## Overview
Hypatia (c. 350–370 – March 415 AD) was a Neoplatonist philosopher, mathematician, and astronomer in Roman Alexandria. She was a renowned teacher and public intellectual whose murder by a Christian mob made her a symbol of the conflict between classical learning and rising religious intolerance, and her legacy has been continuously reinterpreted for centuries.

## Timeline
- **c. 350–370** — Born in Alexandria, daughter of mathematician Theon of Alexandria.
- **Late 4th–early 5th c.** — Teaches philosophy and mathematics in Alexandria, attracting students from across the Mediterranean.
- **391** — Bishop Theophilus destroys the Serapeum; Hypatia's school is tolerated.
- **412** — Theophilus dies; his nephew Cyril becomes bishop after a violent power struggle.
- **414** — Cyril closes synagogues and expels Jews, sparking conflict with the Roman prefect Orestes.
- **March 415** — Hypatia is murdered by a Christian mob in Alexandria.
- **Autumn 416** — Emperors Honorius and Theodosius II issue an edict restricting the parabalani (Cyril's clerical enforcers).
- **Early 420s** — Cyril gains political dominance in Alexandria.

## Body

### Life and Career
Hypatia was the daughter of Theon of Alexandria, a mathematician who headed a prestigious school. She embraced the original Neoplatonism of Plotinus, rejecting the more religious teachings of Iamblichus. A celebrated teacher, she lectured publicly on Plato and Aristotle and was known for her wisdom and self-possession. Despite being a pagan, she was tolerant of Christians and taught many, including Synesius, who later became a bishop. Her reputation for virtue and learning gave her significant influence with Alexandria's political elite.

### Death
Hypatia's murder was the culmination of a political feud between Cyril, the bishop of Alexandria, and Orestes, the Roman prefect. Hypatia advised Orestes, and rumors spread that she was preventing a reconciliation. In March 415, a mob led by a lector named Peter dragged her from her carriage, killed her with shards, and mutilated her body. The historian Socrates Scholasticus presents the murder as politically motivated, while later sources like John of Nikiu allege she practiced "satanic" magic. The event shocked the empire and was widely blamed on Cyril, though no definitive proof of his direct order was found.

### Works and Inventions
Hypatia was primarily a teacher and commentator. She edited the text of Book III of Ptolemy's *Almagest* and wrote commentaries on Diophantus's *Arithmetica* and Apollonius's work on conic sections. Parts of her commentary on the *Arithmetica* may survive in later Arabic manuscripts. She constructed astrolabes and hydrometers (devices for measuring liquid density) but did not invent them, as both were known earlier. Her work focused on preserving and making accessible classical mathematical and astronomical texts.

### Legacy
Hypatia's death made her a "martyr for philosophy," turning later Neoplatonists like Damascius against Christianity. In the Middle Ages, her story contributed to the legend of Saint Catherine of Alexandria. Enlightenment thinkers like Voltaire and Gibbon used her as a symbol of reason martyred by religious fanaticism. The 19th century romanticized her as "the last of the Hellenes." In the 20th and 21st centuries, she became an icon for women's rights and a subject of numerous novels and films, though often with historical inaccuracies, such as falsely linking her death to the destruction of the Library of Alexandria.

## Terms
*   **Neoplatonism:** A philosophical system developed from the teachings of Plotinus, emphasizing a mystical union with the divine.
*   **Mouseion:** A scholarly institution in Alexandria, revived by Theon, where Hypatia taught.
*   **Astrolabe:** A portable device used to calculate date and time based on celestial positions.
*   **Hydrometer:** An instrument used to determine the density or specific gravity of liquids.
*   **Parabalani:** A group of Christian clerics under Cyril's authority, implicated in Hypatia's murder.
*   **Apatheia:** A Neoplatonic ideal of complete liberation from emotions and affections.
*   **Geocentric model:** The Ptolemaic model of the universe where the Sun revolves around the Earth.
*   **Sexagesimal numeral system:** A base-60 numeral system used in ancient astronomical calculations.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Birth Date:** Her exact birth year is uncertain, with scholarly estimates ranging from 350 to 370 AD.
*   **Authorship of Texts:** The extent of her editorial work on the *Almagest* and the authorship of interpolations in Diophantus's *Arithmetica* are debated.
*   **Motives for Murder:** Historians debate whether her death was purely politically motivated, religiously motivated, or a combination of both.
*   **Cyril's Role:** The degree of Bishop Cyril's direct responsibility for ordering or inspiring Hypatia's murder remains unproven.

# Oman

## Overview
Oman is a country on the southeastern Arabian Peninsula, known as the oldest independent state in the Arab world. It has been ruled by the Al Bu Said dynasty since 1744 and emerged as a major maritime power in the 17th and 19th centuries. Today, it is an absolute monarchy with a high-income economy heavily dependent on oil, undergoing modernization while maintaining a distinct cultural identity.

## Timeline
- **c. 2300 BCE** — The Magan civilisation, a source of copper for Mesopotamia, is referenced in Sumerian texts.
- **7th century CE** — Omanis accept Islam; consolidation occurs during the Ridda Wars (632 CE).
- **1507–1650** — The Portuguese colonize coastal Oman, capturing Muscat.
- **1624–1744** — The Yaruba Imams reunite Omanis and expel the Portuguese by the 1650s, establishing a maritime empire.
- **1744** — Ahmad bin Said Al Busaidi is elected Imam, founding the Al Bu Said dynasty, which rules to the present.
- **1856** — Under British arbitration, the Sultanate of Muscat and the Sultanate of Zanzibar are separated.
- **1920** — The Treaty of Seeb grants autonomy to the Imamate in the interior, recognizing the coastal Sultanate.
- **1932–1970** — Sultan Said bin Taimur rules in a feudal, isolationist manner backed by Britain.
- **1963–1976** — The Dhofar War sees pro-Soviet rebels fight the government.
- **1970** — Sultan Qaboos bin Said deposes his father, launching the Omani Renaissance of modernization.
- **2020** — Sultan Qaboos dies; he is succeeded by his cousin, Sultan Haitham bin Tariq.

## Body

### Early History and Islamic Era
Oman's history includes prehistoric sites and the ancient Magan civilisation (c. 2300–550 BCE). Arab tribes, notably the Azd, migrated from Yemen in waves, with a major migration following the collapse of the Marib Dam (120/200 CE). Omanis accepted Islam in the 7th century, with the Rashidun Caliphate defeating rebels at the Battle of Dibba (632 CE). The Ibadi form of Islam, a moderate Kharijite branch, became predominant after scholars returned from Basra. The first elective Imamate was established around 750 CE, creating a maritime empire that later declined due to internal strife and external pressures.

### Imperial Struggles and Colonial Era
From the 16th century, Oman was caught between European powers. The Portuguese controlled its coast from 1507 until expelled by the Yaruba Imams in the 1650s. The resulting Omani Empire competed with the British and Portuguese, extending influence to Zanzibar. Internal civil war (1718–1744) weakened the Yaruba dynasty, leading to the election of the Al Bu Said dynasty. British influence grew through treaties from 1798, culminating in de facto control by the late 19th century. The Treaty of Seeb (1920) divided the country between the coastal Sultanate and the interior Imamate.

### Modern Sultanate and Renaissance
Sultan Said bin Taimur (1932–1970) maintained a reclusive, British-backed rule. The Jebel Akhdar War (1955–1959) saw the Sultanate, with British military support, suppress an Imamate rebellion in the interior. The Dhofar War (1963–1976) threatened the state until Sultan Qaboos seized power in 1970. He initiated the Omani Renaissance, modernizing the economy, abolishing slavery, and ending the Dhofar Rebellion with foreign assistance. Political reforms included the 1996 Basic Statute (constitution) and the introduction of an elected Consultative Assembly, though ultimate power remains with the Sultan. Sultan Haitham bin Tariq succeeded Qaboos in 2020.

### Economy and Society
Oman's economy is a hyper-centralized rentier state, with oil and gas accounting for over 70% of government revenue and 82% of exports (2018). Diversification efforts focus on tourism, logistics, and industry, though innovation and R&D spending are low. The population is approximately 5.46 million, with about half in Muscat. Society is tribal, with Arab, Baloch, and African-descended groups. Islam is the state religion, predominantly Ibadi. Arabic is the official language, with English widely used in business. Media is state-controlled and censored.

## Terms
- **Al Bu Said dynasty**: The ruling dynasty of Oman since 1744, established after the collapse of the Yaruba dynasty.
- **Imamate**: A form of Islamic government led by an Imam, which has been an elective institution in Oman's interior for centuries.
- **Ibadism**: A moderate branch of Islam originating from the Kharijites, predominant in Oman.
- **Omani Renaissance**: The period of rapid modernization and social reform initiated by Sultan Qaboos bin Said after 1970.
- **Rentier state**: An economy where a significant portion of national revenue comes from external rents (e.g., oil) rather than domestic production.
- **Basic Statute**: Oman's constitution, issued in 1996, establishing the legal framework of the state.
- **Dhofar War**: A 1963–1976 insurgency in the Dhofar region against the Sultanate, supported by communist forces.
- **Jebel Akhdar War**: A 1955–1959 conflict between the Sultanate (backed by Britain) and the interior Imamate over control of the interior.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The exact date and nature of early Persian (Achaemenid) control over Oman is debated, with archaeological evidence being limited.
- The origins of the name "Oman" are uncertain, with theories linking it to an ancestral homeland in Yemen or to local Arabic terms.
- The transition from the elective Imamate to the hereditary Al Bu Said dynasty involved complex power struggles, with some scholars debating the exact timeline of the Nabhani dynasty's end.
- The extent of British influence versus Omani sovereignty during the 19th and early 20th centuries is a point of historical analysis, particularly regarding the Sultanate's dependency.

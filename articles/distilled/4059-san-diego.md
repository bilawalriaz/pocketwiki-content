# San Diego

## Overview
San Diego is a major U.S. city on the Pacific coast of Southern California, adjacent to the Mexico–United States border. It is the eighth-most populous city in the U.S. and is known for its mild climate, extensive beaches, long association with the U.S. Navy, and its role as a center for biotechnology and wireless technology. Historically the first European settlement in present-day California, its economy is driven by military/defense, tourism, international trade, and research.

## Timeline
- **1542** — Juan Rodríguez Cabrillo claims the bay for Spain, naming it "San Miguel."
- **1602** — Sebastián Vizcaíno surveys the harbor and names the area "San Diego."
- **1769** — The Presidio of San Diego and Mission San Diego de Alcalá are founded, establishing the first European settlement in California.
- **1821** — San Diego becomes part of the newly independent Mexican territory of Alta California.
- **1846–48** — During the Mexican–American War, San Diego is captured by U.S. forces; the territory is ceded to the U.S. in 1848.
- **1850** — California is admitted as a state; San Diego is incorporated and becomes the county seat.
- **1867** — Alonzo Horton promotes the move of the city center to the bayfront "New Town," now downtown.
- **1915** — The Panama–California Exposition is held in Balboa Park, spurring development.
- **1920s–30s** — Major U.S. Navy presence expands; San Diego becomes a center for aviation and aircraft manufacturing.
- **1940s** — World War II causes rapid population growth and solidifies the city's military economy.
- **1970s–80s** — Post-Cold War defense cuts lead to economic diversification into biotechnology and tourism.
- **2004** — Petco Park opens; the city government changes to a strong mayor system.

## Body

### Early History and Settlement
The area was home to the Kumeyaay people for millennia, with the village of Kosa'aay at the site of present-day Old Town. The first European visit was by Juan Rodríguez Cabrillo in 1542. Permanent colonization began in 1769 with the founding of the Presidio and Mission San Diego de Alcalá by Spanish settlers, making it the first European settlement in California. The mission was secularized by the Mexican government in 1834.

### Mexican and American Periods
San Diego became part of Mexico in 1821. Following the Mexican–American War, the territory was ceded to the U.S. in 1848. California became a state in 1850, with San Diego as its county seat. The city's original location at Old Town was superseded in the late 1860s by a new downtown on the bay, promoted by Alonzo Horton. The arrival of a railroad in 1878 spurred growth.

### Military and Economic Growth
San Diego's economy has long been tied to the military. A significant Navy presence began in 1901 and expanded greatly in the 1920s. World War II transformed the city into a major military and defense hub, causing its population to more than double between 1930 and 1950. After the Cold War, defense cuts prompted a shift toward research, biotechnology, and tourism. The city is now a major center for wireless technology (e.g., Qualcomm) and biotech.

### Geography and Climate
San Diego's geography is defined by mesas, canyons, and the San Diego River, creating a hilly, low-density urban landscape. The climate is classified as Mediterranean or semi-arid, characterized by warm, dry summers and mild winters with most rain falling between December and March. The city experiences significant microclimates due to its topography and coastal influence.

### Demographics and Culture
San Diego's culture reflects its Mexican heritage and border location. The city is diverse, with a large Hispanic or Latino population. It is home to major universities like UC San Diego and San Diego State University. The Hillcrest neighborhood is a historic center for the LGBTQ+ community. The city hosts numerous museums and performing arts venues, many located in Balboa Park.

### Government and Politics
San Diego operates under a strong mayor-council government. Politically, the city has trended Democratic in recent decades after supporting Republican presidential candidates from 1968 to 1988. It is a sanctuary city. The city has experienced several political scandals, including resignations due to corruption and sexual harassment.

## Terms
- **Presidio:** A fortified military settlement; the Presidio of San Diego (1769) was the first European settlement in California.
- **Mission San Diego de Alcalá:** The first Franciscan mission in California, founded in 1769, serving as the southern anchor of El Camino Real.
- **Secularization:** The process (1834) by which the Mexican government dissolved the Spanish missions and distributed their lands.
- **Pueblo:** A type of Spanish colonial municipal government; San Diego lost its pueblo status in 1838 due to population decline.
- **Kumeyaay:** The indigenous people historically inhabiting the San Diego region.
- **Strong Mayor Government:** A system where the mayor serves as the chief executive officer, distinct from a council-manager system.
- **Marine Layer:** A cool, damp layer of clouds that often forms near the coast, causing "May gray" or "June gloom."
- **Grabens:** A geological term for a depressed block of the Earth's crust bordered by parallel faults, like San Diego Bay.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The origins of San Diego's early cultures are debated. Archaeologist Malcolm Rogers hypothesized they were separate from the Kumeyaay, a claim that is disputed and which he later reevaluated.
- The city's sports teams have historically struggled to win major championships, leading some fans to believe in a "curse" on professional sports in San Diego.

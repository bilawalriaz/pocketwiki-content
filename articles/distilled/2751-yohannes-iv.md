# Yohannes IV

## Overview
Yohannes IV was Emperor of Ethiopia from 1871 to 1889. He is remembered for successfully defending Ethiopia against a major Egyptian invasion and for his efforts to unify the empire, though his reign was ultimately ended by his death in battle against Sudanese Mahdists. His policies and conflicts set the stage for the rise of his rival, Menelik II, and the later Italian colonization of Eritrea.

## Timeline
- **11 July 1837** — Born Lij Kahssai Mercha in Tigray.
- **1864–1867** — Rebels against Emperor Tewodros II, defeating regional governors.
- **1868** — Assists the British Expedition to Abyssinia; receives military equipment.
- **11 July 1871** — Defeats and captures Emperor Tekle Giyorgis II at the Battle of Adwa.
- **1875–1876** — Defeats Egyptian forces at the battles of Gundet and Gura.
- **1878** — Convenes the Council of Boru Meda; forces religious conformity.
- **1878** — Menelik of Shewa submits and is re-crowned as a vassal king.
- **1884** — Signs the Hewett Treaty with Britain, allowing Egyptian evacuation.
- **1885** — Italy seizes the port of Massawa.
- **1889** — Killed at the Battle of Gallabat (Metemma) against the Mahdists.

## Body

### Rise to Power
Born into the nobility of Tigray, Yohannes (then known as Kassa) rebelled against Emperor Tewodros II in the 1860s. He gained crucial military support by assisting the 1868 British Expedition to Abyssinia, which ended with Tewodros's suicide. In return, Yohannes received a large cache of modern weapons and a British military instructor. He used this advantage to defeat the reigning emperor, Tekle Giyorgis II, at the Battle of Adwa in 1871, securing the imperial throne.

### Internal Policy and Religious Reform
Yohannes sought to continue the unification policies of Tewodros II. He toured the empire, appointing loyal governors and maintaining order. In 1878, he convened the Council of Boru Meda to settle theological disputes within the Ethiopian Orthodox Church, enforcing a single doctrine. The council also mandated the conversion of Muslims and pagans; it is estimated that by 1880, 500,000 Oromos and 50,000 Jebertis had been forced to convert to Christianity. He crowned Menelik II as King of Shewa in 1878 and Tekle Haymanot as King of Gojjam in 1881, encouraging them to expand southward, though this later fueled rivalry between them.

### Foreign Conflicts
Yohannes's reign was defined by wars on multiple fronts. He decisively defeated a large-scale Egyptian invasion at the battles of Gundet (1875) and Gura (1876). Later, he allied with Britain via the Hewett Treaty (1884) to allow Egyptian troops to evacuate Sudan, hoping for British support to secure the port of Massawa. This hope was dashed when Italy seized Massawa in 1885. While Yohannes attempted diplomacy with Italy, his general Ras Alula engaged in border skirmishes. The most pressing threat came from the Mahdist revolt in Sudan, whose forces sacked the holy city of Gondar in 1888.

### Death and Succession
In March 1889, Yohannes led his army to confront the Mahdists at Gallabat (Metemma). He was mortally wounded by a gunshot during the battle. On his deathbed, he revealed that his nephew, Ras Mengesha, was his biological son and declared him his heir. However, the news of the emperor's death shattered Ethiopian morale, leading to defeat. His body was captured by the Mahdists. The resulting power vacuum allowed Menelik II to seize the imperial title, while Italian expansion into former Tigrayan territories eventually led to the creation of the colony of Eritrea.

## Terms
*   **Neguse Negest**: "King of Kings," the title for the Emperor of Ethiopia.
*   **Ras**: A high-ranking noble title, equivalent to a duke or prince.
*   **Dejazmatch**: A noble title, roughly equivalent to a count or governor.
*   **Šum Tembien**: The traditional ruler of the Tembien province in Tigray.
*   **Mahdist War**: A conflict between the Mahdist State in Sudan and neighboring powers, including Ethiopia.
*   **Council of Boru Meda**: An 1878 religious synod that enforced doctrinal unity in the Ethiopian Orthodox Church and mandated conversions.
*   **Hewett Treaty**: An 1884 agreement between Ethiopia and Britain concerning the evacuation of Egyptian troops from Sudan.
*   **Solomonic dynasty**: The imperial dynasty of Ethiopia, claiming descent from King Solomon and the Queen of Sheba.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   The exact motivations for Yohannes's initial rebellion against Tewodros II are unclear, with sources offering differing explanations.
*   The precise date of his birth is uncertain, with sources giving dates between 1831 and 1837.
*   The long-term effectiveness of the forced conversions mandated at Boru Meda is debated, with indications that suppressed beliefs persisted.

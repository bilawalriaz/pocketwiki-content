# Henry Dunant

## Overview
Henry Dunant (1828–1910) was a Swiss humanitarian and businessman whose witnessing of the aftermath of the Battle of Solferino in 1859 led him to found the Red Cross and advocate for the Geneva Conventions. His work established the modern framework for neutral wartime medical aid, earning him the first Nobel Peace Prize in 1901, though his life was marked by later financial ruin and obscurity.

## Timeline
- **8 May 1828** — Born in Geneva, Switzerland.
- **1859** — Witnesses the aftermath of the Battle of Solferino and organizes civilian aid for wounded soldiers.
- **1862** — Publishes *A Memory of Solferino*, advocating for a neutral relief organization.
- **17 Feb 1863** — Founding meeting of the committee that became the International Committee of the Red Cross (ICRC).
- **22 Aug 1864** — First Geneva Convention is signed by 12 states.
- **1867** — Declares bankruptcy and is expelled from the ICRC.
- **1895** — Rediscovered by a journalist, leading to renewed public support.
- **1901** — Awarded the first Nobel Peace Prize (shared with Frédéric Passy).
- **30 Oct 1910** — Dies in Heiden, Switzerland.

## Body

### Early Life and Business in Algeria
Born to a devout Calvinist family in Geneva, Dunant was influenced by social work from a young age. He pursued a business career, visiting North Africa in 1853 and later establishing a corn-trading company in French-occupied Algeria. When faced with difficulties over land and water rights, he traveled to meet Emperor Napoleon III in 1859 to petition for help, leading him to the war zone in Italy.

### The Battle of Solferino and Humanitarian Vision
On 24 June 1859, Dunant arrived at Solferino just after a major battle between French-Piedmontese and Austrian forces. He was horrified to find approximately 40,000 wounded soldiers with little medical care. He organized local civilians, famously urging them to treat all wounded as "Tutti fratelli" ("All are brothers"). This experience directly inspired his 1862 book, *A Memory of Solferino*, which proposed two key ideas: the creation of permanent, neutral relief societies for wartime and an international agreement to protect them.

### Founding the Red Cross and the Geneva Convention
Dunant's book gained traction, leading to the formation of a five-person committee in Geneva in February 1863, including jurist Gustave Moynier and General Henri Dufour. This committee laid the groundwork for the ICRC. A major diplomatic conference in 1864, organized by the Swiss government, resulted in the first Geneva Convention, which codified protections for wounded soldiers and medical personnel. Dunant's idealism often clashed with Moynier's pragmatism, particularly over the concept of neutrality for aid workers.

### Financial Ruin and Obscurity
In 1867, Dunant's business ventures collapsed, leading to bankruptcy and a social scandal in Geneva. He was forced to resign from the ICRC and spent the next two decades in poverty and obscurity across Europe. He continued to advocate for humanitarian ideas, including disarmament and international arbitration, but was largely forgotten by the movement he helped create.

### Rediscovery and Nobel Prize
In 1895, a journalist's article rediscovered Dunant in the Swiss village of Heiden, sparking renewed support. This culminated in 1901 when he was awarded the first Nobel Peace Prize for his role in founding the Red Cross and the Geneva Convention. The prize was shared with pacifist Frédéric Passy, setting a precedent for honoring both humanitarian and peace activism. Dunant never spent the prize money, using it to settle debts and support charitable causes.

## Terms
- **International Committee of the Red Cross (ICRC):** A humanitarian institution founded in 1863, based in Geneva, which leads the International Red Cross and Red Crescent Movement.
- **Geneva Convention:** A series of international treaties that establish the international legal standards for humanitarian treatment in war. The first was signed in 1864.
- **Calvinist:** A follower of the Protestant theological tradition founded by John Calvin, emphasizing predestination and a disciplined, charitable life.
- **Réveil:** A religious awakening movement in Switzerland in the early 19th century that influenced Dunant's social activism.
- **Tutti fratelli:** Italian for "All are brothers," a slogan used to emphasize impartial aid to all wounded soldiers regardless of side.
- **Nobel Peace Prize:** One of the five Nobel Prizes, awarded annually to individuals or groups for significant contributions to peace, established by Alfred Nobel's will.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The exact influence of Dunant's personal conflicts with Gustave Moynier on the early direction and policies of the Red Cross is a subject of historical analysis.
- The Nobel Committee's decision to split the first Peace Prize between Dunant (humanitarian) and Passy (pacifist) established a lasting precedent for the prize's interpretation, which continues to be debated.
- Some contemporaries argued that the Red Cross and Geneva Conventions, by mitigating the suffering of war, might paradoxically make conflict more politically and socially acceptable.

# Mail

## Overview

The postal system is a physical network for transporting letters, postcards, and parcels, operating as either public or private services under government oversight. Since the mid-19th century, most national systems have functioned as government monopolies with prepaid fees, typically using adhesive stamps or postage meters. Beyond mere delivery, postal authorities often manage telegraph, telephone, savings accounts, and passport services, while the Universal Postal Union (UPU), founded in 1874, coordinates international mail exchange among 192 member states.

## Body

### Ancient Origins

The earliest documented courier service emerged in Egypt under pharaohs around 2400 BCE, using riders to distribute decrees. The first credible formal postal system arose under the Achaemenid Empire: Cyrus the Great (550 BCE) mandated provincial letter reception and delivery, while Darius the Great later expanded it via the Royal Road. Stations called Chapar Khaneh enabled rapid horse exchanges, inspiring Herodotus’s famous tribute to Persian couriers. This model influenced Rome’s cursus publicus under Augustus Caesar (62 BCE–14 CE), which used light carriages and later ox-drawn carts for official correspondence.

### Regional Developments

In India, the Mauryan Empire (322–185 BCE) developed early mail services alongside public infrastructure; chariots called Dagana served as mail vehicles. Under British rule, the Post Office Act XVII of 1837 centralized control, establishing the Indian Post Office on October 1, 1837. China’s Han dynasty (206 BCE–220 CE) initiated relay stations every 30 li; the Tang (618–907 AD) operated 1,639 posthouses with 20,000 employees, though private correspondence was banned. The Mongol Empire under Genghis Khan created the Örtöö network—over 1,400 stations in China alone during Kublai Khan’s reign—with fresh horses every 25–65 km. Japan adopted European models in the mid-19th century, pioneering postal savings and life insurance systems.

### Institutional Evolution

The Thurn und Taxis family pioneered Europe’s first modern public postal service in 1505, offering fee-based access across the Holy Roman Empire. In 1716, Spain established Correos y Telégrafos as a public service. Sir Rowland Hill’s 1837 reform introduced prepayment via the Penny Black stamp, replacing recipient-paid models. The 20th century saw air mail revolutionize long-distance delivery—the world’s first scheduled airmail post service took place in the United Kingdom between the London suburbs of Hendon and Windsor, Berkshire, on 9 September 1911. Railways enabled Railway Post Offices, while automation transformed sorting operations.

### Modern Structure and Technology

Today, national postal systems coordinate through the UPU, which sets international rates and standards. ZIP codes and barcoding facilitate automated sorting: POSTNET uses vertical bars, while two-dimensional dot codes encode routing and payment data. The business model follows four stages—collection, sorting, transportation, and delivery—with last-mile delivery consuming up to 50% of costs. Hybrid mail combines electronic lodgement with physical production near delivery points.

### Economic and Social Shifts

The Internet fundamentally altered postal economics. Email reduced letter volumes—U.S. mail peaked at 213 billion pieces in 2006, declining over 15% thereafter. However, e-commerce shifted focus to parcel delivery, with online auctions and shopping sustaining postal revenues. Electronic alternatives like fax and SMS complemented rather than replaced physical mail, while privacy concerns intensified debates over mail inspection. Military and prison mail remain subject to surveillance for security reasons. Continued drops in letter volume led PostNord to stop delivering letters in Denmark at the end of 2025, and remove mailboxes from streets.

## Terms

- **Cursus Publicus**: Roman state courier system established under Augustus Caesar for official correspondence.
- **Chapar Khaneh**: Persian relay stations where couriers exchanged tired horses for fresh ones.
- **Örtöö**: Mongol Empire’s messenger and postal station network spanning Eurasia.
- **Barcoding**: Machine-readable encoding on mailpieces using vertical bars (POSTNET) or dot matrices for automated sorting.
- **Hybrid Mail**: Electronic submission of mail directly to postal providers for digital sorting and localized physical production.
- **Franking**: Prepaid postage method using licensed machines to create postage-prepaid envelopes.
- **Last Mile Problem**: Final delivery stage from sorting center to recipient, representing the most labor-intensive and costly phase.
- **Philately**: The study and collection of postage stamps.
- **Registered Mail**: Tracked correspondence with constant monitoring through the postal system, typically for valuable items.
- **Spoke–Hub Distribution**: Centralized sorting model concentrating mail flow through major hubs rather than direct point-to-point connections.

## Debates and Open Questions

The transition from government monopoly to competitive markets remains contested. Countries including Sweden (1 January 1993), New Zealand (1998 and 2003), Germany (2005 and 2007), Argentina and Chile opened up the postal services market to new entrants. Critics argue privatization undermines universal service obligations, while proponents claim competition improves efficiency. The sustainability of postal systems amid declining letter volumes and rising parcel demands continues to challenge operators globally, exemplified by PostNord’s 2025 decision to cease letter delivery in Denmark.

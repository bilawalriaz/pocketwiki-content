# Senegal

## Overview
Senegal is a West African nation and the westernmost country on the African mainland. It is recognized for its long-standing political stability and democratic transitions since independence in 1960. The country's history is marked by pre-colonial empires, French colonial rule, and a modern identity shaped by diverse ethnic groups, Islam, and a blend of traditional and French cultural influences.

## Timeline
- **6th–14th centuries** — Kingdoms like Takrur and the Jolof Empire emerge.
- **13th–14th centuries** — The Jolof Empire unites several kingdoms in the region.
- **15th century** — Portuguese, followed by other Europeans, arrive on the coast.
- **1677** — France gains control of the island of Gorée.
- **1850s** — French colonial expansion onto the mainland begins.
- **25 November 1958** — Senegal becomes an autonomous republic within the French Union.
- **20 June 1960** — Senegal gains full independence as part of the Mali Federation.
- **20 August 1960** — Senegal and French Sudan (Mali) separate and become independent states.
- **1981** — Léopold Sédar Senghor transfers power to Abdou Diouf.
- **1999** — Opposition leader Abdoulaye Wade wins the presidency in a free election.
- **March 2012** — Macky Sall is elected president.
- **March 2024** — Bassirou Diomaye Faye wins the presidential election.

## Body

### Etymology and Geography
Senegal is named after the Senegal River, which forms its northern and eastern border. The name's origin is debated, possibly deriving from the Zenaga people, a Serer religious term, or a Wolof phrase meaning "our canoe." The country lies on the Atlantic coast, nearly surrounding The Gambia. Its landscape consists mainly of rolling sandy plains of the Sahel, with the highest point at 648 m (2,126 ft). The climate is tropical with distinct dry and wet seasons.

### History
Archaeological evidence shows continuous habitation. Pre-colonial history includes kingdoms like the Jolof Empire (14th century), which was a voluntary confederacy. Islam was introduced early through trade and contact with the Almoravid dynasty. European powers competed for trade from the 15th century, with France eventually establishing control. French colonial expansion on the mainland began in the 1850s, facing resistance from leaders like Lat-Dior and the Serer kings. Senegal became an autonomous republic in 1958 and gained independence in 1960 after a brief union with French Sudan. Léopold Sédar Senghor was the first president, advocating "African socialism." The country has experienced peaceful transfers of power, notably in 1981, 2000, 2012, and 2024. A low-level separatist conflict in the Casamance region has persisted since 1982.

### Government and Politics
Senegal is a semi-presidential republic. The president is elected every five years. The government has been characterized by relative stability and democratic transitions. Political culture is quasi-democratic, with significant influence from Muslim religious leaders (marabouts). The country has a multi-party system and an independent judiciary. In 2024, Bassirou Diomaye Faye became the youngest president. Senegal has announced the withdrawal of French troops by the end of 2025.

### Demographics and Culture
Senegal's population is about 18 million. The largest ethnic groups are Wolof (39%), Fula (27.5%), and Serer (16%). French is the official language, but Wolof is the most widely spoken lingua franca. Over 30 languages are spoken. The population is predominantly Muslim (97.2%), with Sufi brotherhoods like the Tijaniyya and Murīdiyya being highly influential. Culture is rich in traditions like storytelling by griots, and the concept of "teranga" (hospitality) is central to national identity. Wrestling is the most popular sport, and football is also major, with the national team winning the 2022 Africa Cup of Nations.

### Economy
Senegal is a developing nation classified as a heavily indebted poor country. Key industries include food processing, mining, tourism, and services. Agriculture and fishing are vital; fishing provides 3.2% of GDP and 600,000 jobs but faces severe overfishing. The country is a member of regional economic organizations like ECOWAS and WAEMU. It has achieved full internet connectivity and private activity accounts for 82% of GDP.

## Terms
- **Sahelian**: Refers to the semi-arid region south of the Sahara Desert, characterized by grasslands and savanna.
- **Lingua franca**: A common language used for communication between people who do not share a native language.
- **Sufi tariqas**: Islamic mystical orders or brotherhoods that are central to Senegalese religious life.
- **Griots**: West African storytellers, musicians, and oral historians who preserve cultural heritage.
- **Teranga**: A Wolof word meaning hospitality, considered a core value of Senegalese culture.
- **Mbalax**: A popular Senegalese music genre rooted in Serer percussion traditions.
- **Marabouts**: Muslim religious leaders and teachers in Senegal who hold significant social and political influence.
- **Casamance conflict**: A low-level separatist insurgency in southern Senegal that began in 1982.
- **Heavily indebted poor country (HIPC)**: A classification for countries with high poverty and debt overhang, eligible for special assistance.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The exact etymology of the name "Senegal" is debated, with multiple linguistic theories.
- The Casamance conflict, despite ceasefires, remains unresolved as of 2025.
- The role and influence of French military and economic presence have been a source of significant domestic tension, leading to the announced withdrawal of French troops.
- The effectiveness of government institutions in enforcing women's rights protocols is criticized by feminists, highlighting a gap between legal commitments and social practices.

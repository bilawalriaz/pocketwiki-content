# South Asia

## Overview
South Asia, the world's most populous and densely populated region, is a southern sub-region of Asia defined by geography and culture. It is home to a quarter of the global population and encompasses immense diversity in languages, religions, and political systems.

## Timeline
- **c. 3300–1300 BCE** — Indus Valley Civilisation flourishes in the northwest.
- **c. 1900–500 BCE** — Vedic period; Indo-Aryans migrate, Sanskrit diffuses.
- **c. 563–483 BCE** — Life of the Buddha; Buddhism and Jainism arise.
- **8th century CE** — Arab conquest of Sindh introduces Islam as a political power.
- **1192 CE** — Delhi Sultanate is founded.
- **1526 CE** — Babur establishes the Mughal Empire after the Battle of Panipat.
- **1858 CE** — British Crown rule begins in India.
- **1947 CE** — British India is partitioned into India and Pakistan.
- **1971 CE** — Bangladesh secedes from Pakistan after a liberation war.
- **1985 CE** — SAARC is established.
- **2007 CE** — Afghanistan becomes SAARC's eighth member.

## Body

### Definition and Geography
South Asia's boundaries are inherited from the British Indian Empire. Core territories are India, Pakistan, and Bangladesh, with Nepal, Bhutan, Sri Lanka, and the Maldives generally included. Afghanistan is often included but may also be classified as Central Asian. The region is dominated by the Indian subcontinent, a landmass formed by the collision of the Indian and Eurasian plates, creating the Himalayas. It is bounded by the Indian Ocean to the south and mountains to the north.

### History
Settled life emerged around 9,000 years ago, evolving into the Indus Valley Civilisation. By 1200 BCE, an archaic Sanskrit had diffused from the northwest. The Vedic period saw early state-forms and the "Hindu synthesis." Between 800 and 400 BCE, urbanisation spurred ascetic movements like Buddhism and Jainism. Islam arrived as a political power in the 8th century, leading to the Delhi Sultanate in the 13th century. The Mughal Empire, founded in 1526, brought two centuries of relative peace. British colonial rule began in the 18th century and was formalized in 1858. The 1947 Partition created India and Pakistan amid violence. The most recent nation-forming event was the 1971 Bangladesh Liberation War.

### Society and Culture
South Asia is socially mixed, with numerous language groups and religions. The largest spoken language is Hindustani, followed by Bengali, Telugu, and Tamil. Scripts are divided along religious lines, with Muslims typically using the Arabic alphabet and non-Muslims using Brahmi-derived scripts. The region is religiously diverse, with Hindus making up about 68% and Muslims 31% of the population. It is the birthplace of Indian religions (Hinduism, Buddhism, Jainism, Sikhism), which spread to East and Southeast Asia. Cricket is the most popular sport, while field hockey is the official national sport of many nations.

### Economy and Development
India has the largest economy, accounting for almost 82% of South Asia's GDP, followed by Bangladesh and Pakistan. The region became the world's fastest-growing from late 2014, driven by India's expansion. However, regional trade is low, at about 5% of total trade. Poverty remains a challenge, with about 24.6% below the international poverty line in 2011, though significant progress has been made.

### Governance and Politics
Political systems vary widely. India is a secular federal parliamentary republic and the world's most populous democracy. Pakistan is a federal parliamentary Islamic republic with a history of military rule. Sri Lanka is a unitary semi-presidential republic and Asia's oldest sustained democracy. Bangladesh is a unitary parliamentary republic. Afghanistan has been a theocratic Islamic emirate since 2021. India is the dominant geopolitical power. Regional cooperation is pursued through SAARC, though India-Pakistan tensions have led India to emphasize sub-regional groups.

## Terms
- **Indian subcontinent**: A geological term for the landmass that collided with the Eurasian Plate, forming the Himalayas. Often used interchangeably with South Asia.
- **Indus Valley Civilisation**: The first major civilisation in South Asia, flourishing c. 3300–1300 BCE.
- **Vedic period**: The era c. 1900–500 BCE, named after the Vedic religion of migrating Indo-Aryans.
- **Hindu synthesis**: The merging of Vedic-Brahmanic ideas with other traditions, forming classical Hinduism.
- **Śramana movements**: Ascetic movements (c. 800–400 BCE) challenging Brahmanism, leading to Buddhism and Jainism.
- **Delhi Sultanate**: A series of Muslim dynasties ruling parts of South Asia from the 13th to 16th centuries.
- **Mughal Empire**: An Islamic empire (1526–18th century) known for its architecture and cultural synthesis.
- **Partition of India**: The 1947 division of British India into India and Pakistan, with massive displacement.
- **SAARC**: The South Asian Association for Regional Cooperation, an eight-member economic and political organization founded in 1985.

## Debates and Open questions
- The precise boundaries of South Asia are ambiguous, with no consensus on including Afghanistan, Myanmar, or other territories.
- The terms "Indian subcontinent" and "South Asia" are used interchangeably, but "South Asia" is often preferred as more neutral.
- The origins and links between the Indus Valley Civilisation and later religious traditions are disputed.
- Identification with a pan-South Asian identity is found to be significantly low among regional populations.

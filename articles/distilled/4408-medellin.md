# Medellín

## Overview
Medellín is Colombia's second-largest city and the capital of the department of Antioquia, located in the Andes Mountains. Once notorious for extreme violence linked to drug cartels, it has undergone a dramatic transformation into a globally recognized model of innovative urban development, social inclusion, and economic dynamism.

## Timeline
- **1616** — Francisco de Herrera Campuzano founds the settlement "Saint Lawrence of Aburrá" (San Lorenzo de Aburrá).
- **1675** — Queen Mariana of Austria founds the "Town of Our Lady of Candelaria of Medellín," officially naming the region "Medellín."
- **1826** — Medellín is named the capital of the Department of Antioquia by the National Congress of Gran Colombia.
- **1905-1951** — The city's population increases sixfold, from 59,815 to 358,189.
- **1948** — Political violence following the murder of Jorge Eliecer Gaitán leads to rural-to-urban migration and slum growth.
- **1950s** — The "Medellín Master Plan" (Plan Piloto) is created for city expansion, though little is implemented.
- **1980s-1990s** — The city suffers a crisis of extreme violence as the home of the Medellín Cartel and Pablo Escobar.
- **2002** — President Álvaro Uribe orders "Operation Orion" to disband urban militias.
- **2004** — The MetroCable gondola system begins operation, connecting hillside barrios to the city center.
- **2013** — Medellín is named the "most innovative city in the world" by the Urban Land Institute.
- **2016** — The city wins the Lee Kuan Yew World City Prize for sustainable urban development.

## Body

### History and Development
The Aburrá Valley was first explored by the Spanish in 1541. The first permanent settlement was founded in 1616, but the city's official founding is dated to 1675. Its early growth was fueled by its strategic location between gold mines and the provincial capital. After Colombian independence, Medellín became the capital of Antioquia and developed as a commercial center, first exporting gold and later coffee. The 20th century saw rapid industrialization and population growth, but also severe social problems from political violence and the drug trade. Since the late 1990s, innovative urban planning, including the Metro system and social programs, has driven a remarkable recovery.

### Urban Innovation and Social Policy
Medellín's transformation is characterized by a "local development state" model, funded partly by profits from the city-owned utility company, Empresas Públicas de Medellín (EPM). Key initiatives include the MetroCable and a giant outdoor escalator to integrate poor hillside neighborhoods, and "library parks" built in underserved areas to provide cultural and educational resources. Social programs like "Medellín Solidaria" provide cash grants, while "Cultura E" supports entrepreneurship through business centers and microloans. These efforts have drastically reduced homicide rates and extreme poverty.

### Economy
Medellín is Colombia's second-most important economic center. Its economy is led by the powerful Grupo Empresarial Antioqueño, a conglomerate of companies in banking, food, and cement. The city is a major industrial hub for textiles, fashion, and manufacturing, and hosts Latin America's biggest fashion show, Colombiamoda. The Medellín Metropolitan Area produces about 67% of Antioquia's GDP and over 10% of Colombia's economy.

### Culture and Society
The culture is strongly linked to the broader "Paisa" culture, known for its Spanish accent, cuisine, and hospitality. The city is a major cultural center with numerous museums, theaters, and festivals, most famously the Festival of the Flowers. It is recognized as a UNESCO "Design City." The population is predominantly White/Mestizo (93.4%), with significant Afro-Colombian and immigrant communities. Football is the most popular sport, with two major professional clubs.

## Terms
- **Comuna:** An administrative division of the urban area of Medellín.
- **Corregimiento:** An administrative division for the rural townships within the municipality.
- **Grupo Empresarial Antioqueño:** A powerful group of private-sector companies from Antioquia that dominates the regional economy.
- **MetroCable:** A gondola lift system integrated with the metro, used for public transit in steep, low-income neighborhoods.
- **Paisa:** A term for people from the Antioquia region and neighboring departments, referring to their distinct culture and identity.
- **Plan Piloto:** The "Medellín Master Plan" created in the 1950s to guide the city's expansion.
- **Silletero:** A flower farmer who carries elaborate floral arrangements on a wooden frame (silleta) during the Festival of the Flowers.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The effectiveness and fairness of the "Banco de las Opportunidades" microloan program is debated, with critics arguing its interest rates are too high and loan terms too short.
- Significant socioeconomic inequality persists, with crime rates remaining high in the poorest communities despite overall citywide improvements.
- The city's model of development, while lauded, faces ongoing challenges in fully integrating all communities and sustaining economic growth equitably.

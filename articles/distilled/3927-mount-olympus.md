# Mount Olympus

## Overview
Mount Olympus is the highest mountain in Greece, renowned for its exceptional biodiversity and its central role in Greek mythology as the home of the Twelve Olympian gods. Its unique geological and ecological features have made it a site of national and international significance, protected as Greece's first National Park and a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

## Timeline
- **c. 200 million years ago** — Sedimentary rock that forms Olympus is laid down in a shallow sea.
- **c. 1 million years ago** — Glaciers cover the mountain, creating its plateaus and depressions.
- **Antiquity** — The mountain is the mythological home of the Greek gods and a border between Thessaly and Macedon.
- **1938** — Mount Olympus is declared Greece's first National Park.
- **1981** — UNESCO proclaims Olympus a "Biosphere Reserve."
- **2 August 1913** — The summit of Mytikas is first reached by Frédéric Boissonnas, Daniel Baud-Bovy, and Christos Kakkalos.
- **June 2016** — The Olympus National Park Information Center opens in Litochoro.
- **July 2026** — The wider area is inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List.

## Body

### Geography and Geology
Mount Olympus is an extensive massif with 52 peaks, the highest being Mytikas at 2,917.727 metres. Its shape was formed by rain and wind, creating an isolated tower near the Aegean Sea. The mountain is composed of sedimentary rock from 200 million years ago, with its current plateaus and gorges shaped by glaciers around one million years ago. Its morphology features deep gorges, smooth peaks over 2,000 metres, and dramatic central rocky peaks like Stefani (2,902 m).

### Mythology and Name
The origin of the name "Olympos" is unknown, with theories suggesting a pre-Greek root meaning "mountain." In Greek mythology, Olympus was the home of the Twelve Olympian gods. By the 5th century BC, the Thessalian peak was identified as the Pan-Hellenic representative of this mythological seat. The region at its northern foot, Pieria, was associated with the nine Muses.

### History and Human Activity
In antiquity, the massif formed a border between Thessaly and Macedon. During the Ottoman Empire, it was a base for klephts and armatoloi (irregular fighters). The first recorded ascent of the summit occurred in 1913, shortly after the region's liberation from Ottoman rule. Today, Olympus is a major hiking destination, with over 10,000 visitors annually, most starting from the town of Litochoro.

### Climate and Ecology
The climate is Mediterranean with continental influence, varying from hot, dry summers in the foothills to severe, snowy winters at high elevations. The mountain hosts exceptional biodiversity, with about 1,700 plant species, including 23 local endemics like *Ramonda heldreichii*. Four sequential flora zones exist, from Mediterranean maquis to alpine tundra. The fauna includes 32 mammal species, such as Balkan chamois and wild boar, and 108 bird species.

### Protection and Access
The National Park, established in 1938, protects the mountain's natural and cultural values. It is now 24,000 hectares and part of the Natura 2000 network. Access is primarily from Litochoro, with organized refuges and climbing routes. The final section to Mytikas is a non-technical rock scramble (YDS class 3).

## Terms
- **Massif:** A compact group of mountains.
- **Topographic prominence:** A measure of a peak's independence, calculated as the vertical distance between it and the lowest contour line encircling it but no higher peak.
- **Maquis:** A shrubland biome typical of the Mediterranean, featuring evergreen shrubs and small trees.
- **Alpine tundra:** A treeless, high-altitude ecosystem characterized by low temperatures and short growing seasons.
- **Endemic:** A species native and restricted to a certain place.
- **Klephts:** Greek guerrilla fighters who waged war against the Ottoman Empire.
- **Armatoloi:** Christian irregular soldiers in the Ottoman Empire, often recruited to police regions.
- **YDS class 3:** A climbing difficulty rating indicating a rock scramble requiring the use of hands for balance, with significant exposure.

## Debates and Open Questions
The exact origin of the name "Olympos" remains unknown and is a subject of scholarly speculation. While the mountain's mythological status is clear, the precise location of the "home of the gods" in ancient belief is not tied to a single peak. The systematic study of the mountain's fauna is noted as not yet complete.

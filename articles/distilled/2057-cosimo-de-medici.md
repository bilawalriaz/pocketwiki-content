# Cosimo de' Medici

## Overview
Cosimo de' Medici (1389–1464) was an Italian banker and politician who became the de facto ruler of Florence during the Renaissance. He established the Medici family as its effective leaders for generations, using his immense wealth to control politics, sponsor a vast cultural revival, and create a balance of power in Italy that enabled the Renaissance to flourish.

## Timeline
- **1389** — Born in Florence to Giovanni di Bicci de' Medici.
- **c. 1415** — Named a "Priore" of the Republic of Florence; marries Contessina de' Bardi.
- **1420** — His father Giovanni retires, leaving leadership of the Medici Bank to Cosimo and his brother Lorenzo.
- **1429** — Giovanni dies, leaving his sons 179,221 florins.
- **1433** — Imprisoned by the anti-Medici party and exiled from Florence.
- **1434** — Returns from exile to dominate Florentine politics for the next 30 years.
- **1436** — Brunelleschi completes the dome of Santa Maria del Fiore with Medici funding.
- **1439** — Instrumental in moving the Ecumenical Council of Ferrara to Florence.
- **1444** — Founds the first public library in Florence at San Marco.
- **1447** — Death of Filippo Maria Visconti, Duke of Milan.
- **1450** — Francesco Sforza is acclaimed Duke of Milan, establishing a key political balance.
- **1464** — Dies at Careggi; succeeded by his son Piero. Posthumously honored as *Pater Patriae*.

## Body

### Banking and Early Life
Cosimo inherited his wealth and banking expertise from his father, Giovanni, who founded the Medici Bank. The bank's profits were primarily derived from its role as the papal depositario generale, managing Church finances. After the Medici lost their near-monopoly on papal finances in 1415, they regained priority following the insolvency of the rival Spini Bank in 1420. Cosimo expanded the bank across Europe, making it the preferred bank for the papacy due to its far-flung branches.

### Political Power and Exile
Cosimo's power stemmed from his wealth, which he used to control votes in Florence's municipal councils. He maintained a facade of republican modesty, being viewed as "first among equals" rather than an autocrat. In 1433, his political rivals, led by Rinaldo degli Albizzi, had him imprisoned and exiled. His exile was short-lived; the flight of capital from Florence forced his recall in 1434. He then worked to quash factionalism through constitutional changes to secure his influence.

### Foreign Policy and Balance of Power
After the death of Milan's ruler in 1447, Cosimo supported the mercenary Francesco Sforza to seize power in Milan. This created a crucial balance of power between Florence/Milan and Venice/Naples, fostering nearly half a century of peace that enabled the Renaissance. He also worked to keep foreign powers like France and the Holy Roman Empire out of Italian affairs. In 1439, he helped bring the Ecumenical Council to Florence, which inspired renewed interest in Greek arts and literature.

### Patronage of Arts and Learning
Cosimo spent over 600,000 florins on art and culture, viewing spending as more pleasurable than earning. He commissioned the Palazzo Medici and works by artists like Donatello, Fra Angelico, and Brunelleschi. His patronage was a civic duty, enriching Florence's life. He founded the first public library at San Marco in 1444 and financed extensive book-collecting missions. In philosophy, he supported Marsilio Ficino, commissioned the first complete Latin translation of Plato, and established a Platonic Academy in Florence in 1445.

## Terms
*   **Signoria:** The governing council of the Republic of Florence, which Cosimo controlled through influence.
*   **Pater Patriae:** "Father of the Fatherland," an honorific title awarded to Cosimo posthumously by the Signoria.
*   **Condottiere:** A mercenary soldier for hire; Francesco Sforza was a condottiere who became Duke of Milan with Cosimo's support.
*   **Depositario generale:** A papal financial manager; the Medici Bank held this key position, giving it control over Church finances.
*   **Studia humanitatis:** The core curriculum of Renaissance humanism, including grammar, rhetoric, history, poetry, and moral philosophy.
*   **Neo-Platonism:** A philosophical system revived in the Renaissance, blending Plato's ideas with other traditions, which Cosimo patronized through Ficino.

## Debates and Open Questions
The source notes that Cosimo's power, while immense, was not absolute and was constantly resisted by Florence's legislative councils. His intervention in Milan to install Sforza was unpopular among his fellow citizens, who resented financing it. The text also highlights the scholarly debate on the extent of his personal influence versus that of his grandson, Lorenzo the Magnificent, who is often deemed the greatest Medici patron.

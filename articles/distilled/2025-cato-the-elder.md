# Cato the Elder

## Overview
Marcus Porcius Cato (234–149 BC), known as Cato the Censor, was a Roman statesman, soldier, and writer who became a symbol of traditional Roman values. He is most famous for his staunch conservatism, his opposition to the growing influence of Greek culture (Hellenization), and his relentless advocacy for the destruction of Rome's rival, Carthage. His writings, especially *De agri cultura*, are foundational texts of Latin literature.

## Timeline
- **234 BC** — Born in Tusculum to a plebeian family.
- **218–201 BC** — Fights with distinction in the Second Punic War.
- **204 BC** — Serves as quaestor in Sicily, clashing with Scipio Africanus.
- **195 BC** — Elected consul; unsuccessfully opposes repeal of the Lex Oppia.
- **195–194 BC** — Conducts a successful, brutal campaign in Hispania Citerior.
- **191 BC** — Serves as legate at the Battle of Thermopylae against Antiochus III.
- **184 BC** — Elected censor; enforces strict public morality and public works.
- **c. 160 BC** — Writes *De agri cultura*, the oldest extant Latin prose work.
- **157 BC** — Serves on a failed embassy to Carthage, solidifying his anti-Carthaginian stance.
- **149 BC** — Dies in Rome, having spent his final years urging war on Carthage.

## Body

### Early Life and Military Career
Cato was a *novus homo* (new man), the first in his family to enter the Roman Senate. His early life on a Sabine farm instilled a lifelong devotion to agriculture and simple living. He gained military experience in the Second Punic War, fighting at battles like the Metaurus. His reputation for frugality and traditional values attracted the patronage of the patrician Lucius Valerius Flaccus, who guided him into politics.

### Political Rise and Censorship
Cato held the major magistracies in sequence: quaestor, aedile, praetor, and consul. As consul in 195 BC, he famously but unsuccessfully argued against repealing the Lex Oppia, a law restricting female luxury. His subsequent military command in Hispania was marked by ruthless efficiency and severity. He later served with distinction in Greece against the Seleucid king Antiochus III. His election as censor in 184 BC was the pinnacle of his influence. In this role, he rigorously enforced public morality, expelled senators for misconduct, taxed luxuries, and oversaw major public works, including the first known basilica (Basilica Porcia).

### Opposition to Hellenism and Carthage
Cato was the leading voice against the adoption of Greek culture, which he believed corrupted Roman austerity. He viewed Greek philosophy, medicine, and art as threats to traditional values. This cultural conservatism was matched by his political stance: after a 157 BC embassy to Carthage, he became convinced of its resurgent threat and ended every speech with the phrase "Carthago delenda est" ("Carthage must be destroyed"), helping to incite the Third Punic War.

### Writings
Cato was the first major author to write history in Latin. His *Origines* was a seven-book history of Rome and Italy. His only surviving complete work, *De agri cultura* ("On Agriculture"), is a practical manual on farm management that provides invaluable details on Roman rural life, economy, and the use of slave labor. He also composed numerous speeches and moral maxims.

## Terms
- **Novus homo**: A "new man"; the first in a family to be elected to the Roman Senate.
- **Censor**: A magistrate responsible for conducting the census, supervising public morality, and managing state finances and public works.
- **Hellenization**: The adoption of ancient Greek culture, language, and ideas by other societies, which Cato opposed.
- **Lex Oppia**: A sumptuary law passed in 215 BC to restrict women's wealth and luxury, which Cato defended.
- **Plebeian**: A commoner in ancient Rome, as opposed to the patrician aristocracy.
- **Patrician**: A member of the aristocratic ruling class in early Rome.
- **Legatus**: A high-ranking officer or lieutenant, often a senator serving under a general.
- **Publicani**: Private contractors who bid for the right to collect taxes or undertake public works projects.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Date of Birth**: His birth year is deduced from conflicting ancient reports of his age at death, with sources like Cicero giving 234 BC and others like Livy suggesting he was 90.
- **Knowledge of Greek**: While he publicly disparaged Greek culture and reportedly gave a speech in Athens through an interpreter, some evidence suggests he may have learned Greek later in life or had earlier exposure.
- **Role in Prosecuting the Scipiones**: It is debated whether Cato directly prosecuted Scipio Africanus and his brother, or if his spirit and rhetoric merely animated the attacks against them.

# Dōgen

## Overview
Dōgen Zenji (1200–1253) was a Japanese Zen Buddhist monk and the founder of the Sōtō school of Zen in Japan. He is a pivotal figure in Buddhist history for establishing a distinct Japanese Zen tradition centered on the practice of *zazen* (sitting meditation) and for producing a vast body of philosophical writings that remain central to Sōtō Zen today.

## Timeline
- **1200** — Born into a noble family in Kyoto.
- **1212** — Enters Mount Hiei to study Tendai Buddhism.
- **1217** — Begins training at Kennin-ji under Myōzen.
- **1223** — Travels to Song dynasty China with Myōzen.
- **1225** — Meets his teacher Tiāntóng Rújìng at Mount Tiāntóng.
- **1227** — Receives Dharma transmission; returns to Japan.
- **1230** — Leaves Kyoto due to tensions with the Tendai school.
- **1243** — Relocates his community to Echizen province.
- **1246** — Renames his temple Daibutsu-ji to Eihei-ji.
- **1253** — Dies in Kyoto, shortly after falling ill.

## Body

### Early Life and Training
Dōgen was born into the Minamoto clan and entered the Tendai school on Mount Hiei at age thirteen. He became troubled by the Tendai doctrine of *hongaku* (original enlightenment), which posits that all beings are inherently enlightened. This led to his central question: if enlightenment is innate, why is practice necessary? Finding no answer in Japan, he traveled to China in 1223 to study Chan (Zen) Buddhism.

### China and Enlightenment
In China, Dōgen trained under various masters but was initially dissatisfied with the prevalent emphasis on *kōan* (paradoxical riddles for meditation). In 1225, he met Tiāntóng Rújìng, a master of the Cáodòng (Sōtō) lineage. Under Rújìng, Dōgen had a breakthrough realization upon hearing the phrase "cast off body and mind." He received Dharma transmission in 1227, settling his lifelong spiritual question.

### Founding the Sōtō School
Upon returning to Japan, Dōgen promoted *zazen* as the core Buddhist practice. Facing suppression from the established Tendai school, he eventually moved his community north to Echizen province in 1243. There, he founded Eihei-ji, which remains a head temple of Sōtō Zen. He spent his final years teaching and writing there, producing his major works.

### Teachings: Zazen and Practice-Verification
Dōgen taught that *zazen*, specifically *shikantaza* ("just sitting"), is the direct expression of enlightenment. He described the correct mental state as *hishiryō* ("non-thinking"), a state beyond conceptual thought. A foundational concept is *shushō-ittō* ("oneness of practice-verification"), meaning that practice and enlightenment are not separate; the act of sitting in meditation is itself the realization of enlightenment. He did not reject other Buddhist rituals but saw them as secondary to zazen.

### Teachings: Buddha-Nature and Being-Time
Dōgen reinterpreted *busshō* (Buddha-nature) as the totality of all reality, including impermanent and inanimate phenomena like grass and trees. He taught that impermanence itself is Buddha-nature. Another key metaphysical concept is *uji* ("Being-Time"), which asserts that all existence is dynamic, momentary, and inseparable from time.

### Writings and Legacy
Dōgen's magnum opus is the *Shōbōgenzō* ("Treasury of the True Dharma Eye"), a collection of 95 fascicles on Zen philosophy and practice. He also wrote instructional texts like *Fukanzazengi* and *Bendōwa*. His lineage faced challenges after his death, but his teachings were preserved and later systematized. He is co-founder of the Sōtō school with Keizan Jōkin (1268–1325).

## Terms
- **Zazen**: Sitting meditation, the central practice in Dōgen's Zen.
- **Shikantaza**: "Just sitting"; a form of zazen involving alert, objectless awareness.
- **Hishiryō**: "Non-thinking"; the mental state in zazen beyond thought and non-thought.
- **Shushō-ittō**: "Oneness of practice-verification"; the unity of cultivation and enlightenment.
- **Busshō**: Buddha-nature; for Dōgen, the reality of all things, including impermanence.
- **Uji**: "Being-Time"; the concept that all being is dynamic and temporal.
- **Kōan**: A paradoxical question or statement used in Zen training.
- **Hongaku**: The Tendai doctrine of original enlightenment, which Dōgen questioned.
- **Sōtō**: The school of Zen founded by Dōgen in Japan, based on the Chinese Cáodòng lineage.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The interpretation of Dōgen's concept of *uji* (Being-Time) is debated, with some scholars comparing it to Western philosophy and others arguing it emphasizes momentary existence.
- The exact date and circumstances of the compilation of his koan collection, the *Shinji Shōbōgenzō*, are disputed.
- His lineage faced immediate conflict after his death over the introduction of esoteric practices, leading to schisms within the early Sōtō school.

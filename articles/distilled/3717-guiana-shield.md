# Guiana Shield

## Overview
The Guiana Shield is a 1.7 billion-year-old Precambrian geological formation in northeast South America, forming one of the three cratons (stable continental crust) of the South American plate. It is a region of immense ecological significance, hosting vast tropical forests and exceptional biodiversity, including unique tabletop mountains called tepuis and world-famous waterfalls like Angel Falls.

## Timeline
- **c. 1.7 billion years ago** — Formation of the Guiana Shield craton.
- **Archean Eon** — Formation of the oldest rocks, the Imataca Complex.
- **Early Proterozoic** — Formation of the Pastora Supergroup and Supamo Complex south of the Guri Fault.
- **Early to Middle Proterozoic** — Deposition of the Roraima Group sediments (1.8 to 1.4 Ga).
- **Neogene Period** — Deposition of thick sediments in the Sub-Andean Trough, separating the shield from the Andes.
- **2014** — Colombia designates a 250-hectare area of the shield as a Ramsar Wetland.

## Body

### Geology and Geomorphology
The Guiana Shield is composed of ancient Precambrian rocks. Its oldest core is the Archean Imataca Complex of gneisses. South of the Guri Fault lie Early Proterozoic metavolcanic and granitic rocks. Overlying these are sub-horizontal layers of sedimentary rocks from the Roraima Group, including sandstones and conglomerates.

The shield's higher elevations form the Guiana Highlands, characterized by flat-topped tepuis and rounded granite peaks. The highest point is Pico da Neblina (2,995 m) on the Brazil-Venezuela border. The shield's western edge is the Chiribiquete Plateau, separated from the Andes by the Sub-Andean Trough. Three main upland areas exist: the Guiana Highlands proper, the Tumucumaque Uplands, and the Chiribiquete Plateau.

### Ecology
The Guiana Shield is one of the world's most biodiverse regions, with high levels of endemism (species found nowhere else). It supports over 3,000 vertebrate species, including 1,168 freshwater fish and 269 amphibians (54% endemic). Plant diversity is also high, with 13,367 vascular plant species recorded, approximately 40% of which are endemic.

The region contains the largest expanse of tropical forest on any Precambrian shield. Protected areas include the Iwokrama Forest (Guyana), Central Suriname Nature Reserve (UNESCO World Heritage Site), and several national parks in Venezuela, Brazil, and French Guiana. Emerging ecological threats include invasive plant species like *Poa annua* and the presence of the bacteria *Helicobacter pylori*.

## Terms
- **Craton**: A large, stable block of the Earth's crust that forms the nucleus of a continent.
- **Precambrian**: The vast span of geological time before the Cambrian Period, covering about 88% of Earth's history.
- **Tepui**: A table-top mountain or mesa found in the Guiana Highlands, characterized by sheer cliffs and flat summits.
- **Metasediment/Metavolcanic**: Sedimentary and volcanic rocks that have been altered by heat and pressure (metamorphism).
- **Endemic**: A species that is native to and found only in a specific, limited geographic area.
- **Gneiss**: A coarse-grained metamorphic rock formed from high heat and pressure.
- **Sub-Andean Trough**: A sedimentary basin running along the western rim of the shield, filled with Neogene sediments.

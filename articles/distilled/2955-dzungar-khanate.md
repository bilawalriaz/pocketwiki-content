# Dzungar Khanate

## Overview
The Dzungar Khanate was the last great nomadic empire of Inner Asia, ruled by the Oirat (Western) Mongols. At its height in the 17th and early 18th centuries, it dominated a vast territory from Siberia to Tibet and from Mongolia to Kazakhstan, challenging the Qing and Russian empires. Its destruction by the Qing dynasty in the mid-18th century, which included the genocide of the Dzungar people, led to the creation of modern Xinjiang and a major demographic reshaping of the region.

## Timeline
- **c. 1620** — The Oirat tribes unite in the Junggar Basin, forming the Dzungar Khanate.
- **1678** — Galdan receives the title Boshogtu Khan, making the Dzungars the leading Oirat tribe.
- **1680** — Galdan conquers the Yarkent Khanate (Tarim Basin).
- **1688** — Galdan defeats the Khalkha Mongols, driving them to submit to the Qing dynasty.
- **1696** — Qing forces defeat Galdan at the Battle of Jao Modo; he dies in 1697.
- **1717** — Dzungars conquer Tibet but are expelled by a Qing expedition in 1720.
- **1727–1745** — Reign of Galdan Tseren, during which the khanate reaches its greatest power.
- **1745** — Death of Galdan Tseren triggers a civil war and succession crisis.
- **1755** — Qing army invades, captures the Dzungar leader Dawachi, and dissolves the khanate.
- **1755–1757** — Amursana's rebellion against the Qing leads to the genocide of the Dzungar people.
- **1759** — Qing completes the conquest of the Tarim Basin, establishing control over Xinjiang.

## Body

### Origins and Rise
The Dzungars were an Oirat (Western Mongol) people originally from northwestern Mongolia. After submitting to Genghis Khan in 1208, they remained distinct from the eastern Khalkha Mongols. Internal strife and pressure from the Khalkhas in the 16th and early 17th centuries led to their consolidation in the Junggar Basin. Under leaders like Erdeni Batur (r. 1634–1653), they expanded west, defeating Kazakh forces and establishing a capital at Ghulja. The khanate's power was cemented by Galdan Boshugtu Khan (r. 1671–1697), a former Buddhist monk who unified the Oirats, conquered the Tarim Basin, and defeated the Khalkhas.

### Expansion and Conflict with Neighbors
Galdan's expansion brought the Dzungars into direct conflict with the Qing dynasty. After his defeat by the Kangxi Emperor in 1696, his successor Tsewang Rabtan continued wars against the Kazakhs and Qing. Under Galdan Tseren (r. 1727–1745), the khanate reached its zenith, dominating the Kazakh hordes and controlling a vast territory. However, the costly wars and internal strife weakened the state. After Galdan Tseren's death, a civil war erupted between claimants like Dawachi and Amursana, drawing in neighboring powers.

### Downfall and Genocide
The Qing dynasty exploited the Dzungar civil war. In 1755, a Qing army swiftly captured Dawachi. When Amursana, a rival claimant, rebelled against Qing control, the Qianlong Emperor ordered a brutal campaign of extermination. The Dzungar population, estimated at around 600,000, was devastated by war, smallpox, and systematic massacre. This genocide emptied Dzungaria, allowing the Qing to resettle the area with Han, Hui, Uyghur, and other peoples, creating the modern demographic of Xinjiang. The Qing framed this conquest as unifying "inner and outer" territories into "one family."

### Society and Culture
The Dzungars were Tibetan Buddhists who created a sophisticated nomadic state. They practiced animal husbandry and limited agriculture, using slave labor from the Tarim Basin. Uniquely in Central Asia, they developed a domestic arms industry, manufacturing firearms and bronze cannons. Their society was regulated by the Oirat Mongol Legal Code of 1640, and they contributed to Mongolian literature through the creation of the Clear Script.

## Terms
- **Oirat**: The "Western Mongols," a distinct group of Mongol tribes that included the Dzungars, Khoshuts, Torghuts, and Dörbets.
- **Khong Tayiji**: A title meaning "crown prince," used by Dzungar rulers before they assumed the title of Khan.
- **Boshogtu Khan**: A high title bestowed by the Dalai Lama upon Galdan, signifying supreme religious and secular authority.
- **Tarim Basin**: A large depression in southern Xinjiang, historically home to oasis city-states like Kashgar and Yarkand.
- **Yarkent Khanate**: A Chagatai Khanate in the Tarim Basin, conquered by the Dzungars in 1680.
- **Khalkha**: The "Eastern Mongols" of Outer Mongolia, who submitted to the Qing after defeats by the Dzungars.
- **Qing dynasty**: The Manchu-led dynasty of China (1644–1912) that ultimately destroyed the Dzungar Khanate.
- **Jiao**: A term meaning "extermination," used in Qing orders during the campaign against the Dzungars.
- **Xinjiang**: The name given by the Qing to the newly conquered territories of Dzungaria and the Tarim Basin, meaning "New Frontier."

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Scale of Genocide**: Historians debate the exact Dzungar population before the conquest (estimates range from 400,000 to 600,000+) and the precise causes of their destruction, weighing the roles of deliberate Qing policy, disease (smallpox), and flight.
- **Qing Motives**: Scholars analyze whether the Qing invasion was primarily a defensive response to Dzungar aggression or an opportunistic expansionist move to eliminate a rival nomadic empire.
- **Amursana's Role**: The motivations and actions of Amursana, who allied with the Qing before rebelling, are central to understanding the final collapse and the subsequent genocide.

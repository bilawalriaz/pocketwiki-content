# Chinese literature

## Overview
Chinese literature spans over three millennia, from the earliest oracle bone inscriptions to contemporary online novels. It evolved from classical texts in Literary Chinese to modern works in the vernacular, profoundly shaping and reflecting Chinese culture, philosophy, and society. Its development was accelerated by printing technologies and marked by distinct golden ages in poetry, prose, and fiction across successive dynasties.

## Timeline
- **c. 11th–7th century BC** — Compilation of the *Shijing* (Classic of Poetry).
- **770–256 BC** — Hundred Schools of Thought; major philosophical classics written.
- **c. 389 BC** — Compilation of the *Zuo Zhuan*, an early narrative history.
- **145–90 BC** — Sima Qian writes the *Shiji* (Records of the Grand Historian).
- **990–1051** — Bi Sheng invents movable type printing.
- **618–907** — Tang Dynasty: Golden age of poetry (Li Bai, Du Fu).
- **960–1279** — Song Dynasty: Flourishing of *ci* lyrics and encyclopedias.
- **14th–18th centuries** — Development of mature novels in written vernacular Chinese.
- **1897** — First survey book of Chinese literature published (in Japanese).
- **1910s–1920s** — New Culture Movement promotes vernacular literature.
- **1942** — Mao Zedong's *Talks at the Yan'an Forum* set guidelines for socialist literature.
- **1966–1976** — Cultural Revolution: Literature becomes highly politicized.
- **2000** — Gao Xingjian wins the Nobel Prize in Literature.

## Body

### Pre-classical and Classical Texts
Chinese literature originated from oral traditions, including musical practices, divination, and ritual. The foundational classics emerged during the Eastern Zhou's Hundred Schools of Thought (770–256 BC). The most influential were the Confucian **Four Books and Five Classics**, which later formed the basis of the imperial examination system. Key Daoist works include the *Dao De Jing* and *Zhuangzi*. Military science was represented by Sun Tzu's *The Art of War*. These texts were primarily philosophical and didactic, establishing enduring ideas and prose styles.

### Historical Writing and Encyclopedias
A tradition of accurate court records began by 841 BC. The definitive early historical work is Sima Qian's *Shiji* (c. 90 BC), which established the model for official dynastic histories. This tradition culminated in the **Twenty-Four Histories**. China also produced massive encyclopedias, such as the Tang *Yiwen Leiju* and the Ming *Yongle Encyclopedia*, which contained 50 million characters. The Qing *Complete Classics Collection of Ancient China* exceeded 100 million characters.

### Classical Poetry
The poetic tradition began with the folk and ceremonial *Shijing* and the emotionally intense *Chuci*. During the Han Dynasty, the descriptive *fu* form and Music Bureau (*yuefu*) poetry developed. The five-character line became standard after the Han. The Tang Dynasty is considered the golden age of poetry, with masters like Li Bai and Du Fu perfecting regulated verse. The *ci* lyric form, set to musical tunes, flourished in the Song Dynasty. Poetry composition remained a core scholarly skill through the Ming and Qing.

### Classical Prose and Fiction
Early prose was shaped by philosophical texts. The Tang Dynasty saw a neoclassical prose movement led by Han Yu, advocating a return to simple, forceful style. Vernacular fiction grew from oral storytelling traditions (*bianwen*, *pingshu*) and became popular from the Song Dynasty onward. The novel as a mature form evolved between the 14th and 18th centuries, with masterpieces like the 18th-century *Dream of the Red Chamber*.

### Modern Literature (Late Qing to Present)
Modern literature began in the late Qing (1895–1911) with reformist fiction and translations of Western works. The New Culture Movement (1910s–1920s) championed the vernacular language, with Lu Xun as a pioneering stylist. The Republican Era (1912–49) saw the rise of leftist, modernist, and female writers. After 1949, literature was controlled by the state under socialist realism. The Cultural Revolution (1966–76) severely restricted artistic expression. Post-1978 reforms led to "scar literature" and experimental writing. Contemporary literature is diverse, commercialized, and includes a massive online literature sector.

## Terms
- **Literary Chinese (Wenyan):** The classical written language, based on ancient texts, used for formal writing until the 20th century.
- **Vernacular Chinese (Baihua):** The spoken language of the people, promoted for modern literature in the New Culture Movement.
- **Shijing (Classic of Poetry):** The oldest existing collection of Chinese poetry, dating from the 11th to 7th centuries BC.
- **Ci:** A type of lyric poetry written to fit the meter of specific, pre-existing musical tunes.
- **Four Books and Five Classics:** The core Confucian texts that formed the basis of the imperial examination system.
- **Shiji (Records of the Grand Historian):** The foundational work of Chinese historiography, written by Sima Qian.
- **Socialist Realism:** The official artistic doctrine in Maoist China, requiring art to depict revolutionary struggle realistically.
- **New Culture Movement:** An intellectual reform movement in the 1910s-20s that promoted science, democracy, and vernacular literature.
- **Online Literature (Wangwen):** Fiction serialized and consumed on digital platforms, often in genres like fantasy and romance.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The origins of modern Chinese literature are debated, with scholars tracing them from the 17th century to the late Qing period.
- The stylistic merits and distinctiveness of Song poetry relative to Tang poetry have been debated since the Song Dynasty itself.
- The authenticity of early historical texts like the *Bamboo Annals* is questioned.
- The ethical and artistic value of AI-assisted translation for Chinese online literature is a current debate.

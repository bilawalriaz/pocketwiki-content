# Mount Kilimanjaro

## Overview
Mount Kilimanjaro is a large dormant volcano in Tanzania and the highest mountain in Africa. It is the world's highest free-standing mountain above sea level, a major hiking destination, and its rapidly shrinking glaciers are a significant indicator of climate change.

## Timeline
- **c. 2.4 million years ago** — Eruptive activity at the Shira volcanic center begins.
- **c. 1 million years ago** — The Mawenzi and Kibo volcanic cones begin erupting.
- **1848** — German missionary Johannes Rebmann becomes the first European to report the mountain's existence.
- **1889** — Hans Meyer and Ludwig Purtscheller make the first confirmed ascent of Uhuru Peak.
- **1912** — The first ascent of Mawenzi's highest summit is made.
- **1973** — Mount Kilimanjaro is incorporated into Kilimanjaro National Park.
- **2011** — Over 80% of the mountain's ice cover, present in 1912, has disappeared.

## Body

### Geology and Glaciers
Kilimanjaro is a stratovolcano composed of three distinct cones: the extinct Shira and Mawenzi, and the dormant Kibo. Uhuru Peak, on Kibo's crater rim, is the highest point at 5,895 m. The mountain possesses Africa's largest glaciers, which are remnants of a once-extensive ice cap. This ice cap is shrinking rapidly due to decreased specific humidity and solar radiation, with over 80% lost since 1912. It is projected to disappear entirely by the mid-21st century.

### Human History and Exploration
The mountain's name, "Kilimanjaro," has disputed origins, possibly meaning "mountain of greatness" or "unclimbable." The Chagga people lived on its slopes in sovereign states until 1963. While described in classical sources, Rebmann was the first European to report it in 1848. After several attempts by others, Meyer and Purtscheller reached the summit in 1889. The summit was renamed Uhuru Peak ("Freedom Peak") after the Zanzibar Revolution in 1964.

### Ecology and Climate
Kilimanjaro hosts diverse ecological zones, from cultivated foothills to montane forests, heathlands, and an arctic summit. It is home to endemic species like the giant groundsel (*Dendrosenecio kilimanjari*). The climate is defined by two rainy seasons and strong daily wind patterns. The summit experiences an ice-cap climate with average temperatures around -7 °C.

### Tourism and Climbing
As one of the Seven Summits, Kilimanjaro is a major trekking destination with seven official routes. The Marangu ("Coca-Cola") route is the easiest, while the Machame route is popular for its acclimatization profile. The climb is not technically difficult but poses a serious risk of altitude sickness; studies show over 70% of trekkers experience some form of it. The park generates significant revenue and employment for guides, porters, and cooks.

## Terms
- **Stratovolcano**: A tall, conical volcano built up by many layers of hardened lava and other ejecta.
- **Dormant**: A volcano that is not currently erupting but is expected to erupt again.
- **Uhuru Peak**: The highest summit on Mount Kilimanjaro, located on the rim of the Kibo crater.
- **Caldera**: A large cauldron-like hollow that forms shortly after the emptying of a magma chamber in a volcanic eruption.
- **Fumarole**: A vent in or near a volcano, through which hot sulfurous gases emerge.
- **Montane forest**: A forest ecosystem found in mountains.
- **Endemic**: A species native and restricted to a certain place.
- **Altitude sickness**: A condition caused by ascending to a high altitude too quickly, with symptoms including headache and nausea.
- **Sublimation**: The transition of a substance directly from the solid to the gas state, without passing through the liquid state.

## Debates and open questions
- The origin and meaning of the name "Kilimanjaro" is disputed, with multiple etymologies proposed from Chagga and Swahili languages.
- The exact age of Kilimanjaro's glaciers is debated; ice core data suggests some may be around 11,700 years old, while other analysis indicates an age of only 800 years.
- The primary cause of the glaciers' recent shrinkage is debated, with evidence pointing more to decreased specific humidity than to direct temperature increases.

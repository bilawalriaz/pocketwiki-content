# Mackenzie River

## Overview
The Mackenzie River is Canada's longest river system and the largest river flowing into the Arctic Ocean from North America. It forms a vast, thinly populated watershed draining about 20% of Canada, serving as a critical transportation corridor and ecological lifeline in the boreal forest and tundra of the Northwest Territories.

## Timeline
- **c. 30,000 years ago** — Laurentide Ice Sheet covers most of northern Canada during the last glacial maximum.
- **c. 13,000 years ago** — Massive glacial lake outburst floods from Lake Agassiz scour the Mackenzie channel.
- **>10,000 years ago** — Mackenzie valley is believed to be a path for initial human migration from Asia to North America.
- **14 July 1789** — Explorer Alexander Mackenzie reaches the river's mouth on the Arctic Ocean.
- **16 August 1825** — Sir John Franklin becomes the next European to reach the river's mouth.
- **1858–1859** — Catholic missions are founded at Fort Simpson, Fort Norman, and Fort Good Hope.
- **1880s** — Steamboat service begins on the river.
- **1920s** — Oil is discovered at Norman Wells, beginning industrialization; European trappers increase, depleting fur populations.
- **1928** — A severe influenza epidemic kills as many as one in ten Indigenous people along the river.
- **1940s** — The Canol pipeline, a WWII project, is built from Norman Wells to Alaska and quickly abandoned.
- **1968** — The W.A.C. Bennett Dam is completed on the upper Peace River.
- **1997** — The Nagwichoonjik (Mackenzie River) National Historic Site is designated at Tsiigehtchic.
- **2008** — Researchers successfully extract natural gas from methane hydrates in the Mackenzie Delta.

## Body

### Geography and Flow
The Mackenzie River system's ultimate source is Thutade Lake in British Columbia. Its main stem, 1,738 km long, flows from Great Slave Lake to the Arctic Ocean, forming a massive delta. The river is frozen for most of the year, breaking up in May or June. Its average discharge at the mouth is 9,910 m³/s, second in Canada only to the St. Lawrence. About 60% of its water comes from the western half of the basin, with the Liard and Peace rivers being major contributors. Large lakes like Great Slave and Great Bear regulate the flow from the east.

### Drainage Basin and Geology
The Mackenzie drainage basin covers 1,805,000 km², nearly 20% of Canada. Permafrost underlies about three-quarters of the watershed. The river system is geologically young, formed as the Laurentide Ice Sheet retreated. Before glaciation, most tributaries flowed east to Hudson Bay; the ice sheet's weight depressed the land, capturing the system and establishing its current northwest flow to the Arctic. The river carries a large sediment load, primarily from its western tributaries.

### Ecology
The watershed is one of North America's largest intact ecosystems, with 63% forested and 18% wetlands. Over 93% of the forest is virgin old-growth. The basin hosts 53 fish species, none endemic, with fauna similar to the Mississippi system due to past Ice Age connections. Major deltas serve as critical resting and breeding areas for migratory birds, though populations have declined since the 1960s due to altered water levels from dams. The Mackenzie estuary is a calving area for beluga whales.

### Human History and Use
Indigenous peoples, including the Inuvialuit and Gwich'in, have lived along the river for thousands of years. The river was the main route for European exploration and later for the fur trade, which was limited by harsh conditions. Industrialization began with oil discovery at Norman Wells in the 1920s. Today, the river is a vital transportation link for barges and ice roads, connecting isolated communities. Agriculture is concentrated in the southern Peace River valley. While there are no dams on the main stem, major hydroelectric dams on tributaries like the Peace River have altered downstream ecosystems.

## Terms
- **Boreal forest**: A biome characterized by coniferous forests, covering much of the Mackenzie basin.
- **Permafrost**: Ground that remains frozen for two or more consecutive years, underlying most of the watershed.
- **Delta**: A landform created by sediment deposition at the mouth of a river; the Mackenzie Delta is the second-largest Arctic delta.
- **Proglacial lake**: A lake formed from glacial meltwater, such as the ancient Lake McConnell.
- **Pingos**: Hills of earth-covered ice found in the Mackenzie Delta.
- **Muskeg**: A swampy, boggy terrain typical of the boreal forest, composed of moss and peat.
- **Braided river**: A river with multiple, interweaving channels, common in the Mackenzie's lower reaches.
- **Methane hydrate**: A solid form of methane trapped in ice, found in the Mackenzie Delta.

## Debates and Open questions
- The exact path and timing of the initial human migration through the Mackenzie valley over 10,000 years ago remain debated due to sparse archaeological evidence.
- The proposed Mackenzie Valley Pipeline has not been built due to environmental concerns and economic factors, leaving the future of regional oil and gas development uncertain.
- The ecological impacts of hydroelectric dams on the Peace River, particularly on downstream nutrient flow and wildlife, are a subject of ongoing concern.

# Megalith

## Overview
A megalith is a large stone used to construct a prehistoric structure or monument, either alone or with other stones. Found worldwide, with over 35,000 identified in Europe alone, they are significant as evidence of complex prehistoric societies capable of large-scale engineering and organized labor. Most were built between the Neolithic and Bronze Ages (c. 9000 BC to 1200 BC), serving diverse purposes from burial sites to astronomical markers and ceremonial centers.

## Timeline
- **c. 9000 BC** — Earliest known ceremonial structures built at Göbekli Tepe, Turkey.
- **c. 7400 BC** — A large monolith is submerged in the Strait of Sicily.
- **c. 5000 BC** — Atlantic Neolithic period begins, spreading agriculture along western Europe.
- **c. 4800 BC** — Major constructions begin in Brittany, France (Barnenez).
- **c. 3600 BC** — Megalithic temples built in Malta (Ġgantija, Mnajdra).
- **c. 3400 BC** — Newgrange passage grave constructed in Ireland.
- **c. 2500 BC** — Climax of megalithic Bell-beaker culture; Stonehenge built.
- **c. 2000 BC** — Chalcolithic gives way to Bronze Age in western/northern Europe.
- **c. 1200 BC** — Last vestiges of megalithic tradition in the Mediterranean after Bronze Age Collapse.

## Body

### Types and Definitions
"Megalith" describes both single stones and structures built from multiple shaped stones. Common types include the **menhir** (single upright stone), **dolmen** (stone table, often a burial chamber originally covered by a mound), **stone circle** (cromlech), and **alignment** (rows of stones, as at Carnac). **Megalithic tombs** are aboveground burial chambers built of large slabs, differing from earthen long barrows. The most common European tomb type is the **portal tomb** (dolmen), followed by **passage graves** (chambers accessed by a long passage, like Newgrange) and **gallery graves** (axially arranged chambers).

### Geographic Distribution
Megaliths are found globally. In Europe, they are concentrated in Atlantic regions (Brittany, Ireland, Iberia) and the Mediterranean (Malta, Sardinia). The oldest European megaliths are believed to be in northwest France. In Asia, the greatest concentration of megalithic burials (dolmens) is in the Korean Peninsula, with an estimated 30,000 examples. Living megalithic traditions persist in parts of Indonesia (Sumba, Nias) and among the Toraja of Sulawesi. In Africa, notable sites include Nabta Playa in Egypt (an astronomical device from the 5th millennium BC) and Senegambian megaliths used for ancestral reverence. West Asia has very early examples at Göbekli Tepe and Atlit Yam, and dolmens are found from Syria to Yemen.

### Purposes and Construction
Theories on purpose include use as burial sites, astronomical markers, territorial boundaries, and religious symbols. While early 20th-century scholars proposed a single global "Megalithic culture," modern dating disproves this, showing regional variations. Construction methods are debated; while wooden rollers are often suggested, experiments show they are impractical on uneven ground. Contemporary megalith-building cultures, like in Sumba, use timber sledges and large labor forces, sometimes involving thousands of people and significant ritual feasting.

## Terms
- **Menhir**: A single upright standing stone erected in prehistoric times.
- **Dolmen**: A stone table, typically a burial chamber consisting of a large capstone supported by several upright stones, often originally covered by a mound.
- **Passage Grave**: A megalithic tomb with a chamber accessed by a long, straight passageway, usually covered by a circular mound.
- **Cromlech**: A Welsh term for a stone circle, sometimes used generally for megalithic structures.
- **Neolithic**: The New Stone Age, characterized by the development of agriculture and pottery.
- **Chalcolithic**: The Copper Age, a transitional period between the Neolithic and Bronze Age.
- **Orthostat**: A large stone set upright as part of a structure.
- **Capstone**: A large flat stone placed horizontally atop upright stones to form a roof.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The primary function of many megalithic tombs is debated; while many contain human remains, their use may have been more ritual than purely funerary.
- The connection between druids and megaliths, proposed in the 19th century, is considered unproven but persists in popular imagination.
- The exact methods used to transport and erect massive stones in prehistory remain a subject of experimental archaeology and scholarly disagreement.
- The relationship between different regional megalithic traditions (e.g., whether European traditions were influenced by earlier West Asian ones like Göbekli Tepe) is uncertain.

# Murray Gell-Mann

## Overview
Murray Gell-Mann (1929–2019) was a preeminent American theoretical physicist whose work fundamentally shaped the modern understanding of elementary particles. He introduced the concept of quarks, the fundamental constituents of matter, and developed key theoretical frameworks like the "eightfold way" and quantum chromodynamics (QCD), which are cornerstones of the Standard Model of particle physics. His contributions earned him the 1969 Nobel Prize in Physics.

## Body

### Early Life and Education
Gell-Mann was born to Jewish immigrants in New York. He was a prodigious student, graduating from Yale at 18 and earning his Ph.D. from MIT in 1951 under Victor Weisskopf. After postdoctoral work, he joined Caltech in 1955, where he remained for most of his career.

### Classification of Particles and the Quark Model
In the 1950s, Gell-Mann worked on newly discovered cosmic ray particles (kaons and hyperons). He proposed the quantum number "strangeness" to explain their behavior. In 1961, inspired by a conversation with mathematician Richard Block, he introduced a novel classification scheme for hadrons (particles made of quarks) called the "eightfold way," named for the octets of particles it contained. This scheme was later explained by his 1964 postulate, made independently by George Zweig, that hadrons are composed of more fundamental particles he named "quarks." This model superseded the eightfold way and earned him the Nobel Prize.

### Weak Interactions and Quantum Chromodynamics
In 1958, Gell-Mann and Richard Feynman developed the V-A (vector minus axial vector) theory of the weak interaction, explaining its chiral structure following the discovery of parity violation. In the 1970s, he was a co-inventor of quantum chromodynamics (QCD), the gauge theory of the strong interaction. QCD explains how quarks are confined within hadrons via the exchange of gluons and forms a major part of the Standard Model.

### Later Work and Legacy
Gell-Mann introduced current algebra in the 1960s to extract predictions from quark models. He also contributed to the seesaw theory of neutrino masses and helped keep string theory alive during its early years. He co-founded the Santa Fe Institute in 1984 to study complex systems. Outside physics, he had wide-ranging interests in archaeology, linguistics, and birdwatching.

## Terms
- **Hadron**: A composite particle made of quarks, held together by the strong force (e.g., protons, neutrons, mesons).
- **Quark**: A fundamental particle and a constituent of hadrons. Gell-Mann coined the name.
- **Strangeness**: A quantum number proposed by Gell-Mann to classify certain hadrons and explain their decay patterns.
- **Eightfold Way**: A 1961 classification scheme for hadrons based on their symmetries, which predicted the existence of new particles.
- **V-A Theory**: A theory of the weak interaction developed with Feynman, describing its chiral (handed) structure.
- **Quantum Chromodynamics (QCD)**: The theory of the strong interaction, describing how quarks and gluons interact and are confined.
- **Gluon**: The gauge boson that mediates the strong force between quarks in QCD.
- **Standard Model**: The theoretical framework describing elementary particles and three of the four fundamental forces (electromagnetic, weak, strong).

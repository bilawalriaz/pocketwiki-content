# Fuzhou

## Overview
Fuzhou is the capital of Fujian province in southeastern China, situated on the Min River estuary. It is a major historical, economic, and cultural center for the region, with a history spanning over two millennia. The city is the core of the Mindong linguistic and cultural area and has been a significant port and hub for trade, education, and scientific research.

## Timeline
- **c. 3000 BC** — Tanshishan neolithic culture flourishes in the Fuzhou area.
- **202 BC** — King Wuzhu establishes the walled city of Ye, considered the founding of Fuzhou.
- **110 BC** — Han dynasty annexes Minyue, incorporating the Fuzhou area into China proper.
- **282 AD** — East Lake, West Lake, and a canal system are constructed in Ye.
- **725 AD** — The city is formally renamed Fuzhou.
- **909 AD** — The Min Kingdom is proclaimed, with Fuzhou as a key center.
- **1842** — The Treaty of Nanjing makes Fuzhou one of five treaty ports open to Western trade.
- **1884** — The Battle of Fuzhou results in the destruction of the Qing Fujian Fleet.
- **1949** — The People's Liberation Army occupies Fuzhou.
- **1984** — Fuzhou is designated one of China's first "Open Coastal Cities."
- **2016** — Fuzhou Metro Line 1 opens, the first rapid transit in Fujian.

## Body

### History and Development
The area shows ancient genetic links to Austronesian peoples and was home to neolithic cultures like Tanshishan (c. 3000 BC). During the Warring States period, it was part of Minyue. The city's traditional founding date is 202 BC, when King Wuzhu established the walled city of Ye. It was renamed Fuzhou in 725 AD. The city prospered during the Tang and Song dynasties, becoming a center for culture and education. It was a treaty port after the First Opium War (1842) and saw significant foreign missionary activity. Fuzhou was occupied by Japanese forces during World War II (1938-1945) and was heavily bombed by KMT aircraft in the 1950s. Since the 1980s, it has undergone rapid economic development as an "Open Coastal City."

### Geography and Climate
Fuzhou has a humid subtropical climate (Köppen: Cfa) with long, hot, humid summers and short, mild, dry winters. It is influenced by the East Asian monsoon, with torrential rain in late May, and is liable to typhoons in late summer and early autumn. The annual mean temperature is 20.5 °C (68.9 °F). Snow is very rare.

### Culture and Society
The local culture is distinct, with the majority speaking Fuzhounese (Eastern Min). Traditional mainstream religions are Mahayana Buddhism and Taoism, often practiced simultaneously. Local folk religions, such as the worship of Lady Linshui, are also prominent. Fuzhou cuisine is one of the four styles of Fujian cuisine, known for light, umami-rich flavors and dishes like fish balls and Buddha Jumps over the Wall. Traditional crafts include bodiless lacquerware, paper umbrellas, and horn combs.

### Economy and Infrastructure
Fuzhou is Fujian's political, economic, and cultural center. Its economy includes banking, timber-working, engineering, papermaking, and textiles. It was designated an "Open Coastal City" in 1984, attracting foreign investment. The city is a railway hub with high-speed rail connections and is served by Fuzhou Changle International Airport. The Fuzhou Metro system opened in 2016. The port at Mawei has a history as a major shipyard and remains active, with ferry links to Taiwan.

## Terms
- **Minyue**: An ancient state and people in what is now Fujian, considered among the Baiyue of southeastern China.
- **Treaty Port**: A port opened to foreign trade and residence by treaty after a military defeat, as per the Treaty of Nanjing (1842).
- **Fuzhounese**: The prestige form of the Eastern Min language spoken in Fuzhou.
- **Mindong**: The linguistic and cultural region of eastern Fujian, centered on Fuzhou and Ningde.
- **Hualin Temple**: A Buddhist temple in Fuzhou founded in 964, housing one of China's oldest surviving wooden structures.
- **Sanfang Qixiang**: "Three Lanes and Seven Alleys," a historic residential and commercial district in central Fuzhou.
- **Bodiless Lacquerware**: A traditional Fuzhou craft where lacquer is applied over a removable clay or paper-mâché mold.
- **Nature Index**: A database tracking research output, in which Fuzhou ranks among the global top 50 cities.

## Debates and Open Questions
The extent of Japanese command and control over Fuzhou city itself during World War II, as opposed to the port and estuary, is uncertain. The city's name has numerous historical romanizations (e.g., Foochow, Hokchew), reflecting different linguistic influences over time.

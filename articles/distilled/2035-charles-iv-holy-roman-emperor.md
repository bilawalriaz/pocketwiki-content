# Charles IV, Holy Roman Emperor

## Overview
Charles IV (1316–1378) was Holy Roman Emperor and King of Bohemia whose reign marked a transformation of the Empire and a cultural golden age for Bohemia. He is best known for promulgating the Golden Bull of 1356, which regulated imperial elections for centuries, and for making Prague the Empire's intellectual and political center.

## Timeline
- **14 May 1316** — Born in Prague to John of Bohemia (Luxembourg) and Elizabeth of Bohemia (Přemyslid).
- **1331** — Gained military experience in Italy with his father.
- **1334** — Named Margrave of Moravia.
- **11 July 1346** — Elected King of the Romans in opposition to Emperor Louis IV.
- **26 August 1346** — Father killed at the Battle of Crécy; Charles escaped wounded.
- **2 September 1347** — Crowned King of Bohemia.
- **1348** — Founded the University of Prague, the first in Central Europe.
- **17 June 1349** — Re-elected King of the Romans after Louis IV's death.
- **5 April 1355** — Crowned Holy Roman Emperor in Rome.
- **1356** — Promulgated the Golden Bull, regulating imperial elections.
- **4 June 1365** — Crowned King of Burgundy at Arles, the last emperor to receive this crown.
- **1373** — Acquired the Margraviate of Brandenburg for his son Wenceslaus.
- **29 November 1378** — Died in Prague; buried there.

## Body

### Early Life and Accession
Charles was born to the Luxembourg dynasty through his father, John of Bohemia, and the Přemyslid dynasty through his mother, Elizabeth. He was educated in France and was fluent in five languages. From 1333, he administered Bohemian lands due to his father's absence and was named Margrave of Moravia in 1334. In 1346, he was elected King of the Romans by prince-electors opposing Emperor Louis IV, but his position was weak; he was derisively called a "Priest's King." His father and many knights died at the Battle of Crécy in 1346. Civil war was averted when Louis IV died in 1347. Charles consolidated power by founding the University of Prague in 1348, which made the city a cultural center, and by winning re-election in 1349.

### Imperial Rule and the Golden Bull
Charles was crowned Emperor in 1355, focusing on securing the Imperial crown peacefully. His most significant act was the Golden Bull of 1356, which formally established the electoral process for the King of the Romans, ending papal influence in elections and stabilizing imperial politics for four centuries. He pursued a dynastic policy, expanding Bohemian crown lands through purchases and inheritance, including parts of Silesia and the annexation of Brandenburg in 1373. He organized peace-keeping confederations of imperial states, notably the Swabian Landfriede of 1370. In Italy, he pursued a less interventionist policy, avoiding local conflicts and seeking accommodation with the Papacy.

### Cultural Legacy and Death
Charles IV's reign is remembered as the Golden Age of Bohemia. He rebuilt Prague on the model of Paris, establishing the New Town, and patronized arts and architecture, including Charles Bridge, Karlštejn Castle (to house the Imperial Regalia), and the work of architect Peter Parler on St. Vitus Cathedral. He corresponded with humanists like Petrarch. He secured the succession by having his son Wenceslaus elected King of the Romans in 1376. Charles died in 1378 after dividing his lands among his sons and nephews. He is revered in the Czech lands as *Pater Patriae* (father of the country).

## Terms
- **Holy Roman Emperor**: The elected monarch of the Holy Roman Empire, a multi-ethnic complex of territories in Western and Central Europe.
- **King of the Romans**: The title for the elected king of the Empire before being crowned Emperor by the Pope.
- **Golden Bull of 1356**: An edict that codified the process for electing the King of the Romans, naming seven prince-electors and regulating the election.
- **Prince-electors**: The seven (later more) nobles who had the right to elect the King of the Romans.
- **Margrave**: A noble title for a ruler of a border march (military frontier district) of the Holy Roman Empire.
- **Přemyslid dynasty**: The ruling dynasty of Bohemia from the 9th century until 1306.
- **Luxembourg dynasty**: A European royal dynasty of the High Middle Ages.
- **Landfriede**: A peace-keeping confederation of imperial estates, organized by the crown to maintain public order.
- **Imperial Regalia**: The ceremonial objects of the Holy Roman Emperor, including the crown.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The image of Charles as a wise, pious, and peace-loving king was partly constructed by Charles himself and remains influential, though its accuracy is debated.
- His legacy is contentious in some aspects, particularly regarding his dynastic ambitions and the centralizing efforts that met resistance from Bohemian estates.
- His policy in Italy, which involved virtually abandoning imperial rights there, was criticized by contemporaries like Petrarch but may have been a pragmatic choice to secure the imperial title.

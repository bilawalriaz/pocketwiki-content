# Kigali

## Overview
Kigali is the capital and largest city of Rwanda, serving as the nation's administrative, economic, and cultural hub. Founded in 1907 as a German colonial outpost, it became the national capital at independence in 1962. The city is notable for its rapid post-genocide reconstruction and growth, its role in hosting refugees, and its status as a relatively safe and clean African capital.

## Timeline
- **1907** — Founded by German colonial administrator Richard Kandt as his headquarters.
- **1916** — Captured by Belgian forces during World War I.
- **1962** — Becomes the capital of independent Rwanda.
- **1973** — President Juvénal Habyarimana seizes power in a bloodless coup.
- **April 1994** — President Habyarimana is assassinated, triggering the Rwandan genocide.
- **4 July 1994** — The Rwandan Patriotic Front (RPF) seizes control of Kigali, ending the genocide.
- **2006** — Kigali is restructured as one of Rwanda's five provinces.
- **2013** — A city masterplan is adopted to develop Kigali into a modern, decentralized city by 2040.
- **January 2020** — Administrative reforms centralize power in the city-wide council, reducing district autonomy.
- **July 2024** — The UK-Rwanda asylum plan is cancelled.

## Body

### History and Founding
The area was part of the Kingdom of Rwanda from the 15th century. The city's modern founding is dated to 1907, when German resident Richard Kandt established his headquarters on Nyarugenge Hill for its central location, views, and security. Foreign trade began shortly after. Belgium took control during World War I and administered Rwanda from Kigali, though the regional capital was in Bujumbura (Burundi). Kigali remained small, with a population of only 6,000 at independence in 1962.

### The Genocide and Reconstruction
Kigali was not directly affected by the initial Rwandan Civil War (1990-1994). The assassination of President Habyarimana near Kigali airport in April 1994 was the catalyst for the genocide, which killed over 500,000 Tutsi. The RPF resumed fighting and, after encircling the city, captured Kigali on 4 July 1994. Post-genocide, the city has experienced rapid population growth due to migration and high birth rates, with much of the damaged infrastructure rebuilt.

### Governance and Administration
Kigali is a province-level administrative unit divided into three districts: Gasabo, Kicukiro, and Nyarugenge. A 2020 reform transferred significant power from the district mayors to the city-wide council, which is composed of elected and presidential appointees. The city hosts the president's residence and most government ministries.

### Economy and Demographics
Kigali is Rwanda's economic hub, with an economy dominated by the service sector. It is a major port of entry and business center. As of the 2022 census, the population was 1,745,555. The population is young, with 73% under 30. The city has a higher proportion of working-age adults than the national average due to rural-to-urban migration.

### Geography and Climate
Located in central Rwanda, the city is built on rolling hills with an average elevation of 1,567 m (5,141 ft). It has a tropical savanna climate (Aw) with two rainy seasons. The Nyabarongo River forms part of its western and southern borders.

## Terms
- **Primate city**: A city that is disproportionately larger and more influential than any other in the country.
- **Umuganda**: A monthly day of mandatory community service in Rwanda.
- **Mutuelles de santé**: Community-based health insurance providers in Rwanda.
- **Rwandan Patriotic Front (RPF)**: The rebel group that ended the 1994 genocide and has led Rwanda's government since.
- **Hillywood**: A portmanteau for Rwanda's film industry, combining "Hollywood" with Rwanda's nickname, "land of a thousand hills."
- **Cassiterite**: An ore used to obtain tin, mined near Kigali.
- **Tap & Go**: A cashless payment card system used on public buses in Kigali.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **City Founding**: While 1907 is the accepted modern founding date, Rwandan scholar Alexis Kagame promoted an alternative theory that the city was established as a capital in the 14th or 16th century, though direct evidence is lacking.
- **Urban Development**: The city's rapid modernization and masterplan have been accompanied by forced evictions of residents from informal housing, with groups like Human Rights Watch accusing the government of removing poor people and children from the streets.
- **Press Freedom**: International observers such as Reporters Without Borders and Freedom House state that Rwandan media lacks press freedom and is subject to government censorship or self-censorship.

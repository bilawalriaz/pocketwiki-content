# Vikings

## Overview
The Vikings were a seafaring people from Scandinavia (modern Denmark, Norway, Sweden) who raided, traded, and settled across Europe and beyond from the late 8th to the late 11th centuries. This period, known as the Viking Age, profoundly shaped the political, social, and linguistic development of Northern and Eastern Europe, leading to the formation of new states and cultures.

## Timeline
- **793** — Viking raid on Lindisfarne, England, marks the traditional start of the Viking Age.
- **c. 860–870** — Swedish Vikings (Varangians) establish trade routes and settlements in Eastern Europe, founding Kievan Rus'.
- **c. 870** — Norwegian Vikings begin the colonization of Iceland.
- **c. 980** — Greenland is settled by Vikings.
- **985–986** — Erik the Red establishes a Norse colony in Greenland.
- **c. 1000** — Leif Erikson reaches North America (Vinland).
- **1013–1014** — Sweyn Forkbeard becomes King of England.
- **1016–1035** — Cnut the Great rules England, Denmark, and Norway.
- **1043** — The semi-legendary stronghold of Jomsborg is destroyed.
- **1066** — Death of Harald Hardrada at the Battle of Stamford Bridge; Norman conquest of England.

## Body

### Expansion and Settlement
Viking expansion was driven by a combination of factors, including population pressure, the search for wealth and trade, and the political fragmentation of neighboring regions. Using their advanced longships, they raided coastal and riverine settlements from the Mediterranean to the Caspian Sea. They established major trade hubs like Hedeby and Dublin, and founded political entities such as the Duchy of Normandy and the Rus' state in Eastern Europe. Their voyages reached as far as Baghdad and North Africa, and they were the first Europeans to settle in North America, at L'Anse aux Meadows, Newfoundland, c. 1000.

### Society and Culture
Viking society was stratified into three classes: *jarls* (aristocracy), *karls* (free peasants), and *thralls* (slaves). They spoke Old Norse and used a runic alphabet for inscriptions. While often depicted as pagans, they gradually converted to Christianity between the 8th and 12th centuries. Their culture was rich in oral poetry (skaldic verse), law, and craftsmanship, as seen in their intricate metalwork, woodcarving, and shipbuilding. Archaeological evidence, such as burial sites and runestones, provides key insights into their daily life, beliefs, and social structure.

### Trade and Economy
The Vikings were extensive traders, using a bullion economy based on the weight of precious metals, primarily silver. They exported goods like furs, amber, walrus ivory, and slaves, while importing spices, silk, glassware, and wine from as far as China and Byzantium. Their trade networks, such as the "Highway of Slaves" along the Volga River, connected Scandinavia to the Islamic world and the Byzantine Empire, facilitating a vast exchange of goods and ideas.

### End of the Viking Age
The Viking Age concluded with the consolidation of Scandinavian kingdoms (Denmark, Norway, Sweden) and their assimilation into Christian Europe. The establishment of centralized monarchies, the spread of Christianity, and the decline of the slave trade reduced the incentive for raiding. The Norman conquest of England in 1066 is often cited as the symbolic end, as the Normans, themselves of Viking descent, became integrated into the broader European feudal system.

## Terms
*   **Longship:** A fast, flexible, and shallow-draught ship used for warfare, exploration, and trade, central to Viking mobility.
*   **Knarr:** A broader, deeper-hulled merchant vessel designed for carrying cargo over long distances.
*   **Runes:** The characters of the Old Norse alphabet, used for inscriptions on stones, wood, and metal.
*   **Varangians:** The name given to Vikings, particularly Swedes, who traveled east along Russian rivers to trade and serve as mercenaries.
*   **Thrall:** A slave in Viking society, forming the lowest social class.
*   **Karls:** Free peasants and landowners in Viking society.
*   **Jarls:** The aristocratic class, comprising wealthy landowners and leaders.
*   **Thing:** An assembly of free men in Norse society that served as a legislative and judicial body.
*   **Skaldic poetry:** A complex form of Old Norse poetry composed by court poets, often praising rulers or recounting historical events.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Motives for Expansion:** Scholars debate the primary drivers of Viking expansion, citing theories ranging from population pressure and lack of resources to political ambition, trade opportunities, and a reaction to Christian aggression under Charlemagne.
*   **The Role of Women:** While legal codes suggest Viking women had significant rights (e.g., to divorce and inheritance), the extent of their social and political power, and the reality behind the "shieldmaiden" legend, remains debated. Archaeological finds like the Birka female warrior burial continue to fuel discussion.
*   **The "Viking" Identity:** The term "Viking" may have originally described an activity (raiding) rather than a fixed ethnic group. Genetic studies suggest Viking bands were often diverse, with members from various regions joining for expeditions.
*   **Jomsborg:** The existence and exact location of the semi-legendary Viking stronghold of Jomsborg remain unconfirmed by archaeology.

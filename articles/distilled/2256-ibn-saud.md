# Ibn Saud

## Overview
Abdulaziz bin Abdul Rahman Al Saud (Ibn Saud) was the founder and first King of Saudi Arabia, reigning from 1932 until his death in 1953. He is a pivotal figure who unified much of the Arabian Peninsula through conquest and diplomacy, establishing the modern Saudi state. His reign saw the discovery of petroleum, which transformed the region's economy and global significance.

- **1938** — Petroleum is discovered in Saudi Arabia.

## Body

### Early Life and Rise to Power
Born into the Al Saud family, a power in central Arabia, Ibn Saud was exiled with his family in 1891 after their defeat by the Al Rashid. After a decade in Kuwait, he led a raid to recapture Riyadh in 1902, marking the start of his three-decade conquest. He consolidated control over Najd by 1921 and, with the support of the Ikhwan—a military-religious brotherhood he founded—conquered the Hejaz in 1925. His victory was bolstered by his support for Islamic revivalists, which strengthened pan-Islamism.

### Consolidation and Conflict
Ibn Saud's expansion was facilitated by strategic alliances, notably with the British. The 1915 Treaty of Darin made his lands a British protectorate, providing him with arms and a monthly stipend. After World War I, he defeated the Al Rashidi and negotiated the 1922 Treaty of Uqair, which recognized his territorial gains. However, his alliance with the Ikhwan collapsed when he forbade further raids into British-protected territories. The Ikhwan rebellion was suppressed by 1929, allowing Ibn Saud to enforce his Wahhabi-inspired ideology, which included reforms to traditional Hajj practices.

### Unification and Oil
On 23 September 1932, Ibn Saud formally united his domains into the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia. His reign was transformed by the 1938 discovery of petroleum, for which he had granted a concession in 1933. He later granted substantial authority over oil fields to American companies. This new wealth allowed him to enforce social order, settle nomadic tribes, and invest in infrastructure, such as a railway built with oil royalties. He maintained Saudi neutrality in World War II but favored the Allies, meeting with U.S. President Franklin D. Roosevelt in 1945 to establish a foundation for future relations.

### Foreign Policy and Later Years
Ibn Saud secured his frontiers through diplomacy and limited conflict, such as the 1934 Saudi-Yemeni War, which resulted in the Treaty of Taif. He suppressed unauthorized Ikhwan raids into British mandates and signed frontier agreements. In his later years, he delegated duties to his son, Crown Prince Saud, while favoring another son, Faisal, as a more capable successor. He died in 1953, having fathered 45 sons, all subsequent Saudi kings descending from him.

## Terms
- **Ikhwan**: A military-religious brotherhood founded by Ibn Saud to assist in his conquests, later suppressed after rebelling against his authority.
- **Wahhabism**: A conservative Islamic doctrine followed by the Al Saud, emphasizing a return to what its adherents believe are the original practices of Islam.
- **Hajj**: The annual Islamic pilgrimage to Mecca, which Ibn Saud's religious reforms sought to regulate.
- **Zakat**: An obligatory form of almsgiving in Islam, which Ibn Saud decreed the collection of after conquering the Hejaz.
- **Bayaa**: An oath of allegiance, as sworn to Ibn Saud in the Great Mosque of Mecca in 1926.
- **Qaimmaqam**: A district administrator; Ibn Saud was named qaimmaqam of southern Nejd by the Ottomans in 1905.
- **Shura Council**: An advisory council; Ibn Saud established one for the Hejaz in 1927.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The source notes Ibn Saud's "highly ambivalent opinion of the Jews," where he expressed both antisemitic tropes and, in some instances, considered non-Zionist Jews as "good friends of the Arabs."
- His views on women's education are presented as encouraging, based on a conversation where he stated, "It is permissible for women to read."
- The succession was a point of tension; Ibn Saud favored his son Faisal's capabilities but kept Saud as crown prince for stability, leading to rivalry between the brothers.

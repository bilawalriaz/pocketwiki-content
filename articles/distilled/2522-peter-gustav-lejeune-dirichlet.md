# Peter Gustav Lejeune Dirichlet

## Overview
Johann Peter Gustav Lejeune Dirichlet (1805–1859) was a German mathematician who made foundational contributions to number theory, analysis, and mathematical physics. He is credited with creating analytic number theory and giving the modern formal definition of a function, fundamentally shaping the direction of 19th-century mathematics.

## Timeline
- **13 February 1805** — Born in Düren, First French Empire (now Germany).
- **1822–1826** — Studies mathematics in Paris, proving the case n=5 for Fermat's Last Theorem.
- **1825** — Lectures on his Fermat proof at the French Academy of Sciences.
- **1827** — Awarded an honorary doctorate from the University of Bonn.
- **1831** — Becomes a permanent member of the faculty at the University of Berlin.
- **1832** — Marries Rebecka Mendelssohn; becomes the youngest member of the Prussian Academy of Sciences.
- **1837** — Publishes his theorem on arithmetic progressions, founding analytic number theory.
- **1855** — Succeeds Carl Friedrich Gauss as professor at the University of Göttingen.
- **5 May 1859** — Dies in Göttingen, several months after his wife.

## Body

### Early Life and Studies
Dirichlet was born in Düren and showed a strong interest in mathematics before age 12. Despite his family's modest means, they supported his education. He studied at gymnasia in Bonn and Cologne but left without the Abitur due to poor Latin. In 1822, he moved to Paris to study higher mathematics, as opportunities in Germany were limited. There, he privately studied Gauss's *Disquisitiones Arithmeticae* and worked as a tutor. His first major result, a partial proof of Fermat's Last Theorem for the case n=5, brought him immediate fame and led to connections with mathematicians like Fourier and Poisson.

### Career in Berlin
Returning to Prussia in 1825, Dirichlet secured a teaching position at the University of Breslau with the help of Alexander von Humboldt and Gauss. He earned an honorary doctorate and habilitation despite his lack of formal credentials. In 1831, he moved to Berlin, where he taught at the University and the Military Academy. He became a member of the Prussian Academy of Sciences in 1832. His marriage to Rebecka Mendelssohn connected him to a prominent intellectual family. In Berlin, he advised future mathematicians like Kronecker and Lipschitz and collaborated closely with Carl Jacobi. His liberal political views caused difficulties after the failed 1848 revolution.

### Later Life in Göttingen
In 1855, following Gauss's death, Dirichlet accepted a professorship at the University of Göttingen. The lighter teaching load allowed more time for research and interaction with younger mathematicians like Richard Dedekind and Bernhard Riemann. Dedekind later edited and published Dirichlet's lectures on number theory. Dirichlet died in Göttingen in 1859, a few months after his wife's death.

### Mathematical Research
**Number Theory:** Dirichlet's main field, where he introduced fundamental tools. In 1837, he proved his theorem on arithmetic progressions using analysis, creating analytic number theory. For this, he introduced Dirichlet characters and L-functions. He also proved the first class number formula for quadratic forms and the Dirichlet unit theorem. He first used the pigeonhole principle in his proof of Dirichlet's approximation theorem and contributed to Fermat's Last Theorem (cases n=5, n=14) and the biquadratic reciprocity law.

**Analysis:** In 1829, he provided the first rigorous conditions for the convergence of Fourier series, correcting errors by Cauchy and introducing Dirichlet's test, the Dirichlet kernel, and the Dirichlet integral. He also studied the Dirichlet problem for the Laplace equation, proving solution uniqueness via the Dirichlet principle (minimizing Dirichlet energy).

**Concept of Function:** While analyzing Fourier series convergence, Dirichlet defined a function as a rule assigning a single finite y to any x. This is credited as the modern definition, replacing the older view of a function as an analytic formula.

**Other Fields:** He worked in mathematical physics on potential theory, heat diffusion, and hydrodynamics. He also contributed to probability theory, improving on Laplace's central limit theorem approximations.

## Terms
- **Analytic Number Theory:** A branch of number theory that uses methods from mathematical analysis to solve problems about integers.
- **Dirichlet Characters:** Multiplicative functions used in number theory to study properties of integers in arithmetic progressions.
- **L-functions:** Complex functions associated with Dirichlet characters, central to analytic number theory.
- **Pigeonhole Principle:** A basic counting argument stating that if more items are placed into fewer containers, at least one container must hold more than one item.
- **Dirichlet Problem:** The problem of finding a function that solves a specified partial differential equation (like Laplace's equation) in the interior of a given region, taking prescribed values on the boundary.
- **Dirichlet Principle:** The method of finding a solution to a boundary-value problem by minimizing a certain energy integral (Dirichlet energy).
- **Fourier Series:** A way to represent a function as a sum of simple sine and cosine waves.
- **Habilitation:** A post-doctoral qualification in some European academic systems required to teach independently at a university.

## Debates and Open Questions
The attribution of the modern concept of a function to Dirichlet is debated. While he is commonly credited, mathematician Imre Lakatos argued that Dirichlet had "no idea of this concept," citing his discussion of piecewise continuous functions where he noted a function could have two values at points of discontinuity. The Dirichlet divisor problem, concerning the error term in the asymptotic formula for the divisor function, remains an unsolved problem in number theory.

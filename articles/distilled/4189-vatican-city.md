# Vatican City

## Overview
Vatican City is the world's smallest independent state, an ecclesiastical monarchy enclaved within Rome, Italy. It serves as the temporal seat of the Holy See, the central governing body of the Catholic Church, and is a unique sovereign entity whose existence is defined by its religious and diplomatic functions rather than traditional nationhood.

## Timeline
- **326** — Constantinian Basilica of St. Peter built over the believed tomb of Saint Peter.
- **756–1870** — The Papal States, a large territory in central Italy, are ruled by the popes.
- **1309–1377** — The Avignon Papacy; popes reside in France.
- **1870** — Rome is annexed by the Kingdom of Italy; the "Roman Question" begins.
- **11 Feb 1929** — The Lateran Treaty is signed, establishing the independent Vatican City State.
- **1939–1941** — Systematic excavation of the ancient necropolis under St. Peter's Basilica.
- **1943–1944** — German occupation of Rome; Vatican City maintains neutrality during WWII.
- **1970** — Pope Paul VI disbands the Pontifical Military Corps, except the Swiss Guard.
- **1984** — A new concordat modifies the Holy See's relationship with Italy.
- **1984** — Vatican City is added to the UNESCO World Heritage Sites list.
- **8 May 2025** — Pope Leo XIV (Robert Francis Prevost) is elected.

## Body

### History and Establishment
The area known as Vatican Hill was historically a marshy, ominous district outside ancient Rome. Its modern significance began with the construction of the Constantinian basilica over St. Peter's tomb in the 4th century. For centuries, popes held secular power as rulers of the Papal States, but their primary residence was often the Lateran Palace. After the Avignon Papacy, popes returned to Rome and eventually settled at the Vatican. The modern state was created by the 1929 Lateran Treaty, which resolved the "Roman Question"—the dispute over the pope's temporal power after Italy seized Rome in 1870. The treaty established Vatican City's independence and reaffirmed Catholicism's special status in Italy.

### Governance and Politics
Vatican City is a unitary absolute elective monarchy. The pope is the sovereign, holding supreme legislative, executive, and judicial power. He is supported by the Pontifical Commission for Vatican City State, a body of cardinals, and the Governorate, which handles daily administration. The Holy See, a distinct sovereign entity under international law, conducts diplomacy and foreign relations on the state's behalf. Vatican City is not a UN member but holds permanent observer status. Its legal system is separate from Italy's, though Italy agrees to prosecute crimes committed within Vatican territory upon request.

### Territory and Security
The state covers 44 hectares, enclosed by historic walls. Its territory includes St. Peter's Basilica, the Apostolic Palace, the Sistine Chapel, and the Vatican Gardens, which account for about half the area. The Swiss Guard, founded in 1506, is the pope's personal security force. The Gendarmerie Corps provides police and security services. Italy is responsible for the state's military defense, as Vatican City is a neutral state with no armed forces of its own. The entire territory is a UNESCO World Heritage Site and is protected under the Hague Convention.

### Economy and Demographics
The economy is supported by donations, museum fees, stamp and souvenir sales, and publications. It uses the euro via a special agreement with the EU. The Vatican Bank (IOR) conducts global financial activities. As of 2024, the population is 882 residents, composed of clergy, religious members, and lay employees. Citizenship is granted based on appointment to work for the Holy See (*jus officii*), not by birth or territory. Italian is the primary working language.

### Culture and Science
Vatican City houses immense cultural heritage, including Renaissance art by Michelangelo, Raphael, and others. The Vatican Apostolic Library and Museums are of global importance. Scientific research is conducted by the Pontifical Academy of Sciences and the Vatican Observatory, which operates telescopes in Arizona. The state also fields its own national football team and athletics association.

## Terms
- **Holy See**: The central governing body of the Catholic Church, a sovereign entity under international law, distinct from Vatican City State.
- **Lateran Treaty**: The 1929 agreement between the Holy See and Italy that established Vatican City's independence.
- **Roman Question**: The political dispute (1870–1929) over the temporal power of the pope after the loss of the Papal States.
- **Pontifical Commission for Vatican City State**: The cardinal-led body that exercises legislative authority in the pope's name for the state.
- **Swiss Guard**: The military corps responsible for the personal security of the pope, founded in 1506.
- **Jus officii**: The principle by which Vatican citizenship is granted based on appointment to a position serving the Holy See.
- **Sede vacante**: The period of a papal vacancy, during which the Cardinal Camerlengo administers temporal powers.
- **Extraterritorial properties**: Holy See properties in Italy (e.g., major basilicas, Castel Gandolfo) that enjoy special legal status similar to foreign embassies.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The **"Roman Question"** was formally resolved by the Lateran Treaty, but the relationship and financial agreements between the Holy See and Italy continue to be modified, as seen in the 1984 concordat.
- The Vatican's **financial transparency** has been a subject of international scrutiny, leading to reforms under Pope Francis, including the establishment of a Secretariat for the Economy and audits of its accounts.
- The **legal and moral status** of the Holy See's diplomatic neutrality, especially during historical events like World War II, remains a topic of scholarly analysis and debate.

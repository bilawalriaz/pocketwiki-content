# Gibraltar

## Overview
Gibraltar is a British Overseas Territory at the southern tip of the Iberian Peninsula, controlling the narrow Strait of Gibraltar where the Mediterranean Sea meets the Atlantic Ocean. Its strategic location has made it a point of contention between European powers for centuries, most notably between Spain and Britain. Today, it is a densely populated, self-governing territory with a unique multicultural identity, whose sovereignty remains a key issue in Anglo-Spanish relations.

## Timeline
- **c. 50,000 years ago** — Neanderthal habitation evidenced at Gorham's Cave.
- **711** — Tariq ibn Ziyad's Berber army lands near the Rock, beginning the Islamic conquest of Iberia.
- **1160** — Almohads build a permanent settlement and castle, naming it Medinat al-Fath.
- **1462** — Gibraltar is captured by Castile from the Emirate of Granada.
- **1704** — Anglo-Dutch forces capture Gibraltar during the War of the Spanish Succession.
- **1713** — Treaty of Utrecht cedes Gibraltar to Great Britain in perpetuity.
- **1779-1783** — The Great Siege of Gibraltar by Spanish and French forces is unsuccessful.
- **1967** — Gibraltarians vote overwhelmingly to remain under British sovereignty in a referendum.
- **2002** — Gibraltarians reject a proposal for shared sovereignty with Spain in a referendum.
- **2020** — The UK and Gibraltar leave the European Union.
- **2026** — A treaty is signed to remove border controls with Spain and allow Gibraltar's participation in the Schengen Area.

## Body

### History and Sovereignty
Gibraltar's recorded history begins with Neanderthal habitation. It was later known to the ancients as Mons Calpe, one of the Pillars of Hercules. The modern name derives from the Arabic *Jabal Ṭāriq* ("Mount of Tariq"), after the 8th-century Berber leader. A permanent settlement was established by the Almohads in 1160. Control shifted between Nasrid, Castilian, and Marinid powers before its capture by Castile in 1462. In 1704, Anglo-Dutch forces seized it during the War of the Spanish Succession, and it was ceded to Britain by the 1713 Treaty of Utrecht. Spain has never relinquished its claim. Gibraltarians have twice rejected Spanish sovereignty in referendums (1967 and 2002). Following Brexit, a 2026 treaty established an open border with Spain and Gibraltar's participation in the Schengen Area, while reaffirming British sovereignty.

### Governance and International Relations
Gibraltar is a British Overseas Territory with almost complete internal self-governance. Its head of state is the British monarch, represented by a Governor. The UK government is responsible for defence and foreign policy. All local political parties oppose any transfer of sovereignty to Spain. Gibraltar was part of the EU as a dependent territory but left with the UK in 2020. Its post-Brexit relationship with the EU is governed by a 2026 treaty that removes routine border controls with Spain and enables participation in the Schengen Area and EU Customs Union, though sovereignty remains British.

### Geography and Climate
Gibraltar covers 6.8 km², dominated by the 426 m-high Rock of Gibraltar, a Jurassic limestone formation. It has a Mediterranean climate with mild, rainy winters and warm, dry summers. The territory has negligible natural resources and relies on desalination for its water supply. The Upper Rock Nature Reserve is home to around 230 Barbary macaques, the only wild monkeys in Europe.

### Economy and Demographics
Gibraltar's economy is based on financial services, online gambling, maritime services, and tourism. It has an extremely low unemployment rate, but over half its labour force are frontier workers resident in Spain. The population of about 34,000 is one of the world's most densely populated. The official language is English, but most residents are bilingual in Spanish and speak a unique vernacular called Llanito. The population is predominantly Roman Catholic.

## Terms
- **British Overseas Territory**: A territory under the jurisdiction and sovereignty of the United Kingdom but not part of it.
- **Strait of Gibraltar**: The narrow waterway separating the Iberian Peninsula from Africa, connecting the Atlantic Ocean and the Mediterranean Sea.
- **Treaty of Utrecht (1713)**: The treaty that ended the War of the Spanish Succession and ceded Gibraltar to Great Britain.
- **Sovereignty referendum**: A public vote on the political status of a territory. Gibraltar held key referendums in 1967 and 2002.
- **Schengen Area**: An area of European countries that have abolished passport and other types of border control at their mutual borders.
- **Llanito**: A unique vernacular language of Gibraltar based on Andalusian Spanish with strong British English influences.
- **Barbary macaque**: An endangered species of monkey native to North Africa and the only wild primate population in Europe, found on the Rock of Gibraltar.
- **Frontier workers**: Employees who live in one country (e.g., Spain) but work in another (e.g., Gibraltar).

## Debates and Open questions
The primary ongoing debate is the sovereignty of Gibraltar. Spain maintains a claim to the territory, while the UK and the overwhelming majority of Gibraltarians insist on its right to self-determination under British sovereignty. The practical implications of this dispute are seen in negotiations over border controls and the terms of Gibraltar's relationship with the EU post-Brexit. The 2026 treaty attempts to resolve border issues but explicitly states that British sovereignty is unaffected, leaving the core political dispute unresolved.

# Agra

## Overview
Agra is a historic city in Uttar Pradesh, India, situated on the Yamuna River. It rose to global prominence as the capital of the Mughal Empire during its golden age in the 16th and 17th centuries, a period that produced iconic architectural masterpieces like the Taj Mahal. Today, it is a major industrial and tourist hub, forming a key part of India's Golden Triangle circuit.

## Timeline
- **1504–1505** — Sultan Sikandar Lodi rebuilds Agra and makes it the seat of government.
- **1526** — Mughal Emperor Babur defeats the Lodis at the First Battle of Panipat and acquires Agra.
- **1558** — Emperor Akbar founds the modern city on the right bank of the Yamuna.
- **1565** — Akbar commissions the construction of the Agra Fort.
- **1632–1648** — Shah Jahan builds the Taj Mahal in memory of his wife Mumtaz Mahal.
- **1648** — Shah Jahan shifts the capital to Delhi.
- **1761** — The Jats of Bharatpur conquer Agra after a month-long siege.
- **1785** — The Marathas conquer Agra.
- **1803** — The British East India Company takes control of Agra.
- **1857** — Agra is a centre of the Indian Rebellion of 1857.
- **1983** — The Taj Mahal and Agra Fort are designated UNESCO World Heritage Sites.
- **2024** — The first section of the Agra Metro begins operations.

## Body

### Pre-Mughal and Mughal Eras
Agra's recorded history begins with its rebuilding by Sultan Sikandar Lodi in 1504–1505. The city's golden age commenced with the Mughal Empire. After Babur's victory in 1526, Agra became the empire's foremost city and capital under emperors Babur, Humayun, Akbar, Jahangir, and Shah Jahan. Akbar transformed it into a major political, cultural, and economic centre, building the Agra Fort and the city itself on the river's right bank. Shah Jahan later raised its architectural glory with the Taj Mahal. The capital was shifted to Delhi in 1648, but Agra remained the empire's second capital.

### Post-Mughal Decline and British Rule
Following the Mughal decline, Agra's control passed through several hands in the late 18th century, including the Jats, Marathas, and finally the British East India Company in 1803. It served as the capital of the North-Western Province from 1836 to 1858. The city was a centre of the 1857 Indian Rebellion, after which its administrative importance waned under the British Raj, though it developed as a railway and commercial centre.

### Post-Independence and Modern Agra
After Indian independence in 1947, Agra developed into an industrial city with a significant tourism industry. The Taj Mahal and Agra Fort became UNESCO World Heritage Sites in 1983. The city faces environmental challenges, notably severe air pollution and pollution of the Yamuna River, which threaten its monuments. It is part of the Golden Triangle tourist circuit with Delhi and Jaipur.

### Monuments and Architecture
Agra's most famous monument is the Taj Mahal, a UNESCO World Heritage Site and one of the New Seven Wonders of the World. Built by Shah Jahan between 1632 and 1648, it is a masterpiece of Mughal architecture blending Indian, Persian, and Islamic styles. The Agra Fort, a massive red sandstone fortress commissioned by Akbar in 1565, served as a royal residence and military base. Other significant Mughal-era structures include the tomb of I'timād-ud-Daulah (the "Baby Taj") and Akbar's Tomb at Sikandra.

### Economy and Demographics
Agra's economy relies on tourism, footwear and leather manufacturing, and agriculture. With a population of roughly 1.6 million, it is the fourth-most populous city in Uttar Pradesh. Hinduism (80.68%) and Islam (15.37%) are the predominant religions. The city is a major railway junction and is connected by expressways to Delhi and Lucknow. The Agra Metro, with its first section operational in 2024, is under construction.

## Terms
- **Mughal Empire:** A major Islamic empire in the Indian subcontinent, founded in 1526, under which Agra reached its peak.
- **Pietra Dura:** An inlay technique using cut and fitted, highly polished colored stones to create images, used extensively in the Taj Mahal.
- **Charbagh:** A Persian-style garden layout divided into four quadrants, symbolizing paradise, used in Mughal architecture.
- **UNESCO World Heritage Site:** A landmark or area with legal protection by an international convention administered by the United Nations.
- **Golden Triangle:** A popular tourist circuit in India connecting Delhi, Agra, and Jaipur.
- **Taj Trapezium Zone:** A 10,400 km² area around the Taj Mahal with strict pollution controls, established by a 1996 Supreme Court ruling.
- **Lok Sabha:** The lower house of India's bicameral Parliament.
- **Vidhan Sabha:** The lower house of a state legislature in India.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The exact etymology of the name "Agra" is debated, with proposals including the Hindi word for "salt pan" and a Sanskrit term related to Krishna's forests.
- The identity of the chief architect of the Taj Mahal is not definitively established, though the Persian architect Ustad Ahmad Lahori is probably the candidate.

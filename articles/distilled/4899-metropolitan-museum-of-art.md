# Metropolitan Museum of Art

## Overview
The Metropolitan Museum of Art (the Met) is an encyclopedic art museum in New York City, the largest in the Americas and fourth-largest globally by floor area. Founded in 1870, its mission is to inspire and educate through a permanent collection spanning 5,000 years of global art.

## Timeline
- **1870** — Incorporated by New York State Legislature.
- **1872** — Opens to the public at 681 Fifth Avenue.
- **1880** — Moves into first portion of current Central Park building.
- **1938** — The Cloisters, dedicated to medieval art, opens in Fort Tryon Park.
- **1969** — Nelson Rockefeller donates 3,000-piece collection, creating the Michael C. Rockefeller Wing.
- **1975** — The Robert Lehman Collection, a "museum within a museum," opens.
- **1978** — The Temple of Dendur is installed in a new wing after being gifted by Egypt.
- **2018** — Ends century-old free admission for out-of-state visitors, instituting a $25 fee. |
- **2020** — Closes for over five months due to the COVID-19 pandemic.
- **2021** — Receives a $125 million donation, the largest in its history, from Oscar L. Tang and Agnes Hsu-Tang. |
- **2026** — Announces merger with Neue Galerie New York, set for completion in 2028.

## Body

### Founding and Mission
Founded in 1870 by American philanthropists, artists, and businessmen, the Met was created as a national institution for public art education. Its founding legislation required collections be open to the public free of charge, a policy maintained until 2018. First director Luigi Palma di Cesnola oversaw early growth, including the purchase of the Cesnola Collection of Cypriot antiquities.

### The Collection
The Met's permanent collection, curated by 17 departments, contains approximately 1.5 million works. Major holdings include:
*   **Ancient World:** Egyptian art (over 26,000 pieces, including the Temple of Dendur), Greek and Roman art (over 17,000 objects), and Ancient Near Eastern art (over 7,000 pieces).
*   **Asian Art:** Over 35,000 pieces, with strength in Chinese, Japanese, and Indian art.
*   **European Art:** European paintings (over 2,600 works), sculpture and decorative arts (over 50,000 pieces), and medieval art (over 10,000 objects, split between the main building and The Cloisters).
*   **Americas and Africa:** The Michael C. Rockefeller Wing houses over 11,000 works from Africa, Oceania, and the Americas. The American Wing displays art from the 18th to early 20th centuries.
*   **Specialized Departments:** Notable collections include Arms and Armor, the Costume Institute (over 35,000 costumes and accessories), Drawings and Prints (1.2 million prints), and Musical Instruments (about 5,000 examples).

### Architecture and Sites
The main Fifth Avenue building, opened in 1880, is an accretion of over 20 structures totaling more than 2 million square feet. The Cloisters, a separate Fort Tryon Park building, is dedicated to medieval European art and incorporates salvaged architectural elements from five medieval cloisters. A third site, the Neue Galerie, will become the Met's newest location in 2028.

### Governance and Finances
The museum is governed by a board of trustees and is a private corporation, though the City of New York owns the building and provides utilities. Its endowment was $3.3 billion as of 2021. Following financial setbacks in 2015-2018, the museum changed its admission policy in 2018, charging $25 to out-of-state and foreign visitors while maintaining a pay-what-you-wish policy for New York State residents.

### Controversies and Repatriation
The Met has faced criticism and legal challenges regarding collection provenance. It has been subject to lawsuits from Italy and Turkey, resulting in the repatriation of hundreds of looted antiquities. A 2021 ProPublica report highlighted issues with the Diker Collection of Native American art, noting potential violations of the Native American Graves Protection and Repatriation Act (NAGPRA). In response, the museum updated its collecting practices and hired a provenance research team. In 2025, it returned a 2,700-year-old bronze griffin head to Greece.

## Terms
*   **Encyclopedic Museum:** A museum whose collections cover a wide range of cultures, time periods, and art types.
*   **Deaccessioning:** The process by which a museum permanently removes an object from its collection, often to sell it to fund new acquisitions or collection care.
*   **Provenance:** The documented history of an object's ownership, from its creation to its present location.
*   **NAGPRA:** The Native American Graves Protection and Repatriation Act, a 1990 U.S. law requiring institutions receiving federal funding to repatriate Native American human remains, funerary objects, and sacred items.
*   **Cloister:** A covered walk in a convent, monastery, college, or cathedral, typically with a wall on one side and a colonnade open to a quadrangle on the other. The Met's medieval branch incorporates five such structures.
*   **Period Room:** A gallery installation that recreates the interior of a room from a specific historical period and location.
*   **Open Access:** A policy allowing the public to freely use, share, and build upon copyright-free digital images of artworks in the museum's collection.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Admission Policy:** The 2018 shift from free admission to a mandatory fee for most visitors sparked debate about balancing public access with financial sustainability.
*   **Repatriation Ethics:** Ongoing discussions center on the museum's responsibility for objects with contested or incomplete provenance, particularly looted antiquities and Indigenous cultural items.
*   **Deaccessioning Practices:** The museum's history of selling collection items to fund acquisitions, especially during the tenure of director Thomas Hoving, remains a point of ethical and professional debate.
*   **Indigenous Art and NAGPRA:** The handling of the Diker Collection has raised questions about compliance with NAGPRA and the ethical display of funerary and sacred objects.

# Zanzibar

## Overview
Zanzibar is a semi-autonomous region of Tanzania, consisting of several islands in the Indian Ocean, most notably Unguja and Pemba. Historically an independent sultanate and a major hub for the spice and slave trades, it is now a key tourist destination known for its historic Stone Town and unique wildlife. Its political status as an autonomous region within Tanzania, combined with its economic reliance on tourism and spices, makes it a distinct and significant part of the country.

## Timeline
- **c. 1000 AD** — Bantu-speaking ancestors of the Hadimu and Tumbatu arrive from the mainland.
- **1498** — Vasco da Gama's visit marks the beginning of European influence.
- **1503/1504** — Zanzibar becomes part of the Portuguese Empire.
- **1698** — Zanzibar comes under the influence of the Sultanate of Oman.
- **1832/1840** — Sultan Said bin Sultan moves his capital from Muscat to Stone Town.
- **1856** — The Sultanate of Zanzibar is established as a separate entity from Oman.
- **1890** — The Heligoland–Zanzibar Treaty makes Zanzibar a British protectorate.
- **27 August 1896** — The Anglo-Zanzibar War, the shortest war in history, occurs.
- **10 December 1963** — The British protectorate ends; Zanzibar becomes an independent constitutional monarchy.
- **12 January 1964** — The Zanzibar Revolution overthrows the Sultanate.
- **April 1964** — Zanzibar merges with Tanganyika to form the United Republic of Tanzania.

## Body

### History and Political Status
Zanzibar has been inhabited for at least 20,000 years. By the 10th century, it was a central Swahili trading town, with local elites acting as brokers between African hinterland and Indian Ocean traders. Portuguese influence began in 1498, but they maintained a limited presence until being expelled with Omani assistance in 1698. The Omani Sultanate later made Zanzibar its capital, developing a clove-based economy heavily reliant on the slave trade. British influence grew through anti-slavery treaties, culminating in a protectorate in 1890. Zanzibar gained independence in 1963, but a revolution a month later led to its merger with Tanganyika in 1964. Today, it is an autonomous region with its own government, though the extent of its sovereignty is debated.

### Economy
Zanzibar's economy is based on spices, tourism, and fishing. The clove industry, introduced by Omani sultans, was once dominant but has declined significantly since the 1970s due to global market changes and past socialist policies. Tourism has grown rapidly, from 19,000 visitors in 1985 to 917,000 in 2025, becoming a major source of foreign currency. The islands also export seaweed and raffia. Energy is primarily supplied via submarine cables from the mainland, though supply has been unreliable. There is potential for oil discovery near Pemba Island, but its exploitation is a point of contention between the Zanzibar and Tanzanian governments.

### Demographics and Culture
The population was 1,889,773 in the 2022 census. The people are ethnically diverse, primarily Swahili, with Arab, Persian, and Indian minorities. Swahili (specifically the Kiunguja dialect) is the official language, with Arabic also spoken. The population is almost entirely Muslim. Zanzibar's culture is reflected in its architecture, particularly in the UNESCO World Heritage site of Stone Town, and its music, especially taarab. The Zanzibar International Film Festival is a major annual event. The islands are home to endemic species like the Zanzibar red colobus and face environmental pressures from tourism and climate change.

## Terms
- **Sultanate:** A state ruled by a sultan, a monarch title used in some Muslim countries.
- **Protectorate:** A state that is controlled and protected by another.
- **Swahili:** A Bantu language and culture that developed along the East African coast through interaction between Bantu peoples and traders from the Persian Gulf, Arabia, and India.
- **Clove:** An aromatic flower bud, originally from the Moluccas, that became Zanzibar's primary cash crop under Omani rule.
- **Stone Town:** The historic core of Zanzibar City, a UNESCO World Heritage Site known for its Swahili architecture.
- **Zanzibar Revolution:** The 1964 uprising that overthrew the Sultanate and led to the merger with Tanganyika.
- **Autonomous Region:** A territory that has a degree of self-governance within a larger state.
- **Daladala:** Privately owned minibuses that serve as the primary public transportation in Zanzibar.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Sovereignty:** There is ongoing disagreement between the Zanzibar government and the Tanzanian union government regarding the extent of Zanzibar's autonomy and sovereignty.
- **Political Tensions:** The political landscape has been marked by recurring violence and contested elections between the ruling Chama Cha Mapinduzi (CCM) and opposition parties like the Civic United Front (CUF) and ACT-Wazalendo.
- **Environmental Impact:** The rapid growth of tourism and fishing industries, combined with climate change threats like sea-level rise, poses significant environmental challenges to the islands' ecosystems.

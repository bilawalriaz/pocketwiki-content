# Pope John XXIII

## Overview
Pope John XXIII (1881–1963) was the head of the Catholic Church from 1958 until his death. Though elected as a likely short-term "caretaker" pope, he surprised the world by convening the historic Second Vatican Council, which modernized the Church's liturgy and its engagement with the modern world. He is remembered for his pastoral warmth, his efforts to improve relations with Jews and Eastern Orthodox churches, and his advocacy for peace during the Cold War.

## Body

### Early Life and Priesthood
Angelo Giuseppe Roncalli was born into a family of sharecroppers in Lombardy, Italy. He was ordained a priest in 1904 after completing a doctorate in theology. His early career included service as a secretary to the Bishop of Bergamo and a stint as a stretcher-bearer in the Italian Army during World War I. In 1921, he was appointed Italian president of the Society for the Propagation of the Faith.

### Diplomatic and Episcopal Service
From 1925, Roncalli served in a series of diplomatic posts for the Vatican. As Apostolic Visitor (later Delegate) in Bulgaria, Turkey, and Greece, he worked to build relationships in predominantly non-Catholic societies. During World War II, he used his diplomatic position to help save thousands of Jewish refugees from the Nazis through actions such as delivering immigration certificates and arranging "baptisms of convenience." He was appointed Apostolic Nuncio to France in 1944. In 1953, he was made a cardinal and Patriarch of Venice.

### Election and Papacy
At age 76, Roncalli was unexpectedly elected pope in 1958 after a long conclave. He chose the name John, unused for over 500 years, to honor his father and the humble parish of his baptism. As pope, he broke with tradition by making pastoral visits around Rome, including to a prison and a children's hospital. His "Ostpolitik" policy engaged in dialogue with Communist Eastern Europe, and he sought to improve relations with the Russian Orthodox Church. In international affairs, he intervened during the 1962 Cuban Missile Crisis to promote peace.

### The Second Vatican Council
John XXIII's defining act was calling the Second Vatican Council (Vatican II) in 1959, the first ecumenical council in nearly a century. He opened its first session on 11 October 1962 with the *Gaudet Mater Ecclesia* address, which set a tone of pastoral renewal rather than condemnation. The council, completed after his death, led to major reforms including a revised liturgy, a new approach to the modern world, and a stronger emphasis on ecumenism.

### Doctrinal and Social Teachings
In doctrinal matters, John XXIII was a traditionalist but emphasized the Church's pastoral role. He issued the encyclical *Pacem in terris* (1963), a landmark document on human rights and peace addressed to "all people of good will." He advocated for the rights of the unborn, the elderly, and the poor. He also established a commission to study questions of birth control. In 1960, he removed the word "perfidious" from the Good Friday prayer for Jews, a significant gesture of reconciliation.

### Death and Sainthood
Diagnosed with stomach cancer in 1962, John XXIII died on 3 June 1963. His cause for canonization was opened in 1965. He was beatified in 2000 and canonized by Pope Francis in 2014, bypassing the traditional requirement for a second miracle due to his virtuous life and his role in convening Vatican II. His feast day is celebrated on 11 October, the anniversary of the council's opening.

## Terms
*   **Apostolic Nuncio:** A permanent diplomatic representative of the Holy See to a country or international organization.
*   **Ostpolitik:** A policy of engagement and dialogue with Communist countries in Eastern Europe, pursued by John XXIII to ease tensions.
*   **Second Vatican Council (Vatican II):** An ecumenical council of the Catholic Church held from 1962 to 1965, which enacted significant liturgical and doctrinal reforms.
*   **Ecumenism:** The principle or aim of promoting unity among the world's Christian churches.
*   **Pacem in terris:** An encyclical written by John XXIII in 1963, addressing the need for peace and human rights in the modern world.
*   **Canonization:** The official declaration by the Pope that a deceased member of the faithful is a saint and worthy of universal veneration.
*   **In pectore:** A Latin term meaning "in the breast," referring to a cardinal appointed by the Pope whose name is kept secret.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **The "Roncalli Dossier":** The International Raoul Wallenberg Foundation has submitted extensive research to Yad Vashem advocating for John XXIII to be recognized as "Righteous Among the Nations" for his efforts to save Jews during the Holocaust, a title he has not yet received.
*   **Relationship with Padre Pio:** Historical accounts differ on John XXIII's attitude toward Padre Pio. Some sources indicate skepticism and criticism, while others suggest his views were generally positive but influenced by negative information, and that he later recognized Padre Pio's holiness.
*   **Crimen sollicitationis:** A 1962 document bearing John XXIII's seal instructed bishops to handle cases of clerical sexual abuse in strict secrecy, a practice that has been heavily criticized in the context of the Church's later abuse scandals.

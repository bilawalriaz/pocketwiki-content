# Cai Lun

## Overview
Cai Lun (c. 50–62 – 121 CE) was a Chinese eunuch official of the Eastern Han dynasty whose major innovation in papermaking around 105 CE led to its large-scale manufacture and global spread. While not the original inventor of paper, his improvements using tree bark and hemp made it a practical, widespread medium, profoundly impacting the history of communication and knowledge.

## Timeline
- **c. 50–62 CE** — Born in Guiyang Commandery (modern Leiyang, Hunan).
- **By 75 CE** — Arrives at the imperial court in Luoyang, serving Emperor Ming.
- **c. 80 CE** — Promoted to Xiao Huangmen (imperial messenger) under Emperor Zhang.
- **82 CE** — Interrogates Consort Song and her sister at Empress Dou's behest; they commit suicide.
- **88 CE** — Emperor He ascends; Cai is made Zhongchang shi (chief eunuch) and Shangfang Ling (Prefect of the Palace Workshop).
- **92 CE** — Survives Emperor He's coup against the Dou family.
- **97 CE** — His workshop responsibilities expand to include ceremonial weapons.
- **105 CE** — Publicly declares his improved papermaking process using tree bark, hemp, rags, and fishnets.
- **110 CE** — Appointed to oversee the creation of a definitive edition of the Five Classics.
- **114 CE** — Enfeoffed as Marquis of Longting.
- **121 CE** — Ordered to the Ministry of Justice; commits suicide.

## Body

### Early Life and Court Service
Cai Lun was born into a poor family in southern China. He arrived at the imperial court in Luoyang by 75 CE, where he served as a eunuch. He gained influence through loyalty to Empress Dou, helping her secure her adopted son as heir. This led to his appointment as *Zhongchang shi* (chief eunuch) and *Shangfang Ling* (Prefect of the Palace Workshop) in 88 CE, where he oversaw the production of instruments and weapons.

### Innovation in Papermaking
Before Cai, writing was done on heavy bamboo slips or expensive silk. In 105 CE, Cai announced a new papermaking process. He used a pulp of tree bark, hemp waste, old rags, and fishnets, which was boiled, beaten, and spread on screens to dry. This "Cai Hou paper" was cheaper and more practical than previous materials, leading to its rapid adoption across China. His contribution is seen as a pivotal innovation that enabled the widespread use of paper, rather than a sudden invention.

### Final Years and Legacy
Cai maintained influence under Empress Dowager Deng, overseeing scholarly projects. After her death in 121 CE, Emperor An, whose grandmother Cai had helped persecute, ordered him to the Ministry of Justice. Expecting a death sentence, Cai committed suicide. His papermaking improvements are considered one of China's Four Great Inventions, spreading via the Arab world to Europe by the 12th century. In China, he is deified as the god of papermaking and remains a cultural icon, though he is less known outside East Asia.

## Terms
- **Eunuch:** A castrated man serving in the imperial court, eligible for specific roles like managing the harem.
- **Zhongchang shi:** The highest political counselor position exclusive to eunuchs during the Han dynasty.
- **Shangfang Ling:** The prefect in charge of the Palace Workshop, which produced instruments, weapons, and other imperial goods.
- **Bamboo and wooden slips:** The primary writing surfaces in China before paper, which were heavy and inconvenient.
- **Pulp:** A mixture of fibrous material (like bark or rags) boiled and beaten into a slurry for papermaking.
- **Four Great Inventions:** The compass, gunpowder, papermaking, and printing, traditionally credited as key Chinese innovations.
- **Deification:** The process of being declared a god, as Cai Lun was for papermaking in the Tang dynasty.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Inventor vs. Innovator:** Archaeological evidence shows paper existed in China from the 3rd century BCE. Scholars debate whether Cai Lun invented paper or, as the term *zào yì* ("to initiate the idea") suggests, significantly improved an existing process.
- **Credit and Patronage:** There is speculation that Cai may have taken credit for the work of others, similar to later figures like Feng Dao with printing.
- **Historical Sources:** The main biography of Cai Lun was written nearly 300 years after his death, leading to questions about its accuracy. Some major historical annals omit his papermaking achievements, possibly due to bias against eunuchs.

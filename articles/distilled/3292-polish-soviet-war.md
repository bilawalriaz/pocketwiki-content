# Polish–Soviet War

## Overview
The Polish–Soviet War (1919–1921) was a major conflict fought between the newly independent Second Polish Republic and Soviet Russia for control of disputed territories in modern-day Ukraine and Belarus. It was a pivotal struggle that determined the eastern borders of interwar Poland and halted the westward spread of communist revolution into Central Europe, shaping the geopolitical landscape of the region for two decades.

## Timeline
- **14 Feb 1919** — First clashes between Polish and Soviet forces begin.
- **25 Apr 1920** — Poland launches the Kiev offensive with Ukrainian allies.
- **7 May 1920** — Polish and Ukrainian forces capture Kiev.
- **Jun 1920** — Soviet Red Army launches a major counteroffensive.
- **12–25 Aug 1920** — Decisive Battle of Warsaw ("Miracle on the Vistula") halts the Soviet advance.
- **18 Oct 1920** — Armistice goes into effect.
- **18 Mar 1921** — Peace of Riga treaty is signed, ending the war.

## Body

### Background and Early Conflict
Following World War I and the Russian Revolution, both Poland and Soviet Russia sought to define their borders in the power vacuum left by the collapse of empires. Poland, under Józef Piłsudski, aimed to restore its historic borders and create a federation of states (the Intermarium) to counter Russian influence. Soviet Russia, led by Vladimir Lenin, sought to reclaim former imperial territories and saw Poland as a route to spread communist revolution into Europe. Initial fighting in 1919 saw Polish forces advance eastward, occupying much of present-day Lithuania and Belarus, while the Soviets were preoccupied with the Russian Civil War.

### The Kiev Offensive and Soviet Counterattack
In April 1920, Piłsudski launched the Kiev offensive to secure a pro-Polish Ukrainian state and push the front eastward. Allied with the defeated Ukrainian People's Republic under Symon Petliura, Polish forces captured Kiev in May. However, the offensive lacked local support, and a massive Soviet counteroffensive in June, led by Mikhail Tukhachevsky, soon pushed the Poles back. By August, Soviet troops had reached the outskirts of Warsaw, and the survival of the Polish state appeared uncertain.

### The Battle of Warsaw and Polish Victory
In a decisive turn, Polish forces achieved a stunning victory at the Battle of Warsaw (12–25 August 1920), often called the "Miracle on the Vistula." Polish intelligence, which had broken Soviet ciphers, provided crucial information. A subsequent Polish counteroffensive drove the Red Army back, leading to a series of Polish victories and the stabilization of a new front line. The battle halted the westward advance of communism and cemented Poland's position as a major Eastern European power.

### Peace of Riga and Aftermath
The war ended with the Peace of Riga (March 1921), which divided the disputed territories of Belarus and Ukraine between Poland and Soviet Russia. Poland's eastern border was set about 200 km east of the Curzon Line. The treaty forced Poland to abandon its alliance with Petliura and recognize the Ukrainian and Byelorussian Soviet Socialist Republics, undermining Piłsudski's Intermarium vision. The conflict resulted in approximately 100,000 deaths and left a legacy of instability, as the Soviet Union never formally accepted the border settlement.

## Terms
- **Intermarium**: Piłsudski's concept for a federation of Central and Eastern European states, led by Poland, to counter Russian and German influence.
- **Curzon Line**: A demarcation line proposed by the Allied Supreme War Council in 1919 as a possible ethnic boundary for Poland, later used as a reference in peace negotiations.
- **Peace of Riga**: The 1921 treaty that formally ended the war, establishing the Polish-Soviet border and partitioning Belarus and Ukraine.
- **Kiev Offensive**: Poland's 1920 military campaign to capture Kiev and install a friendly Ukrainian government.
- **Battle of Warsaw**: The 1920 battle where Polish forces repelled the Soviet advance, considered a turning point of the war.
- **Polish–Ukrainian War**: A 1918–1919 conflict between Poland and the West Ukrainian People's Republic over control of Eastern Galicia, which Poland won.
- **Litbel**: The short-lived Socialist Soviet Republic of Lithuania and Belorussia, a Soviet puppet state established in 1919.
- **Żeligowski's Mutiny**: A 1920 Polish military operation that led to the capture of Vilnius from Lithuania, later formalized by annexation.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Who won the war?** Historians debate the outcome: it is often seen as a Polish military victory but a political defeat, as the Peace of Riga did not achieve Piłsudski's maximalist goals and partitioned Ukraine and Belarus.
- **Aggressor vs. Defender:** The perception of Poland as the aggressor, particularly with the Kiev offensive, damaged its international reputation, while the Soviets framed the conflict as a defense against Allied intervention.
- **Impact on World Revolution:** The Soviet failure at Warsaw is widely considered a major setback for Lenin's strategy of exporting communist revolution to Western Europe.
- **Legacy of the Riga Settlement:** The treaty created a Poland with a large minority population and a border the Soviet Union later overturned in 1939, contributing to the instability of the interwar period.

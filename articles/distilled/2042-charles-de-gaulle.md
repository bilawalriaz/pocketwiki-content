# Charles de Gaulle

## Overview
Charles de Gaulle (1890–1970) was a French general and statesman who led the Free French Forces against Nazi Germany in World War II and later founded the Fifth Republic, serving as its first president. A defining figure in modern French history, he advocated for national independence, a strong executive, and a foreign policy asserting France's role as a major power independent of the United States and the Soviet Union.

## Body

### Early Life and Military Career
De Gaulle was born into a devout, conservative Catholic family. He entered the army in 1909, graduating from Saint-Cyr in 1912. He served with distinction in World War I, where he was wounded several times and captured at Verdun in 1916, spending 32 months as a prisoner of war. During the interwar period, he became a vocal advocate for modernizing the army through mobile armoured divisions, a view at odds with prevailing static defence doctrine. His ideas were outlined in his 1934 book *Vers l'Armée de Métier*.

### World War II and the Free French
At the outbreak of World War II, de Gaulle commanded an armoured division. Following France's defeat in 1940, he was appointed Undersecretary for War but refused to accept the armistice. He fled to England and, on 18 June 1940, broadcast his famous Appeal of 18 June, exhorting the French to continue fighting. He became the leader of the Free French Forces, which grew from a base in French Equatorial Africa. After the Allied landings in North Africa in 1942, he moved to Algiers and became chairman of the French Committee of National Liberation. He returned to France in June 1944 and became head of the Provisional Government, overseeing the liberation and the legal purge (*Épuration légale*) of collaborators.

### The Fifth Republic and Domestic Policy
After resigning in 1946, de Gaulle remained politically active but largely out of power until the Algerian War threatened to collapse the unstable Fourth Republic. In May 1958, he was called back to form a government, with the mandate to draft a new constitution. The resulting Fifth Republic, established by referendum in September 1958, created a strong presidency. As president, de Gaulle oversaw economic modernization, including the introduction of a new franc. This period of growth, from 1945 to 1974, is known as the *Trente Glorieuses*. He resolved the Algerian War by granting independence in 1962, a decision that angered many French settlers (*Pieds-Noirs*) and led to assassination attempts against him.

### Foreign Policy and "Grandeur"
De Gaulle pursued a foreign policy of "grandeur" and national independence. He developed France's independent nuclear deterrent (*Force de Frappe*), making France the world's fourth nuclear power in 1960. He withdrew France from NATO's integrated military command in 1966. He fostered a close partnership with West Germany via the Élysée Treaty (1963) to create a European counterweight to the superpowers, but he opposed a supranational Europe and vetoed British entry into the European Economic Community (EEC) in 1963 and 1967. He also pursued an independent line in the Cold War, recognizing the People's Republic of China in 1964 and criticizing U.S. involvement in Vietnam.

### Final Years
De Gaulle was re-elected in 1965 but faced widespread student and worker protests in May 1968. Although his party won a larger majority in subsequent elections, he lost a 1969 referendum on decentralization and resigned. He retired to write his memoirs and died in 1970.

## Terms
- **Appeal of 18 June**: De Gaulle's 1940 radio broadcast from London urging the French to resist Nazi occupation.
- **Free French Forces**: Resistance forces formed by de Gaulle in 1940 to fight Nazi Germany and Vichy France.
- **Provisional Government of the French Republic (GPRF)**: The interim government of France from 1944 to 1946, led by de Gaulle.
- **Fifth Republic**: France's current republican regime, established in 1958 with a constitution granting significant powers to the president.
- **Force de Frappe**: France's independent nuclear deterrent, developed under de Gaulle.
- **Élysée Treaty**: A 1963 treaty of friendship between France and West Germany, symbolizing post-war reconciliation.
- **Trente Glorieuses**: The period of exceptional economic growth in France from 1945 to 1974.
- **Pieds-Noirs**: French citizens born in Algeria who were repatriated after its 1962 independence.
- **Gaullism**: A political philosophy emphasizing national independence, a strong state, and pragmatic foreign policy.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Legitimacy of the Free French**: De Gaulle's escape to England and claim to lead the resistance were contested by the Vichy regime and some contemporaries as treasonous.
- **Algerian War**: His decision to grant Algerian independence is debated as either a pragmatic necessity to end a brutal war or a betrayal of French settlers and military hardliners.
- **Relationship with Allies**: His contentious relationships with Allied leaders, especially U.S. President Franklin D. Roosevelt, are analyzed for their impact on post-war European politics.
- **Legacy and Style**: Historians debate whether his autocratic style and "politics of grandeur" strengthened France's international position or isolated it. His handling of the May 1968 protests is also examined.

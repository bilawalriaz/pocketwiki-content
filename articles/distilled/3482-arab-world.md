# Arab world

## Overview
The Arab world is a region spanning West Asia and North Africa, united primarily by the Arabic language and a shared cultural identity. It comprises 22 member states of the Arab League, though definitions vary. The region is geopolitically significant due to its strategic location, vast petroleum reserves, and the central role of the Arab-Israeli conflict and other major modern conflicts.

## Timeline
- **7th–8th centuries** — Early Muslim conquests lead to the rapid expansion of Arab rule and the spread of Islam and the Arabic language.
- **13th century** — The Abbasid Caliphate falls to Mongol invasions.
- **By 1570** — The Ottoman Empire controls most of the Arab world.
- **Late 19th century** — Arab nationalism arises within the declining Ottoman Empire.
- **1945** — The Arab League is formed to represent Arab interests and pursue political unification (Pan-Arabism).
- **1948** — Establishment of the State of Israel, triggering the ongoing Arab–Israeli conflict.
- **1958–1961** — The United Arab Republic, a short-lived political union of Egypt and Syria, exists.
- **1960–1971** — Most Arab states gain independence from European colonial powers.
- **1979** — Egypt–Israel peace treaty is signed.
- **1980–1988** — The Iran–Iraq War, a major conventional conflict, is fought.
- **1990–1991** — Iraq invades Kuwait, leading to the Persian Gulf War.
- **2010–present** — The Arab Spring protests begin, demanding democratic rights and leading to prolonged conflicts in Libya and Syria.

## Body

### Definition and Terminology
The Arab world is most commonly defined as the 22 member states of the Arab League. The League defines an Arab as a person whose language is Arabic, who lives in an Arab country, and who sympathizes with Arab aspirations. An alternative "ancillary linguistic" definition includes peoples and territories united by Arabic language or culture, potentially including diaspora populations. While Arabic is the official language in all member states, local vernaculars (Darija in the Maghreb, Aammiyya in the Mashriq) are widely spoken. Some non-member states like Chad and Israel recognize Arabic as an official language but are not considered part of the Arab world.

### History and Conflicts
Arab expansion began with the 7th–8th century Muslim conquests. The region later came under Ottoman rule until the empire's collapse after World War I, leading to European colonial mandates. Arab nationalism grew in the 19th century, culminating in the formation of the Arab League in 1945 to pursue Pan-Arabism, or political unification. This ideology largely waned by the 1980s, replaced by individual nationalisms and Pan-Islamism.

The region's modern history is marked by major conflicts. The 1948 establishment of Israel sparked the Arab–Israeli conflict, involving several wars between 1948 and 1973. The Iran–Iraq War (1980–1988) was a prolonged conventional war. The 1990 Iraqi invasion of Kuwait led to the Persian Gulf War. Internal conflicts include the Lebanese Civil War (1975–1990), the Western Sahara conflict, and the ongoing Somali Civil War. The Arab Spring, beginning in 2010, saw widespread protests against authoritarian regimes, leading to civil wars in Libya and Syria.

### Geography and Economy
The Arab world stretches over 13 million square kilometers, straddling Africa and Asia. It is divided into the eastern Mashriq and the western Maghreb. Economically, the region is diverse, with some of the world's wealthiest and poorest nations. Petroleum is a critical resource; the Arab world holds an estimated 46% of the world's proven oil reserves and a quarter of its natural gas reserves. Five Persian Gulf states are among the top global hydrocarbon exporters. The Gulf Cooperation Council (GCC) is a key economic organization. As of 2021, the total population was 456 million with a GDP of $2.85 trillion.

### Demographics and Society
Arabic is the official language, with Modern Standard Arabic used formally and local dialects spoken informally. The population is predominantly Muslim, with Sunni Islam as the majority faith, though Shia majorities exist in Iraq and Bahrain. Significant Christian communities are present in several countries. Literacy rates vary, averaging 78% for adults, with notable gender disparity. Most Arab states are classified as authoritarian regimes, with limited democratic freedoms. The Arab film industry is diverse, with Egypt historically being the largest producer.

## Terms
- **Arab League:** A regional organization founded in 1945 to coordinate political, cultural, and economic affairs among its 22 member states.
- **Pan-Arabism:** An ideology advocating for the political unification of Arab peoples into a single nation-state.
- **Mashriq:** The eastern part of the Arab world, generally referring to the Levant and Mesopotamia.
- **Maghreb:** The western part of the Arab world, comprising North African countries west of Egypt.
- **Darija / Aammiyya:** Local vernacular dialects of Arabic spoken in the Maghreb and Mashriq, respectively, as opposed to formal Modern Standard Arabic.
- **Rentier state:** A state that derives all or a substantial portion of its national revenues from the rent of indigenous resources to external clients, often leading to specific political and economic structures.
- **Diglossia:** A situation where two dialects or languages are used by a single language community in different social contexts, as with Modern Standard Arabic (formal) and local dialects (informal).

## Debates and open questions
- The precise definition of the "Arab world" is debated, particularly regarding the inclusion of non-Arab League states with Arabic-speaking populations or official recognition of the language.
- The feasibility and desirability of Pan-Arabism as a unifying political project remain contested, having largely been superseded by nationalism and Islamism since the 1980s.
- The impact of European colonialism in drawing modern state borders, often without local consultation, continues to influence regional instability and identity.

# Mont Blanc

## Overview
Mont Blanc is the highest mountain in the Alps and Western Europe, rising 4,807.3 m (15,771.9 ft) on the Franco-Italian border. It is a major symbol of mountaineering, a popular but dangerous climbing destination, and the center of a long-standing border dispute. Its massif is a key site for studying geology, climate, and the impacts of tourism and climate change.

## Timeline
- **8 August 1786** — First recorded ascent by Jacques Balmat and Michel-Gabriel Paccard.
- **1808** — First woman to reach the summit, Marie Paradis.
- **1860** — Treaty of Turin annexes Savoy to France, placing Mont Blanc on the new border.
- **1893** — Janssen Observatory built on the summit; abandoned by 1909 due to ice movement.
- **1950** — Air India Flight 245 crashes into the mountain, killing 48.
- **1965** — Mont Blanc Tunnel opens, connecting France and Italy.
- **1966** — Air India Flight 101 crashes, killing 117, including Homi J. Bhabha.
- **1999** — A fatal fire in the Mont Blanc Tunnel kills 39 people.
- **2003** — Post-heatwave measurement records summit height as 4,808.45 m (15,775.9 in).
- **2023** — Summit height measured at a record low of 4,805.59 m (15,776.4 ft) after drought.

## Body

### Geology and Climate
The Mont Blanc massif is formed from a granite batholith (a large intrusion of igneous rock) forced up through older gneiss and mica schists during the late Palaeozoic Variscan orogeny. The summit sits at the contact point of these rock types. The landscape was further shaped by faulting during the later Alpine orogeny and by glaciation. The climate is cold and temperate, heavily influenced by altitude. The summit is a permanent ice cap with temperatures around −20 °C (−4 °F). Precipitation varies greatly with elevation, peaking at mid-altitudes before decreasing near the summit.

### Ownership Dispute
The border between France and Italy generally follows the Alpine watershed, but its precise location near the summit is disputed. Italian officials claim the border follows the watershed, splitting the summit. French officials claim the border avoids the summit, placing it entirely in France. The dispute stems from differing interpretations of the 1860 Treaty of Turin and subsequent surveys. The contested area on Mont Blanc is approximately 65 hectares (160 acres).

### Climbing and Tourism
The most popular route is the Goûter Route, typically taking two days. Other classic routes include La Voie des 3 Monts and the Italian normal route. The summit is ascended by an average of 20,000 people annually. While technically straightforward for the prepared, the climb is arduous and dangerous, with risks including rockfall, avalanches, and altitude sickness. An estimated 6,000 to 8,000 alpinist fatalities have occurred on the mountain, with 10-20 deaths reported annually in recent years. The Goûter couloir is a particularly hazardous section.

### Infrastructure and Incidents
Key infrastructure includes the Mont Blanc Tunnel (opened 1965) and the Vallot emergency shelter. The mountain has been the site of two major air disasters (1950, 1966) and a fatal tunnel fire in 1999. Notable exploits include the first ascent (1786), the first female ascent (1808), and various speed records. Incidents involving children attempting the climb have also occurred.

### Elevation and Climate Change
The summit's height varies due to its ice cap. Official measurements have fluctuated, with a high of 4,810.02 m (15,781 ft) in 2013 and a low of 4,805.59 m (15,776.4 ft) in 2023 following drought. Climate change is causing glacial melt and increased rockfall, making routes more dangerous. For example, the Grand Mulets route was blocked by crevasses in 2015, and a new crevasse opened in 2016 on the final summit approach.

## Terms
- **Batholith**: A large mass of intrusive igneous rock, formed from cooled magma deep in the Earth's crust.
- **Watershed**: The line dividing adjacent drainage basins; often used to define mountain borders.
- **Variscan orogeny**: A mountain-building event in the late Palaeozoic era.
- **Alpine orogeny**: The ongoing mountain-building phase that formed the Alps.
- **Köppen climate classification Cfb**: A temperate climate with warm summers and no dry season.
- **Goûter Route**: The most popular climbing route to the summit of Mont Blanc.
- **Refuge**: A mountain hut providing shelter for climbers.
- **Serac**: A block or column of glacial ice, often formed by intersecting crevasses.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The precise location of the Franco-Italian border at the summit remains officially disputed between the two nations.
- The exact summit elevation is not permanent due to the ice cap and is subject to change from year to year, influenced by weather and climate.
- The impact of climate change on the mountain's glaciers, rock stability, and the long-term viability of classic climbing routes is a major ongoing concern.

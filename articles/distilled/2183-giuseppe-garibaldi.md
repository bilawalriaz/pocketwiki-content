# Giuseppe Garibaldi

## Overview
Giuseppe Garibaldi (1807–1882) was an Italian general, revolutionary, and republican whose military campaigns and charismatic leadership were instrumental in the Unification of Italy (Risorgimento). He is celebrated as one of Italy's "fathers of the fatherland" and a global symbol of 19th-century revolutionary liberalism and nationalism, known as the "Hero of the Two Worlds" for his exploits in South America and Europe.

## Timeline
- **4 July 1807** — Born in Nice (then part of the French Republic).
- **1833** — Meets Giuseppe Mazzini in Genoa; joins the Young Italy movement.
- **1835–1848** — Fights in South America, joining the Ragamuffin War in Brazil and later commanding the Italian Legion in the Uruguayan Civil War.
- **1848** — Returns to Italy; fights in the First Italian War of Independence and defends the Roman Republic.
- **1859** — Leads the "Hunters of the Alps" in the Second Italian War of Independence.
- **May–Oct 1860** — Leads the Expedition of the Thousand, conquering Sicily and Southern Italy for the Kingdom of Sardinia.
- **26 Oct 1860** — Meets King Victor Emmanuel II at Teano, handing over conquered territories.
- **17 March 1861** — Kingdom of Italy is proclaimed.
- **1866** — Commands volunteers in the Third Italian War of Independence, fighting in Trentino.
- **1870** — Commands the French Army of the Vosges in the Franco-Prussian War.
- **2 June 1882** — Dies on the island of Caprera.

## Body

### Early Life and Exile
Born in Nice to a Ligurian family, Garibaldi was drawn to a life at sea. In 1833, he met Giovanni Battista Cuneo, a member of Giuseppe Mazzini's secret Young Italy movement, which advocated for a unified Italian republic. After participating in a failed Mazzinian insurrection in Piedmont in 1834, Garibaldi was sentenced to death and fled into exile.

### South American Campaigns
Garibaldi spent 14 years in South America, where he learned guerrilla warfare. He fought in the Ragamuffin War in Brazil (1835) and later in the Uruguayan Civil War (1842–1848), where he raised an Italian volunteer force known as the "Redshirts." He married Anita Ribiero, a skilled fighter who fought alongside him. During this period, he also joined Freemasonry, viewing it as a network for progressive ideals.

### Return to Italy and the First War of Independence
Returning to Italy in 1848, Garibaldi offered his services to the Piedmontese monarchy but was rebuffed. He fought for the provisional government of Milan and later defended the short-lived Roman Republic against French forces in 1849. After its fall, he led a famous retreat with his followers, during which his wife Anita died.

### The Expedition of the Thousand and Unification
In 1860, Garibaldi led his famous "Expedition of the Thousand" volunteers from Genoa to Sicily. Through a combination of military skill and popular support, he conquered Sicily and then mainland Southern Italy. In a pivotal moment, he met King Victor Emmanuel II at Teano, greeting him as King of Italy and handing over the conquered territories, pragmatically subordinating his republican ideals to the goal of a unified monarchy. This led to the creation of the Kingdom of Italy in 1861.

### Later Campaigns and Political Life
Garibaldi continued to fight for Italian unification, leading volunteers in the 1866 war against Austria and making unsuccessful attempts to capture Rome from Papal control in 1862 and 1867. In his later years, he supported the Paris Commune and aligned with socialist and internationalist causes, founding the League of Democracy. He served in the Italian and French parliaments and spent his final years on his farm in Caprera.

## Terms
- **Risorgimento**: The 19th-century movement for Italian unification.
- **Young Italy**: A political movement founded by Giuseppe Mazzini advocating for a unified Italian republic.
- **Redshirts (Garibaldini)**: The volunteer soldiers who followed Garibaldi, named for their distinctive red shirts.
- **Hunters of the Alps (Cacciatori delle Alpi)**: A volunteer unit formed by Garibaldi in 1859 to fight against Austrian forces.
- **Expedition of the Thousand (i Mille)**: Garibaldi's 1860 military campaign with a volunteer force that conquered Sicily and Southern Italy.
- **Carbonari**: A network of secret revolutionary societies active in early 19th-century Italy.
- **Freemasonry**: A fraternal organization that Garibaldi joined, which offered asylum to political refugees and promoted progressive ideals.
- **Irredentism**: A political movement advocating for the annexation of territories considered to be ethnically or historically Italian but under foreign rule.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Pragmatism vs. Idealism**: Scholars debate whether Garibaldi's alliance with the monarchist Cavour and his sacrifice of republican principles was a necessary pragmatic compromise for unification or a betrayal of his core ideals.
- **Social Revolution**: While Garibaldi's conquest of Sicily was aided by peasant revolts against landlords, he ultimately sided with the Sicilian elite, raising questions about his commitment to social reform versus national liberation.
- **Religious Views**: Garibaldi's personal religious convictions are unclear. He was anti-clerical and opposed the Papacy's temporal power, but his statements ranged from deistic to nominally Christian, and he abjured Christianity later in life.

# Prince Eugene of Savoy

## Overview
Prince Eugene of Savoy (1663–1736) was a brilliant military commander and statesman who served the Austrian Habsburgs for over six decades. Born in Paris and rejected by Louis XIV, he became the Empire's leading general, securing decisive victories against the Ottoman Empire and France. His career shaped European power dynamics, and he later served as a key diplomat and patron of the arts in Vienna.

## Body

### Early Life and Ottoman Wars
Born in Paris, Eugene was the youngest son of a noble family, initially destined for the church. After his mother Olympia Mancini fell from favor at Louis XIV's court, he was raised by relatives. In 1683, at 19, he was denied a military commission by Louis XIV, reportedly due to his physique and a scandal involving his mother. He fled to serve Emperor Leopold I, first distinguishing himself at the Battle of Vienna in 1683. He rose rapidly, becoming a field marshal by 1688. His decisive victory at the Battle of Zenta in 1697 ended the Ottoman threat to Hungary for nearly two decades, leading to the Treaty of Karlowitz in 1699.

### War of the Spanish Succession
The death of Charles II of Spain in 1700 sparked a major European war. Eugene commanded Imperial forces in Italy, achieving early victories at Carpi (1701) and Chiari (1701). In 1702, he captured the French commander at Cremona. Appointed President of the Imperial War Council in 1703, he entered the Emperor's inner circle. His partnership with the Duke of Marlborough proved crucial; together they won the Battle of Blenheim in 1704, saving Vienna. Eugene later won a major victory at Turin in 1706, breaking French power in Italy. He rejoined Marlborough for victories at Oudenarde (1708) and the bloody Battle of Malplaquet (1709). After Marlborough's dismissal in 1712, Eugene fought alone, suffering defeat at Denain. He negotiated the Treaty of Rastatt (1714), securing favorable terms for Austria.

### Later Military and Diplomatic Career
Eugene's final great military triumphs came against the Ottomans. He won the Battle of Petrovaradin in 1716 and, in a daring move, defeated a larger relief force to capture Belgrade in 1717. The Treaty of Passarowitz (1718) secured Austria's gains. In the 1720s, he became a master diplomat, navigating the complex "Cold War" between European powers. He secured crucial alliances, including the Second Treaty of Vienna (1731), which guaranteed the Pragmatic Sanction securing the Habsburg inheritance for Maria Theresa. His final command in the War of the Polish Succession (1733–1735) was hampered by declining health and inadequate resources, but he conducted a successful defensive campaign.

### Personal Life and Legacy
Eugene never married, stating a soldier should not. His closest companion was Countess Eleonore Batthyány. He was a major patron of the arts, commissioning Baroque palaces like the Belvedere in Vienna and amassing a vast art collection and library. He died in 1736, immensely wealthy but without direct heirs. His military legacy was profound; he was considered one of history's greatest commanders by Napoleon and mentored the young Frederick the Great of Prussia. His strategic insight and ability to seize battlefield opportunities were highly influential.

## Terms
*   **Feldmarschall**: The highest military rank in the Holy Roman Empire's army, equivalent to Field Marshal.
*   **Holy League**: A coalition formed in 1684 by Emperor Leopold I, Poland, and Venice to counter the Ottoman Empire.
*   **Grand Alliance**: The coalition of powers (including Austria, England, and the Dutch Republic) formed to oppose Louis XIV of France during the War of the Spanish Succession.
*   **Pragmatic Sanction**: An edict issued by Emperor Charles VI in 1713 to ensure his daughter, Maria Theresa, could inherit his vast Habsburg domains.
*   **President of the Imperial War Council (Hofkriegsratspräsident)**: The chief administrative and strategic head of the Austrian military, a position Eugene held from 1703.
*   **Treaty of Karlowitz (1699)**: The treaty ending the Great Turkish War, which saw the Ottoman Empire cede significant territories in Hungary and Transylvania to the Holy League.
*   **Treaty of Rastatt (1714)**: The treaty between France and Austria that ended the War of the Spanish Succession, negotiated by Eugene and Marshal Villars.
*   **Quadruple Alliance (1718)**: An alliance of Austria, France, Britain, and the Dutch Republic formed to enforce the Utrecht settlement and oppose Spanish expansion.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Military Style**: Historians debate whether Eugene was a true innovator or a master of existing tactics. He is noted for his boldness and ability to seize the moment in battle, but his later campaigns in the Low Countries showed a more conservative, siege-oriented approach.
*   **Administrative Legacy**: A key criticism is that Eugene left no lasting institutional reform or "school of officers." The Austrian army's poor performance in the War of the Polish Succession, shortly after his death, is often attributed to his failure to create a system that could function without his personal command.
*   **Personal Life**: Contemporary allegations, notably from Elizabeth Charlotte, Duchess of Orléans, accused Eugene of "depravity." Modern historians largely dismiss these as hostile propaganda from a French court he had defeated, noting no other contemporary sources corroborate them.

# Che Guevara

## Overview
Ernesto "Che" Guevara (1928–1967) was an Argentine Marxist revolutionary and key leader of the Cuban Revolution. His legacy is deeply polarizing: revered as an icon of rebellion and anti-imperialism by leftist movements, while critics condemn him for authoritarianism and violence. His image has become a global symbol of counterculture and revolution.

## Timeline
- **14 May 1928** — Born in Rosario, Argentina.
- **1951–1952** — Undertakes an 8,000 km motorcycle journey across South America.
- **1953–1954** — Travels to Guatemala; witnesses the CIA-backed overthrow of President Jacobo Árbenz.
- **1955** — Meets Fidel and Raúl Castro in Mexico City; joins the 26th of July Movement.
- **2 Dec 1956** — Sails to Cuba aboard the yacht *Granma* to launch a revolutionary campaign.
- **1957–1958** — Rises to prominence as a commander in the Sierra Maestra guerrilla campaign.
- **31 Dec 1958** — His forces capture Santa Clara, the decisive battle of the Cuban Revolution.
- **1959–1965** — Holds key posts in the Cuban government, including Minister of Industries and President of the National Bank.
- **Oct 1962** — Plays a key role in bringing Soviet nuclear missiles to Cuba, precipitating the Cuban Missile Crisis.
- **24 Feb 1965** — Delivers a speech in Algiers criticizing the Soviet Union and calling for world revolution.
- **1965** — Leads a failed guerrilla campaign in the Congo.
- **1966–1967** — Leads a guerrilla insurgency in Bolivia.
- **8 Oct 1967** — Captured by Bolivian forces in the Yuro ravine.
- **9 Oct 1967** — Executed by firing squad in La Higuera, Bolivia.

## Body

### Early Life and Political Awakening
Born to an upper-class Argentine family, Guevara developed an early "affinity for the poor." A severe asthmatic condition shaped his resilient character. As a medical student, he undertook two long journeys across South America, most famously a 1952 motorcycle trek with Alberto Granado. Witnessing poverty, disease, and exploitation, he concluded Latin America's problems required political, not just medical, solutions, fostering a view of the continent as a single entity needing liberation.

### From Guatemala to Cuba
After medical school, Guevara traveled to Guatemala in 1953, supporting President Jacobo Árbenz's social reforms. The 1954 CIA-assisted coup that overthrew Árbenz at the behest of the United Fruit Company solidified Guevara's anti-imperialist ideology and belief in armed struggle. Fleeing to Mexico, he met Fidel Castro, joined his revolutionary movement, and sailed to Cuba in 1956. In the ensuing guerrilla war, Guevara rose to become Castro's second-in-command, proving a ruthless and effective military leader. His forces captured Santa Clara in a decisive victory, leading to the overthrow of dictator Fulgencio Batista.

### Role in the Cuban Government
After the revolution, Guevara held powerful positions. He oversaw revolutionary tribunals at La Cabaña fortress, where hundreds of Batista's former officials were executed. As Minister of Industries and President of the National Bank, he spearheaded agrarian reform and a successful national literacy campaign. He was central to training the militia that repelled the Bay of Pigs Invasion and in secret negotiations that brought Soviet nuclear missiles to Cuba, precipitating the 1962 Cuban Missile Crisis. Guevara advocated for a "New Man" (*el Hombre Nuevo*)—a selfless, morally-driven citizen motivated by collective good rather than material incentives—a philosophy guiding his economic policies.

### International Revolutionary and Final Campaign
Guevara grew critical of the Soviet Union, viewing it as a complicit imperialist power. In 1965, he left Cuba to foment revolution abroad. His first attempt, supporting rebels in the Congo, failed due to poor discipline among local fighters and lack of support. In 1966, he secretly entered Bolivia to lead a guerrilla insurgency. The campaign failed due to a lack of local support, poor radio equipment, and effective counter-insurgency by the Bolivian army, advised by U.S. Special Forces. Guevara was captured and executed in 1967.

## Terms
*   **26th of July Movement:** The revolutionary organization led by Fidel Castro that overthrew the Batista regime in Cuba.
*   **Foco theory:** A Marxist-Leninist strategy of guerrilla warfare, championed by Guevara, positing that a small, dedicated vanguard can create conditions for a popular revolution.
*   **New Man (*el Hombre Nuevo*):** Guevara's concept of an ideal socialist citizen who is selfless, morally driven, and motivated by collective goals rather than material rewards.
*   **Agrarian reform:** The redistribution of agricultural land, a key policy of the Cuban Revolution aimed at breaking up large estates and providing land to peasants.
*   **Imperialism:** The policy of extending a country's power and influence through diplomacy or military force; a central concept in Guevara's critique of U.S. and Soviet foreign policy.
*   **Proletarian internationalism:** The Marxist principle that workers of the world should unite across national boundaries to achieve their liberation.
*   **Bay of Pigs Invasion:** The failed 1961 landing in Cuba by U.S.-trained Cuban exiles, intended to overthrow Castro's government.
*   **Cuban Missile Crisis:** The 1962 confrontation between the U.S. and the Soviet Union over the installation of nuclear missiles in Cuba, which brought the world to the brink of war.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Legacy as Icon vs. Executioner:** Guevara is deeply polarizing. Admirers like Nelson Mandela and Jean-Paul Sartre see him as an inspirational revolutionary and humanist. Critics, including exiled Cuban prisoners and historian Álvaro Vargas Llosa, condemn him as a ruthless executioner who endorsed violence and suppressed dissent.
*   **Economic Philosophy:** Guevara's economic policies in Cuba, prioritizing moral incentives over material ones and rapid industrialization, are widely considered to have failed, leading to economic decline and a later return to sugar monoculture.
*   **Role in the Missile Crisis:** The extent of Guevara's personal responsibility for bringing Soviet missiles to Cuba, and his subsequent anger at the Soviet Union for withdrawing them without Cuban consent, remains a point of historical analysis.
*   **The "Che Myth":** Scholars debate how a figure who advocated violent revolution and worked in a one-party state became a global, often commercialized, icon of rebellion and youth culture. His image, particularly the *Guerrillero Heroico* photograph, has transcended its political origins.

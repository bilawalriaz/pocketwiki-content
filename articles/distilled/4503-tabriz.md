# Tabriz

## Overview
Tabriz is a major city in northwestern Iran and the capital of East Azerbaijan province. It is the sixth-most-populous city in Iran and has historically served as a capital for several dynasties, including the Ilkhanate, Safavids, and Qajars. Its strategic location near the borders of Armenia and Azerbaijan, along the Silk Road, made it a pivotal commercial, cultural, and political hub, especially for trade between the Middle East, the Caucasus, and Europe. The city is renowned for its historical monuments, its world-famous hand-woven carpets, and its role as a center for intellectual and revolutionary movements in modern Iranian history.

## Body

### Etymology and Early History
The name Tabriz may derive from *tap-riz* ("flowing hot"), referencing local thermal springs. Alternative theories link it to an Armenian word for "revenge" or an ancient 8th-century BC stronghold named Tarui-Tarmakisa. The earliest archaeological evidence is an Iron Age graveyard from the 1st millennium BC. The city was chosen as a capital by several rulers starting from the era of Atropates. Its early Islamic development began after the Arab conquest, with major rebuilding in 791 AD.

### Medieval and Early Modern Prominence
After the Muslim conquest, Tabriz grew into a major commercial hub on the Silk Road. It was chosen as the capital of the Ilkhanate in 1295, reaching great splendor under Ghazan Khan. The city later served as the capital for the Qara Qoyunlu (1375–1468) and Aq Qoyunlu (1469–1501) dynasties. In 1501, Ismail I made it the capital of the Safavid Empire, but the capital was moved to Qazvin in 1555 due to Ottoman threats. The city was repeatedly sacked by Ottomans and Timur and suffered devastating earthquakes, notably in 1721 and 1780.

### Modern Era and Political Significance
During the Qajar dynasty (1794–1925), Tabriz was the traditional residence of the crown prince. Its proximity to Europe made it a gateway for early modernization in Iran. The city was central to the Iranian Constitutional Revolution (1905–1911), led by local figures Sattar Khan and Bagher Khan. It was occupied by Russian forces during this period and again during World War I. After WWII, it briefly became the capital of the Soviet-backed Azerbaijan People's Government (1946–1947). Tabriz played a major role in the 1979 Iranian Revolution and subsequent unrest over minority rights.

### Demographics, Language, and Culture
The population is predominantly Azerbaijani, speaking Iranian Azerbaijani (a Turkic language) as their native language, with Persian as the second language. The majority are Shia Muslims, a conversion enforced by the Safavids in 1501. The city is a center for Azerbaijani music, particularly the *ashugh* (traveling bard) tradition, and is home to the renowned "Tabriz School" of Persian miniature painting. Its cuisine is famous throughout Iran, and it is designated a World Carpet Weaving City.

### Economy and Industry
Tabriz is the largest economic hub in northwest Iran, with a diversified economy based on heavy industries (automobiles, petrochemicals, machinery), textiles, and food production. It is a major center for handcrafts, especially the durable and high-quality Tabrizi rugs. The Grand Bazaar of Tabriz, a UNESCO World Heritage Site, remains a vital commercial center. The city hosts the annual TEXPO trade fair.

## Terms
- **Ilkhanate:** A Mongol khanate established in the 13th century, ruling over Iran and surrounding regions.
- **Safavid dynasty:** The ruling dynasty of Iran from 1501 to 1736, which established Twelver Shia Islam as the state religion.
- **Qajar dynasty:** The ruling dynasty of Iran from 1789 to 1925.
- **Iranian Azerbaijani:** A Turkic language spoken by the Azerbaijani people in Iran, mutually intelligible with modern Turkish dialects.
- **Ashugh:** A traditional traveling bard or minstrel in Azerbaijani culture, who sings and plays the *saz*.
- **Saz:** A long-necked lute used in traditional Azerbaijani and Turkish folk music.
- **Mugham:** A traditional system of Azerbaijani art music, similar to Persian classical music.
- **Grand Bazaar of Tabriz:** One of the oldest and largest covered bazaars in the Middle East, a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

## Debates and open questions
- The etymology of "Tabriz" is debated, with theories linking it to thermal springs, Armenian history, or an ancient Assyrian stronghold.
- The exact date of the city's founding is uncertain, with earliest elements claimed to be from the 3rd-4th century AD or the 7th century.
- The claim that the legendary Garden of Eden was near Tabriz, suggested by Egyptologist David Rohl, has not been accepted by the scholarly establishment.

# Rostov-on-Don

## Overview
Rostov-on-Don is a major port city and administrative center in southern Russia, situated on the Don River near the Sea of Azov. It serves as a crucial economic, cultural, and logistical hub for the region, with a population over one million. Its strategic location has made it a historically contested site and a key transportation node connecting southwestern Russia with the Caucasus and beyond.

## Body

### Early History and Founding
The area around the Don River mouth has been culturally and commercially important since ancient times, home to Scythian and Sarmatian tribes and the Greek colony of Tanais. The modern city began in 1749 with a custom house and fortress built to control trade with Turkey. It grew as a merchant settlement and, after the late 18th-century incorporation of Ottoman Black Sea territories, lost some military importance but gained commercial significance. Officially renamed Rostov-on-Don in 1806, it developed into a major trade center and communications hub in the 19th century, aided by railway connections and heavy industry fueled by Donets Basin coal and Krivoy Rog iron ore. In 1928, it merged with the adjacent Armenian settlement of Nakhichevan-on-Don.

### 20th Century and World Wars
Rostov was briefly occupied by Germany in 1918 and was a key battleground during the Russian Civil War. In the Soviet era, the regional government moved from Novocherkassk to Rostov in 1928. The city suffered significant damage in World War II, being occupied by Nazi forces twice (1941 and 1942-43) due to its strategic railway junction and access to the Caucasus. A massacre of up to 30,000 Russian Jews occurred at Zmievskaya Balka in 1942. Post-war reconstruction took a decade.

### Modern Era and Economy
Rostov's economy is driven by its favorable position at trading crossroads. The 1952 Volga–Don Canal connected it to five seas, enhancing its role as a major river port for passengers and freight (e.g., wheat, timber, minerals). Modern industry includes helicopter and farm machinery manufacturing. The city is a transportation hub with an international airport (Platov, opened 2017), major railway stations, and federal highways. It hosted the 2018 FIFA World Cup, for which new infrastructure was built. Strategically, it houses the Russian Southern Military District and was a logistical hub during the 2022 invasion of Ukraine and the 2023 Wagner rebellion.

### Culture and Demographics
Rostov is a diverse cultural center. The 2021 census recorded a population of 1,142,162. The city features numerous theaters, museums, libraries, and religious sites, including the Cathedral of the Nativity of the Blessed Virgin Mary. It has a humid continental climate with warm, humid summers and moderately cold winters. The city's symbols include a coat of arms and flag adopted in 1996, with historical roots in 19th-century designs.

## Terms
- **Urban Okrug**: An administrative division in Russia with a status equal to that of districts, incorporating a city and its surrounding territory.
- **Uyezd**: A historical type of administrative subdivision of the Russian Empire.
- **Volga–Don Shipping Canal**: A canal completed in 1952 that connects the Volga and Don rivers, linking the Caspian Sea to the Sea of Azov and Black Sea.
- **Port of Five Seas**: A nickname for Rostov-on-Don after 1952, indicating navigable access to the Black, Azov, Caspian, White, and Baltic Seas via canal and river systems.
- **Marshrutka**: A routed minibus, typically a 17-passenger Mercedes Sprinter, used as public transport in the city.
- **Zmievskaya Balka**: The site near Rostov-on-Don where up to 30,000 Russian Jews were massacred by German forces in 1942.
- **Southern Military District**: A military district of the Russian Armed Forces headquartered in Rostov-on-Don.

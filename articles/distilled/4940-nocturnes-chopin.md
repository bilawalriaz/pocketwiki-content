# Nocturnes (Chopin)

## Overview
Frédéric Chopin composed 21 nocturnes for solo piano between 1827 and 1846. These works are considered among the finest short pieces for the piano and remain central to the modern concert repertoire. While Chopin did not invent the nocturne, he popularized and significantly expanded the form, building upon the foundation established by Irish composer John Field.

## Timeline
- **1810** — Frédéric Chopin is born.
- **c. 1827–1846** — Chopin composes his 21 nocturnes.
- **1832** — Chopin meets John Field, who hears his nocturnes.
- **1830s–1840s** — Nocturnes 1–18 are published during Chopin's lifetime, in groups of two or three.
- **1849** — Chopin dies; Nocturnes 19 and 20 are published posthumously.
- **1938** — Nocturne No. 21 is published and later included in the standard set.

## Body

### Influences and Development
Chopin was a great admirer of John Field, who was already an accomplished composer by the time of Chopin's birth. Field's influence is evident in Chopin's use of a song-like right-hand melody, a left-hand accompaniment of broken chords, and extensive use of the sustain pedal. However, Chopin innovated upon this foundation. He introduced a more freely flowing rhythm, expanded the musical structure by drawing inspiration from Italian and French opera arias and sonata form, and used counterpoint to create dramatic tension. Composer Franz Liszt and others have noted the influence of Vincenzo Bellini's *bel canto* arias on Chopin's nocturnes. The resulting "Chopin nocturne" is often described as a synthesis of Field's form with a classic/romantic sound reminiscent of Mozart.

### Reception and Legacy
Initially, Chopin's nocturnes received mixed reviews from critics, though many later retracted their negative assessments. While the popularity of individual pieces has varied, the nocturnes as a whole hold a significant place in piano repertoire, with Op. 9 No. 2 in E♭ major and Op. 27 No. 2 in D♭ major being particularly enduring. Chopin's work influenced later composers such as Johannes Brahms and Richard Wagner, and was admired by contemporaries like Felix Mendelssohn, Robert Schumann, and Franz Liszt. The most important later composer of nocturnes was Gabriel Fauré, who composed thirteen works in the genre. Other composers who wrote solo piano nocturnes include Erik Satie, Alexander Scriabin, and Samuel Barber.

### Publication and Numbering
The 18 nocturnes published during Chopin's lifetime (Nos. 1–18) were issued in the order of composition. However, Nocturnes 19 and 20 were actually written first, before Chopin left Poland, and were published after his death. Nocturne No. 21 was not originally titled "nocturne" but has been included in the standard set since its publication in 1938. A spurious work, the Nocturne in C-sharp minor (sometimes called Nocturne No. 22), exists but is not considered authentic.

## Terms
*   **Nocturne:** A short musical composition, typically for piano, that is inspired by or evokes the night.
*   **Bel canto:** An Italian operatic style emphasizing beautiful, lyrical singing and elegant vocal lines.
*   **Counterpoint:** The relationship between two or more independent melodic lines sounding simultaneously.
*   **Sustain pedal:** The rightmost pedal on a piano, which lifts the dampers from the strings, allowing notes to ring out and blend.
*   **Sonata form:** A musical structure typically consisting of an exposition, development, and recapitulation, commonly used in the first movement of a sonata.
*   **Aria:** A self-contained piece for one voice, with or without orchestral accompaniment, typically part of a larger work like an opera.

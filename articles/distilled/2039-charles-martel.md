# Charles Martel

## Overview
Charles Martel (c. 688–741), nicknamed "The Hammer," was a Frankish political and military leader who, as Mayor of the Palace, was the de facto ruler of the Franks from 718 until his death. He restored centralized Frankish authority, won a pivotal victory against an Umayyad invasion at the Battle of Tours (732), and is traditionally credited with laying the groundwork for the Carolingian dynasty, which would produce his grandson Charlemagne.

## Timeline
- **c. 688** — Born to Pepin of Herstal and Alpaida.
- **December 714** — Death of Pepin of Herstal; civil war begins.
- **715** — Escapes imprisonment; acclaimed Mayor of the Palace in Austrasia.
- **716** — Defeated at the Battle of Cologne (his only career defeat).
- **April 716** — Wins the Battle of Amblève, a turning point.
- **21 March 717** — Defeats Neustrians at the Battle of Vinchy.
- **718** — Defeats alliance of King Chilperic II and Duke Odo at the Battle of Soissons.
- **732** — Wins the Battle of Tours against an Umayyad army.
- **734** — Subdues Frisia after the Battle of the Boarn.
- **737** — Merovingian king Theuderic IV dies; throne left vacant.
- **22 October 741** — Dies at Quierzy-sur-Oise; realm divided among his sons.

## Body

### Rise to Power and Civil War
Charles was the son of Pepin of Herstal, the powerful Mayor of the Palace. After Pepin's death in 714, a civil war erupted between his designated heir (a child, Theudoald) and the Neustrian nobles. Charles, initially imprisoned by his stepmother Plectrude, escaped and rallied support. After a defeat at Cologne, he won a decisive victory at the Battle of Amblève (716), which marked the turning point in his struggle. He subsequently defeated the Neustrian forces at Vinchy (717) and Soissons (718), securing his position as the undisputed Mayor of the Palace for all Frankish kingdoms.

### Consolidation and Expansion
From 718 to 732, Charles consolidated power through military campaigns. He campaigned against the Saxons to secure the northern border, seized West Frisia, and intervened repeatedly in Bavaria and Alemannia to reassert Frankish suzerainty over these increasingly independent duchies. He appointed and deposed Merovingian kings, including Chlothar IV and Theuderic IV, demonstrating his control over the monarchy.

### The Battle of Tours and Later Campaigns
In 731, Charles attacked Aquitaine, breaking a treaty with its duke, Odo. This conflict may have prompted Odo to seek aid from the Umayyad Emirate of al-Andalus. An Umayyad army under Abd al-Rahman al-Ghafiqi advanced north, possibly on a raid, and was defeated by Charles at the Battle of Tours (732). While its strategic importance is debated, the victory enhanced Charles's prestige. He later reorganized Burgundy, fully conquered Frisia, and dealt with revolts in Provence.

### Final Years and Legacy
In 737, the Merovingian throne became vacant, and Charles ruled without a king. He used the ensuing peace to integrate outlying regions into the Frankish church, establishing dioceses in Bavaria and supporting the missionary Boniface. He died in 741, having divided his realm between his sons Carloman and Pepin the Younger. Pepin would later become the first king of the Carolingian dynasty, and his son Charlemagne would extend the Frankish empire into an empire.

## Terms
*   **Mayor of the Palace:** The chief minister and de facto ruler in the Merovingian Frankish kingdoms, controlling the royal treasury, patronage, and land grants.
*   **Merovingians:** The ruling dynasty of the Franks from the 5th to the 8th century, who by Charles's time had become figurehead kings (*rois fainéants*).
*   **Austrasia:** The northeastern Frankish kingdom, the traditional power base of the Pippinid/Mayors of the Palace.
*   **Neustria:** The northwestern Frankish kingdom, often in political conflict with Austrasia.
*   **Suzerainty:** The exercise of sovereign authority over a subordinate state or region.
*   **Interregnum:** A period when a throne is vacant.
*   **Carolingian Dynasty:** The dynasty founded by Charles Martel's son Pepin the Younger, which included Charlemagne.
*   **Umayyad Emirate of al-Andalus:** The Muslim-ruled state in the Iberian Peninsula from which the army at Tours originated.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Legitimacy:** The accusation that Charles was "illegitimate" is considered by modern historians to be a later political smear, as the distinction between wives and concubines was unclear in 8th-century Francia.
*   **Significance of the Battle of Tours:** Historians debate its importance. Some, like Edward Gibbon, view it as a pivotal moment that saved Christian Europe from Islamic conquest. Others, like Alessandro Barbero, argue its significance is overstated, suggesting the Umayyad force was on a raid, not a campaign of conquest, and that the battle was primarily an episode in Frankish struggles for control of Aquitaine.
*   **Military Reforms:** The theory that Charles confiscated church lands to fund a revolutionary military reform (creating a heavy cavalry) is contested. Historian Paul Fouracre argues there is insufficient evidence for a decisive change in Frankish military organization or tactics during his reign.
*   **Historical Memory:** Charles's legacy is politically charged. He has been appropriated by modern far-right groups, while his historical role remains a topic of debate in contemporary French politics.

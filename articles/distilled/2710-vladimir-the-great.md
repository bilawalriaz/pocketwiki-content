# Vladimir the Great

## Overview
Vladimir I Sviatoslavich (c. 958–1015), known as "the Great," was a Grand Prince of Kiev who consolidated Kievan Rus' and, most significantly, Christianized it in 988. His conversion from paganism to Eastern Orthodox Christianity was a pivotal event that shaped the religious, cultural, and political identity of Eastern Europe for centuries.

## Timeline
- **c. 958** — Born as the illegitimate youngest son of Sviatoslav I.
- **970** — Appointed Prince of Novgorod by his father.
- **972** — Flees abroad after his brother Yaropolk murders their brother Oleg.
- **978** — Returns with a Varangian army, deposes and kills Yaropolk, becomes Grand Prince of Kiev.
- **980–985** — Expands territory, suppresses rebellions, and campaigns against neighboring tribes.
- **987** — Sends envoys to study major religions; agrees to aid Byzantine Emperor Basil II.
- **988** — Baptized at Chersonesus; marries Byzantine princess Anna; begins Christianization of Rus'.
- **989** — Destroys pagan monuments and establishes churches, including the Church of the Tithes.
- **991** — Founds the city of Belgorod.
- **1014** — His son Yaroslav stops paying tribute; Vladimir prepares to attack but falls ill.
- **15 July 1015** — Dies at Berestove; his body is dismembered and relics distributed to churches.

## Body

### Rise to Power and Consolidation
Vladimir was the youngest, illegitimate son of Sviatoslav I. After his father's death in 972, a fratricidal war erupted. Vladimir was forced to flee but returned in 978 with a Varangian army. On his way to Kiev, he captured Polotsk by force after its princess, Rogneda, rejected his marriage proposal. He then seized Kiev, killing his brother Yaropolk, and became ruler of all Kievan Rus'. By 980, he had consolidated his realm to the Baltic Sea and secured its frontiers.

### Pagan Rule and Religious Exploration
As a pagan prince, Vladimir expanded his territories through military campaigns. He erected a major pagan temple in Kiev dedicated to six gods, including Perun and Slavic deities, in an attempt to unify the diverse pagan beliefs of his subjects. However, the persecution and killing of Christians, such as Fyodor and his son Ioann, prompted him to consider a state religion. According to the *Primary Chronicle*, he sent envoys to study Islam, Latin Christianity, Judaism, and Byzantine Christianity, with his emissaries being most impressed by the service in Constantinople.

### Conversion and Christianization
Vladimir's conversion in 988 is linked to a marriage alliance with the Byzantine Empire. After capturing the Crimean town of Chersonesus, he negotiated to marry Emperor Basil II's sister, Anna. He was baptized, taking the Christian name Basil, and married her. Arab sources provide an alternative account, stating Vladimir agreed to convert and send 6,000 troops to help Basil II suppress a revolt in exchange for the marriage. Returning to Kiev, Vladimir destroyed pagan idols and established churches, initiating the Christianization of Kievan Rus'.

### Christian Reign and Legacy
Following his conversion, Vladimir introduced Byzantine law but reformed its harsher elements, abolishing capital punishment and torture. He promoted literacy, established schools, and engaged in charity. He minted coins and regulated foreign trade. His reign ended in conflict with his son Yaroslav. Vladimir is venerated as a saint by both the Eastern Orthodox and Roman Catholic Churches. He is a national symbol in Belarus, Russia, and Ukraine, and his conversion marks the transition from the Varangian to the Christian period in Eastern Slavic history.

## Terms
*   **Knyaz:** A Slavic title for a prince or ruler.
*   **Druzhina:** The prince's military retinue or personal guard.
*   **Varangians:** Norsemen, particularly from Scandinavia, who served as warriors, traders, and rulers in Eastern Europe.
*   **Pechenegs:** A semi-nomadic Turkic people who were frequent adversaries of Kievan Rus'.
*   **Boyars:** The highest rank of the feudal aristocracy in Kievan Rus', serving as the prince's council.
*   **Primary Chronicle (Tale of Bygone Years):** A foundational historical text of Kievan Rus', covering events up to 1110.
*   **Christianization:** The process of converting a population or state to Christianity.
*   **Relics:** The physical remains of a saint or venerated person, believed to have spiritual power.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **The Narrative of Conversion:** The canonical story from the *Primary Chronicle* is barely mentioned in contemporary Byzantine sources. Historians suggest the detailed account may be a later product (c. 1040s) created to elevate the status of the Rus' church as independent and equal to Constantinople's.
*   **Motivations for Conversion:** Scholars debate whether Vladimir's conversion was primarily a political move to secure a marriage alliance and military support from Byzantium, a genuine religious conviction, or a strategic tool to unify his diverse realm under a single faith.
*   **Historiographical Contention:** The appropriation of Kievan Rus' as part of national history is a topic of contention between Ukrainophile and Russophile schools of historiography, with Vladimir claimed as a foundational figure by modern Belarus, Russia, and Ukraine.

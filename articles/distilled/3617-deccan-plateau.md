# Deccan Plateau

## Overview
The Deccan Plateau is a vast, ancient tableland covering 422,000 km² in southern India, forming the core of the Indian peninsula. It is geologically significant for its volcanic origins and has been a cradle of major Indian kingdoms and cultures, shaping the subcontinent's history, ecology, and demographics.

## Timeline
- **c. 8000 BCE** — Ash mounds associated with Neolithic cultures date to this period.
- **c. 1000 BCE** — Iron technology spreads through the region.
- **66 mya** — Massive volcanic eruption creates the Deccan Traps at the end of the Cretaceous period.
- **6th century BCE – 14th century CE** — Rule by various dynasties including Satavahanas, Chalukyas, Rashtrakutas, and Hoysalas.
- **Late Middle Ages** — Vijayanagara Empire rules the south; Bahmani Kingdom and Deccan Sultanates rule the north.
- **15th century CE** — Europeans arrive; French and British later vie for control.
- **Early 18th century CE** — Maratha Empire briefly captures the region.
- **Late 18th century CE** — British East India Company consolidates power after defeating the Mysore Kingdom.
- **1857** — British Empire takes direct control from the East India Company.
- **1947** — Indian independence; region organized into Bombay, Hyderabad, Madras, and Mysore States.
- **1950s** — States reorganized on linguistic lines, creating modern states like Maharashtra and Karnataka.
- **2014** — Telangana is created by bifurcating Andhra Pradesh.

## Body

### Geology and Formation
The Deccan Plateau is one of the oldest and most stable landforms in India, with an average elevation of about 600 m (2,000 ft). Its defining feature is the Deccan Traps, formed by multiple layers of basaltic lava from a massive volcanic eruption at the end of the Cretaceous period (66 mya). This event, the second-largest volcanic eruption ever recorded on land, released ash and gases that may have contributed to dinosaur extinction. The lava deposits, over 2 km thick, cover more than 500,000 km². Underlying the traps are ancient granite and sedimentary rocks from the Precambrian era and the formation of Gondwanaland.

### Geography and Climate
The plateau is bounded by the Western and Eastern Ghats, which separate it from the coastal plains. It slopes gently west to east, causing major rivers like the Godavari, Krishna, and Kaveri to flow eastward to the Bay of Bengal. The Western Ghats block rain-bearing winds, creating a semi-arid rain shadow region in the plateau's interior. The climate is largely semi-arid in the north and tropical elsewhere, with hot, dry summers and a monsoon season from July to September.

### History and Political Control
The region has a long history of rule by powerful kingdoms, including the Pallavas, Cholas, Satavahanas, and Vijayanagara Empire. In the medieval period, the Bahmani Kingdom and its successor Deccan Sultanates controlled the north. European powers arrived in the 15th century, leading to British colonial dominance by the 19th century. After independence in 1947, the region was reorganized into modern Indian states based on language.

### Demographics and Economy
The largest linguistic groups are Dravidian (Telugu, Kannada, Tamil) and Indo-Aryan (Marathi). Agriculture is the primary occupation, with rice as the staple crop, though irrigation is needed in dry areas. Urban centers like Bengaluru and Hyderabad are major hubs for the Information Technology industry, driving economic growth.

### Culture
The region features diverse traditions, including distinct clothing (sari, dhoti), cuisine (rice-based, with regional variations like Hyderabadi biryani), and performing arts (Kuchipudi, Bharatanatyam). Temple architecture varies by style: Dravidian (south), Vesara (hybrid), and Nagara (north).

## Terms
- **Deccan Traps:** A large igneous province of multiple basaltic lava layers formed by volcanic activity 66 million years ago.
- **Gondwanaland:** An ancient supercontinent that included the Indian subcontinent before it collided with Asia.
- **Rain Shadow:** A dry area on the leeward (downwind) side of a mountain range, where the mountains block precipitation.
- **Orographic Precipitation:** Rain or snow caused by air being forced to rise over a mountain range.
- **Linguistic Reorganization:** The 1950s process of redrawing Indian state boundaries based on dominant languages.
- **Perennial River:** A river that flows year-round, typically fed by consistent rainfall or groundwater.
- **Gopuram:** A large, ornate entrance tower, a hallmark of Dravidian temple architecture.
- **Garbhagriha:** The innermost sanctum of a Hindu temple, housing the primary deity.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The exact contribution of the Deccan Traps eruption to the Cretaceous–Paleogene extinction event (including dinosaur extinction) is debated among scientists.
- Historians and geographers have used varying definitions of the Deccan's boundaries, sometimes based on linguistic lines, political borders, or physical features like the Tapti or Godavari rivers.

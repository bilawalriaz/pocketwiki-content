# Hyderabad, Pakistan

## Overview
Hyderabad is the second-largest city in Pakistan's Sindh province, serving as the capital of the Hyderabad Division. Founded in 1768, it was a major historical and commercial center on the Indus River before the British shifted the provincial capital to Bombay in 1840. Its modern identity is shaped by post-Partition demographic shifts and ethnic tensions, while its economy remains a key industrial and agricultural hub.

## Body

### Founding and Early Dynasties
Hyderabad was founded in 1768 by Mian Ghulam Shah Kalhoro on the eastern bank of the Indus River, on a limestone ridge known as Ganjo Takkar ("Bald Hill"). The move was prompted by frequent floods at the previous Kalhora capital, Khudabad. The city was named in honor of Ali, the fourth caliph, meaning "Lion City." A fort, Pacco Qillo ("Strong Fort"), was built in 1769. After the Kalhoras, the Talpur dynasty captured the city in 1775 and made it their capital in 1789, further developing its fort and attracting artisans and traders, which made it a major regional center.

### British Colonial Period
The British defeated the Talpurs at the Battle of Hyderabad in 1843 and transferred the capital of Sindh to Bombay Presidency in 1840. Despite this, Hyderabad's economy initially prospered through new trade networks. Merchants exported "Sindwork" handicrafts to Bombay, Egypt, and as far as Japan. The British built infrastructure including a jail (1851), municipality (1853), schools, and a rail network connecting Hyderabad to Karachi via the Kotri Bridge (1900). By 1901, the population was 69,378, with Hindus forming the largest religious group.

### Modern Demographics and Ethnic Tensions
The 1947 Partition of India caused a massive demographic shift. Most of the city's Hindu population, which had been the majority, migrated to India. They were replaced by Urdu-speaking Muslim refugees (Muhajirs) from India, who made up 66% of the population by 1951. This created a new ethnic dynamic. Tensions between Sindhi and Muhajir communities escalated, leading to violent riots in 1971, 1972, and severe ethnic violence in the late 1980s, including the "Hyderabad Massacre" of 1988. The city became ethnically segregated, with Muhajirs concentrating in Latifabad and Sindhis in Qasimabad.

### Economy and Industry
Hyderabad is a major industrial and commercial center. The Sindh Industrial Trading Estate, established in 1950, hosts 439 industrial units. Key industries include textiles, sugar, cement, and manufacturing. The city is particularly famous for producing almost all of Pakistan's ornamental glass bangles, an industry employing an estimated 300,000-350,000 people. It is also a commercial hub for agricultural produce from the surrounding fertile plains.

## Terms
- **Kalhora Dynasty**: The ruling dynasty of Sindh that founded Hyderabad in 1768.
- **Talpur Dynasty**: The dynasty that succeeded the Kalhoras, ruling Hyderabad from 1789 until the British conquest in 1843.
- **Pacco Qillo**: The "Strong Fort" built in 1769, which served as a royal residence and later a British arsenal.
- **Muhajirs**: Urdu-speaking Muslims who migrated from India to Pakistan after the 1947 Partition.
- **Sindwork**: A style of handicrafts produced in Hyderabad and exported globally during the British colonial era.
- **Ganjo Takkar**: The "Bald Hill," a limestone ridge on which Hyderabad was founded.
- **Bhaiband**: A mercantile segment of the Lohana Hindu caste that dominated trade in colonial Hyderabad.
- **Purdah**: The practice of seclusion, for which wealthy homes used large windows as a status marker.

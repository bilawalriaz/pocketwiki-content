# Mosul

## Overview
Mosul is a major city in northern Iraq and the capital of Nineveh Governorate, situated on the Tigris River. It is Iraq's second-largest city and has historically served as a strategic commercial and cultural hub due to its central location. The city is renowned for its ancient Assyrian heritage, with the ruins of Nineveh on its eastern side, and its diverse population of Arabs, Kurds, Assyrians, and other groups.

## Timeline
- **c. 6000 BC** — The ancient city of Nineveh is settled.
- **c. 700 BC** — King Sennacherib makes Nineveh the capital of the Neo-Assyrian Empire.
- **612 BC** — Nineveh falls to a coalition of Medes and Babylonians, ending the Assyrian Empire.
- **637/641 AD** — Mosul is annexed to the Rashidun Caliphate.
- **1127 AD** — Mosul becomes the center of the Zengid dynasty.
- **1262 AD** — The Mongols destroy Mosul after a nine-month siege.
- **1538 AD** — Ottoman Sultan Suleiman the Magnificent captures Mosul from Safavid Persia.
- **1918 AD** — British forces occupy Mosul after World War I.
- **1926 AD** — The League of Nations confirms Iraq's possession of Mosul.
- **2003 AD** — Mosul falls to U.S. and Kurdish forces during the Iraq War.
- **2014 AD** — The Islamic State (IS) captures Mosul.
- **2017 AD** — Iraqi forces, with international support, fully recapture Mosul after a major battle.

## Body

### Ancient and Medieval History
The area has been part of Assyria since the 25th century BC. The nearby city of Nineveh became the magnificent capital of the Neo-Assyrian Empire around 700 BC, only to be destroyed in 612 BC. The settlement of Mosul (possibly on the site of Xenophon's "Mépsila" from 41 BC) later succeeded Nineveh as a key Tigris crossing point. It was annexed by the Rashidun Caliphate in the 7th century, initiating a period of Arab, Kurdish, and Turkic influx. Mosul flourished under dynasties like the Zengids and the atabeg Badr al-Din Lu'lu', but was devastated by the Mongols in 1262.

### The Mosul School of Metalwork
In the 13th century, Mosul became a renowned center for luxury brass items inlaid with silver. This "Mosul School" peaked between 1220 and 1275, with artisans like Ibrahim ibn Mawaliya pioneering techniques. The craft featured distinct workshop marks, such as an octagon symbol, and recorded master-apprentice relationships. After the Mongol invasion, Mosul craftsmen emigrated, influencing metalwork in Mamluk Syria and Egypt. The school's legacy is debated; some scholars argue only a few items are definitively from Mosul, while others defend a distinct local tradition.

### Ottoman and Modern Period
The Ottomans captured Mosul in 1538, governing it through local notables like the Jalili family until the 19th century. After World War I, Mosul became part of British Mandate Iraq, with its borders confirmed by the League of Nations in 1926. The discovery of oil in the late 1920s boosted its economy. Under Saddam Hussein, the city was a military and Ba'athist stronghold, but its demographics were altered by "Arabisation" policies. The 2003 invasion led to instability, and in 2014, IS captured the city, causing a humanitarian crisis and destroying much of its heritage. A major battle to retake Mosul ended in 2017, leaving the city heavily damaged.

### Demographics and Culture
Mosul has a Sunni Arab majority, with significant Kurdish, Assyrian, Turkmen, and Yazidi minorities. It is a historic center for Assyrians, though their population has declined due to conflict and persecution. The city is home to the University of Mosul and numerous historical sites, including the Great Mosque of al-Nuri (with its leaning minaret) and ancient churches. Much of this heritage was destroyed by IS, including the Prophet Jonah's Mosque and St. Elijah's Monastery.

## Terms
- **Neo-Assyrian Empire**: The final and most powerful phase of the Assyrian Empire (911–605 BC), which made Nineveh its capital.
- **Zengid dynasty**: A Muslim dynasty that ruled Mosul and other parts of Upper Mesopotamia in the 12th–13th centuries.
- **Inlaid brasswork**: A metalworking technique where silver is set into engraved brass to create decorative patterns, perfected in Mosul.
- **Mosul School**: A term for the distinct tradition of metalworking and miniature painting associated with 13th-century Mosul.
- **Arabisation**: A Ba'athist policy of altering demographics by settling Arabs in traditionally non-Arab areas like Nineveh.
- **Islamic State (IS)**: A jihadist group that controlled Mosul from 2014 to 2017, known for destroying cultural heritage.
- **Nineveh Governorate**: The administrative region of Iraq for which Mosul serves as the capital.

## Debates and Open Questions
Scholars debate the authenticity and origin of many artifacts attributed to the "Mosul School" of metalwork, with some arguing that only a small number of items can be definitively linked to the city. The exact site of Xenophon's ancient town "Mépsila" is also uncertain, with some identifying it with modern Mosul and others with a location further north. The extent to which the city's social fabric was permanently altered by the rise of Salafism under Saddam Hussein and the subsequent IS occupation remains a critical question for its future.

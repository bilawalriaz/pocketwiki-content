# Rajaraja I

## Overview
Rajaraja I (947–1014), also known as Rajaraja the Great, was a Chola emperor whose reign from 985 to 1014 transformed the Chola kingdom into a vast, well-organized empire. He is celebrated for his extensive military conquests across southern India and Sri Lanka, his powerful navy that projected Chola influence across the Indian Ocean, and his significant cultural and administrative reforms, including the construction of the Brihadisvara Temple.

## Timeline
- **3 November 947** — Birth of Arulmozhi Varman (Rajaraja I).
- **Mid-985** — Ascends the Chola throne after the death of Uttama Chola.
- **c. 988** — Early victory at Kandalur Salai in present-day Kerala.
- **993** — Conquest of the Anuradhapura kingdom in northern Sri Lanka.
- **998–1004** — Campaigns annex Gangapadi, Nolambapadi, and Tadigaipadi in Karnataka.
- **1000** — Initiates a major land survey and assessment, reorganizing the empire into *valanadus*.
- **1003–1004** — Defeats the Western Chalukyas in the Battle of Annigeri.
- **1004** — Conquers the Gangavadi province and defeats a Chalukyan invasion.
- **1006** — Marries his daughter Kundavai to the Eastern Chalukya prince Vimaladitya, cementing an alliance over Vengi.
- **1010** — Completes and dedicates the Brihadisvara (Peruvudaiyar) Temple in Thanjavur.
- **1014** — Rajaraja I dies and is succeeded by his son, Rajendra Chola I.

## Body

### Early Life and Accession
Rajaraja was born Arulmozhi Varman, son of Chola king Parantaka II (Sundara Chola). His accession ended a period of competing claims to the throne following the reign of his great-grandfather, Parantaka I. After a complex succession involving his uncle Uttama Chola, Rajaraja ascended the throne in mid-985, inheriting a kingdom centered on Thanjavur–Tiruchirappalli that was recovering from earlier attacks.

### Military Conquests and Empire Building
Rajaraja transformed the Chola kingdom into a powerful empire through relentless campaigns. He conquered the Pandya and Chera territories in the south, assuming the title *Mummudi Chola* ("Three Crowned") to symbolize his rule over the three major Tamil kingdoms. In 993, he exploited a civil war in Sri Lanka to conquer the Anuradhapura kingdom, establishing a provincial capital at Polonnaruwa. His northern campaigns brought him into conflict with the Western Chalukyas, whom he defeated in battles like Annigeri and Gangavadi, extending Chola authority to the Tungabhadra River. He also secured control over the strategic Vengi kingdom through military victory and a dynastic marriage alliance. His powerful navy secured the Maldives and coastal regions, controlling vital Indian Ocean trade routes.

### Administration and Cultural Patronage
Rajaraja implemented significant administrative reforms. In 1000, he ordered a comprehensive land survey and assessment, reorganizing the empire into administrative units called *valanadus*. This replaced hereditary local rulers with dependent officials, allowing for closer royal control. He strengthened local self-governments while instituting a system of audit and accountability. Culturally, he is famed for commissioning the Brihadisvara Temple in Thanjavur, a masterpiece of Dravidian architecture. He also patronized the recovery and compilation of Shaivite hymns into the *Thirumurai*, earning him the title *Thirumurai Kanda Cholar* ("The One Who Found Thirumurai"). He sent the first Chola diplomatic mission to China to promote trade.

## Terms
- **Valanadu**: An administrative unit into which the Chola empire was reorganized following Rajaraja I's land survey and assessment project in 1000 CE.
- **Mummudi Chola**: A title meaning "Three Crowned," adopted by Rajaraja I to symbolize his sovereignty over the Chola, Pandya, and Chera kingdoms.
- **Thirumurai**: The compiled collection of Shaivite Tamil hymns, whose first seven books (the *Tevaram*) were recovered and organized during Rajaraja's reign.
- **Brihadisvara Temple**: Also known as Peruvudaiyar Temple, the massive Shiva temple built by Rajaraja I in Thanjavur and completed in 1010 CE.
- **Anuradhapura**: The ancient capital of the Sinhalese kingdom in Sri Lanka, conquered by Rajaraja I in 993 CE.
- **Vengi**: A strategically important kingdom on the eastern coast of India, brought under Chola influence through Rajaraja's military campaigns and a marriage alliance.
- **Western Chalukyas**: A major Deccan dynasty that came into direct conflict with the expanding Chola Empire under Rajaraja I.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The identity of the Pandya royal figure Amarabhujanga, captured during Rajaraja's southern campaigns, is debated: he may have been a Pandya prince, a Pandya general, or a prince of the Kongu Chera dynasty.
- The exact nature of the conflict at Kandalur Salai is uncertain, including whether it primarily involved Chera or Pandya forces.
- Historians debate whether the personal animosity between Rajaraja I and the Western Chalukya king Satyashraya was a primary driver of their conflicts, similar to earlier Chalukya-Pallava rivalries.

# Riyadh

## Overview
Riyadh is the capital and largest city of Saudi Arabia, serving as the nation's political, administrative, and economic center. Its modern form emerged from an 18th-century walled oasis town, expanding dramatically in the 20th century following the discovery of oil and the establishment of the Saudi kingdom. Today, it is a major global metropolis and a focal point of the country's Vision 2030 development plan.

## Body

### Historical Development
The area was known as Hajr in the pre-Islamic era and served as a regional capital. The name Riyadh, meaning "gardens," refers to the oasis towns that were enclosed by a wall built in 1737 by Dahham ibn Dawwas. The city became the capital of the First Saudi State in the late 18th century but declined after its fall in 1818. It was re-established as the capital of the Second Saudi State in 1825. After a period of Rashidi control, Ibn Saud captured the city in 1902, eventually founding the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia in 1932 with Riyadh as its capital.

### Urban Expansion and Planning
Until the 1930s, Riyadh was a small, walled city. Expansion began with the construction of new administrative buildings. Following the start of commercial oil production in the 1940s, the city grew rapidly. A grid pattern was introduced in 1953, influenced by American city planning. Major master plans by Doxiadis Associates (1972) and SCET International (1982) guided growth, though both underestimated the city's explosive population increase. The modern strategy, MEDSTAR, focuses on managing growth and creating a polycentric city with multiple business hubs.

### Economy and Governance
Riyadh is the political heart of Saudi Arabia, hosting the king, Consultative Assembly, Council of Ministers, and most government ministries. It is also the economic hub, with headquarters for major Saudi companies like SABIC and Almarai, and regional headquarters for over 500 international firms. The city's economy has diversified from its agricultural origins to manufacturing and finance, with the King Abdullah Financial District as a key development. Vision 2030 projects aim to further boost tourism and economic diversification.

### Demographics and Culture
Riyadh's population grew from 40,000 in 1935 to over 7 million in 2022, driven by economic opportunity and immigration. The population is approximately 64% Saudi, with Indians and Pakistanis as the largest expatriate groups. The city is a cultural center, home to the National Museum and the annual Jenadriyah festival. It hosts major sports events and is developing a significant esports scene, including the Esports World Cup.

## Terms
*   **Wadi Hanifa:** A valley on whose eastern bank Riyadh is located.
*   **Najd:** The central plateau of the Arabian Peninsula where Riyadh is situated.
*   **First Saudi State:** An Islamic state founded in 1744 by an alliance between Muhammad ibn Abd al-Wahhab and Muhammad bin Saud, with Diriyah as its capital.
*   **Second Saudi State:** Restored by Turki ibn Abdullah in the early 19th century, with Riyadh as its capital from 1825.
*   **Vision 2030:** Saudi Arabia's strategic framework to reduce oil dependence and diversify its economy.
*   **Diplomatic Quarter:** The area in western Riyadh where most foreign embassies are located.
*   **At-Turaif Palace:** A UNESCO World Heritage Site in Diriyah, the original home of the House of Saud.
*   **MEDSTAR:** The metropolitan development strategy for Riyadh adopted in the 2000s to manage urban growth.

## Debates and Open Questions
The source notes that the exact origin of the name "Riyadh" is debated, with the best-known reference coming from the 18th-century period of Dahham ibn Dawwas. Urban planning documents consistently underestimated Riyadh's growth rate, raising questions about the long-term efficacy of fixed master plans versus adaptive strategies. The World Bank has warned of a "looming poverty crisis" if the capital's economy does not further diversify from oil dependence.

# Toulouse

## Overview
Toulouse is the fourth-largest city in France and the capital of the Occitania region. It is a major European hub for the aerospace industry and a historic center of Occitan culture, with a heritage spanning from Roman times to the present day.

## Timeline
- **2nd century BC** — Roman military outpost Tolosa founded; later refounded on the Garonne.
- **5th century** — Capital of the Visigothic Kingdom; fell to the Franks in 507.
- **1229** — Treaty of Paris: County of Toulouse submits to the French Crown; University of Toulouse founded.
- **1271** — County annexed to the royal domain after the death of its last count.
- **1443** — Second Parlement of France established in Toulouse.
- **1666–1681** — Construction of the Canal du Midi linking Toulouse to the Mediterranean.
- **1814** — Last Franco-British battle on French soil (Battle of Toulouse).
- **1963** — Designated a French "balancing Metropolis," boosting aerospace industry.
- **2001** — AZF factory explosion causes 31 deaths.
- **2016** — Becomes the prefecture of the new Occitania region.

## Body

### Historical Development
Founded by Romans as Tolosa, the city became a Visigothic capital before falling to the Franks. It was the center of the independent County of Toulouse until the Albigensian Crusade against Catharism led to its submission to the French Crown in 1229. This period saw the founding of the University of Toulouse (1229) and the Dominican Order (1215). As part of France, it became a judicial capital with a powerful Parlement and prospered from the woad trade. The 17th-century Canal du Midi boosted its economy. The city's modern growth was driven by its selection as an aerospace hub in 1963.

### Architecture and Heritage
Toulouse is known as "La Ville rose" (The Pink City) for its distinctive pink terracotta brick architecture. Key landmarks include the Basilica of St. Sernin, a major Romanesque pilgrimage church and UNESCO site. The city developed a distinct "Southern French Gothic" style, exemplified by the Cathedral of Saint-Étienne and the Convent of the Jacobins, characterized by austere exteriors and vast single naves for preaching. The Renaissance brought wealthy mansions (hôtels particuliers), while the 18th century saw the construction of the Canal du Midi (UNESCO site) and the Capitole facade.

### Economy and Aerospace
Toulouse is the center of the European aerospace industry, hosting the headquarters of Airbus, ATR, and the French Space Agency's (CNES) main space center. The aerospace sector, along with high-tech research, drives the city's economy and rapid population growth. The Toulouse metropolitan area is one of the fastest-growing in France.

### Education and Culture
The University of Toulouse, founded in 1229, is one of Europe's oldest. The city is a major student center with numerous prestigious engineering and business schools. It is the seat of the Académie des Jeux Floraux, a guardian of Occitan literary culture. Cultural institutions include the Théâtre du Capitole (opera/ballet) and several museums. In 2023, Toulouse was named a UNESCO City of Music.

## Terms
- **Capitouls**: The historical municipal council of notables who governed Toulouse from the 12th century until the French Revolution.
- **Occitania**: The cultural region of southern France where Occitan languages were historically spoken; Toulouse is its unofficial capital.
- **Southern French Gothic**: An austere architectural style from the 13th century, emphasizing large single naves for preaching, developed in response to Catharism.
- **Foraine brick**: The large, flat, pinkish-orange brick traditionally used in Toulouse's architecture.
- **Canal du Midi**: A 17th-century canal linking Toulouse to the Mediterranean Sea, a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
- **Aéropostale**: A pioneering airmail company based in Toulouse in the 1920s, famous for its pilots like Antoine de Saint-Exupéry.
- **Catharism**: A dualistic Christian heresy that flourished in southern France in the 12th–13th centuries, leading to the Albigensian Crusade.
- **Parlement of Toulouse**: The second-highest court of appeal in the Kingdom of France, established in 1443, which made Toulouse a judicial capital.

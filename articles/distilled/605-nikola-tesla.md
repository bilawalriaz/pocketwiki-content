# Nikola Tesla

## Overview

Nikola Tesla (1856–1943) was a Serbian-American engineer and inventor whose alternating current (AC) system became the foundation of modern electrical power distribution. His polyphase AC induction motor and related patents, licensed by Westinghouse in 1888, won the "War of Currents" against Edison's direct current (DC) system and enabled large-scale electricity generation and transmission. Beyond power systems, Tesla pioneered wireless communication, radio remote control, X-ray imaging, and high-frequency phenomena, though many later projects—including the Wardenclyffe Tower—ended in financial failure. His legacy was revived posthumously when the SI unit of magnetic flux density was named the tesla in 1960.

## Body

### Early Life and Education

Tesla was born into an ethnic Serb family in the Military Frontier of the Austrian Empire. His father, a priest, and his mother, a skilled craftsman with an eidetic memory, influenced his early intellectual development. Tesla showed exceptional ability in mathematics and physics, performing integral calculus mentally, which led teachers to suspect cheating. He contracted cholera in 1873, an illness that prompted his father to promise him engineering education if he recovered. Tesla enrolled at the Imperial-Royal Technical College in Graz in 1875 but left in 1878 without graduating, possibly due to gambling and womanizing.

### Career Beginnings: Edison and Continental Edison

In 1882, Tesla joined the Continental Edison Company in Paris, gaining practical experience in electrical engineering by installing incandescent lighting systems. In 1884, he emigrated to the U.S. and worked briefly at the Edison Machine Works in New York. Tensions arose over unpaid bonuses, including a disputed $50,000 offer for redesigning generators. Tesla left after six months, citing broken promises and lack of recognition.

### AC System and the Induction Motor

In 1887, Tesla partnered with Alfred Brown and Charles Peck to form the Tesla Electric Company. That year, he developed his AC induction motor, which used polyphase current to generate a rotating magnetic field—a principle he claimed to have conceived in 1882. Patented in May 1888, the motor was self-starting and avoided commutators, reducing sparking and maintenance. Westinghouse Electric licensed Tesla’s patents in July 1888 for $60,000 plus royalties, hiring him as a consultant. This partnership became central to the adoption of AC power systems.

### Wireless Power and High-Voltage Experiments

From the 1890s onward, Tesla pursued wireless power transmission and communication. Inspired by Hertz’s radio wave experiments, he developed the Tesla coil—an oscillating transformer producing high-voltage, high-frequency AC. He demonstrated wireless lighting using inductive and capacitive coupling, claiming it could transmit power globally. In 1899, he conducted large-scale experiments in Colorado Springs, generating artificial lightning and observing anomalous radio signals he speculated might be of extraterrestrial origin.

### Wardenclyffe Tower and Financial Collapse

In 1901, J.P. Morgan funded Tesla’s Wardenclyffe Tower project on Long Island, intended for transatlantic wireless communication and power transmission. However, Marconi’s successful transatlantic radio transmission in 1901 undermined investor confidence. The project stalled by 1905, and Tesla mortgaged the property to cover debts. He never completed the tower, marking the end of his major industrial ventures.

### Later Years and Decline

After Wardenclyffe, Tesla lived in New York hotels, often in debt. He continued proposing inventions—including a bladeless turbine, a “death ray” (Teleforce), and cosmic ray energy devices—but few were commercially viable. He gave annual birthday press conferences, sharing increasingly speculative ideas. At his 1935 birthday party, Tesla claimed his mechanical oscillator had caused an earthquake in his East Houston Street lab in 1898 and described a concept he called "telegeodynamics" for transmitting vibrations through the ground for communication and mineral detection. In 1937, he received the Order of the White Lion from the Czechoslovak ambassador. Tesla died alone in New York City on 7 January 1943 at age 86. His estate was seized by the FBI on 9 January 1943 due to wartime concerns about his papers and purported beam weapon plans, but John G. Trump’s analysis found nothing hazardous. New York City Mayor Fiorello La Guardia delivered a eulogy at Tesla’s funeral at the Cathedral of St. John the Divine.

## Terms

- **Alternating Current (AC):** Electric current that periodically reverses direction, enabling efficient long-distance power transmission.
- **Polyphase System:** An electrical system using multiple alternating currents of the same frequency but different phases to generate rotating magnetic fields.
- **Induction Motor:** A type of AC motor that uses electromagnetic induction to produce torque without electrical contacts.
- **Tesla Coil:** A resonant transformer circuit producing high-voltage, high-frequency alternating current.
- **Rotating Magnetic Field:** A magnetic field that rotates in space, used to drive AC motors.
- **War of Currents:** Late 19th-century competition between AC and DC power systems, primarily between Westinghouse/Thomson and Edison.
- **Wardenclyffe Tower:** Tesla’s unfinished project for wireless communication and power transmission on Long Island.
- **Telautomaton:** Tesla’s term for a wirelessly controlled vehicle, demonstrated via radio-controlled boat in 1898.
- **Bremsstrahlung:** Braking radiation produced when charged particles decelerate rapidly, used to describe Tesla’s X-ray generation method.
- **Teleforce:** Tesla’s proposed particle-beam weapon, popularly known as the “death ray.”

## Debates and Open Questions

Several aspects of Tesla’s life and work remain debated:

- **The $50,000 Bonus Incident:** Whether Thomas Edison personally offered and reneged on a bonus remains disputed, with conflicting accounts in Tesla’s autobiography.
- **Colorado Springs Signals:** Tesla claimed to have detected intelligent signals from space, possibly Mars. Most scholars attribute these to natural atmospheric noise or terrestrial interference.
- **Cosmic Ray Motor (1931):** Tesla claimed a motor running on cosmic rays, but no evidence supports its feasibility or construction.
- **Death Ray Feasibility:** While Tesla described a defensive particle beam weapon, modern physicists consider such technology impractical given the energy requirements.
- **Patent Priority in Radio:** Tesla’s wireless tuning patents were posthumously recognized by the U.S. Supreme Court in 1943, though Marconi is often credited with inventing radio.
- **Relationship with Mark Twain:** Their friendship is well-documented, but the depth of their collaboration on scientific ideas is sometimes exaggerated in popular culture.

# Kofi Annan

## Overview
Kofi Annan (1938–2018) was a Ghanaian diplomat who served as the seventh Secretary-General of the United Nations from 1997 to 2006. He was the first officeholder elected from within the UN staff itself and shared the 2001 Nobel Peace Prize with the organization. His tenure focused on UN reform, combating HIV/AIDS, and promoting the doctrine of "Responsibility to Protect," though he faced criticism over the UN's failures in Rwanda and the Oil-for-Food Programme.

- **18 August 2018** — Dies in Bern, Switzerland, at age 80.

## Body

### Early Life and Education
Kofi Annan was born into a prominent Fante family in Kumasi. His name follows Akan tradition: "Kofi" for a boy born on Friday, and "Annan" meaning fourth-born. He attended the elite Mfantsipim boarding school, graduating in 1957, the year Ghana gained independence. He studied economics at the Kumasi College of Science and Technology and Macalester College in the US, earning a diploma in International Relations from the Graduate Institute in Geneva, and later a master's in management from MIT.

### UN Career and Path to Secretary-General
Annan joined the UN in 1962. After a stint in Ghana, he returned to the UN, rising to head of personnel for the UN High Commission for Refugees and later director of administrative management services. In 1992, he was appointed Deputy Under-Secretary-General for Peacekeeping, becoming Under-Secretary-General in 1993. His handling of the 1995 Bosnia crisis, where he authorized NATO air strikes, made him a favorite of the United States. After Secretary-General Boutros Boutros-Ghali was vetoed for a second term, Annan was elected as his successor in 1996.

### Secretary-General: Reform and Initiatives
Annan's tenure was marked by ambitious UN reform. He proposed strategic management, a deputy secretary-general, and a 10% reduction in posts. He championed the Millennium Development Goals, launched in 2000, and the UN Global Compact, a framework for businesses to adopt human rights and environmental principles. He was instrumental in establishing the Global Fund to Fight AIDS, Tuberculosis and Malaria in 2002. A central intellectual contribution was the "Responsibility to Protect" (RtoP) doctrine, endorsed by the General Assembly in 2005, which argued that state sovereignty entails a duty to protect populations from genocide and war crimes.

### Criticisms and Controversies
Annan faced significant criticism. He was accused of passivity during the 1994 Rwandan genocide; he later acknowledged he "could and should have done more." The Oil-for-Food Programme scandal implicated his son, though an independent inquiry led by Paul Volcker found no evidence of illegal actions by Annan himself, while criticizing UN management. He also faced pressure over the UN's handling of the Iraq disarmament crisis and was criticized by some for not expanding the Security Council.

### Post-UN Career and Legacy
After leaving the UN, Annan founded the Kofi Annan Foundation to promote global governance. He mediated in crises, notably leading the Kenya National Dialogue and Reconciliation Process in 2008. He served as the UN–Arab League Joint Special Envoy for Syria in 2012, resigning in frustration over international inaction. He chaired The Elders and led the Advisory Commission on Rakhine State in Myanmar, whose report on the Rohingya crisis was released weeks before a major humanitarian disaster. He died in 2018 and received a state funeral in Ghana.

## Terms
- **Secretary-General**: The chief administrative officer of the United Nations.
- **Under-Secretary-General**: A high-ranking official within the UN Secretariat.
- **Millennium Development Goals (MDGs)**: Eight international development goals established following the 2000 Millennium Summit.
- **UN Global Compact**: A non-binding United Nations pact to get businesses worldwide to adopt sustainable and socially responsible policies.
- **Responsibility to Protect (RtoP)**: A political commitment that states have a responsibility to protect their populations from genocide, war crimes, ethnic cleansing, and crimes against humanity.
- **Oil-for-Food Programme**: A UN program established in 1995 to allow Iraq to sell oil in exchange for food, medicine, and other humanitarian needs, which was later embroiled in scandal.
- **The Elders**: An international organization of public figures noted as senior statesmen, peace activists, and human rights advocates, founded by Nelson Mandela.
- **Fante**: An ethnic group native to the central coastal region of Ghana.

## Debates and Open questions
- **Effectiveness of UN Leadership**: Scholars debate whether Annan's reformist vision was hampered by the UN's bureaucratic structure and the political constraints imposed by member states, particularly the United States.
- **Responsibility to Protect (RtoP)**: The doctrine remains controversial, with debates centering on its potential for abuse as a justification for humanitarian intervention and its inconsistent application in conflicts like Syria.
- **Legacy in Myanmar**: The failure of the Annan Commission's recommendations to prevent the 2017 Rohingya crisis raises questions about the limits of international advisory bodies in the face of entrenched domestic opposition.

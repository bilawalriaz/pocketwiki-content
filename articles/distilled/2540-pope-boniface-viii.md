# Pope Boniface VIII

## Overview
Pope Boniface VIII (r. 1294–1303) was a powerful and controversial pontiff who asserted unprecedented claims of papal supremacy over both spiritual and temporal rulers. His reign was defined by bitter conflicts with King Philip IV of France and other monarchs, culminating in his abduction and death. He also made lasting contributions to canon law and established the first Catholic Jubilee year.

## Timeline
- **c. 1230** — Born Benedetto Caetani in Anagni, Italy.
- **1294** — Elected Pope on 24 December, succeeding the abdicated Celestine V.
- **1298** — Published the *Liber Sextus*, a major codification of canon law.
- **1296** — First conflict with Philip IV of France over taxation of clergy; issued bull *Clericis laicos*.
- **1300** — Proclaimed the first Catholic Jubilee year in Rome.
- **1302** — Issued the bull *Unam sanctam*, declaring papal supremacy over secular rulers.
- **1303** — Abducted at Anagni by forces of Philip IV; died a month later on 11 October.
- **1311–12** — Council of Vienne declares his memory orthodox, ending a posthumous heresy trial.

## Body

### Early Life and Church Career
Benedetto Caetani was born into a baronial family with papal connections. He entered the Roman Curia in 1264, serving as a secretary on diplomatic missions to France and England. He accumulated numerous church benefices and was created cardinal deacon in 1281. As a cardinal, he acted as a papal legate in various European disputes.

### Papacy and Claims of Supremacy
Boniface VIII succeeded Pope Celestine V, who had abdicated. He immediately moved the papal court back to Rome and imprisoned his predecessor. His papacy was marked by aggressive assertions of papal authority. He systematized canon law with the *Liber Sextus* (1298) and established the first Jubilee in 1300, which drew massive crowds and revenue. His central doctrine, articulated in the bull *Unam sanctam* (1302), was that submission to the Roman pontiff was "absolutely necessary for salvation."

### Conflict with Philip IV of France
The major conflict of Boniface's reign was with King Philip IV, who sought to consolidate the French nation-state by taxing the clergy and reducing church influence. Boniface initially forbade clerical taxation without papal consent (*Clericis laicos*, 1296). Philip retaliated with economic embargoes, forcing Boniface to concede in the bull *Etsi de statu* (1297). The feud escalated, leading Boniface to summon French bishops to Rome and issue *Unam sanctam*. In 1303, Philip's minister Guillaume de Nogaret and Sciarra Colonna attacked Boniface at Anagni, capturing him for three days. The pope died a month later from the trauma.

### Other Conflicts and Legacy
Boniface intervened in European affairs, excommunicating Frederick III of Sicily and placing Florence under interdict. His political meddling in Florence, inviting Charles of Valois to intervene, led to the exile of Dante Alighieri, who later damned the pope in his *Divine Comedy*. After Boniface's death, Philip IV pressured Pope Clement V to stage a posthumous heresy trial. The Council of Vienne (1311–12) ultimately declared the charges unfounded and closed the case.

## Terms
- **Canon Law**: The system of laws and legal principles made and enforced by the hierarchical leadership of the Church.
- **Liber Sextus**: A 1298 collection of papal decretals (legal decisions) issued by Boniface VIII, which became a key part of the *Corpus Juris Canonici*.
- **Unam sanctam**: A 1302 papal bull asserting that both spiritual and temporal power were under the pope's jurisdiction, and that submission to the Roman pontiff was necessary for salvation.
- **Clericis laicos**: A 1296 papal bull forbidding lay authorities from taxing the clergy without prior papal approval.
- **Jubilee**: A special year of remission of sins and universal pardon, proclaimed by Boniface VIII for 1300, involving pilgrimage to Rome.
- **Papal Legate**: A personal representative of the pope sent on a diplomatic or ecclesiastical mission.
- **Interdict**: A major ecclesiastical censure that suspends certain sacraments and services in a territory.
- **Estates-General**: The assembly of the three estates (clergy, nobility, commons) of France, first convened by Philip IV in 1302 to counter the pope.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Abdication of Celestine V**: Scholars debate the degree to which Boniface pressured his predecessor into abdicating, an act considered unprecedented and controversial.
- **Posthumous Trial**: The charges of heresy against Boniface VIII, including accusations of sodomy, were largely driven by Philip IV's political agenda. The Council of Vienne's dismissal of the case suggests the charges were not substantiated, but the trial itself highlights the conflict between papal and royal authority.
- **Character and Legacy**: Historical accounts vary, depicting Boniface as either a defender of church liberty or an arrogant temporal prince. His portrayal in Dante's *Inferno* as a simoniac has cemented a negative cultural legacy, though modern historians assess his actions within the context of the struggle for papal supremacy.

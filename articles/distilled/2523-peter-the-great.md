# Peter the Great

## Overview
Peter the Great (1672–1725) was Tsar and later the first Emperor of Russia, whose radical reforms transformed the country from a medieval tsardom into a major European power. His reign was defined by aggressive westernization, military expansion, and the creation of a modern, centralized state, fundamentally altering Russia's political, social, and cultural landscape.

## Timeline
- **1682** — Becomes Tsar jointly with half-brother Ivan V; Sophia acts as regent.
- **1689** — Overthrows Sophia and becomes effective ruler.
- **1695–96** — Captures the Ottoman fortress of Azov, initiating Russia's naval expansion.
- **1697–98** — Travels on the "Grand Embassy" to Western Europe, studying shipbuilding and technology.
- **1700** — Begins the Great Northern War against Sweden; introduces the Julian calendar.
- **1703** — Founds Saint Petersburg on the Neva River.
- **1709** — Decisive Russian victory at the Battle of Poltava.
- **1711** — Establishes the Governing Senate as the highest state institution.
- **1717–18** — Second embassy to Western Europe; death of his son Alexei.
- **1721** — Treaty of Nystad ends the Great Northern War; Russia acquires Baltic territories.
- **1721** — Officially proclaimed Emperor of All Russia.
- **1722** — Issues the Table of Ranks, reforming state service based on merit.

## Body

### Early Life and Accession
Peter grew up in a period of court intrigue. After the death of his half-brother Feodor III in 1682, a power struggle erupted between the Miloslavsky and Naryshkin families. Peter and his weak-minded half-brother Ivan V were proclaimed joint tsars, with Peter's half-sister Sophia acting as regent. Peter was largely sidelined until 1689, when he overthrew Sophia and began to assert his authority. He became sole ruler upon Ivan V's death in 1696.

### Ideology and Reforms
Peter adopted a pragmatic, Protestant-influenced worldview, viewing the state as the primary instrument for transforming a "rude" and "stubborn" populace into useful subjects. He believed the monarch's duty was to serve the state's interests, even through harsh measures. His reforms were comprehensive: he created the Governing Senate (1711) and Collegiums (1717) to modernize administration, and the Table of Ranks (1722) to replace birth-based nobility with merit-based service. He introduced the Julian calendar (1699), reformed the alphabet, and mandated Western dress and beard taxes to break with tradition. He promoted education, founded the Russian Academy of Sciences (1724), and controlled the Orthodox Church through the Holy Synod (1721).

### Military Campaigns and Expansion
Peter's reign was dominated by wars for maritime access. After capturing Azov from the Ottomans, he launched the Great Northern War (1700–1721) against Sweden to gain Baltic territory. Despite an early defeat at Narva (1700), he rebuilt the army and won a decisive victory at Poltava (1709). The Treaty of Nystad (1721) granted Russia Ingria, Estonia, Livonia, and parts of Karelia. He also fought the Russo-Turkish War of 1710–11 and the Russo-Persian War of 1722–23, expanding Russian influence in the Caucasus and Caspian region.

### Cultural and Social Impact
Peter's "cultural revolution" forcibly westernized Russian elite society. He founded Saint Petersburg (1703) as a "window to the West," making it the capital in 1712. He established Russia's first newspaper (1703), promoted shipbuilding and industry, and collected scientific specimens for his Kunstkamera. His policies, including the poll tax and the dissolution of traditional monastic roles, caused significant social disruption and were brutally enforced, as seen in the suppression of the Streltsy rebellions.

## Terms
- **Tsardom**: The Russian state ruled by a Tsar before Peter's imperial reforms.
- **Streltsy**: Elite Russian military corps that repeatedly rebelled against Peter's authority.
- **Grand Embassy**: Peter's 1697–98 incognito tour of Western Europe to learn technology and seek alliances.
- **Table of Ranks**: A 1722 law establishing a hierarchy of state service positions based on merit, not birth.
- **Collegium**: A central government department established by Peter to replace the old, inefficient administrative system.
- **Holy Synod**: A council of clergymen that replaced the Patriarch of Moscow, placing the Orthodox Church under state control.
- **Poll Tax**: A direct tax on individuals introduced by Peter, replacing taxes based on land or households.
- **Petrine Baroque**: An architectural style introduced by Peter, characterized by Western European influences.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Modernizer vs. Tyrant**: Historians debate whether Peter was a visionary who propelled Russia forward or a brutal autocrat whose reforms oppressed the people and created a repressive administrative system.
- **Legacy of Autocracy**: Scholars question whether Peter's centralization of power strengthened the state or entrenched a liability by making the autocracy overly dependent on coercion.
- **Cultural Impact**: There is debate over whether his westernization was a profound cultural revolution or a superficial imposition that created a lasting divide between the elite and the common people.
- **Economic Effect**: Some historians argue his reforms accelerated development, while others contend they stifled progress and set Russia back for over a century.

# Kind of Blue

## Overview
*Kind of Blue* is a 1959 studio album by Miles Davis, widely regarded as the greatest jazz album ever recorded and one of the most influential albums in music history. It marked a pivotal shift from bebop's complex chord changes to modal jazz, using scales as the basis for improvisation, which gave musicians greater creative freedom and influenced countless artists across genres.

## Timeline
- **1953** — George Russell publishes *Lydian Chromatic Concept of Tonal Organization*, a theoretical foundation for modal jazz.
- **1958** — Davis records the title track of *Milestones*, his first major modal composition.
- **Late 1958** — Davis assembles the sextet for *Kind of Blue*, including pianist Bill Evans.
- **March 2, 1959** — First recording session: "So What," "Freddie Freeloader," and "Blue in Green" are recorded.
- **April 22, 1959** — Second recording session: "All Blues" and "Flamenco Sketches" are recorded.
- **August 17, 1959** — Album is released by Columbia Records.
- **2002** — Selected by the Library of Congress for the National Recording Registry.
- **2003** — Ranked #12 on *Rolling Stone*'s "500 Greatest Albums of All Time."
- **2019** — RIAA certifies the album quintuple platinum (5 million units).
- **2026** — Vince Guaraldi's *A Charlie Brown Christmas* surpasses it as the best-selling jazz album in the US.

## Body

### Background and Concept
By late 1958, Miles Davis led a successful hard bop band but was dissatisfied with bebop's complex chord progressions, which he felt hindered creativity. Influenced by George Russell's theoretical work on chord/scale unity, Davis sought a new approach. He implemented his first major modal composition on the title track of his 1958 album *Milestones*. For *Kind of Blue*, he decided to create an entire album based on modality, a "return to melody." He rehired pianist Bill Evans, who had studied Russell's concepts, to help realize this vision.

### Recording and Production
The album was recorded in two sessions at Columbia's 30th Street Studio in New York City. Davis provided the musicians with only sketches of scales and melodies, giving them minimal rehearsal and brief instructions. This approach yielded a spontaneous, atmospheric sound. Contrary to legend, the album was not recorded in one continuous take; only "Flamenco Sketches" was completed in a single try. Pianist Wynton Kelly, who had replaced Evans in the band, played on "Freddie Freeloader," possibly to assuage his feelings. The album was produced by Irving Townsend, though Teo Macero, who later became Davis's primary producer, has often been incorrectly credited.

### Composition and Style
*Kind of Blue* is entirely modal, meaning improvisation is based on scales (modes) rather than chord changes. Davis gave each performer a set of scales for each piece, granting them more freedom with melody. The album's five tracks are modal sketches: "So What" uses two modes; "Freddie Freeloader" is a standard twelve-bar blues; "Blue in Green" is a ten-measure cycle; "All Blues" is a twelve-bar blues in 6/8 time; and "Flamenco Sketches" consists of five scales played in sequence. While Davis is credited as the composer, scholars believe Bill Evans co-wrote "Blue in Green" and "Flamenco Sketches."

### Reception and Legacy
Upon release, *Kind of Blue* was immediately acclaimed as Davis's masterpiece. It became his best-selling album and cemented his status as a jazz icon. The album is consistently ranked among the greatest of all time and is often recommended as an introductory jazz record due to its melodic accessibility and relaxed improvisation. Its influence extended beyond jazz, inspiring rock musicians like Duane Allman and Pink Floyd's Richard Wright. The album's sidemen—Coltrane, Evans, Adderley, Kelly, Chambers, and Cobb—all went on to highly successful careers. Davis himself later dismissed his modal period, but the album's stature has only grown, being honored by the U.S. Congress in 2009 for its 50th anniversary.

## Terms
- **Modal Jazz:** A style of jazz that uses musical modes (scales) as the basis for improvisation and composition, rather than chord progressions.
- **Hard Bop:** A style of jazz that evolved from bebop in the mid-1950s, incorporating influences from blues, gospel, and rhythm and blues, often with complex chord changes.
- **Bebop:** A style of jazz developed in the 1940s characterized by fast tempos, complex chord progressions, and virtuosic improvisation.
- **Chord Changes:** The sequence of chords in a piece of music that traditionally provides the harmonic structure for jazz improvisation.
- **Lydian Chromatic Concept:** A music theory by George Russell that proposed chord/scale unity and provided a theoretical foundation for modal jazz.
- **Sextet:** A musical ensemble of six performers.
- **Three-track tape:** An early multitrack recording format used for the album's sessions.
- **National Recording Registry:** A list of sound recordings deemed "culturally, historically, or aesthetically significant" by the Library of Congress.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Authorship of "Blue in Green" and "Flamenco Sketches":** While the album credits Davis as the sole composer, many scholars and fans believe Bill Evans co-wrote or wrote these pieces. Evans claimed co-credit on his own recording, and the Davis estate acknowledged his authorship in 2002.
- **Producer Credit:** The original LP did not credit a producer. Subsequent reissues have variously credited Irving Townsend, Teo Macero, or both. Historical analysis indicates Townsend was the primary producer for the original sessions, with Macero playing a minor, apprentice-like role.
- **Impact of Ornette Coleman:** The arrival of Ornette Coleman and his free jazz innovations in 1959 initially muted the impact of *Kind of Blue*, a fact that reportedly irritated Davis. The two artists offered different alternatives to bebop's rules.

# Leopold II of Belgium

## Overview
Leopold II was the King of the Belgians from 1865 to 1909 and the founder and sole owner of the Congo Free State, a private colonial territory he ruled from 1885 to 1908. His domestic reign saw significant modernization in Belgium, but his legacy is overwhelmingly defined by the brutal exploitation and atrocities committed in the Congo under his personal rule, which resulted in a massive population decline and an international scandal.

## Timeline
- **9 April 1835** — Born in Brussels, son of King Leopold I.
- **17 December 1865** — Becomes King of the Belgians after his father's death.
- **1876** — Founds the International African Society to pursue colonial ambitions.
- **1878** — Hires Henry Morton Stanley to explore and establish a colony in the Congo.
- **1884–1885** — Berlin Conference recognizes his sovereignty over the Congo Free State.
- **1890s** — Global rubber price rise leads to intensified forced labor and atrocities in the Congo.
- **1893** — Universal male suffrage introduced in Belgium (with plural voting).
- **1902** — Italian anarchist attempts to assassinate Leopold in Brussels.
- **1908** — Under international and domestic pressure, Leopold cedes the Congo Free State to Belgium.
- **17 December 1909** — Dies at Laeken; reign of 44 years is the longest in Belgian history.

## Body

### Domestic Reign
Leopold II's reign in Belgium was marked by political and social reform. His government saw the introduction of universal male suffrage in 1893, the right for workers to form unions, and laws against child labor. He was a prolific builder, commissioning numerous public works and urban projects in Brussels, Ostend, and Antwerp, earning him the nickname "Builder King." He also strengthened Belgium's military defenses to preserve its neutrality, most notably during the Franco-Prussian War.

### Acquisition of the Congo
Driven by a belief that colonies were key to national greatness, Leopold pursued colonial territory for Belgium. After failed attempts to acquire colonies in Asia and Africa, he established the International African Society in 1876 as a front for his ambitions. He hired explorer Henry Morton Stanley to lay claim to the Congo region. Through diplomatic maneuvering, the Berlin Conference of 1884–1885 recognized his personal sovereignty over the vast Congo Free State, an area 76 times larger than Belgium.

### Exploitation and Atrocities in the Congo
Leopold ruled the Congo Free State as a private enterprise, never visiting the territory himself. He extracted a fortune, initially from ivory and later from rubber after prices soared in the 1890s. His administration, enforced by the mercenary Force Publique, used systematic brutality, including forced labor, torture, murder, and the mutilation of hands to enforce rubber quotas. This regime led to a catastrophic population decline, with estimates ranging from 1 million to 15 million deaths due to violence, disease, and famine.

### International Scandal and Relinquishment
Reports of atrocities, investigated by British consul Roger Casement and publicized by activists like E. D. Morel and writers such as Mark Twain and Arthur Conan Doyle, sparked an international scandal. Under mounting pressure from the Belgian Parliament and global opinion, Leopold was forced to cede the Congo Free State to Belgium in 1908. The Belgian government paid him a large sum, and he destroyed the colony's archives before his death.

## Terms
- **Congo Free State**: The private colony in Central Africa, now the Democratic Republic of the Congo, owned and ruled personally by Leopold II from 1885 to 1908.
- **Force Publique**: The mercenary army used by Leopold II to enforce his rule and rubber quotas in the Congo Free State.
- **Berlin Conference (1884–1885)**: A meeting of European powers that formalized the "Scramble for Africa" and recognized Leopold's claim to the Congo.
- **Builder King**: A nickname for Leopold II in Belgium, referring to the many buildings and public works he commissioned.
- **Universal male suffrage**: The right of all adult men to vote, introduced in Belgium in 1893 but tempered by plural voting.
- **Casement Report**: A 1904 report by British consul Roger Casement detailing the atrocities and exploitation in the Congo Free State.
- **Congo Reform Association**: The first mass human rights movement, founded in 1907 to campaign against abuses in the Congo.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Death Toll**: The exact population decline in the Congo under Leopold's rule is debated, with estimates ranging from 1 million to 15 million. Historians cite the lack of reliable pre-colonial censuses as a key difficulty.
- **Leopold's Motives**: While his exploitation was for personal enrichment, some historians argue his imperialism was also driven by a desire to ensure Belgium's prosperity and embellishment.
- **Legacy and Statues**: Leopold's legacy remains highly controversial. In Belgium, debates continue over the removal of statues erected during the colonial era, while in the Congo, his role is a point of historical contention.

# Muslim ibn al-Hajjaj

## Overview
Imam Muslim (d. 875 CE) was a pivotal 9th-century Islamic scholar from Nishapur, renowned for compiling *Sahih Muslim*, one of the two most authoritative collections of hadith (sayings and actions of the Prophet Muhammad) in Sunni Islam. His work, alongside *Sahih al-Bukhari*, forms the core of the Sunni hadith canon and is essential for understanding Islamic law and theology.

## Timeline
- **c. 202–206 AH / 817–822 CE** — Born in Nishapur, Khorasan (modern Iran). Exact date is debated.
- **Mid-9th century** — Studies hadith across the Arabian Peninsula, Egypt, Iraq, and Syria.
- **Mid-9th century** — Settles in Nishapur; meets and befriends al-Bukhari.
- **Mid-9th century** — Composes his hadith collection, *Sahih Muslim*.
- **25 Rajab, 261 AH / May 875 CE** — Dies in Nishapur at age 55 (per al-Hakim al-Nishapuri).
- **11th century** — Al-Khatib al-Baghdadi's *History of Baghdad* becomes a key biographical source.

## Body

### Biography and Lineage
Muslim ibn al-Hajjaj was of Arab origin, belonging to the Banu Qushayr tribe, which had migrated to Persian territory during the Rashidun Caliphate expansion. His birth year is uncertain, with sources suggesting 202, 204, or 206 AH. He studied under major hadith scholars, including al-Bukhari, and taught students who became renowned scholars, such as al-Tirmidhi and Ibn Khuzayma. He died in Nishapur and was buried in its suburb, Nasarabad.

### *Sahih Muslim*
In the mid-9th century, Muslim compiled *Sahih Muslim*, a collection of hadith he deemed entirely authentic (*sahih*). It is one of the six canonical hadith books in Sunni Islam and, with *Sahih al-Bukhari*, is considered among the most authoritative. The two collections share a substantial overlap, with approximately 2,326 traditions and many narrators in common. The total number of hadith in *Sahih Muslim* varies from three to twelve thousand, depending on counting methods.

### Legacy and Reception
The scholar Ishaq ibn Rahwayh was the first to recommend Muslim's work. However, early reception was mixed; Abu Zur'a al-Razi criticized Muslim for omitting material he himself considered authentic and for including weak transmitters. Over time, *Sahih Muslim* gained immense stature and is now regarded as the second most authentic hadith collection in Sunni Islam, after *Sahih al-Bukhari*.

### Other Works
Besides his hadith collection, Muslim authored *Al-Kuna wa al-Asma*, a work on narrator names and kunyas (honorifics), and *Al-Tamyiz*, a study of hidden defects in hadith.

## Terms
- **Hadith**: A record of the sayings, actions, or approvals of the Prophet Muhammad, forming a primary source for Islamic law and guidance.
- **Muhaddith**: A scholar specializing in the study, authentication, and transmission of hadith.
- **Sahih**: Literally "sound" or "authentic"; describes a hadith that meets strict criteria of reliability and chain of transmission.
- **Sahihayn**: The term for the two most authentic hadith collections, *Sahih al-Bukhari* and *Sahih Muslim*.
- **Kunya**: An honorific name in Arabic, often beginning with "Abu" (father of) or "Umm" (mother of).
- **Illal**: Hidden defects in a hadith that affect its authenticity, studied by hadith critics.
- **Huffaz**: Plural of *hafiz*; scholars who have memorized vast numbers of hadith and their chains of transmission.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Date of birth**: Historians disagree on whether Muslim was born in 202, 204, or 206 AH, with no consensus from early hadith masters.
- **Early criticism**: Abu Zur'a al-Razi's objections about omitted material and weak transmitters highlight early scholarly debate over the collection's comprehensiveness and rigor.
- **Canonization process**: The gradual acceptance of *Sahih Muslim* as second only to *Sahih al-Bukhari* was not immediate, as seen in the mixed early reactions.

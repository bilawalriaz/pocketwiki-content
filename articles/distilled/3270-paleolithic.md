# Paleolithic

## Overview
The Paleolithic, or Old Stone Age, is the longest period of human prehistory, spanning from the first use of stone tools by hominins c. 3.3 million years ago to the end of the Pleistocene epoch c. 11,650 years ago. It encompasses the evolution of early human ancestors into anatomically and behaviorally modern humans, their global expansion, and the development of foundational technologies, social structures, and symbolic thought. Understanding this era is crucial for tracing the origins of human culture, technology, and adaptation.

## Timeline
- **c. 3.3 million years ago** — Earliest known use of stone tools by hominins.
- **c. 2.6 million years ago** — Oldowan stone tool industry begins in Africa.
- **c. 1.8–1.65 million years ago** — Acheulean tool industry (e.g., hand axes) emerges with *Homo ergaster*.
- **c. 2 million–1.5 million years ago** — Hominins (*Homo erectus*) begin migrating out of Africa into Eurasia.
- **c. 500,000 years ago** — *Homo heidelbergensis* arrives in Europe; later evolves into Neanderthals.
- **c. 300,000 years ago** — Anatomically modern *Homo sapiens* emerges in Africa.
- **c. 50,000 years ago** — Major increase in artifact diversity; first evidence of art, fishing, and symbolic behavior.
- **c. 50,000–40,000 years ago** — *Homo sapiens* expands out of Africa, reaching Australia and Europe.
- **c. 30,000–25,000 years ago** — Peak of Upper Paleolithic art (e.g., cave paintings, Venus figurines) and complex tools (bow and arrow).
- **c. 11,650 years ago** — End of the Paleolithic and Pleistocene epoch; transition to the Holocene and Mesolithic/Neolithic periods.

## Body

### Technology and Tools
Paleolithic technology is defined by the development of stone tools, though wood, bone, and organic materials were also used. The earliest industry, the Oldowan (c. 2.6 million years ago), produced simple choppers and flakes. It was replaced by the more sophisticated Acheulean industry (c. 1.8 million years ago), known for hand axes. By the Middle Paleolithic (c. 200,000 years ago), the prepared-core technique allowed for more controlled tool production, including hafted stone-tipped spears. The Upper Paleolithic saw a burst of innovation, including microliths, bows, atlatls, nets, and the first ceramic art (Venus of Dolní Věstonice, c. 29,000–25,000 BP). The control of fire, possibly as early as 1.5 million years ago, became common by the Middle Paleolithic, aiding in cooking, protection, and warmth.

### Human Evolution and Expansion
The Paleolithic saw the evolution of multiple hominin species. Early *Homo erectus* expanded from Africa into Eurasia by c. 2 million years ago. Later, *Homo heidelbergensis* evolved into Neanderthals in Europe. Anatomically modern *Homo sapiens* emerged in Africa c. 300,000 BP and began a major global expansion around 50,000 BP. By the end of the Upper Paleolithic, humans had reached Australia, the Americas via the Beringia land bridge, and the Arctic. Multiple hominin groups, including Neanderthals and Denisovans, coexisted and interbred with *Homo sapiens* before going extinct by the start of the Upper Paleolithic.

### Diet and Social Organization
Paleolithic people were hunter-gatherers living in small, nomadic bands (typically 20–100 members). Their diet was opportunistic and varied by region, including meat, fish, shellfish, plants, and insects. The shift to cooking improved nutrition and food safety. Social structures are believed to have been largely egalitarian, with flexible gender roles and communal decision-making. Trade networks for materials like ochre existed by the Middle Paleolithic. While some evidence suggests complex, hierarchical societies in resource-rich Upper Paleolithic areas, the general model is one of small, mobile groups with a high degree of leisure time.

### Art, Symbolism, and Belief
Symbolic behavior emerged gradually. Early evidence includes ochre use and simple engravings in the Middle Paleolithic. The Upper Paleolithic produced undisputed art, including cave paintings, animal carvings, and "Venus" figurines. The purpose of this art is debated, with theories ranging from hunting magic and shamanistic rituals to fertility symbols or social markers. Burials, sometimes with grave goods, appear by the Middle Paleolithic (c. 100,000 BP), suggesting belief in an afterlife or ritual care for the dead. The earliest undisputed shaman burial dates to c. 30,000 BP. Music likely existed, with bone flutes being the oldest known instruments.

## Terms
- **Paleolithic**: The Old Stone Age; the earliest and longest phase of human prehistory, characterized by stone tool use and hunter-gatherer lifestyles.
- **Hominin**: A member of the human lineage after its split from the lineage leading to chimpanzees.
- **Oldowan**: The earliest stone tool industry (c. 2.6 million years ago), producing simple choppers and flakes.
- **Acheulean**: A stone tool industry (c. 1.8 million years ago) characterized by bifacial hand axes and cleavers.
- **Prepared-core technique**: A Middle Paleolithic method of shaping a stone core to control the production of flakes for tools.
- **Microlith**: Small, sharp stone tools or points used in composite tools like arrows or harpoons.
- **Megafauna**: Large animals, many of which (e.g., woolly mammoths) went extinct at the end of the Pleistocene.
- **Egalitarian**: A social system with relatively equal status among individuals; a common model for Paleolithic bands.
- **Shaman**: A religious practitioner believed to interact with the spirit world, often through altered states of consciousness.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Causation of megafauna extinctions**: Was it primarily climate change at the end of the Pleistocene, human overhunting, or a combination of factors?
- **Neanderthal behavior**: To what extent did Neanderthals use projectile weapons, create symbolic art, and interbreed with *Homo sapiens*?
- **Origins of art and ritual**: Did symbolic behavior begin in the Lower Paleolithic with *Homo erectus*, or is it uniquely Upper Paleolithic?
- **Social structure**: Were most Paleolithic societies fundamentally egalitarian, or did complex hierarchies and warfare exist in some regions?
- **Dietary proportions**: What was the precise balance of plant vs. animal foods in Paleolithic diets, and how did it vary by time and location?

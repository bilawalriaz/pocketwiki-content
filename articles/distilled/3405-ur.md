# Ur

## Overview
Ur was a major Sumerian city-state in ancient Mesopotamia, located in modern-day Iraq. It was a significant religious, political, and economic center for millennia, particularly during the Early Bronze Age and the Ur III period (c. 2112–2004 BC), when it was the capital of a powerful empire. Its importance is underscored by its association with the moon god Nanna, its lavish Royal Tombs, and its possible identification as the biblical "Ur of the Chaldees."

## Timeline
- **c. 3800 BC** — City dates from the Ubaid period.
- **c. 26th century BC** — First recorded king, Mesannepada, of the First Dynasty of Ur.
- **c. 2500–2400 BC** — Royal Tombs of Ur constructed (Early Dynastic IIIa period).
- **c. 2334–2154 BC** — Ur is part of the Akkadian Empire; Sargon installs his daughter Enheduanna as high priestess.
- **c. 2047–2030 BC** — Ur-Nammu founds the Third Dynasty of Ur (Ur III), builds the Ziggurat.
- **c. 2029–1982 BC** — Reign of Shulgi, greatest king of the Ur III empire.
- **c. 1940 BC** — Ur III empire falls to the Elamites.
- **19th century BC** — Ur is conquered by Hammurabi of the Old Babylonian Empire.
- **6th century BC** — Last Babylonian king, Nabonidus, restores the Ziggurat.
- **Early 5th century BC** — Ur is abandoned.
- **1922–1934** — Major excavations led by Sir Leonard Woolley uncover the Royal Tombs.
- **2015–present** — Joint Iraqi and international archaeological work resumes.

## Body

### Geography and Early Settlement
Ur was originally a coastal city near the Persian Gulf, though the coastline has since shifted inland. The site, now called Tell el-Muqayyar ("mound of bitumen"), is a large mound on the south bank of the Euphrates. Evidence of occupation begins in the Ubaid period (c. 5500–3700 BC). A flood layer found by early excavators was once linked to the biblical Great Flood, but later dating showed such layers across Mesopotamia are from different times.

### Society, Culture, and Religion
Ur was a major urban center, as confirmed by the wealth of its Royal Tombs (c. 25th–24th century BC). These tombs contained luxury items imported from across the ancient world, demonstrating Ur's extensive trade networks. The city's patron deity was the moon god Nanna (Sin in Akkadian), and its name derives from "the abode of Nanna." The Ziggurat of Ur, a massive temple platform, was built by Ur-Nammu in the 21st century BC and later restored. Ur was also a center of Sumerian literature; the high priestess Enheduanna, daughter of Sargon of Akkad, composed hymns there and is a potential first identifiable author in history.

### Political History
Ur's political history is marked by periods of independence and foreign domination. After its First Dynasty, it was often under the control of neighboring powers like Lagash and Uruk. It became a key city in the Semitic-speaking Akkadian Empire (c. 2334–2154 BC). Following the Akkadian collapse, the Gutians controlled the region until Utu-hengal of Uruk expelled them. His governor, Ur-Nammu, then established the powerful Third Dynasty of Ur (Ur III), creating a highly centralized bureaucratic state. This empire, possibly the world's largest city at the time, fell around 1940 BC to the Elamites. Ur later came under the control of the Old Babylonian, Kassite, Neo-Assyrian, and Neo-Babylonian empires before being abandoned in the early 5th century BC due to environmental changes.

### Archaeology and the Royal Tombs
The site was first excavated in the 1850s by John George Taylor. The most significant work was led by Sir Leonard Woolley from 1922 to 1934. He uncovered the Ziggurat and the famous Royal Tombs, which contained about 1,850 burials. Sixteen were "royal tombs" with immense treasures, including the Standard of Ur and the Lyres of Ur. The tombs revealed evidence of mass human sacrifice, with attendants killed by blows to the head. Recent excavations (2015–present) have uncovered new cuneiform tablets and urban areas.

## Terms
*   **Sumerian:** The non-Semitic people and language of southern Mesopotamia before the rise of Akkad.
*   **Cuneiform:** The wedge-shaped writing system developed by the Sumerians.
*   **Ziggurat:** A massive, stepped pyramidal temple platform, a hallmark of Mesopotamian architecture.
*   **Ur III Period:** The Third Dynasty of Ur (c. 2112–2004 BC), a Neo-Sumerian renaissance and imperial age.
*   **Ubaid Period:** A prehistoric period in Mesopotamia (c. 5500–3700 BC) characterized by early village life.
*   **Akkadian Empire:** The first empire in Mesopotamia, founded by Sargon of Akkad, uniting Sumerian and Akkadian-speaking peoples.
*   **High Priestess:** A chief religious official; Enheduanna's appointment was a political act to legitimize Akkadian rule.
*   **Royal Tombs of Ur:** Lavish burials from the Early Dynastic IIIa period, containing wealth and evidence of sacrifice.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Biblical Identification:** Ur is a candidate for "Ur of the Chaldees," the birthplace of Abraham in Genesis, but this is debated due to anachronisms (the Chaldeans arrived much later) and alternative site proposals.
*   **Enheduanna's Authorship:** Scholars are unsure if Enheduanna personally composed her hymns or if they were written by scribes on her behalf.
*   **Flood Layer:** The flood layer found at Ur was initially seen as evidence for the biblical Great Flood, but radiocarbon dating shows such layers across Mesopotamia are not contemporaneous.
*   **Royal Tomb Sacrifices:** Woolley initially theorized the attendants in the Royal Tombs died willingly by poison, but modern analysis shows they were killed by blows to the head.

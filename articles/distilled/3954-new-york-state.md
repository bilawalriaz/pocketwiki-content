# New York (state)

## Overview
New York is a state in the northeastern United States, bordering New England, Pennsylvania, New Jersey, and Canada. It is the fourth-most populous state, with over 20 million residents, and its geography ranges from the urban density of New York City to the Adirondack Mountains. Historically one of the original Thirteen Colonies, New York became a global center of finance, culture, and immigration, earning the nickname "Empire State." Its development was propelled by the Erie Canal, and it remains a major economic and political hub.

## Body

### Early History and Colonial Era
The region was inhabited for thousands of years by Algonquian and Iroquoian tribes. European exploration began with Giovanni da Verrazzano in 1524 and Henry Hudson in 1609. The Dutch established trading posts and the colony of New Netherland. In 1664, the English seized the colony, renaming it New York. The area was contested during the French and Indian War (1754–1763).

### Revolution and Statehood
New York was central to the American Revolution, hosting major battles like Long Island and Saratoga. The state's constitution was adopted in 1777, and it ratified the U.S. Constitution in 1788. New York City served as the first national capital under the Articles of Confederation. The Iroquois Confederacy was largely displaced after the war, with many members relocating to Canada.

### 19th Century Growth
The opening of the Erie Canal in 1825 was transformative, linking the Hudson River to the Great Lakes and making New York City the nation's premier commercial port. This spurred economic growth, settlement of Upstate New York, and the rise of major corporations. The state was a center of both abolitionist activism and the cotton trade leading up to the Civil War, which it supported strongly.

### Immigration and Urbanization
From the mid-19th century, New York City became the primary port of entry for immigrants to the United States. Processing occurred first at Castle Garden (1855–1890) and then at Ellis Island (1892–1954), through which over 12 million immigrants passed. This influx fueled the growth of New York City into a global metropolis and profoundly shaped the state's demographics and culture.

### 20th and 21st Centuries
New York's economy expanded through finance, media, and technology, though it faced deindustrialization in the late 20th century. The September 11, 2001, terrorist attacks caused massive loss of life and destruction in Lower Manhattan. The state has since rebuilt and continues to be a global leader in finance, culture, and innovation, while grappling with issues like climate change, as evidenced by Hurricane Sandy in 2012.

## Terms
- **Iroquois Confederacy:** A powerful alliance of Native American tribes (originally five, later six) in the region, including the Mohawk and Seneca.
- **New Netherland:** The 17th-century Dutch colony that encompassed parts of present-day New York, New Jersey, and Connecticut.
- **Erie Canal:** An artificial waterway completed in 1825 that connected the Hudson River to Lake Erie, drastically reducing shipping costs and boosting New York's economy.
- **Ellis Island:** The federal immigration station in New York Harbor that operated from 1892 to 1954, processing millions of immigrants.
- **Upstate/Downstate:** Informal terms distinguishing the New York City metropolitan area (Downstate) from the rest of the state (Upstate).
- **Adirondack Park:** A large, constitutionally protected "forever wild" forest preserve in northern New York, larger than Yellowstone, Everglades, Glacier, and Grand Canyon national parks combined.
- **Silicon Alley:** A metonym for the high-technology and entrepreneurship ecosystem centered in the New York City metropolitan region.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The precise boundary between "Upstate" and "Downstate" New York is a matter of great contention and has no official definition.
- New York has seen several secession movements, including proposals for a state of Long Island, a state of Niagara in the west, and for New York City to become its own state.
- The state's population growth has been uneven, with robust growth in the New York metropolitan area and stagnation or decline in some Upstate cities for decades.

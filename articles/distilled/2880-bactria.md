# Bactria

## Overview
Bactria was an ancient Iranian civilization in Central Asia, located in a fertile plain between the Amu Darya (Oxus) River and the Hindu Kush mountains, within modern Afghanistan, Tajikistan, and Uzbekistan. Its strategic position on major trade routes, including the Silk Road, made it a wealthy and coveted region, leading to a complex history of conquest and cultural synthesis by Persian, Greek, nomadic, and Islamic powers. It was a key center of Zoroastrianism and later a crucible for Greco-Buddhist culture.

## Timeline
- **c. 2200–1700 BC** — Bactria–Margiana Archaeological Complex (Oxus civilization) flourishes.
- **6th century BC** — Bactria becomes a satrapy of the Achaemenid Persian Empire.
- **328 BC** — Alexander the Great conquers Bactria.
- **c. 245 BC** — Diodotus I declares independence, founding the Greco-Bactrian Kingdom.
- **2nd century BC** — Greco-Bactrians expand into India; kingdom later overrun by nomadic Yuezhi.
- **c. 127 BC** — Chinese envoy Zhang Qian visits Bactria, then under Yuezhi suzerainty.

- **3rd century AD** — Sasanian King Shapur I conquers western Kushan territories.
- **663 AD** — Umayyad Caliphate captures Balkh, beginning Islamization.
- **875–999 AD** — Samanid Empire, founded by a Persian from Balkh, promotes New Persian.

## Body

### Geography and Early History
Bactria was a fertile plain irrigated by rivers flowing from the Hindu Kush, noted for its agricultural productivity. It was protected by mountain ranges on three sides. The region is celebrated in the Zoroastrian Avesta as one of the "sixteen perfect Iranian lands." Archaeologically, the Bronze Age Bactria–Margiana Archaeological Complex (c. 2200–1700 BC) represents an early urban culture in the area. Some scholars propose Bactria was the homeland (*Airyanem Vaejah*) of the Indo-Iranians.

### Persian and Macedonian Rule
Bactria was annexed by the Achaemenid Empire, likely by Cyrus the Great in the 6th century BC, and became a special satrapy often ruled by a crown prince. The Persians deported Greeks to Bactria, establishing communities there. After the fall of the Achaemenids, Bactria was a center of resistance against Alexander the Great, who eventually conquered it through force and diplomacy, marrying the local noblewoman Roxana. Following Alexander's death, Bactria became part of the Seleucid Empire.

### The Greco-Bactrian and Indo-Greek Kingdoms
Around 245 BC, the Seleucid satrap Diodotus I declared independence, founding the Greco-Bactrian Kingdom. This Hellenistic state became powerful due to Bactria's fertility, eventually expanding into South Asia. The kingdom was marked by internal strife and usurpations. Its rulers, like Menander I, often adopted local cultures and religions, including Buddhism. Greek political power in Bactria ended in the late 2nd century BC with invasions by nomadic peoples, including the Sakas and later the Yuezhi.

### Kushan, Sasanian, and Islamic Periods
The Yuezhi, who gave their name to the region's Chinese designation *Daxia*, were unified by Kujula Kadphises to form the Kushan Empire. Bactria was later contested by the Sasanian Empire, which established the Kushano-Sasanian Kingdom. The Islamic conquest began in the 7th century. The city of Balkh (ancient Bactra) became a center of an "Iranian Renaissance" in the 8th–9th centuries, where New Persian emerged as a literary language. The Samanid Empire, founded by a Persian from Balkh, spread Persian language and culture, leading to the decline of the Bactrian language.

## Terms
*   **Satrapy:** A province of the ancient Achaemenid (Persian) Empire, governed by a satrap.
*   **Bactrian:** An Eastern Iranian language, the common tongue of the region until the early medieval period, written in the Greek alphabet.
*   **Greco-Bactrian Kingdom:** A Hellenistic state (c. 256–120 BC) in Central Asia formed after the breakup of the Seleucid Empire.
*   **Kushan Empire:** A syncretic empire (1st–3rd centuries AD) that controlled much of Central Asia and northern India, founded by the Yuezhi.
*   **Samanid Empire:** A Persianate Muslim dynasty (875–999 AD) that ruled Eastern Iran and Transoxiana, fostering a revival of Persian culture.
*   **Yuezhi:** An ancient nomadic people who migrated from the borders of China into Bactria, later founding the Kushan Empire.
*   **Zoroastrianism:** An ancient Iranian religion based on the teachings of the prophet Zoroaster, for which Bactria was a key early center.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   The exact location and nature of the legendary homeland *Airyanem Vaejah* mentioned in the Avesta is debated, with Bactria being a primary candidate.
*   The precise sequence and identity of the nomadic tribes (Sakas, Yuezhi, etc.) that overthrew the Greco-Bactrian Kingdom are complex and not fully resolved.
*   The relationship between the Tokharians of Bactria and the Tocharian people and languages of the Tarim Basin is a subject of scholarly clarification, as they are distinct groups.

# Corazon Aquino

## Overview
Corazon Aquino was the 11th president of the Philippines and the first woman to hold the office. She rose to prominence as the leader of the 1986 People Power Revolution, a non-violent movement that ended the 20-year authoritarian rule of Ferdinand Marcos and restored democracy. Her presidency was foundational in re-establishing democratic institutions and civil liberties after a period of dictatorship.

## Timeline
- **August 21, 1983** — Assassination of her husband, opposition leader Benigno "Ninoy" Aquino Jr.
- **November 3, 1985** — President Marcos announces a snap election.
- **February 7, 1986** — Presidential election held, marred by allegations of massive fraud.
- **February 22–25, 1986** — People Power Revolution; Aquino is sworn in as president.
- **February 25, 1986** — Aquino issues Proclamation No. 1, beginning her transitional government.
- **January 22, 1987** — Mendiola Massacre occurs, killing at least 12 protesters.
- **February 2, 1987** — The 1987 Constitution is ratified by nationwide plebiscite.
- **1988** — Congress passes the Comprehensive Agrarian Reform Law (CARP).
- **1989** — Autonomous Region in Muslim Mindanao (ARMM) is created.
- **December 1989** — The most serious coup attempt against her government is staged.
- **June 1991** — Mount Pinatubo erupts, causing widespread devastation.
- **June 30, 1992** — Aquino peacefully hands over power to her successor, Fidel Ramos.

## Body

### Rise to the Presidency
Born into a prominent political family, Corazon Aquino married Senator Benigno Aquino Jr. in 1954. After his assassination upon returning from exile in 1983, she became the symbolic leader of the opposition to President Ferdinand Marcos. Following Marcos's call for a snap election in 1985, Aquino ran for president. The February 1986 election was declared fraudulent, but a parallel count by the National Citizens' Movement for Free Elections (NAMFREL) showed Aquino had won. This led to the People Power Revolution, where military defections and massive civilian support forced Marcos from power. Aquino was inaugurated on February 25, 1986.

### Constitutional and Legal Reforms
Aquino's first acts were to abolish the Marcos-era constitution and establish a transitional government. She appointed a Constitutional Commission that drafted the 1987 Constitution, ratified by plebiscite. This constitution restored the bicameral Congress, established a bill of rights, and limited presidential powers to a single six-year term. Her administration enacted major legal reforms, including the Family Code of 1987, the Administrative Code of 1987, and the 1991 Local Government Code, which devolved powers to local units.

### Socio-Economic Policies
Aquino's economic agenda focused on dismantling crony capitalism monopolies and restoring international credit. She honored the large foreign debt inherited from Marcos, a move aimed at improving the country's financial reputation. Her administration pursued market liberalization and passed the Comprehensive Agrarian Reform Law (CARP) in 1988 to redistribute agricultural land. However, her own family's Hacienda Luisita became a controversy for using stock distribution instead of land redistribution. The economy saw modest growth but was hampered by coup attempts and natural disasters.

### Peace, Conflict, and Foreign Relations
Aquino's government pursued peace talks with the Moro National Liberation Front (MNLF), leading to the creation of the Autonomous Region in Muslim Mindanao (ARMM) in 1989. This was opposed by the Moro Islamic Liberation Front (MILF) and led to the formation of the terrorist group Abu Sayyaf. She released political prisoners, including communist leaders, but peace talks with the Communist Party of the Philippines collapsed after the Mendiola Massacre. Regarding U.S. military bases, Aquino supported their continued presence, but after the Mount Pinatubo eruption, the Philippine government served notice for their closure by 1992.

### Challenges and Natural Disasters
Aquino's presidency faced numerous challenges, including several coup attempts, the deadliest in August 1987 and December 1989. The Mendiola Massacre in 1987, where state forces killed protesting farmers, led to cabinet resignations. The country also suffered major natural calamities, including the 1990 Luzon earthquake, the 1991 eruption of Mount Pinatubo, and Tropical Storm Thelma in 1991. A severe electricity crisis, partly due to the deactivation of the Bataan Nuclear Power Plant, also plagued her term.

### Post-Presidency and Death
After leaving office in 1992, Aquino remained active in politics, opposing attempts to amend the constitution's term limits and later supporting the ouster of President Joseph Estrada in 2001. She later expressed regret for this. In 2008, she was diagnosed with colorectal cancer and died on August 1, 2009. Her funeral drew massive public mourning. Her son, Benigno Aquino III, later became the 15th president of the Philippines.

## Terms
*   **People Power Revolution:** The 1986 non-violent mass demonstrations that peacefully ousted President Ferdinand Marcos and restored democracy.
*   **Crony Capitalism:** An economic system where business success depends on close relationships between business people and government officials, as practiced under Marcos.
*   **Batasang Pambansa:** The unicameral legislature that served as the Philippine Congress during the Marcos regime, abolished by Aquino.
*   **Comprehensive Agrarian Reform Law (CARP):** The 1988 law aimed at redistributing agricultural lands from landowners to tenant-farmers.
*   **Autonomous Region in Muslim Mindanao (ARMM):** A self-governing region created in 1989 following peace talks with the MNLF.
*   **Mendiola Massacre:** A 1987 incident where state security forces fired on peaceful agrarian protesters near the presidential palace, killing at least 12.
*   **Bataan Nuclear Power Plant (BNPP):** A nuclear power plant built during the Marcos era that Aquino deactivated, citing safety and corruption concerns.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Agrarian Reform Implementation:** Critics argued Aquino's administration was slow to implement land reform and that her family's Hacienda Luisita used a stock distribution scheme to circumvent the spirit of CARP.
*   **Economic Management:** Her decision to honor the Marcos-era foreign debt is debated; supporters say it restored creditworthiness, while critics argue it burdened the economy.
*   **Handling of Insurgencies:** The release of communist prisoners and the subsequent collapse of peace talks after the Mendiola Massacre remain points of contention regarding her approach to the insurgency.
*   **Legacy of Political Instability:** Her presidency was marked by numerous coup attempts and cabinet infighting, raising questions about her administration's ability to consolidate power and ensure stability after the revolution.

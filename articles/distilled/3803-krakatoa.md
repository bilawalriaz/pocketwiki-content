# Krakatoa

## Overview
Krakatoa (Krakatau) is a volcanic island group in Indonesia's Sunda Strait, most famous for its catastrophic 1883 eruption—one of the deadliest and most powerful in recorded history. The event destroyed most of the original island, created a massive caldera, and triggered deadly tsunamis, killing at least 36,417 people. The site remains volcanically active, with a new island, Anak Krakatau ("Child of Krakatoa"), emerging in 1927 and continuing to erupt, including a major collapse in 2018 that caused a tsunami.

## Timeline
- **c. 416 AD** — A legendary eruption of "Kapi" (possibly Krakatoa) is recorded in the Javanese *Pustaka Raja*, but its historical reliability is dubious.
- **9th–16th centuries** — Seven eruptive events are tentatively recorded during Java's Sailendra dynasty.
- **May 1680** — A significant eruption is observed by Dutch travelers.
- **20 May 1883** — A series of lesser eruptions begins, releasing steam and ash plumes.
- **27 August 1883** — Four massive explosions culminate the eruption, destroying two-thirds of Krakatoa island and creating a caldera.
- **27 August 1883** — The explosion is heard ~4,800 km away; tsunamis and pyroclastic flows kill at least 36,417 people.
- **29 December 1927** — A submarine lava dome emerges in the 1883 caldera, marking the birth of Anak Krakatau.
- **August 1930** — Anak Krakatau breaks water permanently, forming a new island.
- **22 December 2018** — A flank collapse of Anak Krakatau triggers a deadly tsunami, reducing the island's height from over 400 m to 110 m.
- **July 2026** — The most recent recorded eruption at Anak Krakatau begins.

## Body

### The 1883 Eruption
The cataclysmic eruption began with lesser activity in May 1883. On 27 August, four colossal explosions occurred. The third was the most violent, with a pressure wave that circled the globe three and a half times. The eruption ejected approximately 25 km³ of rock and propelled ash to a height of 80 km. The sound was heard over 4,800 km away. The primary causes of death were tsunamis (up to 30 meters high) and pyroclastic flows. The official Dutch death toll was 36,417, though some estimates exceed 120,000. The eruption destroyed the islands of Perboewatan and Danan, and most of Rakata, leaving only remnants of the original Krakatoa.

### Anak Krakatau and Modern Activity
Geologist Rogier Verbeek predicted new activity would occur between the former cones of Perboewatan and Danan. This was confirmed in 1927 when a submarine dome emerged. After several ephemeral islands were eroded, Anak Krakatau formed permanently in 1930. It has erupted periodically since. On 22 December 2018, a large collapse of its southwestern flank caused a tsunami with waves up to five meters, killing over 400 people. The island's height was reduced from over 400 m to 110 m. A new cinder cone has since grown from the collapse scar.

### Etymology and Early History
The name "Krakatoa" likely derives from Sanskrit *karka* or *karkaṭa*, meaning "lobster" or " crab," as seen in early records like the 15th-century *Bujangga Manik* ("Rakata"). The earliest Western mention is on a 1611 map as "Pulo Carcata." The Dutch established a naval station in 1620 and a penal colony in 1809, but the islands were uninhabited by the 1880s. A significant eruption was documented in 1680.

### Biological Research and Conservation
The 1883 eruption provided a unique case study in island biogeography—the study of species distribution on islands. Researchers debated whether the islands were completely sterilized. The first post-eruption visit in 1884 found only a spider, but life recolonized rapidly via ocean currents and birds. In 1916, a German pumice miner, Johann Handl, inadvertently introduced black rats. Krakatoa was declared a nature reserve in 1921 and is now part of the UNESCO World Heritage Site "Ujung Kulon National Park and Krakatau Nature Reserve."

## Terms
- **Caldera:** A large volcanic crater formed by the collapse of a volcano following an eruption.
- **Pyroclastic flow:** A fast-moving current of hot gas and volcanic matter that flows down the side of a volcano.
- **Volcanic Explosivity Index (VEI):** A measure of the explosiveness of volcanic eruptions; Krakatoa's 1883 eruption was a VEI 6.
- **Subduction zone:** A region where one tectonic plate sinks beneath another; Krakatoa sits above the subduction of the Indo-Australian Plate beneath the Eurasian Plate.
- **Island biogeography:** The study of the distribution of species on islands, influenced by factors like isolation and distance from source populations.
- **Phreatomagmatic eruption:** An explosive eruption caused by the interaction of magma with water.
- **Pumice:** A lightweight, porous volcanic rock formed from frothy lava.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **The 535 AD eruption hypothesis:** Some researchers have postulated a violent eruption of Krakatoa in 535 AD caused global climate changes, but drilling projects in the Sunda Strait have found no geological evidence for this event.
- **The "Krakatoa problem":** A central debate in biogeography is whether the 1883 eruption completely sterilized the islands or if some indigenous life survived, which affects models of ecological recolonization.
- **The 416 AD event:** The historical reliability of the *Pustaka Raja*'s account of a catastrophic eruption of "Kapi" is highly dubious, and no geological evidence substantiates it.

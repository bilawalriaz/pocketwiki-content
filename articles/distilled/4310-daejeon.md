# Daejeon

## Overview
Daejeon is South Korea's fifth-largest metropolis and a major national hub for administration, research, and transportation. Its significance stems from its central location, its role as a government center relocated from Seoul, and its concentration of leading universities and research institutes, making it the country's premier science and technology city.

## Timeline
- **1481** — The name "Daejeon" (Hanja for "Great Fields") first appears in the geography book *Tongguk yŏji sŭngnam*.
- **1900** — The Gyeongbu Line railway route is finalized, leading to the establishment of Daejeon Station in 1904 and spurring urbanization.
- **1914** — Hoedeok and Jinjam counties are merged to form Daejeon County, named after the village with the train station.
- **1989** — Daejeon is elevated to a Directly Governed City (now Metropolitan City), absorbing the abolished Daedeok County.
- **1993** — The city hosts the international Expo '93, boosting its national profile.
- **1997** — The Daejeon Government Complex opens, consolidating national administrative functions moved from Seoul.
- **2002** — Daejeon World Cup Stadium hosts matches for the FIFA World Cup.
- **2007** — Daejeon Subway Line 1 begins full operations.
- **2013** — Sejong Special Self-Governing City is established north of Daejeon, leading to a population outflow from Daejeon.
- **2027** — Daejeon is scheduled to be the main host city for the Summer World University Games.

## Body

### Etymology and Early History
The city's name, Daejeon (大田), is the Hanja (Chinese character) translation of the native Korean place name "Hanbat" (한밭), meaning "Great Fields." This name first appeared in records in 1481. Archaeological evidence suggests human habitation in the area since the Paleolithic era. During the Proto-Three Kingdoms period, the region was part of the Mahan confederacy, possibly including states like Sinheunguk and Naebiri-guk. In the Three Kingdoms period, it became part of Baekje territory, known as Usul-gun, and was a frequent battleground between Baekje, Goguryeo, and Silla.

### Administrative Evolution
After the unification of the Three Kingdoms by Silla, the area was part of Ungcheonju. During the Goryeo dynasty, it was divided into counties like Hoedeok-hyeon and Jinjam-hyeon, which gained administrative autonomy. The modern city's core was originally a small village within Gongju. The pivotal change came with the arrival of the railway in 1904, which caused rapid growth. In 1914, Japanese colonial authorities merged Hoedeok and Jinjam counties with a township from Gongju to create Daejeon County. The city expanded significantly in the 1960s-1980s and was elevated to a metropolitan city in 1989.

### Modern Development and Government Hub
From the 1980s, the South Korean government began relocating national administrative functions from Seoul to Daejeon to promote balanced development. This culminated in the opening of the Government Complex in 1997, housing numerous national agencies. The city's status as a science hub was solidified with the presence of KAIST and the Daedeok Innopolis research cluster. However, the establishment of Sejong City in 2013 as a new administrative capital has led to a net population outflow from Daejeon to its northern neighbor.

### Geography and Climate
Daejeon is located in a central lowland valley, approximately 170 km south of Seoul. It is surrounded by mountains, including Gyeryongsan National Park to the west, and is traversed by three rivers that flow into the Geum River. The city has a monsoon-influenced, four-season climate with hot, humid summers and cold, dry winters.

### Research and Education
Daejeon is South Korea's foremost research and development center. The Daedeok Innopolis complex hosts over 800 corporations, 35 government research institutions, and six universities. Key institutes include KAIST, the Institute for Basic Science, the Korea Atomic Energy Research Institute, and the Electronic and Telecommunications Research Institute (ETRI), which developed CDMA technology. The city is also a major educational center with 23 universities and colleges.

### Culture and Sports
The city features numerous parks and cultural sites, including the Expo Science Park from the 1993 World Expo and the Daejeon Hanbat Arboretum. It is home to professional sports teams like the Daejeon Hana Citizen football club and the Hanwha Eagles baseball team. The Daejeon World Cup Stadium was a venue for the 2002 FIFA World Cup.

## Terms
- **Hanja**: Chinese characters used in the Korean writing system.
- **Chaebol**: Large, family-owned South Korean conglomerates (e.g., Samsung, LG).
- **Daedeok Innopolis**: A major science and technology park in Daejeon housing numerous research institutes and corporate R&D centers.
- **Metropolitan City (Gwangyeoksi)**: A high-level administrative division in South Korea for major cities, equivalent to a province.
- **KTX/SRT**: South Korea's high-speed rail systems.
- **Mahan**: One of the Three Han states in the Proto-Three Kingdoms period of Korea.
- **Hyanggyo**: Confucian schools established during the Goryeo and Joseon dynasties.
- **Seowon**: Private Confucian academies in the Joseon dynasty.
- **Tashu**: Daejeon's public bicycle-sharing system.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The exact location of the Proto-Three Kingdoms state of Sinheunguk is debated, with theories placing it in Daejeon, Seosan, or Yesan County.
- The historical administrative affiliation of the Daejeon area during the Three Kingdoms period (e.g., whether Jinhyeon-hyeon belonged to Baekje's Northern or Eastern Division) is subject to scholarly interpretation.
- The long-term demographic and economic impact of the establishment of Sejong City on Daejeon remains a significant open question.

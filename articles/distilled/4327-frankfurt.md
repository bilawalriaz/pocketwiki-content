# Frankfurt

## Overview
Frankfurt am Main is Germany's fifth-largest city and a major global hub for finance, commerce, and transportation. Located on the river Main in the state of Hesse, it is the heart of the Rhine-Main metropolitan region and home to the European Central Bank. Its history as an imperial city and free city-state, combined with its post-WWII reconstruction as a financial center, has shaped its modern identity as a diverse, international metropolis known for its skyline and economic power.

## Body

### History and Political Status
Frankfurt was first mentioned in 794 and became an Imperial Free City in 1372, a status it held for centuries. It was a key site for Holy Roman Empire coronations from 1562 until 1792. The city lost its sovereignty in 1806, regained it in 1815, and was the seat of the 1848 Frankfurt Parliament, which attempted to create a unified German constitution. Following the Austro-Prussian War, Prussia annexed Frankfurt in 1866. It became part of Hesse in 1945. The city was heavily bombed in WWII, destroying its medieval center, and was later considered for West Germany's capital but lost to Bonn.

### Economy and Finance
Frankfurt is a leading global financial center, hosting the European Central Bank (ECB), the Deutsche Bundesbank, and the Frankfurt Stock Exchange. It is home to the headquarters of major German banks like Deutsche Bank and Commerzbank, as well as numerous international financial institutions. The city's economy is further supported by the Frankfurt Trade Fair (Messe Frankfurt), one of the world's largest, and Frankfurt Airport, Germany's busiest and a primary hub for Lufthansa. The financial district's skyscrapers have earned it the nickname "Mainhattan."

### Geography and Demographics
The city is located on the river Main, south of the Taunus mountains. Its urban area forms a continuous conurbation with Offenbach am Main. Frankfurt is culturally diverse, with over 51% of its population having a migrant background as of 2015. It is a major center for commerce, culture, education, and transportation, with a skyline featuring many of Europe's tallest skyscrapers. The city has a temperate oceanic climate.

### Culture and Landmarks
Frankfurt boasts over 30 museums, many along the "Museumsufer" (Museum Embankment). Key landmarks include the Römer (city hall), Frankfurt Cathedral (site of imperial coronations), St. Paul's Church (seat of the 1848 Parliament), and the Alte Oper concert hall. The city is also known for its culinary specialties like Apfelwein (apple wine) and Grüne Soße (green sauce). It is a major center for trade fairs, including the world's largest book fair.

## Terms
- **Reichsstadt**: An Imperial Free City of the Holy Roman Empire, subordinate only to the Emperor.
- **Messe**: A trade fair; Frankfurt's Messe is one of the world's largest.
- **S-Bahn**: Suburban commuter rail system.
- **U-Bahn**: Underground or light rail system.
- **Apfelwein**: Regional apple wine or hard cider, a traditional drink.
- **Grüne Soße**: A traditional Frankfurt sauce made with seven fresh herbs.
- **Mainhattan**: A nickname for Frankfurt's skyline, combining the river Main and Manhattan.
- **Museumsufer**: A concentration of museums along both banks of the Main river.

## Debates and Open Questions
The article notes that Frankfurt's high crime rate statistics are often criticized for being misleading, as they are calculated based on the administrative population while the city hosts a much larger daily population of commuters and travelers. The choice of Bonn over Frankfurt as West Germany's capital in 1949 was influenced by political concerns that selecting Frankfurt might weaken support for eventual reunification and a return to Berlin.

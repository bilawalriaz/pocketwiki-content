# Cardiff

## Overview
Cardiff is the capital and largest city of Wales, serving as the nation's main commercial, cultural, and administrative centre. Its growth from a small Roman fort to a major city was driven by its strategic location as a coal port during the Industrial Revolution, and it now anchors the Cardiff Capital Region, home to roughly half of Wales's population.

## Timeline
- **c. 6000 BC** — Archaeological evidence shows early Neolithic settlement in the area.
- **AD 75** — Romans establish a fort near the mouth of the River Taff.
- **1081** — William I orders construction of Cardiff Castle within the Roman fort walls.
- **1327** — Cardiff is declared an English staple port.
- **1536** — Laws in Wales Acts make Cardiff the county town of Glamorganshire.
- **1801** — Census records a population of only 1,870.
- **1830s–1870s** — Rapid growth begins with the building of docks for coal export.
- **1889** — Cardiff becomes a county borough.
- **1905** — King Edward VII grants Cardiff city status.
- **1955** — Cardiff is proclaimed the capital city of Wales.
- **1999** — The National Assembly for Wales (now the Senedd) is established in Cardiff Bay.
- **2021** — Census records the unitary authority population at 362,400.

## Body

### Origins and Early History
Archaeological sites like the St Lythans burial chamber indicate settlement from at least 6000 BC. The area was part of the Silures tribe's territory before the Roman conquest. A Roman fort was established in AD 75, later accompanied by a civilian settlement. After the Roman departure, the area was ruled by Welsh kingdoms until the Norman Conquest. William I began building Cardiff Castle in 1081, which became the heart of the Norman Marcher Lordship of Glamorgan. The medieval town was small, with a population between 1,500 and 2,000, and was burned by Owain Glyndŵr in 1404.

### Industrial Growth and Port Development
Cardiff was a minor town until the 19th century. The construction of docks by the Marquess of Bute, linked to the coalfields of the South Wales Valleys, transformed it into the world's largest coal port. Population grew rapidly, overtaking Merthyr and Swansea by 1881. Although Barry's docks surpassed Cardiff's in coal exports after 1901, Cardiff remained the administrative centre of the coal trade, where the price of coal was set. The city also developed a steelworks at East Moors in 1891.

### City Status, Capital Designation, and Modern Regeneration
Cardiff was granted city status in 1905 and proclaimed the capital of Wales in 1955, a decision influenced by political factors. The city's economy suffered with the decline of coal and the closure of the East Moors Steelworks in 1978. Regeneration efforts, particularly the redevelopment of Cardiff Bay, have been central to its modern revival. The establishment of the Senedd (Welsh Parliament) in Cardiff Bay in 1999 cemented its role as the national administrative centre.

### Economy and Media
Cardiff is the main engine of the Welsh economy, contributing nearly 20% of Welsh GDP. Its economy is dominated by finance, business services, public administration, education, and health. The city is a major centre for television and film production, hosting BBC Cymru Wales, S4C, and ITV Wales, with studios like Roath Lock producing shows such as *Doctor Who* and *Sherlock*. Tourism is also significant, with over 21 million visitors in 2017.

### Demography and Language
Cardiff's population is growing and ethnically diverse. The Welsh language has seen a resurgence, with speakers doubling between 1991 and 2021 to 12.2% of the population, aided by Welsh-medium education. The city is also home to speakers of at least 94 other languages, reflecting its diverse communities and large student population.

### Transport
Cardiff Central is the largest railway station in Wales. The city is connected by the M4 motorway and has an international airport at Rhoose. The South Wales Metro is an integrated transport system under development, centered on Cardiff. The Taff Trail provides a 55-mile walking and cycling route.

## Terms
- **Senedd**: The Welsh Parliament, based in Cardiff Bay since 1999.
- **Marcher Lordship**: A territory on the border between Norman England and Welsh kingdoms, ruled by a lord with significant autonomy.
- **Vicus**: A civilian settlement associated with a Roman military fort.
- **County Borough**: A type of local government district that is both a county and a borough, a status Cardiff held from 1889 to 1974.
- **Unitary Authority**: A type of local government that is responsible for all local services within its area, established in Cardiff in 1996.
- **Cardiff Capital Region**: A combined authority area with a population of 1.5 million, roughly half of Wales's total.
- **Fairtrade City**: A status granted to cities committed to supporting Fairtrade products, which Cardiff received in 2004.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The decision to make Cardiff the capital in 1955 is noted to have been influenced by political considerations (marginal Conservative constituencies) rather than a reasoned assessment of a capital's functions.
- The 1997 Welsh devolution referendum saw Cardiff voters reject the National Assembly for Wales by 55.4% to 44.2%, partly attributed to a preference for a British rather than exclusively Welsh identity in the city.
- The 2001 census population figures for Cardiff were disputed by the city council, which argued they seriously under-reported the population, particularly ethnic minorities in inner-city areas.

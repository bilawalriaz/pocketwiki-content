# Lysistrata

## Overview
*Lysistrata* is an ancient Greek comedy by Aristophanes, first performed in 411 BCE, that uses a sex strike to satirize the Peloponnesian War. The play is significant as a masterpiece of Old Comedy that critiques war and gender roles, and its central premise has inspired countless adaptations and protest movements.

## Timeline
- **424 BCE** — Aristophanes' *Knights* wins first prize at the Lenaia.
- **421 BCE** — The Peace of Nicias ends the first phase of the Peloponnesian War.
- **413 BCE** — Athens suffers a catastrophic defeat in the Sicilian Expedition.
- **411 BCE** — *Lysistrata* and *Thesmophoriazusae* are produced; a brief oligarchic revolution occurs in Athens.

## Body

### Plot and Themes
The play centers on Lysistrata, an Athenian woman who persuades women from warring Greek city-states to withhold sex from their husbands to force a peace. The women swear an oath and seize the Acropolis, which holds the state treasury, to cripple the war effort. The men, driven to desperation, eventually agree to negotiate. The comedy explores the battle of the sexes, the absurdity of war, and the potential for female agency in a male-dominated society.

### Historical Context and Conventions
Produced two years after the Sicilian disaster and during political turmoil, the play reflects Athens's war-weariness. It marks a shift in Aristophanes' style from Old Comedy conventions. Notable innovations include a divided Chorus (Old Men vs. Old Women) that later unifies, symbolizing reconciliation. The play lacks a traditional parabasis (a direct address to the audience) and features an informal agon (debate) between Lysistrata and a magistrate.

### Legacy and Influence
The play's core idea—a sex strike to end conflict—has been widely adapted. Early examples include John Fletcher's *The Woman's Prize* (c. 1611). Modern adaptations include the 2015 film *Chi-Raq* by Spike Lee, which transposes the plot to contemporary Chicago, and the 2003 Lysistrata Project, a global peace protest using readings of the play.

## Terms
- **Old Comedy**: The earliest genre of Athenian comedy, characterized by topical satire, a chorus, and direct audience address.
- **Parabasis**: A conventional speech in Old Comedy where the chorus addresses the audience directly.
- **Agon**: A formal, rhetorical debate between characters in a Greek comedy.
- **Peloponnesian War**: The protracted conflict (431–404 BCE) between Athens and its empire against the Peloponnesian League led by Sparta.
- **Acropolis**: The fortified hilltop in Athens containing key state buildings and the treasury.
- **Lenaia**: An ancient Athenian festival in January where dramatic competitions were held.
- **Scythian Archers**: The Athenian police force, often of foreign origin.

## Debates and Open Questions
The play's tone is a subject of scholarly debate: is it a genuine anti-war and feminist statement, or primarily a bawdy comedy that uses these themes for laughs? The effectiveness and sincerity of the women's political action versus the play's reliance on sexual humor and stereotypes remain points of analysis.

# Mars

## Overview
Mars is the fourth planet from the Sun and the second-smallest in the Solar System. A cold, desert world with a thin carbon dioxide atmosphere, it possesses roughly 38% of Earth's gravity and a surface area equivalent to all of Earth's dry land. Its distinct red color comes from iron oxide dust. Geological evidence indicates Mars once had liquid water and a thicker atmosphere, making it a primary target in the search for past or present extraterrestrial life. Since the 1960s, it has been explored by a continuous succession of robotic orbiters, landers, and rovers, and is the leading candidate for future human exploration.

## Timeline
- **~4.5 Ga** — Mars forms via runaway accretion from the protoplanetary disk; core differentiates.
- **~4.5–3.5 Ga (Noachian)** — Heavy bombardment creates oldest surfaces; Tharsis bulge forms; possible oceans; global magnetic field lost.
- **~3.5–2.9 Ga (Hesperian)** — Widespread volcanic activity creates lava plains; catastrophic flooding carves immense outflow channels.
- **~2.9 Ga–present (Amazonian)** — Current era; low impact rates; Olympus Mons forms; minor recent volcanic/aqueous activity (<20 Ma).
- **1534 BCE** — Egyptian astronomers record Mars's retrograde motion.
- **1609** — Kepler uses Tycho Brahe's data to determine Mars orbits in an ellipse, not a circle.
- **1877** — Asaph Hall discovers moons Phobos and Deimos; Schiaparelli maps "canali" (channels), later mistranslated as "canals."
- **1965** — Mariner 4 achieves first successful flyby, returning first close-up images; reveals thin atmosphere, no canals.
- **1971** — Mariner 9 becomes first spacecraft to orbit another planet; Mars 3 achieves first soft landing (contact lost seconds later).
- **1997** — Mars Pathfinder lands with *Sojourner* rover; begins continuous robotic presence on Mars.
- **2023** — Ten active spacecraft operate at Mars (eight orbiters, two rovers); *Perseverance* caches samples for future return.

## Body

### Formation and Early Evolution
Mars formed ~4.5 billion years ago (Ga) through runaway accretion in the protoplanetary disk. Its position farther from the Sun allowed volatile elements with low boiling points (chlorine, phosphorus, sulfur) to remain abundant, unlike on Earth where the young Sun's solar wind pushed them outward. The planet differentiated into a metallic core, silicate mantle, and crust. A massive impact ~4 Ga by a Pluto-sized body likely created the Borealis basin, covering 40% of the planet, and caused the **Martian dichotomy**: the smooth, low-lying northern plains versus the heavily cratered southern highlands. Mars lost its global magnetosphere ~4 Ga, likely due to asteroid strikes disrupting the core dynamo, exposing the atmosphere to stripping by the solar wind.

### Geological Periods
Martian history is divided into three primary periods named after type localities:
*   **Noachian (4.5–3.5 Ga):** Formation of oldest surfaces; intense cratering; formation of Tharsis volcanic upland; extensive valley networks and possible oceans indicate liquid water; magnetosphere ceases.
*   **Hesperian (3.5–3.3/2.9 Ga):** Dominated by massive volcanic eruptions creating vast lava plains (ridged plains); catastrophic groundwater releases carve immense **outflow channels** (e.g., Ma'adim Vallis, 700 km long).
*   **Amazonian (3.3/2.9 Ga–present):** Low cratering rates; diverse geology; growth of Olympus Mons and other Tharsis volcanoes; recent activity includes Athabasca Valles lava flows (~200 Ma) and Cerberus Fossae water flows (<20 Ma).

### Internal Structure
Mars is differentiated like Earth but with a thicker, rigid lithosphere (~250 km vs. Earth's ~100 km) due to sluggish mantle convection and lack of plate tectonics.
*   **Crust:** Average 42–56 km thick (thinner under Isidis Planitia at 6 km, thicker under Tharsis at 117 km); enriched in radioactive elements.
*   **Mantle:** Silicate; highly heterogeneous, preserving dense impactor fragments (~4 km across) from 4.5 Ga because no plate tectonics recycled them. A basal liquid silicate layer (150–180 km thick) sits above the core.
*   **Core:** Radius ~1,650–1,675 km (half planetary radius); iron-nickel enriched with light elements (S, O, C, H); at least partially molten (2000–2400 K). A solid inner core is debated: a 2023 InSight study suggested absence, while a 2025 reanalysis detected a ~613 km radius solid inner core.
*   **Seismicity:** InSight detected >450 marsquakes (2019), confirming tectonic activity driven by planetary cooling and Tharsis loading, not plate boundaries (though Valles Marineris may represent a relic plate boundary with 150 km transverse motion).

### Surface Geology and Major Features
The surface is primarily **tholeiitic basalt**, covered globally by fine iron(III) oxide dust (nanophase Fe₂O₃, goethite). Soil is slightly alkaline (pH 7.7) but contains 0.6% perchlorate (toxic to humans).
*   **Volcanoes:** Tharsis region hosts massive shield volcanoes. **Olympus Mons** is ~600 km wide; local relief >21 km (twice Mauna Kea base-to-peak); total elevation change from Amazonis Planitia ~26 km (3× Everest). Tallest or second-tallest mountain in Solar System (rivaled only by Rheasilvia on Vesta).
*   **Valles Marineris:** Canyon system 4,000 km long, up to 7 km deep (vs. Grand Canyon: 446 km, 1.8 km). Formed by crustal collapse due to Tharsis swelling; possibly a relic plate boundary.
*   **Impact Craters:** 43,000 craters ≥5 km diameter. Largest exposed is **Hellas** (2,300 km wide, 7 km deep). Craters show "rampart" ejecta morphology suggesting ground ice/water at impact.
*   **Polar Caps:** **Planum Boreum** (N) and **Planum Australe** (S). Layered deposits of water ice and dust; seasonal CO₂ frost/snow. South cap volume could cover planet 11 m deep if melted.
*   **Caves:** Seven "skylight" entrances on Arsia Mons flanks ("seven sisters"), 100–252 m wide, >73–96 m deep; potential radiation shelters.

### Atmosphere and Climate
*   **Composition:** 96% CO₂, 1.93% Ar, 1.89% N₂, traces O₂/H₂O. Dusty (1.5 µm particulates) giving tawny/pink sky.
*   **Pressure:** Mean 600 Pa (0.6% Earth); range 30 Pa (Olympus Mons) to 1,155 Pa (Hellas Planitia). Scale height 10.8 km (vs. Earth 6 km) due to low gravity.
*   **Loss:** No magnetosphere allows solar wind to strip ions; MAVEN orbiter measures ongoing loss.
*   **Methane:** Detected intermittently; origin debated (biological vs. serpentinization/geological). No consensus.
*   **Climate:** Axial tilt 25.19° (similar to Earth) creates seasons ~2× longer. High orbital eccentricity (0.09) makes southern summers hotter (+30°C) and shorter; northern seasons milder. Surface temps: −110°C to 35°C. Global dust storms (up to 160 km/h) occur near perihelion, raising global temp.
*   **Radiation:** Surface avg 0.64 mSv/day (vs. 0.5 mSv/day in LEO). Hellas Planitia lowest (0.342 mSv/day); lava tubes potentially 0.064 mSv/day.

### Hydrology: Past and Present
*   **Present:** Water exists as ice (polar caps, mid-latitude glaciers, subsurface), vapor (thin clouds, frost), and possibly transient brines. Liquid water unstable at surface pressure (<1% Earth); only possible briefly at lowest elevations. Recurring Slope Lineae (RSL) are dark streaks on slopes; 2015 data suggested briny flows, later studies favor dry granular flows. Subsurface ice detected widely (e.g., Utopia Planitia volume = Lake Superior; Valles Marineris).
*   **Past:** Abundant evidence for liquid water: dendritic valley networks (Noachian, imply precipitation/runoff), outflow channels (Hesperian, imply catastrophic aquifer release), crater lakes/deltas/alluvial fans, and aqueous minerals (hematite, goethite, jarosite, gypsum, silica). Deuterium/hydrogen ratio (5–7× Earth) implies massive water loss; ancient ocean (size of Arctic) hypothesized for northern plains but controversial (climate models struggle to sustain warmth). Curiosity found subsurface hydration up to 4% at 60 cm depth.

### Moons: Phobos and Deimos
Two small, irregular bodies discovered 1877 by Asaph Hall.
*   **Phobos:** ~22 km diameter; orbits 9,376 km (below synchronous altitude); rises west, sets east, period 11 hrs. Tidal forces decaying orbit; will crash or form ring in ~50 Myr. Low density/porous; spectral data suggests phyllosilicates (Mars-like) rather than chondritic (asteroid-like).
*   **Deimos:** ~12 km diameter; orbits 23,460 km (near synchronous); rises east, very slowly.
*   **Origin Debated:** 1) Captured asteroids (low albedo, composition) but circular equatorial orbits are atypical. 2) Giant impact ejecta reaccretion (similar to Moon formation; supported by Phobos porosity/mineralogy). 3) Remnants of a larger, destroyed moon. A 2023 study of Deimos's inclination suggests Mars had a ring system 3.5–4 Ga from a moon 20× Phobos mass.

### Exploration History
*   **Early Telescopic:** Kepler (1609) solves orbit; Galileo (1610) first telescope view; Schiaparelli (1877) maps "canali"; Lowell popularizes "canals"/intelligent life hypothesis; Antoniadi (1909) debunks canals with large telescope.
*   **First Flybys/Orbiters:** Mars 1 (1963, USSR, lost); Mariner 4 (1965, NASA, first success); Mariner 9 (1971, first orbiter of another planet).
*   **Landers:** Mars 3 (1971, USSR, first soft landing, failed at 20 sec); Vikings 1/2 (1976, NASA, first successful biology experiments—inconclusive); Pathfinder/*Sojourner* (1997, first rover).
*   **Continuous Presence (1997–present):** Orbiters (Odyssey, MRO, MAVEN, TGO, Hope, Tianwen-1) + Rovers (Spirit, Opportunity, Curiosity, Perseverance, Zhurong). As of 2023: 10 active spacecraft.
*   **Future:** Sample return (NASA/ESA 2026 concept; China Tianwen-3 2028/2030); Rosalind Franklin rover (ESA, 2028+); Crewed missions proposed (NASA 2030s study deemed unfeasible; China 2033 goal; SpaceX Starship development for colonization).

### Habitability and Life
*   **Past:** Significantly more habitable (thicker atmosphere, liquid water, magnetosphere). Viking Labeled Release experiment (1976) showed metabolic-like response; Gilbert Levin maintains it detected life; consensus remains abiotic.
*   **Present:** Surface hostile: high UV/ionizing radiation, perchlorates, low pressure, no magnetosphere. Organics must be buried to survive. Methane/formaldehyde detections ambiguous. Impact glass could preserve biosignatures.
*   **Cheyava Falls (2024):** Rock sampled by Perseverance designated "potential biosignature"; origin undetermined.

## Terms
- ****Martian Dichotomy**** — The sharp north–south divide: northern lowlands (smooth, young, thin crust) vs. southern highlands (cratered, old, thick crust).
- ****Noachian/Hesperian/Amazonian**** — The three major geological time periods of Mars, defined by crater density and dominant processes (impacts/water, volcanism/floods, recent slow change).
- ****Tharsis**** — Vast equatorial volcanic plateau/bulge (~5,000 km wide, up to 10 km high) hosting the largest volcanoes; its weight drove global tectonics (e.g., Valles Marineris).
- ****Outflow Channels**** — Immense, scoured channels (up to 100s km wide, 1000s km long) carved by catastrophic release of groundwater, not gradual river erosion.
- ****Rampart Craters**** — Impact craters with fluidized, lobate ejecta blankets, interpreted as evidence for subsurface ice/volatiles mobilized during impact.
- ****Perchlorate (ClO₄⁻)**** — Oxidizing salt (~0.6% in soil) toxic to humans; lowers water freezing point, enabling brines; destroys organics under UV radiation.
- ****Recurring Slope Lineae (RSL)**** — Seasonal dark streaks on warm slopes; hypothesized as brine flows but now often interpreted as dry granular flows.
- ****Areoid**** — The reference surface for zero elevation on Mars (analogous to Earth's geoid), defined by 610.5 Pa atmospheric pressure (triple point of water).
- ****Synchronous Orbit**** — Orbital altitude where period matches planetary rotation; Phobos orbits below it (decaying), Deimos just above it.
- ****Serpentinization**** — Abiotic geochemical reaction of olivine with water/CO₂ producing hydrogen and methane; a non-biological methane source on Mars.

## Debates and open questions
*   **Core State:** Does a solid inner core exist? (2023 vs. 2025 InSight analyses conflict).
*   **Dichotomy Origin:** Giant impact (Borealis basin) vs. endogenic mantle convection/degree-1 convection?
*   **Ancient Ocean:** Did a large northern ocean exist? Deuterium enrichment supports it; climate models struggle to keep it liquid.
*   **Methane Source:** Biological (extant life) vs. abiotic (serpentinization, clathrate release, UV degradation of organics)? Why is it variable and localized?
*   **RSL Mechanism:** Wet (brine seepage) vs. dry (granular flow triggered by thermal creep/CO₂ sublimation)?
*   **Viking Biology:** Did the Labeled Release experiment detect metabolism? Consensus says no (perchlorates explain reactivity), but PI Levin dissents.
*   **Moons' Origin:** Captured asteroids vs. giant impact ejecta vs. remnant of prior moon system? Phobos's porosity/mineralogy favors impact origin.
*   **Present Habitability:** Can any known terrestrial extremophile survive surface conditions (radiation, perchlorates, desiccation)? Or only deep subsurface?
*   **Sample Return Feasibility:** Technical, budgetary, and planetary protection challenges for NASA/ESA and China missions.

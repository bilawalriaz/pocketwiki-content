# Lolita

## Overview
*Lolita* is a 1955 novel by Vladimir Nabokov, narrated by the French literature professor Humbert Humbert. It details his obsession with and sexual abuse of his 12-year-old stepdaughter, Dolores Haze, whom he calls "Lolita." The novel is a landmark of 20th-century literature, celebrated for its complex style and controversial subject matter, exploring themes of obsession, unreliable narration, and the erasure of a victim's identity.

## Timeline
- **1953** — Nabokov completes the manuscript.
- **1955** — Published in Paris by Olympia Press after rejection by U.S. and U.K. publishers.
- **1958** — First U.S. edition published by G.P. Putnam's Sons; becomes a bestseller.
- **1962** — Stanley Kubrick's film adaptation released.
- **1967** — Nabokov's Russian translation published in New York.
- **1997** — Adrian Lyne's film adaptation released.
- **2008** — *Approaches to Teaching Nabokov's Lolita* published, addressing pedagogical challenges.
- **2023** — Banned or restricted in several U.S. school districts.

## Body

### Plot and Narration
The novel is presented as a memoir by Humbert Humbert, who writes from jail awaiting trial. He recounts his lifelong obsession with "nymphets"—girls aged 9 to 14—triggered by a childhood love for Annabel Leigh, who died young. In 1947, he becomes the lodger of Charlotte Haze and becomes sexually obsessed with her 12-year-old daughter, Dolores. He marries Charlotte to remain close to Dolores. After Charlotte discovers his diary and is killed in an accident, Humbert abducts Dolores, beginning a cross-country trip of sexual abuse. He later discovers that the playwright Clare Quilty, who had been following them, took Dolores from a hospital. After finding a pregnant, 17-year-old Dolores married and in poverty, Humbert gives her money and then murders Quilty. He is arrested and dies in prison; Dolores dies in childbirth.

### Style, Interpretation, and Controversy
The novel is narrated by Humbert, an unreliable narrator whose eloquent, self-justifying prose is filled with wordplay and literary allusions. Critics debate whether it is an "erotic novel" or a literary work that uses erotic motifs to expose the mind of a predator. Nabokov stated he cared not for public morals but for "aesthetic bliss." A central critical theme is the silencing of Dolores; her perspective is absent, and her identity is overwritten by Humbert's fantasy of "Lolita." This solipsism is seen by some as a metaphor for totalitarianism. The novel's moral complexity is highlighted by its ability to seduce readers into sympathizing with Humbert before revealing his monstrousness.

### Publication and Reception
After multiple rejections, *Lolita* was published in France by Olympia Press, a publisher of pornography. Initial reviews were scarce until Graham Greene praised it, sparking a public controversy in the UK and France, where it was banned. The first U.S. edition was a commercial success. The novel has since been included in numerous "best of" lists and translated into many languages. It remains controversial, with ongoing debates about its depiction of child sexual abuse and its frequent banning in schools.

### Adaptations and Cultural Legacy
*Lolita* has been adapted into two major films (1962, 1997), an opera, ballets, and several stage plays. The name "Lolita" has entered popular culture to describe a precociously seductive young girl, often divorced from the novel's context of victimization. The novel has inspired numerous literary pastiches and derivative works, including *Reading Lolita in Tehran* (2003) and *My Dark Vanessa* (2020), and is referenced extensively in music, film, and television.

## Terms
- **Nymphet**: A term coined by Humbert to describe a sexually precocious girl between the ages of 9 and 14, whom he sees as a supernatural being.
- **Unreliable narrator**: A narrator whose credibility is compromised, forcing the reader to question the truth of the story. Humbert is a classic example.
- **Solipsism**: The philosophical idea that only one's own mind is sure to exist. Critics use it to describe Humbert's complete disregard for Dolores's independent reality.
- **Doppelgänger**: A literary double. The mysterious Clare Quilty is seen as Humbert's dark double.
- **Olympia Press**: The Parisian publisher that first released *Lolita* in 1955, known for publishing both literary works and pornography.
- **Cryptomnesia**: Hidden memory, where a forgotten memory resurfaces as a new idea. A theory suggests Nabokov may have been influenced by a 1916 German story also titled "Lolita."

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Is it an erotic novel?** Critics are divided. Some classify it as erotic literature, while others, including Nabokov, argue it is a literary novel that uses eroticism to critique its narrator.
- **The nature of Humbert's unreliability:** Scholars debate whether his unreliability stems from deliberate deceit, self-delusion, or cognitive distortion, and how this affects the reader's moral judgment.
- **Was it rape?** While Humbert's actions are legally and morally rape, some critics and even Nabokov's biographer have downplayed the term, a view vigorously disputed by others who argue it blames the victim.
- **Plagiarism or influence?** The discovery of a 1916 German short story titled "Lolita" with a similar premise has sparked debate about whether Nabokov had cryptomnesia or if the similarities are coincidental.

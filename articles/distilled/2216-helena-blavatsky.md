# Helena Blavatsky

## Overview
Helena Petrovna Blavatsky (1831–1891) was a Russian-born mystic and writer who co-founded the Theosophical Society in 1875. She became the primary founder of Theosophy, a belief system positing a universal "Ancient Wisdom" underlying all religions. Her life blended claims of esoteric training with accusations of fraud, but she profoundly influenced the spread of Eastern religious ideas in the West and modern Western esotericism.

## Timeline
- **12 August 1831** — Born in Yekaterinoslav, Russian Empire (now Dnipro, Ukraine).
- **1849** — Married Nikifor Blavatsky; shortly after, began claiming extensive world travels.
- **1873** — Arrived in New York City; met Henry Steel Olcott.
- **7 September 1875** — Co-founded the Theosophical Society with Olcott and William Quan Judge in New York.
- **1877** — Published *Isis Unveiled*, outlining her Theosophical worldview.
- **1879** — Moved to India with Olcott; began publication of *The Theosophist*.
- **May 1880** — Formally converted to Buddhism in Ceylon (Sri Lanka) with Olcott.
- **1882** — The Theosophical Society purchased its Adyar headquarters in Madras, India.
- **1885** — Left India due to ill health and the Coulomb Affair controversy; settled in Europe.
- **1888** — Published *The Secret Doctrine*; established the Esoteric Section of the Society.
- **8 May 1891** — Died of influenza in London, England.

## Body

### Early Life and World Travels
Born into an aristocratic family, Blavatsky had a mobile childhood. She claimed to have discovered her great-grandfather's occult library in her youth. After marrying Nikifor Blavatsky in 1849, she soon left him, describing nine years of world travels through Europe, the Americas, Asia, and the Middle East. She claimed to have encountered spiritual adepts, the "Masters of the Ancient Wisdom," notably Master Morya, who sent her to Tibet for training. The veracity of these travels is heavily disputed due to a lack of independent verification and her own conflicting accounts.

### Founding Theosophy and Early Writings
By the early 1870s, Blavatsky was involved in the Spiritualist movement but argued that entities contacted by mediums were not spirits of the dead. After moving to New York City in 1873, she met Henry Steel Olcott. Together, they co-founded the Theosophical Society in 1875, aiming to study comparative religion, philosophy, and science, and to investigate unexplained laws of nature. In 1877, Blavatsky published *Isis Unveiled*, which posited that all world religions stemmed from a single "Ancient Wisdom" connected to Hermeticism and Neoplatonism. The book was a commercial success despite negative reviews.

### Years in India and Controversy
In 1879, Blavatsky and Olcott moved to India, where Theosophy gained rapid popularity, partly by championing Indian religions against British colonial rule. They allied with the Hindu reform movement Arya Samaj and formally converted to Buddhism in 1880. The Society was rocked by the "Coulomb Affair" in 1884, when former associates accused Blavatsky of faking paranormal phenomena. This led to a damaging 1885 report by the Society for Psychical Research, accusing her of fraud and espionage. The Society later retracted the report's findings in 1986, though questions remain.

### Final Years and Major Works
Blavatsky returned to Europe in 1885 due to failing health. She settled in London, established the Blavatsky Lodge, and published her magnum opus, *The Secret Doctrine* (1888). This work detailed her complex cosmogony, including theories of "Root Races" (like the Aryans and Atlanteans) and a sevenfold model of the human being. She also published *The Key to Theosophy* and *The Voice of the Silence*. She died of influenza in 1891.

## Terms
- **Theosophy**: A belief system meaning "divine wisdom," positing a universal "Ancient Wisdom" underlying all religions and seeking to synthesize science, religion, and philosophy.
- **Masters (Mahatmas)**: Spiritual adepts (e.g., Master Morya, Koot Hoomi) whom Blavatsky claimed were real beings who guided her and transmitted Theosophical teachings.
- **Ancient Wisdom**: The core Theosophical concept of a single, primordial spiritual truth known to ancient sages and underlying all world religions.
- **Root Races**: A central doctrine describing seven evolutionary stages of humanity, including the Hyperboreans, Lemurians, Atlanteans, and the current fifth, "Aryan," race.
- **Karma**: The law of cause and effect governing reincarnation, where actions in one life determine circumstances in future lives.
- **Reincarnation**: The belief in the rebirth of the soul in a new physical body after death.
- **Esoteric**: Knowledge intended for a small group of initiated people, as opposed to public (exoteric) teachings.
- **Spiritualism**: A 19th-century movement based on the belief that the living can communicate with the spirits of the dead, often through mediums.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Authenticity of Travels and Masters**: The central debate concerns whether Blavatsky's claimed travels, especially to Tibet, and her encounters with the "Masters" were real or fabricated, given the lack of independent evidence and her contradictory accounts.
- **Plagiarism and Authorship**: Critics argue that Blavatsky's writings were heavily plagiarized from older esoteric sources. The authorship of the "Mahatma Letters" is also disputed, with some scholars claiming she wrote them herself.
- **The Coulomb Affair and SPR Report**: The accusations of fraud and the 1885 Society for Psychical Research report, which accused Blavatsky of being a Russian spy and a charlatan, remain contentious. While the SPR retracted its report in 1986, many questions about the alleged paranormal phenomena were left unanswered.
- **Interpretation of Doctrines**: Scholars debate whether Blavatsky's cosmological theories, like the Root Races, were literal history or symbolic narratives. Her ideas on race have also been criticized for containing antisemitic and racist elements, though some biographers argue she would have disavowed later racist interpretations.

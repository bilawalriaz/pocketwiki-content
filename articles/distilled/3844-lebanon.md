# Lebanon

## Overview
Lebanon is a small, multi-confessional country in the Levant region of West Asia, historically a crossroads of civilizations and trade. Its modern identity is defined by a complex power-sharing system among its 18 recognized religious groups, a history of civil war and foreign intervention, and a severe ongoing economic and political crisis.

## Timeline
- **c. 5000 BC** — Evidence of early settlement found in Byblos.
- **3200–539 BC** — Part of Phoenicia, a maritime civilization.
- **64 BC** — Region annexed by the Roman Republic.
- **1861** — Mount Lebanon Mutasarrifate established as an autonomous Ottoman province for Maronite Christians.
- **1920** — Greater Lebanon established under French Mandate.
- **1943** — Independence from Free France; National Pact establishes confessional government.
- **1975–1990** — Lebanese Civil War.
- **2005** — Withdrawal of Syrian troops following the assassination of Rafic Hariri.
- **2020** — Beirut port explosion; deepening economic collapse.
- **2024** — Escalated Israel–Hezbollah conflict; Israeli invasion of southern Lebanon.
- **2025** — Joseph Aoun elected president; Nawaf Salam forms new government.

## Body

### Antiquity and Medieval Rule
Human habitation in Lebanon dates to 5000 BC. From 3200 to 539 BC, it was part of Phoenicia, a seafaring civilization credited with inventing the oldest verified alphabet. The region was later incorporated into the Roman, Byzantine, and successive Arab Islamic caliphates. The 11th century saw the establishment of Christian Crusader states, which fell to the Ayyubids and Mamluks. The Maronite Christian community, established in the mountains during the late Roman period, maintained a degree of autonomy through these successive rulers.

### Ottoman Rule and the French Mandate
Lebanon came under Ottoman rule in the early 16th century. Following the 1860 Mount Lebanon civil war, the Ottoman Empire, under European pressure, created the Mount Lebanon Mutasarrifate as an autonomous province for Maronite Christians. After World War I and the collapse of the Ottoman Empire, the region became a French Mandate. France established "Greater Lebanon" in 1920, uniting the Mount Lebanon Mutasarrifate with other regions to form the basis of the modern state.

### Independence and Civil War
Lebanon gained independence from Free France in 1943, establishing a confessional system where major religious groups are apportioned specific political powers (the National Pact). This system, while initially stable, was shattered by the Lebanese Civil War (1975–1990), which killed an estimated 150,000 people. The war involved complex alliances among Christian, Muslim, and Palestinian factions, and led to military occupations by Syria (1976–2005) and Israel (1985–2000). The Taif Agreement of 1989 ended the war but did not fully dismantle the sectarian political structure.

### Post-War Conflicts and Crisis
Since the civil war, Lebanon has experienced periods of instability, including the 2006 Lebanon War with Israel and the spillover from the Syrian civil war, which brought over a million refugees. A massive economic crisis, described by the World Bank as one of the worst since the 19th century, began in 2019. It has caused the currency to collapse, poverty to soar, and basic services to fail. The 2020 Beirut port explosion further devastated the country. In 2024, a major conflict erupted between Israel and Hezbollah, leading to an Israeli invasion and a subsequent ceasefire agreement.

## Terms
- **Confessionalism**: A system of government where political power is apportioned among religious communities.
- **National Pact**: An unwritten 1943 agreement that established the confessional power-sharing system, assigning key offices to specific sects.
- **Taif Agreement**: The 1989 accord that ended the civil war, adjusted the Christian-Muslim power ratio in parliament to 1:1, and called for Syrian withdrawal.
- **Maronites**: An Eastern Catholic Christian sect historically centered in Mount Lebanon.
- **Hezbollah**: A Shiite Islamist militant group and political party, founded in the early 1980s with Iranian support.
- **Mutasarrifate**: An administrative division of the Ottoman Empire; the Mount Lebanon Mutasarrifate was an autonomous province.
- **Levant**: A large region in the Eastern Mediterranean, including modern-day Lebanon, Syria, Jordan, Israel, and Palestine.
- **Druze**: An ethno-religious group originating from Ismaili Islam, with a distinct faith.

## Debates and Open questions
- The relative sizes of Lebanon's religious communities are a sensitive and debated issue, as no official census has been conducted since 1932 to avoid upsetting the confessional political balance.
- The full implementation of the Taif Agreement, particularly the de-confessionalization of the political system, remains unfulfilled and is a point of ongoing political contention.
- The future role and integration of Hezbollah within the Lebanese state, especially following the 2024 conflict and the weakening of its ally Syria, is a critical open question.

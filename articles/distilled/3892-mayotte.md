# Mayotte

## Overview
Mayotte is a French overseas department and region in the Mozambique Channel, part of the Comoros archipelago. It is France's poorest department but significantly wealthier than its neighbors, making it a magnet for migration. Its status evolved from a sultanate to a French colony, and it chose to remain French when the Comoros gained independence, becoming a full department in 2011. The territory faces major challenges including poverty, illegal immigration, and a young, rapidly growing population.

## Timeline
- **c. 1500** — The Maore sultanate is established on the island.
- **1841** — Sultan Andriantsoly sells Mayotte to France; slavery is abolished.
- **1974-1976** — Mayotte votes in referendums to remain French, unlike the other Comoros islands.
- **29 March 2009** — A referendum approves changing Mayotte's status to a full French department.
- **31 March 2011** — Mayotte officially becomes the 101st department of France.
- **1 January 2014** — Mayotte becomes an outermost region of the European Union.
- **11 November 2018** — A major seismic event is recorded globally, later linked to a new undersea volcano.
- **December 2024** — Cyclone Chido causes extreme damage; a state of emergency is declared.

## Body

### Geography and Environment
Mayotte consists of two main islands, Grande-Terre and Petite-Terre, and several islets. It is geologically the oldest of the Comoro Islands, with volcanic activity ceasing about 1.4 million years ago. The island is surrounded by a 160 km coral reef that encloses one of the world's largest and deepest lagoons (1,500 km²). This unique double barrier reef system supports immense biodiversity, with over 1,300 plant species (half endemic) and thousands of marine species. Protected areas cover 100% of its marine territory and about 14% of its land.

### History and Politics
Populated from East Africa and later by Arabs who introduced Islam, Mayotte was sold to France in 1841. After abolition, the economy shifted from sugar to crops like ylang-ylang. Mayotte was the only Comorian island to vote against independence in 1974 and 1976. Following a 2009 referendum, it became a full French department in 2011, a status that brought French law, social welfare, and taxes, while gradually phasing out customary Islamic law. The independent Comoros continues to claim the island.

### Society and Demographics
Mayotte has a very young population (median age under 17 in 2019) and high population growth (3.8% annually). It is densely populated, with 48% of the population being foreign nationals in 2019, primarily from the Comoros. Illegal immigration is a critical issue, with dangerous sea crossings ("kwassa kwassa") resulting in many deaths. The population is predominantly Muslim (95%), following the moderate Shafi'i tradition. French is the official language, but native languages Shimaore and Kibushi are widely spoken.

### Economy and Infrastructure
The economy relies on French financial transfers and has grown rapidly since departmentalization, though it remains the poorest in France. GDP per capita is about 26% of metropolitan France's. Agriculture is small-scale, with key crops being ylang-ylang, vanilla, and food staples. Fishing and nascent aquaculture are important. Infrastructure includes over 230 km of paved roads, but public transport is lacking. The main port is Longoni, and the airport is on Petite-Terre.

### Social Challenges
Mayotte faces severe socioeconomic issues. Over 83% of the population lives below the French poverty line. Unemployment is high (30% overall, 43% for youth), and 40% of dwellings are corrugated metal shacks. The health system is strained, with severe medical personnel shortages and high rates of obesity and diabetes. Education outcomes are the worst in France, with high illiteracy and school dropout rates. Delinquency is high, though lower than in some other overseas departments.

## Terms
- **Outermost region (OMR):** A special status for certain EU territories at the periphery, subject to specific derogations from EU law.
- **Departmental collectivity:** A unique administrative status Mayotte held before 2011, combining departmental and regional powers.
- **Shimaore:** The most widely spoken native language of Mayotte, a dialect of Comorian.
- **Kibushi:** A native Malagasy language spoken in parts of Mayotte, heavily influenced by Shimaore and Arabic.
- **Jardin mahorais:** A traditional agroforestry small-scale farming system.
- **Kwassa kwassa:** The term for the fragile, makeshift boats used by migrants crossing from the Comoros to Mayotte.
- **Balladur visa:** A visa requirement imposed on Comorians in 1995 to control migration to Mayotte.
- **La chimique:** A dangerous synthetic drug mixture circulating in Mayotte since 2011.

## Debates and open questions
- **Sovereignty dispute:** The independent Union of the Comoros continues to claim Mayotte as its own, a position supported by some UN General Assembly resolutions, though France maintains sovereignty based on local referendums.
- **Immigration policy:** The effectiveness and humanitarian impact of France's interception operations and the "Balladur visa" are debated, with some Comorians blaming the visa for deadly illegal crossings.
- **Integration challenges:** The pace and social cost of aligning Mayotte's legal, educational, and social systems with metropolitan French standards are ongoing issues, given deep-seated poverty and cultural differences.

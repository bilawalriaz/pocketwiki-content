# W. G. Grace

## Overview
William Gilbert Grace (1848–1915) was an English cricketer widely regarded as one of the sport's all-time greatest players. His 44-season first-class career (1865–1908) spanned the transition from amateur to professional cricket and the birth of Test matches. He revolutionized batting technique, dominated the sport for decades, and became one of the most famous and controversial figures in Victorian England.

## Timeline
- **18 Jul 1848** — Born in Downend, near Bristol.
- **1865** — First-class debut for Gentlemen of the South; begins representing Gentlemen v Players.
- **1866** — Scores maiden first-class century (224* for England vs Surrey).
- **1869** — Becomes a member of Marylebone Cricket Club (MCC).
- **1870** — Gloucestershire County Cricket Club gains first-class status; Grace is captain.
- **1871** — Scores 10 centuries in a season (annus mirabilis); first to score 2,000 first-class runs in a season.
- **1873** — First player to achieve the "double" of 1,000 runs and 100 wickets in a season.
- **1876** — Scores 344 for MCC vs Kent, the first triple century in first-class cricket.
- **1879** — Qualifies as a medical practitioner (LRCP & MRCS).
- **1880** — Scores England's first Test century (152 vs Australia); brothers E.M. and Fred also play.
- **1882** — Plays in the match that gave birth to "The Ashes" legend.
- **1895** — Achieves his "Indian Summer," scoring 1,000 runs in May and nine centuries for the season.
- **1899** — Plays his final Test match; becomes captain/manager of London County Cricket Club.
- **23 Oct 1915** — Dies in Mottingham, London, aged 67.

## Body

### Early Life and Development
Grace was born into a cricket-obsessed family in Downend, near Bristol. His father, a local doctor, established a practice pitch and encouraged all his children to play. Under the coaching of his uncle Alfred Pocock, Grace and his brothers developed exceptional skills. He was educated locally but never attended university, as his father insisted he pursue medicine. He enrolled at Bristol Medical School in 1868, though his qualification was delayed by his cricketing commitments until 1879.

### Rise to Dominance
Grace's first-class debut came in 1865. He quickly became the dominant force in English cricket, especially in the prestigious Gentlemen v Players fixture, where his batting helped the amateur Gentlemen end a long losing streak against the professional Players. His reputation was cemented in 1871 with a record-breaking season. He became the cornerstone of Gloucestershire County Cricket Club, which was formed from his family's club and achieved Champion County status in 1876 and 1877. His 1876 season included the first triple century in first-class cricket.

### The All-Rounder and Controversial Figure
Grace was a complete all-rounder, excelling in batting, bowling, and fielding. He was an opening batsman renowned for his complete command of every stroke, which contemporary critics considered unparalleled. As a bowler, he used a roundarm action to deliver accurate, medium-pace deliveries. Despite his nominal amateur status, he was highly controversial for making more money from cricket than any professional through expenses, testimonials, and management roles. He was also known for his extreme competitiveness and gamesmanship.

### Test Cricket and Later Career
Test cricket began during Grace's career, and he played 22 matches for England, all against Australia. He scored England's first Test century in 1880. His only overseas Test tour was to Australia in 1891–92, which was marred by his poor sportsmanship and the team's failure. After a period focused on his medical practice in the 1880s, he experienced a remarkable "Indian Summer" in 1895 at age 47. He retired from Test cricket in 1899 and from first-class cricket in 1908, having played for London County in his final years.

### Legacy
Grace is credited with transforming batting from an accomplishment into a science, making utility the criterion of style. He played on pitches considered unfit by modern standards and on grounds without boundaries. His fame was immense; he was arguably the most famous Englishman of his time, rivaling Queen Victoria. He died in 1915, and his legacy endures through memorials at Lord's and his induction into the ICC Cricket Hall of Fame.

## Terms
- **First-class cricket:** The highest standard of domestic cricket, involving teams representing counties or other major institutions.
- **Test cricket:** The international form of the game, played between national teams.
- **Gentlemen v Players:** A prestigious annual match between amateur (Gentlemen) and professional (Players) cricketers.
- **Annus mirabilis:** Latin for "year of wonders," referring to an exceptionally successful year.
- **The double:** Achieving 1,000 runs and 100 wickets in a single first-class season.
- **Gamesmanship:** The use of legal but ethically questionable tactics to gain an advantage.
- **Roundarm bowling:** A bowling action where the arm is extended horizontally, legalised in 1864.
- **Overarm bowling:** The modern bowling action where the arm rotates vertically over the shoulder, legalised in 1864.
- **Indian Summer:** A period of renewed success late in a career.

## Debates and open questions
- **Amateur vs. Professional Status:** Grace was a nominal amateur but earned more money from cricket than any professional, sparking ongoing debate about his financial dealings and the hypocrisy of the amateur-professional divide.
- **Gamesmanship:** His reputation for gamesmanship, such as the incident where he allegedly kept runs after the ball lodged in his shirt, remains a point of discussion regarding his sportsmanship.
- **Greatest Cricketer?:** While widely hailed as the greatest cricketer of his era and a transformative figure, comparisons with later greats like Don Bradman are subjective and debated.

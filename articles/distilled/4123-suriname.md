# Suriname

## Overview
Suriname is a small, ethnically diverse country in northern South America, notable for being the only independent nation outside Europe where Dutch is the official language. Its history is defined by Indigenous settlement, Dutch colonial rule reliant on enslaved African labor, and a complex post-independence path marked by political instability and economic shifts. The country is characterized by its vast rainforest cover, which is central to its ecology and economy.

## Timeline
- **c. 3000 BC** — Indigenous settlement of the area begins.
- **1667** — Treaty of Breda transfers the colony from English to Dutch control.
- **1863** — The Netherlands abolishes slavery in Suriname, followed by a 10-year transition period.
- **1954** — Suriname becomes a constituent country of the Kingdom of the Netherlands.
- **25 November 1975** — Suriname gains full independence from the Netherlands.
- **25 February 1980** — A military coup led by Dési Bouterse overthrows the government.
- **7 December 1982** — The "December murders" of 15 political opponents occur at Fort Zeelandia.
- **1986-1992** — A civil war is fought between the army and Maroon rebel groups.
- **1998** — The Central Suriname Nature Reserve is established.
- **2010** — Dési Bouterse is elected president.
- **29 November 2019** — Bouterse is convicted for the 1982 killings and sentenced to 20 years.
- **6 July 2025** — Jennifer Geerlings-Simons is elected as Suriname's first woman president.

## Body

### Colonial History and Slavery
Indigenous peoples, including the Lokono and Kalina, inhabited Suriname for millennia before European contact. Dutch and English settlers established plantation colonies in the 17th century, with the Dutch gaining permanent control via the 1667 Treaty of Breda. The colony's economy was built on sugar and other commodity crops, driven by the labor of approximately 300,000 enslaved Africans brought over from the mid-1600s to the early 1800s. Treatment was notoriously brutal, leading many enslaved people to escape and form independent Maroon communities in the interior. Slavery was abolished in 1863, but former slaves were forced into a 10-year wage-labor transition. To replace the labor force, the Dutch recruited indentured servants from British India and the Dutch East Indies (modern Indonesia), creating Suriname's diverse demographic foundation.

### Independence and Political Turmoil
Suriname became a constituent country of the Kingdom of the Netherlands in 1954 and achieved full independence in 1975. This transition was accompanied by mass emigration to the Netherlands. Post-independence politics were marked by ethnic polarization and corruption. In 1980, a military coup led by Dési Bouterse overthrew the elected government. His regime was characterized by repression, including the 1982 execution of political dissidents. A brutal civil war with Maroon groups further destabilized the country in the late 1980s. Although democratic elections were held in 1987 and 1991, Bouterse remained influential. He was later convicted in absentia for drug trafficking in the Netherlands and, in 2019, was sentenced to 20 years in prison for the 1982 murders, though he had served as president from 2010 to 2020.

### Geography, Economy, and Society
Suriname is the smallest country in South America by population and area, with over 90% of its territory covered by rainforest. Its population of about 625,000 is highly diverse, with no ethnic majority; major groups include Indo-Surinamese (27.4%), Maroons (21.7%), Creoles (15.7%), and Javanese (14%). The economy is heavily dependent on natural resources, particularly gold, oil, and formerly bauxite. Agriculture, including rice and bananas, is also important. Dutch is the official language, but Sranan Tongo, an English-based creole, is widely spoken. The country is religiously diverse, with significant Christian, Hindu, and Muslim populations. Most inhabitants live along the northern coast, making it one of the least densely populated countries on Earth.

## Terms
- **Maroons:** Communities of descendants of escaped African slaves who formed independent societies in the Surinamese interior.
- **Constituent country:** A non-sovereign territory that is part of a larger sovereign state (Suriname was one within the Kingdom of the Netherlands from 1954-1975).
- **Sranan Tongo:** An English-based creole language that serves as a widely used lingua franca in Suriname.
- **Indentured labourers:** Workers contracted for a set period under conditions often likened to slavery, recruited from Asia after abolition.
- **Bauxite:** The primary ore of aluminium, historically a major export and revenue source for Suriname.
- **Carbon negative:** A status indicating a country removes more carbon dioxide from the atmosphere than it emits, which Suriname has achieved due to its extensive forests.
- **Ethnogenesis:** The process by which a group of people develops a distinct cultural identity, as seen with the formation of Maroon tribes.
- **Diglossia:** A situation where two languages are used under different conditions within a community, as with Dutch (formal) and Sranan Tongo (informal) in Suriname.

## Debates and open questions
- The legacy and ongoing political influence of Dési Bouterse, despite his criminal convictions, remains a central and divisive issue in Surinamese politics.
- The country's economic future is tied to the sustainable management of its natural resources, particularly balancing gold and oil extraction with the conservation of its vital rainforests.
- The precise causes and responsibilities for the "December murders" of 1982 continue to be a source of legal and political contention, as evidenced by the amnesty law passed in 2012 and the subsequent court conviction.

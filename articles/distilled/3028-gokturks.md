# Göktürks

## Overview
The Göktürks were a medieval Turkic people who established the First Turkic Khaganate in the 6th century, succeeding the Rouran Khaganate as the dominant power in Inner Asia. They shaped the political geography, culture, and identity of Turkic peoples across the Eurasian steppes, and their legacy influenced later states and ethnic groups.

## Timeline
- **439** — The Ashina clan, ancestors of the Göktürks, flees to the Rouran Khaganate after the fall of Northern Liang.
- **552** — Bumin Qaghan overthrows the Rouran, establishing the First Turkic Khaganate.
- **c. 576** — The Göktürk empire reaches its peak, expanding into Europe and clashing with the Byzantine Empire.
- **583** — The empire splits into Eastern and Western Turkic Khaganates following a civil war.
- **615** — Shibi Khagan besieges Emperor Yang of Sui at Yanmen.
- **630** — Tang dynasty captures Illig Qaghan, subjugating the Eastern Turks.
- **639** — Following a failed assassination attempt on Emperor Taizong, Göktürks are settled north of the Yellow River.
- **682** — Ilterish Qaghan and Tonyukuk revolt, restoring the Second Turkic Khaganate (the "Eastern" or "Second" empire).
- **744** — The Uyghur Khaganate, allied with the Tang, defeats the last Göktürk khagan, ending their rule.

## Body

### Origins and Etymology
The Göktürk rulers originated from the Ashina clan, first attested in 439. Chinese sources link them to the Xiongnu confederation, though this is debated. They lived for generations north of the Altai Mountains as metalworkers for the Rouran Khaganate. The name "Türk" likely derives from an Old Turkic term meaning "created, born" or "ripe, vigorous." "Göktürk" is interpreted as "Celestial Turk" or "Blue Turk," possibly referencing the sky-blue color associated with the east in Central Asian cardinal systems and the cult of heavenly ordained rule. The name initially applied only to the Göktürk khanates and their subjects, not to other Turkic groups like the Uyghurs or Kyrgyz.

### Rise, Division, and Conflict with China
Under Bumin Qaghan and his sons, the Göktürks overthrew the Rouran in 552 and rapidly expanded. Internal division led to a split into Eastern and Western Khaganates around 583. The Eastern Turks, weakened by civil war, initially submitted to the Sui dynasty. After the Sui collapsed, they intervened in Chinese civil wars. The Tang dynasty, established in 618, faced constant raids. In 630, Emperor Taizong defeated the Eastern Turks, capturing their khagan and incorporating the Mongolian Plateau into the Tang sphere. Göktürk nobles were integrated into the Tang court, though a failed revolt in 639 led to their resettlement north of the Great Wall.

### Revival and Final Decline
In 682, Ilterish Qaghan and the general Tonyukuk led a successful revolt, restoring the Göktürk Khaganate (the Second Turkic Khaganate). This revived empire intervened in regional conflicts but gradually weakened after the death of Bilge Qaghan. In 744, the Uyghur Khaganate, allied with the Tang, destroyed the last Göktürk state and took control of the Mongolian Plateau.

### Religion and Legacy
The Göktürks practiced a polytheistic religion centered on Tengri, the sky god, who granted legitimacy to the khagan. They worshipped various nature spirits and ancestral figures. The Ashina dynasty's legacy persisted in later Turkic states like the Khazars and possibly the Karakhanids. The Göktürks are remembered as foundational figures in Turkic history and identity.

## Terms
- **Khagan**: The supreme ruler or emperor of a nomadic empire.
- **Ashina**: The ruling clan of the Göktürk Khaganates.
- **Tengri**: The supreme sky god in the Turkic and Mongolic religious tradition.
- **Orkhon inscriptions**: Stone monuments from the 8th century detailing Göktürk history and ideology.
- **Rouran Khaganate**: The Mongolic empire that preceded the Göktürks as the dominant power in the steppes.
- **Sui dynasty**: The Chinese dynasty (581–618) that preceded the Tang and contended with the Göktürks.
- **Tang dynasty**: The Chinese dynasty (618–907) that conquered and later lost control over the Göktürks.
- **Uyghur Khaganate**: The Turkic empire that succeeded the Göktürks on the Mongolian Plateau.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The exact ethnic origin of the Ashina clan is debated; Chinese sources link them to the Xiongnu, but this may be a literary topos rather than a direct lineage.
- The meaning of "Kök" in "Göktürk" is uncertain due to limited attestation, with theories ranging from "sky/blue" to "great."
- The degree of ethnic and linguistic homogeneity within the early Göktürk population is unclear, as their elite adopted non-Turkic titles and the Orkhon inscriptions contain non-Turkic words.

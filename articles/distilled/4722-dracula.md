# Dracula

## Overview
*Dracula* is an 1897 Gothic horror novel by Bram Stoker, presented as a collection of letters, diaries, and newspaper clippings. It follows the invasion of England by the Transylvanian vampire Count Dracula and the group led by Professor Van Helsing who hunt him. The novel is a seminal work of Gothic fiction that has profoundly shaped the modern popular conception of vampires and remains one of the most adapted literary characters in history.

## Timeline
- **1890** — Stoker begins researching and writing notes for the novel.
- **1893–1896** — Stoker writes most of the manuscript during summer holidays in Scotland.
- **May 1897** — *Dracula* is published by Archibald Constable and Company in the UK.
- **1899** — An American edition is published by Doubleday & McClure.
- **1922** — F. W. Murnau's unauthorized film *Nosferatu* is released, leading to a lawsuit.
- **1931** — Bela Lugosi stars in the first major American film adaptation.
- **1958** — Christopher Lee first portrays Dracula for Hammer Film Productions.
- **1972** — McNally and Florescu's *In Search of Dracula* popularizes the Vlad the Impaler link.
- **1992** — Francis Ford Coppola's *Bram Stoker's Dracula* is released.
- **2015** — *Guinness World Records* names Dracula the most portrayed literary character.

## Body

### Plot
English solicitor Jonathan Harker visits Count Dracula's castle to assist with a property purchase, only to discover the Count is a vampire. Dracula travels to England, where he preys on Lucy Westenra. A group led by Professor Van Helsing eventually destroys the undead Lucy. Dracula then targets Harker's fiancée, Mina Murray, forcing a race against time to destroy his boxes of earth and kill him before she becomes a vampire. The hunters pursue Dracula back to Transylvania, where he is killed, freeing Mina.

### Composition and Publication
Bram Stoker researched extensively, creating over 100 pages of notes. He took the name "Dracula" from a history of Wallachia and Moldavia, believing it meant 'devil'. The novel was published in May 1897. Early reviews were mixed; some praised its terrifying atmosphere, while others found it overly horrific. It did not bring Stoker significant fame or fortune during his lifetime. A copyright filing error in the United States later placed the novel in the public domain there, facilitating numerous adaptations.

### Style and Genre
The novel is an epistolary work, a narrative composed of documents. This structure creates a sense of realism and collaborative narration, as the story is pieced together from multiple perspectives. It is a key work of Gothic literature, blending supernatural horror with contemporary Victorian settings and anxieties. The Count embodies typical Gothic villainy but is firmly placed in the modern era.

### Critical Interpretation
Scholars analyze *Dracula* through various lenses. It is frequently read as a story of "reverse colonisation," reflecting Victorian fears of foreign invasion and racial pollution. The novel is rich with themes of transgressive sexuality, gender roles, and the "New Woman." It also engages with contemporary concerns about disease, degeneration, and the conflict between science, religion, and superstition. Some critics interpret Dracula as a symbol of feudalism or capitalist exploitation.

## Terms
*   **Epistolary novel:** A novel told through a collection of documents, such as letters, diary entries, and newspaper articles.
*   **Gothic fiction:** A genre of literature that combines horror, death, and romance, often set in atmospheric, mysterious locations.
*   **Vampire:** A mythical creature, typically a reanimated corpse, that sustains itself by drinking the blood of the living.
*   **Fin de siècle:** A French term meaning "end of the century," referring to the cultural period in Europe during the 1890s.
*   **New Woman:** A feminist ideal that emerged in the late 19th century, representing women seeking greater social and economic independence.
*   **Reverse colonisation:** A literary theme where a foreign or "Other" entity invades and threatens the colonising power's homeland.
*   **Degeneration theory:** A 19th-century pseudo-scientific concept that feared the biological and moral deterioration of society.
*   **Alienism:** The historical term for the study and treatment of mental illness.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Inspiration for Count Dracula:** While Vlad the Impaler (Vlad III) is popularly cited, scholars debate the directness of this link, noting Stoker's research did not focus on Vlad's cruelty. Other proposed inspirations include Henry Irving and Elizabeth Báthory, though evidence is contested.
*   **Critical Reception:** Scholars disagree on whether the novel had a mixed or successful initial reception. Some note contemporary praise, while others highlight negative reviews.
*   **Irish Novel?:** Debate exists over whether *Dracula* can be considered an Irish novel, given Stoker's background and potential allegories for Irish politics and famine.
*   **Sexuality and Gender:** Critics continue to debate the novel's stance on the "New Woman," with some seeing it as a reactionary text and others noting Stoker's ambivalence.
*   **"Dracula's Guest":** Scholars debate whether the posthumously published short story "Dracula's Guest" was a discarded first chapter of the novel or a separate work.

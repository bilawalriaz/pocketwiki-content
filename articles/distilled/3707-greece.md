# Greece

## Overview
Greece, officially the Hellenic Republic, is a country in Southeastern Europe with a population of around 10 million. It is considered the cradle of Western civilisation and the birthplace of democracy, philosophy, and the Olympic Games. Its rich historical legacy, from ancient city-states to the modern parliamentary republic, has profoundly shaped global culture, politics, and thought.

## Timeline
- **c. 3200 BC** — Cycladic culture flourishes in the Aegean Sea.
- **c. 3100–1100 BC** — Minoan civilisation on Crete; Mycenaean civilisation on the mainland.
- **776 BC** — First Olympic Games, traditionally marking the end of the Greek Dark Ages.
- **508 BC** — Cleisthenes institutes democracy in Athens.
- **480–479 BC** — Greek city-states defeat the Persian Empire in the Greco-Persian Wars.
- **336–323 BC** — Alexander the Great conquers the Persian Empire, spreading Greek culture.
- **146 BC** — Greece is annexed by the Roman Republic.
- **1204** — Fourth Crusade fragments the Byzantine Empire.
- **1460** — Ottoman conquest of mainland Greece is completed.
- **1821–1832** — Greek War of Independence leads to the establishment of a modern nation-state.
- **1974** — Military junta collapses; transition to the current parliamentary republic begins.
- **1981** — Greece becomes the tenth member of the European Communities (now the EU).

## Body

### Ancient and Classical Periods
Greece is home to the first advanced European civilisations, including the Minoan (c. 3100–1100 BC) and Mycenaean (c. 1750–1100 BC). The subsequent Greek Dark Ages ended with the first Olympic Games in 776 BC. The Classical period (c. 5th–4th centuries BC) saw the rise of independent city-states (*poleis*), the invention of democracy in Athens (508 BC), and a cultural boom in philosophy, drama, and science. After defeating Persia, internal conflicts like the Peloponnesian War (431–404 BC) weakened the *poleis*, allowing Philip II of Macedon to unite most of Greece by the 4th century BC. His son, Alexander the Great, conquered a vast empire, inaugurating the Hellenistic period, which spread Greek language and culture from Egypt to India.

### Roman, Byzantine, and Ottoman Rule
Greece was annexed by Rome in 146 BC, becoming part of the Roman Empire and later its eastern continuation, the Byzantine Empire, where Greek culture and language were dominant. The Byzantine Empire fragmented after the Fourth Crusade in 1204, and most Greek lands fell under Ottoman control by the mid-15th century. The Greek Orthodox Church played a key role in preserving Greek identity during Ottoman rule.

### Modern Nation-State
The Greek War of Independence (1821–1832), supported by Britain, France, and Russia, established the modern Kingdom of Greece. The 20th century was marked by territorial expansion, the catastrophic Greco-Turkish War (1919–1922), and a subsequent population exchange with Turkey. Political instability led to a military dictatorship (1967–1974). Following the junta's collapse, Greece transitioned to its current parliamentary republic in 1974. It joined the European Communities in 1981 and adopted the euro in 2001. The country faced a severe government-debt crisis from 2010 to 2018, requiring international bailouts and austerity measures.

### Economy and Society
Greece has a developed, high-income economy driven by services (85%), particularly tourism and shipping. It is the world's largest merchant marine by capacity. The population is aging, with a low fertility rate of 1.4 children per woman. The Greek Orthodox Church is the "prevailing" faith, with about 97% of citizens identifying as Orthodox. The country has 21 UNESCO World Heritage Sites and a vibrant cultural scene in arts, cuisine, and sports.

## Terms
- **Polis (pl. poleis):** An independent city-state, the fundamental political unit of ancient Greece.
- **Hellenistic period:** The era following Alexander the Great's death (323 BC), marked by the spread of Greek culture across the eastern Mediterranean and Near East.
- **Byzantine Empire:** The eastern continuation of the Roman Empire, with its capital in Constantinople, where Greek was the dominant language and culture.
- **Metapolitefsi:** The transition from military dictatorship to democracy in Greece beginning in 1974.
- **Kallikratis Programme:** A 2011 administrative reform that reorganized Greece into 13 regions and 332 municipalities.
- **Eurozone crisis:** The severe sovereign debt crisis that affected Greece and other eurozone countries, beginning in 2010.
- **Mediterranean diet:** A dietary pattern traditional to Greece, emphasizing olive oil, vegetables, legumes, and fish.
- **Blue Zone:** A region where people allegedly live longer than average; the Greek island of Icaria is one.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The interpretation of early human remains from the Apidima Cave (dated to 200,000 years ago) is debated, with some scholars suggesting they represent archaic humans rather than early modern humans.
- The extent of decline, fragmentation, and depopulation in Greece during the early Byzantine period (4th–7th centuries) is considered outdated by some historians, who point to evidence of institutional continuity and prosperity.
- The causes and handling of the Greek government-debt crisis (2010–2018) remain contentious, with debates focusing on the roles of domestic fiscal mismanagement, the design of the eurozone, and the severity of imposed austerity measures.

# Exploration

## Overview
Exploration is the process of discovering new information or territory, driven by an expectation of discovery. It is a fundamental activity for humans and many other organisms, shaping history through migration, cultural exchange, and scientific advancement. Understanding its types and methods reveals how knowledge of the world—from geography to space—has been built and continues to expand.

## Timeline
- **c. 3000 BC** — Early writings about exploration appear in ancient Egypt.
- **2nd century AD** — Ptolemy becomes one of the earliest influential thinkers on exploration.
- **5th–15th century AD** — Most exploration is conducted by Chinese and Arab explorers.
- **c. 14,000 years ago** — Humans cross the Ice Age land bridge from Siberia to Alaska, settling the Americas.
- **15th–17th centuries** — The Age of Discovery begins, driven by rediscovered classical works, unsafe land routes, and a desire for conquest.
- **17th century** — Exploration becomes increasingly motivated by scientific discovery and knowledge expansion.
- **1570** — Abraham Ortelius publishes the first modern atlas, *Theatrum Orbis Terrarum*, depicting all known continents.
- **Mid-20th century** — Development of efficient rockets enables physical space exploration.

## Body

### Geographical Exploration
Geographical exploration involves discovering lands remote from the explorer's origin. Human history features two major eras: the first saw migrations out of Africa and the settlement of new continents, with cultures developing in isolation. The second era, over the last 10,000 years, increased cross-cultural exchange through trade and exploration. Early writings date to ancient Egypt, with Ptolemy (2nd century AD) as a key early thinker. Between the 5th and 15th centuries, Chinese and Arab explorers were prominent. The subsequent Age of Discovery was partly driven by unsafe land routes and conquest, but the 17th century added motives like scientific discovery. This accumulated knowledge allowed for the creation of world maps and the first modern atlas.

### Other Domains of Exploration
**Underwater exploration** uses direct or remote observation to study underwater environments. It is crucial for managing and conserving ocean resources, yet less than 10% of the ocean has been mapped in detail, and its biodiversity is poorly known. **Space exploration** uses astronomy and technology to explore outer space, conducted via telescopes, robotic probes, and human spaceflight. Its rationales include scientific research, national prestige, and ensuring humanity's future survival. **Urban exploration** involves investigating manmade structures, often abandoned ruins, for photography and historical documentation, sometimes involving trespassing and legal risks. **Geological exploration** for minerals has evolved from direct observation of rock outcrops to using geophysical and geochemical tools to identify anomalies, followed by detailed assessment through methods like drilling and seismic surveys.

### Modes of Exploration
Exploration can be systematic, following a flexible plan to accommodate unexpected discoveries. It can be diagnostic, using logic and evidence to identify causes of phenomena, as in medicine or engineering. It is also pursued for first-hand experience, often for personal satisfaction (diversive and intrinsic) or to verify information (extrinsic). In animals, exploratory behavior is directed toward gathering environmental information, typically following a sequence of search, attention, approach, and investigation, which can be interrupted by flight or a return to search.

## Terms
- **Exploration**: The process of exploring, an activity with an expectation of discovery.
- **Geographical exploration**: The practice of discovering lands and regions remote from the explorer's origin.
- **Age of Discovery**: A period beginning in the 15th century when European explorers, inspired by rediscovered classical works and seeking new trade routes, undertook major voyages of exploration.
- **Underwater exploration**: The exploration of any underwater environment by direct or remote observation.
- **Space exploration**: The use of astronomy and space technology to explore outer space.
- **Urban exploration**: The exploration of manmade structures, often abandoned or hidden, for documentation and photography.
- **Geological exploration**: The search for mineral deposits using methods ranging from direct observation to geophysical and geochemical tools.
- **Systematic exploration**: An orderly investigation following a flexible plan that adapts to unexpected findings.
- **Diagnostic exploration**: An examination that explores evidence to identify the nature and cause of a phenomenon.
- **Intrinsic exploration**: Exploration motivated by personal satisfaction and the pursuit of first-hand experience.

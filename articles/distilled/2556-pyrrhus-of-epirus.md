# Pyrrhus of Epirus

## Overview
Pyrrhus (319/318–272 BC) was a Greek king of Epirus and one of the most formidable opponents of early Rome during the Hellenistic period. A brilliant but restless general, his costly victories against Rome gave rise to the term "Pyrrhic victory." His campaigns in Italy and Sicily represented a major attempt to check Roman expansion, but his failure ultimately paved the way for Rome's dominance over the Mediterranean.

## Timeline
- **c. 319 BC** — Born to Aeacides of Epirus and Phthia.
- **307 BC** — First becomes king of Epirus at age 11, installed by Illyrian ally Glaucias.
- **302 BC** — Fights at the Battle of Ipsus, the largest battle of the Wars of the Diadochi.
- **297 BC** — Restored to Epirus with support from Ptolemy I Soter.
- **289 BC** — Defeats Demetrius's general Pantauchus in a personal duel, earning the nickname "Eagle."
- **288 BC** — Co-rules Macedonia with Lysimachus after driving out Demetrius.
- **280 BC** — Invades Italy to aid Tarentum; defeats Romans at the Battle of Heraclea.
- **279 BC** — Wins a costly victory at the Battle of Asculum, inspiring the term "Pyrrhic victory."
- **278–276 BC** — Conquers most of Sicily from Carthage but is later expelled.
- **275 BC** — Defeated at the Battle of Beneventum; abandons his Italian campaign.
- **274 BC** — Seizes the Macedonian throne from Antigonus II Gonatas.
- **272 BC** — Killed in a street battle at Argos after a failed assault on Sparta.

## Body
**Early Life and First Reign**
Pyrrhus was born into the royal Aeacid house of Epirus, a second cousin to Alexander the Great. His early life was marked by instability; his father was dethroned and killed, and the family fled to Illyria. With Illyrian support, Pyrrhus first became king of Epirus in 307 BC at age 11 but was soon ousted by a rival claimant. He then served in the Wars of the Diadochi (successor wars to Alexander's empire), fighting at the Battle of Ipsus (302 BC) and later being taken hostage to Egypt, where he married and gained the support of Ptolemy I.

**Consolidation and Expansion in Greece**
Restored to Epirus in 297 BC, Pyrrhus quickly eliminated his co-ruler Neoptolemus. He engaged in a long struggle with Demetrius Poliorcetes, another of Alexander's successors. In 288 BC, Pyrrhus and Lysimachus drove Demetrius from Macedonia, and Pyrrhus briefly co-ruled the kingdom. After being expelled by Lysimachus in 284 BC, he rebuilt his power in Epirus. His Greek empire reached its zenith around 286 BC, controlling Epirus, half of Macedonia, and Thessaly.

**The Pyrrhic War and Campaign in Italy**
In 280 BC, Pyrrhus invaded Italy at the request of the Greek city of Tarentum, which was threatened by Rome. He arrived with a formidable army, including war elephants loaned by Ptolemy II. He won two major battles against the Romans—at Heraclea (280 BC) and Asculum (279 BC)—but suffered irreplaceable losses. After Asculum, he reportedly said another such victory would ruin him, coining the phrase "Pyrrhic victory." Unable to force a decisive peace, he withdrew to Tarentum.

**Sicilian Campaign and Final Years**
In 278 BC, Pyrrhus sailed to Sicily to aid Greek cities against Carthage. He quickly conquered most of the island but alienated the Sicilians through heavy taxation and autocratic rule. Facing renewed Carthaginian attacks and a revolt, he abandoned Sicily in 276 BC, reportedly predicting it would become a battleground for Rome and Carthage. Returning to Italy, he was defeated at the Battle of Beneventum (275 BC) and lost all his Italian gains except Tarentum. He then seized Macedonia again in 274 BC but was killed in a street battle at Argos in 272 BC while attempting to conquer the Peloponnese.

## Terms
*   **Diadochi**: The rival generals, governors, and relatives of Alexander the Great who fought for control over his empire after his death.
*   **Phalanx**: A dense infantry formation of spearmen, a hallmark of Macedonian and Hellenistic warfare.
*   **Pyrrhic victory**: A victory achieved at such a devastating cost to the victor that it is tantamount to defeat.
*   **Hellenistic period**: The era following the death of Alexander the Great (323 BC) characterized by the spread of Greek culture and the rule of Greek-speaking dynasties across the Near East.
*   **Magna Graecia**: The coastal areas of Southern Italy that were extensively settled by Greeks.
*   **Eponym**: A person after whom something is named; in this context, a king whose name was used to designate a year.
*   **Companions**: The elite cavalry and personal staff of a Macedonian-style king, owing him direct loyalty.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Casualty Figures**: Ancient sources provide conflicting numbers for battles like Heraclea and Asculum, making precise losses for Pyrrhus and the Romans difficult to ascertain.
*   **Legacy and Ranking**: While Hannibal reportedly ranked Pyrrhus among the greatest generals, his strategic judgment is debated. His constant warfare drained his kingdom's resources and ultimately failed to secure a lasting empire.
*   **Impact on Rome**: Pyrrhus's campaign is seen as a critical test for the early Roman Republic. His failure to halt Roman expansion in Italy is considered a pivotal moment that allowed Rome to eventually dominate the Mediterranean.

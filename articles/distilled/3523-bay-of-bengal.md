# Bay of Bengal

## Overview
The Bay of Bengal is the northeastern arm of the Indian Ocean, situated between the Indian subcontinent and the Indochinese peninsula. It is a vital maritime region for trade, biodiversity, and climate, hosting the world's largest submarine fan and being a primary zone for tropical cyclones affecting South Asia.

## Timeline
- **c. 20 million years ago** — Sediments from the Himalayas begin forming the Bengal Fan.
- **Ancient times** — Known as *Mahodadhi*, *Sinus Gangeticus*, or *Vaṅgasāgara*.
- **9th–12th century** — Chola dynasty controls the bay, calling it the "Chola Sea."
- **16th century** — Portuguese build trading posts at Chittagong and Satgaon.
- **1864** — Calcutta Cyclone causes ~80,000 deaths.
- **1970** — Bhola cyclone kills 100,000–500,000 in Bangladesh.
- **1999** — Odisha Super Cyclone strikes India.
- **2004** — Sumatra-Andaman earthquake and tsunami originate at the Sunda Trench.
- **2007** — Malabar 2007, the largest wargame in the bay, is held.
- **2012** — ITLOS settles the Bangladesh-Myanmar maritime dispute.
- **2014** — UN tribunal settles the India-Bangladesh maritime dispute.
- **2020** — Super Cyclonic Storm Amphan becomes the costliest bay cyclone.

## Body

### Geography and Extent
The Bay of Bengal covers 2,600,000 km². Its official limits, defined by the International Hydrographic Organization, run from Cape Negrais (Myanmar) through the Andaman and Nicobar Islands to the southwest, and from Adam's Bridge (Sri Lanka) to the northwestern tip of Sumatra in the south. It is bordered by India, Bangladesh, Myanmar, Sri Lanka, and Indonesia.

### Rivers and Geology
Major river systems, including the Ganges, Brahmaputra, and Irrawaddy, drain into the bay, depositing nearly 1,000 million tons of sediment annually. This sediment has formed the Bengal Delta and the Bengal Fan, the world's largest submarine fan, which is about 3,000 km long and up to 16.5 km thick. The bay's floor is part of the Indo-Australian Plate, which subducts beneath the Burma Microplate at the Sunda Trench, a zone of significant seismic activity.

### History and Human Activity
Historically, the bay was a hub for maritime kingdoms like Kalinga and the Chola dynasty. It later became a key route for European trading companies, notably the British East India Company. Today, its coast hosts major ports such as Chittagong, Kolkata, and Chennai. The region is also rich in cultural and religious sites, including the Sun Temple at Konark and the Jagannath Temple at Puri.

### Economic and Geostrategic Importance
The bay is crucial for international trade, supported by initiatives like BIMSTEC. It is a central arena for geopolitical interests, with naval activities from India, China, and the United States. Territorial disputes over maritime boundaries and natural gas reserves have occurred, with recent settlements between Bangladesh, Myanmar, and India. The bay's fisheries produce about 6 million tonnes annually, supporting millions of coastal fishermen.

### Oceanography and Climate
The bay experiences a seasonal reversal of currents: the northward-flowing East Indian Current from January to October and the southwestward-flowing East Indian Winter Jet for the rest of the year. It is highly prone to tropical cyclones, with severe storms like the 1970 Bhola cyclone and 2020's Amphan causing massive devastation. Ocean acidification has been observed, with pH levels falling from 7.95 in 1994 to 7.75 in 2014.

### Ecology and Transboundary Issues
The bay is a biodiversity hotspot with coral reefs, mangroves (including the Sundarbans), and endangered species like the olive ridley sea turtle and royal Bengal tiger. Key transboundary issues include fisheries overexploitation, habitat degradation, and pollution from sewage, plastics, and industrial waste. An "Asian brown cloud" of air pollution also affects the region annually.

## Terms
- **Submarine Fan**: An underwater delta-like deposit of sediment at the base of a continental slope; the Bengal Fan is the world's largest.
- **Tectonic Plate**: A massive, irregularly shaped slab of solid rock forming part of the Earth's lithosphere.
- **Sunda Trench**: A deep oceanic trench formed by the subduction of the Indo-Australian Plate beneath the Burma Microplate.
- **Cyclone**: A rotating storm system with winds of at least 119 km/h (74 mph) originating over tropical waters.
- **BIMSTEC**: Bay of Bengal Initiative for Multi-Sectoral Technical and Economic Cooperation, a regional group.
- **Hypoxia**: A condition in water with very low oxygen levels, harmful to marine life.
- **Persistent Organic Pollutants (POPs)**: Toxic chemicals that persist in the environment and accumulate in living organisms.
- **Turbidity Current**: A current of sediment-laden water that flows down a slope, transporting material to the deep sea.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The exact historical names and extents of the bay in ancient texts are subject to scholarly interpretation.
- The long-term impacts of ocean acidification and climate change on the bay's unique ecosystems and fisheries are ongoing areas of research.
- The effectiveness of regional cooperation (e.g., BOBLME) in managing transboundary pollution and overfishing remains a challenge.

# Muscat

## Overview
Muscat is the capital and most populous city of Oman, situated on the Gulf of Oman near the strategic Strait of Hormuz. Its history as a major trading port dates back to at least the 1st century CE, and its modern development began with the accession of Sultan Qaboos bin Said in 1970. The city's economy is dominated by trade, petroleum, and liquefied natural gas.

## Timeline
- **6th millennium BC** — Evidence of communal activity at Ras al-Hamra burial sites.
- **1st century CE** — Greek geographer Ptolemy identifies the port as Cryptus Portus.
- **3rd century AD** — Port falls to a Sassanid invasion under Shapur I.
- **1507** — Portuguese admiral Afonso de Albuquerque attempts to conquer Muscat.
- **1650** — Omani forces under the Imam capture Muscat, ending Portuguese rule.
- **1793** — The capital of Oman is transferred from Rustaq to Muscat.

- **1970** — Qaboos bin Said stages a coup, beginning rapid modernization.
- **2007** — Cyclone Gonu causes extensive damage to the city.

## Body
### History and Governance
Muscat has been a significant port since antiquity, ruled by various local tribes and foreign powers including the Persians, Portuguese (1507–1650), and Ottomans. The expulsion of the Portuguese in 1650 marked a shift to local Omani control. The 18th century saw civil war and Persian incursions, leading to the rise of the Al Bu Sa‘id dynasty, which has ruled Oman since. In the 19th century, Muscat's naval power peaked under Sultan Said bin Sultan, who expanded influence to East Africa but later moved his capital to Zanzibar. The 20th century was marked by internal conflict between the coastal Sultanate and interior tribes, culminating in the Dhofar Rebellion (1962). Sultan Qaboos bin Said's 1970 coup ended the rebellion, unified the country, and launched Muscat's modern infrastructural and economic development.

### Geography and Climate
Muscat is located in northeast Oman, bordered by the Gulf of Oman and dominated by the Hajar Mountains. The coastline features deep water forming natural harbors at Muttrah and Muscat. The geology includes volcanic rocks like serpentinite and diorite. The climate is hot and arid (Köppen BWh), with very hot, humid summers and warm winters. Annual rainfall is about 10 cm, mostly from December to April, though recent tropical cyclones have brought heavy precipitation.

### Economy and Demographics
Muscat's economy is centered on trade, petroleum (via Petroleum Development Oman), and liquefied natural gas. The Muscat Securities Market, established in 1988, is the country's principal stock exchange. Port Sultan Qaboos is a key trading hub, though it has been surpassed regionally by Dubai's Jebel Ali. The population was 1.72 million in the 2022 census. The city is multi-ethnic, with a large expatriate community historically including Persians, Balochs, Sindhis, and Gujarati Hindus. Arabic is the predominant language, with English and various Indian languages also spoken. Islam is the main religion, predominantly Ibadi.

### Infrastructure and Culture
Modern Muscat features landmarks like the Sultan Qaboos Grand Mosque and the Royal Opera House (opened 2011). Transport includes Muscat International Airport, a modern bus network (Mwasalat), and extensive roadways. Shopping districts range from traditional souks in Muttrah to large malls like Oman Avenues and Mall of Oman. Omani culture reflects its maritime history, with traditional shipbuilding and a diverse heritage from its historical connections to the Indian Ocean world.

## Terms
- **Wilayat**: Administrative districts or provinces within a governorate.
- **Corniche**: A waterfront promenade or road along a coast.
- **Ibadi**: A branch of Islam distinct from Sunni and Shia, predominant in Oman.
- **Souk**: A traditional marketplace or bazaar in Arab countries.
- **Dhow**: A traditional sailing vessel with one or more masts, used in the Red Sea and Indian Ocean region.
- **Sabkha**: A type of coastal salt flat or desert vegetation ecosystem.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The origin of the name "Muscat" is disputed, with proposed derivations from Arabic, Old Persian, and other languages.
- Scholars are divided on which ancient port identified by Ptolemy—Cryptus Portus or Moscha Portus—corresponds to modern Muscat.
- The exact start date of the Hindu merchant community's settlement in Muscat is debated, with arguments for the 15th century.

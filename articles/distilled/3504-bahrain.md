# Bahrain

## Overview
Bahrain is a small archipelago nation in the Persian Gulf, historically significant as the ancient Dilmun civilization and a major pearl-fishing center. It transitioned from a British protectorate to independence in 1971 and is now a semi-constitutional monarchy. Its economy, once reliant on pearls and oil, has diversified into banking and tourism, making it a high-income economy, though it faces political tensions and human rights concerns.

- **2002** — Bahrain is declared a semi-constitutional monarchy; King Hamad becomes head of state.

- **2020** — Bahrain normalizes relations with Israel.
- **2026** — Bahrain suffers casualties in the Iran war and launches retaliatory airstrikes.

## Body

### History and Political System
Bahrain's history includes periods under the Dilmun civilization, Persian, Portuguese, and British influence. The Al Khalifa family has ruled since 1783. It became a British protectorate in the 19th century and gained independence in 1971. In 2002, it transitioned to a semi-constitutional monarchy with a bicameral National Assembly. The king retains wide executive powers, including appointing the prime minister and the upper house of parliament. The country has experienced periodic unrest, notably a popular uprising from 1994–2000 and major protests in 2011, which led to a government crackdown. Human rights organizations have criticized the government for suppressing dissent and violating the rights of political opposition and the Shia Muslim population.

### Economy
Bahrain is a high-income economy and was one of the first post-oil economies in the Persian Gulf, having invested heavily in banking and tourism. Oil still accounts for 60% of export receipts and 70% of government revenues. The country has pursued economic diversification through its "Vision 2030" plan. It hosts many multinational financial institutions in its capital, Manama. Bahrain introduced a Value Added Tax (VAT) in 2019, later increased to 10% in 2022. The country faces long-term challenges including high public debt, unemployment, and the depletion of oil and water resources.

### Geography and Climate
Bahrain is a flat, arid archipelago comprising 33 natural islands and numerous artificial ones, with a total area of about 780 km². Its highest point is the 134 m Mountain of Smoke. The climate features mild winters and very hot, humid summers. Environmental issues include desertification, coastal degradation from oil spills, and the salinization of its principal aquifer, the Dammam Aquifer. Climate change poses threats of sea-level rise and more frequent extreme weather.

### Demographics and Society
Bahrain's population was 1,588,670 in 2024, with a slight majority of expatriates. It is one of the world's most densely populated countries. The population is ethnically and religiously diverse; the majority are Muslim, with a Sunni minority ruling over a Shia majority that often reports discrimination. Arabic is the official language, but English is widely used. The country has a universal healthcare system and a diverse education system with both public and private institutions.

## Terms
*   **Dilmun:** An ancient Bronze Age civilization and trade center located in the area of modern-day Bahrain.
*   **Al Khalifa:** The Sunni Muslim royal family that has ruled Bahrain since 1783.
*   **Sharia:** Islamic law; Article 2 of Bahrain's constitution makes it a principal source for legislation.
*   **Semi-constitutional monarchy:** A system where a monarch retains significant executive power but shares some authority with elected bodies.
*   **National Assembly (al-Majlis al-Watani):** Bahrain's bicameral parliament, consisting of an appointed Shura Council and an elected Council of Representatives.
*   **Vision 2030:** A long-term economic plan to transform Bahrain into a diversified and sustainable economy.
*   **Dammam Aquifer:** The principal underground water source in Bahrain, which is facing salinization.
*   **Baharna:** The ethnic Arab Shia population of Bahrain.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Sectarian Tensions:** There is ongoing debate about the extent of political and economic discrimination against the Shia Muslim majority by the Sunni-led government.
*   **Human Rights:** International organizations and governments frequently debate and criticize Bahrain's record on human rights, particularly regarding freedom of expression, treatment of dissidents, and use of torture.
*   **Economic Sustainability:** Scholars and policymakers question the long-term viability of Bahrain's economy given its high public debt, reliance on oil revenues, and limited natural resources.
*   **Etymology:** The exact meaning of the name "Bahrain" ("the two seas") is disputed, with theories referring to the bay east and west of the island, salt and fresh water, or the Persian Gulf and a mainland lake.

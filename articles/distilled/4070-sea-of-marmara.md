# Sea of Marmara

## Overview
The Sea of Marmara is a small inland sea entirely within Turkey that connects the Black Sea and the Aegean Sea via the Bosporus and Dardanelles straits. It is geographically significant as it separates the European and Asian parts of Turkey and is considered the smallest sea in the world. Its basin is densely populated and faces major environmental pressures.

## Timeline
- **c. 3000 BC** — Marmara Island is a source of marble, giving the sea its name.
- **Classical Antiquity** — Known as the Propontis by the Greeks, meaning "in front of the sea" (the Black Sea).
- **Mythological Era** — The Argonauts fight King Cyzicus on an island in the Propontis after a storm.
- **1999 (August)** — The Marmara Earthquake (Izmit earthquake) occurs, with its epicentre under the sea.
- **1999 (November)** — The Düzce earthquake strikes the region.
- **1999 (December)** — The tanker *Volgoneft-248* breaks apart, spilling over 1,500 tonnes of oil.
- **2021 (Jan-June)** — A severe mucilage (sea snot) outbreak disrupts the ecosystem and fishing.

## Body

### Name and History
The sea is named after Marmara Island, which is rich in marble (Greek *mármaron*). In classical antiquity, it was called the Propontis, from the Greek for "in front of the sea," as it was the approach to the Black Sea for ancient Greek sailors. A storm on the Propontis features in Greek mythology involving the Argonauts.

### Geography and Hydrology
The sea is defined by the International Hydrographic Organization with boundaries at the Dardanelles and a line between Cape Rumili and Cape Anatoli. Its south coast is indented with several gulfs. The surface water has an average salinity of about 22 parts per thousand, higher than the Black Sea but lower than most oceans. The bottom water is much saltier (around 38 parts per thousand), similar to the Mediterranean, and this dense layer does not mix with the surface. Rivers like the Susurluk and Biga flow into it, primarily from the Anatolian side.

### Islands
There are two main island groups. The northern Princes' Islands are an inhabited archipelago accessible by ferry from Istanbul. The southern Marmara Islands include Marmara Island, Avşa, and the prison island of Imralı, which has held PKK leader Abdullah Öcalan since 1999. A few other islands, like the privately owned Koç Adası, are also present.

### Environmental Challenges
The North Anatolian Fault runs beneath the sea, causing major earthquakes like the 1999 Marmara Earthquake. The sea faces severe pollution from the surrounding population, which constitutes about one-third of Turkey's population. Historical waste dumping has caused species loss and led to fishing bans in some areas. Recent crises include the 1999 oil spill and the 2021 mucilage outbreak, a thick biological substance that harmed habitats and the fishing industry.

## Terms
*   **Propontis:** The ancient Greek name for the Sea of Marmara, meaning "in front of the sea" (the Black Sea).
*   **Mucilage:** A thick, sticky, gelatinous substance produced by marine organisms; a major pollution event in 2021.
*   **Salinity:** The salt concentration in water, measured in parts per thousand (ppt).
*   **North Anatolian Fault:** A major active fault line running under the Sea of Marmara, responsible for significant earthquakes.
*   **Archipelago:** A group or chain of islands.
*   **Gulf:** A portion of the sea partially enclosed by land, such as the Gulf of İzmit.

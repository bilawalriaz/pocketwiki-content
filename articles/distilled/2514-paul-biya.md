# Paul Biya

## Overview
Paul Biya has been the president of Cameroon since 1982, making him one of the world's longest-serving and oldest current heads of state. His rule is characterized by an authoritarian, one-party dominant system, marked by contested elections, suppression of opposition, and a major ongoing conflict with Anglophone separatists. His long tenure is central to understanding modern Cameroonian politics and its international relations.

## Timeline
- **13 February 1933** — Born in Mvomeka'a, Cameroon.
- **30 June 1975** — Appointed Prime Minister by President Ahmadou Ahidjo.
- **6 November 1982** — Becomes President following Ahidjo's resignation.
- **6 April 1984** — Survives a military coup attempt.
- **1985** — Ruling party transformed into the Cameroon People's Democratic Movement (CPDM).
- **11 October 1992** — Wins first multiparty presidential election with ~40% of the vote; results disputed.
- **10 April 2008** — National Assembly votes to remove presidential term limits.
- **12 June 2006** — Signs Greentree Agreement with Nigeria, resolving the Bakassi peninsula dispute.
- **October 2016** — Large-scale Anglophone protests begin in Northwest and Southwest regions.
- **October 2017** — Anglophone separatists declare independence as "Ambazonia."
- **27 October 2025** — Re-elected for an eighth consecutive term; protests and fraud allegations follow.

## Body

### Rise to Power
Paul Biya, an ethnic Beti, rose rapidly as a bureaucrat under President Ahmadou Ahidjo in the 1960s. He served as Secretary-General of the Presidency from 1968 to 1975 and was appointed Prime Minister in 1975. A 1979 law made the Prime Minister the constitutional successor to the President. When Ahidjo resigned unexpectedly in November 1982, Biya succeeded him. A rift soon developed, leading Ahidjo into exile. Biya consolidated power after surviving a 1984 coup attempt, widely believed to have been orchestrated by Ahidjo, which resulted in significant violence.

### Authoritarian Rule and Political Reforms
Biya's regime is widely considered a dictatorship. He initially introduced limited political reforms within a one-party system before accepting multiparty politics in the early 1990s under pressure. He won the 1992 election with a plurality of 40%, but the results were strongly disputed. He has been re-elected in subsequent elections (1997, 2004, 2011, 2018, 2025) by large margins, with consistent allegations of voting irregularities and fraud from opposition and observers. In 2008, his government removed presidential term limits, allowing his continued rule. His party, the CPDM, dominates the National Assembly, and the constitution grants him sweeping executive, legislative, and judicial powers.

### The Anglophone Crisis
Since 2016, Biya's government has faced its strongest opposition from the Anglophone population in the Northwest and Southwest regions. Protests against perceived marginalization and the imposition of French in courts and schools escalated into a violent conflict. In 2017, separatists declared independence as "Ambazonia." The government's response has involved severe repression, with allegations from human rights groups of extrajudicial killings, torture, and burning of villages by state forces. Biya has admitted the state failed to properly integrate the former British Southern Cameroons.

### Foreign Relations
Biya's foreign policy is characterized by pragmatic alliances. France remains a key supporter, supplying weapons and military training. Relations with the United States have focused on counterterrorism against Boko Haram. A major diplomatic achievement was the peaceful resolution of the Bakassi peninsula border dispute with Nigeria, finalized through the 2006 Greentree Agreement following an International Court of Justice ruling. Cameroon also maintains strong ties with China and Israel, the latter providing military equipment and training.

## Terms
*   **CPDM (Cameroon People's Democratic Movement):** The ruling political party, formerly the Cameroon National Union (CNU), which dominates the government.
*   **Anglophone Crisis:** The ongoing conflict between the central government and separatists in the English-speaking Northwest and Southwest regions.
*   **Ambazonia:** The name of the self-declared state proclaimed by Anglophone separatists in 2017.
*   **Bakassi Peninsula:** A fertile, oil-rich peninsula whose sovereignty was transferred from Nigeria to Cameroon in 2008 following an ICJ ruling and the Greentree Agreement.
*   **BIR (Bataillon d'Intervention Rapide):** An elite Cameroonian military unit that reports directly to the president and has been implicated in human rights abuses during the Anglophone Crisis.
*   **Structural Adjustment Plan:** An economic program adopted under Biya from the IMF and World Bank, involving privatization and reduced social spending.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Electoral Legitimacy:** The credibility of Biya's repeated electoral victories is a central debate, with consistent allegations of systemic fraud from opposition parties and international observers.
*   **Succession:** Given Biya's advanced age (93 in 2026), speculation about his health and potential successor is ongoing. His son, Franck Biya, is often mentioned in this context.
*   **Resolution of the Anglophone Conflict:** The causes of the crisis—whether primarily political marginalization or linguistic/cultural division—and the path to its resolution remain deeply contested. Biya's government has been criticized for prioritizing military suppression over political dialogue.

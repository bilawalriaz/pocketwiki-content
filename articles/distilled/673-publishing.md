# Publishing

## Overview
Publishing is the process of making content—literature, music, software, and other media—available to the public in physical or digital form. It has evolved from manual scribal copying to a global, multi-format industry encompassing books, journals, newspapers, websites, and multimedia platforms. The sector ranges from large multinational conglomerates to independent presses and self-publishing authors, shaped by technological shifts, legal frameworks, and market consolidation.

## Body

### Types of Publishers and Formats
Publishing spans multiple formats and organizational models. **Newspaper publishing** delivers current news through print and digital platforms, often subsidized by advertising. **Journal publishing** involves peer-reviewed academic or technical articles, typically field-specific. **Magazine publishing** produces periodic content with creative layouts covering specific interests. **Book publishing** includes fiction and non-fiction in print, e-book, and audiobook formats, each with a registered ISBN. **Directory publishing** organizes indexed business or service data, now largely online. **Textbook publishing** serves educational needs, increasingly integrated with online learning platforms. **Catalog publishing** presents product ranges for browsing and purchase, evolving into e-commerce databases. **Web publishing** repurposes content for digital access via websites, apps, and platforms. **Advertising** provides revenue or subsidies for publishers, with ROI-driven strategies across channels. **Tie-in publishing** extends major franchises (e.g., Star Wars, Harry Potter) into spin-off books, games, and merchandise.

### Book Publishing Sub-Divisions
The book publishing landscape includes four major publisher types. **Mainstream publishers** produce large print runs, bear all costs, and distribute through established networks; authors surrender rights in exchange for royalties and sometimes advances. In 2013, Penguin (owned by Pearson) and Random House (owned by Bertelsmann) merged, narrowing the mainstream publishing industry to a handful of big publishers as it adapted to digital media. The merger created the largest consumer book publisher globally, with a global market share of more than 25 percent. As of 2022, approximately 80% percent of the United States trade market for books was controlled by the "Big Five" publishing houses: Penguin Random House, Hachette, HarperCollins, Simon & Schuster, and Macmillan. In November 2020, ViacomCBS agreed to sell Simon & Schuster, the third largest book publisher in the United States, to Penguin Random House in a deal that, if it had gone through, would have formed the largest publishing company in the world. On November 2, 2021, the United States Department of Justice filed a lawsuit (U.S. v. Bertelsmann SE & CO. KGaA, et al.) to block the merger on antitrust grounds, and on October 31, 2022, the D.C. District Court ruled in favor of the Department of Justice, filing a permanent injunction on the merger. **Small presses** (independent or indie) operate similarly but with more variable terms and often no advances. **Hybrid publishers** share costs and risks with authors, though they face scrutiny due to vanity press misrepresentation. **Vanity presses** publish any work at the author's expense, often with poor quality and deceptive practices. **Self-publishing** allows authors to retain all rights and manage all stages independently, often hiring professionals as needed.

### Recent Developments
**Accessible publishing** uses XML markup and multiple formats (larger print, dyslexia-friendly fonts, Braille, DAISY, audiobooks) to serve readers with disabilities. **Green publishing** minimizes environmental impact through print-on-demand technology and just-in-time manufacturing. Online publishing eliminates physical production entirely, enabling direct e-book distribution. Niche online marketing helps authors engage readers directly. Some publishers exhibit political bias or homophily.

### Legal and Historical Context
**Publication** legally means distributing copies to the public, requiring copyright holder consent under the Berne Convention. The Universal Copyright Convention defines Publication as reproduction in tangible form and general public distribution. **Privishing** (private publishing, but not to be confused with self-publishing) is a modern term for publishing a book but printing so few copies or with such lack of marketing, advertising, or sales support that it effectively does not reach the public. Historically, publishing began with writing and became practical with printing. Early European printed works before 1501 are called **incunables** or **incunabula**. The US publishing industry began with a printing press in Massachusetts in 1638, establishing New England as an early hub. Philadelphia also became significant, with William Bradford setting up the first paper mill and Benjamin Franklin opening his own press. By the mid-19th century, New York City became the industry's center. A U.S.-based study in 2016 that surveyed 34 publishers found that straight, able-bodied, white females overwhelmingly represent the publishing industry in the US. A survey in 2020 by the same group found there has been no significant statistical change in the lack of diversity since the 2016 survey. University presses, starting with Cornell in 1869, have faced financial pressures reducing output in the 21st century.

## Terms
- **ISBN**: International Standard Book Number, a unique identifier assigned to each published book edition.
- **Peer review**: Evaluation of scholarly work by experts in the same field before publication.
- **Incunable/Incunabulum**: Early printed books produced in Europe before 1501.
- **Print-on-demand**: Digital printing technology producing books only after an order is placed, reducing waste and inventory costs.
- **Royalty**: Payment to an author based on a percentage of book sales.
- **Advance on royalties**: Upfront payment to an author, typically recouped from future royalties.
- **Big Five**: The five dominant U.S. trade book publishers: Penguin Random House, Hachette, HarperCollins, Simon & Schuster, and Macmillan.
- **Privishing**: Publishing a book with minimal distribution or marketing, effectively preventing public access.
- **Tie-in publishing**: Creating derivative works (books, games, merchandise) based on existing media franchises.
- **Hybrid publishing**: Model where publication costs and risks are shared between author and publisher.

## Debates and Open Questions
The distinction between **hybrid publishing** and **vanity presses** remains contentious, as vanity presses often rebrand as hybrids to avoid negative associations. The **Society of Authors** and the **Writers' Guild of Great Britain** have called for reform of the paid-for publishing sector. **Diversity** in the American publishing industry has been an issue for years, with the least amount of diversity in higher-level editorial positions. The **antitrust implications** of major publisher mergers continue to be debated, as evidenced by the blocked 2022 Penguin Random House–Simon & Schuster merger.

# Johannesburg

## Overview
Johannesburg, South Africa's most populous city and economic hub, was founded in 1886 following a gold rush on the Witwatersrand. Its rapid growth was fueled by mining, but its history is deeply marked by apartheid-era segregation, urban struggles, and ongoing challenges with inequality and infrastructure. Today, it is a sprawling metropolis that functions as the continent's financial capital while grappling with its complex legacy.

- **1886** — Johannesburg founded; name first used on 3 October.
- **1896** — Population surpasses 100,000.
- **30 May 1900** — British forces occupy the city during the Second Boer War.
- **1917** — Anglo-American Corporation, a future mining giant, established in Johannesburg.

- **2022** — Census records city population as 4,803,262.

## Body

### Founding and Early Growth
The region was originally inhabited by San hunter-gatherers and later by Sotho-Tswana communities. The city's modern history began with the discovery of gold on the Witwatersrand in 1884, triggering a massive gold rush. Johannesburg was established in 1886 on farmland, and its population exploded to over 100,000 within a decade. The city's name likely derives from several men named "Johannes" involved in its early surveying and governance, though precise records are lost.

### Apartheid and Urban Segregation
The apartheid system (1948-1994) enforced strict racial separation. Johannesburg's economy depended on skilled white workers and unskilled black workers from across Southern Africa, who were forced to live apart. The government constructed massive townships like Soweto ("South-Western Townships") for black families, which grew far beyond their intended capacity. The city became a focal of resistance, from the 1960 Sharpeville massacre protests to the 1976 Soweto Uprising, where police fired on student demonstrators, killing about 575 people. The Rivonia Trial (1963-64) made Nelson Mandela a global symbol of anti-apartheid struggle.

### Post-Apartheid Challenges
The end of apartheid led to the administrative unification of the city with surrounding townships, but the spatial legacy of segregation persists, making Johannesburg one of the world's most unequal cities. The central business district (CBD) declined in the 1980s-90s due to high crime and capital flight to suburbs like Sandton. In the 21st century, the city faces severe infrastructure degradation, mismanagement, and high crime rates. It has also experienced outbreaks of xenophobic violence against migrants from other African countries.

### Economy and Infrastructure
Johannesburg is the economic engine of South Africa, producing 16% of the national GDP. While gold mining no longer occurs within city limits, it remains the headquarters for major mining companies. The economy is now dominated by services, finance, and retail, anchored by the Johannesburg Stock Exchange (JSE), Africa's largest. The city is a major transport hub, served by O.R. Tambo International Airport and a complex network of freeways, though its public transport system, including the Gautrain rapid rail, is still developing.

## Terms
- **Witwatersrand**: A mineral-rich ridge in Gauteng, South Africa, where the main gold-bearing reef was discovered, leading to the founding of Johannesburg.
- **Apartheid**: A system of institutionalized racial segregation and discrimination enforced in South Africa from 1948 until the early 1990s.
- **Soweto**: A major urban area in the south of Johannesburg, created under apartheid to house black workers; a key site of anti-apartheid resistance.
- **Gauteng**: South Africa's smallest but wealthiest province, of which Johannesburg is the capital.
- **Johannesburg Stock Exchange (JSE)**: The largest stock exchange in Africa and a major global financial market.
- **Gautrain**: A rapid rail system in Gauteng, linking Johannesburg, Pretoria, and O.R. Tambo International Airport.
- **Highveld**: The high-altitude interior plateau of South Africa where Johannesburg is located.
- **Rea Vaya**: A bus rapid transit system in Johannesburg.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The exact origin of the city's name is debated, with several candidates named Johannes (Joubert, Rissik, Meyer, Kruger) proposed, as precise records were lost.
- The spatial legacy of apartheid remains a central challenge; despite unification, Johannesburg is still highly segregated and unequal.
- The effectiveness of recent government interventions to revive the CBD and address infrastructure decay and crime is a major ongoing concern.

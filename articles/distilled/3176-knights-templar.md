# Knights Templar

## Overview
The Knights Templar were a medieval Catholic military order founded in 1118 to protect Christian pilgrims in the Holy Land. They grew into a powerful and wealthy international organization, becoming key players in the Crusades and pioneering early banking systems. Their sudden arrest, torture, and dissolution in the early 14th century, orchestrated by the French king and the Pope, created a legacy of mystery and speculation that persists in popular culture today.

## Timeline
- **1118** — Founded by Hugues de Payens to protect pilgrims; granted headquarters on Jerusalem's Temple Mount.
- **1129** — Officially endorsed by the Catholic Church at the Council of Troyes.
- **1139** — Pope Innocent II's bull *Omne Datum Optimum* exempts the order from local laws and taxes.
- **1147** — Templars receive permission to wear the red cross on their uniforms for the Second Crusade.
- **1187** — Jerusalem is recaptured by Saladin after the Battle of Hattin, a major defeat for the Crusaders.
- **1291** — Fall of Acre marks the loss of the Templars' last major stronghold in the Holy Land.
- **1307** — King Philip IV of France orders the arrest of Templars in his kingdom.
- **1312** — Pope Clement V officially disbands the order at the Council of Vienne.
- **1314** — Last Grand Master, Jacques de Molay, is burned at the stake in Paris.
- **1319** — Portuguese King Denis I refounds the order as the Order of Christ, absorbing Templar assets.

## Body

### Rise and Mission
After the First Crusade, pilgrims traveling to Jerusalem were frequently attacked. In 1119, Hugues de Payens proposed a monastic military order for their protection. King Baldwin II of Jerusalem granted them headquarters on the Temple Mount. Initially poor, the order gained powerful support from Saint Bernard of Clairvaux, leading to official Church endorsement in 1129. The papal bull *Omne Datum Optimum* (1139) granted them extraordinary privileges, including exemption from all authority except the Pope, which fueled their rapid growth.

### Military and Financial Power
The Templars became elite shock troops in the Crusades, known for their discipline and distinctive white mantles with a red cross. While their primary mission was military, most members (about 90%) were non-combatant sergeants and chaplains who managed a vast economic network. By 1150, they pioneered an early form of banking, issuing letters of credit to pilgrims. This financial infrastructure, combined with donations, made them a multinational corporation owning land, farms, fleets, and castles across Europe and the Holy Land.

### Decline and Dissolution
The tide turned against the Crusaders in the mid-12th century. After the loss of Jerusalem in 1187 and the final fall of Acre in 1291, the Templars' military mission waned. Their wealth and political autonomy, described as a "state within a state," bred resentment among European nobility. King Philip IV of France, deeply indebted to the order, orchestrated their downfall. In 1307, he arrested Templars in France, and under torture, many confessed to heresies like spitting on the Cross and worshipping idols. Under pressure from Philip, Pope Clement V disbanded the order in 1312. Grand Master Jacques de Molay was burned at the stake in 1314.

### Legacy and Successors
The dissolution was not uniform. In Portugal, King Denis I refused to persecute the Templars and in 1319 established the Order of Christ, which absorbed many former Templars and their properties. This order became vital in Portugal's maritime discoveries. Elsewhere, Templar assets were transferred to the Knights Hospitaller. The order's dramatic end fueled legends and conspiracy theories. Modern groups like the Freemasons have incorporated Templar symbolism, though historians reject any direct organizational link. The Chinon Parchment, discovered in 2001, revealed that Pope Clement V had absolved the Templars of heresy in 1308, suggesting the persecution was politically motivated.

## Terms
*   **Military Order:** A religious order whose members combine monastic life with military service, sanctioned by the Church.
*   **Papal Bull:** An official public decree or charter issued by a Pope.
*   **Commandery:** A local administrative headquarters or estate of a military order like the Templars.
*   **Letters of Credit:** Financial documents allowing a person to deposit funds in one location and withdraw equivalent value elsewhere, an early banking innovation.
*   **Grand Master:** The lifelong head of the Templar order, overseeing both military and financial operations.
*   **Reconquista:** The centuries-long period in the Iberian Peninsula where Christian kingdoms fought to reclaim territory from Muslim rule.
*   **Chinon Parchment:** A Vatican document from 1308 showing Pope Clement V absolved the Templars of heresy before disbanding them.
*   **Order of Christ:** The Portuguese military order founded in 1319 that absorbed many Templars and their assets after the dissolution.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Nature of the Charges:** Were the heresy charges against the Templars (e.g., worshipping Baphomet, spitting on the Cross) genuine beliefs, or were they fabricated under torture to justify King Philip IV's political and financial motives?
*   **Pope's Role:** To what extent was Pope Clement V a willing participant versus a pawn pressured by Philip IV? The Chinon Parchment suggests he privately absolved them while publicly succumbing to political pressure.
*   **Fate of Templar Wealth:** What happened to the vast Templar treasury and assets not transferred to the Hospitallers? Legends persist of hidden treasures.
*   **Survival and Continuity:** Do any modern organizations, such as the Portuguese Order of Christ or various neo-Templar groups, have a legitimate historical or spiritual succession from the original Knights Templar?

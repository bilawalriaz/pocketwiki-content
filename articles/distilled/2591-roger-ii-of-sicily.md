# Roger II of Sicily

## Overview
Roger II (1095–1154) was the Norman ruler who unified Sicily and southern Italy into a powerful, multicultural kingdom. He transformed a collection of fragmented territories into a centralized state, becoming one of medieval Europe's most significant monarchs through military conquest, administrative reform, and the patronage of a diverse, sophisticated court.

## Timeline
- **1095** — Born in Mileto, Calabria, son of Roger I of Sicily.
- **1105** — Inherits the County of Sicily at age nine, under his mother's regency.
- **1112** — Begins personal rule as Count of Sicily and Calabria.
- **1127** — Claims the Duchy of Apulia and Calabria after the death of William II.
- **1130** — Crowned King of Sicily by Antipope Anacletus II on Christmas Day.
- **1132** — Defeated at the Battle of Nocera by rebel barons.
- **1137** — Suffers his greatest defeat at the Battle of Rignano.
- **1139** — Captures Pope Innocent II; recognized as King of Sicily, Duke of Apulia, and Prince of Capua by the Treaty of Mignano.
- **1140** — Promulgates the Assizes of Ariano, centralizing royal authority.
- **1146–1148** — Conquers Tripoli and other North African territories.
- **1147** — Launches a major naval attack on the Byzantine Empire, sacking Corinth and Thebes.
- **1154** — Dies in Palermo; succeeded by his son William I.

## Body

### Rise to Power
Roger II inherited the County of Sicily in 1105 as a child, with his mother Adelaide del Vasto as regent. He began personal rule in 1112. His major opportunity came in 1127 when William II of Apulia died childless, allowing Roger to claim all Hauteville family lands in southern Italy. This union was resisted by Pope Honorius II and local barons. After a brief papal crusade against him, Roger was invested as Duke of Apulia in 1128. In 1130, he supported Antipope Anacletus II against Innocent II, and in return was crowned King of Sicily.

### Consolidation of the Kingdom
Roger's kingship faced immediate rebellion from peninsular barons and a coalition of European powers supporting Pope Innocent II. A decade of warfare followed (1127–1139), including major defeats for Roger at Nocera (1132) and Rignano (1137). The turning point came in 1139 when Roger ambushed and captured Pope Innocent II. The subsequent Treaty of Mignano formally recognized Roger as King of Sicily, Duke of Apulia, and Prince of Capua. He then reorganized the mainland provinces and, in 1140, issued the Assizes of Ariano. This legislation established royal supremacy over the nobility and created a centralized bureaucracy, effectively founding the Norman Kingdom of Sicily as a coherent state.

### Multicultural Court and Economy
Roger's court in Palermo was a renowned center of learning and multiculturalism, employing Greek, Arab, and Latin administrators. He patronized scholars like the geographer al-Idrisi. His famous Royal Mantle (1133–34), made of Byzantine silk with Arabic inscriptions, symbolized this synthesis. Economically, Roger introduced new coinage to facilitate Mediterranean trade, though it was unpopular locally. Sicily prospered as a trade hub, exporting wheat and other goods, with its merchants protected by a powerful navy.

### Naval Power and Foreign Campaigns
Under admirals like George of Antioch, Roger built a formidable fleet that made Sicily the leading Mediterranean naval power. He conquered parts of North Africa (1146–1148), including Tripoli. During the Second Crusade (1147–48), he attacked the Byzantine Empire, sacking Corinth and Thebes and bringing silk weavers to Palermo, which established the Sicilian silk industry. These campaigns expanded his realm and wealth but were not permanently held.

## Terms
- **Regent**: A person who governs a state because the monarch is a minor, absent, or incapacitated.
- **Investiture**: The formal ceremony of conferring a fief or office upon a vassal by a lord.
- **Antipope**: A claimant to the role of bishop of Rome in opposition to the canonically elected pope.
- **Assizes of Ariano**: A set of laws issued by Roger II in 1140 to centralize royal authority and regulate the kingdom.
- **Ammiratus ammiratorum**: "Emir of Emirs," a title meaning "admiral of admirals," used for the chief naval commander.
- **Tiraz**: A band of embroidered or woven inscription on a textile, often indicating royal manufacture.
- **Ducat**: A gold coin introduced by Roger II, which became a standard currency in Mediterranean trade.
- **Regno**: The Norman Kingdom of Sicily and its mainland possessions.

## Debates and Open Questions
The exact nature and intent of Roger II's multicultural rule is debated. Some scholars see it as a pragmatic, tolerant administration, while others emphasize its use of diverse cultural symbols (like the Royal Mantle) primarily as propaganda to legitimize his new dynasty and project power. The long-term impact and integration of his North African conquests are also unclear, as they were lost shortly after his death.

# Syngman Rhee

## Overview
Syngman Rhee (1875–1965) was a Korean independence activist and the first president of South Korea, serving from 1948 until his ouster in 1960. His authoritarian rule, marked by corruption and repression, was defined by the Korean War and ended with the student-led April Revolution. He is a foundational yet deeply controversial figure in South Korean history.

## Timeline
- **26 March 1875** — Born in Hwanghae Province, Joseon.
- **1899** — Imprisoned for anti-Japanese activities; wrote *The Spirit of Independence*.
- **1904** — Released from prison; moves to the United States.
- **1919** — Becomes first president of the Korean Provisional Government in exile.
- **1925** — Impeached and removed as Provisional Government president.
- **20 July 1948** — Elected first president of the Republic of Korea.
- **25 June 1950** — Korean War begins with North Korean invasion.
- **18 June 1953** — Unilaterally releases anti-communist POWs, disrupting armistice talks.
- **27 July 1953** — Korean War armistice signed; Rhee's government refuses to sign.
- **15 March 1960** — Re-elected president in a widely disputed election.
- **26 April 1960** — Resigns following the April Revolution.
- **19 July 1965** — Dies in exile in Honolulu, Hawaii.

## Body

### Early Life and Independence Activism
Born during the Joseon period, Rhee converted to Christianity at a Methodist school. He became an anti-Japanese activist, was imprisoned from 1899 to 1904, and then moved to the United States, earning degrees from George Washington, Harvard, and Princeton. He became a leading figure in the Korean independence movement abroad, serving as the first president of the Korean Provisional Government from 1919 until his impeachment in 1925 over allegations of misuse of power. He continued independence advocacy in the U.S. and Hawaii.

### Rise to Power and the First Republic
After Japan's surrender in 1945, Rhee returned to U.S.-occupied Korea with American backing, favored for his fluent English and staunch anti-communism. He opposed the U.S.-Soviet trusteeship proposal and emerged as a compromise candidate among fractious Korean factions. On 20 July 1948, he was elected the first president of the Republic of Korea. His administration was immediately authoritarian, enacting laws to curtail dissent and overseeing the suppression of leftist opponents, including the Jeju uprising.

### The Korean War and Post-War Rule
Rhee was president when North Korea invaded on 25 June 1950, starting the Korean War. He established a temporary government in Busan and insisted on reunifying the peninsula by force (*Bukjin Tongil*). His regime was notoriously corrupt, exemplified by the National Defense Corps Incident where tens of thousands of conscripts died from starvation and neglect. Rhee refused to sign the 1953 armistice, fearing it would leave South Korea vulnerable. His unilateral release of anti-communist POWs in June 1953 was a political maneuver to pressure the U.S. into a mutual defense treaty, which was signed in October 1953.

### Downfall and Exile
Post-war South Korea remained impoverished and reliant on U.S. aid. After winning re-election in 1956, Rhee pushed through a constitutional amendment to remove presidential term limits. He won a fourth term in March 1960 after his main opponent died. The subsequent vice-presidential election, won by his ally Lee Ki-poong amid fraud allegations, triggered mass protests. The student-led April Revolution forced Rhee to resign on 26 April 1960. He departed for exile in Hawaii, where he died in 1965.

## Terms
- **Provisional Government of the Republic of Korea:** A government-in-exile for Korea, established in Shanghai in 1919 during Japanese rule.
- **April Revolution:** The 1960 student-led uprising that forced President Syngman Rhee to resign following a fraudulent election.
- **Bukjin Tongil:** A policy advocating the reunification of Korea by conquering the North.
- **Armistice Agreement:** The 1953 agreement that ended the fighting in the Korean War; Rhee's government refused to sign it.
- **Bodo League:** A government-organized "re-education" group for suspected communist sympathizers; many members were massacred at the war's outset.
- **National Defense Corps Incident:** A 1950-51 scandal where corruption led to the deaths of tens of thousands of poorly supplied conscripts.
- **Korea-U.S. Mutual Defense Treaty:** A 1953 treaty guaranteeing U.S. military support for South Korea, a key outcome of Rhee's post-war diplomacy.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Legacy:** Rhee is a polarizing figure. Some view him as the founding father who established the Republic of Korea and secured its alliance with the U.S., while others emphasize his authoritarian rule, corruption, and human rights abuses.
- **Role in the Korean War:** Debates continue over the extent to which Rhee's actions and provocations contributed to the outbreak and prolongation of the war.
- **Economic Stewardship:** His presidency saw limited economic development, leaving South Korea impoverished and aid-dependent, a stark contrast to the later "Miracle on the Han River."

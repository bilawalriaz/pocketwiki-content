# Dhaka

## Overview
Dhaka is the capital and largest city of Bangladesh, serving as the country's primary political, economic, and cultural hub. With an estimated population of 36.6 million, it is the second most populous city in the world and is considered the most densely populated built-up urban area. Its history spans over a millennium, evolving from a Mughal provincial capital to a British colonial center and finally the capital of an independent Bangladesh.

## Timeline
- **1610** — Dhaka becomes the capital of the Mughal province of Bengal, Bihar, and Orissa.
- **1678** — Construction of Lalbagh Fort is commenced by Prince Azam Shah.
- **1717** — The provincial capital is shifted from Dhaka to Murshidabad.
- **1793** — The city formally passes to the control of the British East India Company.
- **1905** — Dhaka becomes the capital of the new province of Eastern Bengal and Assam.
- **1921** — The University of Dhaka is established.
- **1947** — Following the partition of India, Dhaka becomes the administrative capital of East Pakistan.
- **1971** — After the Liberation War, Dhaka becomes the capital of independent Bangladesh.
- **2022** — The first phase of the Dhaka Metro Rail is inaugurated.

## Body

### Mughal and Pre-Colonial Era
The area has been inhabited since the first millennium. Under Mughal rule from 1610, Dhaka (named Jahangirnagar) became a major commercial center and the hub of the global muslin trade. Its prosperity peaked in the 17th and 18th centuries under governors like Shaista Khan, with a population estimated at 900,000. The Mughals built extensive infrastructure, including gardens, mosques, and forts like Lalbagh. Internal Mughal court conflicts led to the capital's relocation in 1717.

### British Colonial Period
British rule began in 1793, initially leading to economic decline as Dhaka's textile industry collapsed. The city's fortunes revived with the first partition of Bengal in 1905, which made it the capital of Eastern Bengal and Assam and spurred modern development, including the University of Dhaka (1921). The British introduced railways, electricity, and Western-style institutions. By 1872, Hindus formed a majority of the city's population, a demographic that shifted dramatically after the 1947 partition of India, which saw a mass exodus of Hindus to West Bengal.

### Post-Independence Bangladesh
After the 1971 Liberation War, Dhaka became the capital of Bangladesh. The city experienced explosive, largely unplanned growth fueled by rural migration, with its population doubling from 6 million to 12 million between 1990 and 2005. This rapid urbanization has led to severe infrastructure challenges, including extreme traffic congestion, inadequate water and sanitation systems, and widespread slums housing nearly one-third of the population. The Buriganga River is severely polluted. Despite these issues, Dhaka is a major economic center, accounting for 35% of Bangladesh's economy, and is a hub for the textile industry and finance.

### Culture and Society
Dhaka is a center of Bengali culture, known for its culinary heritage (especially Kacchi Biryani), rickshaw art, and festivals like Pohela Baishakh (Bengali New Year). Its architecture spans Mughal, British colonial, and modernist styles, with the Jatiya Sangsad Bhaban (National Parliament House) as a prominent landmark. The city is predominantly Muslim, with significant Hindu, Christian, and Buddhist minorities. It is the largest Bengali-speaking city in the world.

## Terms
- **Muslin:** A fine, sheer cotton textile historically produced in the Dhaka region, famous globally during the Mughal era.
- **Jatiya Sangsad Bhaban:** The National Parliament House of Bangladesh, a modernist architectural landmark designed by Louis Kahn.
- **Pohela Baishakh:** The Bengali New Year, celebrated on April 14, a major cultural festival in Dhaka.
- **Kacchi Biryani:** A signature dish of Dhaka's Mughlai cuisine, made with mutton, potatoes, and rice.
- **Hartal:** A form of political protest involving a general strike or shutdown of businesses and transportation.
- **Vested Property Act:** A law used to seize properties left by Hindus who migrated to India after 1947.
- **Bihari:** A term for Urdu-speaking Muslim refugees from eastern India who settled in East Pakistan (now Bangladesh) after 1947.
- **Dhakaiya Kutti:** A distinctive Bengali dialect spoken in Old Dhaka.

## Debates and open questions
- The etymology of the name "Dhaka" is uncertain, with multiple theories involving the dhak tree, the goddess Dhakeshwari, or a drum instrument.
- The exact population of Bihari refugees in Bangladesh is ambiguous, with estimates ranging from 40,000 to over 300,000.
- The long-term sustainability of Dhaka's growth model is a critical question, given its severe congestion, pollution, and infrastructure deficits.

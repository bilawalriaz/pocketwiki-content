# Muhammad Ali

## Overview
Muhammad Ali (born Cassius Marcellus Clay Jr.) was an American professional boxer and activist, widely regarded as one of the most significant sports figures of the 20th century. His global impact stemmed from his extraordinary boxing career, his principled refusal to serve in the Vietnam War, and his role as a cultural icon for civil rights and racial pride.

- **Jun 3, 2016** — Dies in Scottsdale, Arizona, at age 74.

## Body

### Early Life and Amateur Career
Born to Odessa Grady Clay and Cassius Marcellus Clay Sr., he grew up amid racial segregation in Louisville, Kentucky. He began training as an amateur boxer at age 12 after a police officer suggested he learn to box. He won six Kentucky Golden Gloves titles, two national Golden Gloves titles, and the light heavyweight gold medal at the 1960 Summer Olympics. His amateur record was 100–8.

### Professional Rise and Name Change
Clay turned professional in 1960 and quickly rose through the ranks, known for his speed, footwork, and provocative trash talk. On February 25, 1964, he defeated Sonny Liston in a major upset to become heavyweight champion at age 22. Shortly after, he announced his conversion to the Nation of Islam and changed his name to Muhammad Ali, rejecting his birth name as a "slave name."

### Draft Resistance and Exile
In 1967, Ali refused induction into the U.S. Army, citing his religious beliefs and opposition to the Vietnam War. He was convicted of draft evasion, stripped of his boxing titles, and had his license suspended. During his three-and-a-half-year exile from boxing, he became a prominent activist and speaker on college campuses. His conviction was overturned by the Supreme Court in 1971.

### The Comeback and Classic Fights
Ali returned to boxing in 1970. His career featured several historic bouts, including the "Fight of the Century" (1971) and "Thrilla in Manila" (1975) against Joe Frazier, and "The Rumble in the Jungle" (1974) against George Foreman. In the latter, he introduced the "rope-a-dope" strategy, absorbing Foreman's punches on the ropes before knocking him out to regain the title. He became the first three-time heavyweight champion in 1978.

### Later Career, Health, and Legacy
Ali's later career included a loss to Leon Spinks and a comeback to win the title a third time. He fought on until 1981, with his final years in the ring marked by decline. In 1984, he was diagnosed with Parkinson's syndrome. He remained a global humanitarian and public figure until his death. His legacy encompasses his athletic achievements, his stand against the Vietnam War, and his influence on hip-hop culture and civil rights.

## Terms
- **Conscientious objector**: A person who refuses to serve in the armed forces on grounds of freedom of thought, conscience, or religion.
- **Nation of Islam**: A Black nationalist and religious organization founded in the United States.
- **Rope-a-dope**: A boxing strategy where a fighter leans against the ropes to absorb punches from an opponent, tiring them out.
- **Parkinson's syndrome**: A group of neurological disorders that cause movement problems, which Ali was diagnosed with in 1984.
- **Lineal heavyweight champion**: The boxer who has defeated the reigning champion in the ring, tracing a direct lineage of titleholders.
- **Undisputed champion**: A boxer who holds all major world titles in a weight division simultaneously.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The cause of Ali's Parkinson's syndrome is debated; while some attribute it to boxing-related head trauma, he and his physicians disputed this link.
- The story of Ali throwing his 1960 Olympic gold medal into the Ohio River after being refused service at a segregated restaurant is disputed by friends and biographers.
- The "phantom punch" that knocked out Sonny Liston in their 1965 rematch remains controversial, with speculation that Liston took a dive.

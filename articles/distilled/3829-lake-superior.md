# Lake Superior

## Overview
Lake Superior is the largest freshwater lake in the world by surface area, straddling the Canada–U.S. border. It is the highest, westernmost, and northernmost of the Great Lakes, draining into Lake Huron via the St. Marys River. Its immense size, cold temperatures, and geological history make it a unique and powerful ecosystem, historically vital for shipping, mining, and Indigenous cultures.

## Timeline
- **c. 10,000 BC** — First humans (Plano people) arrive after glacial retreat.
- **c. 5000 BC** — Shield Archaic peoples arrive, using copper tools and establishing trade networks.
- **17th Century** — French explorers name it *le lac supérieur* ("the upper lake").
- **1760s** — British take control and anglicize the name to "Superior."
- **1814** — Hudson's Bay Company and North West Company compete in the fur trade.
- **1847** — The first steamboat, the *Independence*, operates on the lake.
- **1914** — International Lake Superior Board of Control established to regulate water levels.
- **1975** — The SS *Edmund Fitzgerald* sinks, killing all 29 crew.
- **1985** — J. Val Klump reaches the lake's deepest point.
- **2007** — A new record low water level is set in September.
- **2019-2020** — New monthly high-water records are set.

## Body

### Name and Hydrography
The Ojibwe name is *gichi-gami*, meaning "great sea." French explorers called it *le lac supérieur* ("upper lake"), which the British later anglicized to Superior. The lake is the world's largest freshwater lake by surface area (31,700 sq mi) and third-largest by volume. It is a dimictic lake, meaning its waters mix thoroughly twice yearly. Its average surface elevation is 600 feet above sea level, and its water levels are regulated by the International Lake Superior Board of Control via structures like the Soo Locks.

### Climate and Geology
Lake Superior moderates the regional climate, creating lake-effect snow and cooler summers. Its northern shore features ancient Precambrian granites of the Canadian Shield. The lake basin lies in the Midcontinent Rift, a Mesoproterozoic rift valley. The modern landscape was carved by the Wisconsin glaciation, which ended about 10,000 years ago, leaving the precursor lake, Lake Minong.

### Human History and Shipping
The region was inhabited by successive Indigenous cultures, including the Anishinaabe (Ojibwe), who became dominant by the mid-18th century. The fur trade, dominated by the Hudson's Bay Company and North West Company, was a major economic force. The lake became a key shipping route for iron ore and grain, with the shipping season typically running from late March to mid-January. The "Graveyard of the Great Lakes" along its southern shore is notorious for shipwrecks, including the SS *Edmund Fitzgerald*.

### Ecology
Lake Superior is oligotrophic, meaning it has low nutrient levels and high oxygen content, supporting over 80 fish species. Native species include lake trout and lake sturgeon. Introduced species, such as sea lamprey and various salmon, have impacted native populations. The lake's cold water inhibits bacterial decay, which is why bodies from shipwrecks often do not resurface.

## Terms
- **Dimictic**: A lake that mixes from top to bottom twice a year, in spring and fall.
- **Oligotrophic**: A lake with low nutrient levels, high oxygen, and typically clear, cold water.
- **Catchment area**: The area of land from which water drains into a lake or river.
- **Midcontinent Rift**: A 1.1-billion-year-old geological rift valley that underlies the Lake Superior basin.
- **Soo Locks**: A set of locks on the St. Marys River that allow ships to bypass rapids and navigate the height difference between Lakes Superior and Huron.
- **Lake-effect snow**: Snow generated when cold air moves over warmer lake water, picking up moisture and depositing it as snow on downwind shores.
- **Anishinaabe**: An ethnic group including the Ojibwe (Chippewa), who have inhabited the Lake Superior region for centuries.
- **Compensating Works**: The regulating structure on the St. Marys River that controls Lake Superior's outflow.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Climate Change Impact**: Studies suggest Lake Superior's surface temperature increased by about 4.5°F between 1979 and 2007, faster than the surrounding air. While some models predict it could be routinely ice-free by 2040, more recent data through 2021 does not support this trend.
- **Nitrate Levels**: Nitrate concentrations in the lake have been rising for over a century. While still below dangerous levels, the cause of this long-term increase is uncertain and may relate to human alterations of the regional nitrogen cycle.

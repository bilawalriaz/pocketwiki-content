# Khalid ibn al-Walid

## Overview
Khalid ibn al-Walid (d. 642) was a preeminent 7th-century Arab military commander for the early Muslim state. Initially opposing Muhammad, he converted to Islam and became one of its most celebrated generals, playing a decisive role in the Ridda Wars and the early conquests of Sasanian Iraq and Byzantine Syria. His career exemplifies the rapid expansion of the early Caliphate.

- **629–630** — Leads forces in the Muslim conquest of Mecca and the Battle of Hunayn.
- **632** — Defeats the rebel leader Tulayha at the Battle of Buzakha.
- **Dec 632** — Defeats and kills the rebel prophet Musaylima at the Battle of al-Yamama.
- **633** — Launches campaigns in Sasanian Iraq, capturing al-Hira.
- **634** — Leads the unconventional desert march to Syria; arrives at Marj Rahit.
- **634–635** — Leads Muslim forces to victory at Ajnadayn and besieges/captures Damascus.
- **Aug 636** — Commands the Muslim army to a decisive victory over the Byzantines at the Battle of Yarmouk.
- **636–638** — Leads the sieges and captures of Homs, Aleppo, and Qinnasrin in northern Syria.
- **638** — Dismissed from supreme command and governorship by Caliph Umar.
- **642** — Dies in Medina or Homs.

## Body

### Early Life and Opposition to Muhammad
Khalid was born into the aristocratic Banu Makhzum clan of the Quraysh tribe in Mecca, a leading family that strongly opposed Muhammad. He first gained military renown commanding the Quraysh cavalry at the Battle of Uhud (625), where his flanking maneuver was instrumental in their victory.

### Conversion and Service Under Muhammad
Khalid converted to Islam around 627–629, after which Muhammad appointed him a commander and gave him the title *Sayf Allah* ("Sword of God"). He participated in key campaigns, including the conquest of Mecca (629–630) and the Battle of Hunayn (630). His career was marked by both decisive action and controversy, such as the unauthorized attack on the Banu Jadhima tribe, for which Muhammad declared himself innocent but did not punish him.

### Commander in the Ridda Wars
Following Muhammad's death in 632, Khalid became a staunch supporter of Caliph Abu Bakr. He was dispatched to suppress tribal rebellions in central Arabia (Najd). He defeated the self-proclaimed prophet Tulayha at the Battle of Buzakha (September 632). His campaign against the Tamim tribe culminated in the controversial execution of their chief, Malik ibn Nuwayra, and Khalid's subsequent marriage to Malik's widow, an act that drew criticism from Umar. Khalid then defeated the rival prophet Musaylima at the Battle of al-Yamama (December 632), a key engagement that secured Medina's control over Arabia.

### Campaigns in Iraq and the March to Syria
After pacifying Najd, Khalid marched into Sasanian Iraq, capturing the major Arab town of al-Hira and securing tribute. The extent of his role in Iraq is debated by modern historians. In 634, Abu Bakr ordered him to reinforce Muslim armies in Syria. Khalid led his army on a celebrated and strategically brilliant march across a waterless desert, a feat that boosted his legendary reputation.

### Conquest of Syria
Appointed supreme commander in Syria, Khalid led Muslim forces to major victories. He captured Bosra (634) and played a key role in the siege and fall of Damascus (634–635). His masterpiece was the Battle of Yarmouk (636), where his tactical genius led to the destruction of the main Byzantine army in Syria, securing Muslim control of the region. He subsequently led the conquests of Homs, Aleppo, and Qinnasrin in northern Syria.

### Demotion and Death
Caliph Umar, concerned about Khalid's growing popularity and citing reasons including his independent command and the Malik ibn Nuwayra incident, dismissed him from supreme command around 636–638. Khalid continued to serve as a lieutenant under his successor, Abu Ubayda, in northern Syria. He was finally dismissed from all posts in 638 and spent his final years in obscurity, dying in 642.

## Terms
*   **Ridda Wars (Wars of Apostasy):** The 632–633 campaigns led by Abu Bakr to re-establish Muslim authority over Arabian tribes that had rebelled or withheld allegiance after Muhammad's death.
*   **Sayf Allah:** An honorary title meaning "Sword of God," bestowed upon Khalid by Muhammad.
*   **Jizya:** A poll tax levied on non-Muslim subjects (dhimmis) in an Islamic state in return for protection and exemption from military service.
*   **Muhajirun ("Emigrants"):** The early Muslims who migrated with Muhammad from Mecca to Medina.
*   **Ansar ("Helpers"):** The natives of Medina who supported and hosted the Muhajirun after the Hijra.
*   **Sasanian Empire:** The last pre-Islamic Persian empire, a major rival to the Byzantine Empire and the early Caliphate.
*   **Byzantine Empire:** The Eastern Roman Empire, the other major power in the region and the primary adversary in the Muslim conquest of Syria.
*   **Ghassanids:** An Arab Christian client kingdom of the Byzantine Empire in the Levant, often opposing the early Muslim armies.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Role in Iraq:** Historians debate whether Khalid's campaign in Iraq was sanctioned by Abu Bakr or a unilateral initiative. Some, like Patricia Crone, question his significant involvement there based on non-Arabic sources.
*   **Execution of Malik ibn Nuwayra:** The circumstances and justification for this event are highly contested in traditional sources. Scholars disagree on whether Malik had apostatized, making his execution lawful, or if Khalid acted improperly.
*   **Reasons for Dismissal:** The primary cause for Umar's dismissal of Khalid is debated. Proposed reasons include his independent command, the Malik ibn Nuwayra affair, his lavish distribution of spoils, Umar's personal enmity, or a political need to appease local Syrian tribes like the Kalb.
*   **The Desert March:** While celebrated, some modern scholars, like Ryan J. Lynch, suggest the detailed narrative of the march across the Syrian desert may be a later literary construct designed to link the conquests of Iraq and Syria.

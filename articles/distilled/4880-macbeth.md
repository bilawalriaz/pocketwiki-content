# Macbeth

## Overview
*The Tragedy of Macbeth* is a tragedy by William Shakespeare, estimated to have been first performed in 1606. It dramatises the physically violent and damaging psychological effects of political ambitions and power. The play is Shakespeare's shortest tragedy and is believed to contain the most allusions to King James I, patron of Shakespeare's acting company.

## Timeline
- **c. 1606** — Estimated first performance of *Macbeth*.
- **1623** — First published in the First Folio; the only source for the text.
- **1664-1668** — Diarist Samuel Pepys records seeing the play multiple times, praising its acting.
- **1744** — David Garrick revives the play, claiming it is "as written by Shakespeare."
- **1849** — Rival performances spark the Astor Place riot in Manhattan, killing 31.
- **1936** — Orson Welles directs an all-black "Voodoo Macbeth" in Harlem.
- **1957** — Akira Kurosawa's Japanese film adaptation *Throne of Blood* is released.
- **1976** — Ian McKellen stars in Trevor Nunn's successful RSC production at The Other Place.
- **1999** — Antony Sher stars in Gregory Doran's successful RSC production at The Swan.
- **2023-2024** — David Tennant and Cush Jumbo star in a Donmar Warehouse production.

## Body

### Plot
A Scottish general, Macbeth, receives a prophecy from three witches that he will become king. Consumed by ambition and spurred by his wife, Lady Macbeth, he murders King Duncan and seizes the throne. Guilt and paranoia lead him to commit further murders, becoming a tyrannical ruler. The violence perpetrated by the couple leads to their insanity and deaths. Macbeth is ultimately killed in battle by Macduff, who was "from his mother's womb untimely ripp'd" (born by Caesarean section), fulfilling a loophole in the witches' prophecy. Order is restored with Malcolm as the new king.

### Sources and Historical Context
Shakespeare's primary source is Holinshed's *Chronicles* (1587), which combines stories of Donwald and King Macbeth. However, Shakespeare altered key details: he set Duncan's murder in Macbeth's own castle, heightening the violation of hospitality, and made Banquo an innocent nobleman rather than an accomplice, likely to flatter King James I, who believed himself descended from Banquo. Scholars widely associate the play with the Gunpowder Plot of 1605, citing allusions to equivocation (the practice of using ambiguous language to conceal the truth) in the Porter's scene and the witches' prophecies. The play was likely written in 1606, a period of political uncertainty following James's accession to the English throne.

### Themes and Critical Analysis
Critics have analyzed *Macbeth* as a tragedy of character, focusing on Macbeth's ambition and imagination. A.C. Bradley saw his poetic imagination as central to his guilt. Others, like Caroline Spurgeon, note the imagery of ill-fitting clothing symbolizing his unfitness for kingship. The play is also read as a tragedy of moral order, where Macbeth's actions invert the natural world, symbolized by disrupted sleep and gender roles. The witches introduce ambiguity and chaos, their prophecies tempting Macbeth while operating in a realm between reality and the supernatural. The play's brevity and fast pace have led some to believe the Folio text is an abridged version, possibly for indoor performance.

### Superstition and Performance History
A long-standing theatre superstition holds that speaking the play's name inside a theatre brings bad luck, leading actors to refer to it as "The Scottish Play." The play has a rich performance history, from Restoration adaptations by Sir William Davenant to 19th-century spectacles and 20th-century psychological interpretations. Notable 20th-century productions include those by Laurence Olivier (1955), Ian McKellen (1976), and Antony Sher (1999). The play has been adapted globally into numerous films, operas (notably by Giuseppe Verdi), and other media.

## Terms
- **Thane**: A Scottish feudal lord or nobleman.
- **Equivocation**: The practice of using ambiguous language to conceal the truth, a central theme linked to the Gunpowder Plot.
- **Prompt book**: A copy of a play used by a theatre company for performances, possibly the source for the Folio text.
- **First Folio**: The 1623 collection of Shakespeare's plays, the only source for *Macbeth*.
- **Great Chain of Being**: A medieval concept of a divinely ordained hierarchical order of the universe, which Macbeth's actions disrupt.
- **Primogeniture**: The right of the firstborn child to inherit the entire estate, a norm the play questions.
- **Caesarean section**: A surgical delivery of a baby, the method of Macduff's birth that fulfills the witches' prophecy.
- **Hecate**: The Greek goddess of witchcraft, whose scenes in the play are possibly later interpolations.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Date of Composition**: While most scholars date the play to 1606, some argue for an earlier date (as early as 1599) or a later date based on specific allusions.
- **Textual Integrity**: Scholars debate whether the First Folio text is a complete, original version or an abridged adaptation from a longer manuscript or prompt book. The inclusion of songs and the character Hecate may be later additions by Thomas Middleton.
- **Banquo's Character**: The extent to which Shakespeare altered Banquo's historical role from accomplice to noble foil, and whether this was primarily for political flattery of King James I.
- **The Witches' Nature**: Interpretations vary on whether the witches are literal supernatural agents, psychological symbols, or embodiments of chaos and political treason.

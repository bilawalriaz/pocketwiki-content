# The Hague

## Overview
The Hague (Dutch: Den Haag) is the administrative capital of the Netherlands and the seat of its government, despite Amsterdam being the official capital. It is a major hub for international law and justice, hosting the International Court of Justice and numerous other global organizations. The city is the third-largest in the Netherlands and forms the core of a large metropolitan area on the North Sea coast.

## Timeline
- **1229** — Floris IV, Count of Holland, acquires a court by the Hofvijver.
- **1242** — The village is first mentioned as "Die Haghe" in a charter.
- **1248** — William II, King of the Romans, begins converting the court into a palace (the Binnenhof).
- **1358** — The Hague becomes the primary residence of the Counts of Holland.
- **1588** — The city becomes the permanent seat of the States General of the Dutch Republic.
- **1806** — The Hague is granted city rights by Louis Bonaparte.
- **1907-1913** — The Peace Palace, headquarters of the Permanent Court of Arbitration, is built.
- **1945** — The Bezuidenhout quarter is mistakenly bombed by the Royal Air Force, causing 511 fatalities.
- **1990** — The municipal government officially adopts the shorter name "Den Haag."
- **2002** — The International Criminal Court is established in the city.

## Body

### Etymology and Early History
The name "Den Haag" derives from the Middle Dutch word *hag(h)e*, meaning "hedge" or "enclosure." The fuller form, 's-Gravenhage ("the count's enclosure"), first appeared in 1347. The settlement originated as a residence for the Counts of Holland in the 13th century, with the Binnenhof ("Inner Court") complex serving as their administrative center. It grew into a city by the Late Middle Ages but did not receive official city rights until 1806.

### Political and Administrative Center
From 1588, The Hague was the seat of the Dutch Republic's government. After Belgian independence in 1830, Amsterdam remained the capital, but the government stayed in The Hague. The city's role solidified in the 19th century as the national government expanded. Today, it houses the Cabinet, the States General (parliament), the Supreme Court, and the Council of State. The King works at the Noordeinde Palace and lives in Huis ten Bosch.

### International City of Peace and Justice
The Hague's global role began with the establishment of the Permanent Court of Arbitration (PCA) in the early 20th century. The construction of the Peace Palace (1907-1913) symbolized this mission. After World War II, the city became the seat of the United Nations' International Court of Justice (ICJ). It later hosted the International Criminal Tribunal for the former Yugoslavia and the International Criminal Court (ICC). Today, it is home to approximately 200 international governmental organizations, including Europol and the Organisation for the Prohibition of Chemical Weapons (OPCW).

### Geography and Demographics
The Hague is the largest Dutch city on the North Sea and is part of the Randstad conurbation. It is divided into eight districts, with notable socioeconomic differences between the wealthier northwestern areas and poorer southeastern neighborhoods. As of 2021, the population was 549,163. The city is culturally diverse, with 34.4% of inhabitants having at least one parent born in a non-Western country. Christianity (29%) and Islam (14.1%) are the most common religions.

### Economy and Culture
The economy is service-oriented, dominated by government and international institutions, which account for 26% of jobs. Major companies like Shell and Aegon have headquarters there. Tourism is significant, with Scheveningen beach being a major attraction. Cultural life centers on the Binnenhof area and squares like the Plein. The city hosts numerous museums, including the Mauritshuis and Kunstmuseum, and is known for events like the annual Prinsjesdag (Prince's Day) parliamentary opening and the Tong Tong Fair, the world's largest Indo culture festival.

## Terms
- **Binnenhof**: The "Inner Court," a complex of buildings in The Hague that houses the Dutch parliament and was originally the residence of the Counts of Holland.
- **Randstad**: A megalopolis in the western Netherlands, including Amsterdam, Rotterdam, Utrecht, and The Hague, with a combined population of over 6.6 million.
- **States General**: The bicameral parliament of the Netherlands, consisting of the Senate and the House of Representatives, which meets in The Hague.
- **Permanent Court of Arbitration (PCA)**: The first global organization for the settlement of international disputes, established in The Hague in 1899.
- **International Court of Justice (ICJ)**: The principal judicial organ of the United Nations, based in the Peace Palace in The Hague.
- **RandstadRail**: A light rail and subway network connecting The Hague with Rotterdam, Zoetermeer, and other nearby cities.
- **Vinex-location**: A large residential area built according to Dutch national spatial planning policy from the 1990s onward, often on the outskirts of cities.
- **Prinsjesdag**: "Prince's Day," the annual opening of the Dutch parliamentary year on the third Tuesday in September, featuring the King's Speech from the Throne.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The city's lack of official city rights for most of its history, despite being the de facto capital, is a unique administrative anomaly.
- The 1945 bombing of the Bezuidenhout quarter by Allied forces, which caused significant civilian casualties, remains a tragic historical event.
- The socioeconomic divide between different districts of the city, reflected in local accents and demographics, is a persistent social issue.

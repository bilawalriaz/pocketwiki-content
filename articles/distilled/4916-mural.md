# Mural

## Overview
A mural is a piece of graphic artwork painted or applied directly to a permanent substrate like a wall or ceiling. Murals are significant because they bring art into the public sphere, often serving as tools for social communication, political expression, and community identity. Their large scale and public nature make them accessible to a wide audience beyond traditional art galleries.

- **c. 32,000 BP** — Chauvet Cave paintings in France are created.
- **c. 3150 BC** — Ancient Egyptian tomb murals appear.
- **1700–1600 BC** — Minoan palace murals are painted in the Neopalatial period.
- **c. 1200–900 BC** — Murals are created at Oxtotitlán and Juxtlahuaca in Mexico.
- **c. 100 BC – AD 79** — Murals are painted in Pompeii.
- **14th century** — Kerala mural painting tradition in India dates from this period.
- **c. 1300** — Fresco technique (painting on wet plaster) is reintroduced in Italy.
- **1906** — Dr. Atl issues a manifesto for Mexico's *pintura mural* movement.
- **1930s** — Mexican muralism movement (Rivera, Orozco, Siqueiros) gains prominence.
- **1980s** — Trompe-l'œil painting experiences a renaissance in Europe.
- **1980s–1989** — Artists paint on the Western side of the Berlin Wall.

## Body

### History and Technique
Murals date back to Upper Paleolithic cave paintings. In the Middle Ages, they were typically executed on dry plaster (*fresco secco*). Around 1300 in Italy, the technique of painting on wet plaster (*buon fresco*) was reintroduced, improving quality. In *buon fresco*, pigment mixed with water is applied to wet lime plaster; a chemical reaction fixes the pigment as the plaster dries, ensuring longevity. Other historical methods include *mezzo-fresco* (on nearly-dry plaster) and the use of tempera (pigment bound in egg) or encaustic (wax-based) colors. Modern murals use oil, water-based, or acrylic paints, and can be created via direct painting, projection, or digital printing and transfer (e.g., Frescography).

### Social and Political Significance
Murals are important for making art public. They are often commissioned by governments or businesses and can serve as tools for social emancipation, political goals, or propaganda. The Mexican mural movement of the 1930s, led by Diego Rivera, David Siqueiros, and José Orozco, exemplified this, with Rivera's *Detroit Industry* (1933) later defended for its artistic merit despite political controversy. Murals have also been used in conflict zones, such as Northern Ireland, where nearly 2,000 have been documented since the 1970s, and on the Berlin Wall, which became a "world's longest canvas" for artists like Keith Haring. They can function as memorials to victims of violence, weaving into the fabric of neighborhoods.

### Contemporary and Interior Design
In contemporary design, murals are commissioned for private homes, schools, and public buildings to create atmosphere or express individuality. The trend for feature walls has increased commissions. Styles range from traditional hand-painting to graffiti and street art aesthetics, which have been adopted by corporate clients. Ethnic murals, like Indian Warli painting, display traditional culture. Tile murals, made from ceramic, glass, or stone pieces, are another form, with historical examples like Portuguese *azulejos*. Modern technology allows for custom-printed tile murals using digital images.

## Terms
- **Fresco**: A mural painting technique using pigments applied to wet (*buon fresco*) or dry (*fresco secco*) plaster.
- **Marouflage**: A technique where a painting is executed on canvas and then attached to a wall with adhesive.
- **Trompe-l'œil**: A French term meaning "trick the eye," referring to realistic imagery that creates an optical illusion of three-dimensional depth.
- **Fresco-secco**: Painting on dry plaster, requiring a binding medium like egg or glue to attach pigments.
- **Mezzo-fresco**: A technique painting on nearly-dry plaster, dominant by the late 16th century.
- **Tempera**: A painting method using pigments bound in an albuminous medium like egg yolk.
- **Azulejo**: A form of Portuguese or Spanish painted, tin-glazed ceramic tilework used decoratively and functionally.
- **Frescography**: A digital manufacturing method for creating murals, addressing personalization and size restrictions.

# Casablanca (film)

## Overview
*Casablanca* is a 1942 American romantic drama film set during World War II, directed by Michael Curtiz and starring Humphrey Bogart and Ingrid Bergman. It tells the story of an American expatriate who must choose between his love for a woman and helping her husband, a Czechoslovak resistance leader, escape Nazi-occupied Europe. Though initially a modest success, the film's reputation grew immensely over time, and it is now considered one of the greatest and most iconic films in cinema history, celebrated for its screenplay, performances, and enduring themes of sacrifice and romance.

## Timeline
- **January 1942** — Warner Bros. purchases the rights to the unproduced play *Everybody Comes to Rick's* for $20,000. |
- **May 25, 1942** — Principal photography begins at Warner Bros. Studios in Burbank, California.
- **August 3, 1942** — Filming concludes.
- **November 26, 1942** — World premiere in New York City, timed to capitalize on the Allied invasion of North Africa.
- **January 23, 1943** — National release in the United States.
- **March 4, 1944** — Wins three Academy Awards: Best Picture, Best Director, and Best Adapted Screenplay.
- **1989** — Selected for preservation in the U.S. National Film Registry as "culturally, historically, or aesthetically significant."
- **January 1, 2038** — Scheduled to enter the public domain in the U.S.

## Body

### Production and Writing
The film is based on the unproduced play *Everybody Comes to Rick's* by Murray Burnett and Joan Alison, inspired by their 1938 trip to Europe. Warner Bros. story editor Irene Diamond convinced producer Hal B. Wallis to buy the rights. The screenplay was written by twins Julius and Philip Epstein and Howard Koch, though they worked separately. Key changes from the play included setting the story before Pearl Harbor to make Rick's motivation more believable and crafting the ending where Rick sacrifices his love for the greater good, a requirement under the Motion Picture Production Code. The film was shot almost entirely on studio sets, with principal photography taking place between May and August 1942.

### Plot and Themes
Set in December 1941, the film follows Rick Blaine, a cynical nightclub owner in Vichy-controlled Casablanca, who possesses "letters of transit" that allow escape from Nazi-occupied Europe. His world is upended by the arrival of his former lover, Ilsa Lund, and her husband, the renowned resistance leader Victor Laszlo. Rick must choose between his personal happiness and helping the couple escape to continue their fight against the Nazis. The film explores themes of sacrifice, idealism, and the moral complexities of wartime, with Rick's journey from isolationism to active resistance mirroring the United States' own path into World War II.

### Music and Legacy
The score, composed by Max Steiner, is built around the song "As Time Goes By" by Herman Hupfeld and the French national anthem "La Marseillaise." The "duel of the anthems" scene, where patrons drown out a German song with "La Marseillaise," became an iconic moment of patriotic defiance. Initially a solid box-office success, *Casablanca*'s reputation grew through television broadcasts and revival screenings, particularly at Harvard University. It is consistently ranked among the greatest films ever made, with its dialogue, characters, and theme song becoming deeply embedded in popular culture.

## Terms
*   **Letters of Transit:** Forged documents in the film that allow bearers to travel freely through German-occupied Europe; a plot device known as a MacGuffin.
*   **Vichy France:** The French government based in Vichy that collaborated with Nazi Germany after the 1940 armistice.
*   **Production Code:** The set of industry moral guidelines (also known as the Hays Code) that governed American film content from the 1930s to the 1960s.
*   **Leitmotif:** A recurring musical theme associated with a particular character, idea, or situation.
*   **Auteur Theory:** A critical framework that credits the director as the primary author or "auteur" of a film.
*   **MacGuffin:** A plot device, often an object or goal, that motivates the characters but is of little actual importance to the story itself.
*   **National Film Registry:** A U.S. program that selects films for preservation as they are "culturally, historically, or aesthetically significant."

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Authorship:** The film is often cited as a key exception to the auteur theory, as its success is attributed to the collaborative system of Hollywood studio filmmaking rather than a single directorial vision.
*   **Character Motivation:** Scholars debate the consistency and complexity of the characters' transformations, particularly Rick's shift from cynicism to heroism and Ilsa's emotional conflict.
*   **Political Allegory:** Some critics, like screenwriter Howard Koch, view the film as a political allegory for America's entry into WWII, with Rick representing the isolationist United States.
*   **Enduring Popularity:** The film's lasting appeal is often analyzed through its use of archetypes, its balance of romance and politics, and its embodiment of wartime sacrifice, which resonated with audiences then and continues to do so.

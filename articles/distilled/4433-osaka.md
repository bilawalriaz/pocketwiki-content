# Osaka

## Overview
Osaka is a major designated city in Japan's Kansai region, serving as the capital of Osaka Prefecture and the largest component of the Keihanshin metropolitan area. Historically Japan's economic hub, it was a vital port and briefly an imperial capital before becoming a dominant commercial center during the Edo period and a major industrial hub in the Meiji era. Today, it is a leading financial, cultural, and culinary center, known for its vibrant urban life and international events like Expo 2025.

## Timeline
- **c. 7,000 BCE** — Jōmon period settlements begin on the Uemachi Plateau peninsula.
- **300 BCE–250 CE** — Yayoi period sees growth of permanent rice-farming settlements.
- **3rd century CE** — Sumiyoshi-taisha shrine is inaugurated near the harbor.
- **Kofun period (300–538)** — Osaka develops into a major regional hub port (Naniwa-tsu).
- **645** — Emperor Kōtoku establishes Naniwa as Japan's capital.
- **1496** — Jōdo Shinshū Buddhists establish Ishiyama Hongan-ji headquarters.
- **1583** — Toyotomi Hideyoshi constructs Osaka Castle.
- **1697** — Rice Exchange established in Dōjima, creating a futures market.
- **1868** — Osaka opens to foreign trade; modern municipality established in 1889.
- **1925** — Osaka becomes Japan's largest and most populous city.
- **1945** — Major U.S. air raids destroy large parts of the city.
- **1970** — Osaka hosts Expo '70, the first World Expo in Asia.
- **2014** — Abeno Harukas, then Japan's tallest building, opens.
- **2025** — Expo 2025 is held on Yumeshima Island.

## Body

### Historical Development
The area was settled early due to its defensible peninsula and fresh water. By the Kofun period, the port of Naniwa-tsu became Japan's most important, facilitating trade and political consolidation. It served as the imperial capital briefly in the 7th and 8th centuries. After the 1583 construction of Osaka Castle, the city flourished in the Edo period as Japan's primary economic center, where merchants developed sophisticated financial systems like rice futures. Following the Meiji Restoration, Osaka transitioned from an economic capital to an industrial hub, growing rapidly and becoming Japan's largest city by 1925. It suffered extensive damage in WWII air raids but was rebuilt, hosting Expo '70 and Expo 2025.

### Economy and Finance
Osaka is a major financial center, home to the Osaka Exchange and headquarters of corporations like Panasonic and Sharp. Historically, it was the center of Japanese commerce, especially during the Edo period when it housed the world's first futures market. While many companies later moved to Tokyo, Osaka remains a key economic node within the Keihanshin metropolitan area, which has a GDP comparable to major global cities. The city's economy is driven by commerce, services, and manufacturing.

### Geography and Climate
The city is bordered by Osaka Bay and surrounded by satellite cities. It has expanded from its 1889 core to its current area of 222 km². The climate is humid subtropical with four distinct seasons: mild winters, hot and humid summers, and a rainy season (tsuyu) in early summer. A second rainy period occurs in September and early October due to tropical weather systems.

### Culture and Urban Life
Osaka is renowned for its food culture, with specialties like okonomiyaki and takoyaki, earning it the reputation of a "food capital." The city is divided into downtown (Kita) and uptown (Minami) areas, each with major shopping and entertainment districts like Dōtonbori and Shinsaibashi. It hosts traditional arts such as bunraku and kabuki, as well as modern entertainment. The city's dialect, Osaka-ben, is a distinctive sub-dialect of Kansai-ben.

## Terms
- **Keihanshin**: The metropolitan region encompassing Osaka, Kyoto, Kobe, and surrounding prefectures, Japan's second-largest urban area.
- **Kofun period**: An era in Japanese history (c. 300–538 CE) characterized by large keyhole-shaped burial mounds.
- **Naniwa**: The ancient name for the Osaka area, used when it served as the imperial capital.
- **Shōtengai**: Traditional Japanese shopping arcades, often covered and pedestrianized.
- **Tsuyu**: The rainy season in Japan, occurring from early June to mid-July.
- **Zainichi Koreans**: Ethnic Koreans who have lived in Japan for generations, with a significant community in Osaka's Tsuruhashi district.
- **Osaka-ben**: The local dialect of the Kansai region, featuring distinct vocabulary and grammar.
- **Integrated resort**: A large complex combining a casino, hotel, shopping, and entertainment facilities, as planned for MGM Osaka.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Osaka Metropolis Plan**: A proposal to reorganize the city's 24 wards into fewer, semi-autonomous special wards similar to Tokyo's. It was rejected in referendums in 2015 and 2020, with the latter failing by a narrow margin (50.6% against).
- **Nuclear Power Dependency**: In 2012, Osaka, as a major shareholder in Kansai Electric Power, led efforts to demand reforms and reduce nuclear power reliance following the Fukushima disaster, highlighting tensions between energy policy and public safety.
- **Cultural Stereotypes**: Historical literature from Edo (Tokyo) depicted Osakans as stingy and shrewd, a stereotype that persists in some forms today, contrasting with the city's self-image of culinary generosity and merchant pragmatism.

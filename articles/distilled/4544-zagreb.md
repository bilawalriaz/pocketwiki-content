# Zagreb

## Overview
Zagreb is the capital and largest city of Croatia, located in the north of the country along the Sava River. It is the country's political, economic, and cultural center, serving as the seat of the central government and hosting the headquarters of most major Croatian companies and institutions. The city's history spans from Roman times to its modern status as a global city and a key transport hub where Central Europe, the Mediterranean, and Southeast Europe meet.

## Timeline
- **1st–5th centuries AD** — Roman town of Andautonia exists near present-day Zagreb.
- **1094** — Hungarian King Ladislaus founds a diocese, marking the first recorded mention of the Zagreb area.
- **1242** — King Béla IV grants the Golden Bull to Gradec, giving it autonomy and exemption from county rule.
- **1557** — Zagreb becomes the Croatian capital.
- **1621** — Zagreb becomes the seat of the Ban of Croatia.
- **1669** — Jesuits found an academy, the forerunner of the University of Zagreb.
- **1850** — Gradec and Kaptol are united into a single settlement; Janko Kamauf becomes the first mayor.
- **1862** — First railway line connecting Zagreb with Zidani Most and Sisak opens.
- **1941–1945** — Zagreb is the capital of the Independent State of Croatia during World War II.
- **1991** — After Croatian independence, Zagreb is proclaimed the capital of the Republic of Croatia.
- **2020** — A 5.5 magnitude earthquake damages historic buildings, including the cathedral.

## Body

### Early History and Unification
The oldest known settlement near Zagreb is the Roman town of Andautonia (1st–5th centuries AD). The name "Zagreb" first appears in 1094 with the founding of a diocese. The city developed as two separate centers: the clerical Kaptol and the fortified merchant town of Gradec. Conflicts between them lasted for centuries until they were united in 1850 under the first mayor, Janko Kamauf. The Golden Bull of 1242 granted Gradec significant autonomy, a key step in its development.

### Political and Administrative Status
Zagreb became the Croatian capital in 1557 and the seat of the Ban of Croatia in 1621. After Croatian independence in 1991, it was confirmed as the national capital. The city holds a special administrative status as a consolidated city-county, separate from the surrounding Zagreb County. It is divided into 17 city districts and is the seat of the national government, parliament, and constitutional court.

### Economy and Transport
Zagreb is Croatia's leading economic center, with a diverse economy including high-tech industries and services. It is the country's primary transport hub, where major road, rail, and air networks converge. The city is home to the Zagreb Stock Exchange and the headquarters of most large Croatian companies. The Zagreb Franjo Tuđman Airport is the busiest in Croatia.

### Culture and Tourism
Zagreb is a major tourist destination, known for its historic Upper Town (Gornji grad), museums, and events like the award-winning Christmas market. The city has a rich cultural scene with numerous theaters, galleries, and festivals. Notable institutions include the Archaeological Museum, the Museum of Contemporary Art, and the Croatian National Theatre. The city is also known for souvenirs like the cravat (tie) and the ball-point pen.

### Geography and Climate
The city is located at the southern slopes of Medvednica mountain, with a diverse geography ranging from low-lying areas along the Sava to foothill districts. Zagreb has an oceanic climate (Köppen: Cfb) bordering a humid continental climate, with four distinct seasons. It is one of Europe's wettest capitals, with an average of 840 mm of precipitation per year. The city is in a seismically active area and experiences frequent earthquakes.

## Terms
- **Golden Bull of 1242**: A charter granted by King Béla IV that gave the town of Gradec autonomy and its own judicial system.
- **Kaptol**: The historical clerical settlement of Zagreb, centered around the cathedral.
- **Gradec**: The historical fortified merchant settlement of Zagreb, now the Upper Town (Gornji grad).
- **Ban of Croatia**: The historical viceroy of the Kingdom of Croatia; Zagreb became the seat of the Ban in 1621.
- **Sabor**: The Croatian Parliament.
- **Consolidated city-county**: An administrative division where city and county functions are merged; Zagreb has this special status.
- **Oceanic climate (Cfb)**: A climate type with warm summers and cool winters, and precipitation year-round.
- **Zagreb Holding**: The city-owned enterprise that manages many public services and utilities.
- **Zagreb Electric Tram**: The public transit authority operating the city's tram and bus network.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The etymology of the name "Zagreb" is unclear and debated. Theories include derivation from a Proto-Slavic word for "hill" or "embankment," a metathesis of a word for "riverbank," or a folk etymology from a verb meaning "to scoop."
- The city's seismic risk is a significant concern. While it experiences around 400 earthquakes a year, most are imperceptible, but officials warn that a strong earthquake could cause thousands of casualties.

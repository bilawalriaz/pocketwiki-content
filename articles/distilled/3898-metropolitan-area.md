# Metropolitan area

## Overview
A metropolitan area is a region centered on a densely populated urban core and its surrounding territories, which are economically and socially integrated through shared industries, transport networks, and commuting patterns. These areas are key economic and political regions, often comprising multiple jurisdictions and municipalities. Their definitions vary significantly by country and agency, making direct comparisons challenging.

## Timeline
- **1950** — Basic concept of metropolitan areas adopted.
- **2006** — German professors introduce the concept of a "regiopolis" for smaller urban centers.
- **2018** — Urban Metric System (UMS) first applied to Canadian cases.
- **2019** — China defines a metropolitan area as a 1-hour commute zone dominated by a supercity.
- **2020** — International agencies agree on a common "Functional Urban Area" definition.
- **2020** — France replaces its metropolitan statistical area with the "functional area" (AAV).
- **2023** — The Dubai-Sharjah-Ajman metropolitan area population reaches ~5.9 million.

## Body

### Definition and Concept
A metropolitan area combines an urban agglomeration with contiguous built-up areas bound to the center by employment and commerce, often forming a "commuter belt." The core concept has remained stable since 1950, but geographic distributions have changed. There is no single global standard; parameters are inconsistent, and population figures can vary by millions. A "polycentric" metropolitan area contains multiple urban agglomerations not connected by continuous development.

### International Frameworks and Variations
In 2020, several UN and international bodies agreed on a common definition: the "Functional Urban Area" (FUA), defined as a city and its commuting zone where at least 15% of employed residents work in the city. This is used by Eurostat and the OECD. Countries apply distinct national definitions:
*   **Asia:** China defines a metropolitan area as a 1-hour commute zone. India defines a metropolitan city as one with over one million people. Indonesia prioritizes planning for key metropolitan areas like Jakarta.
*   **Europe:** Germany defines eleven major metro areas. Italy redefined 14 provinces as "metropolitan cities" in 2001. The UK uses "travel to work areas."
*   **North America:** Canada uses "Census Metropolitan Areas" based on commuting. The U.S. defines "Metropolitan Statistical Areas" (MSAs) around urban centers of at least 50,000 people. Mexico defines "zonas metropolitanas" based on municipal integration.
*   **Oceania & South America:** Australia uses "Greater Capital City Statistical Areas" based on labor markets. Brazil's IBGE defines "Immediate Geographic Areas" for planning.

### Measurement Challenges
Urban data are based on arbitrary, non-comparable definitions. To address this, the Urban Metric System (UMS) was conceived. It uses vector field calculations based on the attractive and repulsive forces of inhabitants and jobs to compute urban area limits consistently. UMS distinguishes different urban area types based on a parameter (β), though most current data still rely on disparate national definitions.

## Terms
*   **Metropolitan Area:** A region with a central urban core and surrounding territories integrated by economic ties and commuting.
*   **Urban Agglomeration:** The contiguous built-up area of a city, which may extend beyond its administrative boundaries.
*   **Commuter Belt:** The zone surrounding a city from which residents regularly travel to work in the urban core.
*   **Functional Urban Area (FUA):** A harmonized definition (city + commuting zone) used by Eurostat and OECD for international comparison.
*   **Metropolitan Statistical Area (MSA):** A U.S. geographic entity defined by the Office of Management and Budget, consisting of a core urban area and integrated counties.
*   **Census Metropolitan Area (CMA):** A Canadian statistical area comprising a core municipality and adjacent municipalities with high commuting integration.
*   **Polycentric Metropolitan Area:** A metropolitan region with multiple urban centers not connected by continuous development.
*   **Urban Metric System (UMS):** A proposed mathematical framework to define urban areas consistently using population and job distribution data.

## Debates and Open Questions
The primary debate centers on the lack of a consistent global definition, which complicates comparative statistics. While the FUA offers a standardized framework, many countries retain unique national definitions for administrative or planning purposes. The fluidity of terms like "metropolitan statistical area" versus colloquial usage ("metro area") adds to the ambiguity. The adoption and effectiveness of new measurement systems like UMS to resolve these disparities is an ongoing development.

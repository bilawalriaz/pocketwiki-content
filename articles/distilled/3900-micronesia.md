# Micronesia

## Overview
Micronesia is a subregion of Oceania comprising approximately 2,000 small islands in the Northwestern Pacific Ocean. It is defined more by geography and colonial history than by a single cultural or ethnic unity, with its modern political landscape shaped by centuries of Austronesian migration, European colonization, and 20th-century geopolitical shifts.

## Timeline
- **c. 3000–1500 BCE** — Austronesian expansion from pre-Han Formosa begins.
- **c. 1500 BCE** — Austronesian voyagers from the Philippines settle the Northern Mariana Islands.
- **1521** — Spanish expedition under Ferdinand Magellan makes first European contact in the Marianas.
- **1832** — Louis Domeny de Rienzi uses the term "Micronesia" (Jules Dumont d'Urville popularizes it in 1832).
- **1888** — Germany annexes Nauru.
- **1899** — Spain sells the Caroline, Mariana, and Marshall Islands to Germany after the Spanish-American War.
- **1920** — After WWI, former German territories become League of Nations mandates; Japan receives the South Seas Mandate.
- **1947** — Former Japanese mandates become the UN Trust Territory of the Pacific Islands, administered by the U.S.
- **1968** — Nauru becomes independent.
- **1976** — The Northern Mariana Islands enter a covenant of political union with the United States.
- **1979** — The Federated States of Micronesia becomes self-governing.
- **1986** — The Republic of the Marshall Islands and the Federated States of Micronesia gain independence in free association with the U.S.

## Body

### Geography and Geology
Micronesia has a tropical marine climate and a total land area of about 2,700 km². The region includes four main archipelagos: the Caroline Islands (divided between Palau and the Federated States of Micronesia), the Gilbert Islands (part of Kiribati), the Mariana Islands (a U.S. territory and commonwealth), and the Marshall Islands. The separate nation of Nauru is also included. Most islands are coral atolls formed from reefs growing on sunken volcanoes, though exceptions like Pohnpei retain a central volcano. The Mariana Islands are a volcanic arc at the world's most active convergent plate boundary.

### Human Settlement and Prehistory
The first inhabitants were Austronesian peoples who migrated from Southeast Asia via the Philippines. The Northern Mariana Islands were settled around 1500 BCE. Further migrations from Sulawesi settled Palau and Yap by around 1000 BCE. Around 800 CE, a second wave from Southeast Asia began the Latte period in the Marianas, reintroducing rice cultivation. Major megalithic construction occurred later, including Nan Madol on Pohnpei (c. 1180 CE) and Leluh on Kosrae (c. 1200 CE).

### European Contact and Colonization
The Spanish were the first Europeans to arrive (1521). In the early 17th century, Spain colonized the Marianas, Carolines, and Guam. After losing the Spanish-American War, Spain sold its remaining Micronesian islands to Germany in 1899. Germany lost its colonies after World War I; Japan received a mandate over most of Micronesia, while Nauru became an Australian mandate. After World War II, the U.S. administered the former Japanese territories as a UN Trust Territory. Most of Micronesia gained independence in the 1970s-1980s, though Guam, the Northern Mariana Islands, and Wake Island remain U.S. territories.

### Politics and Economy
Today, Micronesia consists of six sovereign nations and several U.S. territories. Political control varies, with the sovereignty of Wake Island contested between the U.S. and the Marshall Islands. Economies are heavily dependent on fishing rights sales, government grants (primarily from the U.S.), and remittances from citizens working abroad. Tourism, focused on diving, is a growing but limited sector.

### Demographics and Culture
The people are ethnically diverse but share a Micronesian cultural heritage. Major indigenous groups include the Chamorro, Carolinian, Chuukese, and Nauruan. The languages belong to the Austronesian family, with Micronesian languages being the largest group. Christianity is the predominant religion (over 90%). Traditional arts include wood-carving, weaving, and megalithic architecture. The region's mythology varies by island.

## Terms
- **Austronesian expansion**: The prehistoric seaborne migration of Austronesian peoples from Taiwan across the Indo-Pacific.
- **Atoll**: A ring-shaped coral reef, island, or series of islets that surrounds a lagoon, formed on the rim of a sunken volcano.
- **Latte period**: A cultural phase in the Mariana Islands (c. 800 CE onward) marked by new settlers, stone pillar structures (haligi), and rice cultivation.
- **Nan Madol**: A megalithic complex on Pohnpei, built from basalt lava logs beginning around 1180 CE.
- **South Seas Mandate**: The League of Nations mandate administered by Japan over former German territories in Micronesia after World War I.
- **Trust Territory of the Pacific Islands**: The UN trusteeship administered by the United States after World War II, covering most of Micronesia.
- **Free association**: A status of sovereignty where an independent nation conducts its own affairs but relies on a larger power (like the U.S.) for defense and funding.

## Debates and Open questions
- The term "Micronesia" is well-established but does not correspond to a deep geomorphological, archaeological, linguistic, or cultural unity; the region is a "disparate ensemble."
- The origin of the Nauruan people is not definitively determined, possibly resulting from later Polynesian or Melanesian migration.
- The sovereignty of Wake Island is contested by both the United States and the Republic of the Marshall Islands.

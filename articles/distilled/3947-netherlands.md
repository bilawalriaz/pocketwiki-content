# Netherlands

## Overview
The Netherlands is a Northwestern European country known for its low-lying, flat topography, with about 26% of its land below sea level. It is a parliamentary constitutional monarchy and a highly developed, trade-oriented economy with a global reputation for social tolerance, innovative water management, and a significant historical role in global commerce and culture.

- **2001** — First country to legalize same-sex marriage.

## Body

### Etymology and Identity
The name "Netherlands" means "lower countries," referencing its geography. "Holland" informally refers to the whole country but officially denotes only the North and South Holland provinces. The demonym "Dutch" derives from a Proto-Germanic word meaning "of the people."

### Early History and State Formation
Human traces date back about 250,000 years. The area saw Germanic migrations and was partially incorporated into the Roman Empire. After Roman rule collapsed, the Franks expanded, and by the 5th century, Clovis I united the southern territories. The region later became part of the Frankish Carolingian Empire, which fragmented in the 9th century. Powerful local nobles in counties like Holland and Flanders gained autonomy within the Holy Roman Empire. The Burgundian and later Habsburg rulers united most of the Low Countries. Religious and political conflicts led to the Eighty Years' War (1568–1648) and the eventual independence of the northern provinces.

### The Dutch Republic and Golden Age
The independent Dutch Republic, founded in 1581, became a major economic and naval power in the 17th century. Its trading companies, the Dutch East and West India Companies, established a global colonial network. Amsterdam became Europe's wealthiest city and home to the world's first stock exchange. This era saw a flourishing of art, science, and finance, though the republic was often at war with France and England. By the 18th century, the republic declined due to economic competition and internal strife.

### Modern History
In 1795, the Batavian Republic was established with French backing. After Napoleon's defeat, the Congress of Vienna created the United Kingdom of the Netherlands in 1815. The southern provinces seceded as Belgium in 1830. The Netherlands remained neutral in World War I but was invaded and occupied by Nazi Germany in World War II, suffering significant losses and a devastating famine. After the war, it decolonized its East Indies and other territories. The country became a founding member of the EU, NATO, and other international organizations.

### Geography and Water Management
The Netherlands is a river delta formed by the Rhine, Meuse, and Scheldt. Its flat landscape and low elevation have necessitated extensive water management. Since the 14th century, land reclamation has created polders. The country is protected by a complex system of dikes, canals, and pumping stations, managed by regional water boards. Major projects include the Zuiderzee Works and the Delta Works, built after the 1953 flood disaster. The country is highly vulnerable to climate change and sea-level rise.

### Government and Politics
The Netherlands is a parliamentary constitutional monarchy with a unitary structure. The monarch is the head of state, while executive power lies with the cabinet, led by a prime minister. The bicameral parliament, the States General, consists of the House of Representatives and the Senate. The political system is characterized by coalition governments and a tradition of pillarisation (historical separation of society by religion and ideology). It has a strong record on social tolerance and human rights.

### Economy
The Dutch economy is highly developed, open, and export-oriented, with a focus on services, industry, and agriculture. It is the world's second-largest exporter of agricultural products by value. Key sectors include trade, logistics (with the Port of Rotterdam as Europe's largest), finance, and high-tech manufacturing (e.g., ASML, Philips). The country has a mixed-market economy and is a major hub for international business and finance.

### Demographics and Society
The Netherlands has a population of over 18 million, making it one of Europe's most densely populated countries. The majority are ethnically Dutch, with significant minority communities from former colonies and recent migration. Dutch is the official language, with West Frisian as a secondary official language in Friesland. The society is highly secular, with a declining Christian majority and growing non-religious population. It is known for its liberal social policies, including legalized prostitution, euthanasia, and drug tolerance.

## Terms
- **Polder:** A low-lying tract of land enclosed by dikes that forms a distinct hydrological entity, often reclaimed from the sea or a river.
- **Pillarisation (Verzuiling):** The historical segregation of Dutch society into distinct groups based on religion (Protestant, Catholic) and political ideology, each with their own institutions.
- **Water Board (Waterschap):** A regional democratic body responsible for water management, including the maintenance of dikes, canals, and pumping stations.
- **Randstad:** A megalopolis in the western Netherlands encompassing the four largest cities: Amsterdam, Rotterdam, The Hague, and Utrecht.
- **Delta Works:** A massive series of construction projects in the southwest Netherlands to protect a large area of land around the Rhine-Meuse-Scheldt delta from the sea.
- **Zuiderzee Works:** A hydraulic engineering project begun in 1920 that included the construction of the Afsluitdijk and the creation of several large polders from the former Zuiderzee.
- **Dutch Disease:** An economic phenomenon where the discovery of large natural resource revenues (like natural gas) leads to a decline in other sectors, such as manufacturing, due to currency appreciation.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The historical extent of the decline of the Dutch Republic after the Golden Age is debated among historians, with some arguing the decline was overstated, especially before the mid-18th century.
- The optimal balance between the Netherlands' intensive, export-oriented agricultural sector and the need to reduce nitrogen pollution and meet environmental goals is a major ongoing political and social conflict.
- The country's role as a "conduit country" for international tax avoidance and its status as a significant tax haven remain subjects of international scrutiny and domestic debate.

# Building

## Overview

A building is an enclosed, permanent structure with a roof and walls, serving fundamental human needs for shelter, security, living space, privacy, and storage. Buildings separate the human habitat—of comfort and safety—from the potentially harsh outside world, and they have functioned as canvases for artistic expression while increasingly incorporating sustainable design practices.

## Timeline

- **c. 18,000 BC** — Clear evidence of homebuilding appears
- **Neolithic period** — Buildings become common
- **Modern era** — Interest in sustainable, green building design emerges

## Body

### Definition and Classification

A building is defined as a structure with a roof and walls standing more or less permanently in one place. While the broadest interpretation could include fences or walls, the term typically excludes purely artistic or scientific treatment distinctions. Structural height is measured to the highest architectural detail from street level, with spires and masts sometimes included depending on classification. The boundary between low-rise (generally three stories or fewer) and high-rise buildings remains debated.

### Types of Buildings

Residential buildings range from single-family houses to multi-unit apartment blocks, condominiums, and specialized housing like nursing homes, prisons, and group accommodations. Terms reflect function (holiday cottage, timeshare), size (cottage, great house), value (shack, mansion), construction method (log home, mobile home), style (castle, Victorian), and geography (earth shelter, stilt house). Commercial buildings house businesses without residential occupancy, including stores and restaurants. Industrial buildings support heavy industry like manufacturing, warehouses, and factories. Agricultural buildings are farm outbuildings such as barns. Mixed-use buildings combine multiple functions, most commonly residential and commercial. Complexes group interrelated buildings, such as housing, educational, or hospital complexes.

### Creation and Professional Involvement

Building design and construction involve a collective effort of professionals including real estate developers, financial institutions, planning authorities, surveyors, construction managers, architects, engineers (civil, structural, mechanical, HVAC, plumbing), landscape architects, interior designers, contractors, and facility managers. Projects are subject to zoning ordinances, building codes, fire codes, and life safety regulations. For life safety purposes, vehicles like trailers, ships, and aircraft may be treated as buildings.

### Environmental and Infrastructure Considerations

Buildings require internal infrastructure for heating/cooling, power, telecommunications, water, and wastewater systems, which can be intricate and space-intensive, especially in commercial settings. Conveying systems include elevators, escalators, moving sidewalks, skyways, and underground connections. Buildings face damage risks from storms, explosions, subsidence, landslides, fire, flooding, poor maintenance, and improper alterations.

## Terms

- **Building**: A structure with a roof and walls standing permanently in one place.
- **Green building**: A building designed with sustainable planning and building practices.
- **HVAC**: Heating, Ventilation, and Air Conditioning systems.
- **Low-rise**: Generally a building of three stories or fewer.
- **Mixed-use**: A building incorporating multiple different uses, commonly commercial and residential.
- **Structural height**: The height to the highest architectural detail from street level.
- **Zoning ordinance**: A regulation governing land use in specific areas.

## Debates and Open Questions

The distinction between low-rise and high-rise buildings is a matter of debate, with the general threshold being three stories or fewer for low-rise classification. The classification of spires and masts in structural height measurements varies depending on whether they are used as antennas. The broadest interpretation of "building" could include fences or walls, though this usage is uncommon.

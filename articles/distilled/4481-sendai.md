# Sendai

## Overview
Sendai is the capital of Miyagi Prefecture and the largest city in Japan's Tōhoku region. Founded in 1600 by the daimyō Date Masamune, it is known as the "City of Trees" for its zelkova-lined streets. The city is a major economic, educational, and transportation hub, which was severely impacted by the 2011 Tōhoku earthquake and tsunami.

## Body

### History and Founding
The Sendai area was inhabited as early as 20,000 years ago, but its history as a city begins in 1600. Daimyō Date Masamune relocated his capital to Sendai from Iwadeyama, as it was centrally located in his territories and on the major road to Edo (Tokugawa Ieyasu granted permission after the Battle of Sekigahara). Masamune ordered the construction of Sendai Castle and the surrounding castle town in 1601. The modern city's grid plan is based on his original layout. The city's name was changed from 千代 ("eternity") to 仙台, a name derived from a Chinese poem.

### Modern Development and Disaster
The first railway to Tokyo opened in 1887, integrating Sendai with the national network. Tohoku Imperial University was founded in 1907 and became Japan's first university to admit female students in 1913. The city grew through annexations and became a designated city in 1989. It was heavily bombed on 10 July 1945, destroying much of its historic center. Post-war reconstruction established it as a vital Tōhoku transportation hub. Sendai has a history of major earthquakes, including the 1978 Miyagi earthquake, which catalyzed Japan's earthquake resistance standards. The 2011 Tōhoku earthquake (magnitude 9.0) and subsequent tsunami caused catastrophic damage to coastal areas, including Sendai Airport and port.

### Geography and Climate
Sendai stretches from the Pacific Ocean to the Ōu Mountains, creating diverse geography: eastern plains, a hilly center, and western mountains. Its city core is uniquely built on a terrace 40–60 meters above sea level. The Hirose River, a city symbol, flows through it. The climate is humid subtropical (Köppen Cfa) with warm, wet summers and cool, dry winters. It experiences fewer extreme temperature days than other Japanese capitals.

### Economy and Education
Sendai is the economic center of the Tōhoku region, with a GDP of approximately US$50 billion (2015). Its economy relies heavily on retail and services. It is often called a "branch-office economy" as few major companies are headquartered there. Efforts focus on fostering high-tech ventures from Tohoku University. The city is also called an "Academic City" (学都) for its high concentration of universities, including Tohoku University.

### Culture and Festivals
Sendai's most famous festival is the Tanabata Festival, the largest in Japan, held in summer. In winter, the Pageant of Starlight illuminates the city's zelkova trees. The city is the origin of foods like gyūtan (grilled beef tongue) and zundamochi (mochi with edamame paste). Traditional crafts include Sendai Hira silk and Tsutsumiyaki pottery. Sites of interest include the ruins of Sendai Castle, the Zuihōden tomb of Date Masamune, and the Sendai Mediatheque, a landmark of modern architecture.

## Terms
- **Daimyō**: A powerful feudal lord in pre-modern Japan.
- **Shinkansen**: Japan's high-speed rail network, known as the bullet train.
- **Designated city**: A Japanese city with a population over 500,000 that has been granted special administrative powers similar to a prefecture.
- **Köppen Cfa**: A climate classification for humid subtropical climates with hot, humid summers and cool, dry winters.
- **Gyūtan**: A Sendai specialty of grilled beef tongue.
- **Zundamochi**: A local sweet made of mochi balls with a paste of edamame (green soybeans).
- **Tanabata**: A Japanese star festival celebrated in July.
- **Mikoshi**: A portable Shinto shrine carried during festivals.
- **Kamaboko**: A Japanese fish cake; *sasakamaboko* is shaped like a bamboo leaf.
- **Imoni**: A communal potato stew picnic held by rivers in autumn.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The origin of *robatayaki* (Japanese-style barbecue) is debated; while it originated in Sendai, it was later popularized and developed in Kushiro, Hokkaido, leading many to associate it with that city.
- The 2005 Miyagi earthquake (magnitude 7.2) was initially studied to see if it was the long-expected "Miyagi Oki earthquake." The Headquarters for Earthquake Research Promotion concluded it was not, as its magnitude was too small and its source area did not cover the entire expected region.

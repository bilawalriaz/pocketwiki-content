# Steve Biko

## Overview
Bantu Stephen Biko (1946–1977) was a South African anti-apartheid activist and the ideological founder of the Black Consciousness Movement (BCM). His philosophy focused on psychological empowerment to combat the internalized inferiority of black people under apartheid. His death in police custody in 1977 made him a global symbol of the brutality of the apartheid system and a martyr for the anti-apartheid cause.

## Timeline
- **18 Dec 1946** — Bantu Stephen Biko is born in Tarkastad, Cape Province.
- **1966** — Begins studying medicine at the University of Natal; joins NUSAS.
- **1968** — Co-founds the South African Students' Organisation (SASO) as an independent black student group.
- **1971** — Develops the ideology of "Black Consciousness" as SASO's official doctrine.
- **1972** — Involved in founding the Black People's Convention (BPC) to promote Black Consciousness.
- **Mar 1973** — Placed under a banning order by the government, restricting his movements and activities.
- **17 Aug 1977** — Arrested at a police roadblock for violating his banning order.
- **12 Sep 1977** — Dies from brain injuries in a Pretoria prison cell after being beaten during interrogation.
- **25 Sep 1977** — Attended by ~20,000 people at his funeral in King William's Town.
- **Nov 1977** — Government inquest begins; later accepts police account and declines to prosecute.
- **Dec 1998** — Truth and Reconciliation Commission denies amnesty to five police officers involved in his death.

## Body

### Early Life and Student Activism
Born into a poor Xhosa family in Ginsberg township, Biko's early awareness of apartheid was shaped by his mother's difficult working conditions. He excelled academically but was expelled from the prestigious Lovedale school after being arrested (and later cleared) in connection with his brother's alleged links to the banned Pan Africanist Congress (PAC). At university, he joined the multi-racial National Union of South African Students (NUSAS) but grew frustrated by its white-dominated leadership and what he saw as their paternalism. A pivotal moment came in 1967 when black delegates, including Biko, walked out of a NUSAS conference after being denied proper accommodation, convincing him that black people needed to organize independently.

### The Black Consciousness Movement
In 1968, Biko co-founded the South African Students' Organisation (SASO), which became the vehicle for Black Consciousness. This ideology, influenced by thinkers like Frantz Fanon, was defined as "an attitude of mind" to combat the psychological inferiority imposed by apartheid. Biko popularized the slogan "black is beautiful" to foster pride and dignity. He redefined "black" to include all those oppressed by apartheid—Bantu-speaking Africans, Coloureds, and Indians. The movement focused on community development through Black Community Programmes (BCPs) in healthcare and education, aiming for psychological and economic empowerment. In 1972, the Black People's Convention (BPC) was formed to spread these ideas to the wider population.

### Banning, Activism, and Death
The government viewed Black Consciousness as a threat and placed Biko under a banning order in 1973, severely restricting his movements and speech. Despite this, he continued organizing community projects and worked to unify anti-apartheid groups like the ANC and PAC. He developed a friendship with white liberal journalist Donald Woods, who helped publicize his views. In August 1977, Biko was arrested for breaking his banning order. He was held in detention, where he was severely beaten by security police, suffering fatal brain injuries. He died alone in a cell on 12 September 1977. An initial inquest exonerated the police, but a later Truth and Reconciliation Commission hearing denied them amnesty for their conflicting accounts.

### Ideology and Legacy
Biko's core belief was that the "most potent weapon in the hands of the oppressor is the mind of the oppressed." He argued that white liberals, while well-intentioned, could not lead the black liberation struggle. He envisioned a future non-racial, socialist South Africa with a mixed economy and universal suffrage. His death made him an international icon of resistance. The BCM's influence waned after his death as the ANC became the dominant anti-apartheid force. His legacy is commemorated globally through statues, institutions, and art, though political parties continue to contest ownership of his ideas.

## Terms
*   **Black Consciousness Movement (BCM):** A grassroots ideological movement led by Biko that focused on psychological empowerment and fostering pride and self-reliance among black South Africans.
*   **Apartheid:** The system of institutionalized racial segregation and white-minority rule in South Africa that existed from 1948 until the early 1990s.
*   **Banning Order:** A legal tool used by the apartheid government to restrict an individual's movements, associations, and ability to speak publicly or be quoted.
*   **NUSAS (National Union of South African Students):** A multi-racial student union that Biko initially joined but later rejected for being white-dominated.
*   **SASO (South African Students' Organisation):** An independent black student union founded by Biko in 1968 to advance Black Consciousness.
*   **Black People's Convention (BPC):** A political organization founded in 1972 to promote Black Consciousness ideas among the broader black population.
*   **Black Community Programmes (BCPs):** Community development projects in healthcare, education, and economic self-reliance established by the BCM.
*   **Paternalism:** The policy or attitude of white liberals that Biko criticized for treating black people as incapable of leading their own liberation.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Criticism from Liberals and Nationalists:** During his life, Biko faced criticism from white liberals who saw Black Consciousness as reverse racism, and from African racial nationalists who opposed his inclusive front with Coloureds and Indians.
*   **Allegations of Sexism:** Biko and the BCM have been criticized by activists, including Mamphela Ramphele, for sidelining women and for Biko's personal womanizing.
*   **Contested Legacy:** After apartheid, various political parties, notably the ANC and AZAPO, have claimed Biko's legacy, leading to disputes over his political alignment and the ownership of his ideas.
*   **Circumstances of Death:** While the TRC denied amnesty, the exact sequence of events during Biko's interrogation and the precise motivations of his killers remain subjects of historical scrutiny.

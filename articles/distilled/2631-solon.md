# Solon

## Overview
Solon (c. 630–c. 560 BC) was an archaic Athenian statesman, lawmaker, and poet credited with laying the foundations for Athenian democracy. He is celebrated for his constitutional and economic reforms, which aimed to resolve severe social and political crises in Athens, and is counted among the Seven Sages of Greece. His work is a pivotal, though debated, transition from aristocratic rule toward a more inclusive political system.

## Timeline
- **c. 630 BC** — Born in Athens to a noble family.
- **c. 595 BC** — Led Athenian forces to victory over Megara for control of Salamis.
- **594 BC** — Appointed archon; granted extraordinary powers to reform the state.
- **594 BC** — Enacted major reforms, including the *seisachtheia* (debt relief).
- **After reforms** — Traveled abroad for ten years (to Egypt, Cyprus, Lydia) to prevent repeal of his laws.
- **c. 560 BC** — Died in Cyprus; ashes scattered on Salamis.

## Body

### Biography and Historical Context
Solon was born into a distinguished Athenian family. He gained early fame by leading Athens to recover the island of Salamis from Megara. In 594 BC, he was made archon and given autocratic powers to mediate a severe social crisis between the wealthy nobility and the indebted poor. After implementing his reforms, he traveled abroad for a decade. Upon his return, he opposed the rise of the tyrant Pisistratus, a relative, but failed to prevent it. He died in Cyprus around age 70.

Our knowledge of Solon is limited. His works survive only in fragments, often with later interpolations, and ancient sources like Herodotus and Plutarch wrote centuries after his death. Fourth-century orators often attributed later laws to him.

### The Social Crisis and Rivalries
Athens faced a profound social and political crisis. Ancient sources describe a struggle between the nobles and the common people, where the poor were often enslaved for debt. Modern historians debate the nature of this conflict, identifying three main interpretations: an economic/ideological struggle between rich and poor; a regional rivalry between factions from the hills, plains, and coast; and a conflict between powerful aristocratic clans (*genos*). Solon's own poetry presents him as a mediator between two unruly factions.

### Constitutional Reforms
Solon's reforms restructured Athenian society and government. He repealed most of Draco's harsh laws, except those on homicide. He created a new constitution based on wealth rather than birth, dividing citizens into four classes: the *Pentakosiomedimnoi* (500+ bushels), *Hippeis* (300+), *Zeugitai* (200+), and *Thetes* (below 200). Political office was restricted to the top classes, but he opened the assembly (*Ekklesia*) to all citizens and established a popular court (*Heliaia*), allowing citizens to hold officials accountable.

### Economic and Moral Reforms
Solon's economic reforms aimed to stimulate commerce and relieve the poor. His most famous measure was the *seisachtheia* ("shaking off of burdens"), which annulled debts, abolished debt slavery, and freed enslaved Athenians. He encouraged trade by inviting foreign artisans, promoted olive cultivation for export, and revised weights and measures. His moral reforms targeted social excess, abolishing extravagant dowries and legislating against abuses in inheritance. He also allegedly regulated sexual mores, including pederastic relationships, though accounts of these laws are considered unreliable.

### Poetry and Legacy
Solon wrote poetry to explain and defend his reforms, portraying himself as a champion of moderation. His verses are historically significant as a personal record but are not considered great literature. He was listed among the Seven Sages of Greece. His legacy is complex: he is seen as both the "first people's champion" and a moderate aristocrat whose reforms were a crucial step toward democracy, though their full intent is still debated.

## Terms
- **Archon**: The chief magistrate of ancient Athens.
- **Areopagus**: A council of ex-archons that held significant power in Athens before Solon's reforms.
- **Seisachtheia**: Solon's celebrated debt relief reform, meaning "shaking off of burdens."
- **Ekklesia**: The assembly of all Athenian citizens.
- **Heliaia**: A popular court or jury formed from the citizen body.
- **Pentakosiomedimnoi**: The highest property class in Solon's constitution, eligible for the highest offices.
- **Thetes**: The lowest property class in Solon's constitution, previously excluded from political office.
- **Horos**: A boundary marker indicating a farmer was in debt or under obligation.
- **Hektemoroi**: Farmers who paid or kept a sixth of a farm's yield, often under debt bondage.
- **Genos**: An extended family or clan, a key unit of social and political organization.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Nature of the Crisis**: Was the pre-Solonian conflict primarily economic (rich vs. poor), regional (geographic factions), or based on aristocratic clan rivalries?
- **Intent of Reforms**: Were Solon's reforms a radical move toward democracy, or did they merely give a plutocratic (wealth-based) flavor to an aristocratic system?
- **Interpretation of the *Seisachtheia***: Was it a simple cancellation of debts, the abolition of a feudal-like relationship, or something else?
- **Reliability of Sources**: How much of the account of Solon's life and laws, especially regarding his travels, personal life, and sexual legislation, is historical fact versus later myth or fabrication?

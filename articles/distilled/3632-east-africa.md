# East Africa

## Overview
East Africa is a diverse region at the eastern edge of the African continent, encompassing the Horn of Africa, the African Great Lakes, and the Indian Ocean coast. It is geographically varied, historically significant as a potential cradle of humanity, and culturally shaped by migrations and trade. Today, it is a populous and economically growing region, though it faces challenges from climate variability and past conflicts.

## Timeline
- **c. 300,000 years ago** — Evidence of early modern human behavior found at Olorgesailie, Kenya.
- **c. 200,000 years ago** — Anatomically modern humans (*Homo sapiens*) evolve in the Horn of Africa.
- **c. 7,000 BCE** — Agriculture begins in the Ethiopian Highlands.
- **2500–3000 years ago** — Bantu expansion introduces agriculture to the African Great Lakes region.
- **100–940 CE** — Kingdom of Aksum is a major trading empire in Eritrea and Ethiopia.
- **1498** — Portuguese explorer Vasco da Gama visits Mombasa.
- **1505** — Portuguese conquer Kilwa, beginning their coastal rule.
- **1839** — Omani Arabs move their capital to Zanzibar, consolidating power.
- **1880s–1900s** — European "Scramble for Africa" leads to colonial rule by Britain, Germany, France, Italy, and Portugal.
- **1961–1964** — Tanganyika and Zanzibar gain independence and unite to form Tanzania.
- **1994** — Rwandan genocide against the Tutsi occurs.
- **2011** — South Sudan becomes an independent nation.

## Body

### Definitions and Geography
East Africa's definition varies. In a narrow sense, it refers to Kenya, Tanzania, and Uganda. The broader UN scheme includes 18 states and 4 territories, incorporating the Horn of Africa (Djibouti, Eritrea, Ethiopia, Somalia) and island nations like the Comoros and Seychelles. The region's geography is shaped by the East African Rift, creating varied landscapes from coastal lowlands to highlands. It hosts Mount Kilimanjaro and Mount Kenya, Africa's tallest peaks, and major lakes like Victoria and Tanganyika. The climate is mostly arid or semi-arid, atypical for the equator, with rainfall increasing southward and with altitude. It is vulnerable to drought and flood cycles, influenced by El Niño and the Indian Ocean Dipole.

### Prehistory and Ancient History
East Africa is a key site for human origins. Fossil evidence from Ethiopia, Kenya, and Tanzania supports the "Out of Africa" theory, suggesting modern humans evolved here and dispersed globally. The Bantu expansion, beginning 2500–3000 years ago, was a major migration that spread agriculture and Bantu languages, displacing or absorbing earlier hunter-gatherer populations. On the coast, interactions with Arab and Persian traders led to the Swahili culture and language, a Bantu language with Arabic loanwords. Ancient kingdoms included Aksum (c. 100–940 CE), a trading empire in modern Eritrea and Ethiopia.

### Colonial and Modern History
European exploration began with the Portuguese in the late 15th century, who sought to control Indian Ocean trade. They were later challenged and largely replaced by Omani Arabs, who dominated coastal trade and established Zanzibar as a capital. In the 19th century, European powers colonized the region during the "Scramble for Africa." Britain acquired Uganda and Kenya, Germany gained Tanganyika (later part of Tanzania), France took Madagascar and Djibouti, and Italy controlled parts of Somalia and Eritrea. Ethiopia remained independent until a brief Italian occupation (1936–1941). Post-independence, the region has seen significant growth but also conflicts, including the Rwandan genocide, civil wars in Sudan and South Sudan, and insurgencies like the Lord's Resistance Army. Kenya and Tanzania have enjoyed relative stability.

### Demographics and Economy
The region's population was estimated at over 537.9 million in 2017. Languages include Bantu languages (e.g., Swahili, with over 80 million speakers), Afroasiatic languages (e.g., Amharic, Somali), and Nilotic languages. Christianity and Islam are the main religions. Economies are growing rapidly (around 6% annually), led by Rwanda, Uganda, Kenya, and Tanzania. Agriculture employs most people, while tourism is vital for Kenya, Tanzania, and Uganda. Nairobi and Addis Ababa are major financial hubs.

## Terms
- **Bantu expansion**: A millennia-long migration of Bantu-speaking peoples from West Africa that spread agriculture and languages across central, eastern, and southern Africa.
- **Swahili coast**: The coastal area of East Africa (Kenya, Tanzania) where Swahili culture and language developed through trade with Arab and Persian merchants.
- **East African Rift**: A tectonic plate boundary where the African Plate is splitting into the Somali and Nubian Plates, creating dramatic landscapes.
- **Horn of Africa**: The easternmost projection of Africa, comprising Djibouti, Eritrea, Ethiopia, and Somalia.
- **African Great Lakes**: A region of lakes in central East Africa, including Victoria, Tanganyika, and Malawi, significant for biodiversity and human settlement.
- **Out of Africa theory**: The model that anatomically modern humans evolved in Africa and later dispersed to other continents.
- **Intertropical Convergence Zone (ITCZ)**: A belt of low pressure near the equator that influences seasonal rainfall patterns in East Africa.
- **El Niño**: A climate pattern involving unusual warming of the Pacific Ocean that can increase rainfall in East Africa.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The exact location and timeline of the emergence of anatomically modern humans are debated, with evidence from sites like Jebel Irhoud (Morocco) and Olorgesailie (Kenya) suggesting a broader origin in Africa.
- The causes of East Africa's low rainfall totals are not fully understood, though factors like the Somali Jet and rift valley topography are proposed.
- The precise boundaries of "East Africa" remain fluid, with varying definitions used by the UN, regional organizations, and in common parlance.

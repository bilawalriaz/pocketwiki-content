# Anglo-Dutch wars

## Overview
The Anglo-Dutch Wars were a series of four naval conflicts fought between the Dutch Republic and England/Great Britain from 1652 to 1784. Primarily driven by competition for global trade and colonial dominance, these wars marked the shift in maritime power from the Dutch to the British. The first three wars in the 17th century were closely contested, but by the fourth war a century later, the Royal Navy's overwhelming superiority led to a decisive British victory and the decline of Dutch naval power.

## Timeline
- **1652–1654** — First Anglo-Dutch War; England victorious.
- **1665–1667** — Second Anglo-Dutch War; Dutch victory, highlighted by the Raid on the Medway.
- **1672–1674** — Third Anglo-Dutch War; Dutch victory, preventing an Anglo-French invasion.
- **1688** — Glorious Revolution; Dutch Stadtholder William III becomes King of England, ending major conflicts.
- **1780–1784** — Fourth Anglo-Dutch War; decisive British victory exposes Dutch weakness.
- **1797** — British victory at the Battle of Camperdown during the French Revolutionary Wars.
- **1806–1811** — Britain captures most Dutch colonies, including the Cape Colony and the Dutch East Indies.
- **1814** — Anglo-Dutch Treaty returns most colonies to the Netherlands, except the Cape, Ceylon, and part of Dutch Guyana.

## Body

### Background and Causes
The wars arose from intense commercial rivalry. In the early 17th century, the Dutch Republic built a dominant global trading network, with a merchant fleet larger than all other nations combined. After the English Civil War, Oliver Cromwell's government revitalized the Royal Navy and passed the Navigation Acts (1651) to exclude Dutch middlemen from English trade. This policy, combined with mutual resentment and colonial competition in the Americas and East Indies, created a belligerent mood in England. The Dutch, meanwhile, were politically divided and had reduced their navy after achieving peace with Spain in 1648.

### The First Three Wars (1652–1674)
The first war (1652–54) began over a salute dispute and was marked by English tactical innovations like the "line of battle." Despite initial Dutch successes, England won the war. The second war (1665–67) saw Dutch recovery and culminated in the humiliating Dutch Raid on the Medway, which forced England to sue for peace. The third war (1672–74) was fought as part of the larger Franco-Dutch War; the Dutch navy, under Michiel de Ruyter, successfully repelled a combined Anglo-French invasion fleet, leading the English Parliament to force Charles II to make peace.

### The Fourth War and Later Conflicts
After the Glorious Revolution of 1688, the nations were allies for over a century. However, by the 1780s, Britain had surpassed the Dutch economically and militarily. The fourth war (1780–84) was triggered by Dutch support for the American rebels. With a severely weakened navy, the Dutch suffered a conclusive defeat, losing colonial possessions and exposing the Republic's political fragility. During the subsequent Napoleonic Wars, Britain captured nearly the entire Dutch colonial empire, though most was returned in 1814.

## Terms
- **Navigation Acts:** English laws mandating that imports be carried on English or colonial ships, aimed at crippling Dutch trade.
- **Line of battle:** A naval tactic where ships form a single file to maximize broadside firepower, perfected by the English.
- **Stadtholder:** The chief magistrate and military leader of the Dutch Republic, a position held by the House of Orange.
- **Raid on the Medway:** A 1667 Dutch naval attack that burned and captured English ships in their home port, a major English humiliation.
- **Hollandic Water Line:** A Dutch defensive system of strategic flooding used to halt land invasions.
- **Glorious Revolution:** The 1688 overthrow of King James II of England by Dutch Stadtholder William III, who became king.
- **Ship money:** A tax levied by Charles I to fund naval rebuilding, which contributed to the English Civil War.

## Debates and Open Questions
The source notes that some historians count the conflicts between Britain and the Batavian Republic/Kingdom of Holland during the Napoleonic era as the "Fifth and Sixth Anglo–Dutch wars," indicating a debate on the exact scope and numbering of the wars.

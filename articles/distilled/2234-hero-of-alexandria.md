# Hero of Alexandria

## Overview
Hero of Alexandria was a Greek mathematician and engineer active in Roman-era Alexandria, likely in the 1st or 2nd century AD. He is considered a leading figure of Hellenistic science, renowned for his experimental work in mechanics and pneumatics, and for mathematical contributions like Heron's formula. His writings, preserved through Byzantine and Arabic manuscripts, document early inventions including steam and wind-powered devices, influencing later scientific thought.

## Timeline
- **c. 150 BC – 250 AD** — Scholarly estimates for Hero's active period.
- **62 AD** — A lunar eclipse, possibly observed by Hero, is used as an example in his work *Dioptra*.
- **4th century AD** — First extant mention of Hero in Pappus's *Collection*.
- **1903** — Comprehensive five-volume edition of Hero's works published in Leipzig.

## Body

### Life and Historical Context
Almost nothing is known of Hero's life, including his birthplace. His dates are inferred from references in later works and a lunar eclipse example in his *Dioptra*, which some scholars link to an eclipse in 62 AD, though this is debated. He worked in Alexandria, a cosmopolitan Roman city with a Greek-speaking intellectual community centered on the Mouseion (which included the Library of Alexandria). It is inferred he taught there, as some writings resemble lecture notes. His work on automated devices is considered an early form of research into cybernetics.

### Inventions and Mechanical Works
Hero described numerous devices in works like *Pneumatica* and *Mechanica*. His most famous invention is the aeolipile, a steam-powered reaction engine, though its practical use is debated. Other inventions include a wind-wheel powering an organ (the first documented use of wind power), a vending machine for holy water, self-opening temple doors, and a force pump used in fire engines. He also designed theatrical automata, a self-filling wine bowl, and a device demonstrating air expansion/contraction, a principle related to thermometers. His "Heron's fountain" is a stand-alone fountain using hydrostatic pressure.

### Mathematics
In his work *Metrica*, Hero described algorithms for calculating square roots (Heron's method) and cube roots. He is most famous for Heron's formula, which calculates a triangle's area from its side lengths. He also formulated a shortest-path principle for light, stating that light travels the shortest possible path between two points in the same medium. This principle was later expanded by Ibn al-Haytham and Pierre de Fermat.

### Preservation of Works
Hero's original writings are largely lost. His works survive in Byzantine manuscripts, and some, like *Mechanica* and *Catoptrica*, only in Arabic translations. Several works are attributed to him but are now thought to be by others, such as *Geometrica* and *Stereometrica*. A commentary on Euclid's *Elements* is attested by Arabic sources but is no longer extant.

## Terms
- **Aeolipile**: A rocket-like reaction engine, considered the first recorded steam engine.
- **Pneumatics**: The branch of mechanics dealing with gases and air pressure, a key focus of Hero's work.
- **Automata**: Self-operating machines, especially those designed to mimic life or perform tasks automatically.
- **Heron's formula**: A mathematical formula to calculate the area of a triangle using the lengths of its three sides.
- **Heron's method**: An iterative algorithm for approximating square roots.
- **Dioptra**: An ancient surveying instrument similar to a theodolite, described by Hero.
- **Mouseion**: The "Museum" or scholarly institution in Alexandria, which included the Library of Alexandria.
- **Cybernetics**: The science of communications and automatic control systems in both machines and living things; Hero's automated devices are seen as early research in this field.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Hero's dates**: His active period is uncertain, with estimates ranging from 150 BC to 250 AD. The link to the 62 AD eclipse is a hypothesis based on a vague example in his text.
- **Practicality of inventions**: The aeolipile is often called the first steam engine, but historians debate whether it was used for useful work or was primarily a demonstration or toy.
- **Authorship**: Several works, such as *Geometrica* and *Stereometrica*, are sometimes attributed to Hero but are now considered likely to be by other authors.

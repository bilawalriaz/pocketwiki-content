# Gaspard Monge

## Overview
Gaspard Monge (1746–1818) was a French mathematician and revolutionary-era statesman. He is credited as the inventor of descriptive geometry, the mathematical foundation for technical drawing, and as a father of differential geometry. His work bridged pure mathematics with practical engineering and military science, and he played a key role in founding France's elite educational institutions, most notably the École Polytechnique.

## Body

### Early Life and Founding of Descriptive Geometry
Monge was educated by the Oratorians and began teaching physics at age seventeen. After creating a precise plan of his hometown, he was hired as a draftsman at the military engineering school, the École Royale du Génie. There, he developed his method for solving the complex problem of *défilement*—ensuring fortification walls protected defenders from enemy sightlines. While the established method required a week of calculations, Monge's graphical solution using visibility cones took only two days. This work, initially kept as a military secret, became the basis of descriptive geometry.

### Academic Career and Revolutionary Service
Monge became a professor at the École Royale and later an examiner for naval candidates. The French Revolution redirected his career; he served as Minister of the Navy (1792–1793) and applied his expertise to munitions production. He was instrumental in founding the École Normale and the École Polytechnique, where he taught descriptive geometry. His lectures were published as *Géométrie descriptive* (1799). He later accompanied Napoleon to Italy and Egypt, participating in scientific work and art selection.

### Mathematical and Scientific Contributions
Monge's 1781 memoir on earthworks (*déblais et remblais*) established the theory of curvature for surfaces and is considered an early anticipation of linear optimization problems. His work on the Monge-Kantorovich distance became foundational in optimal transport theory. He also contributed to chemistry, notably with Claude Louis Berthollet, and was among the first to liquefy a pure gas (sulfur dioxide) in 1783–1784. His later publications included works on statics and analytical geometry.

## Terms
- **Descriptive Geometry**: The mathematical system for representing 3D objects in 2D drawings, essential for technical and engineering drawing.
- **Differential Geometry**: A branch of mathematics using calculus and algebra to study the geometry of curves and surfaces.
- **Défilement**: The science of designing fortification walls to protect defenders from direct enemy fire, especially from elevated positions.
- **Defilade**: The safe space within a fortification that is shielded from an attacker's line of sight.
- **Enfilade Fire**: A dangerous form of artillery fire directed along the length of a defensive wall or trench.
- **Curves of Curvature**: Lines on a surface that indicate its direction of bending at any point.
- **Optimal Transportation Problem**: A mathematical problem of finding the most efficient way to move a distribution of mass to another.
- **École Polytechnique**: A prestigious French engineering school founded in 1794, where Monge taught.

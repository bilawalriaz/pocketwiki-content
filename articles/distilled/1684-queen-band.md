# Queen (band)

## Overview
Queen are a British rock band formed in London in 1970, comprising Freddie Mercury (lead vocals, piano), Brian May (guitar, vocals), Roger Taylor (drums, vocals), and John Deacon (bass). They evolved from progressive and hard rock roots into a genre-defying act incorporating pop, opera, dance, and rockabilly, becoming one of the world's best-selling music artists with estimated sales of 250–300 million records. Their 1975 single "Bohemian Rhapsody" pioneered the promotional music video format and topped the UK chart twice; their 1985 Live Aid performance is widely ranked as the greatest live rock show in history. Mercury died of AIDS-related bronchopneumonia in 1991; Deacon retired in 1997. Since 2004, May and Taylor have toured as "Queen +" with vocalists Paul Rodgers (2004–2009) and Adam Lambert (2011–present).

## Body

### Formation and Early Years (1968–1973)
Guitarist Brian May and drummer Roger Taylor played together in Smile from 1968. Farrokh "Freddie" Bulsara, an Ealing Art College student and Smile fan, became close with bassist Tim Staffell. When Staffell quit in 1970, Bulsara joined as lead singer, suggesting the name "Queen" and adopting the surname Mercury. After two short-lived bassists (Mike Grose, Barry Mitchell), John Deacon joined in February 1971, completing the classic line-up. The band recorded demos at De Lane Lea Studios during off-peak hours—Taylor called this "gold dust"—securing a management deal with Trident Studios' Neptune Productions. Their 1973 debut *Queen*, recorded at Trident, mixed heavy metal and progressive rock; critics praised it ("superb" – *Rolling Stone*), but "Keep Yourself Alive" sold poorly.

### Breakthrough and Artistic Peak (1974–1976)
*Queen II* (1974) introduced their signature "layered" sound, fantasy lyrics, and Mick Rock's iconic cover (later basis for the "Bohemian Rhapsody" video). It reached UK No. 5, yielding first hit "Seven Seas of Rhye" (UK No. 10). *Sheer Heart Attack* (1974) diversified into music hall, ragtime, and Caribbean styles; "Killer Queen" (UK No. 2, US No. 12) brought US success. *A Night at the Opera* (1975), then the most expensive album ever (£40,000), featured "Bohemian Rhapsody"—assembled from Mercury's college pieces, using ~180 vocal overdubs. EMI initially refused the 5:55 single; Capital Radio DJ Kenny Everett played it 14 times in a weekend, forcing release. It topped UK for 9 weeks, became the only single to be Christmas No. 1 twice, and its Bruce Gowers-directed video "practically invented the music video seven years before MTV" (*Rolling Stone*). The album went triple platinum US; "You're My Best Friend" (Deacon) hit US Top 10.

### Stadium Dominance and Global Expansion (1977–1982)
*News of the World* (1977) yielded twin anthems "We Will Rock You" and "We Are the Champions", engineered for audience participation. The 1978 *Jazz* album included "Don't Stop Me Now" and the double-A-side "Fat Bottomed Girls"/"Bicycle Race". In 1981, Queen became the first non-American rock band to play Latin American stadiums, drawing 300,000 in Buenos Aires and 131,000 in São Paulo (then largest paying crowd for a single band). "Love of My Life" became a phenomenon there, with audiences singing word-perfect English. The Bowie collaboration "Under Pressure" (1981) topped UK charts. *Greatest Hits* (1981) became the UK's all-time best-seller. *Hot Space* (1982) pivoted to funk/disco/dance, alienating May and Taylor; tensions flared around Mercury's manager Paul Prenter. The band played their last North American shows with Mercury in September 1982; US popularity waned, partly attributed to homophobia toward Mercury's "gay clone look" (*Rolling Stone*).

### Creative Friction and Live Aid Revival (1983–1986)
After a hiatus, *The Works* (1984) returned to rock with "Radio Ga Ga" and "Hammer to Fall", but the cross-dressing "I Want to Break Free" video was banned by MTV in the US. The Works Tour included dates at Sun City, South Africa, violating the UN cultural boycott; the band was fined and blacklisted (Taylor later regretted it). In January 1985, Queen headlined Rock in Rio (300,000/night). At Live Aid (13 July 1985), their 21-minute set—featuring Mercury's "Note Heard Round the World" call-and-response—was voted the greatest rock performance ever (2005 industry poll). Revitalized, they recorded *A Kind of Magic* (1986) for the film *Highlander*. The Magic Tour (mid-1986) was their last with Mercury: 1M+ attendees, including 80,000 in Budapest (behind the Iron Curtain) and a final 120,000+ at Knebworth (9 Aug 1986), where Mercury bid "goodnight and sweet dreams".

### Mercury's Final Years and Death (1987–1991)
Mercury was diagnosed HIV positive in 1987 but denied press reports. *The Miracle* (1989) shifted songwriting to collective credit. *Innuendo* (1991) included "The Show Must Go On", recorded while Mercury could barely walk; May recalled he "completely killed it". On 23 November 1991, Mercury announced his AIDS diagnosis; he died 24 November of bronchopneumonia. "Bohemian Rhapsody"/"These Are the Days of Our Lives" re-release topped UK for five weeks (only single to top chart in four different years). The 1992 Freddie Mercury Tribute Concert at Wembley raised £20M for AIDS charities, televised to 1.2B viewers.

### Post-Mercury Era (1995–Present)
*Made in Heaven* (1995), built from Mercury's 1991 sessions, hit UK No. 1 (20M sales). Deacon retired in 1997. May and Taylor toured as "Queen +" with Paul Rodgers (2004–2009), releasing *The Cosmos Rocks* (2008). Since 2011, they tour with Adam Lambert, playing global stadiums, the 2012 Olympics closing ceremony, and the 2022 Platinum Jubilee. *Bohemian Rhapsody* (2018 film) grossed $900M+, winning Best Actor Oscar for Rami Malek. In 2022, previously "unsalvageable" Mercury track "Face It Alone" was released. In 2024, Sony Music agreed to acquire Queen's catalogue (ex-US/Canada) for ~£1B.

### Music Style and Innovations
Queen fused progressive rock, heavy metal, opera, music hall, gospel, funk, and rockabilly. May's custom-built Red Special guitar (designed to feedback) and Vox AC30 amps defined his tone; he uses a sixpence plectrum. Vocal harmonies (Mercury, May, Taylor) were multi-tracked to mimic choirs—over 180 overdubs on "Bohemian Rhapsody". The "No Synthesisers!" sleeve notes (until *The Game*) clarified that orchestral textures were guitar-based. Songs like "We Will Rock You" and "Radio Ga Ga" were written for stadium participation. Mercury's theatricality drew from Liza Minnelli and showbiz tradition; May cited the Beatles' *White Album* as their "bible" for studio composition.

### Visual Identity and Media
Mercury designed the Queen Crest logo combining members' zodiac signs: two lions (Leo: Deacon, Taylor), crab (Cancer: May), two fairies (Virgo: Mercury), crowned phoenix. Groundbreaking videos: "Bohemian Rhapsody" (diamond formation, Queen II cover reprise); "Radio Ga Ga" (*Metropolis* footage); "I Want to Break Free" (*Coronation Street* spoof, banned by MTV US); "Under Pressure" (silent film clips); "These Are the Days of Our Lives" (B&W to hide Mercury's illness). *We Will Rock You* musical (2002, Ben Elton/Robert De Niro) ran 12 years in London, seen by 20M+ globally. Queen featured in *Guitar Hero*, *Rock Band*, *SingStar*, and the 1998 game *Queen: The eYe*.

## Terms
- **Red Special**: Brian May's custom-built guitar (1963), designed with his father to feedback; used with Vox AC30 amps and a sixpence plectrum.
- **Overdubbing**: Recording additional layers over existing tracks; Queen used ~180 vocal overdubs on "Bohemian Rhapsody" to simulate a choir.
- **Queen Crest**: Logo designed by Mercury combining zodiac signs (Leo lions, Cancer crab, Virgo fairies) with a phoenix and crown.
- **Promotional video**: Short film for a song; Queen's "Bohemian Rhapsody" (1975) is credited with popularising the format pre-MTV.
- **Live Aid**: 13 July 1985 global benefit concert; Queen's 21-minute Wembley set ranked greatest live rock performance ever.
- **A Kind of Magic Tour (1986)**: Queen's final tour with Mercury; 1M+ attendance across Europe, including Eastern Bloc (Budapest).
- **Queen +**: Post-1997 touring branding with guest vocalists (Paul Rodgers 2004–2009; Adam Lambert 2011–present).
- **Made in Heaven (1995)**: Final studio album constructed from Mercury's last 1991 recordings; completed by surviving members.
- **Greatest Hits (1981)**: UK's best-selling album ever (7M+ copies); only album with 1000+ weeks on UK chart.
- **Polar Music Prize (2025)**: International award recognizing Queen's impact on pop culture across decades and genres.

## Debates and open questions
- **US vs. global popularity**: The band stopped touring North America after 1982; sources cite homophobia (Mercury's image), musical shifts (*Hot Space*), and label issues (Capitol Records payola fallout) as factors, but the relative weight is debated.
- **Sun City controversy (1984)**: The band defended playing apartheid-era South Africa as performing for integrated audiences; UN blacklisted them. Taylor (2021) called it a "mistake" despite "best intentions".
- **"No Synthesisers!" sleeve notes**: Originally perceived as anti-synth purism; producer Roy Thomas Baker later revealed it was to correct executives who assumed guitar orchestrations were synths.
- **Mercury's HIV timeline**: Diagnosed 1987 per source; public announcement only 24 hours before death (Nov 1991). Inner circle knew; band continued recording *Innuendo* with Mercury's declining health.
- **Catalogue ownership (2024)**: Sony Music deal (~£1B) covers publishing/recording rights ex-US/Canada; Disney's Hollywood Records retains US/Canada rights in perpetuity; Universal distribution expires 2026/2027.

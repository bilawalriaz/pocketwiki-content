# Najd

## Overview
Najd is a historical and geographical region in the central Arabian Peninsula, forming the core of modern Saudi Arabia. Its name means "highland" in Arabic. Historically remote and arid, it was the heartland from which the House of Saud launched the series of conquests that unified the peninsula into the modern Kingdom of Saudi Arabia in 1932.

## Timeline
- **c. 9,000 years ago** — Al-Magar culture, a Neolithic society practicing agriculture and animal domestication, flourishes in southwestern Najd.
- **5th century AD** — The Kindite Kingdom is established in Najd as a vassal of the Ḥimyarites.
- **624** — The Nejd Caravan Raid occurs, the first military expedition by Muhammad in the area.
- **632** — The Ridda Wars (Wars of Apostasy) are fought to reassert Medinan authority over rebellious tribes in Najd and other regions.
- **1744** — The First Saudi State is founded in Diriyah (near Riyadh) through an alliance between Muhammad bin Saud and the religious leader Muhammad ibn Abd al-Wahhab.
- **1818** — The First Saudi State is destroyed by the Ottoman viceroy of Egypt.
- **1824** — The Second Saudi State is established by Turki ibn Abdullah, with its capital in Riyadh.
- **1891** — The Al Saud family is defeated by the Al Rashid and goes into exile in Kuwait.
- **1902** — Abdulaziz ibn Abdul Rahman (Ibn Saud) recaptures Riyadh, restoring Al Saud rule.
- **1921** — Ibn Saud takes the title Sultan of Najd after defeating the Al Rashid.
- **1924–25** — Ibn Saud conquers the Hejaz region.
- **1932** — The Kingdoms of the Hejaz and Najd are united to form the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia.

## Body

### Geography and Administration
Najd is a plateau ranging from 762 to 1,525 meters in height, sloping west to east. Its boundaries are imprecise but are generally defined by the Hejaz mountains to the west, the Nafud desert to the north, the ad-Dahna desert to the east, and the Rub' al-Khali (Empty Quarter) to the south. The region is divided into three modern provinces: Riyadh, Qassim, and Ḥaʼil. Historically, it was a constellation of smaller provinces centered on towns like Diriyah, Buraidah, and Ḥaʼil. The landscape is marked by escarpments like Tuwaiq, twin mountains near Ḥaʼil, and crucial dry riverbeds (wadis) such as Wadi Hanifa and Wadi al-Rummah, where most settlements are located.

### Ancient and Islamic History
Najd was home to advanced prehistoric cultures like Al-Magar. In the 5th century AD, the Kindite Kingdom was established, making an early attempt to unite Central Arabian tribes. After the Kindites declined, the region saw various tribal conflicts. In the 7th century, military expeditions by the Prophet Muhammad were conducted in Najd. Following Muhammad's death, the Ridda Wars were fought to suppress rebellions led by figures like Musaylima in Yamamah and Malik ibn Nuwayra in Najd, re-establishing Medinan authority. After these wars, Najdi tribes played a significant role in the early Islamic conquests beyond Arabia but the region itself became politically peripheral for centuries.

### The Rise of the Saudi State
In 1744, an alliance between the political leader Muhammad bin Saud and the religious reformer Muhammad ibn Abd al-Wahhab in Diriyah provided the ideological foundation for the First Saudi State. This state expanded rapidly but was destroyed by Ottoman-Egyptian forces in 1818. The Second Saudi State was established in 1824 but faced rivalry with the Al Rashid dynasty. After being exiled in 1891, the Al Saud, led by Abdulaziz (Ibn Saud), recaptured Riyadh in 1902. With the support of the Ikhwan, a Wahhabi-inspired tribal army, Ibn Saud conquered al-Ahsa from the Ottomans (1913) and later the Hejaz (1924–25). Following the defeat of the Ikhwan in 1930, the unification of the Hejaz and Najd was formalized in 1932.

### Culture and Society
Najd's historical isolation from major Islamic empires like the Abbasids and Ottomans shaped a distinct culture. It is a traditional stronghold of the Hanbali school of Islamic jurisprudence and became the center of the Salafi movement, founded by Najdi scholar Muhammad ibn Abd al-Wahhab. The Najdi dialect of Arabic is considered one of the least foreign-influenced. Traditional Najdi architecture features low-contour mudbrick buildings with central courtyards, designed for the desert climate.

## Terms
*   **Najd:** Arabic for "highland"; the central region of the Arabian Peninsula.
*   **Wadi:** A valley or streambed that is dry except in the rainy season.
*   **Ikhwan:** A religiously motivated tribal army that supported Ibn Saud's conquests in the early 20th century.
*   **Salafism:** A reform movement within Sunni Islam advocating a return to the practices of the earliest generations of Muslims (the salaf).
*   **Ridda Wars:** The "Wars of Apostasy" (632–633 AD) fought to reassert the authority of the Medinan caliphate over tribes that had rebelled or renounced Islam after Muhammad's death.
*   **First Saudi State:** The Emirate of Diriyah (c. 1744–1818), the initial political entity established by the House of Saud.
*   **Second Saudi State:** The Emirate of Najd (1824–1891), re-established by Turki ibn Abdullah after the collapse of the first state.
*   **Al Saud:** The royal family of Saudi Arabia, originating in Najd.
*   **Wahhabism:** A strict, puritanical form of Sunni Islam founded by Muhammad ibn Abd al-Wahhab, which became the state ideology of the Saudi states.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   The precise historical boundaries of Najd are debated, as they have fluctuated based on geographical and political definitions over centuries.
*   The exact location and dating of certain early Islamic expeditions in Najd, such as the Dhat ar-Riqa' campaign, are subjects of scholarly discussion regarding their sequence and relation to other events.

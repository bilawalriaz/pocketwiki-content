# Croatia

## Overview
Croatia is a Central and Southeastern European country on the Adriatic Sea, known for over 1,000 islands, diverse geography, and a history shaped by migrations, empires, and independence struggles. It is a developed, high-income parliamentary democracy with a strong tourism sector, having joined the EU in 2013 and the Eurozone and Schengen Area in 2023.

- **2023** — Croatia joins the Eurozone and Schengen Area.

## Body

### Etymology and Early History
"Croatia" derives from Medieval Latin *Croātia*, tracing to a Proto-Slavic term possibly of Iranian origin meaning "guardian." The earliest records appear in 9th-century inscriptions like the Baška tablet. The region was inhabited since prehistory, with Neanderthal fossils at Krapina, later settled by Illyrians and Greeks before becoming part of the Roman Empire in 9 AD. The Roman period ended with Avar and Croat invasions in the 6th–7th centuries.

### Medieval Kingdom and Union with Hungary
Following Slavic migrations, the Duchy of Croatia was established. It gained independence under Duke Branimir in 878 and became a kingdom under King Tomislav in 925. The medieval kingdom peaked in the 11th century. After King Stjepan II's death in 1091 ended the Trpimirović dynasty, a succession war led to a personal union with Hungary in 1102. For centuries, Croatia was ruled by a Hungarian-appointed Ban and its own parliament, the Sabor, while facing threats from the Ottoman Empire and Venice.

### Habsburg Rule and National Revival
Ottoman victories in the 15th–16th centuries caused territorial losses. In 1527, the Croatian Parliament elected Habsburg Ferdinand I as king, securing protection. Croatia was later divided into civilian and military territories, with the Military Frontier under direct Habsburg control. The 19th century saw the Croatian National Revival, a movement for South Slavic unity and language standardization. Following the Austro-Hungarian Compromise of 1867, Croatia-Slavonia gained internal autonomy within the Hungarian part of the empire.

### World Wars and Yugoslavia
After World War I, Croatia joined the Kingdom of Serbs, Croats, and Slovenes (later Yugoslavia). Political tensions culminated in the 1929 royal dictatorship. During World War II, the Axis established the puppet NDH, whose regime committed genocide against Serbs, Jews, and Roma. A multi-ethnic Partisan resistance led by Josip Broz Tito fought the occupiers. Croatia then became a socialist republic within federal Yugoslavia. The 1974 Yugoslav constitution granted significant autonomy to republics.

### Independence and Modern Croatia
Following communism's collapse, Croatia held its first multi-party elections in 1990. It declared independence on 25 June 1991, triggering the War of Independence (1991–1995) against Serbian forces and the Yugoslav People's Army. The war caused significant casualties and displacement. Croatia gained international recognition in 1992 and secured its territorial integrity by 1998. Since independence, it has transitioned to a parliamentary democracy, joined NATO (2009) and the EU (2013), and adopted the euro (2023).

### Geography and Climate
Croatia covers 56,594 km², featuring a continental interior and a long Adriatic coastline with over 1,000 islands. Its terrain includes the Dinaric Alps, the Pannonian Basin, and extensive karst topography. The climate varies from Mediterranean on the coast to continental inland. The country is rich in biodiversity, with forests covering 44% of its land and numerous protected areas, including Plitvice Lakes National Park.

### Governance and Economy
Croatia is a unitary parliamentary republic. Its economy is highly developed and service-dominated, with tourism contributing 10–15% of GDP. It is an emerging energy power with investments in LNG and renewables. Infrastructure includes a modern motorway network and key ports like Rijeka. The country faces challenges like an aging population and low birth rates.

### Demographics and Culture
The population is approximately 3.9 million, predominantly Croat (91.6%) and Catholic. The official language is Croatian. The culture reflects a blend of Central European, Mediterranean, and Balkan influences. Croatia is known for contributions to literature, music, and the arts, and for inventing the necktie (cravat). It has a vibrant film industry and is a popular international filming location.

## Terms
- **Sabor**: The unicameral parliament of Croatia, holding legislative power.
- **Ban**: A historical viceroy, appointed by the king during the period of union with Hungary.
- **Personal Union**: A political arrangement where two or more states share the same monarch but remain separate entities.
- **NDH (Independent State of Croatia)**: A World War II puppet state of Nazi Germany and Fascist Italy.
- **Partisans**: The communist-led, multi-ethnic Yugoslav resistance movement against the Axis occupation.
- **Karst**: A landscape formed from the dissolution of soluble rocks like limestone, characterized by caves and sinkholes.
- **Eurozone**: The group of EU member states that have adopted the euro as their currency.
- **Schengen Area**: An area of European countries that have abolished passport and other types of control at their mutual borders.

## Debates and open questions
- The ethnogenesis of the Croats is debated, with a predominant scholarly consensus proposing a Slavic migration from White Croatia, while an alternative Iranian theory suggests a Sarmatian-Alanic origin.
- The reliability and interpretation of the 10th-century Byzantine text *De Administrando Imperio*, which describes early Croatian settlement, are subjects of scholarly dispute.
- The exact number of casualties and displaced persons during the 1991–1995 War of Independence remains a point of discussion, with various demographic studies providing differing estimates.

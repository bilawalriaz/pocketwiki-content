# Modern dance

## Overview
Modern dance is a genre of Western concert dance that emerged in the late 19th and early 20th centuries as a rebellion against the strictures of classical ballet. It prioritizes expressive movement, emotional authenticity, and social commentary, evolving through distinct periods and techniques to become a foundational form of 20th-century performance art.

## Timeline
- **c. 1880–1923** — Early Modern period: Pioneers like Isadora Duncan and Loie Fuller reject ballet conventions for free, expressive movement.
- **1915** — Ruth St. Denis and Ted Shawn found the Denishawn school, a training ground for future modern dance leaders.
- **c. 1923–1946** — Central Modern period: Martha Graham, Doris Humphrey, and others develop distinct American techniques and vocabularies.
- **1934** — The Bennington Summer School of the Dance is established, becoming a key institution for modern dance training and dissemination.
- **1936** — Katherine Dunham founds Ballet Negre, later developing her own technique blending African and Caribbean movement with ballet.
- **c. 1946–1957** — Late Modern period: Artists like Merce Cunningham and José Limón introduce abstraction and avant-garde ideas, paving the way for postmodernism.
- **1949** — José Limón creates *The Moor's Pavane*, a signature work of his technique.
- **1958** — Alvin Ailey American Dance Theater is founded, drawing on African-American cultural heritage.
- **1960s** — Postmodern dance emerges, rejecting formal technique in favor of improvisation, everyday movement, and performance art.

## Body

### Origins and Free Dance
Modern dance arose from a rejection of classical ballet's rigid vocabulary and costumes, fueled by socioeconomic changes like industrialization and a new focus on physical fitness. Pioneers like Isadora Duncan, Loie Fuller, and Ruth St. Denis championed "free dance," emphasizing natural movement, bare feet, and emotional expression. Duncan drew inspiration from Greek art and nature, while Fuller experimented with lighting and costume. This early work established the torso as the center of movement, a core principle of modern dance.

### Development of American Techniques
In the Central Modern period, choreographers sought to create a distinctively American dance language. Martha Graham developed a technique based on "contraction and release," focusing on the body's center and its relationship to the floor. Doris Humphrey and Charles Weidman created the Humphrey-Weidman technique, centered on "fall and recovery." These systems, along with Katherine Dunham's technique integrating African and Caribbean movement, became foundational training methods still taught worldwide.

### Expansion and Diversification
Throughout the mid-20th century, modern dance diversified. Radical dancers like Anna Sokolow addressed social crises. Merce Cunningham introduced chance procedures and non-linear structure, influenced by composer John Cage. Alvin Ailey blended modern dance with African-American cultural themes, most famously in *Revelations* (1960). This period saw the rise of individualistic choreographic voices and the integration of ideas from other art forms.

### Legacy and Evolution
The legacy of modern dance is seen in its direct lineage to postmodern and contemporary dance. Postmodern dance in the 1960s rejected the formalism of earlier modern dance, embracing improvisation and performance art. Contemporary dance, emerging in the 1950s, often combines modern and ballet elements. The techniques and philosophies of pioneers like Graham, Horton, and Cunningham remain influential, demonstrating the genre's continuous evolution.

## Terms
- **Modern dance**: A genre of Western concert dance that emerged as a rebellion against classical ballet, emphasizing expressive, natural movement and emotional or social themes.
- **Classical ballet**: A highly formalized style of dance with strict techniques, vocabulary, and costumes (e.g., pointe shoes, tutus), which modern dance reacted against.
- **Graham technique**: A modern dance technique developed by Martha Graham, characterized by "contraction and release" of the torso and a focus on the body's center.
- **Concert dance**: Dance performed for an audience in a theatrical setting, as opposed to social or folk dance.
- **Improvisational dance**: Dance created spontaneously in the moment, a key element of early free dance and later postmodern dance.
- **Ausdruckstanz**: German Expressionist dance, a European modern dance form developed by pioneers like Mary Wigman.
- **Postmodern dance**: A movement from the 1960s onward that rejected the formal techniques of modern dance, incorporating everyday movement, improvisation, and interdisciplinary elements.
- **Kinesiology**: The scientific study of human movement, which influenced techniques like Erick Hawkins's somatic approach.
- **Somatic dance technique**: A approach focusing on the dancer's internal physical sensation and awareness, pioneered by Erick Hawkins.

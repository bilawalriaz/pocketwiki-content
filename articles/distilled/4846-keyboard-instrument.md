# Keyboard instrument

## Overview
A keyboard instrument is a musical instrument played using a keyboard—a row of levers pressed by the fingers. The term classifies instruments by the performer's action, not by how sound is produced, encompassing a wide range of families from pipe organs to synthesizers. Historically, the keyboard evolved from ancient organs to the modern piano and electronic instruments, reflecting changes in musical expression and technology.

## Timeline
- **3rd century BC** — The Ancient Greek hydraulis, a pipe organ, is invented.
- **14th century** — The clavichord and harpsichord appear, becoming common until the 18th century.
- **1698** — Bartolomeo Cristofori introduces the first modern piano template in Italy.
- **18th century** — The piano gains widespread adoption, decreasing the popularity of the harpsichord and clavichord.
- **Late 19th century** — Further developments shape the piano into its current form.
- **20th century** — Early electromechanical instruments, such as the Ondes Martenot, appear.
- **Later 20th century** — Electronic keyboards and synthesizers are developed.

## Body

### Classification and Sound Production
Keyboard instruments are categorized by the performer's action on a keyboard, not by sound production method. Families include aerophones (e.g., pipe organ), idiophones (e.g., celesta), chordophones (e.g., harpsichord, piano), and electrophones (e.g., synthesizers). Modern digital keyboards translate key presses into electrical signals to produce sound and can simulate a vast range of tones via sound synthesis and digital sampling.

### Historical Development
The earliest known keyboard instrument was the Ancient Greek hydraulis (3rd century BC), a pipe organ that remained the sole keyboard instrument until the 14th century. Early keyboards often had buttons or levers and typically featured seven naturals per octave. The clavichord and harpsichord emerged in the 14th century. The piano was introduced in 1698 by Bartolomeo Cristofori as the "gravicèmbalo con piano e forte," allowing dynamic control through key-strike force. Its design evolved through the late 19th century into the modern instrument distinct from those known to composers like Mozart and Beethoven.

### Modern and Electronic Keyboards
The 20th century saw the advent of electromechanical instruments like the Ondes Martenot, followed by electronic keyboards and synthesizers. Today, "keyboard" often refers to synthesizers, arrangers, and workstations. These instruments use advanced technology to produce and manipulate sound, allowing performers to control dynamics, phrasing, and other expressive elements.

## Terms
- **Keyboard:** A row of levers pressed by the fingers to play a musical instrument.
- **Aerophone:** A musical instrument that produces sound primarily by causing a body of air to vibrate (e.g., pipe organ).
- **Idiophone:** An instrument that creates sound by the vibration of the instrument itself, without strings or membranes (e.g., celesta).
- **Chordophone:** An instrument that produces sound by vibrating strings (e.g., piano, harpsichord).
- **Electrophone:** An instrument that produces sound primarily by electrical means (e.g., synthesizer).
- **Hydraulis:** An ancient Greek pipe organ that used water pressure to supply air.
- **Clavichord:** A small, early keyboard instrument where strings are struck by tangents.
- **Harpsichord:** A keyboard instrument where strings are plucked.
- **Pianoforte (Piano):** A keyboard instrument where strings are struck by hammers, allowing for dynamic control.
- **Sound Synthesis:** The electronic production of sound, used in modern keyboards to simulate various instruments.

## Debates and Open Questions
In historical musicology, the term "keyboard" is sometimes used when an instrument's specific identity cannot be firmly established, particularly for 18th-century pieces that could be played on a harpsichord, clavichord, or early piano. This ambiguity reflects the overlapping use and competition between these instruments during that period.

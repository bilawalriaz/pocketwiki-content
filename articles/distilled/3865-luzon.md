# Luzon

## Overview
Luzon is the largest and most populous island in the Philippines, serving as the nation's economic and political center. It is the world's 4th most populous island and 15th largest by area, hosting the capital Manila and over half the country's population. Its history spans from ancient human settlement to its role as a global trade hub and colonial battleground, shaping the modern Philippine state.

## Timeline
- **c. 50,000–67,000 years ago** — Fossils of *Homo luzonensis* found in Callao Cave.
- **2500–2000 BCE** — Kapampangans and Sambals settle south Luzon.
- **1200–1000 BCE** — Tagalog settlers arrive in south Luzon.
- **900 CE** — Laguna Copperplate Inscription, oldest known Philippine document, created.
- **12th century** — Flourishing trade of Luzon-made clay jars with Japan.
- **1405** — Chinese governor of Luzon appointed during Zheng He's voyages.
- **Early 1500s** — Portuguese encounter "Luções" (people from Luzon) across Asia.
- **1569** — Spanish expedition first lands in Albay, Luzon.
- **1570–1571** — Spanish conquer Maynila and Tondo; Manila becomes capital.
- **1942** — Imperial Japanese forces capture Luzon.
- **1945** — US forces recapture Luzon.
- **1986** — People Power Revolution ends the Marcos dictatorship.

## Body

### Etymology and Early History
The name "Luzon" likely derives from *lusong*, a Tagalog word for a large wooden mortar used in rice milling. Before European contact, the island was home to diverse peoples and coastal polities like Maynila, Tondo, and Namayan, which were trade-based thalassocracies (maritime states) influenced by Hinduism, Animism, and Islam. The 12th-century trade in Luzon-made clay jars with Japan highlights early regional commerce. By the 1400s, Chinese influence was present, and parts of Luzon were later Islamized through the Sultanate of Brunei.

### Portuguese and Spanish Colonial Era
Portuguese sailors were the first Europeans to record the island, calling its inhabitants "Luções." These Luzones were active as traders and mercenaries across Southeast Asia and the Indian Ocean. The Spanish arrived in 1569, conquering Manila in 1571 and establishing it as the capital of the Spanish East Indies. Luzon became the hub of the Manila Galleon trade, linking the Americas and Asia. The Spanish era saw the founding of Intramuros, the rise of Binondo (the world's oldest Chinatown), and significant migration from Mexico, China, and India. Infrastructure like railways and Asia's first steel suspension bridge were built in the 19th century.

### Revolution, American Rule, and World War II
Resistance to Spanish rule culminated in the Philippine Revolution, leading to the First Philippine Republic in Malolos, Bulacan. After the Spanish-American War, the US took control, leading to the Philippine–American War. The Americans established military bases and rebuilt Manila. During World War II, Japan captured Luzon in 1942; US forces, led by General MacArthur, recaptured it in 1945. Post-war, the Philippines gained independence, and Luzon became the most developed island, though the Marcos dictatorship spurred a large diaspora. The 1986 People Power Revolution restored democracy.

### Geography and Administration
Luzon island covers 109,964.9 km² and is divided into four main sections: Northern, Central, and Southern Luzon, and Metro Manila. It is geologically active, part of the Philippine Mobile Belt with major fault systems like the Philippine Fault. The island is administered through 8 regions and 30 provinces. Metro Manila is the urban core, while the Central Luzon plain is the country's primary rice-producing area.

### Demographics and Economy
Luzon's population was 59.87 million in 2024. Major ethnolinguistic groups include the Tagalog, Ilocano, Kapampangan, and Bicolano. Languages belong to the Austronesian family, with Tagalog and English widely used. Christianity, predominantly Roman Catholicism, is the major religion. The economy is centered in Metro Manila's financial districts, with agriculture (rice, coconuts, fruits) dominating other regions. Key international airports include Ninoy Aquino (Manila) and Clark.

## Terms
- **Thalassocracy**: A state or empire with primary power derived from its dominance of the sea and maritime trade.
- **Polity**: A form or process of civil government or constitution.
- **Datu**: A chief or lord in pre-colonial Philippine societies.
- **Lakan**: A paramount lord or ruler in some pre-colonial Philippine polities.
- **Encomienda**: A Spanish colonial system granting a colonist the right to tribute and labor from a specific area.
- **Manila Galleon**: The Spanish trade route (1565–1815) linking Manila and Acapulco, Mexico.
- **Mestizo**: A person of mixed ancestry, particularly of Filipino and Spanish or Chinese descent.
- **Igorot**: A collective term for various ethnolinguistic groups indigenous to the Cordillera mountains of Luzon.
- **Bangsamoro**: The Muslim people of native Filipino descent in the southern Philippines.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The exact location of the 10th-century Buddhist polity of Ma-i (or Maidh) is debated, with scholars divided between Bay, Laguna and Bulalacao, Mindoro.
- The extent and precise nature of Chinese and Bruneian influence over pre-colonial Luzon kingdoms remain subjects of scholarly discussion.
- The demographic impact and integration of centuries-old migrations (e.g., Chinese, Spanish, Mexican) into Luzon's population are ongoing areas of historical and anthropological study.

# Mustafa Kemal Atatürk

## Overview
Mustafa Kemal Atatürk (c. 1881–1938) was a Turkish field marshal and statesman who founded the Republic of Turkey and served as its first president from 1923 until his death. He led sweeping reforms that transformed the Ottoman successor state into a secular, industrializing nation, with his policies and socio-political theories becoming known as Kemalism. His legacy remains a defining and often debated force in Turkey's political culture and historical narrative.

## Timeline
- **c. 1881** — Born in Salonica, Ottoman Empire.
- **19 May 1919** — Lands in Samsun, launching the Turkish National Movement.
- **23 April 1920** — Opens the Grand National Assembly in Ankara.
- **9 September 1922** — Turkish forces recapture İzmir, ending the Greco-Turkish War.
- **24 July 1923** — Treaty of Lausanne signed, recognizing Turkish sovereignty.
- **29 October 1923** — Republic of Turkey is proclaimed.
- **3 March 1924** — Caliphate is officially abolished.
- **1 November 1928** — New Latin-based Turkish alphabet is adopted.
- **5 December 1934** — Women are granted full political rights (vote and stand for election).
- **10 November 1938** — Atatürk dies in Istanbul at age 57.

## Body

### Early Life and Military Career
Born Mustafa in Salonica, he later received the name Kemal. After military education, he joined opposition movements against Sultan Abdul Hamid II's autocracy. His early military career included service in the Italo-Turkish War (1911–12) and the Balkan Wars (1912–13). He rose to prominence during World War I, notably commanding the defense at Gallipoli. Following the Ottoman defeat, he was not directly involved in the Armenian genocide initiated by the Committee of Union and Progress (CUP), but his nationalist movement later relied on CUP perpetrators and oversaw the expulsion of Christian minorities from Anatolia.

### Turkish War of Independence and Founding of the Republic
In 1919, Atatürk organized the Turkish National Movement against the Allied partition of Anatolia. He established a provisional government in Ankara and led the Turkish National Army to victory against Greece, Armenia, France, and the United Kingdom, culminating in the Treaty of Lausanne (1923). This victory led to the abolition of the Ottoman sultanate (1922) and the proclamation of the Republic of Turkey (1923).

### Domestic Reforms and the One-Party State
As president, Atatürk implemented radical reforms to build a secular, Western-oriented nation-state. This included abolishing the caliphate (1924), replacing Islamic law with secular civil codes modeled on Switzerland and Italy, and promoting Western attire. He introduced the Latin-based Turkish alphabet (1928) to boost literacy and launched extensive educational reforms. A one-party regime under his Republican People's Party (CHP) was cemented by the mid-1920s, suppressing opposition such as the Sheikh Said Rebellion (1924–27). His government pursued a policy of Turkification to create a homogeneous nation.

### Foreign Policy
Atatürk's foreign policy was guided by the motto "Peace at home, peace in the world." He resolved the Mosul dispute with Britain via League of Nations arbitration (1926). He cultivated a pragmatic friendship with the Soviet Union for support but rejected communism. He forged alliances with neighboring states, including Greece, and was instrumental in creating the Balkan Pact (1934) to counter Italian and Bulgarian aggression. He also successfully negotiated the Montreux Convention (1936), regaining full Turkish sovereignty over the Straits.

### Economic Policies
Atatürk pursued state-led economic development (statism) to achieve independence. His government nationalized foreign assets, established state-owned enterprises like Sümerbank and Etibank, and implemented five-year plans. The İzmir Economic Congress (1923) and the founding of İş Bankası (1924) were key early steps. During the Great Depression, he moved toward a mixed economy, encouraging private initiative alongside state control.

## Terms
*   **Kemalism:** The ideology and socio-political theories of Atatürk, based on republicanism, secularism, nationalism, populism, statism, and reformism.
*   **Caliphate:** The political-religious institution claiming leadership of the worldwide Muslim community, abolished by Atatürk in 1924.
*   **Laïcité:** A model of secularism where the state is strictly neutral in matters of religion, which influenced Turkish secular reforms.
*   **Turkification:** A policy of creating a homogeneous Turkish nation through cultural and linguistic assimilation.
*   **Grand National Assembly (GNA):** The parliament established in Ankara in 1920 as the basis of the nationalist resistance and later the legislative body of the Republic.
*   **Statism (Devletçilik):** An economic principle emphasizing state intervention and control in the economy to foster industrialization.
*   **Sun Language Theory:** A linguistic theory promoted by Atatürk proposing that all human languages descended from a Central Asian primal language.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Role in the Armenian Genocide:** While a CUP member, Atatürk was not directly involved. Scholars debate the extent of his criticism or support, noting his movement later relied on genocide perpetrators and prevented the return of Armenian survivors.
*   **Authoritarianism vs. Modernization:** His rule is criticized for instituting a dictatorship and suppressing opposition, but also praised for necessary nation-building in a volatile period. His experiments with multiparty democracy (1924–25, 1930) failed, leading to a prolonged one-party state.
*   **Assimilationist Policies:** His government's Turkification policies, including the 1934 Resettlement Law and suppression of minority languages, are condemned for targeting minorities like the Kurds and continuing persecution of Christians.
*   **Personal Beliefs:** His personal religious views are debated; sources suggest he may have been an agnostic or atheist, despite his public statements and political use of Islamic rhetoric early in his career.

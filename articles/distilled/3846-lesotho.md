# Lesotho

## Overview
Lesotho is a landlocked kingdom entirely surrounded by South Africa, making it one of only three sovereign enclaves in the world. Founded in the early 19th century, it transitioned from a traditional kingdom to a British protectorate and crown colony before achieving independence in 1966. Its modern history is marked by political instability, including periods of military rule and constitutional crises, alongside significant socioeconomic challenges like high HIV prevalence and economic dependence on textile exports and water sales to South Africa.

## Timeline
- **c. 1822** — Basutoland emerges as a single polity under King Moshoeshoe I.
- **1868** — Basutoland becomes a British protectorate after Moshoeshoe I appeals to Queen Victoria.
- **1884** — Basutoland becomes a British Crown colony.
- **1966** — Basutoland gains independence as the Kingdom of Lesotho.
- **1970** — Prime Minister Leabua Jonathan annuls election results, beginning a de facto one-party rule.
- **1986** — A military coup ousts Jonathan; executive powers are granted to King Moshoeshoe II.
- **1993** — Constitutional government is restored after seven years of military rule.
- **1998** — Post-election protests lead to intervention by SADC forces, causing significant damage in Maseru.
- **2002** — Elections are held under a new mixed proportional system; the LCD wins.
- **2014** — An alleged abortive military coup forces Prime Minister Thomas Thabane to flee.
- **2020** — Thomas Thabane resigns as Prime Minister amid a murder investigation.
- **2022** — Sam Matekane is sworn in as Prime Minister after his new party wins elections.
- **2025** — The US imposes a 50% tariff on Lesotho's exports, causing an economic emergency.

## Body

### Formation and Colonial Era
The Basotho nation was formed under King Moshoeshoe I in the 1820s, consolidating various clans in resistance to regional conflicts. The kingdom's territory was reduced by conflicts with Boer settlers and British colonial treaties. After losing western lands to the Boers, Moshoeshoe I accepted British protection in 1868. The territory became a Crown colony in 1884, administered from Maseru, with internal power still wielded by tribal chiefs.

### Independence and Political Instability
Lesotho gained independence in 1966. The Basotho National Party (BNP) ruled until 1970, when Prime Minister Leabua Jonathan annulled an election loss, imprisoning the opposition and triggering a low-level insurgency. Jonathan was overthrown in a 1986 military coup. After a period of military rule, constitutional democracy was restored in 1993. The post-independence era has been characterized by political fragility, including a 1998 post-election crisis that required South African intervention and a 2014 alleged coup attempt.

### Economy and Resources
Lesotho's economy relies on agriculture, manufacturing (primarily textiles for the US market), mining, and remittances from workers in South Africa. A major economic pillar is the Lesotho Highlands Water Project (LHWP), which exports water to South Africa and generates hydroelectric power. Diamond mining is also significant, with the Letšeng mine known for producing high-value gems. The economy is vulnerable to external shocks, as demonstrated by the severe impact of US tariffs in 2025.

### Demographics and Society
The population is 99.7% Basotho, making it one of the world's most ethnically homogeneous nations. Sesotho and English are official languages. The country faces severe health challenges, with one of the world's highest HIV prevalence rates and a high incidence of tuberculosis. Literacy is relatively high for Africa, but gender-based violence is a critical issue, with Lesotho recording the world's highest rape rate in 2008.

## Terms
- **Sovereign enclave:** A country entirely surrounded by the territory of another country.
- **Protectorate:** A territory that is formally protected by a more powerful state but retains some internal autonomy.
- **Crown colony:** A British colony under the direct control of the British government.
- **Common Monetary Area (CMA):** A currency union between Lesotho, Eswatini, Namibia, and South Africa where currencies are pegged to the South African rand.
- **Lesotho Highlands Water Project (LHWP):** A multibillion-dollar engineering project that captures and transfers water from Lesotho to South Africa.
- **SADC:** The Southern African Development Community, a regional intergovernmental organization.
- **SACU:** The Southern African Customs Union, a customs union among five southern African countries.
- **Laws of Lerotholi:** The codified customary law of the Basotho people.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Political weakness:** Scholars argue that Lesotho's lack of border disputes, a remnant of colonialism, has contributed to its political weakness and exploitative government structures.
- **Annexation proposals:** Periodically, movements like the People's Charter Movement have called for Lesotho's practical annexation by South Africa, citing economic collapse and the HIV epidemic. South Africa has consistently rejected this.
- **Economic integration:** An African Union report has called for economic integration with South Africa, and a South African finance minister once suggested a confederation between Lesotho, Eswatini, and South Africa as a political solution.
- **Tariff justification:** The US government's 2025 imposition of a 50% tariff was based on the claim that Lesotho taxes American goods at nearly 100%, an accusation many observers consider unfounded given Lesotho's membership in SACU and its minimal imports from the US.

# Tipu Sultan

## Overview
Tipu Sultan (1751–1799), known as the "Tiger of Mysore," was the ruler of the Kingdom of Mysore from 1782 to 1799. He is a pivotal figure in South Indian history for his fierce resistance against the expansion of the British East India Company, his pioneering use of rocket artillery, and his ambitious administrative and economic reforms. His reign represents the zenith of Mysore's power before its final defeat by the British.

## Timeline
- **1 Dec 1751** — Born in Devanahalli to Hyder Ali.
- **1766** — First military engagement, accompanying his father in the First Anglo-Mysore War.
- **1780** — Commands forces at the Battle of Pollilur, a major victory against the British.
- **6 Dec 1782** — Father Hyder Ali dies; Tipu becomes ruler of Mysore.
- **1784** — Signs the Treaty of Mangalore, ending the Second Anglo-Mysore War.
- **1787** — Signs the Treaty of Gajendragad, concluding the Maratha–Mysore War.
- **1789** — Invades British-allied Travancore, triggering the Third Anglo-Mysore War.
- **1792** — Defeated; signs the Treaty of Seringapatam, ceding half his territory.
- **1799** — Killed on 4 May while defending Seringapatam in the Fourth Anglo-Mysore War.

## Body

### Military Innovations and Conflicts
Tipu Sultan and his father, Hyder Ali, were early adopters of French military training and technology. Tipu significantly expanded the use of **Mysorean rockets**, iron-cased missiles with sharpened blades that could be launched in mass volleys. He deployed up to 5,000 rocketeers, and their effectiveness against British forces, notably at the Battle of Pollilur (1780), was later influential in the development of the Congreve rocket. His primary conflicts were the four **Anglo-Mysore Wars** against the British East India Company. He also fought the **Maratha–Mysore War**, which ended with the Treaty of Gajendragad (1787). His final defeat came when a combined British force, supported by the Marathas and the Nizam of Hyderabad, besieged his capital, Srirangapatna.

### Administration and Economy
Tipu introduced administrative reforms, including a new calendar, a complex **coinage system** with Persian names for denominations, and seven new government departments. He aimed to boost Mysore's economy, which reached its peak under his rule, surpassing Bengal in productivity. Key initiatives included laying the foundation for the Kannambadi dam (later Krishna Raja Sagara) and initiating the **Mysore silk industry** by sending experts to Bengal. He was also a patron of the **Channapatna toys** craft.

### Foreign Relations
Seeking allies against the British, Tipu engaged in extensive diplomacy. He sought support from the **Ottoman Empire**, **Afghanistan** (Durrani Empire), **Persia**, and **France**. He famously corresponded with Napoleon Bonaparte, who dreamed of linking with Tipu to attack British India. Tipu also maintained nominal allegiance to the **Mughal Emperor** Shah Alam II but acted independently. These efforts largely failed to yield direct military aid.

### Religious Policy and Legacy
Tipu Sultan's religious legacy is highly debated. He was a devout Muslim who made endowments to numerous Hindu temples, including the Ranganathaswami Temple at Srirangapatna, and employed many Hindus in high administrative posts. However, historical accounts also describe the persecution, forced conversion, and massacre of certain communities, such as the **Kodavas of Coorg** and **Iyengar Brahmins of Melkote**. Scholars like Irfan Habib and Mohibbul Hasan argue that many negative accounts originate from biased British sources like Mark Wilks, while others cite Tipu's own letters as evidence of coercion. His legacy remains politically charged in modern India.

## Terms
- **Anglo-Mysore Wars**: Four major conflicts (1767–1799) between the Kingdom of Mysore and the British East India Company, often involving other Indian powers.
- **Mysorean Rockets**: Iron-cased rockets developed and mass-deployed by Hyder Ali and Tipu Sultan, noted for their range and effectiveness.
- **Treaty of Mangalore (1784)**: Ended the Second Anglo-Mysore War, restoring the *status quo ante bellum* (pre-war conditions).
- **Treaty of Seringapatam (1792)**: Forced Tipu to cede half his territories and pay a large war indemnity after the Third Anglo-Mysore War.
- **Maratha–Mysore War**: A conflict (1785–1787) between Tipu Sultan and the Maratha Empire, ending with the Treaty of Gajendragad.
- **Mauludi Calendar**: A solar dating system introduced by Tipu, based on the birth year of the Islamic prophet Muhammad.
- **Ryotwari System**: A land revenue system where taxes were levied directly on individual cultivators (*ryots*), which developed in Mysore partly due to military and administrative changes.
- **Jacobin Club of Mysore**: A political club allegedly founded by Tipu with French support in 1794; its existence is debated by historians.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Religious Policy**: The central debate is whether Tipu Sultan was a tolerant ruler who patronized Hindu temples and employed Hindus, or a religious bigot who persecuted non-Muslims. Historians are divided, with some attributing negative accounts to British propaganda and others citing primary sources.
- **Scale of Persecution**: The extent and systematic nature of events like the deportations in Coorg or the killings in Melkote are contested, with scholars like Mohibbul Hasan questioning the reported numbers.
- **Role of Treachery**: The fall of Seringapatna in 1799 is partly attributed to betrayal by Tipu's own ministers. The identities and motivations of these figures (e.g., Mir Sadiq) are subjects of historical inquiry.
- **Economic Assessment**: While his reign saw economic peak, the long-term impact of his policies, including heavy taxation and military expenditure, on Mysore's society is analyzed by economic historians.

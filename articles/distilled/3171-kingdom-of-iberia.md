# Kingdom of Iberia

## Overview
Caucasian Iberia, known natively as Kartli, was a classical and early medieval kingdom in the South Caucasus, centered in present-day eastern Georgia. It was a pivotal state in the region, serving as a cultural and political nucleus for the Georgian people and later forming the core of the unified medieval Kingdom of Georgia. Its history was defined by its strategic position between the Roman/Byzantine and Persian (Parthian and Sasanian) empires, leading to centuries of rivalry, vassalage, and cultural exchange.

## Timeline
- **c. 302 BC** — Pharnavaz I establishes the Pharnavazid dynasty, the first known royal line of Iberia.
- **93 BC** — Armenian prince Artaxias ascends the Iberian throne, founding the Artaxiad dynasty.
- **65 BC** — Roman general Pompey invades Iberia during his war against Mithridates VI of Pontus.
- **224 AD** — The Sasanian Empire replaces the Parthian Empire, altering Iberia's political orientation.
- **c. 319 AD** — King Mirian III declares Christianity the state religion of Iberia.
- **363 AD** — Following the death of Emperor Julian, Rome cedes control of Iberia to Persia.
- **482 AD** — King Vakhtang I Gorgasali leads a major uprising against Sasanian Persia.
- **580 AD** — Sasanian King Hormizd IV abolishes the Iberian monarchy, making it a Persian province.
- **591 AD** — Byzantium and Persia agree to divide Iberia between them.
- **c. 645 AD** — Arab forces reach Iberia, making it a tributary state of the Caliphate.
- **888 AD** — Adarnase IV is crowned "king of Iberia," restoring the monarchy under the Bagrationi dynasty.

## Body

### Name and Origins
The exonym "Iberia" is derived from Persian terms for the Georgians, ultimately tracing back to an Old Persian root meaning "land of the wolves." The native name for the kingdom was Kartli, after its core province. The population, the Iberians, formed the nucleus of the Kartvelian (Georgian) people. The area was initially inhabited by tribes stemming from the Kura-Araxes culture, with the Moschian tribe playing a key role in consolidating the region and founding the capital, Mtskheta.

### Early Kingdom and Roman Influence
The kingdom was formally established by Pharnavaz I (c. 302–c. 237 BC), who created an administrative system and expanded territory. Iberia's history was later shaped by Roman expansion; after invasions in 65 BC and 36 BC, it entered a period of Roman protection while retaining autonomy. This relationship was marked by cultural exchange, including the adoption of Iranian religious practices (like the cult of Mithras) and administrative styles. A decisive shift occurred with the rise of the Sasanian Empire in 224 AD, which pulled Iberia into its sphere of influence.

### Christianity and Sasanian Domination
A major turning point was the adoption of Christianity as the state religion under King Mirian III around 319 AD, which created a lasting cultural link to the Roman/Byzantine world. However, political control fluctuated. After Rome ceded Iberia to Persia in 363 AD, the Sasanians promoted Zoroastrianism and established viceroys (pitiaxae) to exert direct control. King Vakhtang I Gorgasali (447–502) revived the kingdom and led a major but ultimately unsuccessful revolt against Persia.

### Fall and Later Revival
The kingdom's independence was progressively eroded by Sasanian pressure. In 580 AD, the monarchy was abolished, and Iberia became a Persian province. Byzantium and Persia later divided the territory in 591 AD. After the Arab conquests of the mid-7th century, Iberia became a tributary to the Caliphate. The monarchy was eventually restored in the 9th century under the Bagrationi dynasty, with Adarnase IV crowned king in 888 AD. His descendant, Bagrat III, later united the Georgian principalities into a single medieval kingdom.

## Terms
- **Kartli**: The native Georgian name for the core region and kingdom of Iberia.
- **Pharnavazid dynasty**: The first known royal dynasty of Iberia, founded by Pharnavaz I.
- **Sasanian (Sassanid) Empire**: The last Persian imperial dynasty before the Muslim conquest, a major rival to Rome/Byzantium.
- **Marzpan**: A governor or viceroy appointed by the Sasanian Empire to rule a province.
- **Pitiaxae (Bidaxae)**: A viceroy or governor appointed by the Sasanians to oversee their Iberian vassal.
- **Armazi**: A god introduced as a state cult by King Pharnavaz, later associated with Zoroastrianism.
- **Bagrationi dynasty**: The royal dynasty that restored the Georgian monarchy in the 9th century and later unified Georgia.
- **Kartvelians**: The native term for the Georgian people.

## Debates and Open questions
- The historicity of early figures like Azo and the story of an invasion by Alexander the Great are considered legendary, though they may reflect later desires to associate the monarchy with the Hellenistic world.
- The theory of an ethnogenetical kinship between Caucasian Iberians and the inhabitants of the Iberian Peninsula (based on the similar name) was popular in medieval Georgia but is not supported by modern scholarship.
- The precise nature and extent of Zoroastrianism's establishment in Iberia, and its relationship with the indigenous cult of Armazi, is a subject of scholarly interpretation.

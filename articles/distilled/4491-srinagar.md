# Srinagar

## Overview
Srinagar is the largest city and summer capital of the Indian-administered union territory of Jammu and Kashmir. Located in the Kashmir Valley along the Jhelum River and Dal Lake, it is a major cultural, economic, and political hub known for its natural beauty, Mughal gardens, and traditional handicrafts. Its history as a capital and center of learning spans over a millennium, marked by successive Hindu, Muslim, Sikh, and Dogra rule, and it remains central to the ongoing Kashmir conflict.

## Timeline
- **6th century CE** — Founded as "Parvarapura" by King Pravarasena of the Gonanda dynasty, taking the name of an earlier Mauryan capital.
- **1397 CE** — Sultan Sikandar Shahmiri builds the Khanqah-e-Moula mosque, marking a major construction of the Sultanate period.
- **1586** — The Mughal Empire annexes Kashmir, making Srinagar a summer resort and building iconic gardens like Shalimar and Nishat.
- **1753** — Kashmir comes under Afghan Durrani rule; the city is described as deteriorating.
- **1819** — The Sikh Empire takes control; the old name "Srinagar" is restored.
- **1846** — Following the Treaty of Amritsar, Srinagar becomes the summer capital of the Dogra princely state of Jammu and Kashmir.
- **1947** — After partition, Indian forces are airlifted to Srinagar to defend it from Pakistani-backed invaders.
- **1952** — Srinagar is designated the summer capital of the Indian state of Jammu and Kashmir.
- **1990s** — The city becomes the focal point of a violent insurgency, leading to the displacement of Kashmiri Hindus.
- **2019** — Following the revocation of the state's special status, Srinagar becomes the summer capital of the reorganized union territory.

## Body

### Early History and Sultanate Period
According to the *Rajatarangini*, a capital named Srinagari was built by the Mauryan emperor Ashoka. The present city was founded in the 6th century CE as Parvarapura by King Pravarasena of the Gonanda dynasty. It remained the primary capital under subsequent Hindu dynasties. The Sultanate period (14th–16th centuries) saw major urban expansion, the establishment of Islamic spiritual centers like the Khanqah-e-Moula, and the development of Kashmiri handicraft industries, including shawl weaving.

### Mughal, Afghan, and Dogra Rule
The Mughals annexed Kashmir in 1586, and emperors like Jahangir and Shah Jahan developed Srinagar as a summer retreat, constructing famous Mughal gardens. After periods of Afghan and Sikh rule, the Dogra dynasty made Srinagar the summer capital of the princely state in 1846. The Dogras introduced the "Darbar Move," shifting the capital to Jammu in winter. The late 19th and early 20th centuries saw the rise of modern tourism, with houseboats built for British visitors, and the growth of the silk industry.

### Partition, Conflict, and Modern Era
Following the 1947 partition and invasion, Indian forces secured Srinagar. The city became the summer capital of Indian-administered Jammu and Kashmir in 1952. It was the epicenter of the armed insurgency in the late 1980s and early 1990s, which led to widespread violence, the exodus of Kashmiri Hindus, and a heavy military presence. While major violence subsided by the mid-1990s, the city experienced fidayeen attacks in the late 1990s/early 2000s and periodic large protests. In 2019, it became the summer capital of the newly formed union territory after the state's reorganization.

### Geography and Climate
Srinagar lies on both banks of the Jhelum River and is surrounded by lakes, including Dal, Nigeen, and Anchar. The Hokersar wetland, 14 km north, is a vital habitat for migratory birds from Siberia and Central Asia. The city has a humid subtropical climate with four seasons, featuring moderately hot summers, cool winters with snowfall, and year-round precipitation.

### Government and Demographics
The city is administered by the Srinagar Municipal Corporation (SMC). As of the 2011 census, its urban agglomeration had a population of 1,264,202, with a literacy rate of about 70%. The population is predominantly Muslim (96%), with Hindus (2.75%) mostly being migrant workers, as the local Kashmiri Hindu population largely fled in the 1990s.

### Transport and Economy
Transport includes road links via national highways, an international airport, and a railway line connecting to the Indian network via the Banihal tunnel. Water transport is mainly via shikaras on Dal Lake. The economy is driven by tourism, handicrafts, and services. The city is known for its Mughal gardens, houseboats, and traditional crafts like pashmina shawls and papier-mâché.

## Terms
- **Darbar Move:** A system introduced in 1872 where the capital administration shifts between Srinagar (summer) and Jammu (winter).
- **Khanqah:** A building designed specifically for gatherings of a Sufi order, often including a mosque and living quarters.
- **Mughal Gardens:** A style of garden layout using terraces, flowing water, and geometric patterns, exemplified in Srinagar by Shalimar and Nishat Bagh.
- **Shikara:** A traditional wooden boat used for transportation and tourism on Dal Lake.
- **Pashmina:** A fine type of cashmere wool used to make high-quality shawls, a key Kashmiri handicraft.
- **Fidayeen attack:** A suicide attack, often used by militant groups in the Kashmir conflict.
- **Pargana:** An administrative division used in historical Indian governance.
- **Vihara:** A Buddhist monastery or community hall.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The exact founding date and location of the original Mauryan capital "Srinagari" is debated, with the Rajatarangini identifying it as present-day Pandrethan, 3.5 km from modern Srinagar.
- The causes and responsibility for the 1990s exodus of Kashmiri Hindus from Srinagar are a subject of significant political and historical debate.
- The impact of the 2019 reorganization of Jammu and Kashmir and the subsequent security measures on the city's society and economy is an ongoing area of analysis.

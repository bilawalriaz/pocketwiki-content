# Portugal

## Overview
Portugal is a country in Southwestern Europe, occupying the western portion of the Iberian Peninsula and the Atlantic archipelagos of Madeira and the Azores. Its history is defined by the Reconquista, the Age of Discovery, and a transition from monarchy to dictatorship to democracy. Today, it is a developed, democratic republic with a global cultural and linguistic legacy.

## Timeline

- **711** — Muslim Umayyad Caliphate invades the Iberian Peninsula.
- **868** — County of Portugal established within the Kingdom of León.
- **1139** — Afonso Henriques claims the title of King after the Battle of Ourique.
- **1179** — Papal bull *Manifestis Probatum* recognizes Portuguese independence.
- **1297** — Treaty of Alcañices largely establishes Portugal's modern borders.
- **1386** — Treaty of Windsor signed with England, the world's oldest standing alliance.
- **1415** — Conquest of Ceuta marks the beginning of the Portuguese Empire.
- **1498** — Vasco da Gama reaches India by sea.
- **1580** — Iberian Union begins with Philip II of Spain acclaimed king of Portugal.
- **1640** — Portuguese Restoration War ends the Iberian Union.
- **1910** — Monarchy overthrown, establishing the First Republic.
- **1926** — Military coup leads to the Estado Novo dictatorship under Salazar.
- **1974** — Carnation Revolution overthrows the Estado Novo, beginning transition to democracy.
- **1986** — Portugal joins the European Economic Community (EEC).

## Body

### Etymology and Prehistory
The name "Portugal" derives from the Latin *Portus Cale*, meaning 'port of Cale', a town on the Douro River. The region has been inhabited since prehistory, with Neanderthals present until about 37,000 years ago and *Homo sapiens sapiens* arriving around 35,000 years ago. Pre-Celtic tribes and later Celts inhabited the area, with the Lusitanians occupying central inland regions.

### Antiquity to the Reconquista
The Carthaginians and then Romans controlled the region, with the Romans annexing the peninsula by the 2nd century BC. After the fall of Rome, Germanic tribes like the Suebi and Visigoths ruled until the Muslim Umayyad invasion in 711. The Christian Reconquista led to the establishment of the County of Portugal in 868. Independence was formally recognized in 1179, and Portugal's borders were largely fixed by 1297 after the capture of the Algarve.

### Age of Discovery and Empire
Following a succession crisis, the House of Aviz came to power in 1385. Portugal pioneered the Age of Discovery, establishing a global maritime empire. Key events include Vasco da Gama's sea route to India (1498) and Pedro Álvares Cabral's claim of Brazil (1500). The Treaty of Tordesillas (1494) divided new territories with Spain. Portugal became a major participant in the Atlantic slave trade.

### From Iberian Union to Democracy
A succession crisis in 1580 led to the Iberian Union with Spain, which ended with the Portuguese Restoration in 1640. The subsequent period saw economic stagnation and the devastating 1755 Lisbon earthquake. Napoleon's invasion in 1807 forced the royal court to flee to Brazil. The 19th century was marked by political instability, leading to the monarchy's overthrow in 1910. The First Republic was unstable and was replaced in 1926 by the authoritarian Estado Novo dictatorship. This regime ended with the 1974 Carnation Revolution, which initiated a transition to democracy and the dissolution of its colonial empire.

### Government and Politics
Portugal is a unitary, semi-presidential representative democratic republic. Its four sovereignty organs are the president, government, parliament (Assembly of the Republic), and judiciary. The president is the head of state, while the prime minister is the head of government. The country is highly centralized, divided into seven mainland regions and two autonomous regions (Azores and Madeira).

### Economy and Society
Portugal has a high-income, advanced economy based on services, industry, and tourism. It is a member of the European Union and uses the euro. The population is aging, with a low fertility rate (1.4 in 2025) and a high proportion of elderly citizens. Historically a country of emigration, it has become a net recipient of immigrants since 1993. Portuguese is the official language, and the country has a secular state with a Catholic majority.

## Terms
- **Reconquista**: The centuries-long series of campaigns by Christian kingdoms against Muslim rule in the Iberian Peninsula.
- **Age of Discovery**: The 15th and 16th centuries when Portuguese explorers pioneered maritime routes to Africa, Asia, and the Americas.
- **Estado Novo**: The authoritarian, corporatist dictatorship that ruled Portugal from 1933 to 1974.
- **Carnation Revolution**: The largely peaceful military coup of April 25, 1974, that overthrew the Estado Novo regime.
- **Autonomous Region**: A self-governing region of Portugal, specifically the Azores and Madeira, with their own political assemblies and governments.
- **Assembly of the Republic (Assembleia da República)**: The unicameral national parliament of Portugal.
- **Treaty of Windsor**: The 1386 alliance between Portugal and England, recognized as the world's oldest active alliance.
- **Saudade**: A deep emotional state of nostalgic longing, often cited as a core element of Portuguese identity and culture.

## Debates and open questions
- The historicity of the Battle of Covadonga (c. 722), often cited as the start of the Reconquista, and its leader Pelagius, is unestablished.
- The etymology of "Cale," the root of "Portugal," is uncertain, with no consensus on its origin.
- Portugal's sustained economic divergence from Western Europe despite comparable family values and high state capacity is a subject of scholarly analysis, with partial recovery only beginning in the mid-20th century.

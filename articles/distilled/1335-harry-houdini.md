# Harry Houdini

## Overview
Harry Houdini (born Erik Weisz, 1874–1926) was an American escapologist, illusionist, and stunt performer whose death-defying escape acts made him one of the most famous entertainers of the early 20th century. Beyond his stage persona, he was a fierce debunker of fraudulent spiritualists, a pioneering aviator, and a key figure in organizing the global magic community.

## Body

### Early Life and Career Beginnings
Erik Weisz was born in Budapest to a Jewish family and immigrated to the U.S. in 1878 to escape antisemitic discrimination. His father, Rabbi Mayer Sámuel Weisz, lost his position in Appleton, Wisconsin, leading the family into poverty. Young Ehrich took various jobs and made his public debut as a trapeze artist at age nine. He adopted the stage name "Harry Houdini" in homage to French magician Jean-Eugène Robert-Houdin.

### Rise to Fame and Signature Escapes
Houdini's breakthrough came in 1899 when manager Martin Beck recognized his potential in escape acts. By 1900, he was performing across Europe, challenging police forces to restrain him with handcuffs and shackles. His most famous stunts included the Daily Mirror handcuff challenge (1904), the Milk Can Escape (1908), and the Chinese Water Torture Cell (1912). He also performed dangerous publicity stunts like the suspended straitjacket escape and buried-alive acts.

### Spiritualism Debunking
In the 1920s, Houdini dedicated himself to exposing fraudulent spiritualists and mediums. He served on a Scientific American committee offering a cash prize for genuine supernatural demonstrations, which was never claimed. His investigations targeted figures like Mina Crandon ("Margery") and Joaquín Argamasilla. This work strained his friendship with Sir Arthur Conan Doyle, a spiritualism believer who came to view Houdini as an enemy.

### Film and Aviation Careers
Houdini made several films including *The Master Mystery* (1918) and *The Grim Game* (1919), though his film ventures were financially unsuccessful. He also became an aviation pioneer, making one of the first powered flights in Australia in 1910, but abandoned flying after returning to England.

### Death and Legacy
Houdini died on October 31, 1926, from peritonitis, possibly triggered by abdominal punches he received days earlier. He was buried in New York's Machpelah Cemetery with the Society of American Magicians crest on his grave. His extensive collection of magic artifacts and papers was distributed among various institutions, including the Library of Congress and the Harry Ransom Center.

## Terms
- **Escapology**: The art of escaping from restraints or enclosed spaces, often under dangerous conditions
- **Vaudeville**: A theatrical genre of variety entertainment popular in the U.S. from the 1890s to 1930s
- **Spiritualism**: A belief system claiming communication with the dead through mediums
- **Society of American Magicians (S.A.M.)**: The world's largest magic organization, founded in 1902
- **Chinese Water Torture Cell**: Houdini's signature escape where he was suspended upside-down in a water-filled glass cabinet
- **Milk Can Escape**: An escape act where Houdini was handcuffed and sealed inside an oversized milk can filled with water
- **Peritonitis**: Inflammation of the abdominal lining, often fatal before antibiotics

## Debates and Open Questions
The circumstances of Houdini's death remain debated. While officially attributed to peritonitis from appendicitis, some speculate that abdominal trauma from a Montreal incident contributed. His widow Bess conducted annual séances for ten years hoping to contact him, claiming success in 1929 through medium Arthur Ford, though she later admitted it was faked. In 2007, his great-nephew proposed exhuming his body to investigate claims of murder by spiritualists, but no legal action was taken.

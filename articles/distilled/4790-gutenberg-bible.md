# Gutenberg Bible

## Overview
The Gutenberg Bible, printed by Johannes Gutenberg in Mainz during the 1450s, is the earliest major book in Europe produced using mass-produced metal movable type. It marks the beginning of the "Gutenberg Revolution" and the age of printed books in the West, valued for its high aesthetic quality and profound historical significance.

## Timeline
- **c. 1450** — Preparation of the Gutenberg Bible likely begins.
- **1454** — First finished copies become available; Gutenberg's 31-line Indulgence is printed by October 22.
- **March 1455** — Future Pope Pius II reports seeing pages displayed in Frankfurt.
- **1456** — Earliest visible print date on a surviving copy is August 15.
- **1921** — Damaged copy dismantled and sold as "Noble Fragments."
- **1978** — Last sale of a complete copy for $2.4 million. |
- **2009** — A two-volume edition is stolen from Moscow State University.
- **2013** — The stolen Moscow copy is recovered.

## Body

### Text and Production
The book is an edition of the Latin Vulgate, St. Jerome's translation of the Bible. Gutenberg's process, which he called *Das Werk der Bücher* ("the work of the books"), involved dampening paper for ink absorption, pressing it onto inked movable type, and re-inking the type for each sheet. This efficient relief printing method enabled mass production.

### Physical Characteristics
The Bible was printed on "Royal" paper (42 cm × 60 cm full sheet) with a width-to-height ratio of approximately 1:1.4. A complete copy comprises 1,288 pages, typically bound in two volumes. The paper, made of linen fibers, likely originated from Piedmont, Italy. Gutenberg developed a durable, oil-based, varnish-like ink with high metallic content (copper, lead, titanium) that adhered to metal type, unlike traditional water-based manuscript inks.

### Type and Design
The text uses blackletter type styles (Textura and Schwabacher). Gutenberg employed justification—aligning text vertically on both sides of a column—by varying character widths and spacing. The print run evolved: early pages had 40 lines, later increased to 42 lines per page to save paper, resulting in two distinct settings for some folios. The total number of master characters needed was 290, including ligatures.

### Rubrication and Binding
While some rubrics (headings) were initially printed, this was soon abandoned, leaving spaces for hand-added decoration. The spacious margins allowed for optional illuminated decoration, paid for by the buyer. Vellum copies were more expensive and often more highly decorated. Nine copies retain original 15th-century bindings, mostly from Mainz or Erfurt.

### Distribution and Ownership
The Bible sold out quickly, with copies reaching as far as England. A price of 30 florins (about 100g of gold) made them cheaper than manuscript Bibles but still unaffordable for most. Buyers likely included monasteries, universities, and wealthy individuals, often for donation to religious institutions.

### Influence and Legacy
The Gutenberg Bible provided the model for several later printed Bibles, including the 36-line Bible and the first Eggestein Bible. It also influenced the official Clementine Vulgate edition commissioned by the Papacy in the late 16th century.

### Surviving Copies and Fragments
As of 2009, 49 copies are known to exist, with 25 being substantially complete. Fragments represent about 16 additional copies. Most are now held by university libraries and major institutions. The first copy reached North America in 1847. Digital projects, like those at Keio University, have created high-quality reproductions.

## Terms
- **Gutenberg Revolution:** The period of rapid change in book production and culture initiated by Gutenberg's movable-type printing.
- **Latin Vulgate:** The Latin translation of the Bible by St. Jerome, which served as the standard text for the Gutenberg Bible.
- **Movable type:** A system of printing using individual, reusable metal characters for each letter and symbol.
- **Relief printing:** A printing method where the raised surface of the type is inked and pressed onto paper.
- **Blackletter:** A style of medieval script and type characterized by dense, angular strokes.
- **Justification:** The alignment of text to create straight vertical edges on both the left and right sides of a column.
- **Rubrication:** The addition of headings, initials, or other decorative elements in red ink by hand after printing.
- **Vellum:** A high-quality writing material made from animal skin, more expensive than paper.
- **Incunable:** A book printed in Europe before the year 1501.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The exact number of copies printed is uncertain, with historical sources citing either 158 or 180, while modern scholars estimate between 160 and 185.
- The precise duration of the printing process is unknown.
- The attribution of the illumination in the Princeton University copy to the "Master of the Playing Cards" is speculative; only the use of a shared model book is certain.

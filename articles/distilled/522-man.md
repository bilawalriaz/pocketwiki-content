# Man

## Overview
A man is an adult male human, biologically defined by the XY karyotype typically resulting from a Y-chromosome sperm fertilizing an ovum. The SRY gene on the Y chromosome triggers testes development and androgen production, driving primary sex characteristics (testes, penis) and secondary traits like greater muscle mass, facial hair, and a deeper voice. Historically, gender roles have positioned men as dominant in patriarchal structures, granting privileges but also imposing burdens: men are over-represented in dangerous labor, military conscription, violence statistics, and occupational fatalities. Contemporary movements critique these roles, advocating for gender equality while addressing men's specific health and social disparities, such as lower life expectancy and educational attainment gaps in developed nations.

## Timeline
- **c. 3400–3000 BC** — Earliest potential recorded name of a man (Kushim) appears in Sumerian writing at Uruk.
- **c. 3100 BC** — Earliest confirmed names of men (Gal-Sal, En-pap X, Sukkalgir) recorded.
- **1751** — Carl Linnaeus first uses the Mars symbol (♂) to denote the male sex.
- **20th–21st centuries** — Shift in family roles: men take more active roles in child-rearing; same-sex marriage and adoption rights expand in many countries.
- **2016** — Systemic review estimates 0.256% of people self-identify as female-to-male transgender.
- **2017** — Minnesota student survey finds roughly twice as many female-assigned adolescents self-identify as transgender compared to male-assigned.
- **2020** — U.S. data shows ten times as many men died on the job as women; global male literacy at 90% vs. 87% for women.

## Body

### Etymology
The English word "man" derives from Old English *mann*, which originally meant "person" or "human being" regardless of sex. The distinct term for an adult male was *wer* (surviving in "werewolf" and "wergild"). *Mann* narrowed to mean "adult male" in Middle English, replacing *wer*. The root traces to Proto-Indo-European *man-* (cf. Sanskrit *manu-*, Slavic *mǫž*).

### Biology
**Sex Determination and Differentiation**
Humans inherit an X chromosome from the mother and either an X or Y from the father. A Y-carrying sperm yields an XY (male) karyotype. The **SRY gene** (Sex-determining Region Y) on the Y chromosome initiates testes development; testes then secrete hormones (primarily testosterone and anti-Müllerian hormone) that drive male differentiation—Wolffian duct development, penis formation, scrotal closure, and inhibition of Müllerian ducts. Female differentiation is gonad-independent.

**Primary and Secondary Sex Characteristics**
*Primary* characteristics are present at birth and directly involved in reproduction: testes (sperm production), epididymides (sperm storage), vasa deferentia, ejaculatory ducts, accessory glands (seminal fluid), penis, and scrotum. The study of this system is **andrology**.
*Secondary* characteristics emerge at puberty via androgen stimulation: broadened shoulders, increased body hair, enlarged larynx (Adam’s apple), deeper voice, greater height (~10% taller on average), higher lean/bone mass, lower fat mass, larger waist-to-hip ratio, and longer ring finger relative to index finger.

**Health Disparities**
Men fare worse in specific contexts: 81% of violent war deaths (1955–2002, 13 countries) were male; men dominate dangerous occupations and occupational fatalities (10:1 ratio in the U.S., 2020); male life expectancy is lower than female in all recorded countries. Social norms linking masculinity to provision and aggression contribute to stress, alcohol use, and mortality spikes during economic crises. Medical encounters show doctors spend less time with men and may underrate their symptoms.

### Sexuality and Gender Identity
Most men are heterosexual; significant minorities are homosexual or bisexual. Most are **cisgender** (gender identity aligns with male sex assigned at birth). **Trans men** have a male gender identity despite female sex assignment at birth; some pursue hormone therapy or surgery. **Intersex men** possess sex characteristics not fitting typical male biology. A 2016 review estimated 0.256% self-identify as female-to-male transgender; a 2017 Minnesota survey found female-assigned adolescents identified as transgender at roughly twice the rate of male-assigned adolescents.

### Social Role
**Masculinity**
Masculinity is the set of traits associated with men/boys; **manhood** embodies mature masculinity. It is socially constructed but may have biological influences (debated). Both sexes can exhibit masculine traits, but men face stronger stigma for feminine traits, often manifesting as homophobia. Cross-culturally, marriage is the classic threshold from boyhood to manhood; the "triple Ps" (protecting, providing, procreating) remain common markers.

**Relationships**
Male friendships tend to center on shared activities rather than self-disclosure. In heterosexual romance, men are traditionally expected to initiate, plan, and propose.

**Status**
Masculinity confers social status akin to wealth or class. In Western culture, greater masculinity correlates with higher status. **Male privilege** grants more rights/power even without legal codification. This creates "gender-role strain"—pressure to conform to rigid norms.

### History
The earliest potential recorded individual name is **Kushim** (c. 3400–3000 BC, Sumerian Uruk), though it may be a title. The earliest confirmed personal names are **Gal-Sal** and two slaves, **En-pap X** and **Sukkalgir** (c. 3100 BC).

### Fatherhood
Fathers may be biological or adoptive. The 20th–21st centuries saw a shift toward active paternal involvement. Same-sex marriage enables male couples to raise children via adoption/surrogacy. Single fatherhood is rising but remains one-third the rate of single motherhood. In patriarchal societies, men hold "head of household" status and associated privileges.

### Work
Men historically held strenuous, prestigious, or dangerous jobs closed to women. Modern men work longer hours on average, impacting family time. Conscription remains overwhelmingly male-only (only 10 countries include women). Men continue to dominate hazardous occupations and workplace fatalities.

### Education
Men traditionally received more education via single-sex systems. Universal education is not yet global. In many developed nations, men now lag behind women in attainment. Men are more likely to be university faculty. Global male literacy (2020): 90% vs. 87% female; sub-Saharan Africa (72% male) and southwest Asia lag.

### Rights
Men generally hold more legal/cultural rights; misogyny exceeds misandry. Exceptions: male victims of childhood sexual abuse (1 in 6) receive less support; male rape and domestic violence (men are half of heterosexual couple victims) are stigmatized. **Circumcision** is contested as a rights violation. The **fathers' rights movement** advocates for separated fathers. The **men's movement** encompasses pro-feminist (men's liberation) and anti-feminist (manosphere) factions.

### Clothing
Men's clothing prioritizes functionality across formal (suits, shirts, trousers, jackets) and casual (t-shirts, jeans, shorts, swimwear) categories. Cultural and regional traditions drive stylistic diversity.

### Gender Symbol
The Mars symbol (♂) represents the male sex. Linnaeus introduced it for sex in 1751. It matches the planetary symbol for Mars. While often interpreted as Mars' shield and spear, scholar Stearn calls this "fanciful"; evidence favors derivation from Greek *θρ* (contraction of *θοῦρος*, an epithet for Mars).

## Terms
- ****SRY gene**** — Sex-determining Region Y; a gene on the Y chromosome that initiates testes development in the embryo.
- ****Karyotype**** — The number and appearance of chromosomes in a cell; typical male is XY.
- ****Primary sex characteristics**** — Reproductive organs present at birth (e.g., testes, penis).
- ****Secondary sex characteristics**** — Traits emerging at puberty (e.g., facial hair, deeper voice) not directly part of the reproductive system.
- ****Andrology**** — The medical specialty concerned with male reproduction and associated organs.
- ****Cisgender**** — Gender identity aligning with sex assigned at birth.
- ****Trans men**** — Individuals assigned female at birth who identify as men.
- ****Intersex**** — Having sex characteristics (chromosomes, gonads, anatomy) that do not fit typical binary notions of male/female bodies.
- ****Gender-role strain**** — Psychological stress from societal pressure to conform to rigid masculine norms.
- ****Manosphere**** — Collection of anti-feminist online communities focused on men's issues.

## Debates and open questions
- **Biology vs. culture in masculinity**: To what extent are masculine behaviors biologically influenced versus socially constructed?
- **Health paradox**: Men hold structural power yet suffer worse outcomes in violence, occupational death, and life expectancy; how do social norms (provision, aggression) mediate this?
- **Transgender demographics**: Why do female-assigned adolescents identify as transgender at roughly twice the rate of male-assigned adolescents (per 2017 Minnesota data)?
- **Circumcision ethics**: Is non-therapeutic circumcision a human rights violation or a protected religious/cultural practice?
- **Educational reversal**: Causes and implications of men lagging behind women in educational attainment in developed nations.
- **Male victim support**: Why do male victims of sexual abuse and domestic violence receive less support and face greater stigma?

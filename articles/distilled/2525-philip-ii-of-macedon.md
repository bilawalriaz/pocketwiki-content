# Philip II of Macedon

## Overview
Philip II of Macedon (382–336 BC) was the king who transformed Macedon from a peripheral kingdom into the dominant power of Greece in less than 25 years. Through military innovation, diplomacy, and relentless campaigning, he unified Greece under Macedonian hegemony and laid the groundwork for his son Alexander the Great's conquest of the Persian Empire.

## Timeline
- **382/383 BC** — Philip born, youngest son of King Amyntas III.
- **c. 368–365 BC** — Held as a hostage in Thebes; educated by Epaminondas.
- **359 BC** — Becomes king after death of brother Perdiccas III.
- **358 BC** — Defeats Paeonians and Illyrians, securing borders.
- **357 BC** — Captures Amphipolis; marries Olympias of Epirus.
- **356 BC** — Son Alexander born; conquers Crenides (renamed Philippi).
- **352 BC** — Wins Battle of Crocus Field in Thessaly; becomes archon of Thessalian League.
- **348 BC** — Captures and destroys Olynthus, dissolving the Chalcidian League.
- **338 BC** — Defeats Athens and Thebes at Battle of Chaeronea.
- **338/337 BC** — Establishes the League of Corinth; elected hegemon.
- **336 BC** — Assassinated at Aigai; succeeded by Alexander.

## Body

### Rise to Power and Army Reforms
Philip seized the throne in 359 BC after his brother's death in battle against the Illyrians. He first secured Macedon's borders by defeating Paeonian, Thracian, and Illyrian threats. His key innovation was reforming the Macedonian army: he expanded the cavalry from 600 to 4,000, introduced the sarissa (a long pike) to create the Macedonian phalanx infantry formation, and implemented combined arms tactics with siege engines. These changes, along with a system of promotions and rewards, created a professional, disciplined force.

### Expansion and Diplomacy
Philip used a mix of warfare, diplomacy, and marriage alliances to expand. He conquered key cities like Amphipolis and Pydna, gaining access to gold mines at Mount Pangaion to fund campaigns. He intervened in the Third Sacred War (356–346 BC), defeating the Phocians and gaining control of Thessaly. He systematically subdued the Balkans and Greek coastal cities, destroying Olynthus in 348 BC. His diplomatic marriages to seven royal women from Illyria, Epirus, Thessaly, and Thrace secured alliances.

### Hegemony over Greece
After failed sieges of Perinthus and Byzantium, Philip reasserted authority by defeating the combined forces of Athens and Thebes at Chaeronea in 338 BC. This victory led to the formation of the League of Corinth, a Greek federation with Philip as elected hegemon and commander-in-chief for a planned invasion of Persia. He began preparations in 336 BC by sending an advance army into Asia Minor.

### Assassination and Succession
Philip was assassinated in October 336 BC at Aigai by Pausanias of Orestis, a royal bodyguard. Ancient accounts differ on the motive: Aristotle cited a personal grievance against Philip's uncle-in-law Attalus, while later historians like Cleitarchus elaborated a story of romantic jealousy. Some sources implicate Philip's wife Olympias or son Alexander, though modern scholars find this speculative. His death led to the immediate succession of Alexander, who continued the Persian campaign.

### Tomb and Legacy
Philip's tomb was discovered in 1977 at Vergina (ancient Aigai). Tomb II, containing rich artifacts and remains matching Philip's recorded injuries (a damaged right eye and leg wound), is widely identified as his. He founded cities like Philippi and Philippopolis and received honors such as altars and statues in Greek cities, though he was not deified like Alexander.

## Terms
- **Macedonian phalanx**: A dense infantry formation of soldiers armed with the sarissa, a long pike.
- **Sarissa**: A long pike (c. 5–6 meters) used by Macedonian infantry.
- **Hegemon**: Leader or commander-in-chief of a league or alliance.
- **League of Corinth**: A federation of Greek states formed in 338/337 BC under Macedonian leadership.
- **Third Sacred War**: A conflict (356–346 BC) involving the Phocians, Thebans, and others over control of Delphi.
- **Chalcidian League**: A confederation of Greek cities in Chalcidice, dissolved after Philip's destruction of Olynthus in 348 BC.
- **Heroon**: A shrine dedicated to a hero, often a deceased ancestor or notable figure.
- **Combined arms tactics**: The coordinated use of different military branches (infantry, cavalry, siege engines) in battle.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Assassination motive**: The personal grievance of Pausanias (per Aristotle) versus later embellishments involving jealousy or royal conspiracy remain debated.
- **Tomb identification**: While Tomb II at Vergina is generally accepted as Philip's, a 2015 study proposed Tomb I, a claim rejected by Greek authorities based on archaeological evidence.
- **Legacy of conquest**: Some historians speculate that Philip, being older and more pragmatic, might have pursued more limited conquests in Persia compared to Alexander's expansive campaigns.

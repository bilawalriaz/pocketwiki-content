# Guwahati

## Overview
Guwahati is the largest city in northeastern India and the principal gateway to the region. Situated on the south bank of the Brahmaputra River, it serves as the commercial, educational, and administrative hub of Assam, with the state capital, Dispur, located within its metropolitan area. The city's history spans from ancient mythological origins to its modern role as a rapidly growing metropolis facing challenges like flooding and pollution.

## Timeline
- **c. 2nd century BCE – 1st century CE** — Ambari excavations trace the city's period in the Shunga-Kushana era.
- **7th century CE** — Descriptions by Xuanzang reveal the city stretched for about 15 km during King Bhaskaravarman's reign.
- **1577 A.D.** — An inscription mentions ruler Dununtrarāi donated a tank in Pragjyotishpura.
- **1633–1681** — The region came under intermittent Mughal occupation.
- **1824** — Guwahati became the headquarters of the Ahom governor (Borphukan) of Lower Assam.
- **1826** — After the Treaty of Yandabo, Assam was ceded to the British, and Guwahati became the seat of administration.
- **1874** — The British administrative headquarters were moved from Guwahati to Shillong.
- **1 March 1948** — The Gauhati High Court was promulgated.
- **1959–1962** — The Saraighat Bridge was constructed, connecting Northeast India to the rest of the country.
- **1972** — Following the creation of Meghalaya, the Assam capital was shifted to Dispur in Guwahati.
- **1974** — The Guwahati Municipal Corporation was constituted.
- **1994** — The Indian Institute of Technology Guwahati was established.

## Body

### Historical Foundations
The area's legendary history begins with the mythical king Naraka, progenitor of the Kamarupa dynasty. Ancient cities of Pragjyotishpura and Durjaya were its capitals. Archaeological evidence from Ambari shows the city was economically important from the 2nd century BCE to the 9th–11th century CE. It was fortified and served as a key administrative center under the Ahom kingdom until British rule began in 1826.

### Geography and Urban Form
Guwahati lies between the Brahmaputra River and the Shillong plateau foothills. Its urban form radiates from a central core (Pan Bazaar, Paltan Bazaar) with major growth corridors extending south along the Guwahati-Shillong Road, west towards the airport and Saraighat Bridge, and east towards Narengi. The city's natural topography, including wetlands like Dipor Bil, has been altered by rapid urbanization, exacerbating flooding.

### Administration and Infrastructure
Dispur, within Guwahati, is the seat of the Assam government. The city is administered by the Guwahati Municipal Corporation (60 wards) and planned by the Guwahati Metropolitan Development Authority (GMDA). Key infrastructure includes the Lokpriya Gopinath Bordoloi International Airport (the busiest in the Northeast), the Saraighat Bridge, and major railway stations like Guwahati and Kamakhya Junction. The city is connected by National Highways 27, 17, and 15.

### Economy and Education
Guwahati's economy is driven by petroleum (Guwahati Refinery, India's first public sector refinery, established 1962) and tea. The Guwahati Tea Auction Centre is the world's largest CTC tea auction center. Pandu Port is a major riverine terminal. The city is the educational hub of the Northeast, hosting institutions like IIT Guwahati (1994), Gauhati University, and Cotton University.

### Modern Challenges
Rapid population growth (estimated 1.1 million in 2020) has led to slum proliferation and inflation, particularly for food items. Alteration of drainage channels and wetlands has made the city highly susceptible to urban flooding. In 2023, Guwahati was ranked the world's second most polluted city by IQAir.

## Terms
- **Kamarupa**: The ancient kingdom whose capitals included Pragjyotishpura, located in modern Guwahati.
- **Borphukan**: The Ahom governor of Lower Assam, headquartered in Guwahati until 1824.
- **Saraighat Bridge**: A rail-cum-road bridge over the Brahmaputra, completed in 1962, crucial for connecting the Northeast.
- **Dispur**: A neighbourhood in Guwahati that became the capital of Assam in 1972.
- **Guwahati Municipal Corporation (GMC)**: The local governing body for the city, established in 1974.
- **Guwahati Metropolitan Development Authority (GMDA)**: The planning body for the greater metropolitan area, established in 1992.
- **CTC tea**: Crush, Tear, Curl tea; a method of processing black tea, for which the Guwahati auction center is the world's largest.
- **Pandu Port**: An ancient river port on the Brahmaputra, now a major cargo and passenger terminal.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The exact historical boundaries and continuous habitation of the ancient city of Pragjyotishpura are subjects of ongoing archaeological study.
- The primary causes of Guwahati's severe urban flooding are debated, with experts attributing it to human interference (encroachment, concretization) disrupting natural drainage versus other factors.
- The feasibility and timeline for the proposed metro rail project to alleviate traffic congestion remain uncertain.

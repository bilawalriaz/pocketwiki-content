# Hawaii

## Overview
Hawaii is a U.S. island state in the central Pacific Ocean, consisting of 137 volcanic islands. It is unique among U.S. states for its Polynesian cultural heritage, its history as an independent kingdom before annexation, and its extreme demographic diversity. Its strategic location has made it a major military hub and a global tourism destination, but its isolation also creates high living costs and environmental vulnerabilities.

## Timeline
- **c. 1000–1200 CE** — Polynesian settlers arrive, establishing independent chiefdoms.
- **1778** — British Captain James Cook is the first recorded European to visit.
- **1810** — Kamehameha I unifies the islands into the Kingdom of Hawaiʻi.
- **1893** — American and European businessmen overthrow Queen Liliʻuokalani.
- **1898** — The Republic of Hawaiʻi is annexed by the United States via the Newlands Resolution.
- **Dec 7, 1941** — Japan attacks Pearl Harbor, bringing the U.S. into World War II.
- **Aug 21, 1959** — Hawaii is admitted as the 50th U.S. state.
- **1993** — U.S. Congress passes the Apology Resolution for the 1893 overthrow.

## Body

### Geography and Environment
Hawaii is an archipelago of 137 volcanic islands spanning 1,500 miles, formed by a hotspot in the Pacific Plate. The eight main islands are, from northwest to southeast: Niʻihau, Kauaʻi, Oʻahu, Molokaʻi, Lānaʻi, Kahoʻolawe, Maui, and Hawaiʻi (the "Big Island"). The state's tallest mountain, Mauna Kea, is 13,796 ft above sea level but over 33,000 ft from its oceanic base. The climate is tropical, moderated by trade winds, with distinct wet and dry seasons. Hawaii has more endangered species than any other U.S. state. Major environmental threats include climate change-induced drought, sea-level rise, and wildfires, compounded by the impacts of tourism and historical military activity.

### History
Polynesian settlers arrived between 1000 and 1200 CE, developing a caste-based society. After Captain Cook's 1778 arrival, foreign contact led to a devastating population decline from introduced diseases. In 1810, Kamehameha I unified the islands into a sovereign kingdom. American Protestant missionaries arrived in 1820, influencing politics and culture. In 1887, white businessmen forced King Kalākaua to sign the "Bayonet Constitution," stripping royal authority. In 1893, a U.S.-backed coup overthrew Queen Liliʻuokalani. The U.S. annexed Hawaii in 1898, making it a territory. The attack on Pearl Harbor in 1941 was a pivotal WWII event. Hawaii achieved statehood in 1959. The 1993 Apology Resolution acknowledged the overthrow was illegal, fueling the ongoing Hawaiian sovereignty movement.

### Demographics and Culture
Hawaii is one of the most demographically diverse U.S. states, with no racial majority. The largest ancestry groups are Asian (37.2%), multiracial (25.3%), and white (22.9%). Native Hawaiians and other Pacific Islanders make up 10.8% of the population. The culture is a fusion of Native Hawaiian, Asian, and North American influences. English and Hawaiian are official languages, and Hawaiʻi Creole English ("Pidgin") is widely spoken. Traditional practices like hula and lūʻau persist alongside modern customs. The state has the largest Buddhist community in the U.S. and a long history of LGBTQ+ identities, such as the traditional *māhū* (third gender).

### Economy
Hawaii's economy transitioned from a plantation economy (sugar, pineapple) to one dominated by tourism and military defense. Tourism is the largest industry, contributing about a quarter of the gross state product. The U.S. military hosts the Pacific Fleet and 75,000 Defense Department employees. Agriculture remains significant, with exports like coffee and macadamia nuts. The state has one of the highest costs of living in the U.S. due to its isolation and shipping requirements, including the Jones Act. Despite this, it has a high median income and long life expectancy.

## Terms
*   **Archipelago:** A chain or cluster of islands.
*   **Hotspot:** A volcanic area formed by a stationary plume of magma beneath a moving tectonic plate, creating a chain of volcanoes.
*   **Aliʻi:** The hereditary nobility or chiefs in ancient Hawaiian society.
*   **Kapu:** The ancient Hawaiian system of laws and taboos, often related to spiritual beliefs and social regulations.
*   **Heiau:** A traditional Hawaiian temple or sacred place.
*   **ʻOkina:** A glottal stop consonant in the Hawaiian language, represented by a symbol (ʻ).
*   **Kahakō:** A macron over a vowel in Hawaiian orthography indicating a long vowel sound.
*   **Jones Act:** A U.S. federal law requiring goods shipped between U.S. ports to be transported on U.S.-built, owned, and crewed ships.
*   **Māhū:** A traditional Native Hawaiian term for a person of a third gender, with specific social and spiritual roles.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Date of Settlement:** The exact timing of the first Polynesian settlement is debated, with estimates ranging from the 3rd century CE to as late as 900–1200 CE.
*   **Legality of Overthrow and Annexation:** The 1893 overthrow and 1898 annexation are viewed by the Hawaiian sovereignty movement as illegal acts of war and occupation, a position acknowledged by the 1993 U.S. Apology Resolution. The movement seeks redress and some form of self-governance, from federal recognition to full independence.
*   **Impact of Tourism:** While economically vital, tourism is criticized for commodifying Hawaiian culture, causing environmental damage, and creating social strain, leading to debates about sustainable models and limits on visitor numbers.

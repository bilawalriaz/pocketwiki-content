# The Rolling Stones

## Overview

The Rolling Stones are an English rock band formed in 1962, active for over six decades and among the most popular, influential, and enduring acts of the rock era. In the early 1960s, they pioneered the gritty, rhythmically driven sound that came to define hard rock. Rooted in blues and early rock and roll, they were at the forefront of the British Invasion in 1964 and became identified with the youthful counterculture of the 1960s. Their first stable line-up was vocalist Mick Jagger, guitarist Keith Richards, multi-instrumentalist Brian Jones, bassist Bill Wyman, and drummer Charlie Watts, with keyboardist Ian Stewart side-lined from the official line-up by manager Andrew Loog Oldham (though retained as road manager and session/touring keyboardist until his death in 1985). The Jagger–Richards partnership became the band's primary songwriting and creative force.

## Body

### Formation and early success (1962–1964)

Mick Jagger and Keith Richards were childhood friends in Dartford, Kent, losing contact in 1954 when the Jagger family moved. They reunited on 17 October 1961 at Dartford railway station, where Jagger carried Chuck Berry and Muddy Waters records, revealing a shared interest. In March 1962, their group the Blues Boys contacted Alexis Korner's Blues Incorporated; they met Brian Jones, Ian Stewart, and Charlie Watts at the Ealing Jazz Club. Jones, having left Blues Incorporated, advertised for bandmates in *Jazz News* in May 1962. Jones named the band after a Muddy Waters track, "Rollin' Stone," and was its "uncontested leader" in early years.

The settled line-up of Jagger, Jones, Richards, Stewart, Wyman, and Watts played their first gig on 2 February 1963. Oldham, then 19, became manager in May 1963, promoting the Stones as a "raunchy, gamy, unpredictable bunch of undesirables" contrasting with the Beatles. Decca Records gave the band a favourable contract: royalty rates three times the norm, full artistic control, and ownership of master tapes. Their first single, a cover of Chuck Berry's "Come On," reached No. 21 on the UK Singles Chart. Their first US tour in June 1964 was "a disaster," but sessions at Chess Studios produced their first UK No. 1, "It's All Over Now."

### Worldwide fame (1965–1967)

"The Last Time" (1965) was the first Jagger/Richards song to reach No. 1 in the UK; Richards called it "the bridge into thinking about writing for the Stones." "(I Can't Get No) Satisfaction," recorded in May 1965 with a fuzzbox guitar riff, was their first US No. 1. *Aftermath* (1966), the first all-original album, featured Jones' expanded instrumentation: sitar on "Paint It Black," dulcimer on "Lady Jane," marimbas on "Under My Thumb." In early 1967, authorities targeted the band over drug use after the *News of the World* ran a feature on pop stars and drugs. A raid on Richards' home, Redlands, led to charges; Jagger received a three-month sentence and Richards a year, both overturned or reduced on appeal. *Their Satanic Majesties Request* (1967), recorded while awaiting court cases, drew unfavourable reviews as a poor imitation of the Beatles' *Sgt. Pepper*. Oldham left during the sessions.

### Jones' departure, Taylor joins (1968–1970)

*Beggars Banquet* (1968) marked a return to rhythm and blues roots and began their collaboration with producer Jimmy Miller. Controversy over the cover—a public toilet with graffiti—delayed release for six months. Jones, increasingly hindered by drug use, left the band in June 1969, saying "I've left, and if I want to I can come back." He drowned on 3 July 1969. The band played a Hyde Park tribute concert two days later before an estimated 250,000 fans, where stage manager Sam Cutler introduced them as "the Greatest Rock and Roll Band in the World." *Let It Bleed* (1969) featured "Gimme Shelter" with guest vocals by Merry Clayton. The 1969 US tour ended at the Altamont Free Concert, where a fan, Meredith Hunter, was stabbed and beaten to death by Hells Angels providing security. In 1970, the band formed Rolling Stones Records; John Pasche designed the "tongue and lips" logo following Jagger's suggestion to copy the stuck-out tongue of the Hindu goddess Kali.

### Continued success and tax exile (1971–1973)

*Sticky Fingers* (1971), their first album on their own label, featured an Andy Warhol cover with a functioning zipper. Following advice from financial manager Prince Rupert Loewenstein, the band left England as tax exiles, moving to the South of France. *Exile on Main St.* (1972), recorded in the basement of Richards' rented Villa Nellcôte using the Rolling Stones Mobile Studio, reached No. 1 in both the UK and US and is now accepted as one of their best albums. *Goats Head Soup* (1973), containing the worldwide hit "Angie," was the first in a string of commercially successful but critically tepidly received albums. A drug warrant for Richards in France complicated plans for a Pacific tour in 1973.

### Wood replaces Taylor (1974–1977)

Taylor quit at the end of 1974, feeling like a "junior citizen in the band of jaded veterans." Recording sessions for *Black and Blue* (1976) served as auditions; Ronnie Wood, who had already contributed to "It's Only Rock 'n Roll," was chosen. Wood joined for the 1975 Tour of the Americas, a factor in the disbandment of Faces; he was a salaried employee until the early 1990s. In February 1977, Richards was charged with importing narcotics into Canada after heroin was found in his room; he ultimately received a suspended sentence and was ordered to play two charity concerts. This episode strengthened his resolve to stop using heroin and ended his relationship with Anita Pallenberg. By 1977, after punk rock became influential, many viewed the Stones as outdated.

### Commercial peak and turmoil (1978–1988)

*Some Girls* (1978), partly a response to punk with fast, basic, guitar-driven rock and roll, re-established their popularity and reached No. 1 in the US. *Tattoo You* (1981) featured outtakes from earlier sessions, including lead single "Start Me Up." The 1981 American Tour was their biggest and highest-grossing to date. In mid-1982, they signed a new four-album deal with CBS Records for a reported $50 million, then the biggest record deal in history. A rift between Jagger and Richards grew significantly in the 1980s: Jagger signed a solo deal, refused to tour for *Dirty Work* (1986), and the band almost broke up. Richards referred to this period as "World War III." Ian Stewart died of a heart attack in December 1985.

### Comeback and Wyman's departure (1989–2001)

In early 1989, the Stones were inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame. Jagger and Richards set aside their animosity for *Steel Wheels* (1989), heralded as a return to form. The Steel Wheels/Urban Jungle Tour was Wyman's last; his departure was made official in January 1993. Charlie Watts recruited Darryl Jones, a former sideman of Miles Davis and Sting, as Wyman's replacement for *Voodoo Lounge* (1994), which won the Grammy Award for Best Rock Album. The Voodoo Lounge Tour grossed $320 million, the world's highest-grossing tour at the time. In November 1994, the Stones became the first major recording artists to broadcast a concert over the Internet. *Bridges to Babylon* (1997) received mixed reviews but proved the band remained a strong live attraction.

### 40th and 50th anniversaries (2002–2020)

*Forty Licks* (2002) marked forty years as a band. *A Bigger Bang* (2005) was their first new album in almost eight years; the subsequent tour set records, including a free concert for over one million people at Copacabana beach in Rio de Janeiro in February 2006. By November 2006, the Bigger Bang tour was declared the highest-grossing tour of all time, grossing a record-setting $558 million. The band celebrated their 50th anniversary in 2012 with the compilation *GRRR!* and the 50 & Counting tour. *Blue & Lonesome* (2016), an album of 12 blues covers, reached No. 1 in the UK. In 2020, "Living in a Ghost Town" became their first original single since 2012 and their first German No. 1 in 52 years, making them the oldest artists ever to top that chart.

### Watts' death and recent years (2021–present)

Watts died on 24 August 2021 at age 80. The band became the highest-earning live act of 2021. *Hackney Diamonds* (20 October 2023), their first album of original material in 18 years, featured the final two songs Watts recorded with the band and a guest appearance by Wyman. It became their fourteenth UK number-one album. Their latest album, *Foreign Tongues*, was released on 10 July 2026.

## Terms

- **British Invasion**: The mid-1960s wave of British rock and pop acts achieving success in the US.
- **Nanker Phelge**: Pen name used for songs credited to the entire group.
- **Fuzzbox**: Guitar effects pedal producing a distorted, sustained tone; used on "Satisfaction."
- **Tax exile**: Leaving one's home country to avoid high taxation; the Stones left England in 1971.
- **Rolling Stones Mobile Studio**: A control room in a van created in 1968, allowing recording outside standard studio hours; also lent to other artists.
- **Tongue and lips logo**: The band's famous logo designed by John Pasche, based on the stuck-out tongue of the Hindu goddess Kali.
- **Open-G tuning**: Guitar tuning used by Richards from 1969, with the lower 6th string removed, heard on "Honky Tonk Women" and "Brown Sugar."
- **"Ancient art of weaving"**: Richards' term for the interplay between the band's two guitarists.

## Debates and open questions

- The identity of the drummer at the band's first show on 12 July 1962 "has never been confirmed."
- Brian Jones' death by drowning in July 1969 was "under mysterious circumstances."
- The *News of the World* drug article claimed Mick Jagger took Benzedrine at a club, but it was "a case of mistaken identity; the reporter had in fact been eavesdropping on Brian Jones."
- Whether Oldham engineered the band's "Bad Boys" image is debated: Wyman said Oldham "never did engineer it. He simply exploited it exhaustively."

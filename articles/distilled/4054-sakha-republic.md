# Sakha Republic

## Overview
The Republic of Sakha (Yakutia) is a federal subject of Russia and the world's largest subnational entity by area, covering over 3 million square kilometers in the Russian Far East. It is renowned for its extreme subarctic climate, with the coldest recorded temperatures in the Northern Hemisphere, and its rich natural resources, particularly diamonds. The republic's history is defined by the migration of Turkic Sakha people, Russian colonization, Soviet transformation, and a post-Soviet era of cultural revival and economic development centered on mining.

## Timeline
- **c. 2200–1300 BC** — Ymyakhtakh culture, a Late Neolithic culture, originates in the Lena River basin.
- **9th–16th centuries** — Turkic Sakha people migrate from the Lake Baikal region to the middle Lena River.
- **1632** — Pyotr Beketov founds Fort Lensky, the future city of Yakutsk.
- **1638** — Moscow forms the Yakutsk Oblast administrative unit.
- **1782** — Irkutsk Governorate is created; Yakutsk Oblast is later split from it in 1805.
- **1905** — A Sakha national movement emerges during the Russian Revolution of 1905.
- **1922** — The Yakut Autonomous Soviet Socialist Republic (ASSR) is proclaimed.
- **1929–1934** — Major collectivization policies are implemented, causing population decline.
- **1990** — The area declares sovereignty as the Yakutskaya-Sakha Soviet Socialist Republic.
- **1991** — It becomes the Republic of Sakha (Yakutia) within the Russian Federation.
- **2000** — Sakha is incorporated into the newly created Far Eastern Federal District.

## Body

### Geography and Climate
Sakha is the world's largest country subdivision, slightly smaller than India. It is bordered by the Arctic Ocean to the north and spans three time zones. The republic's landscape is defined by permafrost, vast taiga forests dominated by larch trees, and Arctic tundra. Its climate is hypercontinental, featuring the Northern Hemisphere's "Pole of Cold" at Verkhoyansk and Oymyakon, where temperatures have reached −67.8 °C (−90.0 °F). The region is rich in natural resources, producing 99% of Russia's diamonds and significant quantities of oil, gas, gold, and other minerals.

### Early History and Russian Conquest
Before Russian arrival, the region was home to Tungusic and Paleosiberian peoples like the Evenks and Yukaghir. The Turkic Sakha people migrated north from Lake Baikal between the 9th and 16th centuries, introducing a pastoral economy and displacing earlier groups. The Tsardom of Russia conquered the area in the 17th century, establishing Yakutsk in 1632 and imposing a fur tribute on indigenous peoples. While the initial conquest caused a 70% drop in the Sakha population, the Imperial period also saw the expansion of the Yakuts along the Vilyuy River.

### Soviet Era and World War II
The Russian Civil War saw some of its last battles in Sakha, culminating in the 1922 creation of the Yakut ASSR. The early Soviet period fostered Sakha literature but was followed by harsh collectivization (1929–1934) and the Great Purge, which killed many Sakha leaders. Demographics shifted dramatically as Slavic populations, primarily Russians and Ukrainians, migrated to urban and industrial areas, leading to the decline of the Sakha language in cities like Yakutsk.

### Post-Soviet Era
Following the 1991 dissolution of the Soviet Union, Sakha declared sovereignty and gained significant autonomy, including a 1992 agreement to retain 20% of diamond industry profits. The 1990s saw a rise in nationalist movements and a transition to a market economy. Under Vladimir Putin, federal centralization has curtailed some regional autonomy, but the mining-driven economy has remained resilient, with wages outpacing national averages.

### Demographics and Culture
The 2021 census recorded a population of 995,686, with Sakha (55.3%) and Russians (32.6%) as the largest ethnic groups. Both Russian and Sakha (a Turkic language) are official languages. Traditional shamanism and Tengrism have experienced a revival alongside Orthodox Christianity, often in a syncretic "dual belief system." The Sakha epic *Olonkho* is recognized by UNESCO as intangible cultural heritage. The republic's modern culture includes a growing film industry nicknamed "Sakhawood."

## Terms
- **Permafrost**: Ground that remains frozen for two or more consecutive years, severely limiting construction and agriculture.
- **Taiga**: A biome characterized by coniferous forests, which covers about 47% of Sakha.
- **Hypercontinental**: A climate with extreme seasonal temperature variations and low precipitation.
- **Olonkho**: A traditional Yakut epic poem, a masterpiece of oral and intangible heritage.
- **Collectivization**: The Soviet policy of consolidating individual farms and households into collective state-controlled units.
- **Korenizatsiya**: A Soviet policy of promoting native languages and cultures in non-Russian republics, later reversed.
- **Dvoyeverie**: A "dual belief system," or syncretism, blending Orthodox Christianity with traditional shamanistic practices.
- **Statoid**: A general term for any administrative division of a sovereign state.

## Debates and open questions
The source highlights ongoing tensions between federal centralization and regional autonomy, particularly regarding control over natural resources and the protection of Indigenous land rights. The economic benefits of the mining boom are also debated, as social support is noted to favor the less needy while rural poverty remains high.

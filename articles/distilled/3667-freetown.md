# Freetown

## Overview
Freetown is the capital and largest city of Sierra Leone, serving as the nation's primary economic, political, and cultural center. Founded in 1792 as a settlement for freed slaves, its history is central to understanding the development of Sierra Leone and the broader African diaspora. The city's identity is shaped by its diverse population, its role as a major Atlantic port, and its complex colonial past.

## Timeline

- **1789** — The settlement is burned down by King Jimmy, successor to the local chief who sold the land.
- **1792** — Freetown is formally founded by 1,196 African American settlers from Nova Scotia.
- **1808** — The British Crown takes control, making Freetown a Crown Colony.
- **1808–1874** — Freetown serves as the capital of British West Africa and a base for the Royal Navy's anti-slave trade squadron.
- **1827** — Fourah Bay College, the oldest university in West Africa, is founded.
- **1961** — Sierra Leone gains independence, with Freetown as its capital.
- **Late 1990s** — The city experiences fierce fighting during the Sierra Leone Civil War.
- **2017** — A devastating mudslide on Mount Sugar Loaf kills over 300 people.
- **2021** — A fuel tanker explosion in the city kills at least 99 people.

## Body

### Founding and Early Settlement
The area was inhabited for millennia before European contact, with the Bullom people as the earliest known group. The first European-backed settlement, the "Province of Freedom," was established in 1787 by formerly enslaved people from London but was destroyed in 1789. The modern city of Freetown was founded on March 11, 1792, by 1,196 African Americans from Nova Scotia, led by abolitionist Lieutenant John Clarkson. They cleared the jungle and built the city on an American grid pattern. The settlement was for free and freed African Americans, Afro-Caribbeans, and Liberated Africans, whose descendants became the Creole people.

### Colonial Era and Growth
In 1808, the British Crown took direct control, making Freetown a Crown Colony. It became the capital of British West Africa and a key base for the Royal Navy's squadron enforcing the ban on the slave trade. Ships intercepted in the trade brought liberated Africans to Freetown, significantly growing and diversifying the city's population. These liberated Africans, along with West Indian soldiers, formed the core of the Creole community. The city expanded rapidly, and its architecture, with stone foundations and wooden superstructures, became characteristic of Creole "bod oses."

### Modern City and Governance
Freetown is governed by a directly elected City Council and mayor. The city is ethnically diverse, with no single group forming a majority. The Krio language is the primary lingua franca. Politically, the city has been dominated by the All People's Congress (APC) in municipal elections since 2004, though it is a swing city in national presidential elections. The economy revolves around its natural deep-water harbour, one of the largest in the world, which handles exports and supports industries like fish packing and petroleum refining.

## Terms
- **Creole people**: Descendants of the original African American, Afro-Caribbean, and Liberated African settlers of Freetown.
- **Krio language**: An English-based creole language that is the primary lingua franca of Freetown and Sierra Leone.
- **Crown Colony**: A British colony under the direct control of the Crown, as Sierra Leone became in 1808.
- **Liberated Africans**: Enslaved people freed by the British Royal Navy from illegal slave ships and resettled in Freetown.
- **Bod oses**: A style of house with a stone foundation and wooden superstructure, built by Nova Scotian settlers and their Creole descendants.
- **Harmattan**: A dry, dusty wind from the Sahara that blows from December to February, providing Freetown's coolest period.
- **Mixed Commission Courts**: British courts established in Freetown to adjudicate cases related to the suppression of the slave trade.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The understanding and intent behind the 1787 land purchase from the Koya Temne leaders is debated, with questions about whether they comprehended the concept of permanent, exclusive European-style ownership.
- The 1987 bicentennial celebration of Freetown's founding was based on the 1787 settlement, but the city was formally founded in 1792, creating a historical discrepancy in commemoration.

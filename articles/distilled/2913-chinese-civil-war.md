# Chinese Civil War

## Overview
The Chinese Civil War was a protracted conflict between the Kuomintang (KMT)-led Republic of China (ROC) government and the Chinese Communist Party (CCP). Fought intermittently from 1927 to 1949, it determined the political future of mainland China, resulting in the CCP's victory and the establishment of the People's Republic of China (PRC), while the ROC government retreated to Taiwan. The war's outcome created a lasting cross-strait standoff that remains a central issue in East Asian geopolitics.

## Timeline
- **1923** — Sun–Joffe Manifesto establishes the First United Front between the KMT and CCP.
- **12 April 1927** — Shanghai massacre (12 April Incident) marks the violent split between the KMT and CCP.
- **1 August 1927** — CCP launches the Nanchang Uprising, creating the Chinese Red Army and beginning the civil war.
- **October 1934** — CCP begins the Long March to escape KMT encirclement campaigns.
- **12 December 1936** — Xi'an Incident: Chiang Kai-shek is kidnapped, forcing a Second United Front against Japan.
- **7 July 1937** — Japan launches full-scale invasion of China, leading to a nominal KMT-CCP alliance.
- **26 June 1946** — Full-scale civil war resumes after the collapse of post-WWII negotiations.
- **September–November 1948** — CCP wins the decisive Liaoshen Campaign in Manchuria.
- **November 1948–January 1949** — CCP wins the Huaihai Campaign, securing east-central China.
- **1 October 1949** — Mao Zedong proclaims the People's Republic of China in Beijing.
- **7 December 1949** — ROC government completes its retreat to Taiwan.

## Body

### First Phase and the Long March (1927–1937)
The civil war began after the First United Front collapsed in 1927. The CCP, following failed urban uprisings, established rural soviets. The KMT launched five "encirclement campaigns" to destroy them. The fifth campaign, using a blockhouse strategy, forced the CCP to undertake the Long March (1934–35), a strategic retreat of about 12,500 km. Of the 90,000–100,000 who began, only 7,000–8,000 reached the northern Shaanxi base. The march solidified Mao Zedong's leadership within the CCP.

### The Interlude: Second United Front (1937–1945)
The Xi'an Incident forced Chiang Kai-shek to ally with the CCP against Japan. During the war, the CCP's Red Army was nominally integrated into the Nationalist army but primarily conducted guerrilla warfare behind Japanese lines. This strategy allowed the CCP to expand its base areas and grow from about 40,000 troops to over 1 million by 1945. The alliance was marked by minimal cooperation and recurring clashes, such as the 1941 New Fourth Army incident, which effectively ended the united front.

### Second Phase and Communist Victory (1945–1949)
After Japan's defeat, both sides raced to accept surrenders. The CCP, aided by Soviet transfers of captured Japanese weapons in Manchuria, rapidly expanded. The KMT, despite U.S. airlift support and military aid, suffered from hyperinflation, corruption, and low morale. The CCP executed a "passive defense" strategy, trading space for time. Three decisive campaigns in 1948–49—the Liaoshen, Huaihai, and Pingjin—destroyed the KMT's best armies. The CCP captured Nanjing in April 1949 and proclaimed the PRC in October. The ROC government retreated to Taiwan in December.

### Aftermath and the Taiwan Strait
The ROC government in Taiwan, under U.S. protection after the Korean War, continued to claim legitimacy over all China. The PRC shelled ROC-held islands, leading to the First (1954–55) and Second (1958) Taiwan Strait Crises. The U.S. formalized its defense commitment with the Sino-American Mutual Defense Treaty (1954). The ROC lifted martial law in 1987, and cross-strait trade and investment grew, but no peace treaty has been signed. Tensions have resurfaced periodically, including during the 1995–96 and 2022 crises.

## Terms
- **Kuomintang (KMT):** The Nationalist Party of China, which governed the Republic of China on the mainland until 1949 and subsequently in Taiwan.
- **Chinese Communist Party (CCP):** The revolutionary party that established the People's Republic of China in 1949.
- **First United Front (1923–1927):** An alliance between the KMT and CCP, backed by the Soviet Union, to defeat warlords and unify China.
- **Northern Expedition (1926–1928):** A military campaign led by the KMT-CCP alliance to unify China under the Nationalist government.
- **Long March (1934–35):** A strategic retreat by the CCP's Red Army over approximately 12,500 km to escape KMT encirclement campaigns.
- **Second United Front (1937–1945):** A nominal alliance between the KMT and CCP to resist the Japanese invasion during World War II.
- **Liberated Zones:** Areas controlled by the CCP during and after the war with Japan, where it implemented political and economic reforms.
- **People's Liberation Army (PLA):** The military arm of the CCP, which defeated the KMT's National Revolutionary Army.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Cause of KMT Defeat:** Historians debate the relative importance of factors like KMT corruption and hyperinflation, CCP land reform and peasant mobilization, Soviet support for the CCP, and U.S. aid that was ultimately insufficient or mismanaged.
- **Role of Foreign Intervention:** The extent to which Soviet aid (especially in Manchuria) and U.S. support for the KMT determined the outcome is contested. Some argue the KMT's failure was primarily internal, while others emphasize external pressures.
- **Chiang Kai-shek's Leadership:** Chiang's own retrospective analyses, blaming Soviet duplicity and internal decay, are seen by scholars as partially accurate but self-serving, deflecting from his strategic errors and tolerance of corruption.
- **Atrocities and Civilian Suffering:** Both sides committed mass atrocities. The scale and intentionality of violence, such as during CCP land reform campaigns and KMT counter-insurgency, remain subjects of historical research and political sensitivity.

# Sasanian Empire

## Overview
The Sasanian Empire (224–651 AD) was the last great pre-Islamic Persian empire, ruling from the fall of the Parthians to the Arab conquests. It represented a high point of Iranian civilization, reviving Zoroastrianism, centralizing administration, and rivaling Rome/Byzantium for centuries. Its cultural and administrative legacy profoundly influenced the subsequent Islamic world.

## Timeline
- **224** — Ardashir I defeats Artabanus IV at Hormozdgan, ending the Parthian Empire.
- **224–241** — Reign of Ardashir I; founding of the Sasanian dynasty and revival of Zoroastrianism.
- **241–271** — Reign of Shapur I; major wars with Rome, including capture of Emperor Valerian (260).
- **309–379** — Reign of Shapur II; consolidation of power, wars with Rome, and persecution of Christians.
- **498–579** — Second Golden Era under Khosrow I (Anushirvan); administrative reforms and cultural flourishing.
- **602–628** — Byzantine–Sasanian War of 602–628; Sasanian expansion followed by Byzantine counter-offensive.
- **637** — Battle of al-Qādisiyyah; decisive Muslim Arab victory over Sasanian forces.
- **651** — Death of Yazdegerd III, marking the end of the Sasanian Empire.

## Body

### Origins and Early Expansion
The Sasanian Empire was founded by Ardashir I, who rose from being a regional ruler in Pars to overthrow the last Parthian king, Artabanus IV, in 224. This ended the Arsacid dynasty and began a new era. Ardashir and his successors, particularly Shapur I, aggressively expanded the empire eastward and westward, engaging in frequent wars with the Roman Empire. Shapur I achieved significant victories, including the capture of Emperor Valerian in 260, and promoted religious tolerance, notably supporting Manichaeism.

### Administration and Society
The empire was ruled by the *shahanshah* (King of Kings) from the capital at Ctesiphon. It featured a complex, centralized bureaucracy headed by a vizier (*wuzurg framadar*). Society was rigidly stratified into four classes: priests (*asronan*), warriors (*arteshtaran*), commoners (*wastaryoshan*), and artisans (*hutukhshan*). The *dehqans* (landowning knights) formed the backbone of the army and provincial administration. The state religion was Zoroastrianism, which was revitalized and given official status, though other religions like Christianity and Judaism were often tolerated.

### Military
The Sasanian military was renowned for its heavy cavalry, the *clibanarii* and *cataphracts*, who were heavily armored noblemen. Infantry (*paygan*) and war elephants also played key roles. The empire maintained a navy to control the Persian Gulf. Military success was crucial for defending borders against nomadic groups like the Hephthalites and for waging prolonged wars with the Byzantine Empire.

### Religion and Culture
Zoroastrianism was the state religion, with its orthodoxy enforced by powerful priests like Kartir. The empire saw the compilation of the Avesta and the creation of the Avestan alphabet. Other faiths, including Christianity (which split into Nestorian and Jacobite churches), Manichaeism, and Judaism, had significant communities. Culturally, the period was a renaissance, with advances in art, architecture (e.g., the great arch at Ctesiphon), literature (e.g., the *Khwaday-Namag*), and scholarship at centers like the Academy of Gondishapur.

### Relations with Neighbors
The empire's primary rival was the Roman/Byzantine Empire, with which it fought numerous wars over territory and influence in Armenia and Mesopotamia. To the east, it contended with nomadic groups like the Hephthalites and Kidarites. It also had complex relations with India, China, and the Kingdom of Aksum in Yemen, involving trade, diplomacy, and occasional conflict.

### Decline and Fall
The empire was severely weakened by the devastating Byzantine–Sasanian War of 602–628, which exhausted its resources. Internal strife and a rapid succession of rulers followed. When the Muslim Arab armies invaded in the 630s, the Sasanian state, lacking central authority and unity, collapsed quickly. The last king, Yazdegerd III, was killed in 651, and the empire's territories were absorbed into the Islamic Caliphate.

## Terms
- **Shahanshah**: "King of Kings," the title of the Sasanian emperor.
- **Zoroastrianism**: The ancient Iranian religion that served as the state religion, emphasizing the struggle between good and evil.
- **Dehqan**: A class of landowning knights who formed the administrative and military backbone of the empire.
- **Ctesiphon**: The capital city of the Sasanian Empire, located in modern-day Iraq.
- **Clibanarii/Cataphracts**: Elite, heavily armored cavalry units.
- **Avesta**: The primary collection of religious texts of Zoroastrianism.
- **Mazdakism**: A religious and social reform movement that challenged traditional Zoroastrian orthodoxy and class structure.
- **Nestorian Christianity**: A Christian denomination that became the dominant form of Christianity within the Sasanian Empire after separating from the Byzantine church.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The exact nature and extent of religious tolerance within the empire is debated, with policies varying significantly between rulers.
- The degree of continuity between Sasanian administrative practices and those of the early Islamic caliphates is a subject of scholarly discussion.
- The precise causes for the rapid collapse of the empire in the face of the Arab invasions, beyond the exhaustion from the Byzantine war, are analyzed in terms of social, economic, and political fragmentation.

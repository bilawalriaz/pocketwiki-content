# Sándor Petőfi

## Overview
Sándor Petőfi was a Hungarian poet and liberal revolutionary, considered Hungary's national poet. He played a key role in the Hungarian Revolution of 1848, inspiring the revolution with his poem "Nemzeti dal" (National Song). Petőfi's life and work are significant not only in Hungarian literature but also in the country's history, as his poetry and activism contributed to the struggle for independence from the Austrian Empire. His legacy extends beyond Hungary, with his works being translated and appreciated internationally.

## Body
### Early Life and Education
Petőfi was born to a family of Slovak origin in the Kingdom of Hungary. His father, István Petrovics, was a village butcher and innkeeper, and his mother, Mária Hrúz, was a servant and laundress. Petőfi's early life was marked by financial hardship, and he had to leave his studies at a lyceum due to his family's financial difficulties. He worked various jobs, including as a teacher and a soldier, before attending college at Pápa, where he met Mór Jókai, a friend and fellow poet, who later became the godfather of Petőfi's son Zoltán.

### Poetry and Revolution
Petőfi's poetry career began with "popular situation songs," but he soon developed a unique and fresh voice. His poems often dealt with themes of love, wine, and freedom. He was influenced by folk poetry and 19th-century populism, as well as major literary figures of his day. Petőfi's poetry became increasingly subtle and mature, and he became a master of using different voices in his poems. His revolutionary poem "Nemzeti dal" (National Song) is considered one of the key factors that inspired the Hungarian Revolution of 1848.

### Marriage and Family
Petőfi married Júlia Szendrey in 1847, despite her father's opposition. They had a son, Zoltán, born on 15 December 1848. Petőfi's family life was marked by his dedication to his revolutionary ideals, which sometimes put him at odds with his family's more conservative views. Mór Jókai, a close friend of Petőfi's, was the godfather of Zoltán.

## Terms
- **Nemzeti dal**: A poem written by Sándor Petőfi, considered one of the key factors that inspired the Hungarian Revolution of 1848.
- **12 Points**: A list of demands to the Habsburg Governor-General, co-authored by Petőfi and other revolutionaries.
- **János Vitéz**: An epic poem written by Petőfi, notable for its length and clever wordplay.
- **Almanac-poetry**: A type of poetry that was popular in the 19th century, characterized by its use of folkloric elements and traditional song-like verses.
- **Populism**: A literary and political movement that emphasized the importance of folk culture and the common people.

## Debates and open questions
- The exact circumstances of Petőfi's death are still debated among historians, with some theories suggesting that he may have died in Siberia, although there is no conclusive evidence to support this claim.
- The interpretation of Petőfi's poetry and its role in the Hungarian Revolution of 1848 is still a subject of scholarly debate.
- The legacy of Petőfi's work and its impact on Hungarian literature and culture are still being studied and appreciated by scholars and the general public.

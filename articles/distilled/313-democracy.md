# Democracy

## Overview
Democracy is a system of government where political power is vested in the people, derived from the Greek *dêmos* (people) and *krátos* (power/rule). It exists on a spectrum from **direct democracy**, where citizens vote on legislation personally, to **representative democracy**, where citizens elect officials to govern on their behalf. Modern **liberal democracy** combines representative government with constitutional limits on majority power to protect minority rights and civil liberties. Historically rare and restricted to elite classes (excluding women, enslaved people, and non-citizens), democracy expanded through 19th- and 20th-century suffrage movements. While democratic governance correlates with better health, education, and economic outcomes, recent decades have seen "democratic backsliding" in established democracies alongside persistent dissatisfaction with democratic performance.

## Timeline
- **508–507 BC** — Cleisthenes establishes the first documented democracy in Athens (direct, citizen-only).
- **1215** — Magna Carta signed in England, limiting monarchical power and establishing habeas corpus principles.
- **1688–1689** — Glorious Revolution and English Bill of Rights establish parliamentary sovereignty and regular elections in England.
- **1776–1789** — American Revolution and U.S. Constitution create the oldest surviving codified constitution; suffrage limited to white male property owners (~6% of population).
- **1789** — French Revolution adopts Declaration of the Rights of Man; universal male suffrage briefly enacted in 1792.
- **1848** — Revolutions across Europe; France establishes universal male suffrage.
- **1893** — New Zealand becomes first self-governing colony to grant women active universal suffrage.
- **1918–1928** — UK Representation of the People Act (1918) and Equal Franchise Act (1928) expand suffrage to all adults.
- **1948** — UN Universal Declaration of Human Rights mandates periodic, genuine elections by universal suffrage.
- **1970s–1980s** — "Third wave" democratization: Southern Europe, Latin America, Asia, and post-Cold War Eastern Europe transition to democracy.
- **2005–present** — V-Dem Institute documents 17+ consecutive years of global democratic backsliding (autocratization exceeding democratization).

## Body

### Definitions and Core Characteristics
No single consensus defines democracy; over 2,200 adjectives modify the term in English. **Minimalist definitions** (e.g., Joseph Schumpeter) view democracy as a mechanism for competitive elite selection via elections. **Maximalist definitions** require guarantees of civil liberties, human rights, and deliberative quality alongside elections. The UN defines democracy as an environment respecting human rights where the freely expressed will of the people is exercised. Core principles often cited include: **upward control** (sovereignty at lowest authority levels), **political equality**, **legal equality**, **political freedom**, and **rule of law**. **Majority rule** is the dominant decision method, but unchecked it risks "tyranny of the majority"; protections for minorities and individual rights (speech, press, assembly) are considered essential for fair competition and informed voting. **Republics** (rule without a monarch, by consent) overlap with but are distinct from democracies; a state can be a democratic republic (USA) or a constitutional monarchy (UK).

### Historical Origins
Democratic assemblies appear throughout human history independently. **Athens (508 BC)** under Cleisthenes created the first well-documented system: a direct democracy where the Assembly (*ekklesia*) of adult male citizens (approx. 1 in 4 residents) legislated and courts were staffed by random selection (**sortition**). **Sparta** used range voting/shouting in its *ecclesia* (700 BC). The **Roman Republic** introduced popular assemblies and weighted voting favoring elites, inspiring later thinkers. **Vaishali (India, 6th c. BC)** and the **Iroquois Confederacy (c. 1142–1660)** developed early republican/representative forms. In **Medieval Europe**, assemblies like the Icelandic *Althing*, Scandinavian *things*, and the Cortes of León (1188) involved limited franchises. The **Magna Carta (1215)** and Simon de Montfort’s Parliament (1265) rooted English parliamentary tradition. Poland developed "noble democracy" with local *sejmiks* gaining tax authority (1454).

### Modern Evolution: 17th–19th Centuries
England’s **Glorious Revolution (1688)** and **Bill of Rights (1689)** entrenched parliamentary sovereignty, regular elections, and property rights (per North and Weingast). **John Locke’s** social contract theory (1689)—inalienable rights to life, liberty, property; government by consent; right to revolt—became the foundation of liberal democracy. In **Colonial America**, the House of Burgesses (1619) and New England town meetings practiced representative and direct democracy under Crown authority. The **US Constitution (1787)** created a federal republic with checks and balances but left suffrage to states (initially ~6% eligibility). The **French Revolution (1789)** declared universal rights; the 1792 Convention used universal male suffrage. The **Corsican Republic (1755)** uniquely included female suffrage. The **19th century** saw gradual franchise expansion: UK Reform Acts (1832 onward), US abolition of property qualifications (last state 1856), post-Civil War amendments (13th, 14th, 15th) granting Black men nominal voting rights (fully realized only via the 1965 Voting Rights Act), and France’s 1848 universal male suffrage.

### 20th–21st Century Waves and Backsliding
Democratization occurred in **"waves"** (Samuel Huntington): post-WWI (new European states), post-WWII (West Germany, Japan, Italy, decolonization—India becoming the largest democracy), and the **"third wave" (1970s–90s)** (Southern Europe, Latin America, Asia, post-Soviet bloc). The **1948 Universal Declaration of Human Rights** codified elections by universal/equal suffrage as the basis of government authority. By 1960 most states were nominally democratic, but many held sham elections. **Freedom House** counted 123 electoral democracies in 2007 (up from 40 in 1972), covering 58% of world population; only 85 were "liberal democracies" (38% of population). Since **2005**, V-Dem documents **17+ consecutive years of global democratic backsliding** (autocratization exceeding democratization), driven by economic inequality, social discontent, executive aggrandizement, polarization, disinformation, and pandemic management. Notable cases include Poland (judicial appointments), Turkey (purges post-coup), Hungary, the Philippines, and the US/UK. **Autocratization** now affects 44 countries (V-Dem 2026), with population-weighted measures showing faster decline than country averages.

### Theoretical Conceptions
*   **Aggregative Democracy** (Schumpeter, Downs): Democracy aggregates citizen preferences via voting; parties mediate between individuals and state. **Minimalist** view: elections as elite competition. **Median voter theorem**: policy legitimacy derives from proximity to median voter.
*   **Deliberative Democracy** (Habermas, Cohen): Legitimacy requires authentic deliberation free from power distortions (wealth, interest groups) before voting; citizens' assemblies are key mechanisms.
*   **Polyarchy** (Robert Dahl): Requires institutions (free/fair elections, free expression, associational autonomy) ensuring equal consideration of interests; poverty can undermine procedural polyarchy.
*   **Participatory/Consensus/Radical/Inclusive/Cosmopolitan** models emphasize direct engagement, supermajority consensus, challenging hierarchical power, extending democracy to economic/ecological spheres, or global governance (International Criminal Court as a step).

### Institutional Forms
*   **Direct**: Citizens decide policy directly (Swiss cantons Appenzell Innerrhoden/Glarus, Rojava, Zapatistas; tools: referendums, initiatives, recall).
*   **Representative**: Officials elected by district (plurality/majority) or proportional representation; representatives exercise independent judgment.
*   **Parliamentary**: Executive (PM/cabinet) responsible to legislature; removable via vote of no confidence; "loyal opposition" concept.
*   **Presidential**: Popularly elected president (head of state/government) with fixed term; separation of powers risks gridlock (common in Americas, Africa, SE Asia).
*   **Semi-presidential**: Dual executive (president + PM); powers vary (France, Russia).
*   **Constitutional Monarchy**: Monarch ceremonial; democratic parliament governs (UK, Scandinavia, Japan).
*   **Sortition**: Random selection for offices (Athenian model; modern juries, citizens' assemblies).
*   **Consociationalism** (Lijphart): Power-sharing between elite representatives of segmented groups (ethno-religious); requires mutual veto/grand coalition (e.g., EU Council qualified majority voting).

### Measurement and Comparison
Measurement follows conceptual divides: **minimalist** indices (e.g., Cheibub/Gandhi/Vreeland) focus on electoral contestation; **maximalist** indices (V-Dem, Freedom House, Economist Intelligence Unit) incorporate rights, deliberation, equality, and state capacity. **V-Dem** distinguishes electoral, liberal, participatory, deliberative, and egalitarian dimensions. **Seva Gunitsky** warns indices often contradict due to normative trade-offs in conceptualizing democracy. **Coppedge et al. (2011)** propose a historical, multidimensional, disaggregated, transparent framework.

### Non-Governmental Democracy
Democratic principles govern **trade unions, cooperatives, NGOs**, and **corporations** (shareholder democracy: one share/one vote, electing boards). **Workplace democracy** applies to internal firm governance. **Islamic democracy** (Islamocracy) fuses democratic mechanisms with Sharia.

### Justifications
1.  **Legitimacy**: Social contract theory—authority derives from consent of the governed (elections expressing general will).
2.  **Epistemic superiority**: **Condorcet’s Jury Theorem**—if each voter has >50% chance of being correct, majority decisions approach certainty; "wisdom of crowds."
3.  **Economic performance**: Acemoglu & Robinson (*Why Nations Fail*) argue democracies avoid monopolies, enable creative destruction, invest in human capital/health. Empirical studies (1960–2010, 1789–2019) show transitions to democracy yield ~20% higher GDP per capita after 25 years and substantially increase long-term development.

### Democratic Transitions
**Democratization** drivers debated: **Modernization theory** (wealth/education → democracy) challenged by evidence (education often precedes democracy; regimes use schooling for indoctrination). **Peace/stable borders** (Gibler & Owsiak) historically *precede* democracy, not vice versa. **Weapon accessibility** (Quigley): democracy emerges when best weapons are individually accessible (citizen-soldier eras: Greece, 1800s US). **Geography/disease environment** (Acemoglu/Johnson/Robinson): settler mortality shaped colonial institutions → divergent paths. **Coastal access/trade** → property rights → broader power → democracy. **Robert Michels**: democracy as an ideal never fully reached, but striving for it improves conditions (the "buried treasure" fable). **V-Dem (2026)** identifies active democratization in 18 countries (e.g., Bolivia, Poland, Thailand).

**Autocratization** (democratic backsliding): **Levitsky** argues elites/institutions, not voters, must defend democracy. Mechanisms: **coups** (military, foreign-backed), **invasion**, **self-coups** (constitutional suspension, electoral fraud—Peru 1992, Russia 2003/04), **royal coups** (Yugoslavia 1929), and **gradual backsliding** (Enabling Act 1933 Germany; erosion of courts, press, election integrity). **Promotion** tools: voting advice apps, civic education, youth suffrage, proportional representation, reducing presidentialism.

### Media and Democracy
Democratic theory assumes informed voters. **Gurevitch & Blumler** outline media functions: surveillance, agenda-setting, advocacy platforms, cross-cutting dialogue, accountability, civic incentives, independence, audience respect. **Commercial media** prioritize profit, leading to tabloidization, personality/horse-race coverage, spin/conflict focus, and echo chambers via social media—fostering cynicism, polarization, populism, and erosion of trust. **False stories spread faster than true ones** on social media (no truth filters). **Public service broadcasting** (strong in Europe/Japan, weak in US) provides more policy-relevant, impartial content but faces deregulation pressure. **Internet/social media** empower movements (bypassing censorship, organizing protests—Arab Spring) but amplify misinformation. **Censorship/propaganda** sustain non-democratic regimes; independent information undermines them.

## Terms
- ****Direct Democracy**** — Citizens vote on laws/policies personally, without elected intermediaries (e.g., referendums, assemblies).
- ****Representative Democracy**** — Citizens elect officials to make decisions on their behalf; officials retain independent judgment.
- ****Liberal Democracy**** — Representative democracy constrained by constitution/rule of law protecting minority rights and civil liberties from majority tyranny.
- ****Sortition**** — Selection of officials by random lottery (used in Athens, Renaissance Florence, modern juries/citizens' assemblies).
- ****Polyarchy** (Dahl)** — "Rule by many"; a regime with institutions (free/fair elections, free expression, associational autonomy) ensuring equal consideration of interests.
- ****Deliberative Democracy**** — Legitimacy requires authentic, distortion-free deliberation among equals before voting; not mere preference aggregation.
- ****Consociationalism**** — Power-sharing democracy where elites from distinct societal segments (ethnic/religious) govern via mutual veto/grand coalition.
- ****Democratic Backsliding**** — Gradual erosion of democratic institutions (judicial independence, press freedom, election integrity) often by elected leaders.
- ****Self-Coup**** — Incumbent leader illegally seizes extra powers or extends term (e.g., suspending constitution, election fraud).
- ****Shareholder Democracy**** — Corporate governance where shareholders vote (typically one share/one vote) to elect boards/approve major decisions.

## Debates and Open Questions
1.  **Definition and Measurement**: No consensus on minimal vs. maximal criteria; indices (V-Dem, Freedom House, Polity, EIU) frequently disagree on country classifications, reflecting normative disputes about what democracy *is*.
2.  **Modernization vs. Structural Causes**: Does economic development cause democratization? Evidence shows weak/no reliable correlation; peace, geography, disease environment, and weapon technology are proposed as "deep" determinants.
3.  **Voter Competence vs. Elite Responsibility**: Levitsky argues voters prioritize mundane concerns over abstract democratic principles; defense of democracy falls to elites/institutions. Epistemic justifications (Condorcet) require unrealistic voter competence assumptions.
4.  **Media Ecosystem**: Can democracy survive commercial media incentives (polarization, sensationalism, echo chambers) and unregulated social media (viral misinformation)? Public service models are declining.
5.  **Global Trajectory**: Is the post-2005 autocratization wave a temporary recession or a structural reversal of the third wave? Population-weighted decline is steeper than country-count averages.
6.  **Scope of "The People"**: Franchise exclusions persist (non-citizens, under-18, felons in some jurisdictions); debates continue on lowering voting age to 16, non-resident voting, and cognitive capacity thresholds.
7.  **Institutional Design**: Presidentialism vs. parliamentarism (gridlock vs. stability); majoritarian vs. consensus/consociational models (efficiency vs. inclusion); role of sortition vs. election.

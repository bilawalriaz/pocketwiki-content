# Huns

## Overview
The Huns were a nomadic people who created a powerful but short-lived empire in Central and Eastern Europe during the 4th to 6th centuries CE. Their invasions triggered mass migrations of Germanic tribes, contributing to the decline of the Western Roman Empire. Their origins, culture, and legacy remain subjects of significant scholarly debate.

## Timeline
- **c. 370 CE** — Huns arrive on the Volga, causing Goths and Alans to flee westward into the Roman Empire.
- **395** — First large-scale Hunnic attack on the Eastern Roman Empire.
- **434** — Attila and Bleda become joint rulers of the Huns.
- **435** — Treaty of Margus grants Huns trade rights and annual Roman tribute.
- **445** — Bleda dies; Attila becomes sole ruler.
- **451** — Huns invade Gaul, fighting a combined Roman-Visigoth army at the Battle of the Catalaunian Fields.
- **452** — Huns invade Italy, reaching Mantua before withdrawing.
- **453** — Attila dies.
- **c. 454** — Battle of Nedao; Hunnic empire collapses after defeat by a Germanic coalition.
- **469** — Dengizich, last major Hunnic leader in Europe, is defeated and killed.

## Body

### Origins and Identity
The Huns first appeared in European sources around 370 CE, originating in Central Asia. Their exact origins are debated. Since the 18th century, a link has been proposed between them and the Xiongnu, a people from Mongolia and northern China. This connection remains controversial; some scholars argue for political and cultural continuity, while others caution that "Hun" was a prestigious name used by different steppe groups. Archaeogenetic studies show some Hun-era individuals had DNA similar to ancient Mongolian populations, but the evidence is not conclusive.

### Empire and Warfare
The Huns built an empire on the Roman frontier, initially as raiders and mercenaries, then as a dominant power under Attila. Their government evolved from a loose confederation of leaders to a more centralized system under Attila, who ruled with a council of "picked men." The empire controlled a vast territory, likely centered on the Hungarian Plain, and ruled over numerous subject peoples like Goths and Gepids. Their primary military tactic was mounted archery, using high mobility and feigned retreats. They were also capable of siege warfare.

### Society and Culture
Very little is known about Hunnic culture. They were pastoral nomads, herding cattle, horses, sheep, and goats. Their economy became tied to Rome through tribute, trade, and raiding. A distinctive cultural practice was artificial cranial deformation, binding infants' skulls to elongate them. The Hunnic language is almost unknown, with only three words recorded. Their religion is poorly attested but likely included shamanism, divination, and possibly the worship of a war god represented by a sword. They practiced polygamy among elites.

### Material Culture and Archaeology
Archaeological remains conclusively identified as Hunnic are scarce. Key finds include bronze cauldrons, likely used for cooking and ritual, and composite bows. Artifacts show a mix of steppe and Roman influences, with polychrome, cloisonné-style decoration on weapons and jewelry. Clothing likely included tunics, trousers, and felt caps. The lack of stirrups in Hunnic burials suggests they fought primarily with bows from horseback.

## Terms
- **Pastoral nomadism:** A lifestyle based on herding livestock and moving between pastures, rather than farming.
- **Artificial cranial deformation:** The practice of binding infants' heads to create an elongated skull shape, often a marker of elite status.
- **Composite bow:** A powerful, recurved bow made from layers of wood, bone, and sinew, ideal for mounted archery.
- **Cloisonné:** A decorative technique where metal wire compartments (cloisons) are filled with enamel or gemstones.
- **Lamellar armor:** Armor made from small, rectangular plates laced together in horizontal rows.
- **Spangenhelm:** A type of helmet constructed from metal plates (spangen) riveted to a framework.
- **Seax:** A single-edged, knife-like sword or large knife common among Germanic and Hunnic peoples.
- **Spatha:** A long, double-edged sword used by late Roman cavalry and adopted by various "barbarian" groups.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **The Xiongnu-Hun Connection:** Is there a direct historical and genetic link between the Xiongnu of Asia and the European Huns, or are they separate groups sharing a prestigious name?
- **Hunnic Government:** Was the Hunnic empire a centralized state, a loose confederacy, or something in between? How much power did subject kings retain?
- **Extent of Control:** How far did Attila's direct authority extend? Did he control territories like the Pontic Steppe or regions north of the Carpathians?
- **Language:** What language family did Hunnic belong to? The tiny corpus of evidence makes classification impossible.
- **Hungarian Origins:** While mainstream scholarship rejects a direct ethnic link between Hungarians (Magyars) and Huns, the legend has had a powerful influence on Hungarian national identity and politics.

# Bordeaux

## Overview
Bordeaux is a major port city in southwestern France, renowned as a global wine capital and a center for aeronautics and tourism. Its history spans over two millennia, from a Celtic settlement to a Roman commercial hub, an English-held duchy, and a key French port, culminating in its 2007 UNESCO World Heritage listing for its outstanding 18th-century urban architecture.

## Timeline
- **c. 300 BC** — Celtic tribe Bituriges Vivisci settle the area, naming it Burdigala.
- **60 BC** — City comes under Roman rule, becoming a commercial center.
- **107 BC** — Battle of Burdigala: Romans are defeated by the Tigurini.
- **12th–15th c.** — Bordeaux flourishes under English rule after the marriage of Eleanor of Aquitaine to Henry II.
- **1453** — Battle of Castillon leads to French annexation of Bordeaux.
- **18th c.** — Golden age; city becomes France's busiest port and a major center of the slave trade.
- **1793** — Girondist revolutionaries are executed during the French Revolution.
- **1804** — Haiti's independence collapses Bordeaux's colonial trade economy.
- **2007** — The Port of the Moon is inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
- **2020** — Ecologist Pierre Hurmic ends 73 years of right-of-centre mayoral rule.

## Body

### Early History and Medieval Period
The settlement of Burdigala was conquered by Rome around 60 BC, becoming an important commercial center. After periods of sacking by Vandals, Visigoths, and Franks, it re-emerged in the late 6th century as a seat of a county and archdiocese. The city was plundered by Umayyad forces in 732 and later became a stronghold in the wars between the Frankish Carolingians and the Duchy of Aquitaine. From the 12th to 15th centuries, Bordeaux entered a period of prosperity under English rule following the marriage of Eleanor of Aquitaine to Henry II Plantagenêt. This era saw the construction of major landmarks like the Saint-André Cathedral. The city was briefly the capital of an independent state under Edward, the Black Prince, before being annexed by France after the Battle of Castillon in 1453.

### Economic Rise, Revolution, and the Slave Trade
The 18th century was a golden age for Bordeaux, which supplied Europe with colonial goods like coffee, sugar, and cotton, becoming France's busiest port. This prosperity was deeply tied to the transatlantic slave trade; Bordeaux shipowners deported an estimated 150,000 Africans in some 500 expeditions. The wealth generated exacerbated social inequality, contributing to unrest during the French Revolution, when the city was a stronghold of the moderate Girondist faction. The slave revolt in Saint-Domingue (Haiti) and its subsequent independence in 1804 caused the collapse of Bordeaux's colonial trade-based economy.

### Modern Bordeaux
The 19th century saw economic rebuilding, with new infrastructure like the Pont de Pierre bridge. The city's 18th-century urban planning later inspired Baron Haussmann's transformation of Paris. During both World Wars, the French government temporarily relocated to Bordeaux. The city suffered under German occupation in WWII, during which a submarine base was constructed. The late 20th and early 21st centuries have been marked by urban renewal, culminating in the 2007 UNESCO listing of the Port of the Moon. Bordeaux is now a major hub for aeronautics, home to companies like Dassault and ArianeGroup, and a leading tourist destination.

### Wine and Economy
The vine was introduced by the Romans, and wine production has been continuous since. The Bordeaux wine region encompasses about 116,160 hectares, 57 appellations, and 10,000 châteaux, producing approximately 960 million bottles annually. The 1855 Bordeaux Wine Official Classification established the premier cru (First Growth) estates. While facing a generic wine glut, global demand and prices for top labels have soared. Beyond wine, the economy is driven by aeronautics, tourism, and business services. The city is also a major research center, hosting one of the world's two megajoule lasers.

## Terms
- **Château**: In the context of Bordeaux wine, a wine-producing estate or vineyard.
- **Premier cru (First Growth)**: The highest classification in the 1855 Bordeaux Wine Official Classification, denoting the most prestigious estates.
- **Claret**: The British term for red Bordeaux wine.
- **Girondists**: A political faction during the French Revolution, representing the provincial bourgeoisie, which was strong in Bordeaux.
- **Port of the Moon**: The historic port area of Bordeaux, named for its crescent shape along the Garonne River, inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
- **Metropole**: An administrative entity (Bordeaux Metropolis) grouping Bordeaux with 27 suburban municipalities to manage wider metropolitan issues.
- **APS (Alimentation Par le Sol)**: A ground-level power supply system used by the Bordeaux tramway to eliminate overhead cables in the historic center.
- **Megajoule laser**: One of the world's most powerful lasers, located in Bordeaux for fundamental research.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The socioeconomic disparities and mass unrest in Bordeaux prior to the French Revolution are highlighted as a key regional dynamic, with historian Suzanne Desan emphasizing the need to examine "intricate local dynamics."
- The city's historical role and moral responsibility regarding the slave trade remains a point of reflection, with memorials and museum exhibits addressing this past.
- The political shift in 2020, ending 73 years of right-of-centre rule, signals an evolving political landscape in the traditionally conservative city.

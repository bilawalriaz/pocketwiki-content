# Spanish Civil War

## Overview
The Spanish Civil War (1936–1939) was a brutal conflict between the left-leaning Republican government and right-wing Nationalist rebels, serving as a proxy battleground for the ideological struggles of the 20th century. It ended with the victory of General Francisco Franco, who established a dictatorship that lasted until 1975, and is often seen as a precursor to World War II.

## Timeline
- **12 Apr 1931** — Municipal elections lead to the fall of the monarchy and the proclamation of the Second Spanish Republic.
- **17 Jul 1936** — Military coup against the Republican government begins in Spanish Morocco.
- **20 Jul 1936** — Coup leader General Sanjurjo is killed in a plane crash.
- **1 Oct 1936** — General Francisco Franco is appointed supreme leader of the Nationalists.
- **8 Nov 1936** — Nationalist assault on Madrid is repulsed, beginning a three-year siege.
- **26 Apr 1937** — German Condor Legion bombs the Basque town of Guernica.
- **27 Feb 1939** — UK and France recognize the Franco regime.
- **5 Mar 1939** — Republican Colonel Casado leads a coup against his own government to seek peace.
- **1 Apr 1939** — Franco declares victory after Nationalist forces capture Madrid.

## Body

### Background and Outbreak
The war followed years of political instability, including the dictatorship of Primo de Rivera (1923–1930) and the establishment of the Second Republic in 1931. Deep social divisions over land reform, secularism, and regional autonomy intensified. A right-wing government (1933–1935) reversed left-wing reforms, leading to the failed Asturian miners' strike of 1934. The narrow victory of the left-wing Popular Front in the February 1936 elections polarized the country. Political violence escalated, culminating in the assassination of conservative leader José Calvo Sotelo on 13 July 1936, which served as a catalyst for a military coup planned by generals including Emilio Mola and Francisco Franco.

### The Coup and Spanish Revolution
The coup, which began on 17 July 1936, failed to seize control of most major cities. The government's response—arming workers' militias—led to a social revolution in Republican zones, particularly in Catalonia and Aragon, where anarchists and socialists collectivized land and industries. This "Spanish Revolution" created a parallel power structure that often rivaled the Republican government. Meanwhile, the Nationalists, controlling about half the army and the elite Army of Africa, consolidated power in conservative regions. The conflict quickly evolved from a coup into a full-scale civil war.

### Combatants and Foreign Intervention
The war pitted a diverse Republican coalition (socialists, anarchists, communists, regional nationalists) against a unified Nationalist alliance (monarchists, Falangists, conservatives, the Catholic Church). The Nationalists received substantial military aid from Nazi Germany (Condor Legion) and Fascist Italy (CTV), while the Republicans were supported by the Soviet Union and Mexico. Despite a Non-Intervention Agreement signed by 27 nations, including Britain and France, foreign involvement was decisive. The International Brigades, composed of foreign volunteers, fought for the Republic.

### Course of the War
After initial stalemates, the Nationalists, under Franco's unified command, advanced methodically. They captured the northern coast by late 1937. A Republican counter-offensive at the Battle of the Ebro (July–November 1938) was a costly failure. The Nationalists then conquered Catalonia in early 1939, cutting off the Republic. Internal Republican strife, including the May Days (1937) in Barcelona and a final coup by Colonel Casado in March 1939, crippled the war effort. Franco entered Madrid on 28 March and declared victory on 1 April.

### Atrocities and Death Toll
Both sides committed widespread atrocities. The Nationalist "White Terror" involved organized purges and mass executions to consolidate power. The Republican "Red Terror" included spontaneous killings, particularly of clergy and perceived enemies, though it was less systematically organized by the state. The death toll is highly debated, with estimates of violent deaths ranging from 235,000 to over 500,000, including combat, executions, and civilian deaths from bombing and disease. Hundreds of thousands of Republicans fled into exile.

## Terms
*   **Popular Front:** A left-wing coalition of parties (socialists, communists, republicans) that won the February 1936 elections.
*   **Nationalists (Nacionales):** The right-wing rebel faction, an alliance of Falangists, monarchists, conservatives, and the military, led by Francisco Franco.
*   **Caudillo:** The title assumed by Franco, equivalent to "leader" or "dictator."
*   **Army of Africa:** Spain's elite colonial troops based in Morocco, which formed the core of the early Nationalist military force.
*   **Condor Legion:** The German air force contingent that fought for the Nationalists, infamous for the bombing of Guernica.
*   **International Brigades:** Foreign volunteer units that fought for the Republican cause.
*   **Non-Intervention Agreement:** A pact signed by 27 nations (including Britain and France) to avoid involvement in the Spanish Civil War, which was widely ignored.
*   **Spanish Revolution:** The social revolution in Republican zones (1936–1937) involving widespread collectivization of land and industry, led largely by anarchists and socialists.
*   **POUM (Workers' Party of Marxist Unification):** An anti-Stalinist communist party in the Republican zone, suppressed with Soviet backing in 1937.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Cause of the War:** Historians debate whether it was primarily a class struggle, a religious conflict, a struggle between democracy and fascism, or a counter-revolution against the Republic.
*   **Nature of Republican Violence:** Scholars disagree on whether killings in the Republican zone were spontaneous outbursts of anarchy or semi-organized campaigns by leftist groups.
*   **Role of Foreign Intervention:** The extent to which Nationalist victory was due to superior foreign aid versus internal Republican disunity remains a key question.
*   **Death Toll:** Figures vary widely depending on whether they include combat deaths, executions, postwar repression, and indirect deaths from famine and disease.

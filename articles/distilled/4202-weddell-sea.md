# Weddell Sea

## Overview
The Weddell Sea is a large, ice-covered marginal sea of the Southern Ocean, bounded by Antarctica's Antarctic Peninsula and Coats Land. It is a critically important region for global ocean circulation due to its role in forming deep water masses and is known for its treacherous ice conditions, unique ecology, and extreme clarity.

## Timeline
- **1823** — James Weddell discovers the sea; Benjamin Morrell claims to see "New South Greenland" (later disproven).
- **1900** — The sea is renamed in James Weddell's honor.
- **1903** — William Speirs Bruce makes the furthest southern penetration before the modern era.
- **1915** — Ernest Shackleton's ship *Endurance* is trapped and crushed by ice.
- **1986** — Researchers measure the sea's water clarity, finding it comparable to distilled water.
- **2002** — Ice shelves on the east side of the Antarctic Peninsula, formerly covering ~10,000 km², have completely disappeared.
- **2021** — A colony of ~60 million Jonah's icefish is discovered; sponges are found living under the Filchner-Ronne Ice Shelf.
- **2022** — The wreck of Shackleton's *Endurance* is discovered at a depth of 3,008 m.

## Body

### Geography and Ice
The Weddell Sea is defined by the coasts of Coats Land and the Antarctic Peninsula, with its easternmost point at Cape Norvegia. It is approximately 2,000 km wide at its broadest and covers an area of about 2.8 million km². A permanent, massive ice shelf field, the Filchner-Ronne Ice Shelf, covers much of its southern part. The sea is noted for having the clearest water of any sea, with visibility measured to a depth of 80 m. Historically, it has been described as the most treacherous region on Earth due to its dense pack ice and unpredictable "flash freezes."

### Oceanography and Climate
The Weddell Sea is a major site for the formation of deep water masses, a key driver of the global thermohaline circulation. This process, called cabbeling, is caused by differences in water temperature and salinity, primarily driven by brine exclusion from sea ice formation and wind cooling. Circulation is dominated by the cyclonic Weddell Gyre, with a northward-flowing western boundary current exporting modified water to the global ocean. The climate is characterized by strong surface winds along the Antarctic Peninsula, which drive ice drift and create sharp contrasts in conditions between the peninsula's eastern and western sides.

### Geology and History
The Weddell Sea shares a geological history with southernmost South America. It formed as the surviving oceanic extension of the Rocas Verdes Basin, a Jurassic back-arc basin that, unlike its South American counterpart, escaped later compressional tectonics. The sea was discovered by James Weddell in 1823. It is the site of famous historical events, including the entrapment and loss of Shackleton's *Endurance* in 1915 and the 2022 discovery of its wreck.

### Ecology
The Weddell Sea supports abundant wildlife, including Weddell seals, killer whales, humpback whales, and crabeater seals. The Adélie penguin is the dominant penguin species, with a large colony on Paulet Island. The northernmost emperor penguin colony is located just south of Snow Hill Island. Recent discoveries include a massive colony of approximately 60 million Jonah's icefish and the finding of sponges and other suspension feeders living on a boulder under the Filchner-Ronne Ice Shelf at a depth of 1,233 m.

## Terms
- **Weddell Gyre**: A large, cyclonic (clockwise) ocean current system in the Weddell Sea, primarily wind-driven.
- **Thermohaline circulation**: Global ocean circulation driven by differences in water density, which is controlled by temperature (thermo) and salinity (haline).
- **Cabbeling**: The mixing of two water masses of similar density but different temperature and salinity, resulting in a new, denser water mass.
- **Brine exclusion**: The process where salt is expelled from forming sea ice, increasing the salinity and density of the surrounding water.
- **Ice shelf**: A thick, floating platform of ice that forms where a glacier or ice sheet flows down to a coastline and onto the ocean surface.
- **Pycnocline**: A layer in the ocean where water density increases rapidly with depth.
- **Secchi disc**: A simple tool used to measure water clarity or turbidity.

## Debates and Open questions
- The exact volume transports and dynamics of the deep circulation in the western Weddell Sea remain difficult to quantify due to limited data.
- The long-term impact of climate change on the Weddell Gyre, ice shelf stability, and deep water formation is a subject of ongoing research.

# Robert Clive

## Overview
Robert Clive (1725–1774), known as "Clive of India," was a British officer and administrator for the East India Company (EIC). He is credited with establishing the foundation of British rule in Bengal through military conquest and political intrigue, most notably at the Battle of Plassey (1757). His actions significantly expanded EIC power and wealth, making him a central but controversial figure in the history of the British Raj.

## Timeline
- **29 September 1725** — Born at Styche, Shropshire, England.
- **1744** — Arrives in India as an EIC "writer" (clerk) at Fort St. George, Madras.
- **1746** — Escapes French captivity after the fall of Madras during the First Carnatic War.
- **1751** — Leads the Siege of Arcot, gaining fame and the title "heaven-born general."
- **18 February 1753** — Marries Margaret Maskelyne in Madras; returns to England.
- **1755** — Returns to India as deputy governor of Fort St. David.
- **1757** — Wins the Battle of Plassey, overthrowing Nawab Siraj ud-Daulah and installing Mir Jafar.
- **1760** — Returns to England with a vast fortune; created Baron Clive.
- **1765** — Returns to India as Governor and Commander-in-Chief; secures the Mughal *firman* granting EIC the *diwani* (revenue rights) of Bengal, Bihar, and Odisha.
- **1767** — Leaves India for the final time.
- **22 November 1774** — Dies by suicide in London.

## Body

### Early Life and First Journey to India
Clive was born into a gentry family in Shropshire. His youth was marked by unruliness and fighting. In 1744, he sailed to India as a clerk for the East India Company. During the First Carnatic War, he escaped French captivity in Madras and joined the Company's private army, distinguishing himself in its defense.

### The Carnatic Wars and Siege of Arcot
Clive's military reputation was forged during the Second Carnatic War. In 1751, he improvised a daring expedition and captured the fortress of Arcot with a small force. This successful defense, against overwhelming odds, checked French imperial ambitions on the Coromandel Coast and demonstrated the potential for European forces to influence Indian politics through indirect rule.

### The Conquest of Bengal
In 1756, the Nawab of Bengal, Siraj ud-Daulah, captured Calcutta. Clive was dispatched to retaliate. After recapturing the city, he formed a conspiracy with Mir Jafar, the Nawab's commander. At the Battle of Plassey (1757), Clive's small force defeated Siraj ud-Daulah's army, largely because Mir Jafar withheld his troops. Clive installed Mir Jafar as a puppet Nawab, securing vast payments and an annual jagir (land revenue grant) for himself and the EIC.

### Governorship and the *Diwani*
Clive returned to India in 1765 as Governor. Following the EIC's victory at the Battle of Buxar (1764), he secured a *firman* (imperial decree) from the Mughal Emperor Shah Alam II. This granted the EIC the *diwani*—the right to collect revenue—of Bengal, Bihar, and Odisha, making the Company the de facto sovereign ruler of over 30 million people. Clive attempted administrative reforms, including banning officials from accepting gifts, but corruption remained rampant.

### Later Life and Death
Clive retired to England in 1767, having amassed a fortune of over £400,000. He was ennobled as Baron Clive and sat in Parliament. His later years were marred by parliamentary inquiries into EIC corruption and the Bengal famine of 1770, which killed millions and was linked to the Company's revenue policies. He died by suicide in 1774.

## Terms
- **East India Company (EIC):** A British joint-stock company granted a royal charter to trade in the East Indies, which gradually acquired sovereign powers in India.
- **Writer:** The term for a junior clerk or agent in the service of the EIC.
- **Jagir:** A grant of land revenue rights given in lieu of a salary.
- **Diwani:** The right to collect land revenue and administer civil justice, granted by the Mughal emperor.
- **Nawab:** A provincial governor or ruler in the Mughal Empire.
- **Sepoy:** An Indian soldier serving under European command.
- **Firman:** An official decree or order issued by a Mughal emperor.
- **Dual System of Government:** An administrative system introduced by Clive where the EIC controlled revenue collection (*diwani*) while the Nawab retained nominal authority over law and order (*nizamat*).

## Debates and open questions
- **Legacy:** Clive is a deeply controversial figure. He is credited with founding the British Empire in India but is also criticized for the corruption, economic exploitation, and the Bengal famine that followed his conquests.
- **Battle of Plassey:** Historians debate whether Plassey was a true military victory or primarily a political coup achieved through bribery and treachery.
- **Personal Wealth:** The source of Clive's immense fortune has been a subject of historical scrutiny, with debates over whether his gains were within accepted Company practices or constituted corruption.
- **Death:** The exact circumstances of his suicide are debated, with theories ranging from depression and illness to the stress of political attacks.

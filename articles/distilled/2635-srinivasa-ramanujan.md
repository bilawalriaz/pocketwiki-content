# Srinivasa Ramanujan

## Overview
Srinivasa Ramanujan (1887–1920) was an Indian mathematician whose extraordinary intuition and largely self-taught genius produced thousands of novel results in number theory, infinite series, and continued fractions. His work, often formulated without formal proofs, opened new fields of mathematical research and continues to influence modern mathematics and physics.

## Timeline
- **22 Dec 1887** — Born in Erode, British India, into a Tamil Brahmin family.
- **1903** — Obtained a copy of G. S. Carr's *Synopsis*, which inspired his independent study.
- **1910** — Met V. Ramaswamy Aiyer, founder of the Indian Mathematical Society, gaining initial recognition.
- **16 Jan 1913** — Sent a letter with mathematical results to G. H. Hardy at Cambridge.
- **17 Mar 1914** — Traveled to England to collaborate with Hardy and Littlewood.
- **1916** — Awarded a B.A. by Research from Cambridge for work on highly composite numbers.
- **2 May 1918** — Elected a Fellow of the Royal Society.
- **13 Oct 1918** — Elected a Fellow of Trinity College, Cambridge.
- **1919** — Returned to India due to ill health.
- **26 Apr 1920** — Died in Kumbakonam, India, aged 32.
- **1976** — George Andrews rediscovered Ramanujan's "lost notebook."

## Body

### Early Life and Independent Work
Ramanujan was a child prodigy who mastered advanced trigonometry by age 13. After failing to secure a degree due to his exclusive focus on mathematics, he lived in poverty while conducting independent research. In 1910, he met V. Ramaswamy Aiyer, who recognized his talent and helped publish his first paper in the *Journal of the Indian Mathematical Society*. Ramanujan's work, such as his solution to an infinitely nested radical problem, was noted for its novel and unconventional methods.

### Collaboration with Hardy
In 1913, Ramanujan wrote to G. H. Hardy, sending pages of theorems. Hardy, initially skeptical, was amazed by the depth and originality of the results, particularly in continued fractions and infinite series. Hardy arranged for Ramanujan to come to Cambridge in 1914. Their collaboration was a clash of styles: Hardy, an apostle of rigorous proof, mentored Ramanujan in formal mathematics, while Ramanujan relied on profound intuition. During his five years in England, Ramanujan was elected a Fellow of the Royal Society and of Trinity College, Cambridge.

### Mathematical Achievements
Ramanujan independently compiled nearly 3,900 results, most of which were correct. His work includes the Ramanujan prime, the Ramanujan theta function, partition formulae, and mock theta functions. He derived rapidly converging series for π and discovered deep identities related to the gamma function and modular forms. With Hardy, he developed the circle method to study the partition function. His "lost notebook," rediscovered in 1976, contained discoveries from his final year that continue to inspire research.

### Illness, Death, and Legacy
Ramanujan's health deteriorated in England, possibly due to hepatic amoebiasis from earlier dysentery. He returned to India in 1919 and died the following year. His notebooks, containing summaries of his results, have been analyzed for decades. As late as 2012, researchers found that brief comments in his writings were themselves profound number theory results. The Ramanujan Journal was established to publish work in areas he influenced.

## Terms
- **Mathematical analysis:** A branch of mathematics dealing with limits and related theories, such as differentiation, integration, measure, infinite series, and analytic functions.
- **Number theory:** A branch of pure mathematics devoted primarily to the study of the integers and integer-valued functions.
- **Infinite series:** The sum of the terms of an infinite sequence of numbers.
- **Continued fraction:** An expression obtained through an iterative process of representing a number as the sum of its integer part and the reciprocal of another number.
- **Modular form:** A complex analytic function on the upper half-plane that satisfies specific transformation and growth conditions.
- **Partition function:** A function that counts the number of ways a positive integer can be expressed as a sum of positive integers, without regard to order.
- **Mock theta function:** A function that behaves like a modular form but does not transform quite like one; they are the holomorphic parts of harmonic weak Maass forms.
- **Hepatic amoebiasis:** A parasitic infection of the liver caused by *Entamoeba histolytica*, often a complication of amoebic dysentery.

## Debates and open questions
- The exact cause of Ramanujan's death is debated. While tuberculosis was the contemporary diagnosis, a 1994 analysis concluded his symptoms were more consistent with hepatic amoebiasis.
- The extent to which Ramanujan's results were derived through mystical intuition versus rigorous, unrecorded work on slates is discussed. Mathematician Bruce C. Berndt argues he could prove most results but chose not to record the proofs.
- Hardy speculated that had Ramanujan been introduced to modern mathematical ideas earlier, he might have become the greatest mathematician of his time.

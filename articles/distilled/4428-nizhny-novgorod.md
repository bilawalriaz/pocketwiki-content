# Nizhny Novgorod

## Overview
Nizhny Novgorod is a major Russian city at the confluence of the Oka and Volga rivers, serving as the administrative center of the Volga Federal District. Founded in 1221, it evolved from a medieval fortress into a crucial trade hub of the Russian Empire, later becoming a closed Soviet industrial center named Gorky before regaining its original name in 1990. Its strategic location and industrial heritage have made it a key economic and cultural center in modern Russia.

## Body

### Medieval Fortress and Political Center
The city was founded in 1221 as a wooden hillfort for border protection. It survived the Mongol invasion and, after 1264, grew in importance within the Vladimir-Suzdal Principality. Grand Duke Dmitry Konstantinovich (1323–1383) made it his capital, building a stone citadel and patronizing the creation of the Laurentian Codex (1377). After incorporation into Moscow in 1392, the city served as a key stronghold against the Tatars of Kazan, with its formidable Kremlin completed in 1511. Its most famous historical moment came in 1612 when merchant Kuzma Minin and Prince Dmitry Pozharsky rallied a national militia from the city to liberate Moscow, ending the Time of Troubles.

### Commercial and Industrial Rise
In 1817, the transfer of the Makaryev Fair to Nizhny Novgorod transformed it into the trade capital of the Russian Empire, attracting millions annually. The city became a hub for engineering, with the Sormovo Iron Works as a major enterprise. The 1896 All-Russia Exhibition showcased technological innovations. During the Soviet era, the city was renamed Gorky (1932) and became a closed, major industrial center, notably home to the Gorky Automobile Plant (GAZ), built with assistance from Henry Ford in the late 1920s. This period cemented its reputation as "Russian Detroit."

### Soviet Era and Modern Revival
As Gorky, the city was a restricted military-industrial hub, subjected to heavy German air raids during World War II (1941–1943). It remained closed to foreigners until 1990. The metro opened in 1985, and physicist Andrei Sakharov was exiled there from 1980 to 1986. After the reinstatement of its original name in 1990, the city reopened. It has since modernized, hosting the 2018 FIFA World Cup and developing its IT sector, with companies like Intel establishing major R&D centers. The city is a leading center for engineering, particularly in automotive, shipbuilding, and aircraft manufacturing.

## Terms
*   **Kremlin:** The historic fortified center of the city, containing government agencies and key architectural monuments.
*   **Makaryev Fair:** A major annual trade fair transferred to Nizhny Novgorod in 1817, which drove the city's commercial growth.
*   **Closed City:** A Soviet designation for cities with sensitive military or industrial facilities, restricting access to foreigners; Gorky held this status from 1932 to 1990.
*   **Gorky Automobile Plant (GAZ):** A major Soviet automotive factory established in the late 1920s with Henry Ford's help, central to the city's industrial identity.
*   **Stroganov School:** A distinctive style of architecture and icon painting that developed in the city at the turn of the 17th and 18th centuries, patronized by the Stroganov merchant family.
*   **Hyperboloid Tower:** An open-work lattice tower design pioneered by engineer Vladimir Shukhov, with a notable example built near the city in 1929.
*   **Chkalov Stairs:** The longest staircase in Russia, connecting the city's upper and lower embankments, built in the late 1940s.

# Svatopluk I of Moravia

## Overview
Svatopluk I (r. 870–894) was the most powerful ruler of Great Moravia, a medieval Slavic state in Central Europe. His reign marked the empire's peak territorial expansion and influence, but his death triggered its rapid collapse. His story is central to understanding the early political and religious development of the region, including the conflict between Latin and Slavic Christian rites.

## Timeline
- **c. 840** — Svatopluk is born, likely a nephew of Rastislav.
- **863** — Cyril and Methodius arrive in Moravia as missionaries.
- **869** — Svatopluk first appears in Frankish sources as ruler of a principality within Moravia.
- **870** — Svatopluk betrays his uncle Rastislav to the Franks and is later imprisoned by them.
- **871** — After a Moravian rebellion, Svatopluk is released and leads a victory over Frankish forces.
- **874** — Peace of Forchheim: Svatopluk agrees to pay tribute to East Francia.
- **880** — Pope Stephen V addresses Svatopluk as "King" and places Moravia under papal protection.
- **882** — Svatopluk's forces invade the Frankish March of Pannonia.
- **885** — After Methodius's death, Svatopluk expels his Slavic liturgy disciples from Moravia.
- **894** — Svatopluk dies; his realm soon collapses amid succession struggles and Hungarian raids.

## Body

### Rise to Power and Conflict with the Franks
Svatopluk began as a semi-autonomous prince within Great Moravia under his uncle Rastislav. In 870, he betrayed Rastislav to the Franks, who then imprisoned him. After a Moravian rebellion, Svatopluk was released and led the rebels to a decisive victory, becoming the undisputed ruler. He secured a peace treaty with East Francia in 874, agreeing to pay tribute but preserving his autonomy.

### Expansion and Papal Relations
Svatopluk expanded Great Moravia's territory, possibly including parts of modern Poland and Hungary. He cultivated a strong relationship with the papacy; in 880, Pope Stephen V formally addressed him as "King" and placed his realm under the Holy See's protection. This alliance helped legitimize his rule and the Moravian church.

### Religious Policy and the Expulsion of Methodius's Disciples
A major internal conflict involved the liturgy. Svatopluk initially supported the Slavic liturgy used by Methodius but later sought to appease German clergy who opposed it. After Methodius's death in 885, Svatopluk, influenced by the German bishop Wiching, expelled Methodius's disciples. This ended the Slavic liturgy in Moravia and signaled a shift toward Latin Rite dominance, though the exiled scholars carried their work to the Bulgarian Empire.

### The "Wilhelminer War" and Later Reign
From 881 to 884, Svatopluk was embroiled in a conflict with the sons of former Frankish commanders, which devastated the Pannonian region. He eventually submitted to Emperor Charles the Fat in 884. In his later years, he fought against Arnulf of Carinthia, who became East Frankish king in 887. Arnulf recruited Hungarians to aid his campaigns against Moravia, a decision with long-term consequences for the region.

### Death and Collapse of Great Moravia
Svatopluk died in 894 under unclear circumstances. His deathbed advice to his sons to remain united (illustrated by the "bundle of sticks" fable) went unheeded. Internal strife between his sons, Mojmir II and Svatopluk II, and intensified Hungarian raids led to the rapid disintegration of the Great Moravian state. The Czechs withdrew in 895, and the empire collapsed in the early 10th century.

## Terms
- **Great Moravia**: A medieval Slavic state in Central Europe, at its height under Svatopluk I.
- **Suzerainty**: Overlordship or feudal control one state exercises over another.
- **Old Church Slavonic**: The first literary Slavic language, used for liturgy by Cyril and Methodius.
- **Peace of Forchheim (874)**: Treaty where Svatopluk agreed to pay tribute to East Francia in exchange for peace.
- **March of Pannonia**: A frontier territory of East Francia in the middle Danube region.
- **Filioque**: A controversial addition to the Christian Creed, asserting the Holy Spirit proceeds from the Father *and the Son*, a point of dispute between Latin and Eastern churches.
- **Wilhelminer War (881–884)**: A conflict involving Svatopluk, Frankish factions, and Arnulf that ravaged Pannonia.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The exact location of Svatopluk's initial principality within Moravia is debated by historians.
- The extent and permanence of his territorial expansions, particularly into Poland and Pannonia, are questioned due to limited archaeological evidence.
- The circumstances of his death in 894 are unknown, described only as an "unlucky" mishap.
- The historical accuracy of later legends, such as the Hungarian account of him selling his country, is disputed.

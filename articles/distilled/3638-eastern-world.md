# Eastern world

## Overview
The Eastern world, also known as the East or historically the Orient, is an umbrella term for various cultures, nations, and philosophical systems, primarily encompassing Asia, the Mediterranean region, and the Arab world in historical contexts. It is often seen as a counterpart to the Western world, though the regions included are varied and do not share a single common heritage. The division is more cultural and historical than geographical, a product of European history distinguishing Christian Europe from cultures to its east, and was globalized through colonization.

## Timeline
- **Pre-modern** — The term "Eastern world" originally had a literal geographic meaning, referring to the eastern part of the Old World.
- **European colonization** — The East-West dichotomy became global with the colonization of the Americas.
- **Modern era** — The concept of a unified Eastern world is emphasized in the scholarly tradition of Orientalism.

## Body

### Definition and Scope
The Eastern world is an umbrella term whose included regions vary by context. It most often includes Asia, the Mediterranean region, and the Arab world in historical (pre-modern) contexts. In modern times, it is frequently discussed in the context of Orientalism. The various parts of the Eastern world share some common threads, such as being in the "Global South," but they have never historically defined themselves collectively. The term's boundaries are cultural and historical rather than strictly geographical; for example, Australia and New Zealand are grouped with the Western world despite their location, while Central Asian nations of the former Soviet Union are grouped in the East.

### Cultural and Conceptual Basis
The division between 'East' and 'West' (formerly Orient and Occident) is a product of European cultural history, distinguishing Christian Europe from the cultures beyond it to the East. With European colonization of the Americas, this dichotomy became global. The concept of an Eastern or "Oriental" sphere was emphasized by ideas of racial, religious, and cultural differences. This distinction was articulated by Westerners in the scholarly tradition known as Orientalism, which is notable for being a Western conception of a unified Eastern world not limited to any specific region, but rather encompassing all of Asia together.

### Internal Diversity and Identity
The Eastern world is internally diverse, with no single shared common heritage. Asia itself consists of many different countries, ethnic groups, and cultures. The concept is further debated because the term "Asian" is associated with different regional origins in various English-speaking countries. West Asia, which includes parts of the Arab world and Iran, may sometimes be considered "Middle Eastern" and separate from Asia. Countries like the Philippines are geographically in the Eastern world but may be considered Western in some societal aspects due to historical influences. Eastern culture refers to the internally diverse social norms, ethical values, traditional customs, belief systems, political systems, artifacts, and technologies primarily rooted in Asian history, with subgroups and syncretism within regions.

## Terms
- **Eastern world**: An umbrella term for various cultures, nations, and philosophical systems, primarily encompassing Asia, the Mediterranean region, and the Arab world in historical contexts.
- **Western world**: The counterpart to the Eastern world, historically centered on Christian Europe and later expanded through colonization to include the Americas and Oceania.
- **Orientalism**: A scholarly tradition and Western conception that articulates a unified Eastern world, emphasizing racial, religious, and cultural differences from the West.
- **Global South**: A term often used to describe regions that share common threads, including many parts of the Eastern world.
- **Syncretism**: The fusion or amalgamation of different religions, cultures, or schools of thought, noted as occurring within Eastern cultural regions.
- **Settler colonialism**: A form of colonialism where foreign settlers permanently displace and marginalize indigenous populations, cited as a reason for the Western grouping of the Americas and Oceania.

## Debates and Open questions
- The exact boundaries of the Eastern world are debated, as the term's included regions vary by context and are not geographically fixed.
- The concept of a unified "Asian" identity is contested, as the term is associated with different regional origins (e.g., East Asian, South Asian) in various countries.
- West Asia, including parts of the Arab world and Iran, may or may not see itself as part of the Eastern world and is sometimes considered separately as "Middle Eastern."

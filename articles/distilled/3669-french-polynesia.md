# French Polynesia

## Overview
French Polynesia is an overseas collectivity of France in the South Pacific, comprising 121 islands and atolls. It holds a unique political status as an "overseas country" within the French Republic, with significant autonomy but with defense and foreign affairs controlled by France. Its history is marked by Polynesian settlement, European colonization, and a complex modern political landscape often defined by the debate between pro- and anti-independence factions.

## Timeline
- **c. 200 BC** — First settlement of the Marquesas Islands by Polynesians.
- **1521** — Portuguese explorer Ferdinand Magellan sights Puka-Puka.
- **1842** — France declares a protectorate over Tahiti and Tahuata.
- **1880** — France annexes Tahiti, ending its protectorate status.
- **1888** — France annexes the Leeward Islands after the Leewards War.
- **1946** — The territory becomes an overseas territory; Polynesians gain French citizenship.
- **1957** — The territory is renamed French Polynesia.
- **1966-1996** — France conducts nuclear tests at Moruroa and Fangataufa atolls.
- **2003** — French Polynesia becomes an overseas collectivity with expanded autonomy.
- **2013** — The UN re-lists French Polynesia as a non-self-governing territory.

## Body

### History and Colonization
The islands were settled by Austronesian peoples during the Great Polynesian Migration, with the Marquesas settled around 200 BC and the Society Islands around AD 300. European contact began in 1521, with significant visits by explorers like Wallis (1767), Bougainville (1768), and Cook (1769). French influence grew after Catholic missionaries were expelled in 1836, leading to a French protectorate in 1842. France annexed Tahiti in 1880 and consolidated control over other archipelagos by 1889, creating the colony of French Establishments in Oceania.

### Political Status and Governance
French Polynesia is a parliamentary representative democracy within the French Republic. Its government controls local affairs like education and health, while France retains responsibility for justice, defense, and foreign affairs. The territory experienced significant political instability from the mid-2000s to mid-2010s, with the presidency frequently changing between pro-independence leader Oscar Temaru and anti-independence leaders Gaston Flosse and Gaston Tong Sang. Stability returned after 2016 under President Édouard Fritch.

### Nuclear Testing
From 1966 to 1996, France conducted 193 nuclear tests at the Moruroa and Fangataufa atolls in the Tuamotu Archipelago. The tests, initially atmospheric and later underground, were a major source of tension. Protests erupted when testing resumed in 1995 after a moratorium. The last test was on 27 January 1996, and France subsequently signed the Comprehensive Test Ban Treaty.

### Geography and Demographics
The territory's 3,521 km² of land is divided into five archipelagos: the Society Islands (including Tahiti), the Tuamotus, the Gambier Islands, the Marquesas, and the Austral Islands. The population was 278,786 in 2022, with 68.7% living on Tahiti. The official language is French, but Polynesian languages like Tahitian are widely spoken. Christianity is the main religion, with the Maohi Protestant Church being the largest denomination.

### Economy
The economy is dependent on tourism, French financial assistance, and exports like black Tahitian pearls, vanilla, and noni juice. The currency is the CFP franc, pegged to the euro. The GDP was $6.02 billion in 2019. The territory was severely affected by the 2008 financial crisis and the COVID-19 pandemic.

## Terms
- **Overseas collectivity (COM):** A French administrative division with a specific statute of autonomy, distinct from an overseas region.
- **Overseas country (POM):** A designation for French Polynesia within the COM status, granting it administrative autonomy without changing its legal status.
- **Great Polynesian Migration:** The prehistoric migration of Austronesian peoples across the Pacific using celestial navigation.
- **Protectorate:** A territory under the control of a foreign power that maintains local governance but controls defense and foreign affairs.
- **Constructive vote of no confidence:** A parliamentary procedure where a government is only removed if a new government is simultaneously elected.
- **CFP franc:** The currency used in French Polynesia, with a fixed exchange rate to the euro.
- **Himene tārava:** A traditional Polynesian form of unaccompanied vocal Christian music characterized by a unique drop in pitch at phrase endings.
- **Va'a:** A traditional Polynesian outrigger canoe, central to the sport of va'a racing.

## Debates and Open Questions
The central political debate is the question of independence versus maintaining ties with France. This was highlighted by the UN's 2013 re-inscription of French Polynesia as a non-self-governing territory, which made it eligible for a UN-backed independence referendum. The legacy and potential health impacts of French nuclear testing remain a significant issue. There are also ongoing discussions about the balance of power between the central government in Papeete and the more remote island groups.

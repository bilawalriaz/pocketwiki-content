# Chandigarh

## Overview
Chandigarh is a union territory in northwestern India that serves as the shared capital of the states of Punjab and Haryana. It is one of the earliest planned cities in post-independence India, internationally renowned for its modernist architecture and urban design by Le Corbusier. The city represents a significant post-colonial project, conceived as a symbol of a new, modern India free from colonial traditions.

## Timeline
- **1947** — Partition of India leaves East Punjab without a capital after Lahore is ceded to Pakistan.
- **1949** — American town planner Albert Mayer is selected to design the master plan for the new capital.
- **1950** — Architect Matthew Nowicki dies in a plane crash; Mayer is subsequently dropped from the project.
- **1950-51** — Le Corbusier's team is recruited to create a new master plan, significantly modifying Mayer's design.
- **1953** — State government offices are fully shifted from Shimla; the capital is officially inaugurated on 7 October.
- **1966** — The state of Punjab is reorganized; Chandigarh becomes a union territory and shared capital for Punjab and Haryana.
- **1976** — Nek Chand's Rock Garden is inaugurated after being secretly built since 1957.
- **1985** — The Open Hand Monument in the Capitol Complex is completed.
- **2016** — The Capitol Complex is declared a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

## Body

### Conception and Planning
Following the 1947 Partition, the newly formed Indian state of East Punjab needed a new capital, as Lahore was now in Pakistan. Prime Minister Jawaharlal Nehru championed the project as a symbol of India's freedom and future. After initial planning by Albert Mayer and Matthew Nowicki, the project was taken over by Le Corbusier's team in 1950. They replaced the earlier curvilinear "Garden City" plan with a rectilinear grid of "sectors" and a seven-tiered road system. The Capitol Complex, designed to house the government, was intended as an independent India's answer to the British-built complex in New Delhi.

### Construction and Development
Construction began in the early 1950s using local materials and extensive manual labor. The city was built to be a year-round capital, unlike the British practice of moving to Shimla in summer. The first sector developed was Sector 22. Key institutions like Panjab University and the Post Graduate Institute of Medical Education and Research (PGIMER) were established. The city's growth spurred the development of adjacent satellite towns, Mohali in Punjab and Panchkula in Haryana, forming a tri-city metropolitan area.

### Post-Reorganization and Modern Issues
In 1966, Chandigarh was made a union territory to serve as a shared capital, a status that remains contested. The city has faced challenges including illegal settlements, the decline of its renowned cleanliness ranking, and debates over infrastructure projects like a proposed metro. Economically, it is one of India's wealthiest cities with a high per capita income, driven by government employment, services, and an IT park. Culturally, it hosts festivals like the Rose Festival and is home to the unique Rock Garden.

### Postcolonial Significance
Chandigarh was conceived as a tool for national regeneration and modernization, breaking from colonial urban traditions. Le Corbusier's modernist design, emphasizing functionality and low-cost housing, aimed to create an egalitarian "urban utopia." This project is seen as part of a broader decolonization effort, using architecture to forge a new Indian identity. However, critics argue the focus on Corbusier erases the contributions of Indian architects and that the design imposed a Western, middle-class vision that ignored local complexities and created new social hierarchies.

## Terms
- **Union Territory:** A type of administrative division in India directly controlled by the central government.
- **Superblock/Sector:** The basic residential unit in Chandigarh's master plan, containing housing, markets, schools, and parks.
- **Capitol Complex:** The government center of Chandigarh designed by Le Corbusier, containing the Secretariat, High Court, and Palace of Assembly.
- **Garden City Movement:** An urban planning philosophy promoting green spaces and self-contained communities, which influenced the initial plan for Chandigarh.
- **International Style:** A modernist architectural style characterized by minimalism, functional design, and the use of modern materials, which defined Chandigarh's architecture.
- **Rock Garden:** A sculpture garden in Chandigarh created secretly by Nek Chand from waste materials, now a major tourist attraction.
- **Open Hand Monument:** A large kinetic sculpture in the Capitol Complex, adopted as the official symbol of Chandigarh.

## Debates and open questions
- The political status of Chandigarh is a long-standing issue, with both Punjab and Haryana claiming it as their capital. Surveys have shown a majority of residents prefer it remain a union territory.
- Scholarly criticism questions whether the narrative of Chandigarh's development overemphasizes Le Corbusier's role, arguing it was a hybrid project involving significant Indian input and "contested modernities."
- The implementation of Nehru and Le Corbusier's postcolonial vision is critiqued for potentially creating new class-based segregation and imposing a homogenized, Western-influenced identity on a diverse region.

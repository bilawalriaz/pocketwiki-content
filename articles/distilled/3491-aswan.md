# Aswan

## Overview
Aswan is a historic city in Upper Egypt, situated at the first cataract of the Nile. It served as the southern frontier of Ancient Egypt, a vital military and trade hub, and a source of granite for monumental construction. Today, it is a major tourist center and home to significant archaeological sites, many of which were relocated due to the construction of the Aswan High Dam.

## Body
### Ancient History and Significance
Aswan, the ancient city of Swenett (later Syene), was the southernmost town of Ancient Egypt, marking the country's frontier. Its name may derive from an Egyptian goddess or the symbol for "trade." The city's quarries supplied celebrated granite (syenite) for obelisks, statues, and pyramids across Egypt. It was a crucial military garrison and trade post where tolls were levied on Nile traffic. The city's latitude (24° 5′ 23″) was noted by ancient geographers; around 240 BC, Eratosthenes used observations from Aswan to calculate the Earth's circumference.

### Archaeological Discoveries
Aswan and its surroundings are rich in archaeological finds. Excavations have uncovered Old Kingdom tombs at Qubbet el-Hawa, a tomb with 35 Greco-Roman mummies belonging to a tradesman named Tjit, and a Ptolemaic sandstone sphinx at Kom Ombo. Recent underwater surveys between the Aswan dams have revealed rock carvings and stelae depicting New Kingdom pharaohs like Amenhotep III and Thutmose IV. The city is part of the UNESCO World Heritage Site "Nubian Monuments from Abu Simbel to Philae," which includes the relocated Philae Temple and the Unfinished Obelisk in the ancient quarries.

### The Aswan Dams and Lake Nasser
The Aswan Low Dam, built between 1899 and 1906, was the world's largest dam at the time. It was later heightened to control Nile floods for irrigation. The Aswan High Dam (1958–1970) was a pivotal project for Egypt's industrialization, creating the massive artificial Lake Nasser. The dam's construction necessitated the UNESCO-led rescue of Nubian monuments, including the Abu Simbel and Philae temples, which were relocated to higher ground. The reservoir's Egyptian portion is named Lake Nasser, while the Sudanese portion is called Lake Nubia.

### Modern City and Economy
The modern city has expanded to include Elephantine Island. Its economy relies on tourism, agriculture (dates, hibiscus), and industries like quarrying and chemicals (e.g., the KIMA company). Infrastructure includes Aswan International Airport, a railway terminus from Cairo, and the Cape to Cairo Road. The city is part of the UNESCO Creative Cities Network for craft and folk art. Cultural institutions include the Nubian Museum, the Nile Museum, and the Aswan International Women's Film Festival.

## Terms
- **Cataract**: A shallow stretch of a river with rocks and rapids; Aswan is located at the first cataract of the Nile.
- **Syenite**: A type of granite quarried near Aswan, used extensively in ancient Egyptian monuments.
- **UNESCO World Heritage Site**: A location with legal protection for its cultural or natural heritage, as designated by the United Nations.
- **Lake Nasser**: One of the world's largest artificial lakes, formed by the Aswan High Dam on the Nile.
- **Nubia**: A region along the Nile river, encompassing southern Egypt and northern Sudan, with a distinct ancient civilization.
- **Petroglyph**: An image created by removing part of a rock surface by incising, picking, or carving.
- **Hypostyle hall**: A large room with a roof supported by columns, common in ancient Egyptian temples.
- **Nilometer**: A structure used to measure the Nile River's water level, historically used to predict floods and set taxes.

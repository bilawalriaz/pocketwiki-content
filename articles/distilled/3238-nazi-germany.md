# Nazi Germany

## Overview
Nazi Germany was the totalitarian dictatorship that ruled Germany from 1933 to 1945 under Adolf Hitler and the Nazi Party. It transformed Germany into a police state, pursued aggressive territorial expansion, and initiated World War II. The regime's core ideology of racial supremacy led to the Holocaust, the systematic genocide of six million Jews and millions of others, making it one of history's most destructive and morally condemned regimes.

## Timeline
- **30 January 1933** — Adolf Hitler is appointed Chancellor of Germany.
- **27 February 1933** — The Reichstag fire is used as a pretext to suspend civil liberties.
- **23 March 1933** — The Enabling Act grants Hitler dictatorial powers.
- **14 July 1933** — Germany becomes a one-party state.
- **30 June 1934** — The Night of the Long Knives purge consolidates Hitler's power.
- **1 August 1934** — Hitler becomes Führer after President Hindenburg's death.
- **7 March 1936** — German troops reoccupy the demilitarised Rhineland.
- **12 March 1938** — Germany annexes Austria (Anschluss).
- **29 September 1938** — The Munich Agreement cedes the Sudetenland to Germany.
- **1 September 1939** — Germany invades Poland, starting World War II in Europe.
- **22 June 1941** — Germany invades the Soviet Union (Operation Barbarossa).
- **30 April 1945** — Hitler commits suicide in his Berlin bunker.
- **8 May 1945** — Germany signs an unconditional surrender, ending the war in Europe.

## Body

### Rise to Power and Consolidation
The Nazi Party rose to power during the Great Depression, exploiting economic crisis and public discontent with the Weimar Republic. After Hitler was appointed Chancellor in 1933, the regime swiftly dismantled democratic institutions. The Reichstag Fire Decree suspended civil liberties, and the Enabling Act allowed Hitler to pass laws without parliamentary consent. Political parties and trade unions were banned, creating a one-party state. Power was centralised in Hitler's person through the *Führerprinzip* (leader principle), where his word became the supreme law. The government functioned not as a coordinated body but as competing factions vying for Hitler's favour.

### Ideology and Racial Policy
Nazi ideology was a fusion of extreme antisemitism, racial hygiene, eugenics, and territorial expansionism (*Lebensraum*). The regime promoted the concept of a "master race" (Aryan) and identified Jews, Romani, Slavs, and others as racial enemies. Persecution began immediately with boycotts and the 1935 Nuremberg Laws, which stripped Jews of citizenship. This escalated through state-sponsored violence like *Kristallnacht* (1938) to systematic genocide. The "Final Solution," formalised at the 1942 Wannsee Conference, resulted in the Holocaust—the murder of approximately six million Jews in extermination camps. The regime also implemented *Generalplan Ost*, a plan for ethnic cleansing and colonisation in Eastern Europe, and the "Hunger Plan," which deliberately starved millions of Soviet civilians and POWs.

### Economy and Rearmament
To combat mass unemployment, the regime launched massive public works projects (e.g., Autobahnen) and secret rearmament, financed by deficit spending. This economic recovery, coupled with propaganda, boosted the regime's popularity. The economy was geared entirely for war, with military spending dominating the budget. Under Albert Speer, the war economy became increasingly reliant on the plunder of occupied territories and the forced labour of millions of foreign workers and concentration camp inmates. Allied strategic bombing from 1943 onward crippled German industry and infrastructure, contributing to the regime's collapse.

### World War II and Collapse
Hitler's aggressive foreign policy, starting with the remilitarisation of the Rhineland and the annexation of Austria and Czechoslovakia, was met with appeasement until the invasion of Poland in 1939 triggered war with Britain and France. Germany achieved rapid conquests in Europe using *Blitzkrieg* tactics but failed to defeat Britain. The 1941 invasion of the Soviet Union opened a catastrophic two-front war. The defeat at Stalingrad (1943) was a major turning point. By 1944, Germany was being invaded from both East and West. Hitler's refusal to surrender led to massive destruction and death in the war's final months, culminating in his suicide and Germany's unconditional surrender in May 1945.

## Terms
*   **Führerprinzip**: The "leader principle" asserting Hitler's absolute authority and that his word overrode all law.
*   **Lebensraum**: "Living space"; the Nazi ideology that Germans needed to conquer territory in Eastern Europe for settlement.
*   **Gleichschaltung**: The "coordination" or forced alignment of all aspects of German society, culture, and politics with Nazi ideology.
*   **Wehrmacht**: The unified German armed forces (1935–1945), comprising the army, navy, and air force.
*   **SS (Schutzstaffel)**: The elite paramilitary corps under Heinrich Himmler, responsible for security, intelligence, and implementing the Holocaust.
*   **Einsatzgruppen**: Mobile SS death squads that murdered over a million people, primarily Jews, in Eastern Europe.
*   **Blitzkrieg**: "Lightning war"; a military tactic of rapid, coordinated assaults to avoid prolonged conflict.
*   **Nuremberg Laws**: 1935 laws that institutionalised racial theories, stripping Jews of citizenship and prohibiting intermarriage.
*   **Volksgemeinschaft**: The "people's community"; the Nazi ideal of a racially pure, classless national community.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Intentionalism vs. Functionalism**: Historians debate whether the Holocaust was a long-cherished goal of Hitler (intentionalism) or emerged from a series of radicalising decisions by competing state bureaucracies (functionalism).
*   **Hitler's Role**: Scholars like Ian Kershaw emphasise Hitler's central, ideological role, while others stress the autonomous, radicalising actions of the Nazi elite and state apparatus.
*   **German Knowledge and Complicity**: The extent to which ordinary Germans were aware of and complicit in the regime's crimes remains a central question, with evidence showing varying levels of knowledge, from direct participation to wilful ignorance.
*   **Economic Motivations**: There is debate over whether territorial expansion and war were primarily driven by ideological goals (*Lebensraum*) or by economic necessity to sustain the rearmament program and resolve structural weaknesses.

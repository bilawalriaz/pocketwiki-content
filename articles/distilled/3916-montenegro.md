# Montenegro

## Overview
Montenegro is a small, mountainous country in Southeast Europe on the Adriatic Sea. Its history is defined by a long struggle for sovereignty, from medieval principalities and Ottoman rule to modern independence in 2006. Today, it is a parliamentary republic and NATO member, with an economy transitioning to a market system and a society characterized by ethnic and religious diversity.

## Timeline
- **1042** — Principality of Duklja gains independence from the Byzantine Empire.
- **1496** — Montenegro falls to the Ottoman Empire.
- **1696** — The House of Petrović-Njegoš establishes semi-autonomous rule, first as a theocracy.
- **1878** — Independence recognized by the Great Powers at the Congress of Berlin.
- **1910** — Montenegro becomes a kingdom.
- **1918** — Kingdom becomes part of the Kingdom of Serbs, Croats and Slovenes (later Yugoslavia).
- **1941-1944** — Axis occupation and intense partisan resistance during World War II.
- **1945-1992** — Montenegro is a constituent republic of communist Yugoslavia.
- **2003** — Serbia and Montenegro form a state union, replacing the Federal Republic of Yugoslavia.
- **2006** — Montenegro declares independence following a referendum.
- **2017** — Montenegro joins NATO.
- **2023** — Jakov Milatović wins presidential election, ending Milo Đukanović's long dominance.

## Body

### Etymology and Early History
The name "Montenegro" is a Venetian translation of the Montenegrin "Crna Gora" (Black Mountain), referencing the dark, forested slopes of Mount Lovćen. The term first appeared in the 15th century. Before the Slavic migrations in the 6th-7th centuries CE, the region was inhabited by Illyrians. After Roman conquest, it became part of the province of Illyricum. In the Early Medieval period, three principalities—Duklja, Travunia, and Rascia—occupied the territory. Duklja gained independence in 1042 and expanded, but its power waned after the early 12th century. By the 14th century, the region was known as Zeta, ruled by the Balšić and later Crnojević noble families.

### Ottoman Rule and the Path to Independence
The Crnojević dynasty made Montenegro the last free Balkan monarchy before its fall to the Ottomans in 1496. From the late 14th century, Venice also controlled coastal areas like the Bay of Kotor. Within the Ottoman Empire, Montenegro developed a unique autonomy, governed by warlike clans and, from 1515, a theocracy led by the Metropolitanate. A pivotal victory at the Battle of Grahovac (1858) forced the Great Powers to demarcate borders, de facto recognizing Montenegro's independence. This was formally recognized in the 1878 Treaty of Berlin. Under Prince-Bishop and later King Nicholas I, the state modernized and expanded through wars with the Ottomans, becoming a kingdom in 1910.

### World War II and Socialist Yugoslavia
During World War I, Montenegro fought with the Allies but was occupied by Austria-Hungary from 1916. After the war, it was absorbed into the Kingdom of Serbs, Croats and Slovenes. Following the Axis invasion of Yugoslavia in 1941, Italian forces occupied Montenegro. A major anti-fascist uprising began in July 1941, one of the first in occupied Europe. The resistance fractured into communist Partisans and royalist Chetniks, leading to civil war. Montenegro was liberated by the Partisans in December 1944 and became a republic within communist Yugoslavia, later renamed Titograd.

### Independence and Modern Montenegro
After Yugoslavia's dissolution, Montenegro remained in a federation with Serbia. In 1992, a referendum favored union, but political and economic divergence grew. Following the 1999 NATO bombing and political changes in Serbia, a new union was formed in 2003. A 2006 referendum narrowly passed the 55% threshold for independence, which was internationally recognized. The post-independence era has been dominated by Milo Đukanović and his party, marked by privatization, allegations of corruption, and a pro-Western foreign policy. Montenegro joined NATO in 2017 and has been in EU accession negotiations since 2012. A 2020 election ended Đukanović's long rule, leading to political instability and a 2023 election victory for the pro-European "Europe Now" movement.

## Terms
- **Theocracy**: A system of government in which priests rule in the name of God or a god. Montenegro was a theocracy from 1515 under the Metropolitanate.
- **Sanjak**: An administrative district of the Ottoman Empire. Montenegro was briefly a separate sanjak (1514–1528).
- **Knez**: The title for a chieftain of a Montenegrin clan in the early modern period.
- **Zbor**: An annual assembly of Montenegrin clans held in Cetinje.
- **Vladika**: The title "Prince-Bishop" held by the leaders of the Montenegrin theocracy.
- **Hybrid regime**: A political system combining democratic and authoritarian features, a classification applied to Montenegro by Freedom House.
- **D'Hondt method**: A highest averages method for allocating seats in party-list proportional representation, used in Montenegrin parliamentary elections.
- **Euroized**: The status of a country that uses the euro as its official currency without being part of the Eurozone, as Montenegro does.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Democratic Health**: Montenegro's classification has fluctuated between "flawed democracy" and "hybrid regime" in indices like the Economist Democracy Index, citing issues like state capture and corruption.
- **Ethnic and Religious Identity**: The population is multiethnic with no majority, and the relationship between the dominant Serbian Orthodox Church and the smaller, unrecognized Montenegrin Orthodox Church is a source of tension, as seen in the 2018 Law on Religion protests.
- **EU Accession Timeline**: While a long-term goal, the target date for EU membership has been repeatedly revised, with the latest expectation being 2027-2028.
- **Political Instability**: Since 2020, Montenegro has experienced frequent government changes, including a failed coup attempt in 2016 and multiple no-confidence votes.

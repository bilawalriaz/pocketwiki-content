# Slovakia

## Overview
Slovakia is a landlocked Central European nation with a population of over 5.4 million. Its history is defined by successive rule by larger powers—Great Moravia, the Kingdom of Hungary, the Habsburg Empire, and Czechoslovakia—before achieving full independence in 1993. Today, it is a developed, high-income economy and a member of the European Union and NATO, known for its automotive industry and mountainous landscapes.

## Timeline
- **5th-6th centuries** — Slavs arrive in the territory of present-day Slovakia.
- **c. 830** — Great Moravia is established, unifying Slavic tribes.
- **863** — Saints Cyril and Methodius arrive in Great Moravia, creating the first Slavic alphabet.
- **1000** — The Kingdom of Hungary is established, with Slovakia as an integral part.
- **1536** — Bratislava becomes the capital of the Kingdom of Hungary under Habsburg rule.
- **1848** — The Slovak Uprising begins, cementing Slovak national identity.
- **1918** — Czechoslovakia is proclaimed, incorporating Slovak territory.
- **1939** — The first Slovak Republic is established as a client state of Nazi Germany.
- **1945** — Czechoslovakia is re-established after World War II.
- **1948** — The Communist Party takes control in a Soviet-backed coup.
- **1989** — The Velvet Revolution peacefully ends Communist rule.
- **1 January 1993** — Slovakia becomes an independent state after the dissolution of Czechoslovakia (Velvet Divorce).

## Body

### Early History and Statehood
Slavic tribes settled in the region in the 5th-6th centuries. The first significant Slavic state entity was the Principality of Nitra, which became part of Great Moravia around 830. Great Moravia's cultural peak came with the mission of Cyril and Methodius in 863. After Great Moravia's collapse in the early 10th century, the territory was integrated into the Kingdom of Hungary, where it remained for nearly a millennium. This period saw demographic changes, including the arrival of Germans and Jews, and was marked by conflicts with the Ottoman Empire, which controlled southern parts in the 16th-17th centuries.

### National Awakening and Czechoslovakia
A Slovak national identity solidified in the 19th century, notably through the 1848 Uprising. After World War I and the collapse of Austria-Hungary, Slovaks joined with Czechs to form Czechoslovakia in 1918. This state was democratic but faced pressures from revanchist neighbors. Following the 1938 Munich Agreement, Slovakia became a separate, fascist client state allied with Nazi Germany during World War II, participating in the Holocaust. Czechoslovakia was re-established in 1945.

### Communist Rule and Independence
A 1948 Soviet-backed coup made Czechoslovakia a communist state. A period of liberalization, the Prague Spring, was crushed by a Warsaw Pact invasion in 1968. Communist rule ended with the Velvet Revolution in 1989. Political differences led to the peaceful dissolution of Czechoslovakia, and Slovakia became an independent democratic republic on 1 January 1993.

### Modern Slovakia
Since independence, Slovakia has transitioned to a market economy and integrated into Western institutions, joining the EU and NATO in 2004 and adopting the euro in 2009. The economy is advanced and heavily reliant on automotive manufacturing, making it the world's largest per-capita car producer. Politically, the country has experienced periods of instability, with debates over corruption, media freedom, and its foreign policy orientation, particularly regarding Russia and Ukraine.

## Terms
- **Great Moravia:** A 9th-century Slavic state considered a precursor to modern Czech and Slovak statehood.
- **Velvet Divorce:** The peaceful dissolution of Czechoslovakia into the Czech Republic and Slovakia on 1 January 1993.
- **Eurozone:** The group of European Union member states that have adopted the euro as their currency.
- **Schengen Area:** A zone where internal border controls have been abolished, allowing free movement of people.
- **Visegrád Group (V4):** A cultural and political alliance of four Central European countries: Slovakia, Czech Republic, Poland, and Hungary.
- **Bryndza:** A salty sheep milk cheese, a key ingredient in Slovakia's national dish, bryndzové halušky.
- **Tatra Tiger:** A nickname for Slovakia in the 2000s, referencing its rapid economic growth.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The exact borders and territorial extent of Great Moravia under King Svätopluk I remain disputed by modern historians.
- The precise date of Great Moravia's collapse is debated, with some historians placing it at 902 and others at 907.
- Slovakia's current political direction is a subject of debate, with concerns raised about democratic backsliding, corruption, and its pro-Russia stance under the Fico government, contrasted with its EU and NATO commitments.

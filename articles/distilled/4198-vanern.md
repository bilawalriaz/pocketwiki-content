# Vänern

## Overview
Vänern is the largest lake in Sweden and the European Union, and the third-largest in Europe. Located in southwestern Sweden, it is a significant geographical feature formed by glacial activity, with a unique history of connection to the sea and a diverse ecosystem. Its size and location have made it historically important for transportation, fishing, and mythology.

## Timeline
- **c. 10,000 years ago** — Lake reaches its present form after the Quaternary glaciation; initially part of a strait between Kattegat and the Gulf of Bothnia.
- **Post-glacial period** — Becomes part of Ancylus Lake (the Baltic basin) via a strait at Degerfors, Värmland.
- **Later post-glacial** — Cut off from the Baltic Sea by further land uplift.
- **6th century** — The Battle on the Ice of Lake Vänern occurs, recorded in Norse sagas and referenced in *Beowulf*.
- **13th century** — Snorri Sturluson records the myth of Gefjon, likely originally about Vänern, in the *Prose Edda*.
- **1446** — Lidköping is chartered.
- **1583** — Mariestad is chartered.
- **1584** — Karlstad is chartered.
- **19th century** — The Göta Canal is built, linking Vänern to the east coast via Lake Vättern.
- **May 6, 2009** — A Viking ship is discovered at the bottom of the lake.

## Body

### Formation and Geography
The lake's southeastern basin is a depression formed by the erosion of Paleozoic sedimentary rock during the Quaternary glaciation. Its present shape was finalized about 10,000 years ago. After the ice melted, the area was submerged, creating a strait. As the land rebounded from the ice (post-glacial rebound), Vänern first became part of the Baltic basin's Ancylus Lake before being cut off by continued uplift. This former marine connection explains the presence of non-freshwater species like the amphipod *Monoporeia affinis*.

Vänern covers 5,655 km², with a surface 44 m above sea level and a maximum depth of 106 m. It is divided into the western Dalbosjön and eastern Värmlandsjön. Its main inflow is the Klarälven river, and it drains southwest via Göta älv to the Kattegat. The water level is regulated by the Vargön Hydroelectric Power Station.

### Human and Economic Significance
The lake has supported surrounding towns for centuries through fishing and transportation. Key towns include Karlstad, Lidköping, and Vänersborg. The Göta Canal, built in the 19th century, created a waterway linking Vänern to the east coast via Lake Vättern. The lake features in Norse mythology; a story in Snorri Sturluson's *Prose Edda* about the origin of Zealand is believed to have originally described Vänern. A 6th-century battle on its ice is recorded in sagas and *Beowulf*.

### Ecology and Environment
Vänern hosts diverse fish populations. The most common open-water fish are smelt and vendace. Important large species include brown trout and zander. The lake has five distinguished whitefish species. Sport fishing is free and unregulated, while commercial fishing requires permission. Threats to fish habitats include water cultivation, pollution, and M74 syndrome.

Environmental monitoring shows generally stable water quality, though visibility has slightly decreased due to increased algae. Nitrogen levels, problematic from the 1970s to 1990s, are now regulated. Some bays suffer from eutrophication. Common birds include terns, gulls, and great cormorants, whose return has supported an increase in white-tailed sea eagles.

## Terms
- **Quaternary glaciation**: The most recent period of repeated glaciations, ending about 11,700 years ago, which shaped Vänern's basin.
- **Post-glacial rebound**: The rise of land masses after the removal of the weight of ice sheets, which isolated Vänern from the sea.
- **Ancylus Lake**: A freshwater stage of the Baltic Sea basin that existed after the initial post-glacial sea strait closed.
- **Sub-Cambrian peneplain**: A gently tilted, low-relief erosion surface from the Cambrian period, re-exposed by glacial erosion.
- **Eutrophication**: Excessive nutrient enrichment in water bodies, leading to algal blooms and oxygen depletion.
- **M74 syndrome**: A disease affecting salmonid fish embryos, identified as a threat to Vänern's fish populations.
- **Göta älv**: The river draining Vänern southwest to the Kattegat strait.
- **Göta Canal**: A 19th-century waterway connecting Vänern to the east coast via Lake Vättern.

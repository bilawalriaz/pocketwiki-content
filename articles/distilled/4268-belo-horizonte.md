# Belo Horizonte

## Overview
Belo Horizonte is the capital of Minas Gerais, Brazil's second-most populous state, and the country's sixth-largest city. It is historically significant as Brazil's first planned modern city, constructed in the 1890s to replace Ouro Preto as the state capital. The city is a major economic, cultural, and sporting hub, known for its modernist architecture, vibrant music scene, and role as a host for major international events like the FIFA World Cup.

## Timeline
- **1701** — João Leite da Silva Ortiz founds the farm "Curral d'el Rey," the precursor to the city.
- **1889** — Brazil becomes a republic, leading to plans for a new, modern capital for Minas Gerais.
- **1893** — Curral Del Rey is selected as the site for the new capital, named "Cidade de Minas."
- **1897** — Cidade de Minas is inaugurated, designed by urbanist Aarão Reis.
- **1906** — The city is renamed Belo Horizonte ("Beautiful Horizon").
- **1940s** — Mayor Juscelino Kubitschek commissions Oscar Niemeyer to design the Pampulha Complex.
- **1950** — The city hosts matches for the FIFA World Cup.
- **2009** — Belo Horizonte wins the first "Future Policy Award" for its food security programs.
- **2014** — The city is a host venue for the FIFA World Cup, including the Mineirão stadium.

## Body

### History and Planning
The region was settled in the early 18th century, but the modern city was planned in the 1890s to replace the historic mining town of Ouro Preto as the capital of Minas Gerais. Urbanist Aarão Reis designed the city with a symmetrical street grid, inspired by Washington, D.C. Inaugurated in 1897, the city grew rapidly, aided by government subsidies. Its name was changed to Belo Horizonte in 1906. In the 1940s, then-mayor Juscelino Kubitschek and architect Oscar Niemeyer began shaping its modern identity with the Pampulha Complex, a precursor to their later work on Brasília.

### Geography and Climate
Belo Horizonte is built on hills and surrounded by mountains, including the Serra do Curral, which forms its southern boundary. The city has a tropical savanna climate (Aw) due to its inland location and elevation of 852 m (2,795 ft). Summers are humid and rainy, while winters are dry and sunny. The metropolitan area, or "Grande BH," is the third most populous in Brazil, comprising 34 municipalities with a population of around six million.

### Economy
The service sector dominates the economy, accounting for 85% of the city's GDP. Historically an industrial hub for steel and metallurgy due to the region's mineral wealth, Belo Horizonte has diversified into technology, biotechnology, and business tourism. Major companies like Google and CEMIG have a presence here. The city's GDP was R$42 billion in 2008.

### Culture and Architecture
Belo Horizonte is a center for Brazilian modernist architecture, exemplified by the Pampulha Complex, which includes the Church of São Francisco de Assis by Oscar Niemeyer. The city is renowned for its music, having birthed the influential Clube da Esquina movement and being known as the "Capital of Metal." The Circuito Cultural Praça da Liberdade is Brazil's largest cultural circuit. The city is also famous for its neighborhood bars and the annual "Comida di Buteco" festival.

### Sport
Football is the most popular sport, with major teams Atlético Mineiro, Cruzeiro, and América Mineiro. The city's iconic Mineirão stadium, rebuilt for the 2014 World Cup, hosted the infamous 7-1 semifinal. Belo Horizonte also hosted matches for the 1950 World Cup, the 2013 Confederations Cup, and the 2016 Olympic football tournament.

## Terms
- **Planned city:** A city whose layout and construction are pre-designed and controlled, rather than developing organically.
- **Metropolitan area ("Grande BH"):** The legally defined region of 34 municipalities centered on Belo Horizonte, with a combined population of about six million.
- **Craton of São Francisco:** A large, tectonically stable geological unit of central-eastern Brazil on which Belo Horizonte is built.
- **Köppen climate classification (Aw):** A system for categorizing climates; "Aw" denotes a tropical savanna climate with distinct wet and dry seasons.
- **Pampulha Complex:** A set of modernist buildings and a lake in Belo Horizonte, designed by Oscar Niemeyer in the 1940s, considered a landmark of Brazilian architecture.
- **Clube da Esquina:** An influential Brazilian musical movement that originated in Belo Horizonte in the mid-1960s.
- **Circuito Cultural Praça da Liberdade:** A large cultural circuit in central Belo Horizonte featuring eleven museums and cultural spaces in historic buildings.

## Debates and Open Questions
The source notes that the city's original plan prohibited workers from living inside the urban area defined by Avenida do Contorno, which led to accelerated, less-planned growth outside this core. The intense population growth, especially in the last 20 years of the 20th century, was not anticipated by the original planners, presenting ongoing urban challenges.

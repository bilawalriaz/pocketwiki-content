# Damascus

## Overview
Damascus is the capital and largest city of Syria, renowned as one of the oldest continuously inhabited cities in the world. Its strategic location at a crossroads of ancient trade routes has made it a major cultural and political center for millennia, serving as the capital of the Umayyad Caliphate and a key city in the Roman, Crusader, Ayyubid, Mamluk, and Ottoman empires. Today, it remains Syria's political heart, though its global standing has been severely impacted by the Syrian civil war.

## Timeline
- **c. 6300 BC** — Possible early settlement at Tell Ramad on Damascus's outskirts.
- **15th century BC** — First documented mention of Damascus (as *tjmsqw*) in Egyptian records under Thutmose III.
- **965 BC** — Ezron captures Damascus, founding the independent Aramaean kingdom of Aram-Damascus.
- **634 CE** — Damascus is conquered by the Arab Muslim general Khalid ibn al-Walid.
- **661–750** — Damascus serves as the capital of the Umayyad Caliphate.
- **750** — The Abbasid dynasty conquers Damascus; the capital of the Islamic empire moves to Baghdad.
- **1079** — Seljuk Turks take control, making Damascus a capital again.
- **1260** — Mongols briefly capture Damascus; the city soon becomes a Mamluk provincial capital.
- **1516** — The Ottoman Empire conquers Damascus, ruling it for 400 years.
- **1918** — Forces of the Arab Revolt enter Damascus; Faisal is proclaimed King of Syria.
- **1920** — French forces capture Damascus, making it the capital of their mandate.
- **1946** — France withdraws, granting full independence to Syria with Damascus as its capital.
- **1979** — The Ancient City of Damascus is listed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
- **2012–present** — Damascus experiences intense fighting during the Syrian civil war.
- **2024** — Syrian rebels enter Damascus, leading to the collapse of the Ba'athist government.

## Body

### Geography and Climate
Damascus is situated on a plateau 680 meters (2,230 ft) above sea level, 80 kilometers (50 mi) inland from the Mediterranean. It lies in the rain shadow of the Anti-Lebanon Mountains, which block precipitation, giving the city an arid climate. The Barada River, historically fed by mountain snowmelt, provided water and created the fertile Ghouta oasis surrounding the city. The river's flow has diminished significantly due to urban expansion, and its lower aquifers are now polluted.

### Ancient and Medieval History
First settled in the 3rd millennium BC, Damascus rose to prominence as the capital of the Aramaean kingdom of Aram-Damascus in the 10th century BC. It was later conquered by the Assyrians, Persians, and Alexander the Great. Under Roman rule (from 64 BC), the city was redesigned with a grid plan, the remnants of which define the Old City today. After the Muslim conquest in 634 CE, Damascus became the capital of the vast Umayyad Caliphate (661–750), during which the Umayyad Mosque was built. The Abbasid overthrow in 750 shifted power to Baghdad, and Damascus declined. It regained importance under the Seljuq Turks, Ayyubids (notably Saladin), and Mamluks, serving as a key center of Islamic learning and commerce despite Mongol raids and Timur's sack in 1401.

### Modern History and Conflict
Damascus was the center of Arab nationalist sentiment against Ottoman rule and was proclaimed the capital of the short-lived Arab Kingdom of Syria in 1918. It then became the capital of the French Mandate until Syrian independence in 1946. The city was the site of the 1982 Hama massacre's precursor unrest and has been the seat of the Ba'athist government since 1963. During the Syrian civil war, which began in 2011, Damascus saw intense fighting, particularly in its suburbs like Ghouta. The conflict severely damaged its infrastructure and global reputation, leading to it being ranked the world's least livable city in 2019. In December 2024, rebel forces entered the city, toppling the Assad government.

### Demographics and Culture
Damascus is predominantly Arab, with significant Kurdish, Palestinian, and other minorities. The population is majority Sunni Muslim, with sizeable Alawite, Shia, and Christian communities (10–15%). The city is a major center for Islamic scholarship, historically hosting numerous madrasas, and is considered by some the fourth-holiest city in Islam. Its cultural life includes traditional crafts like copper engraving, a vibrant café scene, and numerous museums. The Old City, a UNESCO site, contains over 125 heritage sites, including the Umayyad Mosque, the Citadel, and the ancient Straight Street.

## Terms
- **Aram-Damascus**: An ancient Aramaean kingdom centered on Damascus, prominent from the 10th to 8th centuries BC.
- **Umayyad Caliphate**: The second of the four major Islamic caliphates (661–750), which had Damascus as its capital.
- **Ghouta**: The historically fertile, irrigated oasis surrounding Damascus, watered by the Barada River.
- **Decapolis**: A league of ten cities on the eastern frontier of the Roman Empire, of which Damascus was a member.
- **Madrasa**: An Islamic educational institution, especially for religious instruction.
- **Mamluk**: A soldier of slave origin who served in the armies of various Muslim rulers, forming a dynasty that ruled Egypt and Syria (1250–1517).
- **Bimaristan**: A hospital or medical center in the medieval Islamic world.
- **Dhimmi**: A non-Muslim subject living in an Islamic state, granted protection in exchange for paying a tax (jizya).
- **Ba'athist**: Relating to the Ba'ath Party, the secular, Arab nationalist political party that ruled Syria from 1963 to 2024.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The exact etymology of the city's ancient name, *tjmsqw*, is uncertain.
- The extent of Nabatean control over Damascus in the first century AD is debated, with evidence being inconclusive.
- The impact of the 2024 rebel takeover on the city's future governance, stability, and reconstruction remains an open and critical question.

# Minoan civilization

## Overview
The Minoan civilization was a Bronze Age culture centered on the island of Crete, often regarded as the first advanced civilization in Europe. It flourished through extensive trade, monumental architecture, and distinctive art, but its origins, political structure, and ultimate decline remain subjects of scholarly debate due to its undeciphered writing systems.

## Timeline
- **c. 7000 BC** — First evidence of Neolithic farming communities on Crete.
- **c. 3100 BC** — Development from local Neolithic culture into Early Minoan period.
- **c. 2000 BC** — Complex urban settlements and first palaces emerge (Protopalatial period).
- **c. 1700 BC** — Beginning of the Neopalatial period; palaces rebuilt, Minoan influence peaks.
- **c. 1600 BC** — Eruption of Thera volcano during Late Minoan IA.
- **c. 1450 BC** — Severe destructions across Crete; end of Neopalatial society.
- **c. 1450–1420 BC** — Mycenaean Greeks dominate Crete; Linear B script introduced.
- **c. 1100 BC** — End of the hybrid Minoan-Mycenaean culture following Late Bronze Age collapse.
- **1952** — Decipherment of Linear B script, revealing Mycenaean Greek.

## Body

### Origins and Chronology
The Minoan civilization developed from local Neolithic predecessors around 3100 BC. Its chronology is divided into Early, Middle, and Late Minoan periods, with further subdivisions. An alternative system uses Prepalatial, Protopalatial, Neopalatial, and Postpalatial phases. Absolute dating is difficult; the eruption of Thera, a key chronological marker, is placed around 1600 BC by carbon dating but roughly a century later by synchronism with Egyptian records.

### Society and Economy
Minoan society was organized around large palace complexes (e.g., Knossos, Phaistos) that served as administrative, religious, and economic redistribution centers, not primarily royal residences. The economy was based on agriculture (grains, olives, grapes) and extensive maritime trade, exporting crafts and agricultural products for raw metals. The political structure is unknown; it may have been a collection of independent polities or a unified state. The role of women appears prominent in art and possibly religion, but the society was likely not a matriarchy.

### Writing and Language
The Minoans used two undeciphered scripts: Cretan hieroglyphs and Linear A. The language they encoded is unknown and does not appear to belong to well-attested families like Indo-European. After c. 1450 BC, the Mycenaean conquerors adapted Linear A into Linear B to write their Greek language, which was deciphered in 1952.

### Art and Architecture
Minoan art is characterized by vibrant frescoes, pottery (e.g., Marine Style), and metalwork, often depicting nature, marine life, and ritual scenes like bull-leaping. Architecture featured large, labyrinthine palaces with central courts, advanced plumbing, and distinctive inverted columns. The "Minoan peace" hypothesis—a lack of warfare—is debated; while fortifications are rare, weapons and some defensive structures exist.

### Religion
Minoan religion involved worship at peak sanctuaries and caves, with a pantheon likely including a prominent mother goddess and a young male god. Sacred symbols included the labrys (double axe), horns of consecration, and bulls. The exact nature of deities and rituals is unclear due to the lack of deciphered texts.

### Decline and Legacy
After severe destructions c. 1450 BC, Crete came under Mycenaean control, creating a hybrid culture. This ended around 1100 BC with the Late Bronze Age collapse. The civilization was forgotten until rediscovered by archaeologists in the early 20th century. Genetic studies show Minoans were genetically similar to Neolithic Europeans and modern Cretans.

## Terms
- **Neopalatial**: The peak period of Minoan culture (c. 1700–1450 BC) when palaces were rebuilt and influence expanded.
- **Linear A**: An undeciphered writing system used by the Minoans for their unknown language.
- **Linear B**: A script adapted from Linear A by Mycenaean Greeks to write early Greek; deciphered in 1952.
- **Labrys**: A double-headed axe, a common Minoan religious symbol.
- **Horns of Consecration**: A stylized symbol of bull's horns used in Minoan architecture and religion.
- **Palace economy**: A system where centralized institutions collected and redistributed agricultural surplus.
- **Thera eruption**: A massive volcanic event on Santorini c. 1600 BC that disrupted the Aegean.
- **Mycenaean Greeks**: The mainland Greek civilization that dominated Crete after c. 1450 BC.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Political Structure**: Was Minoan Crete a unified kingdom, a collection of independent city-states, or a theocracy?
- **Function of Palaces**: Were they primarily religious, economic, or administrative centers?
- **Cause of Destructions**: Were the c. 1450 BC destructions caused by Mycenaean invasion, internal revolt, or natural disasters?
- **"Minoan Peace"**: Did the Minoans avoid warfare, or is the evidence simply not preserved?
- **Language Identity**: To which language family does the Minoan language belong?

# New Caledonia

## Overview
New Caledonia is a French overseas territory in the southwest Pacific, notable for its unique political status, significant nickel resources, and a complex demographic history shaped by colonization and a powerful indigenous Kanak independence movement. Its ongoing political evolution, including recent referendums and a proposed new "state" status, makes it a key case study in decolonization and self-determination.

## Timeline
- **c. 1100 BC – AD 200** — First human settlements by Lapita culture peoples.
- **4 September 1774** — British explorer James Cook sights and names the islands.
- **24 September 1853** — France formally annexes New Caledonia.
- **1864** — Nickel discovered; New Caledonia becomes a penal colony.
- **1878 & 1917** — Major Kanak uprisings against colonial rule.
- **June 1940** — Territory rejects Vichy France, supports Free French.
- **1946** — New Caledonia becomes an overseas territory of France.
- **1988** — Matignon Agreements signed after violent "Events," ending a decade of conflict.
- **5 May 1998** — Nouméa Accord signed, setting a 20-year transition toward greater autonomy.
- **4 November 2018** — First independence referendum: 56.7% vote to remain French.
- **4 October 2020** — Second referendum: 53.4% vote to remain French.
- **12 December 2021** — Third referendum (boycotted by pro-independence side): 96% vote to remain French.
- **May 2024** — Riots erupt over proposed electoral reform.
- **July 2025** — Agreement reached to create a "State of New Caledonia" with greater autonomy, pending ratification and a 2026 referendum.

## Body

### Geography and Environment
New Caledonia is part of the ancient continent Zealandia. Its main island, Grande Terre, is divided by a central mountain range. The territory possesses one of the world's largest lagoons and a barrier reef system designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site. The climate is tropical, moderated by oceanic influences. The islands are a global biodiversity hotspot with exceptionally high rates of endemic species in flora (e.g., unique gymnosperms, the flowering plant *Amborella trichopoda*) and fauna (e.g., the tool-making New Caledonian crow, the flightless kagu). However, its nickel industry results in high per-capita CO2 emissions.

### History and Colonization
Human settlement began with Lapita peoples. European contact started with Cook in 1774. France annexed the territory in 1853, establishing it as a penal colony in 1864. The discovery of nickel in 1864 drove economic development but led to the marginalization of the indigenous Kanak people, who were confined to reservations. This sparked major rebellions in 1878 and 1917. The Kanak population declined sharply due to conflict and introduced diseases. During World War II, the territory supported the Free French and became a crucial Allied base.

### Politics and the Independence Movement
New Caledonia has a unique *sui generis* status within the French Republic. The post-war period saw the rise of a pro-independence movement, primarily among Kanaks. A period of violent conflict known as "the Events" (1976–1988) culminated in the Matignon Agreements, which established a power-sharing system. The subsequent Nouméa Accord (1998) initiated a 20-year transition, including three scheduled referendums on independence, all of which resulted in votes to remain French, though the third was boycotted. A 2024 riot over electoral reform led to a new political deal in 2025: the creation of a "State of New Caledonia" with greater autonomy, to be confirmed by a 2026 referendum. Kanak society maintains a parallel system of customary authority.

### Demographics and Society
The population (264,596 in 2025) is diverse. The Kanak people are the largest ethnic group (41.2% in 2019), predominant in the North and Loyalty Islands provinces. Europeans (Caldoches and Metropolitan French) are concentrated in the wealthier South Province. Other significant communities include Wallisians, Futunians, and Tahitians. French is the universal language, but 28 Kanak languages are spoken. Christianity is the main religion, split between Catholicism and Protestantism.

### Economy
The economy is one of the largest in the Pacific, heavily dependent on nickel mining, which holds about 25% of the world's resources. This creates significant inequality between the developed South Province and the less developed North and Loyalty Islands. The territory receives substantial financial support from France. Tourism is underdeveloped. The currency is the CFP franc, pegged to the euro.

## Terms
- **Sui generis collectivity**: A unique legal status within the French Republic, granting New Caledonia a degree of autonomy not found in other overseas territories.
- **Kanak**: The indigenous Melanesian people of New Caledonia, whose social organization is traditionally based on clans.
- **Caldoches**: Europeans born in New Caledonia with ancestral ties to early French settlers, often living in rural areas.
- **Zoreilles/Métros**: Metropolitan French-born migrants to New Caledonia.
- **Nouméa Accord**: The 1998 agreement that defined the 20-year transition period and the process for independence referendums.
- **Customary authority**: The traditional Kanak system of governance based on clans, tribes, and chiefdoms, with jurisdiction over civil matters like marriage and land.
- **Blackbirding**: The euphemism for the 19th-century practice of kidnapping or deceiving Pacific Islanders for forced labor.
- **Zealandia**: The ancient continent that broke off from Gondwana, of which New Caledonia is a fragment.

## Debates and Open questions
- **Future status**: The 2025 agreement to create a "State of New Caledonia" is a major concession by France but has been rejected by the pro-independence FLNKS coalition, which demands a prior independence referendum. The outcome of the proposed 2026 referendum is uncertain.
- **Electoral reform**: The 2024 riots were triggered by a French government bill to expand the electorate, which pro-independence groups saw as a threat to their political influence. The bill was scrapped, but the underlying issue of voter eligibility remains contentious.
- **Economic dependency**: The territory's heavy reliance on nickel and French subsidies poses long-term challenges, especially amid global market volatility and the need for sustainable development.

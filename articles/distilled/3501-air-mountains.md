# Aïr Mountains

## Overview
The Aïr Mountains are a triangular massif in northern Niger's Sahara Desert. Despite its arid location, its altitude creates a "Sahel climate island" that supports unique biodiversity, human communities, and significant archaeological sites, making it a critical ecological and cultural refuge.

## Timeline
- **c. 6000 BCE – 1000 CE** — Period of rock art creation, depicting pastoral life and later warfare.
- **3rd millennium BC** — Process of desertification begins; Tuareg migration into the region.
- **8th–9th centuries** — Tuareg tribes pushed south by Arab invaders; Hausa present in southern Aïr.
- **12th century onward** — Successive Tuareg Kels (tribal groups) control the area.
- **Early 15th century** — Agadez is an outpost of the Songhai Empire.
- **16th century** — Area falls under the Tuareg Sultanate of Agadez.
- **Late 19th century** — French colonial forces arrive.
- **1917** — Tuareg leader Kaocen rises against the French; brutal punitive expedition follows.
- **Mid-20th century** — Sedentary farming expands.
- **1970s–1980s** — Famines end agricultural expansion; towns shrink.
- **1990–1995** — First Tuareg rebellion and government reprisals depopulate villages.
- **Post-1995** — Peace and uranium mining at Arlit bring growth and tourism.
- **2004** — Locust invasion contributes to Second Tuareg Rebellion (2007–2009).

## Body

### Geology and Landscape
The Aïr is a Precambrian to Cenozoic massif of dark peralkaline granite intrusions, rising from a rocky plateau. It consists of nine nearly circular massifs, including Niger's highest point, Mont Idoukal-n-Taghès (2022 m). The terrain features high plateaus, mountain ranges, and broad, sandy valleys (wadis) that once held rivers and now contain waterborne clay and silt. Underground watercourses in these valleys create year-round oases. The massif contains volcanic features like the extinct Arakao caldera and one of the world's largest ring dike systems. To the west, the Iullemmeden Basin contains uranium mineralisation sourced from the Aïr granites.

### Climate and Ecology
Despite low rainfall (50–160 mm/year), the Aïr's altitude (averaging 500–900 m) creates a Sahel climate, making it greener than the surrounding Sahara, especially after seasonal rains. Dry wadi valleys, called "koris," channel rainwater into stone pools (gueltas), forming oases that support forage and farming. The high Bagzane plateau receives enough rain for intensive agriculture. The region's flora connects Saharan and Sahelian zones, with over 430 vascular species recorded. Tree species like *Vachellia tortilis* and *Balanites aegyptiaca* are common, but severe drought and grazing have caused a deficit in tree regeneration. High-altitude areas host rare and endemic species, such as a subspecies of olive (*Olea europaea subsp. laperrinei*), giving them high conservation value.

### Population and History
The town of Agadez is the capital of the Aïr. Historically, the Tuareg population was largely nomadic, herding camels and goats, while sedentary groups, including the Ikelan (a servile caste), tended date palm oases. Oasis agriculture products were traded via camel caravans (Azalai) for salt and goods. The region is famed for rock art from 6000 BCE to 1000 CE, including the "Dabous Giraffes." After Arab invasions pushed Tuareg south, successive Kels controlled the area, which was later part of the Songhai Empire and then the Tuareg Sultanate of Agadez. French colonization in the late 19th century weakened Tuareg control, leading to rebellions, notably Kaocen's revolt in 1917, and brutal punitive expeditions. In the mid-20th century, sedentary farming expanded until famines in the 1970s–80s reversed this trend. The 1990–95 Tuareg rebellion and subsequent peace, followed by uranium mining, reshaped the region's demographics and economy, though unrest continues.

## Terms
- **Massif:** A compact group of mountains.
- **Wadi:** A valley or streambed that is dry except during the rainy season.
- **Guelta:** A stone pool that holds rainwater.
- **Peralkaline granite:** A type of igneous rock with a specific chemical composition, appearing dark in the field.
- **Azalai:** Traditional Tuareg camel caravans for trade.
- **Kel:** A Tuareg tribal group or confederation.
- **Ikelan:** A servile caste within Tuareg society.
- **Sahel:** The ecoclimatic and biogeographic zone of transition between the Sahara Desert to the north and the Sudanian Savanna to the south.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The exact status of the endangered African wild dog in the region is uncertain; it may be extirpated due to human pressures.
- The precise impact of the 2004 locust invasion and subsequent rebellions on the region's stability and tourism industry is ongoing.

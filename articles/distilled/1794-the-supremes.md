# The Supremes

## Overview

The Supremes were an American vocal group formed in Detroit, Michigan, in 1959 as the Primettes. They became the premier act of Motown Records during the 1960s, the most commercially successful of Motown's acts, and the most successful American vocal group, with twelve number 1 singles on the Billboard Hot 1. Their breakthrough is considered to have made it possible for future African-American R&B and soul musicians to find mainstream success. The group's story also encompasses significant changes in lineup, the rise of lead singer Diana Ross, and the eventual disbandment in 1977.

## Timeline

- **1959** — Formed in Detroit as the Primettes; original members: Florence Ballard, Mary Wilson, Diana Ross, Betty McGlown.
- **1960** — Barbara Martin replaces McGlown; the group signs with Motown in 1961 as the Supremes.
- **1962** — Martin leaves; Ross, Ballard, and Wilson continue as a trio.
- **1964** — "Where Did Our Love Go" becomes their first #1 hit; followed by four consecutive #1s.
- **1965** — The group becomes international stars; releases first album by an all-female group to top the Billboard 200.
- **1967** — Berry Gordy renames the group "Diana Ross & the Supremes"; Cindy Birdsong replaces Florence Ballard.
- **1969** — "Someday We'll Be Together" becomes their 12th and final #1 hit; Ross's solo career is announced.
- **1970** — Ross leaves; Jean Terrell replaces her; group name reverts to the Supremes.
- **1972–1973** — Lineup changes: Lynda Laurence and later Scherry Payne join; popularity and sales wane.
- **1976** — "I'm Gonna Let My Heart Do the Walking" is their final Top 40 hit.
- **1977** — The group disbands after a farewell concert in London.

## Body

### Origins and early years (1959–1963)

In 1958, Florence Ballard, a junior high student in Detroit's Brewster-Douglass Housing Projects, met Paul Williams and Eddie Kendricks of the singing group the Primes. Their manager, Milton Jenkins, decided to create a sister group called the Primettes. Ballard recruited her best friend Mary Wilson, and Paul Williams recruited Diana Ross. Mentored by Jenkins and guided by songwriter Jesse Greer, the quartet performed at local events, developing a style inspired by the collegiate dress of Frankie Lymon & the Teenagers. Guitarist Marvin Tarplin was added to allow live singing instead of lip-synching.

After winning a Detroit-Windsor festival in July 1960, the Primettes sought a record deal. Ross asked Smokey Robinson, an old neighbor and leader of the Miracles, to arrange an audition with Motown executive Berry Gordy. Gordy felt they were too young and inexperienced, but the group persisted, contributing hand claps and background vocals at the studio. In January 1961, Gordy agreed to sign them, on condition they change their name; Ballard chose "the Supremes" from a list. Between 1961 and 1963, they released six singles, none charting in the Top 40, earning them the nickname "no-hit Supremes." In December 1963, "When the Lovelight Starts Shining Through His Eyes" peaked at number 23.

### Breakthrough and impact (1964–1966)

In late 1963, Gordy chose Diana Ross as the official lead singer. In spring 1964, the group recorded "Where Did Our Love Go," a song rejected by the Marvelettes. It reached number 1 in August 1964, followed by four more consecutive number 1 hits: "Baby Love," "Come See About Me," "Stop! In the Name of Love," and "Back in My Arms Again." In January 1965, each member received $100,000 in earnings.

The Supremes embraced a glamorous image at the behest of Gordy and Maxine Powell, who ran Motown's in-house finishing school. Unlike many contemporaries, Ross sang in a thin, calm voice, and the group wore high-fashion gowns and performed graceful choreography by Cholly Atkins. They became one of the first black musical acts to achieve complete and sustained crossover success, appearing regularly on television including 17 times on The Ed Sullivan Show. In 1966, their album The Supremes A' Go-Go became the first album by an all-female group to reach number 1 on the US Billboard 200.

### Changes and Ross's departure (1967–1969)

Tension grew as other Motown performers felt Gordy lavished too much attention on Ross. In early 1967, the group was renamed "Diana Ross & the Supremes." Ballard, suffering from depression and alcoholism, was replaced by Cindy Birdsong in July 1967 after a public incident. Ballard's release was finalized in 1968; she later sued Motown unsuccessfully and died in 1976.

Holland–Dozier–Holland left Motown in early 1968 over royalties. From 1967 to 1969, only six of eleven singles reached the Top 20, with "Love Child" (1968) reaching number 1. The group's pop-based sound was eclipsed by the gospel-based soul of artists like Aretha Franklin, and they were criticized for not being "black enough." In 1969, Motown planned a solo career for Ross, selecting Jean Terrell as her replacement. "Someday We'll Be Together," recorded as a solo track for Ross, was released as a Diana Ross & the Supremes single and became their twelfth and final number 1 hit.

### Later years and disbandment (1970–1977)

Diana Ross & the Supremes gave their final performance on January 14, 1970, in Las Vegas. The Terrell-led Supremes scored hits including "Up the Ladder to the Roof," "Stoned Love," and "Nathan Jones." However, Motown's move to Los Angeles and limited promotion led to declining popularity. Lineup changes continued: Cindy Birdsong left in 1972 (replaced by Lynda Laurence), returned in 1973, and left again in 1976 (replaced by Susaye Greene). Jean Terrell left in 1973 and was replaced by Scherrie Payne. The final version of the group released two albums with Holland-Dozier-Holland. Their final Top 40 hit was "I'm Gonna Let My Heart Do the Walking" (1976). The group disbanded after a farewell concert on June 12, 1977, at Drury Lane Theater in London.

## Terms

- **Motown Records**: A Detroit-based record label founded by Berry Gordy, known for its polished pop-soul sound.
- **Holland–Dozier–Holland**: Motown's main songwriting and production team, responsible for most of the Supremes' hits.
- **Billboard Hot 100**: The standard American music chart ranking the most popular songs.
- **Crossover success**: Achieving popularity with both black and white audiences.
- **The Andantes**: Motown backup group; their members sometimes substituted for the Supremes on recordings.
- **The Primes**: The male group for which the Primettes were the sister act; members later formed the Temptations.
- **Hitsville U.S.A.**: The nickname for Motown's recording studio in Detroit.
- **Sock hop**: A casual dance event, typically at a school, where participants danced in socks.

## Debates and open questions

- **Ballard's dismissal**: Florence Ballard claimed that Gordy and Diana Ross conspired to force her out of the group; she sued for $8.7 million in 1971, but the court ruled in favor of Motown.
- **Authorship of recordings**: On many late-1960s singles, neither Mary Wilson nor Cindy Birdsong actually sang; session singers such as the Andantes replaced them, raising questions about the group's true recorded output.
- **The 2000 reunion tour**: The "Return to Love" tour featured Diana Ross with Payne and Laurence, none of whom had been in the group at the same time or sung on the original hit recordings; this caused fan disappointment and criticism.

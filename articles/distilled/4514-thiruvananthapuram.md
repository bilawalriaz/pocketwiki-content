# Thiruvananthapuram

## Overview
Thiruvananthapuram, also known as Trivandrum, is the capital city of the Indian state of Kerala. It is a multifaceted urban center serving as a political, cultural, IT, space research, and maritime hub. Its strategic location on India's southwest coast and its historical role as the capital of the former princely state of Travancore have shaped its development into a major academic and research center.

## Body

### Etymology and History
The city's name, Thiruvananthapuram, means "The City of Lord Anantha" in Malayalam, referring to the deity of the Sree Padmanabhaswamy Temple. It was officially called Trivandrum until 1991. The region was historically ruled by the Ay dynasty, a feudatory of the Cheras, before being conquered by the Kingdom of Venad in the 12th century. In the 18th century, Marthanda Varma expanded this kingdom into Travancore, making Thiruvananthapuram its capital in 1795. The city became a major cultural and intellectual center, especially under Maharajas Swathi Thirunal and Ayilyam Thirunal in the mid-19th century. After Indian independence in 1947, it became the capital of the new state of Kerala in 1956.

### Geography and Climate
Thiruvananthapuram is located on India's west coast near the southern tip of the mainland, characterized by undulating coastal hills. It lies in a moderately earthquake-prone zone (Seismic III Zone). The climate is a border between tropical savanna and tropical monsoon, with stable temperatures year-round (mean max 31–32°C, mean min 23–24°C). The city receives heavy rainfall, averaging 1,835 mm annually, primarily from the southwest monsoon starting in early June.

### Demographics and Administration
As of the 2011 census, the Thiruvananthapuram Municipal Corporation had a population of 957,730, with a literacy rate of 93.72%. The population is predominantly Hindu (68.5%), with significant Christian (16.7%) and Muslim (13.7%) minorities. Malayalam is the dominant language. The city is administered by the Thiruvananthapuram Municipal Corporation (TMC) and is also the seat of the Kerala state government.

### Economy and Infrastructure
The economy is driven by IT, education, aerospace, commerce, and tourism. Thiruvananthapuram is a major IT hub, contributing 40-45% of Kerala's software exports, with Technopark being the largest IT park in India by built-up area. The city is home to key Indian space research institutions, including the Vikram Sarabhai Space Centre (VSSC). Its port-based development centers on the Vizhinjam International Seaport, a deep-water transshipment port close to international shipping lanes. Transport includes an international airport (operational since 1935), major railway terminals, and a well-developed road network.

### Culture and Tourism
Known as the "Evergreen City of India," Thiruvananthapuram is a cultural hub with a legacy in arts, music, and literature. It is the birthplace of the Malayalam film industry. Key cultural landmarks include the Sree Padmanabhaswamy Temple, Napier Museum, and Kuthira Malika Palace. Tourism is significant, with attractions like Kovalam and Varkala beaches, backwaters, and the Agasthyamala Biosphere Reserve. The city hosts major festivals, including the Attukal Pongala, noted as the largest gathering of women in the world.

### Education and Research
Thiruvananthapuram is a major academic center, home to the University of Kerala, APJ Abdul Kalam Technological University, and premier research institutes. These include the Indian Institute of Space Science and Technology (IIST), the Indian Institute of Science Education and Research (IISER), and the Vikram Sarabhai Space Centre. The city also hosts the Government Medical College (founded 1951) and the College of Engineering, Trivandrum (CET), the first engineering college in Kerala.

## Terms
- **Princely State:** A semi-sovereign territory in British India under the nominal control of an Indian ruler.
- **Laterite:** A soil type rich in iron and aluminum, formed in hot and wet tropical areas.
- **Trans-shipment Port:** A port where cargo is transferred from one ship to another for onward journey.
- **TEU (Twenty-foot Equivalent Unit):** A standard unit of cargo capacity for container ships and terminals.
- **Ashtavaidya:** A hereditary group of physician families in Kerala renowned for expertise in all eight branches of Ayurveda.
- **Sadhya:** A traditional vegetarian feast served on a banana leaf, common in Kerala.
- **Kasavu:** The gold zari border on traditional Kerala cotton fabrics.
- **Panchakarma:** A detoxification and rejuvenation therapy in Ayurveda.

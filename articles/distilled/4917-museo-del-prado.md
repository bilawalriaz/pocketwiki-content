# Museo del Prado

## Overview
The Museo del Prado is Spain's principal national art museum, located in Madrid. It is world-renowned for its extensive collection of European art from the 12th to early 20th centuries, particularly Spanish masters, and is considered to have the single best collection of Spanish art. Its holdings are largely based on the former Spanish royal collection, making it a cornerstone of Western art history.

## Body

### Origins and Collection
The Prado's building was designed in 1785 by Juan de Villanueva. It was opened as a museum in 1819 by Ferdinand VII and Queen María Isabel de Braganza to display the Spanish royal collection and demonstrate the merit of Spanish art. The royal collection, begun in the 16th century under Charles V, forms the museum's nucleus and includes masterpieces by artists like Velázquez, Goya, Bosch, and Titian. The collection grew through the incorporation of other museum holdings, such as the Museo de la Trinidad (1872) and the Museo de Arte Moderno (1971), as well as through major bequests, donations, and purchases.

### Architecture and Expansion
The building, part of a grand Enlightenment-era urban scheme for Madrid, was initially used for other purposes, including as a cavalry headquarters during the Napoleonic Wars. The museum has undergone numerous expansions to accommodate its growing collection and public. Significant enlargements occurred in 1918 and with the incorporation of the Casón del Buen Retiro and Salón de Reinos. A major expansion by Rafael Moneo was completed in 2007. A renovation of the Hall of Realms by Norman Foster was approved in 2021.

### Management and Funding
Until the early 2000s, the Prado's annual income was about $18 million, with 80% from the government. A 2003 reform established a public-private partnership, requiring the museum to gradually reduce state support to 50% in exchange for greater financial autonomy. A key bequest came in 1991 from Manuel Villaescusa, whose fortune was used solely for acquiring paintings. The museum's 2004 expansion was state-funded.

## Terms
- **Royal Collection:** The core art collection of the Spanish monarchy, which forms the basis of the Prado's holdings.
- **Golden Triangle of Art:** The area in Madrid containing the Prado, the Thyssen-Bornemisza Museum, and the Museo Reina Sofía.
- **Public-Private Partnership:** A funding model where the museum receives reduced state support in exchange for control over its budget and ability to raise private funds.
- **Bequest:** A gift of personal property by will, a major source of the museum's acquisitions.
- **Nationalization:** The transfer of the museum from royal to state ownership in 1868.

# Don Bradman

## Overview
Sir Donald Bradman (1908–2001) is widely regarded as the greatest batsman in cricket history, with a Test batting average of 99.94—a statistical dominance unparalleled in any major sport. His career spanned the interwar period and post-WWII era, during which he became an Australian national icon and a transformative figure in cricket administration.

## Body

### Early Life and Rapid Rise
Born to English-heritage parents in rural NSW, Bradman developed his batting skills as a child using a cricket stump and golf ball against a water tank. His solo practice honed exceptional timing and reflexes. After standout performances in bush cricket, he was selected for grade cricket in Sydney in 1926–27. He made his first-class debut for New South Wales in 1927, scoring a century, and was fast-tracked into the Australian Test team by 1928, aged 20.

### Test Career and Statistical Dominance
Bradman's Test career began poorly, but he soon established himself as a prolific scorer. His 1930 tour of England, where he scored 974 runs in the Ashes series (a record that still stands), cemented his status as a national hero during the Great Depression. He consistently scored at a level described as "worth three batsmen." His career average of 99.94 is the highest in Test cricket; the next-best average for a player with over 20 innings is 62. He holds records for the most double centuries (12) and the fastest accumulation of 1,000 to 6,000 Test runs.

### Bodyline and Controversy
England devised "Bodyline"—a tactic of fast, short-pitched bowling directed at the batsman's body—to curb Bradman's scoring during the 1932–33 Ashes. The series was acrimonious, causing a diplomatic incident. Bradman's batting average dropped significantly, and some argue the experience permanently altered his technique. The controversy strained his relationships with teammates and administrators.

### Captaincy, War, and "The Invincibles"
Bradman captained South Australia and was appointed Australian captain in 1936. He led Australia to a historic Ashes victory after losing the first two Tests. World War II interrupted his career; he served in the army but was invalided out due to fibrositis (an early term for fibromyalgia). He made a dramatic post-war comeback, captaining the 1948 Australian team, nicknamed "The Invincibles," which completed an unbeaten tour of England—a unique achievement. His final Test innings was a duck, leaving his average at 99.94.

### Administrative Career and Legacy
After retiring, Bradman became a powerful cricket administrator, serving as a selector and chairman of the Australian Cricket Board (ACB) for decades. He advocated for attacking, entertaining cricket. His most significant administrative decision was cancelling a planned 1971–72 Australian tour of South Africa in protest against apartheid, a stance later praised by Nelson Mandela. He was knighted in 1949 and remained a revered national figure until his death.

## Terms
- **Test cricket:** The highest level of international cricket matches, played between national teams.
- **Ashes:** The historic Test series played between Australia and England.
- **Bodyline:** A controversial bowling tactic involving fast, short-pitched deliveries aimed at the batsman's body, used by England against Bradman in 1932–33.
- **First-class cricket:** High-level domestic cricket, including matches like the Sheffield Shield.
- **Batting average:** A statistic calculated by dividing total runs scored by the number of times dismissed.
- **Fibrositis:** An outdated term for fibromyalgia, a condition causing widespread musculoskeletal pain.
- **Sticky wicket:** A damp, difficult batting pitch, often after rain.
- **The Invincibles:** The nickname for Bradman's 1948 Australian team that toured England unbeaten.
- **ACB:** The Australian Cricket Board, now known as Cricket Australia.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Impact of Bodyline:** Did the Bodyline series permanently damage Bradman's technique and mental approach?
- **Administrative decisions:** Was his cancellation of the 1971–72 South Africa tour a principled stand or a political move that ignored public opinion?
- **Personal relationships:** Was Bradman's perceived aloofness a result of his intense focus, the pressures of fame, or his personality?
- **Statistical dominance:** Is his batting average of 99.94 the greatest single achievement in world sport, given the context of different eras and conditions?

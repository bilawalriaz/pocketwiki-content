# Daniel Bernoulli

## Overview
Daniel Bernoulli (1700–1782) was a Swiss mathematician and physicist from the famous Bernoulli family. He is best known for applying mathematics to mechanics, especially fluid mechanics, and for pioneering work in probability and statistics. His principle, a key example of energy conservation, explains the operation of devices like carburetors and airplane wings.

## Body

### Early Life and Family
Daniel Bernoulli was born in Groningen, Netherlands, into the distinguished Bernoulli family of Basel, Switzerland. He was the son of Johann Bernoulli, an early developer of calculus, and nephew of Jacob Bernoulli, a researcher in probability theory. Daniel had a notoriously bad relationship with his father; Johann allegedly banned Daniel from his house after they tied for first in a scientific contest and later plagiarized ideas from Daniel's *Hydrodynamica*. Despite his father's initial discouragement, Daniel studied medicine at Basel, Heidelberg, and Strasbourg, earning a PhD in 1721, while learning mathematics privately from his father.

### Academic Career and Mathematical Work
Bernoulli was a contemporary and friend of Leonhard Euler. He worked in St. Petersburg from 1724 to 1733 but left due to illness, censorship, and salary disputes. He then held chairs in medicine, metaphysics, and natural philosophy at the University of Basel until his death. His major work, *Hydrodynamica* (1738), arranged results as consequences of the conservation of *vis viva* (an early form of energy conservation). He also contributed to the theory of tides, vibrating strings, and published a polynomial root-finding algorithm known as Bernoulli's method.

### Economics, Statistics, and Physics
In his 1738 work on risk measurement, Bernoulli proposed that individuals maximize "utility" (personal satisfaction) rather than monetary gain, explaining the St. Petersburg paradox and founding the theory of risk aversion. He also analyzed censored smallpox data in 1766 to demonstrate inoculation's efficacy. In physics, *Hydrodynamica* laid the foundation for the kinetic theory of gases and explained Boyle's law. He collaborated with Euler on elasticity, leading to the Euler–Bernoulli beam equation. Bernoulli's principle, critical in hydrodynamics, and his 1753 statement of the principle of superposition for vibrating systems were major contributions.

## Terms
- **Bernoulli's principle**: A principle in fluid dynamics stating that an increase in the speed of a fluid occurs simultaneously with a decrease in pressure, a key example of energy conservation.
- **Vis viva**: An early concept of kinetic energy, defined as mass times velocity squared, central to Bernoulli's work.
- **Utility**: An economic term for the personal satisfaction or benefit derived from a decision, used to explain risk aversion.
- **St. Petersburg paradox**: A problem in probability theory where expected monetary value is infinite, which Bernoulli resolved using utility theory.
- **Euler–Bernoulli beam equation**: A differential equation describing the deflection of a beam under load, developed with Leonhard Euler.
- **Principle of superposition**: The idea that the total motion of a vibrating system is the sum of its individual proper vibrations.
- **Kinetic theory of gases**: A theory explaining gas properties (like pressure) through the motion of its particles, which Bernoulli helped found.

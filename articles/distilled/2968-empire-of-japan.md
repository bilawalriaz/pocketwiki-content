# Empire of Japan

## Overview
The Empire of Japan (大日本帝国) was the period of Japanese history from the Meiji Restoration in 1868 to the enactment of the post-war constitution in 1947. It marked Japan's rapid transformation from a feudal society into a modern industrial and military power, which then pursued aggressive expansion across Asia and the Pacific, culminating in its defeat in World War II. This era fundamentally reshaped Japan's political system, society, and its role in the world.

## Timeline
- **3 Jan 1868** — Meiji Restoration begins; Emperor Meiji restored to power.
- **1868–1869** — Boshin War; defeat of Tokugawa shogunate forces.
- **1894–1895** — First Sino-Japanese War; Treaty of Shimonoseki cedes Taiwan to Japan.
- **1904–1905** — Russo-Japanese War; Japan gains influence in Korea and Manchuria.
- **Aug 1910** — Japan formally annexes Korea.
- **1914** — Japan enters World War I on the Allied side, seizing German territories in Asia-Pacific.
- **Sep 1931** — Mukden Incident; Japanese invasion of Manchuria begins.
- **Jul 1937** — Marco Polo Bridge Incident; start of the Second Sino-Japanese War.
- **Sep 1940** — Japan signs the Tripartite Pact with Germany and Italy.
- **7 Dec 1941** — Attack on Pearl Harbor; Japan enters World War II against the Allies.
- **Jun 1942** — Battle of Midway; a decisive naval defeat for Japan.
- **6 & 9 Aug 1945** — Atomic bombings of Hiroshima and Nagasaki.
- **2 Sep 1945** — Formal surrender of Japan; end of World War II.
- **3 May 1947** — New Constitution of Japan comes into force, ending the Empire.

## Body

### Founding and Modernization (1868–1912)
The Empire was established after the Boshin War (1868–1869), which ended the Tokugawa shogunate and restored direct imperial rule under Emperor Meiji. The new government pursued rapid modernization under the slogan "Enrich the Country, Strengthen the Armed Forces" (*fukoku kyōhei*). This involved large-scale industrialization, Western-style political and legal reforms (including the 1889 Meiji Constitution), and the abolition of the feudal class system. Japan's first major military victory in the First Sino-Japanese War (1894–95) resulted in the acquisition of Taiwan and marked its emergence as a regional power.

### Expansion and Militarism (1912–1937)
The Taishō era (1912–1926) saw the rise of "Taishō Democracy" with party politics, but the military retained significant independence. Japan's victory in the Russo-Japanese War (1904–05) led to the annexation of Korea in 1910. After World War I, Japan gained former German territories in the Pacific. Economic turmoil in the 1920s, including the Great Depression, fueled the rise of militarism and ultranationalism. The military, acting independently of the civilian government, engineered the invasion of Manchuria in 1931 and established the puppet state of Manchukuo. This led to Japan's withdrawal from the League of Nations in 1933. The full-scale invasion of China began in 1937.

### World War II and Defeat (1937–1945)
Facing an oil embargo from the United States, Japan attacked Pearl Harbor on December 7, 1941, bringing the U.S. into World War II. The Imperial Japanese Navy and Army achieved rapid conquests across Southeast Asia and the Pacific. The tide turned with Allied victories at the Battle of Midway (1942) and the Guadalcanal campaign (1942–43). From 1944, Allied forces advanced towards Japan through an island-hopping campaign. In 1945, intense firebombing of Japanese cities, the atomic bombings of Hiroshima and Nagasaki, and the Soviet invasion of Manchuria forced Japan's surrender on September 2, 1945.

### End of the Empire
Following surrender, Japan was occupied by Allied forces, primarily led by U.S. General Douglas MacArthur. The occupation government dissolved the Imperial Japanese Armed Forces and enacted a new pacifist constitution in 1947, which formally ended the Empire of Japan and established the modern state of Japan with a symbolic emperor. The Treaty of San Francisco (1951) officially ended the occupation and hostilities.

## Terms
*   **Meiji Restoration:** The 1868 political revolution that restored practical imperial rule and launched Japan's modernization.
*   **Boshin War:** The 1868–1869 civil war between the ruling Tokugawa shogunate and forces seeking to return political power to the Imperial Court.
*   **Zaibatsu:** Large, family-controlled industrial and financial conglomerates that dominated Japan's economy in the pre-war era.
*   **Greater East Asia Co-Prosperity Sphere:** Japan's concept for a bloc of Asian nations led by the Japanese and free of Western powers, used to justify expansionism.
*   **Kempeitai:** The military police arm of the Imperial Japanese Army, responsible for intelligence and internal security.
*   **Tokkō:** The Special Higher Police, a secret police force established to suppress political dissent and "dangerous thoughts."
*   **State Shinto:** The wartime ideology that elevated Shinto to a state-sponsored religion promoting emperor worship and nationalism.
*   **Potsdam Declaration:** The 1945 ultimatum issued by the Allies demanding Japan's unconditional surrender.

## Debates and open questions
Scholars debate the precise political classification of the Empire of Japan, particularly during the 1930s and 1940s. While some historians use terms like "fascist" or "totalitarian," others argue these are inadequate due to the absence of a single dictator with absolute power and a mass fascist party. Alternative terms proposed include "militarism," "authoritarianism," "police state," or "para-fascism." The role of Emperor Hirohito—whether he was a figurehead or an active war leader—remains a significant historical debate.

# Huldrych Zwingli

## Overview
Huldrych Zwingli (1484–1531) was a Swiss theologian and a principal leader of the Reformation in Switzerland. His work established the Reformed tradition, emphasizing the authority of Scripture over church tradition and reshaping worship, which led to the political and religious division of the Swiss Confederation and a lasting legacy in Protestant churches.

## Timeline
- **1484** — Born in Wildhaus, Switzerland.
- **1506** — Ordained as a priest; first Mass celebrated.
- **1518** — Elected Leutpriester (people's priest) of the Grossmünster in Zurich.
- **1519** — Began expository preaching through the New Testament in Zurich.
- **1522** — Publicly challenged Lenten fasting rules (the "Affair of the Sausages"), marking the start of the Swiss Reformation.
- **1523** — First Zurich Disputation; city council supported his right to preach based on Scripture.
- **1525** — Introduced a new communion liturgy, replacing the Catholic Mass in Zurich.
- **1527** — First Anabaptist martyr, Felix Manz, executed in Zurich.
- **1528** — Bern officially adopted the Reformation after a disputation.
- **1529** — Marburg Colloquy with Martin Luther; agreement on 14 articles but split over the Eucharist.
- **1531** — Killed in the Battle of Kappel during the Second Kappel War.

## Body

### Early Life and Influences
Zwingli was born into a farming family and educated in Renaissance humanism at the universities of Vienna and Basel. His early pastoral work in Glarus and Einsiedeln was marked by a growing opposition to the Swiss mercenary system and the influence of the humanist scholar Erasmus. He was elected to the prestigious post in Zurich in 1518, partly due to his reputation as a preacher and his humanist connections.

### The Zurich Reformation
Zwingli's reform program began with his method of continuous biblical preaching (*lectio continua*), starting with the Gospel of Matthew. His public challenge to clerical celibacy and fasting rules in 1522 sparked open controversy. Through a series of disputations with the city council, he secured the right to preach Scripture alone, leading to the removal of images from churches and the replacement of the Mass with a new communion liturgy in 1525. He also oversaw the secularization of church properties to fund social welfare.

### Conflict and Political Division
Zwingli's reforms were rejected by several Catholic cantons, leading to the formation of opposing alliances that divided the Swiss Confederation. A war was narrowly averted in 1529 (First Kappel War). Theologically, Zwingli's views on the Eucharist as a symbolic memorial placed him in direct conflict with Martin Luther, whose doctrine of the Real Presence they could not reconcile at the Marburg Colloquy. Zwingli also clashed with radical reformers like the Anabaptists, who rejected infant baptism, leading to their persecution in Zurich.

### Death and Legacy
In 1531, a food blockade imposed by Zwingli's alliance on Catholic cantons failed. The Catholic cantons attacked, and Zwingli was killed in the ensuing Battle of Kappel. His successor, Heinrich Bullinger, consolidated his reforms. While Zwingli's specific view on the Eucharist was later superseded by John Calvin's doctrine of a spiritual presence within Reformed churches, his emphasis on Scripture, his liturgical reforms, and his model of a godly commonwealth established the foundation of the Reformed tradition.

## Terms
- **Leutpriester**: The "people's priest," a prestigious preaching position at the Grossmünster in Zurich.
- **Lectio continua**: A method of preaching through a book of the Bible sequentially, rather than following the traditional church calendar.
- **Disputation**: A formal public debate on theological issues, often presided over by civil authorities.
- **Anabaptists**: A radical Reformation group that rejected infant baptism, advocating for believer's baptism and a separation of church and state.
- **Real Presence**: The doctrine, held by Lutherans and Catholics, that Christ is truly present in the bread and wine of the Eucharist.
- **Sola Scriptura**: The Reformation principle that the Bible is the sole infallible source of authority for Christian faith and practice.
- **Transubstantiation**: The Catholic doctrine that the bread and wine of the Eucharist become the actual body and blood of Christ while retaining their outward appearance.
- **Consensus Tigurinus**: A 1549 agreement between Bullinger and Calvin that defined a shared Reformed view of the Eucharist, rejecting both Zwingli's symbolism and Luther's corporeal presence.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Independence from Luther**: Scholars debate the extent of Martin Luther's influence on Zwingli's theology. Zwingli himself vigorously asserted his independence, and recent studies support the view that he developed his core ideas independently, though he later sought confirmation from Luther.
- **Theocracy in Zurich**: Historians have debated whether Zwingli's close collaboration with the Zurich city council to implement religious reforms effectively turned the city into a theocracy.
- **Impact of the Eucharistic Dispute**: The failure to reach agreement with Luther at the Marburg Colloquy is seen as a pivotal moment that permanently divided Protestantism into Lutheran and Reformed branches, with lasting political and theological consequences.

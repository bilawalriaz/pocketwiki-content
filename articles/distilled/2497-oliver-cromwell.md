# Oliver Cromwell

## Overview
Oliver Cromwell (1599–1658) was an English statesman and soldier who rose to prominence during the Wars of the Three Kingdoms. He became a leading Parliamentarian commander, played a key role in the execution of King Charles I, and ruled as Lord Protector of the Commonwealth of England, Scotland, and Ireland from 1653 until his death. His legacy is deeply controversial, marked by military prowess, political revolution, and brutal campaigns, particularly in Ireland.

## Body

### Early Life and Religious Conversion
Born into the landed gentry, Cromwell's early adulthood was marked by financial struggle. In the 1630s, he underwent a profound religious conversion, becoming a religious Independent—a Puritan who rejected state-imposed church hierarchy. This faith, which held that God actively guided world events through "chosen people," became the driving force of his actions. He believed his successes were acts of divine providence.

### Military Rise in the Civil Wars
Cromwell joined the Parliamentarian army in 1642 with little experience. He quickly proved a gifted cavalry commander, recruiting disciplined, religiously motivated soldiers regardless of social rank. His "Ironsides" were instrumental in victories at Marston Moor (1644) and Naseby (1645). He was a key architect of the New Model Army, a professional force that won the First English Civil War. His military success gave him immense political influence.

### Regicide and the Commonwealth
After the war, Cromwell rejected settlements that would restore Charles I. When the King sparked a Second Civil War, Cromwell crushed Royalist uprisings. Convinced that God condemned both King and Parliament, he supported Pride's Purge (1648), which purged Parliament of moderates. The resulting Rump Parliament tried and executed Charles I in 1649, establishing the Commonwealth. Cromwell then led brutal but effective campaigns to subdue Ireland (1649–50) and Scotland (1650–51), securing the new republic.

### The Protectorate
Dissatisfied with the Rump Parliament's inefficiency, Cromwell dissolved it in 1653. After a short-lived experiment with the Nominated Assembly, he was installed as Lord Protector under the Instrument of Government, a written constitution. He ruled with a council and parliaments, but his regime relied on military support. He divided England into districts under Major-Generals to enforce moral reform, a deeply unpopular policy. In 1657, he refused the crown but accepted a revised constitution giving him quasi-monarchical powers.

### Death and Legacy
Cromwell died in 1658 and was succeeded by his son Richard, whose weakness led to the Protectorate's collapse and the 1660 Restoration. Cromwell's body was exhumed and posthumously executed in 1661. His historical reputation is fiercely debated: he is seen as a champion of liberty and parliamentary government, or as a military dictator and religious bigot responsible for atrocities, especially in Ireland. His statue outside the Houses of Parliament remains controversial.

## Terms
*   **Lord Protector:** Title held by Cromwell as head of state from 1653 to 1658.
*   **New Model Army:** Professional, centrally funded army created by Parliament in 1645.
*   **Commonwealth:** Period of republican government in England (1649–1653).
*   **Instrument of Government:** 1653 constitution that established the Protectorate.
*   **Rump Parliament:** Remnant of the Long Parliament after Pride's Purge (1648).
*   **Pride's Purge:** December 1648 military coup removing MPs hostile to the army.
*   **Regicide:** The killing of a king; specifically the execution of Charles I in 1649.
*   **Providentialism:** Belief that God directly intervenes in human affairs.
*   **Major-Generals:** Military governors appointed in 1655 to enforce security and reform.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Military dictator or champion of liberty?** His rule relied on military force and he dissolved parliaments, but he operated within constitutional frameworks and sought parliamentary settlement.
*   **The brutality of the Irish campaign:** Historians debate whether massacres at Drogheda and Wexford were standard military practice, deliberate terror, or ethnic cleansing, and Cromwell's personal responsibility.
*   **His religious motivation:** Was his rule driven by sincere Puritan vision or was this a justification for ambition?
*   **The nature of his power:** Was the Protectorate a military dictatorship, a constitutional experiment, or a failed attempt at stable non-monarchical government?

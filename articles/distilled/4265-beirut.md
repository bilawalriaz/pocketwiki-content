# Beirut

## Overview
Beirut is the capital and largest city of Lebanon, situated on a peninsula on the Mediterranean coast. It is one of the oldest continuously inhabited cities in the world, with over 5,000 years of history. As Lebanon's political, economic, and cultural heart, it has been repeatedly devastated and rebuilt, most notably after the Lebanese Civil War (1975–1990) and the 2020 Port of Beirut explosion.

## Timeline
- **c. 3000 BC** — Earliest settlement on an island in the Beirut River.
- **14th century BC** — First attested as "Biruta" in the Amarna letters.
- **140 BC** — Phoenician city destroyed by Diodotus Tryphon; rebuilt as Hellenistic Laodicea.
- **64 BC** — Conquered by Pompey; name restored to Berytus.
- **14 BC** — Becomes a Roman colony with full Italian rights (ius Italicum).
- **551 AD** — Major earthquake destroys the city, killing an estimated 30,000.
- **635 AD** — Conquered by Muslim forces.
- **1110–1291** — Part of the Kingdom of Jerusalem during the Crusades.
- **1888** — Becomes capital of the Vilayet of Beirut under Ottoman rule.
- **1943** — Lebanese independence; Beirut becomes the capital.
- **1975–1990** — Lebanese Civil War divides the city; massive destruction.
- **4 August 2020** — Massive explosion at the Port of Beirut kills over 200 and injures thousands.

## Body

### Etymology and Ancient History
The name Beirut derives from the Phoenician word *bēʾrūt*, meaning "wells." It was known as Berytus under Roman rule, when it became a major Roman colony and legal center. The city's law school was renowned, producing jurists like Papinian and Ulpian. A devastating earthquake in 551 AD ended its prominence, and the school was moved to Sidon.

### Medieval to Ottoman Rule
Beirut was conquered by Muslims in 635 and later became part of the Crusader Kingdom of Jerusalem. Under Ottoman rule from the 16th century, it was often controlled by local Druze emirs. Its modern revival began in the 19th century due to European commercial interests, particularly in silk. It became a cosmopolitan port city and the capital of its own Ottoman vilayet in 1888.

### Modern Capital and Conflicts
After World War I, Beirut was under the French Mandate and became the capital of independent Lebanon in 1943. It grew as a banking and tourism hub, earning the nickname "Paris of the Middle East." The Lebanese Civil War (1975–1990) devastated the city, dividing it into Christian East and Muslim West. Post-war reconstruction, led by the company Solidere, rebuilt the central district but was controversial for destroying heritage and displacing residents. Beirut has since suffered further damage from the 2006 Lebanon War and the 2020 port explosion.

### Geography and Climate
Beirut sits on a triangular peninsula flanked by the Lebanon Mountains. It has a hot-summer Mediterranean climate with mild, rainy winters and hot, humid summers. The city faces environmental challenges, including a severe garbage crisis in 2015–2016 and a lack of green space, with concrete covering 80% of the capital area.

### Demographics and Society
Beirut is religiously diverse, with Christians comprising 40% and Muslims 59% of the population. The civil war led to increased sectarian segregation, though some mixing has returned. Trilingualism in Lebanese Arabic, French, and English is common. Family law remains governed by religious courts.

### Economy
Beirut's economy is service-based, dominated by banking and tourism. It is a regional financial center due to Lebanon's free economic system, banking secrecy laws, and high interest rates. The tourism sector is a major revenue source, though it is vulnerable to political instability.

### Government and Infrastructure
As Lebanon's capital, Beirut hosts the national government, embassies, and international organizations like the UN Economic and Social Commission for Western Asia. Its infrastructure includes Rafic Hariri International Airport and the Port of Beirut. Public transportation is limited, with a new bus network introduced in 2024.

### Culture and Media
Beirut is a cultural hub with numerous museums, universities, and a vibrant nightlife. It is a center for media, hosting many TV networks and newspapers. The city is known for its fashion designers and contemporary art scene.

## Terms
- **Vilayet**: An administrative division of the Ottoman Empire, equivalent to a province.
- **Ius Italicum**: A legal status in the Roman Empire granting a colony the same rights as cities in Italy, including tax exemptions.
- **Solidere**: A private joint-stock company established in 1994 to reconstruct the Beirut Central District.
- **Millet System**: An Ottoman system where religious communities managed their own personal status laws (e.g., marriage, inheritance).
- **Green Line**: The demarcation line that divided Christian East Beirut and Muslim West Beirut during the civil war.
- **Canaanean Blade**: A type of large, finely crafted flint blade from the Neolithic period in the Levant.
- **Mousterian**: A stone tool industry associated with Neanderthals in the Middle Paleolithic period.
- **Acheulean**: A prehistoric stone tool industry characterized by hand-axes, dating from the Lower Paleolithic.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The function of archaeological site BEY194, claimed to be a Phoenician port, is disputed; an international committee dismissed the port theory, and its exact purpose may never be known.
- The reconstruction of the Beirut Central District by Solidere is controversial, criticized for destroying architectural heritage, displacing residents, and creating a privatized, inaccessible space.
- The causes and responsibility for the 2020 Port of Beirut explosion, linked to stored ammonium nitrate, remain subjects of investigation and public protest.

# Chennai

## Overview
Chennai, formerly Madras, is the capital and largest city of Tamil Nadu, India. Located on the Coromandel Coast, it is a major economic, cultural, and transportation hub, serving as the gateway to South India. Its history spans from ancient settlements to a key British colonial port, shaping its modern identity as a diverse, industrial metropolis.

- **2017** — Chennai added to UNESCO Creative Cities Network for music.

## Body

### History and Etymology
Archaeological evidence shows Stone Age settlements near Pallavaram. Historically, the region was ruled by the Chola, Pallava, Pandya, and Vijayanagara kingdoms. The name "Chennai" derives from Chennappa Nayaka, a Vijayanagara general, with the first official use in 1639. "Madras" likely evolved from "Madraspattinam," a pre-colonial fishing village. The British East India Company established a factory in 1639 and built Fort St. George. The city was contested by the French and British in the 18th century, becoming the capital of the British Madras Presidency. After 1947, it was the capital of Madras State, renamed Tamil Nadu in 1969. The city was officially renamed Chennai in 1996.

### Geography and Climate
Chennai lies on the flat Eastern Coastal Plains with an average elevation of 6.7 m. It has a tropical savanna climate (Köppen classification As/Aw) with no extreme seasonal temperature variation. The hottest months are April–June (35–40 °C). Most rainfall (avg. 120 cm annually) comes from the northeast monsoon (October–December), often bringing cyclones and flooding. The city is crossed by the Cooum and Adyar rivers and the Buckingham Canal.

### Demographics and Administration
As per the 2011 census, the city proper had a population of 4.65 million, with the metropolitan area housing over 6.7 million. Tamil is the primary language, with Madras Bashai as a local dialect. The population is majority Hindu (80.7%). The Greater Chennai Corporation, established in 1688, administers the city. As the state capital, it houses the Tamil Nadu Legislative Assembly and Madras High Court.

### Economy
Chennai had an estimated GDP of $196.5 billion in 2024. It is a major industrial and IT hub, known as the "Detroit of India" for its automobile sector, which accounts for over 35% of India's automotive output. It is also a leading exporter of leather and a major center for software services, healthcare, and financial services. The city is a top destination for medical tourism, attracting over 40% of India's medical tourists.

### Culture and Architecture
Called the "Cultural Capital of South India," Chennai is a center for Carnatic music, Bharata Natyam dance, and Tamil cinema (Kollywood). Architecture ranges from 6th–8th century Dravidian temples to colonial Indo-Saracenic buildings like the Madras High Court. The city has a vibrant street food culture and was ranked among National Geographic's "Top 10 food cities" in 2015.

### Infrastructure and Transport
Chennai is a major transportation hub with an international airport, two major ports (Chennai and Ennore), and extensive road and rail networks. It has a suburban railway, MRTS, and a metro system opened in 2015. The city faces challenges with water supply, waste management, and pollution of its rivers.

## Terms
- **Dravidian architecture**: A style of Hindu temple architecture characterized by large gate-pyramids called gopurams.
- **Indo-Saracenic architecture**: A blend of Hindu, Islamic, and Gothic revival styles used in many colonial-era public buildings.
- **Madras Bashai**: A unique linguistic variation of Tamil spoken in Chennai, incorporating words from English and other languages.
- **Kollywood**: The Tamil-language film industry, based in the Kodambakkam neighborhood of Chennai.
- **Carnatic music**: A system of Indian classical music traditionally associated with South India.
- **Bharata Natyam**: A classical dance form that originated in Tamil Nadu.
- **Greater Chennai Corporation**: The civic body that governs the city, established in 1688.
- **Chennai Metropolitan Development Authority (CMDA)**: The nodal agency for planning and development of the Chennai Metropolitan Area.

## Debates and open questions
- The exact origin of the name "Madras" is debated, with theories linking it to "Madraspattinam" or other local terms.
- The first official use of the name "Chennai" is contested, with a 1639 sale deed and a 1646 land grant being cited by different scholars.
- The city's vulnerability to flooding and water scarcity, exacerbated by urbanization and the shrinkage of water bodies, remains a critical environmental and planning challenge.

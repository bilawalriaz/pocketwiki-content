# Alfredo Di Stéfano

## Overview
Alfredo Di Stéfano (1926–2014) was an Argentine-Spanish footballer widely regarded as one of the greatest players of all time. He was the central figure in Real Madrid's unprecedented dominance of the European Cup in the 1950s, scoring in five consecutive finals. His career spanned three continents and three national teams, making him a unique and influential figure in football history.

## Timeline
- **4 July 1926** — Born in Buenos Aires, Argentina.
- **1943** — Begins professional career with River Plate.
- **1947** — Wins Copa América with Argentina; top scorer in Argentine league.
- **1949** — Transfers to Millonarios (Colombia) due to Argentine footballers' strike.
- **1952** — Millonarios wins a friendly tournament at Real Madrid's stadium, impressing president Santiago Bernabéu.
- **1953** — Signs with Real Madrid after a complex transfer dispute involving Barcelona.
- **1956** — Wins first European Cup; becomes a Spanish citizen.
- **1957** — Wins first Ballon d'Or; scores in second European Cup final victory.
- **1958** — Wins third consecutive European Cup; top scorer in European Cup.
- **1959** — Wins second Ballon d'Or; wins fourth consecutive European Cup.
- **1960** — Wins fifth consecutive European Cup in a famous 7-3 final against Eintracht Frankfurt.
- **1964** — Plays final match for Real Madrid in European Cup final loss to Inter Milan.
- **1964–1966** — Plays for Espanyol before retiring.
- **7 July 2014** — Dies in Madrid, Spain.

## Body

### Early Career and Move to Colombia
Di Stéfano began at River Plate in 1943, becoming part of the famed "La Máquina" forward line. A footballers' strike in Argentina in 1949 led to a mass exodus of talent to the newly professional Colombian league, known as "El Dorado." Di Stéfano joined Millonarios, where he became the star of the "Ballet Azul" team, winning three league titles and scoring prolifically.

### The Disputed Transfer and Real Madrid Dominance
In 1952, Millonarios defeated Real Madrid in a friendly, catching the eye of club president Santiago Bernabéu. A complex transfer battle ensued between Real Madrid and Barcelona, who had also agreed a deal with River Plate. FIFA appointed a mediator, who initially proposed Di Stéfano alternate seasons between the Spanish clubs. Barcelona eventually sold its share to Real Madrid, and Di Stéfano signed for the latter in 1953.

At Real Madrid, Di Stéfano became the cornerstone of a legendary team. He led them to eight Spanish league titles and five consecutive European Cup victories (1956–1960), scoring in all five finals he played. His all-around play—scoring, creating, and defending—personified a style later called "total football." He won the Ballon d'Or in 1957 and 1959.

### International Career and Later Years
Di Stéfano played for Argentina (winning the 1947 Copa América), a non-FIFA sanctioned Colombian XI, and finally Spain after obtaining citizenship in 1956. Despite his club success, he never played in a FIFA World Cup due to injuries and political disputes. After leaving Real Madrid in 1964, he played two seasons for Espanyol before retiring in 1966. He later had a successful managerial career, winning titles with Boca Juniors, River Plate, and Valencia.

## Terms
- **El Dorado**: The period (c. 1949–1954) when the Colombian football league, having broken from FIFA, attracted top international talent with lucrative contracts.
- **La Máquina**: ("The Machine") The famous attacking forward line of River Plate in the 1940s, of which Di Stéfano became a part.
- **Ballon d'Or**: An annual award given to the best footballer in Europe, first won by Di Stéfano in 1957.
- **European Cup**: The predecessor to the UEFA Champions League, the premier European club competition.
- **Total Football**: A tactical theory where any outfield player can take over the role of any other player in the team, a style Di Stéfano embodied.
- **Pichichi Trophy**: Awarded to the top scorer in Spain's La Liga, which Di Stéfano won five times.
- **Super Ballon d'Or**: A special award given in 1989 to the best Ballon d'Or winner of the previous 30 years; Di Stéfano is the only recipient.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **The Transfer Dispute**: The exact sequence of events and motivations behind the complex transfer from Millonarios to Real Madrid, bypassing Barcelona, remains a point of historical interest and rivalry between the clubs.
- **Greatest of All Time Status**: While widely considered one of the greatest, debates persist about his ranking relative to other legends like Pelé, Diego Maradona, and Lionel Messi, often focusing on the different eras and competitive contexts.
- **World Cup Absence**: His lack of a World Cup appearance is a significant "what if" in his career narrative, as it denies a direct comparison with peers who shone on that stage.

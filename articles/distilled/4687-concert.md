# Concert

## Overview
A concert is a live musical performance for an audience, ranging from solo recitals to large ensemble shows in venues from small clubs to stadiums. It is the primary historical method for experiencing music and remains a major cultural and economic activity, with modern tours and festivals generating billions in revenue.

## Timeline
- **1672** — John Banister holds the first known public concerts charging admission in London.
- **1678** — Thomas Britton begins weekly subscription concerts in Clerkenwell, London.
- **18th c.** — Concerts featuring works by Haydn and Mozart are popular in English gardens.
- **Post-WW2** — Modern concert formats emerge, exemplified by the 1952 Moondog Coronation Ball.
- **1990** — Paul McCartney sets a ticketed concert attendance record (185,000) in Rio de Janeiro.
- **1994** — Rod Stewart sets a free concert attendance record (est. 3.5 million) in Rio de Janeiro.
- **2017** — Vasco Rossi sets the current ticketed concert attendance record (220,000) in Modena, Italy.
- **2023** — Taylor Swift's The Eras Tour generates a record $1.04 billion in revenue. |

## Body

### History and Development
The first concerts requiring admission appeared in the late 17th century, with John Banister's 1672 London concerts and Thomas Britton's 1678 subscription model. In France, Anne Danican Philidor organized the Concerts Spirituels, public concerts on religious holidays that became a global model. By the 18th century, English concerts featured composers like Haydn and Mozart in elegant garden venues. The modern concert format solidified after World War II.

### Types and Formats
Concerts vary by genre and scale. A **recital** is a solo or small-group performance, often highlighting one composer or instrument; the solo piano recital is attributed to Franz Liszt. **Theatrical concerts** use elaborate staging, lighting, video, and special effects. **Classical concerts** feature orchestral (chamber or symphony orchestra) or choral music. **Rock concerts** are known for high volume and audience participation like moshing.

### Venues and Series
Venues range from intimate nightclubs to large arenas and outdoor amphitheatres. A **concert hall** (e.g., Royal Albert Hall) typically hosts classical music. Concerts can be part of a **tour** (a series of shows in different locations), a **residency** (a series at one venue, often in Las Vegas), or a **festival** (multi-day, multi-artist events, often outdoors).

### Revenue and Attendance
Before recorded music, concerts were musicians' primary income. Modern revenue comes from ticket sales, sponsorships, concessions, and merchandise. The global live music industry earned $28.6 billion in 2023. Attendance records are held by Vasco Rossi (220,000 tickets, 2017) and Rod Stewart (est. 3.5 million for a free show, 1994).

### Psychology and Experience
Performers may experience stage fright or tour-related stress. Audience engagement varies: passive in classical settings, active in rock or folk. The intense sound and crowd environment can induce altered psychological states. Live music stimulates the brain's left amygdala more than recorded music, and audience and performer movement often creates a shared crowd psychology.

## Terms
- **Concert**: A live musical performance for an audience.
- **Recital**: A solo or small-group concert, often focusing on a single performer, composer, or instrument.
- **Chamber Orchestra**: A small-scale orchestra of 10-40 members, primarily strings.
- **Symphony Orchestra**: A large-scale orchestra of 80+ members with strings, woodwinds, brass, and percussion.
- **Concert Tour**: A series of concerts by an artist or group in multiple locations.
- **Concert Residency**: A series of concerts performed at a single location.
- **Music Festival**: A multi-day event featuring numerous artists and often multiple genres.
- **Full House**: A venue at complete audience capacity.

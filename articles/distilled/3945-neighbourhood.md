# Neighbourhood

## Overview
A neighbourhood is a geographically localized community within a larger town, city, or rural area, often defined by social networks and face-to-face interaction. It is a fundamental unit of urban life, serving as a setting for socialization, community control, and service delivery, with definitions and functions varying across historical periods and global cultures.

## Timeline
- **c. 3000 BC** — Archaeological evidence shows social neighbourhoods in the earliest excavated cities.
- **Tang period** — In Chang'an, China, neighbourhoods were state-administered districts with officials controlling daily life.
- **Early 1900s** — Clarence Perry describes the "neighbourhood unit" as a self-contained residential area for urban design.
- **Mid-1970s** — Alfred Kahn describes the "experience, theory and fads" of neighbourhood service delivery.
- **2011** — The UK's Localism Act introduces a right for communities to shape their neighbourhoods through planning.

## Body

### Preindustrial and Historical Neighbourhoods
Neighbourhoods have existed in cities throughout history, arising naturally from social interaction among nearby residents. They functioned as local social units larger than households, often handling basic municipal functions like protection and social regulation informally, a pattern well-documented in historical Islamic cities. While administrative districts for taxation and control were typically larger and sometimes overlapped neighbourhoods, in places like Tang dynasty Chang'an, neighbourhoods were the formal administrative units. These communities often developed social specialization, such as ethnic, economic, or religious concentrations, and were reinforced by rural-to-urban migrants who settled near relatives and acquaintances.

### Sociological Study and Improvement
Neighbourhood sociology studies these local communities, examining their role in social networks and their correlation with various social outcomes. Neighbourhoods have been focal points for service delivery and "service interventions" aimed at improving local quality of life and increasing community control. Community and economic development efforts, often involving non-profit organizations and faith-based groups, have pressured for reinvestment in local neighbourhood infrastructure and housing stock.

### Urban Design and Global Variations
In urban design, the neighbourhood is a key unit of analysis. Clarence Perry's early 20th-century concept of a self-contained "neighbourhood unit" remains influential in New Urbanism, which seeks to revive traditional sociability in planned communities. Globally, the term has specific administrative meanings: in mainland China, it refers to an urban division below the district level, encompassing thousands of families; in the UK, it is an informal term often used for service provision and community planning; in North America, neighbourhoods may have official status through associations that regulate local matters; and in Peru, they are called *urbanizaciones*, or planned communities.

## Terms
- **Neighbourhood**: A geographically localized community within a larger area, defined by social networks and face-to-face interaction.
- **Administrative district**: A formal division of a city used by officials for taxation, record-keeping, and control, often larger than a neighbourhood.
- **Neighbourhood unit**: A self-contained residential area concept in urban design, influential in New Urbanism.
- **Service intervention**: Efforts to deliver local services and increase community control within a neighbourhood.
- **Community Development Corporation (CDC)**: An organization that works on housing and infrastructure reinvestment in local communities.
- **Urbanization (Peru)**: A planned community subdivision within a district, exemplified by Santa Beatriz (1921).
- **Residents' committee (China)**: A supervisory body for smaller residential units within a Chinese neighbourhood.

## Debates and Open Questions
Researchers have not agreed on an exact definition of a neighbourhood, with debates centering on whether it is primarily a spatial area or a functional set of social networks. The boundaries of neighbourhoods are often unclear, especially where they lack official status, leading to questions about where one ends and another begins. The effectiveness and evolving role of neighbourhood-based organizations and service delivery models continue to be studied and discussed.

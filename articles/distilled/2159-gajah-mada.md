# Gajah Mada

## Overview
Gajah Mada (c. 1290 – c. 1364) was the mahapatih (prime minister) and chief general of the Majapahit Empire in Java. He is a legendary figure credited with unifying the vast Nusantara archipelago under Majapahit control, bringing the empire to its peak. His career and famous oath, the Sumpah Palapa, are central to Indonesian national identity and historical narratives of pre-colonial unity.

## Timeline
- **c. 1290** — Gajah Mada is born.
- **1321** — Gajah Mada, as a Bhayangkara commander, helps rescue King Jayanegara from a rebellion.
- **1328** — King Jayanegara is murdered; his half-sister Tribhuwana Wijayatunggadewi becomes queen.
- **1331** — Gajah Mada is sent to quell rebellions in Sadeng and Keta, leading to conflict with rival general Ra Kembar.
- **1334** — Gajah Mada is appointed mahapatih after the retirement of Arya Tadah.
- **c. 1336** — Gajah Mada delivers the Sumpah Palapa oath, vowing to unify the archipelago.
- **1343** — Majapahit forces under Gajah Mada conquer Bedahulu (Bali) and Lombok.
- **1350** — Hayam Wuruk becomes king; Gajah Mada continues as mahapatih and expands the empire eastward.
- **1351** — The Gajah Mada inscription is created, dedicating a temple to the late King Kertanegara.
- **1357** — The Bubat Incident occurs: a diplomatic mission to Sunda ends in battle and the death of the Sunda royal party.
- **c. 1364** — Gajah Mada dies in obscurity at Madakaripura after being demoted following the Bubat tragedy.

## Body

### Rise to Power and the Palapa Oath
Born into an ordinary family, Gajah Mada began his career in the elite Bhayangkara royal guard. He gained prominence by saving King Jayanegara during a 1321 rebellion. Following Jayanegara's death in 1328, Gajah Mada served under Queen Tribhuwana Wijayatunggadewi. After a conflict with a rival general during the Sadeng and Keta rebellions, he was appointed mahapatih in 1334. It was during this tenure that he reportedly swore the Sumpah Palapa, an oath to not rest until he had unified the Nusantara archipelago under Majapahit. The exact meaning of "amukti palapa" is debated, with definitions ranging from "enjoying pleasure" to "enjoying rest."

### Empire Expansion
As mahapatih, Gajah Mada directed a series of military campaigns that expanded Majapahit's influence across the archipelago. He conquered territories including Bali, Lombok, Palembang, Samudra Pasai, Bintan, Tumasik (Singapore), and regions in Kalimantan. Under King Hayam Wuruk, he continued eastward, subjugating areas such as Sumbawa, Flores, Timor, and the Maluku Islands. This expansion brought the modern territories of Indonesia, Singapore, Malaysia, Brunei, southern Philippines, and East Timor under Majapahit suzerainty. A 1351 inscription shows his reverence for the earlier Singhasari king Kertanegara, possibly indicating a familial link.

### The Bubat Incident and Downfall
In 1357, a diplomatic mission to arrange a marriage between King Hayam Wuruk and a Sundanese princess turned catastrophic. Gajah Mada interpreted the marriage as a sign of Sunda's submission to Majapahit, demanding the princess become a concubine rather than a queen. This led to the Battle of Bubat, where the entire Sunda royal party was killed. The tragedy shocked the Majapahit court, and Gajah Mada was blamed for his recklessness. He was demoted and spent his final years in exile at Madakaripura, where he died in 1364.

## Terms
- **Mahapatih**: The prime minister or chief minister of the Majapahit Empire.
- **Bhayangkara**: An elite royal guard unit for the Majapahit king and family.
- **Sumpah Palapa**: The legendary oath sworn by Gajah Mada to unify the Nusantara archipelago.
- **Nusantara**: A term for the Indonesian archipelago and its surrounding regions.
- **Caitya**: A sacred building or temple, as mentioned in the Gajah Mada inscription.
- **Vahana**: In Hindu mythology, the animal mount or vehicle of a deity.
- **Thalassocracy**: A state with primary power based on the sea, like the earlier Srivijaya empire.
- **Kidung**: A type of Old Javanese poem or literary work.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The historicity and exact wording of the Sumpah Palapa oath are debated, as the primary source (the *Pararaton*) was written centuries after the events.
- Scholars debate the meaning of the phrase "amukti palapa" within the oath.
- The cause of King Jayanegara's death is disputed; accounts differ on whether he was murdered by a court physician or by Gajah Mada himself.
- The true nature of Gajah Mada's relationship with the earlier King Kertanegara, and whether he was a grandson, remains speculative.
- The modern popular depiction of Gajah Mada's appearance, based on a 1945 interpretation of a terracotta fragment, is contested by archaeologists.

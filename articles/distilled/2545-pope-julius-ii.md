# Pope Julius II

## Overview
Pope Julius II (r. 1503–1513) was a central figure of the High Renaissance who dramatically expanded the Papal States' power and left a lasting cultural legacy. Known as the "Warrior Pope," he pursued aggressive military campaigns to expel foreign powers from Italy and commissioned iconic works of art, including the rebuilding of St. Peter's Basilica and Michelangelo's Sistine Chapel ceiling.

## Timeline
- **5 Dec 1443** — Born Giuliano della Rovere in Albisola, Republic of Genoa.
- **16 Dec 1471** — Created cardinal by his uncle, Pope Sixtus IV.
- **1 Nov 1503** — Elected Pope, taking the name Julius II.
- **1506** — Founded the Swiss Guard; laid foundation stone for new St. Peter's Basilica.
- **Dec 1508** — Formed the League of Cambrai against Venice.
- **1510** — Reconciled with Venice, forming the Holy League against France.
- **11 Apr 1512** — French forces won the Battle of Ravenna, but were later expelled from Italy.
- **3 May 1512** — Opened the Fifth Lateran Council.
- **21 Feb 1513** — Died in Rome; succeeded by Pope Leo X.

## Body

### Early Life and Cardinalate
Giuliano della Rovere was born into a noble but impoverished family. Educated by his uncle, the future Pope Sixtus IV, he was made a cardinal in 1471. He held multiple bishoprics and served as a papal legate, gaining political and military experience. His career was marked by rivalry with Cardinal Rodrigo Borgia, the future Pope Alexander VI.

### Papal Election and Early Policies
Elected in 1503 after the brief reign of Pius III, Julius II immediately moved to dismantle the power of the Borgia family. He aimed to centralize the Papal States and "free Italy from the barbarians," meaning foreign powers like France and Spain. He personally led military campaigns to reclaim papal territories from local lords in Perugia and Bologna.

### Military Campaigns and the Italian Wars
Julius II's pontificate was defined by complex alliances within the Italian Wars. He first formed the League of Cambrai (1508) with France, the Holy Roman Empire, and Spain to defeat Venice. After achieving his goals in Romagna, he reversed alliances, creating the Holy League (1511) with Venice and Spain to drive the French out of Italy. Despite a major defeat at the Battle of Ravenna (1512), Swiss mercenaries helped force a French retreat. By 1513, he had expanded the Papal States and presented himself as the "liberator of Italy."

### Patronage of the Arts
Julius II was a transformative patron of Renaissance art and architecture. He initiated the construction of the new St. Peter's Basilica (1506), commissioning Donato Bramante. He also commissioned Michelangelo to paint the Sistine Chapel ceiling (1508) and Raphael to decorate the Vatican Stanze. His projects aimed to restore Rome's imperial grandeur.

### Lateran Council and Death
To counter a French-backed council that threatened to depose him, Julius II convened the Fifth Lateran Council in 1512. He used it to affirm papal authority and reform the Church, including measures against simony in papal elections. He died in February 1513, having significantly strengthened the papacy's political and territorial position.

## Terms
- **Papal States**: The territories in central Italy directly ruled by the Pope.
- **Italian Wars**: A series of conflicts (1494–1559) among European powers for control of the Italian Peninsula.
- **League of Cambrai**: A 1508 alliance against the Republic of Venice.
- **Holy League**: A 1511 alliance formed by Julius II against French influence in Italy.
- **Fifth Lateran Council**: The ecumenical council (1512–1517) convened by Julius II to assert papal authority.
- **Simony**: The buying or selling of ecclesiastical privileges, such as church offices.
- **Swiss Guard**: The personal security force for the Pope, established by Julius II in 1506.
- **Damnatio memoriae**: A formal condemnation attempting to erase a person from memory.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Motives for the name "Julius"**: It is speculated he chose it to emulate Julius Caesar, not just Pope Julius I.
- **Goal of Italian unification**: Historians debate whether his military campaigns aimed to create a unified Italian kingdom or simply to expand and secure the Papal States.
- **Legacy of his wars**: While he temporarily expelled foreign powers, his successor Leo X soon restored the pre-war status quo, raising questions about the long-term success of his policies.

# Ernst Haeckel

## Overview
Ernst Haeckel (1834–1919) was a German zoologist, philosopher, and artist who became one of the most influential and controversial popularizers of evolutionary theory in the late 19th century. He coined key biological terms, championed Darwinism in Germany, and developed the influential but disproven "recapitulation theory." His work bridged science, philosophy, and art, but his promotion of scientific racism and social Darwinism has linked his legacy to the later rise of Nazi ideology.

## Timeline
- **16 Feb 1834** — Born in Potsdam, Kingdom of Prussia.
- **1857** — Earned a doctorate in medicine.
- **1862–1909** — Professor of Zoology at the University of Jena.
- **1866** — Published *Generelle Morphologie*, coining terms like "ecology," "phylum," and "phylogeny."
- **1868** — Published *Natürliche Schöpfungsgeschichte*, a popular bestseller promoting Darwinism.
- **1874** — Published *Anthropogenie*, intensifying controversy over embryological drawings.
- **1895–1899** — Published *Die Welträthsel* (*The Riddles of the Universe*).
- **1904** — Published *Kunstformen der Natur*, influencing Art Nouveau.
- **1908** — Awarded the Darwin-Wallace Medal.
- **9 Aug 1919** — Died in Jena.

## Body

### Scientific Career and Recapitulation Theory
Haeckel was a professor of comparative anatomy at Jena for 47 years. He discovered and named thousands of new species, particularly radiolarians, and proposed the kingdom Protista. His central scientific idea was the "Biogenetic Law," summarized by the dictum "ontogeny recapitulates phylogeny." This theory claimed that an individual organism's embryonic development (ontogeny) repeats the evolutionary history of its species (phylogeny). He supported this with embryo drawings that were later shown to be oversimplified and inaccurate, sparking a major controversy. The strong form of his theory has been refuted, though the comparison of embryos remains a valid tool for demonstrating evolutionary relationships.

### Popularization and Philosophy
Haeckel was a flamboyant and effective popularizer of science. His book *Natürliche Schöpfungsgeschichte* (1868) was a massive bestseller that explained his synthesis of Darwinism with German *Naturphilosophie* and Lamarckism. As a philosopher, he advocated for Monism—a pantheistic worldview he saw as compatible with science—and coined the term "world riddle" (*Welträtsel*). His artistic illustrations, especially in *Kunstformen der Natur*, had a significant impact on the Art Nouveau movement.

### Scientific Racism and Political Legacy
Haeckel promoted a racist doctrine of evolutionary polygenism, arguing that human races evolved separately from different proto-human ancestors. He constructed racial hierarchies, placing the Caucasian race at the top and claiming "lower races" were doomed to extinction. He saw social sciences as "applied biology" and was a key figure in Social Darwinism and eugenics. His ideas have been cited as a forerunner to Nazi ideology, though the relationship is debated. The Nazi Party itself was divided; some praised him, while official guidelines later banned his books. The modern Jena Declaration explicitly rejects his racial theories.

### The Asia Hypothesis
Contrary to Darwin's Africa hypothesis, Haeckel claimed the origin of humanity was in Asia, specifically the Indian subcontinent. He postulated the existence of a lost continent, Lemuria, in the Indian Ocean as the homeland of the first humans, from which they migrated to the rest of the world.

## Terms
- **Ontogeny:** The development of an individual organism from embryo to adult.
- **Phylogeny:** The evolutionary history and developmental line of a species.
- **Recapitulation Theory:** The (now disproven) idea that embryonic development repeats the evolutionary stages of ancestors.
- **Monism:** A philosophical view that all reality is essentially one substance, which Haeckel saw as a scientific, pantheistic alternative to religion.
- **Polygenism:** The theory that human races evolved from separate ancestral populations, as opposed to monogenesis (a single origin).
- **Social Darwinism:** The application of evolutionary concepts like "survival of the fittest" to human societies and politics.
- **Protista:** A kingdom of organisms proposed by Haeckel for simple, mostly unicellular life forms.
- **Heterochrony:** A change in the timing or rate of developmental events over evolutionary time.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Embryo Drawings:** Were Haeckel's embryological illustrations intentionally falsified to support his theory, or were they schematic simplifications made in good faith? Recent analyses suggest both legitimate criticisms and unfounded accusations.
- **Influence on Nazism:** To what extent did Haeckel's scientific racism and Social Darwinism directly inspire Nazi ideology? Historians are divided, noting both his influence on Nazi thinkers and the fact that the Nazi regime ultimately banned his books.
- **Legacy of Recapitulation:** While the strong "Biogenetic Law" is rejected, how much do weaker forms of recapitulation and the concept of heterochrony still inform modern evolutionary developmental biology (evo-devo)?

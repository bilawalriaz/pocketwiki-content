# Huế

## Overview
Huế is a centrally located Vietnamese municipality and a former imperial capital, serving as the nation's cultural, educational, and historical heart. Its significance stems from its role as the seat of the Nguyễn dynasty (1802–1945) and its UNESCO-listed complex of imperial monuments, which reflect a unique blend of Vietnamese, French, and Cham influences. The city's geography, straddling the Perfume River and featuring Southeast Asia's largest lagoon, has shaped its climate, economy, and historical vulnerability to flooding and conflict.

## Timeline
- **1306** — Champa cedes the Thuận Hóa region (including modern Huế) to Đại Việt as a wedding dowry.
- **1687** — Phú Xuân (Huế) becomes the provincial capital of the Nguyễn lords.
- **1738–1775** — Phú Xuân is the capital of Đàng Trong (the southern Vietnamese state).
- **1786** — The Tây Sơn rebellion captures Phú Xuân, making it their capital.
- **1802** — Emperor Gia Long reunifies Vietnam, establishes the Nguyễn dynasty, and makes Huế the national capital.
- **1899** — The city is officially renamed from Phú Xuân to Huế under French colonial rule.
- **1945** — Emperor Bảo Đại abdicates; Huế ceases to be the national capital.
- **1968** — The Battle of Huế during the Tet Offensive causes massive destruction and civilian casualties.
- **1999** — Severe flooding causes 600 deaths and affects 600,000 homes.
- **2025** — Huế merges with Thừa Thiên province to become Vietnam's sixth direct-controlled municipality.

## Body

### Historical Significance
The region's history begins with the Sa Huỳnh culture (c. 2,800 years ago) and the Champa kingdom, whose capital Kandarpapura was located nearby. After 1306, the area became part of Đại Việt. In the 17th century, the Nguyễn lords established their power base at Phú Xuân, which became the capital of southern Vietnam. Following the Tây Sơn rebellion and civil wars, Emperor Gia Long unified the country in 1802 and made Huế the imperial capital of the Nguyễn dynasty. The city remained the seat of Vietnamese emperors until 1945. During the French colonial period, it was the capital of the Annam protectorate.

### The Vietnam War and Modern Era
Huế's proximity to the 17th parallel made it a key battleground. The 1968 Battle of Huế, part of the Tet Offensive, resulted in heavy fighting, the destruction of historic buildings by American bombing, and the Massacre at Huế, where an estimated 2,800 to 6,000 civilians and prisoners were killed by the Việt Cộng. After 1975, the communist government initially neglected the city's imperial "feudal" relics, but later reversed this policy. The province was merged into Bình Trị Thiên (1976) before being reformed as Thừa Thiên Huế (1989). In 2025, it was elevated to a direct-controlled municipality.

### Geography and Climate
Huế is divided into four zones: mountains, hills, plains, and lagoons. The Perfume River flows through the city, and the Tam Giang–Cau Hai lagoon (68 km long, 220 km²) is the largest in Southeast Asia. The climate is tropical monsoon, with a cool, humid season (November–March) and a warm season (April–September). Annual precipitation averages 3,200 mm, with 70% falling in the rainy season (September–December), making the area prone to severe flooding.

### Culture and Religion
Huế is the birthplace of the modern áo dài, which evolved from 18th-century court dress reforms. The city is a center of Buddhism, with more monasteries than elsewhere in Vietnam. It was the home of Zen master Thích Nhất Hạnh and the starting point for monk Thích Quảng Đức's 1963 self-immolation protest. Huế cuisine is known for its refined, spicy, and often vegetarian dishes, reflecting its royal heritage.

### Tourism and Economy
Tourism is a major economic driver, centered on the UNESCO Imperial City, the Forbidden Purple City, and the royal tombs along the Perfume River. The economy also relies on seafood, agriculture (fruits like rambutan and durian), and traditional crafts like lantern-making and áo dài production. The Huế Brewery, founded in 1990, is a significant local industry.

## Terms
- **Đàng Trong**: The southern Vietnamese state ruled by the Nguyễn lords from the 16th to 18th centuries.
- **Tây Sơn rebellion**: A major uprising (1771–1802) that overthrew the Nguyễn and Trịnh lords before being unified under Nguyễn Ánh (Emperor Gia Long).
- **Nguyễn dynasty**: The last imperial dynasty of Vietnam (1802–1945), which ruled from Huế.
- **Imperial City**: The walled complex in Huế that served as the political and ceremonial center of the Nguyễn dynasty.
- **Forbidden Purple City**: The innermost enclosure of the Imperial City, reserved exclusively for the emperor and his court.
- **Áo dài**: A traditional Vietnamese long dress, whose modern form developed from 18th-century Huế court attire.
- **Perfume River (Sông Hương)**: The major river flowing through Huế, central to the city's geography and tourism.
- **Tam Giang–Cau Hai lagoon**: The largest lagoon in Southeast Asia, located within Huế's territory.
- **Direct-controlled municipality**: A Vietnamese administrative unit under the direct authority of the central government, like Hanoi or Ho Chi Minh City.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The exact origin of the place name "Huế" is uncertain, with the earliest definitive records appearing in 18th-century French documents.
- The scale and responsibility for the Massacre at Huế remain subjects of historical analysis, with estimates of victims ranging from 2,800 to 6,000.
- The long-term environmental and economic impacts of Huế's rapid development and tourism growth are ongoing concerns.

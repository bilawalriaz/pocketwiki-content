# Valencia

## Overview
Valencia is a major city on Spain's eastern Mediterranean coast, serving as the capital of the Valencian Community. Founded as a Roman colony, its history reflects a succession of rulers and cultural influences, shaping it into a modern economic and cultural hub. Its strategic port and vibrant traditions, like the Falles festival, make it a significant European city.

- **714 AD** — Surrendered to invading Moors.
- **1238** — Conquered by James I of Aragon; becomes capital of the Kingdom of Valencia.
- **15th century** — Valencian Golden Age; becomes one of Europe's largest cities.

- **1707** — Abolition of the Furs of Valencia after the War of the Spanish Succession.

- **1957** — Devastating flood of the Turia River leads to major urban remodelling.
- **2007** — Hosts the America's Cup yacht race.
- **2016** — Falles festival declared a UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage.

## Body

### Historical Development
Valencia was founded as a Roman colony in 138 BC. After the Roman period, it was part of the Visigothic Kingdom before being conquered by Moors around 714 AD. Islamic rule introduced new irrigation and crops. The city became an independent emirate (Taifa) in the 11th century. In 1238, King James I of Aragon conquered Valencia, expelling the Muslim population and establishing it as the capital of a new Christian kingdom.

The 15th century was a "Valencian Golden Age" of economic and cultural expansion, making Valencia one of Europe's largest cities. This prosperity was later undermined by the emergence of Atlantic trade routes, Barbary piracy, and the 1609 expulsion of the Moriscos (descendants of Muslims who had converted to Christianity), which caused a severe economic crisis. The city's political independence ended with the abolition of its ancient laws (Furs) in 1707 following the War of the Spanish Succession.

### Modern Era and 20th Century
The 18th century saw economic recovery, notably in silk manufacturing. The 19th century was marked by the Peninsular War and political instability. In the early 20th century, Valencia was an industrialised city. During the Spanish Civil War (1936-1939), it was heavily bombarded and served as the Republican capital from 1936 to 1937. Under Franco's dictatorship (1939-1975), the Valencian language was suppressed. A catastrophic flood in 1957 led to the diversion of the Turia River, creating a major park. After the return of democracy, Valencia became the capital of the new autonomous Valencian Community in 1982, spurring cultural and urban renewal.

### Geography and Climate
Valencia is located on the fertile alluvial plain of the Turia River on the Mediterranean coast. The nearby Albufera lagoon, now a freshwater body and natural park, is used for rice cultivation. The city has a subtropical semi-arid to Mediterranean climate with mild winters and hot, dry summers. It is known for its mild winters and is susceptible to heavy autumn rainfall events known as "cold drops" (gota fría).

### Economy and Demographics
Valencia is Spain's third-largest city with a population of 824,340. Its economy is predominantly service-oriented (84% of employment), with important industrial (8.5%) and agricultural (1.9%) sectors. The Port of Valencia is one of the busiest in the Mediterranean. The city is a major tourist destination, known for its festivals, cuisine, and modern architecture like the City of Arts and Sciences.

### Culture and Language
Valencia is officially bilingual in Spanish and Valencian (a variety of Catalan). Spanish is the predominant daily language, but Valencian is compulsory in schools. The city is famous for the Falles festival in March, a UNESCO-recognized tradition involving the creation and burning of large satirical sculptures. Other key traditions include the Holy Week processions and the Water Tribunal. Gastronomically, Valencia is the birthplace of paella.

## Terms
- **Taifa:** An independent Muslim-ruled principality in the Iberian Peninsula during the period of fragmentation of the Caliphate of Córdoba.
- **Moriscos:** Muslims in Christian Spain who had converted to Christianity, often under duress, and were later expelled.
- **Furs:** The ancient charters and laws of the Kingdom of Valencia, which granted it political and legal autonomy until their abolition in 1707.
- **Got a fría (Cold Drop):** A meteorological phenomenon of heavy rainfall associated with cut-off low pressure systems, common in the western Mediterranean in autumn.
- **Valencian:** A Romance language, a variety of Catalan, co-official with Spanish in the Valencian Community.
- **Falles:** A traditional Valencian festival held in March, featuring the construction and burning of large artistic monuments (ninots).
- **Nueva Planta decrees:** A set of decrees issued by Philip V after the War of the Spanish Succession, abolishing the separate laws and institutions of the Crown of Aragon, including Valencia.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The official name of the city in the Valencian language is "València," with a grave accent. However, in 2023, the municipal corporation agreed in principle to a dual official denomination "Valencia/Valéncia," with some political groups insisting on a non-standard acute accent ("é") in the Valencian name.
- The precise meaning of the Arabic name for the city during Islamic rule is debated: it could be "City of Joy" (Medina at-Tarab) or "City of Sands" (Medina at-Turab).

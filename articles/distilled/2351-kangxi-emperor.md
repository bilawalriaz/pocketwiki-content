# Kangxi Emperor

## Overview
The Kangxi Emperor (r. 1661–1722) was the third emperor of the Qing dynasty and the second to rule China proper. His 61-year reign is the longest in Chinese history, marking a period of consolidation, military expansion, and cultural flourishing that initiated the "High Qing" era of stability and wealth. He is widely regarded as one of China's greatest emperors.

## Timeline
- **4 May 1654** — Born as Xuanye, third son of the Shunzhi Emperor.
- **7 Feb 1661** — Enthroned at age seven; regents, led by Oboi, hold real power.
- **1669** — Arrests regent Oboi and assumes personal rule.
- **1673–1681** — Suppresses the Revolt of the Three Feudatories in southern China.
- **1683** — Conquers the Kingdom of Tungning in Taiwan, incorporating it into the Qing Empire.
- **1689** — Signs the Treaty of Nerchinsk with Russia, establishing a border.
- **1690s** — Leads campaigns against the Dzungar Mongols in the north and northwest.
- **1701** — Orders reconquest of border towns in Sichuan from Tibetans.
- **1705** — Appoints Lhazang Khan as Regent of Tibet.
- **1707** — Deposes his crown prince, Yinreng, for the first time.
- **1712** — Permanently deposes Yinreng, ending the public succession debate.
- **20 Dec 1722** — Dies; succeeded by his fourth son, the Yongzheng Emperor.

## Body

### Early Reign and Consolidation
Kangxi was enthroned as a child, with power held by regents, most notably Oboi. In 1669, with his grandmother's help, Kangxi arrested Oboi and began personal rule. He prioritized flood control, the Grand Canal, and the threat of southern feudatories. His early reign was marked by efforts to centralize power and win the loyalty of the Han Chinese scholar-bureaucrats, whom he engaged through cultural projects and the civil service examinations.

### Military Expansion and Defense
Kangxi's reign saw significant military consolidation. He suppressed the eight-year Revolt of the Three Feudatories (1673–1681), using primarily Han Chinese Green Standard Army troops. In 1683, he incorporated Taiwan after defeating the Ming loyalist Kingdom of Tungning. He secured the northern frontier through the Treaty of Nerchinsk (1689) with Russia and personally led campaigns against the Dzungar Mongols in the 1690s. He also extended Qing authority into Tibet, eventually sending an expedition to Lhasa in 1720 to oust a Dzungar invasion.

### Cultural and Economic Policy
Kangxi fostered a cultural renaissance to legitimize Manchu rule. He commissioned the *Kangxi Dictionary*, the *Complete Tang Poems*, and the *Complete Classics Collection of Ancient China*. He was interested in Western science and technology through Jesuit missionaries. Economically, his reign saw treasury growth from about 15 million taels in 1668 to a peak of around 50 million taels in the early 1700s, though later years saw decline due to military costs and corruption. In 1712, he froze the land tax, a policy that provided short-term stability but hindered future fiscal reform.

### Christianity and the Chinese Rites Controversy
Initially tolerant, Kangxi issued an Edict of Toleration for Christianity in 1692. However, the "Chinese Rites" controversy—whether Chinese Christians could participate in Confucian ceremonies and ancestor worship—led to conflict. The Pope's condemnation of the rites in 1715 prompted Kangxi to forbid Christian missions in China, viewing them as a source of trouble and a challenge to his authority.

### Succession Disputes
A bitter struggle among his sons, known as the "Nine Lords' War," dominated his later reign. His chosen heir, Yinreng, was deposed twice for misconduct and plotting. The conflict between factions supporting the fourth (Yinzhen) and fourteenth (Yinti) princes continued until Kangxi's death. He left his succession will in a sealed box, to be opened only after his death.

## Terms
- **Eight Banners Army**: The core Manchu military-social system, which was in decline during Kangxi's reign.
- **Green Standard Army**: The primarily Han Chinese military force that Kangxi relied on for major campaigns like the suppression of the Three Feudatories.
- **Feudatories**: Semi-autonomous regions in southern China granted to Ming generals who aided the Qing conquest, whose revocation sparked a major rebellion.
- **Kingdom of Tungning**: The Ming loyalist regime on Taiwan, defeated and annexed by the Qing in 1683.
- **Treaty of Nerchinsk (1689)**: The first formal treaty between the Qing Empire and a European power (Russia), establishing a border.
- **Dzungars**: A confederation of Oirat Mongols in the northwest who were a major military threat to the Qing during Kangxi's reign.
- **Palace Memorial System**: A secret communication channel between Kangxi and trusted officials, bypassing the regular bureaucracy.
- **Chinese Rites Controversy**: A dispute over whether Chinese Christians could practice Confucian rituals, leading to a rupture between the Qing court and the Papacy.
- **Nine Lords' War**: The intense succession struggle among Kangxi's sons during the latter part of his reign.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The primary historical debate concerns the succession: whether Kangxi legitimately chose his fourth son Yinzhen (the Yongzheng Emperor) as heir, or whether Yinzhen forged the will to seize the throne from his brother Yinti.
- The long-term fiscal impact of Kangxi's 1712 decision to freeze the land tax is debated; while it provided stability, it is seen as a policy that prevented necessary modernization of the Qing fiscal system.

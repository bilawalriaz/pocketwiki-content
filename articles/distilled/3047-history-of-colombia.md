# History of Colombia

## Overview
Colombia's history is defined by its rich pre-Columbian cultures, a transformative Spanish colonial period, a turbulent path to nationhood marked by civil wars and political violence, and a recent, complex peace process. Its geographic and climatic diversity has fostered strong regional identities, while its political evolution has oscillated between federalism and centralism, often punctuated by conflict.

- **1st millennium BCE** — Agrarian societies like the Muisca, Quimbaya, and Tairona develop.

- **1538** — Santa Fe de Bogotá is founded.

- **1819** — Battle of Boyacá secures independence; Gran Colombia is formed.
- **1830** — Gran Colombia dissolves; Republic of New Granada emerges.
- **1886** — Republic of Colombia is declared, ending a period of federalism.
- **1899–1902** — Thousand Days' War civil conflict.
- **1903** — Panama secedes from Colombia.
- **1948–1958** — "La Violencia" civil conflict erupts.
- **1958–1974** — National Front power-sharing agreement between Liberals and Conservatives.
- **1964** — FARC guerrilla group is formed.
- **1991** — New Constitution is adopted, incorporating some former guerrillas.
- **2016** — Revised peace agreement with FARC is signed.

## Body

### Pre-Columbian and Colonial Foundations
Human habitation in Colombia dates back approximately 12,000 years. By the 1st millennium BCE, complex agrarian societies had formed, including the Muisca Confederation in the highlands and the Tairona in the Sierra Nevada. The Spanish arrived in 1499, with the first permanent settlement at Santa Marta in 1525. Inland conquest led to the founding of Bogotá in 1538. The region became the Viceroyalty of New Granada, with Bogotá as its capital, part of a Spanish colonial system that restricted trade and fostered early discontent.

### Independence and the Struggle for Nationhood
Independence was led by Simón Bolívar, culminating in the 1819 Battle of Boyacá and the creation of Gran Colombia. This federation dissolved in 1830, leading to the Republic of New Granada. The 19th century saw experimentation with federalism (Granadine Confederation, United States of Colombia) before the centralist Republic of Colombia was declared in 1886. Political conflict between the Conservative and Liberal parties, rooted in differing visions for governance and the role of the Church, dominated this era and led to devastating civil wars, including the Thousand Days' War (1899–1902).

### The Republic: Conflict and the National Front
The assassination of Liberal leader Jorge Eliécer Gaitán in 1948 triggered "La Violencia," a period of extreme partisan violence. To end this, the two parties formed the National Front (1958–1974), a power-sharing agreement that alternated the presidency and guaranteed equal representation. While it ended the worst violence, it also marginalized dissidents and contributed to the rise of guerrilla movements like the FARC (formed 1964) and M-19. Panama seceded in 1903, partly due to U.S. involvement in the Panama Canal.

### Modern Conflict and Peace Processes
From the 1970s onward, the state fought multiple insurgencies (FARC, ELN, M-19) while also confronting powerful drug cartels, leading to widespread violence and corruption. A new Constitution in 1991, which incorporated some former guerrillas, marked a political shift. Peace processes with the M-19 succeeded in the early 1990s, but conflict with the FARC continued. Under President Álvaro Uribe (2002–2010), a military-focused "Democratic Security" policy reduced guerrilla activity but drew human rights criticism. His successor, Juan Manuel Santos, initiated peace talks that resulted in a 2016 agreement with the FARC, for which Santos received the Nobel Peace Prize. The conflict's legacy continues to shape Colombian politics and society.

## Terms
*   **Muisca Confederation:** A loose political alliance of Muisca chiefdoms in the Colombian highlands, noted for its advanced political system and goldworking.
*   **Viceroyalty of New Granada:** A Spanish colonial administrative division, established in 1717 and re-established in 1739, with its capital at Bogotá.
*   **Gran Colombia:** A short-lived republic (1819–1830) that included the territories of modern-day Colombia, Venezuela, Ecuador, and Panama.
*   **National Front:** A 1958–1974 agreement between Colombia's Liberal and Conservative parties to share power equally, ending "La Violencia."
*   **FARC (Fuerzas Armadas Revolucionarias de Colombia):** A Marxist-Leninist guerrilla group formed in 1964, which fought the Colombian state for decades before signing a 2016 peace accord.
*   **La Violencia:** A period of extreme, bipartisan political violence in Colombia from 1948 to 1958, triggered by the assassination of Jorge Eliécer Gaitán.
*   **Cacicazgo:** A pre-Columbian political system headed by a cacique (chief), featuring a pyramidal structure of power.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   The origins and dating of the rock art at Serranía de la Lindosa are debated, with arguments for both prehistoric and post-contact creation.
*   The effectiveness and human rights record of President Uribe's "Democratic Security" policy remain contested, with supporters crediting it for improved security and critics highlighting abuses.
*   The long-term success and implementation of the 2016 peace agreement with the FARC is an ongoing process, with challenges in rural reform, reintegration, and continued violence from other groups.
*   The causes and responsibility for the Palace of Justice siege (1985) and the subsequent deaths are still subjects of investigation and dispute.

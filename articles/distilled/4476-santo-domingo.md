# Santo Domingo

## Overview
Santo Domingo is the capital and largest city of the Dominican Republic, serving as the nation's political, economic, and cultural center. Founded in 1496, it is the oldest continuously inhabited European settlement in the Americas and the site of many "firsts" for the New World, including the first cathedral, university, and fortress. Its historic Colonial Zone is a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

## Timeline
- **1496** — Founded by Bartholomew Columbus; originally named La Nueva Isabela.
- **1498** — Officially becomes the oldest European city in the Americas.
- **1502** — Destroyed by a hurricane; rebuilt on the opposite bank of the Ozama River.
- **1521** — Site of the first major slave revolt in the Americas.
- **1586** — Captured and held for ransom by privateer Sir Francis Drake.
- **1795** — Ceded to France by Spain.
- **1821** — Becomes capital of the independent Republic of Spanish Haiti.
- **1844** — Gains independence from Haiti, becoming the capital of the Dominican Republic.
- **1861** — Re-annexed by Spain.
- **1865** — Spanish rule ends after the Dominican Restoration War.
- **1916** — First U.S. military intervention begins.
- **1930** — Hurricane San Zenón causes major destruction; city is later renamed Ciudad Trujillo.
- **1961** — Following dictator Trujillo's assassination, the city reverts to the name Santo Domingo.
- **1965** — Second U.S. military intervention during a civil war.
- **1990** — Colonial Zone declared a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
- **2013** — Second line of the Santo Domingo Metro opens.

## Body

### History and Colonial Significance
Santo Domingo was founded by the Spanish Empire and served as the first seat of Spanish colonial rule in the New World, the Captaincy General of Santo Domingo. It was the launch point for major expeditions that colonized Puerto Rico, Cuba, and Mexico, and discovered the Pacific Ocean. The city's layout, established after a 1502 hurricane, is preserved in the Colonial Zone. It was the site of the first university, cathedral, castle, monastery, and fortress in the Americas. The city changed hands between Spain, France, and Haiti multiple times in the late 18th and early 19th centuries before becoming the capital of the independent Dominican Republic in 1844. The 20th century saw periods of political instability, two U.S. interventions (1916 and 1965), and the dictatorship of Rafael Trujillo, who renamed the city Ciudad Trujillo from 1936 to 1961.

### Geography and Climate
Located on the southern coast of Hispaniola at the mouth of the Ozama River, Santo Domingo has a tropical climate with high humidity and warm temperatures year-round. It has a borderline tropical rainforest/monsoon climate, with a rainy season from May to November. The city is susceptible to hurricanes, such as Hurricane Georges in 1998. Its natural harbor has historically made it a vital port.

### Government and Economy
Santo Domingo is the center of the Dominican Republic's national government, hosting the presidential office, ministries, Congress, and Supreme Court. It is the country's primary economic hub, concentrating most of its industries, wealth, and corporate headquarters. The city has a modern telecommunications infrastructure and a growing vertical skyline, though it also faces challenges with poverty in some areas.

### Culture and Education
The city is a major cultural center with numerous museums, theaters, and performance companies. The Plaza de la Cultura houses key institutions like the National Theater and Palace of Fine Arts. Santo Domingo is also the educational capital, home to eighteen universities, including the Universidad Autónoma de Santo Domingo (UASD), founded in 1538 and the oldest university in the Americas.

### Transportation
Santo Domingo has an extensive transportation network. The Santo Domingo Metro is the most extensive in the Caribbean and Central America. A system of aerial cable cars (Teleférico) connects low-income areas to the metro. The city is served by two international airports, with Las Américas International Airport (AILA) being the main gateway and busiest cargo airport in the region. The Port of Santo Domingo on the Ozama River is a major hub for cruise ships and freight. The city is the terminus for four national highways and has a complex system of public buses, privately operated bus corridors, taxis, and ride-sharing services.

## Terms
- **Distrito Nacional (D.N.)**: The administrative district coterminous with the city of Santo Domingo.
- **Zona Colonial**: The historic colonial district of Santo Domingo, a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
- **Captaincy General**: A Spanish colonial administrative division; Santo Domingo was the first in the New World.
- **Cacique**: A chief of the indigenous Taíno people.
- **Criollo**: A person of Spanish descent born in the Americas.
- **Peninsular**: A person born in Spain (the Iberian Peninsula).
- **Motoconcho**: A motorcycle taxi.
- **Guagua/Voladora**: A low-quality public bus.
- **Carro Público/Concho**: A shared taxi.

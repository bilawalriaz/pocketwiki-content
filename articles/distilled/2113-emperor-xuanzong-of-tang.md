# Emperor Xuanzong of Tang

## Overview
Emperor Xuanzong of Tang (Li Longji, 685–762) was the longest-reigning emperor of the Tang dynasty (712–756). His rule is a pivotal study in imperial decline: he presided over the dynasty's golden age, the Kaiyuan era, but his later mismanagement of power, trust in corrupt officials, and indulgence in pleasure directly led to the catastrophic An Lushan Rebellion, which shattered the empire and ended its golden age.

## Timeline
- **685** — Li Longji born in Luoyang.
- **710** — Leads coup against Empress Dowager Wei, restoring his father Emperor Ruizong.
- **712** — Ascends the throne as Emperor Xuanzong after Ruizong abdicates.
- **713** — Executes Princess Taiping and her faction, securing sole imperial power.
- **c. 713–741** — Kaiyuan era: period of political stability, economic prosperity, and cultural flourishing.
- **736** — Chancellor Li Linfu gains dominance, marking a turning point toward decline.
- **744** — Yang Guifei enters the imperial court as Xuanzong's favorite consort.
- **751** — Tang suffers major defeats at the Battle of Talas and against Nanzhao.
- **755** — An Lushan launches the An Lushan Rebellion.
- **756** — Rebels capture Chang'an; Xuanzong flees, abdicates to his son Suzong.
- **762** — Xuanzong dies in Chang'an as a retired emperor.

## Body

### Rise to Power
Li Longji, third son of Emperor Ruizong, navigated the dangerous court of Empress Wu Zetian, who killed his mother. After Wu Zetian's overthrow, he cultivated allies among the imperial guards. In 710, following the suspicious death of Emperor Zhongzong, he led a successful coup against the usurping Empress Dowager Wei, enabling his father Ruizong to retake the throne. Despite not being the eldest son, Li Longji was made crown prince due to his achievement, overcoming opposition from the powerful Princess Taiping. He became emperor in 712 when Ruizong abdicated, but initially shared power with his father and the princess.

### The Golden Age (Kaiyuan Era)
After eliminating Princess Taiping's faction in 713, Xuanzong began his Kaiyuan era (713–741). He initially governed with capable chancellors like Yao Chong and Song Jing, who emphasized administrative efficiency and rule of law. The era saw political stability, economic growth, and cultural advances. However, the emperor gradually grew tired of state affairs. The rise of the sycophantic chancellor Li Linfu in the 730s marked a turning point; Li Linfu monopolized power, discouraged criticism, and promoted non-Han generals like An Lushan to border commands, believing they would be personally loyal and less threatening to central authority.

### Decline and Rebellion
Xuanzong's later reign was characterized by neglect and indulgence. He became infatuated with Yang Guifei, the wife of his son, and elevated her and her family to immense power and wealth. Corrupt officials like Li Linfu and later Yang Guozhong controlled the government. Military power became dangerously concentrated in the hands of regional governors (*jiedushi*), especially An Lushan. In 755, An Lushan rebelled, quickly seizing the eastern capital Luoyang. The imperial army, weakened by poor leadership and internal strife, failed to stop him. In 756, as rebels approached Chang'an, Xuanzong fled. During the flight, his own guards mutinied, killing Yang Guozhong and forcing Xuanzong to order the execution of Yang Guifei. He abdicated to his son, Emperor Suzong.

### As Retired Emperor
Xuanzong lived as retired emperor (*taishang huang*) in Chang'an, largely stripped of real power. He was forcibly moved from his palace by the eunuch Li Fuguo, who isolated him from his attendants. Xuanzong died in 762, depressed and in poor health. His reign's failure is historically attributed to his over-trust in treacherous ministers and generals, which enabled the rebellion that permanently weakened the Tang dynasty.

## Terms
- **Jiedushi (Military Governor):** A regional military commander who, by Xuanzong's later reign, held both military and civil administrative power, leading to dangerous regional autonomy.
- **Kaiyuan Era (713–741):** The period of Xuanzong's reign considered the golden age of the Tang dynasty, noted for stability and prosperity.
- **An Lushan Rebellion (755–763):** A devastating civil war led by the general An Lushan that shattered the Tang empire and ended its golden age.
- **Taishang Huang (Retired Emperor):** An abdicated emperor who retains some honorific status but not primary ruling power.
- **Confucian Principles of Succession:** The traditional rule favoring the eldest son of the primary wife as heir, which was set aside for Xuanzong due to his political achievement.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Turning Point of the Reign:** Historians debate whether the true turning point was the rise of Li Linfu in the 730s, the emperor's growing neglect, or the structural problem of empowering regional military governors (*jiedushi*).
- **Role of Yang Guifei:** While traditional accounts blame her and her family for distracting the emperor, modern scholars assess the extent of her actual political influence versus her role as a symbol of court decadence.
- **Legacy of Military Reforms:** The shift from a conscription-based army to a salaried, professional border force under generals like An Lushan is seen by some as a necessary adaptation that, due to political mismanagement, ultimately enabled the rebellion.

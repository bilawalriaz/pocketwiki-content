# Andhra Pradesh

## Overview
Andhra Pradesh is a state on India's southeastern coast, known for its rich history, diverse geography, and significant economic contributions. It is the seventh-largest state by area and the tenth by population, with Telugu as its official language. The state's history spans from prehistoric settlements to modern political developments, including its bifurcation in 2014.

## Timeline
- **c. 800 BCE** — Andhras mentioned in the Aitareya Brahmana of the Rigveda.
- **c. 310 BCE** — Greek historian Megasthenes describes the Andhras.
- **2nd century BCE–3rd century CE** — Satavahana dynasty rules the Deccan from Dhanyakataka.
- **1953** — Andhra State is carved out of Madras State.
- **1956** — Andhra State merges with Telugu-speaking regions of Hyderabad State to form Andhra Pradesh.
- **2014** — Telangana is bifurcated from Andhra Pradesh.
- **2017** — Government of Andhra Pradesh begins operating from new capital, Amaravati.

## Body

### Etymology and Early History
The name "Andhra" is derived from the Old Telugu word *aṉṟu*, possibly used by Indo-Aryan people to refer to the Telugu people. The region's history includes prehistoric stone tools and Neolithic settlements. The Andhras are documented in ancient texts, and the Satavahana dynasty (2nd century BCE–3rd century CE) was a major power, with trade links to the Roman Empire and the development of the Amaravati school of art.

### Medieval and Colonial Rule
After the Satavahanas, various dynasties ruled, including the Eastern Chalukyas, Kakatiyas, and the Vijayanagara Empire. European colonization began in the 17th century, with the British eventually controlling most of the region as part of the Madras Presidency. The region saw social reform movements and contributions to India's independence struggle.

### Post-Independence and Bifurcation
After Indian independence in 1947, the region was part of Madras State. Following the fast unto death of Potti Sreeramulu, Andhra State was formed in 1953. It merged with the Telugu-speaking areas of Hyderabad State in 1956 to create Andhra Pradesh. In 2014, the state was bifurcated, with Telangana becoming a separate state.

### Geography and Environment
The state is divided into Coastal Andhra and Rayalaseema by the Eastern Ghats. Major rivers include the Godavari and Krishna. The climate is predominantly tropical, with significant rainfall from the southwest monsoon. The state has diverse flora and fauna, including protected areas like the Nagarjunsagar-Srisailam Tiger Reserve.

### Demographics and Culture
As of the 2011 census, the population was 49.6 million, with a literacy rate of 67.35%. Telugu is the official language. The culture features classical arts like Kuchipudi dance and Carnatic music, traditional clothing, and distinctive cuisine. The state is also known for its pilgrimage centers and unique geographical indications.

### Administration and Politics
The state has a bicameral legislature and is divided into 28 districts. Politics have been dominated by the Indian National Congress and the Telugu Desam Party. The state's economy is driven by agriculture, industry, and services, with a GSDP of ₹17.62 trillion for 2025-26. Agriculture employs a majority of the population, with rice as the staple crop.

### Infrastructure and Services
The state has an extensive road and rail network, major ports, and airports. It has significant power generation capacity and a growing IT sector. Healthcare and education systems are expanding, with numerous schools, universities, and libraries.

## Terms
- **Satavahana dynasty**: A major ancient Indian dynasty that ruled the Deccan region from the 2nd century BCE to the 3rd century CE.
- **Eastern Ghats**: A discontinuous range of mountains along India's eastern coast, separating coastal plains from the Deccan Plateau.
- **Kuchipudi**: A classical Indian dance form that originated in the village of Kuchipudi in Andhra Pradesh.
- **Geographical Indication (GI)**: A sign used on products that have a specific geographical origin and possess qualities or a reputation due to that origin.
- **Bifurcation**: The division of a state into two separate states, as occurred with Andhra Pradesh and Telangana in 2014.
- **GSDP (Gross State Domestic Product)**: The total value of goods and services produced within a state in a year.
- **Potti Sreeramulu**: An Indian revolutionary who fasted to death in 1952, demanding a separate state for Telugu-speaking people.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The exact etymology of the term "Andhra" is debated among scholars.
- The historical identification of the Satavahana capital Dhanyakataka is a subject of scholarly discussion.
- The implementation and impact of the Land Titling Act of 2023, which was repealed in 2024, remain points of analysis.
- The long-term economic and social effects of the state's bifurcation in 2014 are ongoing areas of study.

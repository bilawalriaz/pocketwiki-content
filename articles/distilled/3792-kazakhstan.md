# Kazakhstan

## Overview
Kazakhstan is the world's largest landlocked country and ninth-largest by area, situated primarily in Central Asia with a small portion in Eastern Europe. A resource-rich, multi-ethnic nation, it gained independence from the Soviet Union in 1991. Its history encompasses nomadic empires, Mongol and Russian conquests, Soviet rule, and a modern transition to a market economy dominated by oil and gas exports, while navigating authoritarian governance and recent political reforms.

## Body

### Etymology and Early History
The name "Kazakh" derives from a Turkic root meaning "free" or "wanderer," reflecting its nomadic culture. Inhabited since the Paleolithic, the territory saw the Botai culture (c. 3700–3100 BC) credited with first horse domestication. Antiquity was dominated by Iranian nomads like the Saka and Scythians. Turkic migrations from the 5th century onward transformed the region. It later became part of the Mongol Empire and then the Golden Horde, a key period in Kazakh ethnic formation.

### The Kazakh Khanate and Russian Conquest
Following the Golden Horde's disintegration, the Kazakh Khanate was established in the 15th century, peaking in the early 16th century. By the 18th century, it had fragmented into three *jüz* (tribal divisions). Weakened by Dzungar invasions and internal strife, the Khanate was gradually absorbed by the Russian Empire, which completed its conquest by the mid-19th century. Russian rule brought administrative control, military garrisons, and large-scale Slavic settlement, disrupting the nomadic economy and provoking uprisings like the Central Asian revolt of 1916.

### Soviet Era and Independence
After the 1917 Russian Revolution, a brief Alash Orda autonomy ended with Bolshevik conquest by 1920. The territory became the Kazakh ASSR, elevated to a full union republic in 1936. Forced collectivization in the late 1920s and 1930s caused a devastating famine, killing an estimated 1.5 million. The Soviet era also saw mass deportations of various ethnic groups, making ethnic Kazakhs a minority by 1959. The Virgin Lands Campaign (1953) accelerated agriculture. Kazakhstan declared sovereignty in 1990 and full independence on 16 December 1991, the last Soviet republic to do so.

### Modern Kazakhstan
Under first president Nursultan Nazarbayev (1991–2019), Kazakhstan transitioned to a market economy, leveraging vast oil and gas reserves to become Central Asia's dominant economic power. The capital moved from Almaty to Astana in 1997. While achieving growth, the political system remained authoritarian. Successor Kassym-Jomart Tokayev has initiated incremental reforms since 2019, including term limits and decentralization, though the government is still classified as authoritarian. Kazakhstan pursues a "multi-vector foreign policy," balancing ties with Russia, China, and the West.

### Economy and Resources
Kazakhstan's economy relies heavily on extractive industries, particularly oil and natural gas, which dominate exports and industrial output. It holds the 11th largest proven petroleum and gas reserves. The country is a leading global uranium producer with significant chromium, lead, and zinc reserves. Agriculture, especially grain, is also important. Since independence, it has attracted substantial foreign direct investment, primarily in energy, and joined the World Trade Organization in 2015.

### Demographics and Culture
With over 20 million people and low population density, ethnic Kazakhs form a majority (71.5%), with Russians as the largest minority (14.4%). The country is officially bilingual (Kazakh and Russian) and secular, with a Muslim majority (69.3%) and significant Christian minority (17.2%). Culture has roots in nomadic pastoralism and oral traditions, including epic poetry and music. The modern state promotes a synthesis of nomadic heritage with Soviet and contemporary influences.

## Terms
- **Jüz (Zhuz):** One of the three major tribal divisions (Senior, Middle, Junior) of the Kazakh Khanate by the 17th century.
- **Golden Horde:** A Mongol and later Turkicized khanate, established in the 13th century, considered a key predecessor to Kazakh ethnic identity.
- **Virgin Lands Campaign:** A Soviet agricultural program initiated in 1953 to cultivate the Kazakh steppe for grain.
- **Multi-vector foreign policy:** Kazakhstan's strategy of maintaining balanced relations with all major global powers.
- **Akim:** The title for a regional or district governor in Kazakhstan's administrative system.
- **Beshbarmak:** A traditional dish of boiled meat served with noodles, often considered the national meal.
- **Küy:** Traditional instrumental music, often performed on the dombra, referencing stories or historical events.
- **Batyr:** An honorary title meaning "hero" or "brave man," historically given to distinguished warriors.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Role of the Golden Horde:** Historiography debates its role in Kazakh state formation. Soviet narratives minimized it, while modern Kazakh and some Western historians view it as a crucial predecessor to the Khanate.
- **Assessment of Kenesary Qasymov:** The leader of the last major armed resistance against Russian rule (1837–1847) is contested, portrayed variously as a national hero or a reactionary.
- **Political Reform Trajectory:** The extent and sincerity of reforms under President Tokayev are debated. While some steps have been taken (e.g., term limits), Kazakhstan remains classified as an authoritarian regime by international observers.

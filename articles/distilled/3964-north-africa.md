# North Africa

## Overview
North Africa is a region on the northern African continent whose boundaries are defined variously, but most commonly include Algeria, Egypt, Libya, Morocco, Tunisia, and Western Sahara. Its history is marked by ancient indigenous civilizations, followed by transformative Arab migrations and Islamic conquests that reshaped its demographic and cultural landscape. The region's geography is dominated by the Sahara Desert, the Atlas Mountains, and the Nile River, and it has been a crossroads of African, Middle Eastern, and Mediterranean influences.

## Timeline
- **c. 2.6 million BCE** — Earliest hominid remains found in Ain-Hnech, Setif.
- **c. 300,000-350,000 years ago** — Possible emergence of early *Homo sapiens* across Africa, with key finds in Jebel Irhoud, Morocco.
- **c. 8000-4000 BCE** — Neolithic Subpluvial period; cave paintings at Tassili n'Ajjer depict daily life.
- **c. 3500 BCE** — Rapid desertification of the Sahara due to Earth's orbital tilt.
- **7th century BCE** — Phoenician colonies established along the Mediterranean coast, including Carthage.
- **146 BCE** — Carthage conquered by Rome; North African territories become the Roman province of Africa.
- **42 AD** — Kingdom of Mauretania annexed by the Roman Empire.
- **5th century CE** — Vandals cross the Strait of Gibraltar and conquer Roman North Africa.
- **640-700 CE** — Early Muslim conquests bring most of North Africa under Muslim rule.
- **11th century** — Banu Hilal and Banu Sulaym migrations into the Maghreb accelerate Arabization.
- **16th-18th centuries** — Spanish Empire conquers several North African coastal cities.
- **1940-1943** — North African Campaign of World War II.
- **1950s-1960s** — All North African states gain independence.

## Body

### Prehistory and Ancient Civilizations
North Africa's human history is ancient. The earliest inhabitants left significant remains, with hominid occupation dating back 2.6 million years. Recent discoveries at Jebel Irhoud, Morocco, suggest early *Homo sapiens* may have been present across Africa 100,000 years earlier than previously thought, supporting a continental-scale emergence of modern humans. The Neolithic Revolution began in parts of North Africa in the 6th millennium BCE, introducing domesticated plants and animals before the Sahara rapidly dried around 3500 BCE. This ecological change set the stage for the rise of dynastic civilizations like ancient Egypt in the Nile Valley.

### Antiquity and Roman Rule
The Phoenicians established colonies, most famously Carthage, which became a major commercial power. After the Punic Wars, Rome conquered Carthage in 146 BCE, making North Africa a vital grain-producing province. The region remained part of the Roman Empire for centuries until the 5th century, when the Vandals invaded. The loss of North Africa's resources is considered a key factor in the decline of the Western Roman Empire.

### Arab Conquests and Islamic Era
The early Muslim conquests reached North Africa by 640 CE, and by 700 CE most of the region was under Muslim rule. This initiated a long process of Islamization and Arabization. Indigenous Berbers formed their own states, but major migrations in the 11th century, notably by the Banu Hilal and Banu Sulaym, dramatically shifted demographics, pushing Berber populations into mountainous areas and spreading Arab culture and language. This era defined the region's dominant cultural and religious identity.

### Modern History and Geopolitics
After periods of loose Ottoman control and European colonial expansion, the entire region came under European occupation by the 19th century. The North African Campaign of World War II was a major theater of conflict. All North African states gained independence in the 1950s and 1960s. The region remains geopolitically significant, often grouped with the Middle East as the MENA region. A current territorial dispute exists over Western Sahara between Morocco and the Polisario Front.

### Demographics and Culture
The population is predominantly Arab, with significant Berber minorities, especially in Algeria and Morocco. Modern Standard Arabic is the official language, with Berber also official in some countries. The region is overwhelmingly Muslim, with notable Christian (Coptic) and small Jewish minorities. Culturally, North Africa blends Arab, indigenous Berber, and African elements, with distinct identities in the Maghreb (western North Africa) and the Nile Valley.

## Terms
- **Maghreb**: The western part of North Africa, typically including Morocco, Algeria, Tunisia, and Libya.
- **Berbers (Imazighen)**: Indigenous ethnic group of North Africa, with a distinct language and culture, predating Arab migrations.
- **Arabization**: The historical process of cultural and linguistic shift towards Arabic and Arab identity in North Africa.
- **Islamization**: The process of widespread conversion to Islam in the region.
- **Sahara**: The world's largest hot desert, covering much of North Africa and acting as both a barrier and a corridor.
- **Atlas Mountains**: A major mountain range across Morocco, Algeria, and Tunisia, forming a geographic and climatic divide.
- **Nile Valley**: The fertile corridor along the Nile River, the cradle of ancient Egyptian civilization.
- **Neolithic Revolution**: The transition from hunting-gathering to agriculture and settled life, beginning in the region around the 6th millennium BCE.
- **Copts**: An ethno-religious group in Egypt, native to the region, who are predominantly Christian.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Scope of North Africa**: There is no singularly accepted definition for the region's boundaries, with variations among the UN, African Union, and other bodies regarding the inclusion of countries like Sudan and Mauritania.
- **Origins of Modern Humans**: While East Africa has traditionally been considered the cradle of humanity, finds in Morocco suggest early *Homo sapiens* may have been widespread across Africa much earlier, leading to debate about the continent-scale emergence of modern humans.
- **Genetic History**: Scholars debate the relative impact of various migrations on the North African genome, including the Arab migrations of the 1st millennium CE versus earlier Neolithic farmer migrations from Western Asia and Europe. There is also discussion on the classification of certain genetic markers as "African" or "Eurasian" given the region's long-standing population connections.

# Wends

## Overview
"Wends" is a historical exonym (a name given by outsiders) used primarily by Germanic peoples to refer to various Slavic groups inhabiting areas of present-day northeast Germany. The term did not denote a single, homogeneous people but was applied to different tribes and regions over time. Its most enduring legacy is the Lusatian Sorbs, a Slavic minority in eastern Germany who maintain their distinct language and culture today.

## Timeline
- **1st millennium AD** — West Slavic tribes migrate into areas between the Elbe and Oder rivers, assimilating remaining Germanic populations.
- **6th century** — Germanic neighbors adapt the older term "Venedi" for these new Slavic inhabitants, calling them "Wends."
- **983** — A major Wendish uprising successfully delays Germanization for about two centuries.
- **1068/69** — A German expedition destroys the major pagan Wendish temple at Rethra.
- **1147** — The Wend Crusade is launched in northeastern Germany.
- **1168** — A Danish crusade captures and destroys the Wendish temple-fortress at Arkona.
- **12th–14th centuries** — The *Ostsiedlung* (German eastward settlement) peaks, leading to large-scale German migration into Wendish lands.
- **1356** — The Golden Bull of the Holy Roman Empire acknowledges the multi-national state and mandates Slavic language instruction for electors' heirs.
- **1540–1973** — Swedish kings use the title "King of the Wends."
- **1854** — A group of Wends departs for Texas, settling in central Texas.
- **1972–1973** — Denmark and Sweden discontinue the royal title "King of the Wends."

## Body

### Term and Origins
The term "Wends" is derived from the Roman-era people known as the Veneti. Germanic peoples first applied this name to the ancient Veneti and later adapted it for the Slavic groups they encountered. For medieval Scandinavians, "Wends" referred to Slavs near the Baltic Sea. For inhabitants of the northern Holy Roman Empire, particularly Saxons, a "Wend" was a Slav living west of the River Oder in the area known as *Germania Slavica*. Southern Germans used the variant "Winde" for Slavs they contacted, such as those in Bavaria Slavica or Slovenes.

### Rise and Tribal Organization (500–1000)
During the Slavic migrations, West Slavs moved into the Elbe-Oder region. They organized into various tribes, such as the Obotrites, Lutici, and Pomeranians, often named after local places. These tribes lived in settlements secured by wooden burghs. Some unified into larger entities, like the Obotrite duchy, while others, like the Lutici alliance, remained independent. In 983, a successful uprising against the Holy Roman Empire's missions and colonies halted German expansion for two centuries.

### Decline and Assimilation (1000–1200)
After 983, the Wends faced increasing pressure from Germans, Danes, and Poles. Key pagan religious centers like Rethra (destroyed 1068/69) and Arkona (destroyed 1168) were captured. The 12th-century Wend Crusade and Danish Northern Crusades further weakened pagan Wendish polities. Concurrently, the *Ostsiedlung* brought large numbers of German settlers, who were invited by local rulers to develop farmland and establish new towns under German law. This process led to the gradual assimilation of most Wends into German-speaking culture, with the Polabian language fading by the 17th or 18th century.

### Legacy and Modern Descendants
The primary surviving descendants of the Wends are the Lusatian Sorbs in eastern Germany, who preserve their West Slavic languages and culture. The term "Wend" was also historically applied to other Slavic groups, including Slovenes and Slovaks. The legacy of the Wends persists in thousands of Central and Northern German place names of Slavic origin (e.g., Berlin, Leipzig). A community of Texas Wends, founded in 1854, also maintains a distinct heritage.

## Terms
- **Exonym**: A name given to a people or group by outsiders.
- **Ostsiedlung**: The medieval eastward expansion and settlement of Germanic peoples into Slavic and other territories.
- **Polabian Slavs**: A group of West Slavic tribes who lived between the Elbe and Oder rivers.
- **March (Mark)**: A medieval border territory, often established to defend against or control neighboring peoples.
- **Pagan**: A follower of a polytheistic religion; in this context, referring to the pre-Christian Slavic faith.
- **Golden Bull of 1356**: A decree that established the constitutional structure of the Holy Roman Empire.
- **Domowina**: The political organization representing the Sorbian minority in Germany.

## Debates and Open Questions
The source notes that the term "Wends" referred to various people, tribes, or groups depending on the context, indicating it was not a self-identifier for a single people. The exact origins and application of the term have evolved, with its use for Slovenes later becoming derogatory. The degree of cultural and genetic assimilation versus survival among different Wendish groups over centuries is a subject of historical study.

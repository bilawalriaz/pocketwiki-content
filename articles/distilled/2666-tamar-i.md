# Tamar I

## Overview
Tamar the Great (c. 1160/1166–1213) was the first woman to rule Georgia in her own right, reigning from 1184 to 1213. Her reign represents the apex of the Georgian Golden Age, a period of significant political, military, and cultural achievement. She consolidated a powerful empire in the Caucasus and is a central, idealized figure in Georgian national memory.

## Timeline
- **c. 1160/1166** — Born to King George III and Queen Burdukhan.
- **1177** — Father George III crushes a noble rebellion; Tamar is proclaimed co-ruler in 1178.
- **1184** — George III dies; Tamar becomes sole monarch, crowned at Gelati Cathedral.
- **1185** — Marries Yury Bogolyubsky.
- **1187** — Divorces and expels Yury after political conflict.
- **1191** — Marries David Soslan, an Alan prince.
- **1195** — Georgian victory at the Battle of Shamkor against the Eldiguzids.
- **1202/1203/1204** — Victory at the Battle of Basian against the Seljuk Sultanate of Rum.
- **1204** — Foundation of the Empire of Trebizond with Georgian aid.
- **1206** — Georgian army captures Kars.
- **1213** — Tamar dies; succeeded by her son George IV (Lasha-Giorgi).

## Body

### Early Life and Accession
Tamar was the daughter of King George III. In 1177, her father suppressed a major noble rebellion, eliminating rival claimants. To secure her succession, he crowned Tamar as co-ruler in 1178. Upon George III's death in 1184, Tamar became sole monarch. She faced immediate opposition from the aristocracy, who questioned her legitimacy as a female ruler and sought to limit royal power. With the support of her aunt Rusudan and the Catholicos-Patriarch, she navigated these challenges, though she was initially forced to make concessions to the nobility.

### Consolidation of Power and Marriages
Tamar's marriage was a critical state matter. Her first husband, Yury Bogolyubsky, proved politically difficult and was divorced in 1187. He later led failed coup attempts. Tamar then chose her second husband, David Soslan, an Alan prince. This marriage provided a capable military partner and helped consolidate her power base with loyal nobles like the Mkhargrdzeli family. David Soslan remained a subordinate consort, while Tamar retained the title *mepe* ("king"), emphasizing her unique sovereign status.

### Foreign Policy and Military Campaigns
Once secure, Tamar pursued an energetic foreign policy, exploiting the decline of the Seljuk Turks. Georgian armies, often led by David Soslan and generals like the Mkhargrdzeli brothers, achieved major victories. They defeated the Eldiguzids at Shamkor (1195) and the Seljuks at Basian (1202/1203/1204). Georgia expanded southward, reducing Shirvan to a tributary and challenging Ayyubid and Seljuk power in Armenia and eastern Anatolia. A significant strategic move was the 1204 establishment of the Empire of Trebizond on the Black Sea, founded with Georgian aid to Tamar's nephews, the Komnenoi princes. This expanded Georgian influence and positioned it as a protector of regional Christians.

### The Golden Age
Tamar's reign saw the Georgian state reach its medieval zenith, forming a pan-Caucasian empire. This era was the apex of Georgian feudalism (*patronq'moba*), where a powerful military elite and expanded commerce brought great wealth. Culturally, it was a period of flourishing architecture, literature, and art. The period is immortalized in Shota Rustaveli's epic poem *The Knight in the Panther's Skin*. Georgia identified strongly with the Christian Byzantine world, and Tamar's court actively supported monasteries and churches across the Eastern Mediterranean.

### Death and Legacy
Tamar died in 1213 of a "devastating disease." Her death was followed within two decades by devastating Mongol invasions that ended Georgian ascendancy. This abrupt decline cemented her reign as a legendary "Golden Age" in national memory. She was later canonized by the Georgian Orthodox Church and remains a potent symbol of national pride, idealized in art, folklore, and modern culture as a wise, pious, and powerful ruler.

## Terms
- **Mepe**: Georgian for "king" or "sovereign," a title applied to Tamar to emphasize her unique position as a ruling monarch, not a consort.
- **Bagrationi dynasty**: The royal dynasty of Georgia, claiming descent from the biblical King David.
- **Patronq'moba**: The Georgian form of feudalism, a system of land tenure and loyalty that reached its peak under Tamar.
- **Catholicos-Patriarch**: The head of the Georgian Orthodox Church.
- **Mkhargrdzeli (Zakarian)**: A powerful Armenian noble family that served as key military commanders and allies to Tamar.
- **Eldiguzids**: A Turkic dynasty that succeeded the Seljuks in parts of Azerbaijan and northwestern Iran, a rival to Georgia.
- **Shah-Armens**: A Turkic dynasty ruling in the Lake Van region of Armenia, another regional rival.
- **Fourth Crusade (1204)**: The Western European campaign that sacked Constantinople, creating a power vacuum Tamar exploited by founding Trebizond.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Exact Birth Year**: Sources give either c. 1160 or c. 1166.
- **Date of Death**: Traditional scholarship places her death in 1213, but some evidence suggests it may have been earlier, in 1207 or 1210.
- **Burial Place**: The traditional location is the Gelati Monastery, but archaeological searches have not found her grave there. Legends suggest secret burial or reinterment in the Holy Land.

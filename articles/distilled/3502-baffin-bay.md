# Baffin Bay

## Overview
Baffin Bay is a large marginal sea of the Arctic Ocean located between Baffin Island (Canada) and Greenland. It is a critical, ice-choked waterway connecting the Arctic and Atlantic Oceans, known for its harsh climate, rich marine life concentrated in a seasonal open-water area called the North Water, and historical significance in the search for the Northwest Passage.

## Timeline
- **c. 500 BC** — The area is first inhabited.
- **c. AD 1200** — Thule (Inuit) peoples replace the earlier Dorset culture.
- **10th–15th centuries** — Norse colonization possibly reaches the bay's shores.
- **1585** — John Davis becomes the first recorded European to enter the bay.
- **1616** — William Baffin charts the bay, naming key sounds; doubts an ice-free passage.
- **1818** — John Ross's voyage confirms Baffin's earlier account.
- **1928 onward** — Modern scientific studies begin.
- **1933** — A 7.3 magnitude earthquake, the largest known north of the Arctic Circle, occurs.
- **1975** — The town of Nanisivik is built to support the first Canadian Arctic mine.
- **2002** — The Nanisivik Mine closes.

## Body

### Extent and Geography
The International Hydrographic Organization defines Baffin Bay's boundaries, connecting it to the Atlantic via Davis Strait and to the Arctic via Nares Strait. It is a deep basin, with the central Baffin Basin reaching 2,136 m (7,008 ft). A cyclonic circulation pattern is driven by the warm West Greenland Current on the east and the cold Baffin Island Current on the west.

### Climate and Ice
The climate is Arctic, with severe winters and cool summers. The bay is ice-covered for most of the year; in winter, 80% is covered by continuous, floating, and fast ice. Ice is most abundant in March and least in August–September. Tides are semidiurnal and strong, with an average height of 4 m (13 ft), contributing to ice collision and crushing.

### The North Water Polynya
A large, stable area of open water called the North Water polynya forms annually in the bay's north near Smith Sound. Covering about 80,000 km² (31,000 sq mi) in summer, it is the largest polynya in the Canadian Arctic and has existed for at least 9,000 years. This area is the focal point for the bay's aquatic life.

### Wildlife
The North Water supports abundant fauna, including about 15,000 of the bay's estimated 20,000 beluga whales. Other key species are walrus, narwhal, seals, bowhead whales, and polar bears. Aquatic mammals depend on open water for breathing. The shores host about 400 plant species, which support caribou and rodents. Fish include resident polar cod and migrating Atlantic species. Hunting is traditionally important but is now restricted, with a polar bear quota of 105 per year.

### Human Activity and Resources
Inuit settlements like Pond Inlet and Clyde River exist on the Canadian coast. The Nanisivik Mine (1975-2002) was the first Canadian Arctic mine. The bay is seismically active, with significant earthquakes in 1933 and 2010. The US Geological Survey estimates significant undiscovered Arctic oil and gas, leading Greenland to offer offshore concessions in Baffin Bay and Davis Strait.

## Terms
- **Marginal sea:** A division of an ocean, partially enclosed by islands, archipelagos, or peninsulas.
- **Polynya:** An area of open water surrounded by sea ice, often recurring.
- **Fast ice:** Sea ice that is "fastened" to the coastline or to the sea floor over shallow shoals.
- **Cyclonic circulation:** A counterclockwise (in the Northern Hemisphere) rotation of ocean currents.
- **Semidiurnal tide:** A tide with two nearly equal high waters and two nearly equal low waters each day.
- **Thule:** The ancestral culture of the modern Inuit, which spread across the Arctic.
- **Northwest Passage:** A sea route connecting the northern Atlantic and Pacific Oceans through the Arctic Archipelago.

## Debates and open questions
- The extent of Norse contact with Baffin Bay is suggested by recent excavations but remains a subject of study.
- The potential for large-scale oil and gas extraction in the bay is a significant economic and environmental consideration, with concessions already offered by Greenland.

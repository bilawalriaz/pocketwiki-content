# Harsha

## Overview
Harshavardhana (590–647 CE) was the emperor of Kannauj who united much of northern India after the fall of the Gupta Empire. His reign marked a period of political consolidation, cultural flourishing, and significant religious patronage, making his court a center for scholars and travelers like the Chinese monk Xuanzang.

## Timeline
- **605 CE** — Death of his father, King Prabhakaravardhana of Thanesar.
- **606 CE** — Harsha ascends the throne at age 16 after his brother Rajyavardhana is murdered.
- **606–612 CE** — Harsha unites small republics and is crowned emperor (Maharajadhiraja) at an assembly.
- **618–619 CE** — Harsha is defeated by Chalukya emperor Pulakeshin II at the Narmada River.
- **647 CE** — Death of Harsha.
- **648–649 CE** — A Chinese diplomatic mission, led by Wang Xuance, is attacked; a retaliatory raid is launched from Tibet and Nepal.

## Body

**Early Life and Ascension**
Harsha was the younger son of Prabhakaravardhana, the first monarch of the Vardhana dynasty with its capital at Thanesar (Sthanvesvara). After his father's death in 605, his elder brother Rajyavardhana became king. Rajyavardhana was later treacherously murdered by Shashanka, the King of Gauda, after intervening in a conflict to rescue their sister Rajyashri. Harsha then ascended the throne at age 16, resolved to rescue his sister and avenge his brother.

**Imperial Expansion and Limits**
Following the collapse of the Gupta Empire, northern India was fragmented. Harsha united the small republics from Punjab to central India, and in April 606, was crowned emperor. He established an empire with the Narmada River as its southern boundary and made Kanyakubja (Kannauj) his capital. His expansion southward was halted when he was defeated by the Chalukya emperor Pulakeshin II. A treaty was then established, designating the Narmada as the border between their empires. While the Chinese traveler Xuanzang claimed Harsha brought "the Five Indias" under his rule in six years, this is considered hyperbole; Harsha was the most powerful northern Indian emperor but did not control the entire subcontinent.

**Religious Policy and Patronage**
Harsha's religious views were eclectic. His seals and inscriptions describe him as a Shaivite (devotee of Shiva), while his court poet Bana also portrays him as such. However, the Buddhist traveler Xuanzang described him as a devout Buddhist who banned animal slaughter, built monasteries and stupas, and organized grand religious assemblies. Historians debate whether Harsha converted to Buddhism later in life or if Xuanzang's account overemphasizes his Buddhist patronage. Harsha was known for his justice and generosity, and his court attracted scholars and artists from across Asia.

**Literary Contributions**
Harsha is widely credited as the author of three Sanskrit plays: *Ratnavali*, *Nagananda*, and *Priyadarsika*. While some sources suggest these were written by his court poet Dhavaka, scholars like Wendy Doniger argue Harsha wrote them himself.

## Terms
*   **Vardhana Dynasty:** The royal family of Thanesar to which Harsha belonged.
*   **Maharajadhiraja:** A title meaning "king of kings," signifying an emperor.
*   **Kanyakubja (Kannauj):** The imperial capital of Harsha's empire in present-day Uttar Pradesh.
*   **Thanesar (Sthanvesvara):** The original capital of Harsha's family in present-day Haryana.
*   **Shaivite:** A devotee of the Hindu god Shiva.
*   **Stupa:** A hemispherical structure used as a Buddhist shrine or monument.
*   **Moksha:** A term for liberation in Indian religions; Harsha held a great assembly by this name every five years.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Religious Identity:** Was Harsha primarily a Shaivite, as his inscriptions and court poet suggest, or a Buddhist, as described by Xuanzang? The debate centers on whether his Buddhist patronage was a state policy or reflected personal belief.
*   **Extent of Empire:** Historians question the accuracy of Xuanzang's claim that Harsha conquered "the Five Indias," viewing it as likely hyperbole for his status as the paramount power in northern India.
*   **Authorship of Plays:** While traditionally attributed to Harsha, some sources suggest his court poet Dhavaka wrote the Sanskrit plays attributed to him.

# Suzhou

## Overview
Suzhou is a prefecture-level city in Jiangsu province, Eastern China, renowned as the cradle of Wu culture and a major historical economic and cultural hub. It is internationally famous for its classical gardens, canals, and silk production, and is now a leading industrial and high-tech manufacturing center within the Yangtze Delta megalopolis.

- **2012** — Suzhou Metro Line 1 begins operation.

## Body

### History and Names
Suzhou is one of the oldest towns in the Yangtze Basin. During the Spring and Autumn period, the area was home to Baiyue tribes. The Zhou lord Taibo is traditionally credited with establishing the state of Wu nearby in the 11th century BC. The city's modern site was founded in 514 BC as Helü City. It has been known by various names, including Gusu, Wu, and Wujun. The name "Suzhou" was first officially used in AD 589; "Su" is a contraction of "Gusu." Strategically located on the Grand Canal, Suzhou flourished as a trade and cultural center. It was described by the Korean official Choe Bu in 1488 as exceeding every other city. The city suffered damage during the Taiping Rebellion (1860) and the Japanese invasion (1937), but its population and economy recovered.

### Geography and Climate
Suzhou is situated on the Lake Tai Plain in the Yangtze River Delta, bordered by Lake Tai to the southwest and the Yangtze River to the north. The terrain is predominantly low-lying and flat, crisscrossed by rivers and canals, earning it the nickname "Venice of the East." The city has a humid subtropical climate with four distinct seasons. The average annual temperature is 17.2 °C (63.0 °F).

### Cityscape and Landmarks
Suzhou's urban core, Gusu District, preserves the historic "Old Town" with its canals and gardens. To protect this heritage, building heights over 24 meters are prohibited in Gusu. Modern skyscrapers are concentrated in the Suzhou Industrial Park (SIP) and Suzhou New District (SND). The city is famous for its over 60 Classical Gardens, a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Key landmarks include the Humble Administrator's Garden, the Lingering Garden, the ancient Pan Gate, the leaning Yunyan Pagoda on Tiger Hill, and the historic Pingjiang Road and Shantang Street districts.

### Economy and Development
Suzhou's economy is driven by a large manufacturing sector, including IT, electronics, and textiles. It is a major destination for foreign investment. The Suzhou Industrial Park (SIP), established in 1994, is a flagship China-Singapore cooperative project. The Suzhou New District (SND), founded in 1992, is a national-level high-tech industrial zone. The Port of Suzhou is the world's busiest inland river port by cargo tonnage. The city's GDP exceeded RMB 1.3 trillion in 2013.

### Culture and Demographics
Suzhou is the origin of Kunqu opera and Suzhou pingtan (storytelling). It is historically renowned for silk production and Suzhou embroidery, one of China's "Four Famous Embroideries." The local dialect is Suzhounese, a Wu language. The population is predominantly Han Chinese, with a significant Korean community. The city is home to several universities, including Soochow University and Xi'an Jiaotong–Liverpool University.

## Terms
- **Prefecture-level city:** An administrative division in China, ranking above a county-level city and below a province.
- **Wu culture:** The culture of the historical Wu region, centered around Suzhou and the southern Yangtze River Delta.
- **Grand Canal:** A UNESCO World Heritage Site and the world's longest canal, connecting Beijing and Hangzhou.
- **Jiangnan:** The region south of the lower Yangtze River, known for its prosperity and distinct culture.
- **Suzhounese:** The local Wu dialect spoken in Suzhou.
- **Kunqu:** One of the oldest forms of Chinese opera, originating in the Suzhou region.
- **Suzhou Industrial Park (SIP):** A major economic zone established in 1994 as a cooperative project between China and Singapore.
- **Suzhou New District (SND):** A national-level high-tech industrial zone established in 1992.

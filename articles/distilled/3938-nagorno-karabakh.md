# Nagorno-Karabakh

## Overview
Nagorno-Karabakh is a mountainous region within Azerbaijan, historically populated by ethnic Armenians. Its status was a source of major conflict between Armenia and Azerbaijan, leading to wars in the 1990s and 2020, and culminating in Azerbaijan's 2023 offensive that dissolved the breakaway Armenian republic and resulted in the mass exodus of the Armenian population.

## Timeline
- **c. 180 BC** — Artsakh becomes a province of the Armenian Kingdom.
- **387 AD** — Region becomes part of the Sassanid satrapy of Caucasian Albania.
- **1805** — Karabakh becomes a protectorate of the Russian Empire via the Kurekchay Treaty.
- **1813** — Persia formally cedes Karabakh to Russia via the Treaty of Gulistan.
- **1921** — Soviet authorities assign Nagorno-Karabakh to the Azerbaijan SSR with autonomous status.
- **1988** — Karabakh Armenians petition for transfer to Armenia; protests begin.
- **1991** — Nagorno-Karabakh Republic (NKR) declared after a referendum; First Nagorno-Karabakh War begins.
- **1994** — Ceasefire ends the First War; NKR controls Nagorno-Karabakh and surrounding Azerbaijani districts.
- **2020** — Second Nagorno-Karabakh War; Azerbaijan regains surrounding districts and parts of Nagorno-Karabakh.
- **2023** — Azerbaijan launches offensive; Republic of Artsakh dissolves; mass Armenian exodus occurs.

## Body

### Etymology and Geography
The name "Nagorno-Karabakh" derives from Russian and Azerbaijani words for "mountainous" or "upper" Karabakh. Armenians historically called the region Artsakh. It is a mountainous area within the Lesser Caucasus, with a total area of about 4,400 km². It does not border Armenia directly but was connected via the Lachin corridor.

### Ancient and Medieval History
The region was part of the Armenian Kingdom from c. 180 BC. After the Arab conquest in the 7th century, Armenian culture flourished, with the first Armenian school opened at Amaras Monastery in the 5th century. From the 9th century, the Armenian House of Khachen ruled as a principality. In the 15th-18th centuries, control shifted between Turkic confederations and Persian dynasties, but Armenian meliks (princes) maintained significant local rule until the mid-18th century, when the Karabakh Khanate was formed.

### Imperial and Soviet Rule
The region became a Russian protectorate in 1805 and was formally annexed by 1813. An 1823 survey indicated Armenians were 97% of the population in the five districts of Nagorno-Karabakh. After the Russian Revolution, control was disputed between Armenia and Azerbaijan. In 1921, Soviet authorities assigned the region to the Azerbaijan SSR as the Nagorno-Karabakh Autonomous Oblast (NKAO), ensuring an Armenian majority. Under Soviet rule, Armenian demographics declined due to emigration and discriminatory policies, while Azerbaijani settlement increased.

### Conflict and Dissolution
In 1988, Karabakh Armenians petitioned for unification with Armenia, sparking protests and violence. After the Soviet collapse, the First Nagorno-Karabakh War (1992-1994) ended with a ceasefire, leaving the NKR in control of the region and surrounding Azerbaijani territories. The Second Nagorno-Karabakh War (2020) saw Azerbaijan regain those territories and parts of Nagorno-Karabakh. Following a 2022-2023 blockade, Azerbaijan launched a September 2023 offensive. The NKR collapsed, agreed to dissolve by January 2024, and almost its entire Armenian population fled, ending a millennia-old presence.

## Terms
- **Artsakh**: The historical Armenian name for the region, used by its Armenian inhabitants.
- **Melik**: A title for Armenian princes who ruled the five principalities in Karabakh from the 15th to 18th centuries.
- **NKAO**: Nagorno-Karabakh Autonomous Oblast, the Soviet-era autonomous region within the Azerbaijan SSR.
- **OSCE Minsk Group**: The international body mediating peace talks between Armenia and Azerbaijan over Nagorno-Karabakh's status.
- **Lachin corridor**: The mountain pass connecting Nagorno-Karabakh to Armenia, a key strategic and humanitarian route.
- **Republic of Artsakh**: The breakaway, internationally unrecognized state governed by ethnic Armenians from 1994 to 2023.
- **Kurekchay Treaty (1805)**: Agreement making Karabakh a protectorate of the Russian Empire.
- **Treaty of Gulistan (1813)**: Treaty formally ceding Karabakh from Persia to the Russian Empire.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Historical Demographics**: Scholars debate the exact historical population ratios, with some sources indicating an overwhelming Armenian majority in the mountainous area by the early 19th century, while others note periods of demographic fluctuation.
- **Soviet Border Decision**: The rationale behind the 1921 Soviet decision to place the Armenian-majority NKAO within the Azerbaijan SSR, rather than Armenia, remains a point of historical contention.
- **Ethnic Cleansing Accusations**: Following the 2023 offensive, political analysts and residents accused Azerbaijan of committing ethnic cleansing, a charge Azerbaijan denies, framing the exodus as voluntary.

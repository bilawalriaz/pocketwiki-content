# Diego Maradona

## Overview
Diego Armando Maradona (1960–2020) was an Argentine footballer widely regarded as one of the greatest players in history. His extraordinary talent, particularly his dribbling and playmaking, made him a global icon, but his career was also marked by controversy and personal struggles. He is most famous for leading Argentina to victory in the 1986 FIFA World Cup, a performance that cemented his legendary status.

## Timeline
- **30 Oct 1960** — Born in Lanús, Buenos Aires Province, Argentina.
- **20 Oct 1976** — Professional debut for Argentinos Juniors at age 15.
- **1979** — Wins FIFA World Youth Championship with Argentina; scores first senior international goal.
- **1982** — Transfers to Barcelona for a then-world record £5 million fee.
- **1984** — Transfers to Napoli for a new world record £6.9 million fee.
- **1986** — Captains Argentina to FIFA World Cup victory; scores "Hand of God" and "Goal of the Century."
- **1987** — Leads Napoli to their first-ever Serie A title.
- **1990** — Leads Argentina to the 1990 World Cup final; Napoli wins a second Serie A title.
- **1991** — Receives 15-month ban from football for a failed cocaine test.
- **1994** — Expelled from the 1994 World Cup for a failed ephedrine test.
- **2000** — Jointly wins FIFA Player of the Century award with Pelé.
- **25 Nov 2020** — Dies of cardiac arrest at age 60 in Buenos Aires Province.

## Body

### Early Life and Debut
Maradona was raised in the shantytown of Villa Fiorito. He received his first football at age three and was recruited to the youth team of Argentinos Juniors at age eight. He made his professional debut for the club on 20 October 1976, becoming the youngest player in the history of the Argentine Primera División. He scored 115 goals in 167 appearances for Argentinos Juniors before transferring to Boca Juniors in 1981, where he won the league title.

### Club Career: Barcelona and Napoli
In 1982, Maradona transferred to Barcelona for a world record fee. He won the Copa del Rey and Copa de la Liga but his time was marked by a severe ankle injury and a notorious brawl in the 1984 Copa del Rey final. He then moved to Napoli for another world record fee in 1984. In Naples, he became a revered figure, leading the club to its first Serie A title in 1986–87 and a second in 1989–90. He also won the Coppa Italia, Supercoppa Italiana, and the UEFA Cup. His success was later overshadowed by a cocaine addiction and a 15-month ban from football.

### International Career
Maradona debuted for Argentina at age 16. After being omitted from the 1978 World Cup squad, he starred in the 1979 FIFA World Youth Championship. His defining moment came at the 1986 World Cup, where he captained Argentina to victory, scoring five goals and providing five assists. His two goals in the quarter-final against England—the controversial "Hand of God" and the "Goal of the Century"—are iconic. He also captained Argentina to the 1990 World Cup final and was expelled from the 1994 tournament for a failed drug test.

### Player Profile and Legacy
Maradona was a classic number 10 playmaker, renowned for his exceptional dribbling, vision, and free-kick ability. His low centre of gravity and balance allowed him to withstand physical challenges. He was a charismatic leader whose personality profoundly influenced his teams. He is considered one of the greatest players of all time, winning the FIFA Player of the Century award jointly with Pelé. His life and career have become a significant part of Argentine cultural identity.

### Managerial Career and Death
After retiring, Maradona had a modest managerial career, coaching clubs in Argentina, the UAE, Mexico, and Belarus, and managing the Argentina national team from 2008 to 2010. He died on 25 November 2020 from a cardiac arrest. An autopsy found no drugs or alcohol in his system but noted significant heart and organ damage. Seven medical professionals were later charged with homicide in connection with his care following hospital discharge.

## Terms
- **Number 10**: The jersey number traditionally worn by a team's primary playmaker or attacking midfielder.
- **Playmaker**: A player who controls the flow of a team's attacking play, often through passing and vision.
- **Dribbling**: The skill of moving the ball forward while maintaining close control, often evading opponents.
- **Free kick specialist**: A player highly skilled at scoring or creating chances from direct free kicks.
- **Serie A**: The top professional football league in Italy.
- **FIFA World Cup**: The premier international football tournament, held every four years.
- **Golden Ball**: The award given to the best player at a FIFA World Cup tournament.
- **Hand of God**: The name given to Maradona's controversial first goal against England in the 1986 World Cup quarter-final, scored by hand.
- **Goal of the Century**: The name given to Maradona's second goal against England in the 1986 World Cup, voted the greatest goal in World Cup history.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Greatest of All Time (GOAT)**: Maradona is perpetually part of the debate over the greatest footballer, often compared to Pelé and Lionel Messi.
- **The "Hand of God" Goal**: The legitimacy and sportsmanship of his most famous goal remain a point of discussion, symbolizing his "cunning" style.
- **Impact of Personal Life**: Scholars and fans debate how his off-field struggles with addiction and discipline affected his career longevity and peak performance.
- **Cultural Icon vs. Controversial Figure**: His status as a divine figure in Argentina contrasts with his global reputation as a flawed genius, raising questions about the nature of sporting heroism.

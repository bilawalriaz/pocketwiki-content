# Manga

## Overview
Manga are comics or graphic novels originating from Japan, characterized by a distinct artistic style developed in the late 19th century. They are a major part of Japanese publishing and have achieved massive global popularity, significantly influencing comics markets worldwide and serving as a key component of Japan's cultural exports.

## Timeline
- **12th century** — Origins traced to emakimono (scrolls) like *Chōjū-jinbutsu-giga*.
- **1798** — The term "manga" first comes into common usage with works like Santō Kyōden's *Shiji no yukikai*.
- **1874** — The first manga magazine, *Eshinbun Nipponchi*, is published.
- **1950s-1960s** — Post-war explosion of creativity; key figures like Osamu Tezuka (*Astro Boy*) and Machiko Hasegawa (*Sazae-san*) innovate styles.
- **1969** — The Year 24 Group of female artists debuts, transforming shōjo manga.
- **1980s-1990s** — Manga begins significant international distribution, especially in the U.S. and Europe.
- **1995** — The Japanese manga market peaks at ¥586.4 billion.
- **2000s** — Digital distribution and web manga platforms begin to rise.
- **2020** — The Japanese market expands to ¥612.6 billion, driven by digital sales during the COVID-19 pandemic.
- **2021** — Manga accounts for 76% of U.S. comics and graphic novel sales.
- **2024** — The Japanese domestic manga market reaches a record high of ¥704.3 billion, with digital sales at ¥512.2 billion.

## Body

### Definition and Characteristics
Manga are Japanese comics or graphic novels. In Japan, the term refers to all forms of cartooning and animation, but internationally it specifically denotes Japanese comics. They are typically printed in black-and-white due to cost and artistic reasons. Stories are often serialized in large magazines before being collected into tankōbon (collected volumes). A manga artist (mangaka) usually works with assistants and an editor from a publishing company.

### History and Genres
Manga's history spans from ancient scrolls to modern digital formats. Post-WWII, artists like Osamu Tezuka popularized a "cinematographic" style. The medium is broadly categorized by target demographic: shōnen (boys), shōjo (girls), seinen (young men), and josei (young women). Genres range from action and romance to horror and erotica. A notable subgenre is gekiga, which focuses on gritty, adult-oriented themes.

### Industry and Market
Since the 1950s, manga has been a major Japanese industry. The market experienced a peak in 1995, a period of stability, and then rapid growth from 2020 onward, largely due to digital sales. As of 2024, digital manga (¥512.2 billion) significantly outsells print manga (¥200 billion). The top global comics publishers are Japanese companies like Shueisha and Kodansha.

### International Influence
Manga has gained a massive worldwide readership. In the U.S., it made up 76% of comics sales in 2021. France is the largest manga importer, with manga representing 55% of comics sold in 2021. The medium has influenced local comics traditions, leading to terms like "manhwa" (Korean), "manhua" (Chinese), and "OEL manga" (Original English Language). Manga's global growth is attributed to digital availability, bookstore chains, and anime streaming.

### Digital Evolution
The internet has enabled new distribution models. Web manga, often published on platforms like Pixiv or Twitter, allows amateurs to gain professional followings (e.g., *One-Punch Man*). Major publishers now run digital-only magazines and apps. While traditional print magazines are declining, digital sales have driven overall market growth, surpassing print sales in Japan by 2020.

## Terms
- **Mangaka**: A Japanese comic artist or writer.
- **Tankōbon**: A collected volume of chapters from a serialized manga, similar to a graphic novel.
- **Shōnen**: Manga marketed towards young boys, often featuring action and adventure.
- **Shōjo**: Manga marketed towards young girls, often focusing on romance and interpersonal relationships.
- **Seinen**: Manga marketed towards young adult men, often with more complex or mature themes.
- **Josei**: Manga marketed towards young adult women, often depicting realistic romance and daily life.
- **Gekiga**: A style of manga storytelling that is thematically somber, adult-oriented, and drawn in a gritty, realistic fashion.
- **Dōjinshi**: Fan-made or self-published works, often parodies or continuations of popular series.
- **Web manga**: Manga published primarily on the internet, often preceding or bypassing traditional print serialization.

## Debates and Open Questions
Scholars debate the origins of modern manga. One view stresses continuity with traditional Japanese art and aesthetics. The other emphasizes post-WWII U.S. cultural influences, such as American comics and Disney cartoons. The impact of digital distribution on the traditional print-based manga industry and creative process remains an ongoing area of transformation and discussion.

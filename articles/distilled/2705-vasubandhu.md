# Vasubandhu

## Overview
Vasubandhu (fl. 4th–5th century CE) was an Indian Buddhist monk and philosopher whose work shaped both Abhidharma and Mahayana thought. Initially a scholar of the Sarvastivada school, he later co-founded the Yogacara school with his half-brother Asanga. His writings, especially on "consciousness-only" (vijñapti-mātra), are foundational in Tibetan and East Asian Buddhism, influencing metaphysics, logic, and devotional practice.

## Timeline
- **c. 4th century CE** — Vasubandhu is born, likely in Gandhara or Central India.
- **Early career** — Studies Sarvastivada Abhidharma in Gandhara and Kashmir.
- **Mid-career** — Composes the *Abhidharmakośakārikā* (Verses on the Treasury of the Abhidharma).
- **Later period** — Converts to Mahayana Buddhism, possibly under influence of his brother Asanga.
- **Later period** — Writes key Yogacara treatises like the *Twenty Verses* and *Thirty Verses on Consciousness Only*.
- **Later period** — Defeats Samkhya philosophers in debate before Gupta king Vikramaditya.
- **Late life** — Builds monasteries and hospitals using debate winnings.
- **c. 100 years old** — Dies while visiting Ayodhya.

## Body

### Early Life and Abhidharma Work
Vasubandhu was born in the 4th century CE, with sources differing on whether his birthplace was Gandhara or Central India. He first studied the Sarvastivada (or Vaibhasika) school, which held that all dharmas (phenomenal events) exist in past, present, and future. He later studied with the Sautrantika school, which argued only the present moment exists. His major early work, the *Abhidharmakośakārikā*, is a verse summary of Sarvastivada Abhidharma. Its auto-commentary critiques some Sarvastivada views from a Sautrantika perspective while defending others. This text remains the primary source for non-Mahayana Abhidharma in Tibet and East Asia.

### Conversion to Mahayana and Yogacara Philosophy
After converting to Mahayana, Vasubandhu developed the Yogacara school's core doctrine of "consciousness-only" (vijñapti-mātra). This theory posits that all experience is a projection of consciousness, and external objects do not exist independently. His *Twenty Verses* and *Thirty Verses* use arguments like dreams and mass hallucinations to support this view. He also outlined the theory of eight consciousnesses, including the "storehouse consciousness" (ālayavijñāna) that stores karmic seeds and explains continuity across rebirths.

### Critique of the Self and Momentariness
Vasubandhu defended the Buddhist doctrine of no-self (anatman) against both Hindu views of an eternal soul (atman) and the Buddhist Personalist school's concept of a person distinct from the five aggregates (skandhas). He argued the self is a conceptual fiction with no causal efficacy. He also championed the Sautrantika doctrine of momentariness (kṣaṇavāda), where dharmas arise and perish in a single instant, explaining karma through the storehouse consciousness.

### Logic and Later Influence
Vasubandhu contributed to Buddhist logic with works like *Vādavidhi* (Rules for Debate), which helped formalize Indian logical traditions. His later treatises, such as the *Discourse on the Pure Land*, influenced East Asian Pure Land Buddhism. He is revered as a patriarch in several Buddhist traditions and is counted among the Seventeen Nalanda Masters.

## Terms
- **Abhidharma**: Buddhist philosophical analysis of experience into its ultimate constituents (dharmas).
- **Dharma**: In this context, a fundamental, momentary constituent of experience or reality.
- **Sarvastivada**: A Buddhist school holding that all dharmas exist in past, present, and future.
- **Sautrantika**: A Buddhist school emphasizing the authority of sutras and the doctrine of momentariness.
- **Yogacara**: A Mahayana school focusing on meditation and the doctrine of consciousness-only.
- **Vijñapti-mātra**: "Consciousness-only" or "appearance-only," the Yogacara doctrine that reality is a mental projection.
- **Ālayavijñāna**: "Storehouse consciousness," a foundational consciousness that stores karmic seeds.
- **Anatman**: The Buddhist doctrine of "no-self," denying a permanent, independent soul.
- **Kṣaṇavāda**: The theory of momentariness, that all phenomena exist only for a single instant.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Two Vasubandhus Theory**: A now largely discredited 20th-century theory proposed by Erich Frauwallner that two different authors named Vasubandhu wrote the Abhidharma and Yogacara texts. Scholarly consensus now favors a single author whose thought evolved.
- **Interpretation of Consciousness-Only**: Scholars debate whether Vasubandhu's philosophy is a form of phenomenology, epistemological idealism, or transcendental idealism similar to Kant's.
- **Attribution of Works**: The authorship of some texts, like the *Trisvabhāvanirdeśa* (Exposition on the Three Natures), is questioned by some scholars.

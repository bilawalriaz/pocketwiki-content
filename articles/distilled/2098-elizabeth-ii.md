# Elizabeth II

## Overview
Elizabeth II was Queen of the United Kingdom and other Commonwealth realms from 1952 until her death in 2022. Her 70-year reign was the longest of any British monarch and any queen regnant in history, spanning a period of immense political change, decolonization, and social transformation. She served as a constitutional monarch and a symbol of continuity, navigating the evolution of the British Empire into the Commonwealth of Nations.

## Timeline
- **21 April 1926** — Born in London, first child of the Duke and Duchess of York.
- **1936** — Father becomes King George VI; she becomes heir presumptive.
- **1947** — Marries Philip Mountbatten; makes famous 21st birthday pledge of service.
- **6 February 1952** — Becomes Queen upon the death of her father, George VI.
- **2 June 1953** — Coronation at Westminster Abbey, first to be televised.
- **1977** — Silver Jubilee celebrations.
- **1992** — Describes the year as her "annus horribilis" due to family crises and the Windsor Castle fire.
- **31 August 1997** — Death of Diana, Princess of Wales; Queen's public response follows.
- **9 September 2015** — Becomes the longest-reigning British monarch.
- **9 April 2021** — Death of her husband, Prince Philip.
- **6 February 2022** — Platinum Jubilee begins, marking 70 years on the throne.
- **8 September 2022** — Dies at Balmoral Castle, aged 96.

## Body

### Early Life and Accession
Born in 1926, Elizabeth was not initially expected to become queen. Her father, the Duke of York, unexpectedly became King George VI in 1936 after the abdication of his brother, Edward VIII. Educated privately, she began public duties during World War II, joining the Auxiliary Territorial Service. In 1947, she married Prince Philip of Greece and Denmark, who renounced his titles and became a British subject. They had four children: Charles, Anne, Andrew, and Edward. Elizabeth became queen in 1952 at age 25 while on a tour in Kenya.

### Reign and Constitutional Role
As a constitutional monarch, Elizabeth reigned through significant political upheaval, including the Troubles in Northern Ireland, UK devolution, and the decolonization of Africa. She was advised by over 170 prime ministers across her realms. Her role was largely ceremonial and symbolic, but she performed key constitutional duties such as appointing prime ministers and granting Royal Assent to legislation. She maintained the monarchy's popularity despite occasional republican sentiment and intense media scrutiny of her family, particularly during the 1990s.

### Public Image and Duties
Elizabeth was the most widely traveled head of state in history, undertaking hundreds of state visits and Commonwealth tours. She was patron of over 600 charities and her public image evolved from a glamorous "fairytale Queen" to a steadfast symbol of duty and tradition. Her popularity dipped during family crises in the 1990s, notably after the death of Diana, Princess of Wales, but generally remained high. She marked major milestones with Silver, Golden, Diamond, and Platinum Jubilees.

### Later Years and Death
In her later years, Elizabeth's public engagements reduced due to mobility issues, though she continued her core duties. She celebrated her Platinum Jubilee in 2022, the first British monarch to do so. Her husband, Prince Philip, died in 2021 after 73 years of marriage. Elizabeth died peacefully at Balmoral Castle on 8 September 2022, aged 96. Her state funeral was held at Westminster Abbey, and she was interred in the King George VI Memorial Chapel at Windsor Castle.

## Terms
*   **Constitutional Monarch:** A sovereign who reigns but does not rule, acting on the advice of elected ministers and performing ceremonial duties.
*   **Heir Presumptive:** The person first in line to the throne who could be displaced by the birth of a closer relative (e.g., a son to her father).
*   **Commonwealth of Nations:** A voluntary association of 56 independent countries, most of which are former territories of the British Empire.
*   **Regnant:** A reigning monarch who holds the title in their own right, as opposed to a consort.
*   **Annus Horribilis:** Latin for "horrible year," used by Elizabeth to describe 1992.
*   **Operation London Bridge:** The codename for the plan for the announcement of the Queen's death and the period of national mourning.
*   **Civil List:** The annual grant from Parliament to fund the monarch's official duties (replaced by the Sovereign Grant in 2012).

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Political Influence:** The extent of Elizabeth's private political influence and opinions remains a subject of speculation, as she maintained strict public neutrality. Reports of her views on issues like apartheid South Africa or Scottish independence are based on second-hand accounts.
*   **Monarchy's Future:** Her death sparked renewed debate about the future of the monarchy in the Commonwealth realms, with some nations considering becoming republics.
*   **Media and Privacy:** The tension between the royal family's right to privacy and intense public and media interest, which peaked during the 1990s and after Diana's death, remains an ongoing issue for the institution.

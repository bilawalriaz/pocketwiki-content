# Burkina Faso

## Overview
Burkina Faso is a landlocked West African nation with a complex history of pre-colonial kingdoms, French colonization, and post-independence political instability. It is one of the world's least developed countries, facing significant challenges from a jihadist insurgency, food insecurity, and recurrent coups, most recently in 2022. Its identity and politics are shaped by its diverse ethnic groups, predominantly the Mossi, and its recent pivot away from traditional Western alliances.

## Timeline
- **c. 11th-13th centuries** — Mossi people establish kingdoms like Ouagadougou and Yatenga.
- **1896** — France colonizes the territory as part of French West Africa.
- **1 March 1919** — French Upper Volta colony is established.
- **5 August 1960** — Upper Volta gains full independence from France.
- **4 August 1983** — Thomas Sankara comes to power in a coup.
- **4 August 1984** — Country is renamed Burkina Faso.
- **15 October 1987** — Sankara is assassinated; Blaise Compaoré takes power.
- **31 October 2014** — Compaoré is ousted by popular protests after 27 years.
- **24 January 2022** — Military coup deposes President Roch Marc Christian Kaboré.
- **30 September 2022** — Captain Ibrahim Traoré seizes power in a second coup.
- **18 January 2024** — Burkina Faso announces exit from ECOWAS and is suspended from the African Union.

## Body

### History and Politics
The region's history includes ancient Iron Age cultures and the medieval Mossi kingdoms. French colonization began in 1896, leading to the creation of the colony of Upper Volta in 1919. After independence in 1960, the country experienced chronic political instability, marked by numerous coups. A pivotal period began with Thomas Sankara's 1983 coup, during which the country was renamed Burkina Faso in 1984. Sankara implemented radical social and economic reforms but was assassinated in 1987 by his colleague Blaise Compaoré, who then ruled for 27 years. Compaoré was ousted in 2014, leading to a brief democratic transition. Since 2022, military juntas have taken power, citing failure to contain a spreading jihadist insurgency. The current junta under Ibrahim Traoré has suspended the constitution and aligned with the Alliance of Sahel States (AES), distancing itself from France and regional bodies like ECOWAS.

### Economy and Resources
Burkina Faso's economy is based primarily on subsistence agriculture and livestock, with cotton as a main cash crop. It is a significant gold producer, with gold accounting for roughly 70% of export earnings. However, the country remains one of the least developed in the world, with high poverty rates. The economy faces severe challenges from climate shocks, poor infrastructure, and the ongoing security crisis, which has disrupted mining and agriculture. Foreign investment has declined sharply since the 2022 coups.

### Society and Culture
The population is ethnically diverse, with the Mossi being the largest group. Over 60 indigenous languages are spoken, with Mooré being the most widespread. French was demoted from an official language to a "working language" in 2024. Islam is the predominant religion (63.8%), followed by Christianity (26.3%). The country has a rich tradition of oral literature, music, and crafts. It hosts major cultural events like the FESPACO film festival and the SIAO craft fair. Education and health indicators are low, with high rates of food insecurity and malnutrition, particularly in rural areas.

## Terms
- **Jihadist insurgency:** An ongoing armed conflict in the Sahel region, involving groups like Jama'at Nasr al-Islam wal Muslimin (JNIM) and Ansarul Islam, which has destabilized Burkina Faso since 2015.
- **Coup d'état:** The sudden, illegal seizure of power from a government, which has occurred frequently in Burkina Faso's post-independence history.
- **ECOWAS:** The Economic Community of West African States, a regional political and economic union. Burkina Faso announced its exit in 2024.
- **Alliance of Sahel States (AES):** A mutual defense pact formed in 2023 by the military juntas of Burkina Faso, Mali, and Niger.
- **Mossi:** The largest ethnic group in Burkina Faso, historically organized into kingdoms and speaking the Mooré language.
- **Mooré:** The most widely spoken language in Burkina Faso, used by about half the population.
- **FESPACO:** The Panafrican Film and Television Festival of Ouagadougou, a major biennial film festival.
- **Sahel:** The semi-arid region south of the Sahara Desert that includes northern Burkina Faso, characterized by drought and food insecurity.

## Debates and open questions
- The role of foreign powers, particularly France, in Burkina Faso's internal politics is a subject of debate, with allegations of involvement in past coups.
- The effectiveness and human rights record of the military junta's campaign against jihadist insurgents are under scrutiny, with reports of mass graves and civilian massacres.
- The long-term viability of the Alliance of Sahel States (AES) and the country's new geopolitical alignment away from traditional partners is uncertain.
- The future of democratic governance and constitutional rule in Burkina Faso remains unclear following the suspension of the constitution.

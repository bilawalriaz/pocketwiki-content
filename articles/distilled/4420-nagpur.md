# Nagpur

## Overview
Nagpur is a major city in central India, serving as the second capital and third-largest city of Maharashtra. Known as the "Orange City" for its citrus trade and the "Tiger Capital of India" for its proximity to tiger reserves, it is a significant commercial, political, and cultural hub. The city is historically important as the birthplace of the Rashtriya Swayamsevak Sangh (RSS) and the site of B. R. Ambedkar's mass conversion to Buddhism, which launched the Dalit Buddhist movement.

## Timeline
- **c. 8th century BCE** — Human existence around Nagpur is evidenced by megalithic burial sites.
- **1702** — Gond King Bakht Buland Shah founded the city of Nagpur.
- **1803** — The British East India Company defeated the Maratha Bhonsale dynasty in the Second Anglo-Maratha War, leading to British influence.
- **1853** — The British took direct control of Nagpur after the death of the last Maratha ruler.
- **1867** — The first railway line in Nagpur opened, connecting it to the Bombay-Bhusaval line.
- **1920** — The non-cooperation movement was launched at the Nagpur session of the Indian National Congress.
- **1925** — K. B. Hedgewar founded the Rashtriya Swayamsevak Sangh (RSS) in Nagpur.
- **1956** — Following Indian state reorganization, Nagpur became part of the new state of Maharashtra.
- **14 October 1956** — B. R. Ambedkar and his followers converted to Buddhism at Deekshabhoomi, Nagpur.
- **8 March 2019** — Nagpur Metro rail operations began.

## Body

### History and Etymology
Nagpur's history spans millennia, with early megalithic cultures and later rule by dynasties like the Vakatakas and Rashtrakutas. The city's name derives from the Nag River ("Nag" meaning cobra, "pur" meaning city). It was formally founded in 1702 by Gond King Bakht Buland Shah. The Maratha Bhonsale dynasty later ruled the city until the British East India Company took control in the 19th century, making Nagpur the capital of the Central Provinces and Berar. After Indian independence and state reorganization, it became the second capital of Maharashtra via the Nagpur Pact.

### Geography and Climate
Located at the geographical center of the Indian subcontinent, Nagpur lies on the Deccan Plateau with a mean altitude of 310.5 meters. The city features natural and artificial lakes, with the Nag River forming its drainage. It has a tropical wet and dry climate, characterized by extremely hot summers (temperatures can exceed 47°C) and mild winters. The region experiences significant heat waves, thunderstorms, and dust storms during the pre-monsoon season.

### Demographics and Administration
As of the 2011 census, Nagpur's municipal population was 2,405,665. Hinduism is the majority religion (69.46%), followed by Buddhism (15.57%). Marathi is the most-spoken language (60.63%). The city is administered by the Nagpur Municipal Corporation (NMC) and serves as the seat for the winter session of the Maharashtra state legislature. It also hosts a bench of the Bombay High Court.

### Economy
Nagpur is the largest economic center in central India, with an estimated nominal GDP of ₹202,438 crore (US$21 billion) in 2023-24. Its economy is driven by agriculture (notably oranges), mining, power generation, and a growing IT and defense manufacturing sector. The Multi-modal International Hub Airport at Nagpur (MIHAN) is a major project integrating an airport, SEZ, and industrial hub. The city is a key logistics and railway junction.

### Culture and Tourism
Nagpur's culture reflects its diverse population. It is famous for its distinctive Varhadi or Saoji cuisine, known for its spicy non-vegetarian dishes. Key cultural events include the Kalidas Festival and the Orange City Literature Festival. Major tourist attractions include Deekshabhoomi (the world's largest hollow stupa), the Dragon Palace Temple, and several tiger reserves like Pench and Tadoba, which are accessible from the city.

### Transport
Nagpur is a major transportation hub. It is served by Dr. Babasaheb Ambedkar International Airport and is a junction for two major national highways (NH44 and NH53). The city has a long railway history and is a divisional headquarters for Indian Railways. The Nagpur Metro, operational since 2019, provides rapid transit. Road connectivity is expanding with expressways like the Mumbai–Nagpur Expressway.

## Terms
- **Dalit Buddhist movement**: A socio-political movement initiated by B. R. Ambedkar in 1956, encouraging Dalits to convert to Buddhism to escape the caste-based discrimination of Hinduism.
- **Gond kingdom**: A historical kingdom ruled by the Gond people, which controlled the Nagpur region before the Maratha conquest.
- **Maratha Empire**: A major Indian power that dominated much of the Indian subcontinent in the 17th and 18th centuries; the Bhonsale dynasty ruled Nagpur on its behalf.
- **Smart Cities Mission**: A Government of India initiative to develop 100 cities across the country, making them citizen-friendly and sustainable.
- **Vidarbha**: The eastern region of Maharashtra, of which Nagpur is the major city and commercial center.
- **Deekshabhoomi**: A sacred Buddhist site in Nagpur where B. R. Ambedkar and his followers converted to Buddhism in 1956.
- **MIHAN (Multi-modal International Hub Airport at Nagpur)**: An integrated project including an airport, special economic zone (SEZ), and township.
- **Nagpur Pact**: An informal agreement between political leaders that ensured Nagpur would host the winter session of the Maharashtra state legislature after it lost its capital status in 1956.

## Debates and open questions
- The precise founding date of Nagpur is cited as 1702 "according to different sources," indicating some historical debate.
- The city's economic importance relative to Mumbai and Pune is noted to have "gradually declined" after the merging of Vidarbha into Maharashtra due to a period of state government neglect, though it has since recovered.

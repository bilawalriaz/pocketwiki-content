# Brazilian Highlands

## Overview
The Brazilian Highlands (Planalto Brasileiro) are an extensive geographical region covering most of eastern, southern, and central Brazil, accounting for roughly half of the country's land area. This region is critically important as it is home to the vast majority of Brazil's population. Formed by ancient basaltic lava flows and shaped by erosion, the Highlands are now geologically stable but are recognized for their immense diversity of biomes, climates, soils, and species.

## Timeline
- **Ancient past** — Dramatic geophysical activity, including basaltic lava flows, forms much of the region.
- **Post-formation** — Erosion shapes the Highlands, creating sedimentary deposits and wearing down mountains.
- **Pre-20th century** — The Atlantic Rainforest and cerrado vegetation extensively cover the plateaus.
- **2022** — Census records Brazil's population as 203,062,512, the majority living in or near the Highlands.

## Body

### Major Divisions and Geography
The Brazilian Highlands are divided into three main areas due to their size and diversity. The **Atlantic Plateau** extends along the eastern coast and includes several mountain ranges; it was once almost completely covered by the Atlantic Rainforest, of which only 7.3% remains. The **Southern Plateau** advances inland in the south and south-central portions, featuring sedimentary rocks partially covered by basaltic lava spills that form fertile "purple land." This area was also largely covered by Atlantic Rainforest, with araucaria highland forest and cerrado grasslands now occupying much of the space. The **Central Plateau** occupies the central portions with sedimentary and crystalline formations; approximately 85% was once covered by cerrado vegetation, of which only a small portion remains intact.

### Mountain Ranges and Highest Point
Several adjoining or enclosed mountain ranges are considered part of the Brazilian Highlands. Key ranges, from north to south, include the Serra da Borborema, Chapada Diamantina, Serra do Espinhaço, Serra do Caparaó, Serra da Mantiqueira, Serra do Mar, and Serra Geral. The highest point in the Brazilian Highlands is the Pico da Bandeira in the Serra do Caparaó, which reaches 2,891 meters (9,485 ft).

## Terms
- **Brazilian Highlands (Planalto Brasileiro):** An extensive geographical region covering most of eastern, southern, and central Brazil.
- **Basaltic lava flows:** Ancient volcanic activity that formed much of the region's rock.
- **Sedimentary deposits:** Layers of material formed by the erosion and deposition of rock particles.
- **Atlantic Rainforest:** A highly biodiverse forest biome that once covered large parts of the Atlantic Plateau.
- **Cerrado:** A vast tropical savanna ecoregion of Brazil, characterized by grasslands and scattered trees.
- **Araucaria highland forest:** A forest biome dominated by araucaria pine trees, found in southern Brazil.
- **Crystalline formations:** Rock structures formed from the cooling and solidification of magma or the alteration of existing rock.

# Manaus

## Overview
Manaus is the capital and largest city of the Brazilian state of Amazonas, situated at the confluence of the Negro and Amazon rivers in the heart of the Amazon rainforest. It is a major industrial and scientific hub, historically defined by its rubber boom wealth and later revitalized by a federal Free Economic Zone. Its unique location makes it a critical center for studying Amazonian biodiversity and sustainability.

## Timeline
- **1669** — Founded as the Fort of São José do Rio Negro by the Portuguese.
- **1832** — Elevated to town status and named "Manaus."
- **1835** — The Cabanagem revolt, involving rebels from the Upper Amazon, is crucial to the formation of the state of Amazonas.
- **1848** — Legally transformed into a city named "Cidade da Barra do Rio Negro."
- **1856** — Officially renamed "Manaus."
- **Late 1800s** — Rubber boom makes Manaus one of the richest cities in South America.
- **1896** — The Amazonas Opera House (Teatro Amazonas) is inaugurated.
- **1967** — The Manaus Free Trade Zone (ZFM) is established, sparking major economic and demographic growth.
- **2014** — Hosts four matches of the FIFA World Cup.
- **2017** — A major prison riot begins in Manaus and spreads to other cities.
- **2020-2021** — Suffers severe COVID-19 outbreaks, with an estimated 76% infection rate in the first wave.

## Body

### History and Foundation
European colonization began in 1499, but the Portuguese built the Fort of São José da Barra do Rio Negro in 1668–1669 to secure the region. The settlement grew around the fort, and missionaries built a chapel in 1695. The city's name evolved from "Lugar da Barra" to "Manaus" in 1832, briefly changed to "Cidade da Barra do Rio Negro" in 1848, and reverted to "Manaus" in 1856. The Cabanagem revolt (1835), a multi-ethnic uprising against the white elite, was instrumental in the political integration that formed the state of Amazonas.

### The Rubber Boom and Bust
In the late 19th century, Manaus became the center of the Amazon rubber boom, earning immense wealth and the nickname "Paris of the Tropics." This era funded extravagant European-style architecture, including the Teatro Amazonas opera house. The boom ended abruptly when rubber seeds were smuggled to Southeast Asia, breaking Brazil's monopoly. The city fell into poverty, and its early electrical grid became too expensive to run.

### Modern Economy and the Free Zone
In the 1960s, the Brazilian government established the Manaus Free Trade Zone (ZFM) to develop the Amazon region. This federal project, formalized in 1967, offered major tax incentives that attracted national and international investment, particularly in electronics and manufacturing. The ZFM transformed Manaus into a major industrial center and one of Brazil's largest economies, driving rapid population growth.

### Geography and Climate
Manaus is located in the central Amazon rainforest, an area of unparalleled biodiversity. The city has a tropical monsoon climate (Am), with high humidity, average annual rainfall of about 2,300 mm, and constant heat due to its proximity to the equator. A notable hydrological feature is the "Meeting of Waters," where the dark Negro River and the muddy Solimões River flow side-by-side for about 6 km without mixing due to differences in current speed, temperature, and density.

### Demographics and Culture
The city is the seventh-largest in Brazil, with a population over 2 million. Genetic studies indicate ancestry is 45.9% European, 37.8% Native American, and 16.3% African. While historically Catholic, the city has a growing Protestant population. Cultural life is marked by annual festivals, including the Amazonas Opera Festival and the Folklore Festival. The Teatro Amazonas, reopened in 1997 after decades of disuse, is a central cultural landmark.

## Terms
*   **Free Economic Zone (ZFM):** A federal project in Manaus offering tax exemptions to attract industry and develop the Amazon region.
*   **Cabanagem:** A 1835 revolt of blacks, Native Americans, and mestizos against the white political elite, crucial to the formation of Amazonas state.
*   **Meeting of Waters:** The natural phenomenon where the Negro and Solimões rivers flow together without mixing for several kilometers.
*   **Tropical Monsoon Climate (Am):** A climate classification with a short dry season but sufficient annual rainfall to support rainforest.
*   **Pied Tamarin:** An endangered primate species whose habitat is found in the Manaus region.
*   **Teatro Amazonas:** The historic opera house built during the rubber boom, symbolizing the city's past wealth.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   The precise scientific reasons for the "Meeting of Waters" phenomenon are not definitively settled, though differences in current speed, volume, and density are the primary factors.
*   The environmental and social impacts of the Free Trade Zone's industrial model on the surrounding Amazon rainforest remain a subject of ongoing analysis and debate.
*   The city's infrastructure, particularly road connections to the rest of Brazil, is highly limited, making it largely dependent on river and air transport.

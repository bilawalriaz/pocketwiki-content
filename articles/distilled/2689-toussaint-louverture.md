# Toussaint Louverture

## Overview
François-Dominique Toussaint Louverture (1743–1803) was a former slave who became the preeminent leader of the Haitian Revolution, transforming a slave rebellion into a successful war for independence. His military and political leadership was instrumental in establishing Haiti as the first independent black republic in the Americas and the second independent nation in the Western Hemisphere. He is celebrated as one of the "Fathers of Haiti."

## Timeline
- **20 May 1743** — Born into slavery on the Bréda plantation in Saint-Domingue.
- **c. 1772–1776** — Manumitted (freed) and became an *affranchi* (ex-slave).
- **14 August 1791** — Attended secret meeting planning the slave revolt; nominated Georges Biassou as leader.
- **29 August 1791** — Major slave rebellion begins in northern Saint-Domingue.
- **29 August 1793** — Made his famous declaration at Camp Turel, calling for liberty and equality.
- **4 February 1794** — French Republic officially abolishes slavery in its colonies.
- **4 May 1794** — Officially switched allegiance from Spain to Republican France.
- **1796** — Defeated rival Jean-Louis Villatte; promoted to commander of the West Province.
- **30 April 1798** — Signed a treaty with Britain for the withdrawal of British troops.
- **1799–1800** — Defeated rival André Rigaud in the War of the South, unifying French Saint-Domingue.
- **January 1801** — Invaded and took control of Spanish Santo Domingo, uniting the entire island.
- **7 July 1801** — Promulgated a constitution naming himself Governor-General for Life.
- **7 June 1802** — Arrested by French forces under false pretenses and deported to France.
- **7 April 1803** — Died in prison at Fort de Joux, France.
- **1 January 1804** — Haiti declares independence, completing the revolution Louverture began.

## Body

### Early Life and Manumission
Toussaint Louverture was born into slavery, the eldest son of Hyppolite and Pauline, both enslaved Africans from the Allada and Aja ethnic groups. He was raised on the Bréda plantation, where he learned to handle livestock and gained a reputation for discipline. He was a devout Catholic and received some education from Jesuit missionaries. Louverture was manumitted between 1772 and 1776, becoming an *affranchi* (freed slave). As a free man, he became a salaried employee, an overseer, and eventually a slave owner himself, purchasing several coffee plantations. He married twice: first to Cécile, with whom he had three children, and later to Suzanne Simone-Baptiste.

### Role in the Haitian Revolution
The Haitian Revolution began in 1791 as a slave revolt against French colonial rule. Louverture, then nearly 50, initially served as a lieutenant to the rebel leader Georges Biassou and allied with the Spanish of neighboring Santo Domingo. He gained a reputation for military skill and discipline. In 1794, following the French Republic's abolition of slavery, Louverture switched his allegiance to France. He systematically defeated his rivals, including other black leaders and the mulatto general André Rigaud, and by 1801, he controlled the entire island of Hispaniola.

### Governance and Constitution
As the dominant leader, Louverture focused on restoring the colony's economy by reinstating the plantation system with paid labor. He negotiated trade treaties with Britain and the United States. In 1801, he promulgated a constitution that made him Governor-General for Life and permanently abolished slavery, but it also affirmed Saint-Domingue's status as a French colony. The constitution established Catholicism as the state religion and mandated forced labor, reflecting Louverture's belief that economic viability was essential for freedom.

### Downfall and Legacy
Louverture's assertion of autonomy, particularly his 1801 constitution, alarmed Napoleon Bonaparte, who sent a military expedition in 1801 to reassert French control. After a period of conflict, Louverture was tricked into surrendering in June 1802 and was deported to France. He died in prison in 1803. His lieutenant, Jean-Jacques Dessalines, continued the fight, leading Haitian forces to victory. Haiti declared independence in 1804. Louverture's actions laid the foundation for this victory, and he is remembered as a foundational figure of Haitian national identity.

## Terms
- **Affranchi**: A freed slave in the French colonial system.
- **Code Noir**: A decree passed by Louis XIV in 1685 defining the conditions of slavery in the French colonial empire.
- **Gens de couleur libres**: Free people of color in the French colonies, including *affranchis*, free blacks, and mixed-race individuals.
- **Grands blancs**: The wealthy, white planter class in Saint-Domingue.
- **Petits blancs**: The poorer, white commoner class in Saint-Domingue.
- **Governor-General-for-life**: The supreme executive position Louverture created for himself in the 1801 constitution.
- **Manumission**: The act of a slave owner freeing their slaves.
- **Vodou**: A religion practiced by enslaved Africans in Saint-Domingue, blending African spiritual traditions with elements of Catholicism.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Motivation for switching sides**: Historians debate whether Louverture's 1794 switch from Spain to France was primarily driven by sincere abolitionist ideals, personal ambition, or resentment over his treatment by the Spanish.
- **Role in the Gonaïves attack**: Louverture's involvement in the 1794 attack on the Spanish garrison at Gonaïves, which preceded his official switch to the French side, is suspected but not definitively proven.
- **Attitude toward slavery**: While he fought for abolition, Louverture's later policies of forced labor and his ownership of slaves before the revolution lead to questions about the consistency of his views on freedom and labor.
- **Cause of death**: The exact cause of his death in prison is uncertain, with suggested causes including exhaustion, malnutrition, pneumonia, and tuberculosis.

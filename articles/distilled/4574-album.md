# Album

## Overview
An album is a collection of audio recordings, typically music, issued as a single unit on a physical or digital format. It was the dominant format for recorded music consumption from the mid-1960s to the early 21st century, a period known as the "album era." The concept evolved from bound collections of 78 rpm records to the modern digital album, shaping how music is created, packaged, and experienced.

## Timeline
- **Early 1900s** — 78 rpm records introduced; each side holds ~3 minutes of sound.
- **c. 1910** — Bound "record albums" sold to store 78 rpm discs.
- **1930s** — Record companies issue curated collections of 78s in albums with artwork.
- **1948** — Columbia Records introduces the 33⅓ rpm vinyl LP (long play) record.
- **1948** — RCA introduces the 45 rpm format, disrupting the singles market.
- **1964** — The 8-track tape format is created.
- **1963** — Philips introduces the Compact Cassette prototype.
- **1980s** — Cassette popularity peaks with the Sony Walkman; CD format emerges.
- **1990s** — Cassette sales decline sharply as CDs become dominant.
- **2000s** — CDs become the primary physical distribution format for albums.
- **2010s** — Digital downloads and streaming services (e.g., Spotify) rise, changing album consumption.

## Body

### Definition and Structure
An album is a collection of tracks (individual songs or recordings) typically containing 40 to 80 minutes of music. The term originated from bound books of blank pages used to collect items. In music, it first described collections of 78 rpm records and was later applied to the vinyl LP, cassette, CD, and digital formats. An album usually has more tracks than an extended play (EP) or single.

### Recording and Production
Most albums are **studio albums**, recorded in a controlled studio environment to minimize noise and allow for precise mixing of different takes and parts. **Live albums** are recorded in one continuous take without overdubbing, often at a concert venue with an audience. Modern technology allows for multitrack recording, where artists can record parts separately and combine them later.

### Physical and Digital Formats
The album format has evolved through several media:
*   **Vinyl LPs:** The standard from 1948, holding ~22 minutes per side.
*   **8-track Tapes:** Popular from the mid-1960s to late 1970s, now obsolete.
*   **Compact Cassettes:** Peaked in the 1980s, declined in the 1990s, and saw a minor revival in the 2010s.
*   **Compact Discs (CDs):** Became the dominant physical format in the 1990s and 2000s, holding up to 80 minutes.
*   **Digital Albums:** Files (e.g., MP3, FLAC) downloaded or streamed via platforms like Spotify, freeing albums from physical length constraints.

### Types of Albums
Beyond standard studio and live albums, common types include:
*   **Solo Album:** Released under a single artist's name, often a member of a group.
*   **Compilation Album:** A collection of tracks from various sources, such as "greatest hits."
*   **Tribute/Cover Album:** An album of cover versions of another artist's songs.
*   **Posthumous Album:** Released after an artist's death, often containing unreleased material.

## Terms
*   **Album Era:** The period from the mid-1960s to early 21st century when the album was the dominant music format.
*   **LP (Long Play):** A vinyl record format introduced in 1948, playing at 33⅓ rpm and holding about 22 minutes per side.
*   **Track:** An individual song or recording on an album.
*   **Title Track:** A track that shares its name with the album.
*   **Bonus Track:** An extra piece of music included on an album, often for promotional reasons.
*   **Studio Album:** An album recorded in a controlled studio environment.
*   **Live Album:** An album recorded in a single, continuous take, often at a concert.
*   **Compilation Album:** A collection of previously released tracks, often thematically grouped.
*   **Multitrack Recording:** A technique allowing different parts of a song to be recorded separately and mixed together.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   The early 21st century has been described by some observers as experiencing the "death of the album" due to the shift toward single-track digital consumption and streaming.
*   There are mixed opinions on the merits of posthumous albums, with debates centering on artistic intent versus the desire to hear an artist's unpublished work.
*   The formal definition of an "album" varies by chart and award organization (e.g., minimum track count or playing time).

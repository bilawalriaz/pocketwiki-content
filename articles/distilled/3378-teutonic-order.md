# Teutonic Order

## Overview
The Teutonic Order was a Catholic religious and military order founded c. 1190 in Acre, Kingdom of Jerusalem. Originally established to aid Christian pilgrims and run hospitals, it evolved into a powerful crusading military force that conquered and ruled a monastic state in Prussia and Livonia during the Northern Crusades. After losing its territorial power, it transformed into a purely religious and charitable order, which it remains today.

## Timeline
- **c. 1190** — Founded as a hospital brotherhood in Acre during the Third Crusade.
- **1198** — Transformed into a military order based on the Knights Templar model.
- **1211** — Invited to Transylvania (Burzenland) by Hungary to defend against Cumans.
- **1226** — Invited to Prussia by Duke Konrad I of Masovia to subdue the Old Prussians.
- **1237** — Absorbed the defeated Livonian Brothers of the Sword.
- **1309** — Moved its headquarters from Venice to Marienburg (Malbork) in Prussia.
- **1346** — Purchased the Duchy of Estonia from Denmark.
- **1410** — Decisively defeated by Poland-Lithuania at the Battle of Grunwald.
- **1466** — Lost western Prussia to Poland in the Second Peace of Thorn.
- **1525** — Grand Master Albert secularized the Order's Prussian lands, becoming a Polish vassal.
- **1809** — Napoleon dissolved the Order's remaining German territories.
- **1929** — Re-established as a purely Catholic religious order under a new Rule.

## Body

### Foundation and Early Crusades
The Order originated from a hospital founded by German merchants in Acre c. 1190. It was recognized by the Pope in 1192 and transformed into a military order in 1198. Under Grand Master Hermann von Salza (1209–1239), it shifted from a hospice brotherhood to a primarily military order. It first gained territory in 1211 when invited to Transylvania by Hungary, but was expelled in 1225 after its growing power alarmed the king.

### The Prussian State
In 1230, the Order arrived in Prussia at the invitation of Duke Konrad I of Masovia. With privileges from the Holy Roman Emperor and the Pope, it conducted a brutal 50-year conquest of the pagan Old Prussians, who were largely killed, deported, or assimilated. The Order established a sovereign monastic state, encouraging German immigration and building castles and towns like Königsberg. It ruled Prussia as a sovereign entity under papal and imperial authority.

### Expansion and Conflicts
The Order expanded by absorbing the Livonian Brothers of the Sword in 1237, creating the Livonian Order. It fought for centuries against Lithuania, Poland, and the Novgorod Republic. A major turning point was the 1410 Battle of Grunwald, where a Polish-Lithuanian army shattered the Order's military power, though it retained most of its territory. Internal strife and wars with Poland followed, leading to the Thirteen Years' War (1454–1466). The defeated Order lost western Prussia to Poland in the Second Peace of Thorn, becoming a Polish fief.

### Secularization and Modern Era
In 1525, Grand Master Albert of Brandenburg converted to Lutheranism, secularized the Order's Prussian lands, and became a Polish vassal. The Order lost its Livonian territories later in the 16th century. It continued as a charitable and ceremonial body in the Holy Roman Empire until Napoleon dissolved its German territories in 1809. Outlawed by Nazi Germany in 1938, it was re-established in 1945. Today, it is a Catholic religious order focused on charitable work. A Protestant branch, the Bailiwick of Utrecht, also continues.

## Terms
- **Teutonic Knights**: Common name for members of the Teutonic Order.
- **Grand Master (Hochmeister)**: The highest officer of the Order, elected by the general chapter.
- **Landmeister**: The highest officer of a national chapter (e.g., Prussia, Livonia, Germany).
- **Commandery (Kommende)**: The smallest administrative unit of the Order, ruled by a commander (Komtur).
- **Bailiwick (Ballei)**: A regional administrative division combining several commanderies.
- **Old Prussians**: The pagan Baltic people conquered and largely assimilated by the Order.
- **Northern Crusades**: Crusades conducted by the Order and others against pagan peoples in the Baltic region.
- **State of the Teutonic Order**: The sovereign monastic state the Order established in Prussia and Livonia.
- **Livonian Order**: The branch of the Teutonic Order formed from the absorbed Livonian Brothers of the Sword.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The exact scale of the 1308 massacre of Gdańsk's inhabitants by the Order is unknown and debated by historians, with estimates ranging from 60 to 10,000.
- The degree to which the Order's conflicts with Lithuania and Poland were driven by genuine crusading zeal versus political and economic ambition is a subject of historical analysis.
- The Order's historical legacy is contested, having been appropriated by German nationalists and Nazis, while also being portrayed as a hostile forerunner of German aggression in Polish nationalism.

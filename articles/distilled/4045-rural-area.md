# Rural area

## Overview
Rural areas are geographic regions outside towns and cities, characterized by low population density and small settlements. They are defined by their economic reliance on land-based industries like agriculture and forestry, but face significant challenges including demographic decline, poverty, and limited services. The definition of "rural" varies significantly by country for statistical and administrative purposes.

## Body

### Definition and Characteristics
A rural area is a geographic area located outside towns and cities, typically with a low population density and small settlements. Agricultural and forestry areas are commonly described as rural. The core economic and social dynamics of rural areas stem from their relationship with land-based industries such as agriculture, forestry, and resource extraction. These economies can be vulnerable to boom-bust cycles and extreme weather events like droughts.

### Demographic and Economic Challenges
Larger economic forces encouraging urbanization have led to significant demographic declines known as **rural flight**. Economic incentives encourage younger populations to move to cities for education and jobs, leaving behind older, less educated, and less wealthy populations. This cycle contributes to poorer services like healthcare, education, and infrastructure. According to the Food and Agricultural Organization, three quarters of the global impoverished live in rural areas.

### Regional Definitions
Definitions of "rural" vary internationally. In Canada, the OECD defines a "predominantly rural region" as having over 50% of its population in communities with a density below 150 people/km². Statistics Canada uses a population count outside settlements of 1,000 or fewer inhabitants. In the United Kingdom, DEFRA uses census data, defining rural areas as local government areas with over 26% of their population in rural settlements or market towns. In India, definitions vary by agency; the National Sample Survey Organization defines rural areas by population density (up to 400/km²), clear village boundaries, and at least 75% of the male working population in agriculture.

### Development and Policy
Some communities have successfully encouraged economic development through policies like increased access to electricity or internet. Historically, development policies focused on large extractive industries. Recent approaches emphasize sustainable development and economic diversification. In France, the government launched the "Agria Rural" plan in 2019 to redynamize rural areas, focusing on initiatives like energy transition. Germany has a policy of equal living conditions, viewing rural areas as equivalent to urban areas, and uses "village renewal" as a development approach.

## Terms
- **Rural flight**: The demographic trend of people moving from rural areas to urban centers, driven by economic incentives.
- **Predominantly rural region**: A statistical classification (e.g., in Canada) for regions where over 50% of the population lives in rural communities.
- **Market town**: A settlement with historical permission to hold a street market, used in UK rural definitions.
- **Panchayat**: A local governing council in Indian villages.
- **Sustainable development**: A recent policy approach focusing on economic diversification and environmental balance in rural communities.
- **Village renewal**: A German approach to developing and supporting countryside communities.
- **Peri-urban areas**: Rural areas located near cities, often considered suburban.

## Debates and Open Questions
The source highlights ongoing scholarly and administrative debates about how to define "rural," noting that "different countries have varying definitions... for statistical and administrative purposes." There is no single global standard, with definitions based on population density, settlement size, economic activity, or administrative boundaries.

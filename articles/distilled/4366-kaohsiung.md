# Kaohsiung

## Overview
Kaohsiung is a major port city and special municipality in southern Taiwan. It is the island's largest city by area and third most populous, serving as the economic center of the south and home to Taiwan's largest and busiest harbor, the Port of Kaohsiung. Its history as a trading village has evolved into a modern industrial and cultural hub.

## Timeline
- **17th century** — Founded as a small trading village named Tancoia (or Takau).
- **1624** — Dutch East India Company establishes control; area is part of Dutch Formosa.
- **1684** — Qing dynasty annexes Taiwan; town is renamed Fongshan County.
- **1895** — Taiwan ceded to Japan; Takau is developed as a port and industrial center.
- **1945** — Republic of China takes control; Kaohsiung City and County are established.
- **1979** — Kaohsiung City is upgraded to a special municipality.
- **10 December 1979** — The Kaohsiung Incident, a pro-democracy protest, is suppressed.
- **25 December 2010** — Kaohsiung City merges with Kaohsiung County to form a larger special municipality.
- **31 July 2014** — A series of gas explosions in the city center kills 31 people.

## Body

### Etymology and Early History
The area was originally inhabited by the Makatao people of the Siraya tribe. Hoklo immigrants in the 16th-17th centuries called the region "Takau," possibly meaning "bamboo forest" in the Siraya language. The Dutch named it Tancoia. After the Dutch were expelled in 1662, the village was renamed but reverted to Takau by the late 1670s. Under Japanese rule in 1920, it was renamed Takao (高雄), which was adapted to the Mandarin pronunciation "Kaohsiung" after 1945.

### Japanese Rule and Modern Development
Japan ceded Taiwan in 1895 and systematically modernized Takao, developing its harbor and connecting it to the North-South Railway. By the 1930s, Japan's Southward Policy positioned Kaohsiung as an industrial center. The city was heavily bombed during World War II. After ROC control began in 1945, the damaged port was restored, and Kaohsiung overtook Keelung as Taiwan's major port. It became a special municipality in 1979.

### Economy and Infrastructure
Kaohsiung's economy is anchored by the Port of Kaohsiung, which handles over 67% of Taiwan's container volume. Key industries include manufacturing, steel-making, oil refining, shipbuilding, and freight transport. The city is classified as a "Sufficiency" level global city. Major infrastructure includes Kaohsiung International Airport (Taiwan's second busiest), the Kaohsiung MRT, and the High Speed Rail connection at Zuoying. Recent public works like the Pier-2 Art Center aim to boost tourism and culture.

### Geography and Climate
Located on Taiwan's southwestern coast, the city spans from the urban harbor to the Yushan Range, making it Taiwan's largest by area (2,952 km²). It has a tropical savanna climate with warm temperatures year-round (monthly means 20–29°C) and is one of Taiwan's sunniest areas. The average annual rainfall is about 1,885 mm.

### Demographics and Culture
As of 2025, Kaohsiung has a population of approximately 2.72 million. The majority are Han Chinese (Hoklo, Hakka, Waishengren), with indigenous groups (Bunun, Rukai, etc.) in mountain districts. The city is a major cultural center with numerous museums, temples, and parks. Notable sites include the Fo Guang Shan Monastery, the Dome of Light art installation, and the National Kaohsiung Center for the Arts. Traditional "wet" markets coexist with modern retail.

### Politics
Since the late 1990s, Kaohsiung has leaned politically toward the Pan-Green Coalition, contrasting with the Pan-Blue leaning north. The 1979 Kaohsiung Incident was a pivotal pro-democracy event. The city is divided into 38 districts, with Lingya and Fongshan as administrative centers.

## Terms
- **Special municipality**: A top-level administrative division in Taiwan, equivalent to a province.
- **Port of Kaohsiung**: Taiwan's largest container port and a major global shipping hub.
- **Pan-Green Coalition**: A political coalition in Taiwan generally favoring Taiwanese identity and independence.
- **Pan-Blue Coalition**: A political coalition in Taiwan generally favoring closer ties with China.
- **Kaohsiung Incident**: A 1979 pro-democracy protest and subsequent crackdown that became a catalyst for Taiwan's democratization.
- **Makatao**: An indigenous Austronesian people who historically inhabited the Kaohsiung area.
- **Hoklo**: A Han Chinese subgroup whose ancestors migrated from Fujian province.
- **Waishengren**: Han Chinese who migrated to Taiwan from mainland China after 1945.
- **TEU (Twenty-foot Equivalent Unit)**: A standard unit of cargo capacity for container ships and terminals.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The origin of the name "Takau" is debated, with theories linking it to the Siraya language for "bamboo forest" or to the name of the Makatao tribe.
- The city suffers from Taiwan's economic North–South divide, which remains a center of political debate.
- There is a push for Kaohsiung City to annex the Port of Kaohsiung (currently under national administration) to facilitate better regional planning.

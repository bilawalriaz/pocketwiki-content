# Coimbatore

## Overview
Coimbatore is a major industrial and commercial city in Tamil Nadu, India, known as the "Manchester of South India" for its extensive textile industry. Its strategic location near the Palakkad Gap made it a historic trade hub, and post-independence industrialization transformed it into a leading center for manufacturing, engineering, and IT, ranking among India's fastest-growing cities.

## Timeline
- **c. 1st–4th centuries CE** — Region ruled by Cheras during the Sangam period; serves as eastern entrance to the Palakkad Gap trade route.
- **10th century** — Medieval Cholas conquer the Kongu Nadu region.
- **1550s** — Madurai Nayaks take control and introduce the Palayakkarar system, dividing Kongu Nadu into 24 Palayams.
- **1799** — British East India Company annexes Coimbatore to the Madras Presidency after the Anglo-Mysore Wars.
- **1801** — Coimbatore region is the area of operations for Dheeran Chinnamalai during the Second Poligar War.
- **1804** — Coimbatore established as the capital of the newly formed Coimbatore district.
- **1866** — Coimbatore accorded municipality status; Robert Stanes becomes first chairman.
- **1920s–1930s** — Textile boom due to the decline of Mumbai's cotton industry; city emerges as an industrial center.
- **1981** — Coimbatore constituted as a municipal corporation.
- **1998** — Bombings by Al Ummah kill 58 people and injure over 200.
- **2012** — Coimbatore Airport granted international status.
- **2023** — National Crime Records Bureau report ranks Coimbatore among the top three safest cities in India.

## Body

### History and Administration
The region has a long history of rule by dynasties like the Cheras, Cholas, Vijayanagara Empire, and Nayaks, before British annexation in 1799. It became a district capital in 1804 and a municipality in 1866. The city played a role in the Indian independence movement and saw rapid post-independence growth. It is administered by the Coimbatore Municipal Corporation (established 1981) and is the headquarters of Coimbatore district. The city is a traditional stronghold of Dravidian parties.

### Economy and Industry
Coimbatore is a major manufacturing, engineering, and textile hub. Its textile industry, fed by surrounding cotton fields, earned it the nickname "Manchester of South India." The city is a leading producer of auto components, wet grinders (with "Coimbatore Wet Grinder" as a Geographical Indication), pumps (supplying ~50% of India's requirements), and jewellery. It is also a significant IT and BPO center. The economy is driven by over 25,000 small, medium, and large industries.

### Geography and Climate
The city lies at 427 m above sea level on the Noyyal River, bordered by the Western Ghats. It has a hot semi-arid climate (Köppen BSh) with a wet season from September to November due to the northeast monsoon. The average annual rainfall is around 600 mm. The city is in a biodiversity hotspot, with urban wetlands hosting numerous bird species and the surrounding region home to threatened fauna like elephants and tigers.

### Demographics and Culture
As per the 2011 census, the city had a population of 1,050,721 within municipal limits, with a high literacy rate of 82.43%. Tamil is the predominant language, with Kongu Tamil as the local dialect. The population is predominantly Hindu, with significant Muslim and Christian minorities. The city has a reputation for entrepreneurship and is considered cosmopolitan. Major cultural events include the Mariamman festivals and the annual music festival.

### Transport and Infrastructure
Coimbatore is well-connected by air (Coimbatore International Airport), rail (Coimbatore Junction is a major station), and road (six arterial roads and five National Highways). Public transport includes town buses and proposed BRTS. The city has a robust healthcare sector with around 750 hospitals and is a major educational hub with numerous universities, engineering colleges, and research institutes.

## Terms
- **Palakkad Gap**: A principal mountain pass in the Western Ghats connecting Kerala to Tamil Nadu, historically a key trade route.
- **Palayakkarar system**: A feudal administrative system introduced by the Nayaks, dividing the Kongu Nadu region into 24 Palayams (towns).
- **Geographical Indication (GI)**: A sign used on products that have a specific geographical origin and possess qualities or a reputation due to that origin (e.g., Coimbatore Wet Grinder, Kovai Cora Cotton).
- **Kongu Nadu**: A historical region in western Tamil Nadu, of which Coimbatore is a part.
- **Sangam period**: The era in Tamil history (c. 3rd century BCE to 3rd century CE) associated with the Sangam literature and the rule of the Chera, Chola, and Pandya dynasties.
- **Municipal Corporation**: A local government body for large urban areas in India, with greater administrative and financial powers than a municipality.
- **Biodiversity hotspot**: A biogeographic region with a significant reservoir of biodiversity that is under threat from human habitation.

## Debates and open questions
- The etymology of "Coimbatore" is debated, with theories linking it to chieftain Kovan/Koyan or goddess Kovaiamma.
- The city faces ongoing environmental challenges, including air pollution, inadequate waste management, and degradation of water bodies like the Noyyal River and urban tanks.
- Proposals for a metro rail system have been discussed and shelved multiple times, with feasibility studies completed in 2021 for five corridors.

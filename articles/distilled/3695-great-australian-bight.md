# Great Australian Bight

## Overview
The Great Australian Bight is a large oceanic bight (open gulf) off the southern coast of mainland Australia. It is a region of significant ecological importance, characterized by dramatic cliffs and highly biodiverse waters that serve as critical habitat for endangered marine mammals. Its formation is tied to the breakup of the supercontinent Gondwana, and it remains a focal point for conservation debates regarding potential resource extraction.

## Timeline
- **c. 50 million years ago** — The Great Australian Bight forms as Gondwana breaks apart, separating Antarctica from Australia.
- **1627** — Dutch navigator François Thijssen first encounters the bight's western margins.
- **1802** — English navigator Matthew Flinders accurately charts the coastline during his circumnavigation of Australia.
- **Late 1960s** — Exploration for oil and gas in the bight begins.
- **2016** — BP withdraws its exploration plans; the Australian Senate inquires into oil/gas production.
- **2017** — Chevron withdraws from the exploration project.
- **2019** — Equinor's exploration plans are approved, but it withdraws in 2020 citing profitability.

## Body

### Extent and Geography
Two definitions for the bight's boundaries exist. The International Hydrographic Organization (IHO) defines a larger area, while the Australian Hydrographic Service (AHS) uses a smaller, 1,160 km stretch from Cape Pasley to Cape Carnot. The bight is generally considered part of the Indian Ocean, though the AHS classifies it as part of the Southern Ocean. Its coastline features ancient rock formations, including the Baxter and Bunda Cliffs, and lies south of the arid Nullarbor Plain.

### Natural History and Ecology
The bight's waters are highly biodiverse, particularly in zooplankton, due to seasonal upwelling events that bring nutrients to the surface along the Eyre Peninsula. This creates a fertile marine hotspot supporting planktivorous fish like sardines and anchovy, which in turn attract juvenile southern bluefin tuna. The region is critical habitat for the endangered southern right whale, which migrates there to calve and breed, and the near-threatened Australian sea lion. The macroalgal community is one of the world's most diverse, though overall species patterns are still poorly understood.

### Human Activity and Conservation
The bight has been exploited for fishing, notably for southern bluefin tuna. Since the 1960s, there have been repeated attempts to explore for oil and gas. These proposals, from companies like BP, Statoil/Equinor, and Chevron, have faced significant community and political opposition due to the risk of catastrophic environmental damage. All major exploration projects have been withdrawn, citing economic or strategic reasons, but the potential for future proposals remains a concern for the region's fragile ecosystems.

## Terms
- **Bight**: A large, open bay or gulf.
- **Oligotrophic**: A body of water that is low in nutrients and biological productivity.
- **Upwelling**: An oceanographic process where cold, nutrient-rich water from the deep ocean rises toward the surface.
- **Aeolianite**: A sedimentary rock formed from the cementation of sand deposited by wind.
- **Karst**: A landscape formed from the dissolution of soluble rocks like limestone, characterized by sinkholes and caves.
- **Zooplankton**: Small, drifting animals that are a crucial component of marine food webs.
- **Endemic**: Native and restricted to a certain place or region.
- **Gondwana**: An ancient supercontinent that included present-day Africa, South America, Antarctica, Australia, and the Indian subcontinent.

## Debates and Open questions
The primary debate concerns the balance between economic development and environmental protection. While exploration for oil and gas has been repeatedly proposed and withdrawn, the potential for future projects persists. Significant scientific uncertainty remains regarding the full scope of biodiversity in the bight and the precise impacts that resource extraction or other human activities would have on its complex ecosystems.

# Ghana

## Overview
Ghana is a West African nation with a rich history of pre-colonial kingdoms, European trade, and colonial rule. It became the first sub-Saharan African country to gain independence in 1957, serving as a model for decolonization and Pan-Africanism. Today, it is a multi-ethnic, multi-religious democracy with a mixed economy, recognized for its political stability and role in regional affairs.

## Timeline
- **11th century** — Bonoman kingdom exists in the south; Kingdom of Dagbon in the north.
- **15th century** — Portuguese begin European trade, establishing the Portuguese Gold Coast.
- **1482** — Elmina Castle is completed by the Portuguese.
- **1874** — Britain establishes control, creating the British Gold Coast.
- **1900** — The War of the Golden Stool marks the final defeat of the Ashanti Empire by the British.
- **1947** — The United Gold Coast Convention calls for self-government.
- **1949** — Kwame Nkrumah forms the Convention People's Party (CPP).
- **6 March 1957** — Ghana achieves independence, unifying four British colonial territories.
- **1 July 1960** — Ghana becomes a republic; Kwame Nkrumah is elected president.
- **24 February 1966** — Nkrumah's government is overthrown in a military coup ("Operation Cold Chop").
- **1992** — A new constitution is promulgated, restoring multi-party democracy (Fourth Republic).
- **2024** — John Mahama is elected president again.

## Body

### Etymology and Pre-colonial Kingdoms
The name "Ghana" derives from the title "Kaya Maghan" (ruler of gold) of the rulers of the medieval Wagadu Empire, a term used by Arab traders. The name was adopted in the 20th century as a symbol of pre-colonial prestige and national unity. The earliest kingdoms in the area were Bonoman (11th century) and Dagbon. From the 17th century, Akan states like Adansi, Asante, and Denkyira emerged, with the Asante Empire becoming dominant in the south by the 19th century. The Ga-Dangme and Ewe peoples also established themselves in their respective regions.

### European Contact and Colonialism
European trade began with the Portuguese in the 15th century, focused on gold. They were followed by the Dutch, Swedes, Danes, and Germans, who built over 30 forts and castles along the coast, later becoming central to the Atlantic slave trade. Britain eventually established control, creating the British Gold Coast. A century of conflict, including the Anglo-Ashanti wars, culminated in British victory in 1900. The modern borders were formed by unifying the Gold Coast, Ashanti, the Northern Territories, and British Togoland.

### Independence and Post-Independence Politics
The independence movement, led by Kwame Nkrumah and the CPP, achieved sovereignty on 6 March 1957. Nkrumah declared a republic in 1960 and promoted Pan-Africanism but established an authoritarian, one-party state. His government was overthrown in 1966, leading to a period of alternating military and civilian rule. Stability returned with the Fourth Republic in 1993. Ghana is now a unitary presidential democracy dominated by two parties (NDC and NPP), noted for peaceful elections and transfers of power.

### Economy and Resources
Ghana is a middle-income country with a mixed economy. Its economy is driven by services (50% of GDP), manufacturing, and extractive industries. It is the world's second-largest cocoa producer and a major gold producer, having surpassed South Africa in 2019. Significant hydrocarbon reserves were discovered in 2007 (Jubilee Oil Field). The country has a growing industrial and digital technology sector. Challenges include poverty, particularly in rural areas, and past issues with electricity shortages ("dumsor").

### Demographics and Society
Ghana has a population of over 35 million, with a young median age of 21. The largest ethnic group is the Akan (47.3%). English is the official language, with 11 government-sponsored languages, including Akan, Ewe, and Ga. Christianity (71.3%) is the largest religion, followed by Islam (16.8%). The country has a universal health care system (NHIS) and a free basic education system. Human rights concerns include the criminalization of homosexuality and the historical existence of witch camps.

### Culture
Ghanaian culture is expressed through cuisine (e.g., banku, fufu, jollof rice), clothing, and arts. Traditional textiles include Kente cloth (hand-woven, symbolic strips) and Adinkra cloth (printed with symbolic motifs). Music genres include highlife and hiplife. The country's architecture ranges from traditional round huts in the north to colonial-era forts and castles, which are UNESCO World Heritage Sites. Media freedom is constitutionally guaranteed and is considered among the most unfettered in Africa.

## Terms
*   **Akan:** The largest ethnic group in Ghana, comprising several subgroups like the Asante and Fante.
*   **Asante Empire:** A powerful pre-colonial Akan kingdom centered in Kumasi, known for its gold trade and centralized bureaucracy.
*   **Pan-Africanism:** A worldwide movement that aims to encourage and strengthen bonds of solidarity between all indigenous and diaspora people of African descent.
*   **Fourth Republic:** The current republican government of Ghana, established by the 1992 constitution after a period of military rule.
*   **Dumsor:** A Ghanaian term for persistent, irregular, and unpredictable electric power outages.
*   **Kente:** A type of silk and cotton fabric made of interwoven cloth strips, native to the Akan ethnic group, with symbolic colors and patterns.
*   **Adinkra:** Visual symbols, originally created by the Akan people of Ghana, that represent concepts, aphorisms, and aspects of life.
*   **Highlife:** A music genre that originated in Ghana in the 19th and 20th centuries, blending African rhythms with Western instruments.
*   **NHIS (National Health Insurance Scheme):** Ghana's universal health care system designated for Ghanaian nationals.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   The historical connection between the modern Ghanaian people and the ancient Empire of Ghana (Wagadu) is debated. Early 20th-century scholars like J.B. Anaman and J.B. Danquah promoted a migration theory linking the Akan to the ancient empire, but this is considered by some historians, like Jack Goody, to lack linguistic and evidence.
*   The sustainability of Chinese-financed infrastructure projects in Ghana is a concern, with questions raised about environmental impacts and transparency.
*   The effectiveness and social impact of laws regarding LGBTQ+ rights and witchcraft accusations remain contentious issues within Ghanaian society and politics.

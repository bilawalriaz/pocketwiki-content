# William Wilberforce

## Overview
William Wilberforce (1759–1833) was a British politician and philanthropist who became the leading parliamentary figure in the campaign to abolish the Atlantic slave trade. His 20-year campaign, rooted in his evangelical Christian faith, culminated in the Slave Trade Act of 1807. He later supported the movement for the complete abolition of slavery, which was achieved shortly before his death.

## Timeline
- **24 Aug 1759** — Born in Kingston upon Hull, Yorkshire.
- **1780** — Elected MP for Kingston upon Hull at age 21.
- **1785** — Undergoes an evangelical Christian conversion.
- **1787** — Agrees to lead the parliamentary campaign against the slave trade.
- **12 May 1789** — Makes first major abolition speech in the House of Commons.
- **1791** — First parliamentary bill to abolish the slave trade is defeated.
- **23 May 1806** — Foreign Slave Trade Bill receives royal assent.
- **25 Mar 1807** — Slave Trade Act 1807 passes Parliament.
- **1826** — Resigns from Parliament due to failing health.
- **29 Jul 1833** — Dies three days after hearing the Slavery Abolition Act's passage is assured.

## Body

### Early Life and Conversion
Wilberforce was born into a wealthy merchant family in Hull. After his father's death, he was sent to live with evangelical relatives in London, where he was influenced by Methodism. He later attended St John's College, Cambridge, where he was a social, non-studious student and a close friend of William Pitt. He entered Parliament in 1780 as an independent MP. In 1785, during a European tour, he underwent a profound evangelical conversion, which redirected his life's purpose toward Christian morality and social reform.

### Campaign Against the Slave Trade
In 1787, abolitionists including Thomas Clarkson and Granville Sharp persuaded Wilberforce to lead the fight against the slave trade in Parliament. The British trade was a major economic enterprise, with ships carrying an estimated 11 million Africans into slavery, about 1.4 million of whom died during the voyage. Wilberforce began his parliamentary campaign in 1789, arguing the trade was morally reprehensible. His first bill was defeated in 1791, and the campaign faced setbacks due to the French Revolution's association with radicalism and the outbreak of war with France. After Pitt's death in 1806, a new tactic was used: a bill banning British subjects from aiding the slave trade to French colonies, which passed in 1806. This paved the way for the definitive Slave Trade Act of 1807, which Wilberforce saw passed.

### Other Reforms and Conservative Views
Wilberforce was a leading figure in the "Clapham Sect" of evangelicals. He championed moral and religious reform, founding the Society for the Suppression of Vice and supporting missionary work. He advocated for causes like prison reform, animal welfare (co-founding the RSPCA), and the education of the poor. However, his views were deeply conservative; he supported repressive legislation like the "Gagging Bills," opposed trade unions, and initially resisted Catholic emancipation. Critics accused him of ignoring domestic injustices while campaigning abroad.

### Final Campaign for Emancipation
After 1807, Wilberforce focused on the complete abolition of slavery. He resigned from Parliament in 1826 due to poor health but remained a figurehead for the cause. In 1823, he published an "Appeal" calling slavery a national crime. He passed leadership of the parliamentary campaign to Thomas Fowell Buxton. The Slavery Abolition Act, which abolished slavery throughout most of the British Empire, was introduced in 1833. Wilberforce died on 29 July 1833, having learned its passage was assured.

## Terms
*   **Atlantic slave trade:** The forced transportation of enslaved Africans across the Atlantic Ocean, primarily to the Americas, which was a major economic system for European colonial powers.
*   **Evangelical Anglican:** A member of the Church of England emphasizing personal conversion, the authority of the Bible, and active Christian faith in daily life.
*   **Clapham Sect:** A group of wealthy, influential evangelical Anglicans in the early 19th century who campaigned for social and moral reform.
*   **Middle passage:** The horrific, often fatal, stage of the triangular slave trade where enslaved Africans were transported across the Atlantic.
*   **Rotten borough:** A parliamentary constituency with a very small electorate, allowing a single patron to control the seat, which was a target of reformers.
*   **Society for the Suppression of Vice:** An organization founded by Wilberforce in 1798 to enforce laws against public immorality like blasphemy and prostitution.
*   **Sierra Leone Company:** A company established in 1792 by abolitionists, including Wilberforce, to create a free colony for former slaves in West Africa.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Motivations for Abolition:** Historian Eric Williams argued abolition was driven by economic decline in the sugar industry, not humanitarianism. More recent historians note the industry was still profitable, renewing focus on the moral and evangelical motivations of Wilberforce and his allies.
*   **Wilberforce's Role vs. Clarkson's:** Wilberforce's sons' biography emphasized his leadership, downplaying Thomas Clarkson's crucial work in research and public mobilization. Historians now view their partnership as essential to the campaign's success.
*   **Effectiveness of Tactics:** Wilberforce's decision in 1789 to allow parliamentary hearings on evidence is criticized for prolonging the slave trade. His trusting, deferential nature is also seen as having sometimes hindered the campaign.

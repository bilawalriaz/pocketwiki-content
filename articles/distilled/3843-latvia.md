# Latvia

## Overview
Latvia is a Baltic state in Northern Europe with a population of 1.83 million. Its modern history is defined by a struggle for sovereignty, having been ruled by Teutonic, Swedish, Polish-Lithuanian, and Russian powers before establishing independence in 1918. This independence was interrupted by Soviet and Nazi occupations during World War II, followed by 45 years as a Soviet republic. Latvia restored its independence in 1991 and has since developed into a democratic, high-income economy and a member of the EU and NATO.

## Body

### Early History and Foreign Rule
The territory was settled by Proto-Baltic tribes around 3000 BC. By 900 AD, four Baltic tribes (Curonians, Latgalians, Selonians, Semigallians) and the Finnic Livonians inhabited the area. In the 13th century, German crusaders conquered the region, forming the crusader state of Terra Mariana (Livonia). After the Livonian War (1558–1583), the territory was divided, with most falling under the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth. In the 17th and early 18th centuries, Sweden and Russia vied for control. The Great Northern War (1700–1721) resulted in most of Latvia becoming part of the Russian Empire, which was completed by the annexation of the Duchy of Courland and Semigallia in 1795.

### National Awakening and Independence
During the 19th century, economic development and the rise of a Latvian bourgeoisie fueled a nationalist movement known as the First National Awakening. World War I created a power vacuum, allowing the People's Council of Latvia to proclaim independence on 18 November 1918. A war of independence followed against German and Soviet forces. A democratic constitution, the Satversme, was adopted in 1922. In 1934, Prime Minister Kārlis Ulmanis staged a coup, establishing an autocratic regime.

### World War II and Soviet Occupation
The Molotov–Ribbentrop Pact (1939) placed Latvia in the Soviet sphere of influence. In 1940, the USSR forcibly incorporated Latvia. The German invasion of 1941 led to a brutal occupation during which Latvia was administered as part of Reichskommissariat Ostland, and the Holocaust was perpetrated. Soviet forces reoccupied Latvia in 1944–1945. The subsequent Soviet period involved collectivization, industrialization, and extensive immigration from other Soviet republics, which significantly altered the country's ethnic composition. By 1959, ethnic Latvians comprised only 62% of the population.

### Restoration of Independence and Modern Latvia
The Singing Revolution, a peaceful protest movement, began in 1987. In 1990, Latvia declared the restoration of independence, which was fully achieved in August 1991 after the failed Soviet coup. The country transitioned to a market economy and a democratic parliamentary system. Latvia joined NATO and the European Union in 2004 and the Eurozone in 2014. A significant political and social issue has been the status of the large ethnic Russian minority, many of whom became non-citizens after independence. In 2023, Edgars Rinkēvičs became the EU's first openly gay head of state.

## Terms
*   **Saeima:** The unicameral parliament of Latvia, consisting of 100 members elected by proportional representation.
*   **Satversme:** The constitution of Latvia, originally adopted in 1922, suspended during Soviet rule, and reaffirmed in 1990.
*   **Terra Mariana:** The medieval name for the crusader state established in the territory of modern Latvia and Estonia.
*   **Singing Revolution:** The non-violent movement (1987–1991) through which the Baltic states regained independence from the USSR.
*   **Non-citizens:** A legal status for persons, primarily from the former Soviet Union, who settled in Latvia during the occupation and did not automatically receive citizenship after 1991.
*   **Russification:** Policies implemented by the Russian Empire and later the Soviet Union to promote Russian language and culture, diminishing local national identities.
*   **Internal devaluation:** An economic adjustment policy involving wage cuts and austerity to restore competitiveness without devaluing a currency, used by Latvia during the 2008–2009 financial crisis.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Language Policy:** The transition from Russian to Latvian as the sole language of instruction in schools has been a contentious issue, with debates over minority rights, integration, and national identity. A 2012 referendum to make Russian a second official language was rejected by 74.8% of voters.
*   **Citizenship and Integration:** The large population of non-citizens (14.1% in 2015) remains a political and social challenge, raising questions about democratic inclusion and social cohesion.
*   **Historical Memory:** The interpretation of the Soviet period and the roles of collaborators and victims during the Nazi and Soviet occupations continue to be subjects of scholarly and public debate.

# Kazan

## Overview
Kazan is the capital and largest city of the Republic of Tatarstan, Russia, and the fifth-largest city in the country. Situated at the confluence of the Volga and Kazanka rivers, it is a major industrial, cultural, and religious center known for its vibrant mix of Tatar and Russian cultures. Historically the capital of the Khanate of Kazan, it was conquered by Ivan the Terrible in 1552 and has since evolved into a significant modern metropolis, hosting major international events like the 2013 Summer Universiade and the 2018 FIFA World Cup.

## Timeline
- **c. 1005 AD** — Estimated founding of the urban settlement at the site of modern Kazan.
- **1438** — New Kazan founded as the capital of the Kazan Khanate by Ulugh Muhammad.
- **1552** — Conquered by Tsar Ivan the Terrible, becoming part of the Tsardom of Russia.
- **1774** — Largely destroyed during Pugachev's Rebellion.
- **Late 18th c.** — Rebuilt in stone under Catherine the Great; mosques permitted again.
- **1804** — Kazan Imperial University founded.
- **1920** — Became the capital of the Tatar Autonomous Soviet Socialist Republic (ASSR).
- **2005** — Celebrated its millennium; Kul Sharif Mosque and Kazan Metro opened.
- **2013** — Hosted the Summer Universiade.
- **2018** — Co-hosted the FIFA World Cup.
- **2024** — Hosted the BRICS Games.

## Body

### Etymology and Founding
The name "Kazan" means "boiler" or "cauldron" in Tatar and other Turkic languages, likely referring to the city's location in a depression. Archaeological evidence, including a Czech coin from the 10th century, supports an official founding date around 1005 AD. The city was a border post between Volga Bulgaria and Finno-Ugric peoples. The new Kazan, founded in 1438 by Ulugh Muhammad, became the capital of the Khanate of Kazan, flourishing as a trade and craft center.

### Russian Conquest and Imperial Era
The city was conquered by Ivan the Terrible in 1552, leading to the forced Christianization and deportation of much of the Tatar population. Kazan was largely destroyed by fires, including during Pugachev's Rebellion in 1774. It was subsequently rebuilt in stone under Catherine the Great. In the 19th century, it became an important center for Oriental Studies, industry, and Tatar cultural revival after the 1905 Revolution.

### Soviet and Modern Periods
Kazan was a center of revolutionary activity in 1917 and became the capital of the Tatar ASSR in 1920. During World War II, it was a major hub for relocated military industries. After the Soviet Union's dissolution, it re-emerged as a center of Tatar identity. Since 2000, the city has undergone massive renovation, celebrating its millennium in 2005 and hosting numerous international sports and political events.

### Economy and Infrastructure
Kazan is a leading industrial and financial center in Russia, with major sectors in mechanical engineering, petrochemicals, and IT. It hosts Russia's largest IT-park. The city has a comprehensive public transport system, including buses, trams, trolleybuses, a metro (opened 2005), and a major river port. Kazan International Airport is a key hub.

### Demographics and Culture
The population is approximately 1.3 million, with a near-even split between ethnic Tatars (48.8%) and Russians (46.9%). The predominant faiths are Islam (Sunni, Hanafi school) and Eastern Orthodox Christianity. The city's symbol is the mythical dragon Zilant. The Kazan Kremlin, a UNESCO World Heritage Site, is its architectural heart, featuring the Kul Sharif Mosque and the Söyembikä Tower.

## Terms
*   **Khanate of Kazan:** A Tatar state that existed from the 15th to 16th centuries, with Kazan as its capital.
*   **Pugachev's Rebellion (1773–75):** A major uprising of peasants and border troops led by Yemelyan Pugachev against Catherine the Great, which severely damaged Kazan.
*   **Tatar ASSR:** The Tatar Autonomous Soviet Socialist Republic, established in 1920 within the Russian SFSR.
*   **Kul Sharif Mosque:** A major mosque rebuilt inside the Kazan Kremlin and dedicated in 2005.
*   **Söyembikä Tower:** A leaning tower in the Kazan Kremlin, named after the last queen of the Kazan Khanate.
*   **Zilant:** A mythical dragon-like creature, the historical symbol of Kazan.
*   **Barabız (barabus):** A historic winter public transport sledge used in Kazan from the 19th to early 20th centuries.
*   **Bistä (Posad):** A historical district; Kazan was divided into the Russian Qazan Bistäse and the Tatar İske Tatar Bistäse.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   The exact founding date of Kazan is debated, with archaeological evidence supporting c. 1005 AD, but the city officially celebrated its millennium in 2005 based on this date.
*   The origin of the city's name has multiple legends, though the most likely explanation is its geographical location in a depression.

# D. S. Senanayake

## Overview
Don Stephen Senanayake (1884–1952) was a Ceylonese statesman who led the island's independence movement and became its first Prime Minister. As the "Father of the Nation," he established key institutions for the new state, though his policies, particularly regarding citizenship, remain controversial.

## Timeline
- **20 October 1884** — Born in Botale, Hapitigam Korale.
- **1915** — Imprisoned for 46 days without charges during the Sinhalese-Muslim riots.
- **1924** — Elected unopposed to the Legislative Council of Ceylon from Negombo.
- **1931** — Elected to the State Council; becomes Minister of Agriculture and Lands.
- **1943** — Becomes Leader of the House in the State Council.
- **1946** — Forms the United National Party (UNP).
- **24 September 1947** — Invited to form the first cabinet as Prime Minister.
- **4 February 1948** — Ceylon marks independence with the ceremonial opening of parliament.
- **15 November 1948** — Ceylon Citizenship Act becomes law.
- **1949-1951** — Establishes the Army, Navy, and Air Force Acts.
- **1951** — S.W.R.D. Bandaranaike resigns from cabinet and forms the Sri Lanka Freedom Party.
- **22 March 1952** — Dies in Colombo following a horse-riding accident.

## Body

### Early Life and Career
Born to a wealthy family in Botale, Senanayake was educated at S. Thomas' College, Mutwal. After a brief stint as a clerk, he joined the family business, managing plantations and the Kahatagaha Graphite Mine. He introduced rubber as a commercial crop to the family estates.

### Entry into Politics
Senanayake's political activism began with the temperance movement. The 1915 Sinhalese-Muslim riots, during which he and his brothers were imprisoned without charges, catalysed the modern independence movement. He was elected to the Legislative Council in 1924 and later to the State Council in 1931, where he served as Minister of Agriculture and Lands for fifteen years. In this role, he established the Land Development Ordinance and introduced agricultural modernisation policies.

### Path to Independence
During World War II, Senanayake played a key role in food supply and civil defence. He became Leader of the House in 1942 and used the 1943 Whitehall Declaration to advance constitutional change. After forming the UNP in 1946, he negotiated with the British government, using the recommendations of the Soulbury Commission. Following the 1947 general election, his UNP formed a coalition government, and he became the first Prime Minister. Independence was achieved on 4 February 1948.

### Premiership and National Development
As Prime Minister, Senanayake focused on nation-building. He launched the Gal Oya scheme, relocating over 250,000 people to spread the population and increase food production. He established the armed forces through the Army, Navy, and Air Force Acts and pursued a pro-Western foreign policy, hosting the 1950 Commonwealth Conference of Foreign Ministers which led to the Colombo Plan.

### Controversial Policies and Political Challenges
His government passed the Ceylon Citizenship Act of 1948, which denied citizenship to over 700,000 Indian Tamils, making them stateless. This was opposed by several political parties. Internally, he faced a major challenge when his powerful cabinet member, S.W.R.D. Bandaranaike, resigned in 1951 over policy differences and formed the opposition Sri Lanka Freedom Party.

### Death and Legacy
Senanayake died on 22 March 1952 from injuries sustained after falling from a horse. He is remembered as the "Father of the Nation" for securing independence and establishing state institutions. However, his citizenship laws are a source of lasting criticism, particularly among Tamils. His son, Dudley Senanayake, succeeded him as Prime Minister.

## Terms
- **Temperance movement:** A social movement advocating for moderation or abstinence from alcohol, which became a platform for political activism in Ceylon.
- **Legislative Council of Ceylon:** The unicameral legislature of British Ceylon before 1931.
- **State Council of Ceylon:** The unicameral legislature established in 1931 under the Donoughmore Constitution, which included a Board of Ministers.
- **Soulbury Commission:** A British commission formed in 1944 to recommend constitutional reforms for Ceylon, leading to the Soulbury Constitution.
- **Dominion status:** A form of self-governance within the British Empire, which Senanayake initially sought before pushing for full independence.
- **United National Party (UNP):** The political party formed by Senanayake in 1946 by amalgamating three pro-Dominion parties.
- **Gal Oya scheme:** A major irrigation and settlement project initiated by Senanayake to relocate people and boost agricultural production.
- **Ceylon Citizenship Act:** A 1948 law that defined citizenship criteria, effectively disenfranchising most Indian Tamils.
- **Colombo Plan:** A regional cooperative economic and social development initiative launched in 1950, following a conference hosted by Senanayake.

## Debates and open questions
- The primary debate concerns the legacy of the Ceylon Citizenship Act. While it is seen as a foundational act of the new nation by some, it is criticized for deliberately disenfranchising the Indian Tamil population, creating a stateless community and sowing seeds of ethnic tension.
- There is discussion about the nature of the independence achieved. Senanayake negotiated for dominion status and maintained strong ties with Britain, including a defence pact and British military bases, leading to questions about the completeness of sovereignty at independence in 1948.

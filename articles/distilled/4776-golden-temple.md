# Golden Temple

## Overview
The Golden Temple (Harmandir Sahib) in Amritsar, Punjab, is the holiest shrine and pre-eminent spiritual site of Sikhism. It is a central pilgrimage destination and a symbol of Sikh faith, equality, and resilience, having been destroyed and rebuilt multiple times throughout its history. The complex includes the sanctum sanctorum, the sacred pool (sarovar), and the Akal Takht, the chief seat of Sikh religious authority.

## Timeline
- **1577** — Guru Ram Das completes the sacred pool (sarovar).
- **1604** — Guru Arjan installs the Adi Granth (Sikh scripture) in the temple.
- **1764** — Sikhs rebuild the temple after its destruction by Ahmad Shah Durrani.
- **1809** — Maharaja Ranjit Singh renovates the temple in marble and copper.
- **1830** — Ranjit Singh overlays the sanctum with gold leaf, giving it the name "Golden Temple."
- **1919** — Jallianwala Bagh massacre occurs near the temple after a Baisakhi gathering.
- **1925** — Shiromani Gurdwara Parbandhak Committee (SGPC) takes control of the temple's management.
- **1984** — Operation Blue Star: Indian Army attacks the temple complex, causing major damage and deaths.
- **1999** — Sikhs rebuild the Akal Takht after removing earlier government repairs.

## Body

### History and Construction
The site was chosen by Guru Amar Das, the third Sikh Guru. His successor, Guru Ram Das, founded the town of Ramdaspur (later Amritsar) and completed the sacred pool in 1577. Guru Arjan, the fifth Guru, initiated the construction of the first Harmandir Sahib in 1581, completing it in 1589. He placed the Adi Granth within it in 1604. The temple was repeatedly destroyed by Mughal and Afghan forces, notably by Ahmad Shah Durrani in 1757 and 1762. It was rebuilt by Sikhs in 1764. Maharaja Ranjit Singh later renovated it with marble, copper, and gold leaf in the early 19th century.

### Architecture and Complex
The temple's architecture is a synthesis of Indo-Islamic Mughal and Hindu Rajput styles. The sanctum sanctorum, a two-story structure covered in gold leaf, sits in the middle of the Amrit Sarovar (pool of nectar). It is accessed by a causeway and features four entrances, symbolizing openness to all people. The complex includes the Akal Takht (Throne of the Timeless), the chief center of Sikh religious authority, and the Ramgarhia Bunga towers, originally built as watchtowers. The pool is maintained by volunteers through *kar seva* (community service).

### Daily Ceremonies and Langar
The Guru Granth Sahib (Sikh scripture) is treated as a living Guru. Each evening (*sukhasan*), it is carried in a palanquin to its bedroom in the Akal Takht. Each morning (*prakash*), it is returned to the sanctum. The complex houses a *langar*, a free community kitchen serving vegetarian meals to over 100,000 people daily, embodying the Sikh principle of equality.

### Modern History and Conflicts
The Golden Temple was a center for the Singh Sabha movement (1883–1920s), which reformed Sikhism, and the Punjabi Suba movement (1947–1966), which led to the creation of a Punjabi-speaking state. In the early 1980s, it became the site of a conflict between the Indian government and Sikh militants led by Jarnail Singh Bhindranwale. This culminated in Operation Blue Star (June 1984), a military assault that caused significant damage to the temple and Akal Takht and resulted in thousands of deaths. The complex was rebuilt afterward.

## Terms
- **Gurdwara**: A Sikh place of worship.
- **Sarovar**: A sacred pool or tank, central to many gurdwaras.
- **Akal Takht**: The "Throne of the Timeless," the highest seat of Sikh temporal authority.
- **Adi Granth / Guru Granth Sahib**: The central religious scripture of Sikhism, regarded as the eternal Guru.
- **Langar**: A free community kitchen and dining hall, open to all, serving vegetarian meals.
- **Kar Seva**: Voluntary community service, often involving physical labor for a religious cause.
- **Sangat**: The Sikh congregation or community.
- **Hukam**: An edict or writ issued by the Akal Takht.
- **Sukhasan**: The evening ceremony of putting the Guru Granth Sahib to rest.
- **Prakash**: The morning ceremony of opening and installing the Guru Granth Sahib.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The exact appearance and design of the original temple built by the Sikh Gurus before 1764 is unknown and debated, as no surviving records exist.
- The claim that the Sufi saint Mian Mir laid the foundation stone of the first temple is considered unsubstantiated by some scholars, with traditional sources stating Guru Arjan did so.
- The casualty figures and events of Operation Blue Star (1984) remain disputed between official government accounts and independent reports.

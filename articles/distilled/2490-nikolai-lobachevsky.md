# Nikolai Lobachevsky

## Overview
Nikolai Lobachevsky (1792–1856) was a Russian mathematician who revolutionized geometry by developing a consistent non-Euclidean system, now called hyperbolic or Lobachevskian geometry. His work challenged a 2,000-year-old axiom, demonstrating that alternative, logically sound geometries are possible, which profoundly influenced the development of modern mathematics.

- **1855** — Published *Pangeometry*.
- **24 Feb 1856** — Died in poverty in Kazan.

## Body

**Development of Non-Euclidean Geometry**
For centuries, mathematicians tried to prove Euclid's fifth postulate (the parallel postulate) from his other axioms. Lobachevsky took a revolutionary approach: he developed a geometry where this postulate is not true. In his system, for a given line and a point not on it, there exists more than one line through the point that does not intersect the original line. This created a consistent "hyperbolic geometry" where the sum of angles in a triangle is less than 180 degrees. He first presented these ideas in 1826 and published them in 1829–1830, making him the first to publish such a non-Euclidean system.

**Other Mathematical Contributions and Career**
Beyond geometry, Lobachevsky developed a method for approximating roots of algebraic equations, now known in Russia as the Lobachevsky method. He also provided an early definition of a function as a correspondence between two sets of real numbers. He had a long academic career at Kazan University, rising to full professor and rector, but was dismissed in 1846 and died in poverty.

**Legacy and Recognition**
Lobachevsky's work was initially met with rejection, such as when his 1829 paper was turned down by the St. Petersburg Academy. However, it later inspired the development of differential geometry and encouraged mathematicians to challenge other accepted "axioms." William Kingdon Clifford called him the "Copernicus of Geometry." His contributions are honored with an asteroid, a lunar crater, a university, and a mathematics prize.

## Terms
- **Hyperbolic Geometry:** A non-Euclidean geometry where the parallel postulate is replaced; through a point not on a given line, there are at least two lines that do not intersect the given line.
- **Euclid's Fifth Postulate (Parallel Postulate):** The axiom in Euclidean geometry stating that for any line and a point not on it, there is exactly one line through the point that does not intersect the given line.
- **Non-Euclidean Geometry:** Any geometry that rejects or modifies Euclid's fifth postulate.
- **Angle of Parallelism:** In hyperbolic geometry, the angle that depends on the distance of a point from a given line, determining the limiting parallel lines.
- **Lobachevsky Integral Formula:** A fundamental study on Dirichlet integrals attributed to Lobachevsky.
- **Dandelin–Gräffe Method:** A numerical method for approximating roots of algebraic equations, independently discovered by Lobachevsky.
- **Rector:** The head of a university.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Independent Discovery:** Lobachevsky developed his geometry independently of János Bolyai. While Carl Friedrich Gauss also explored similar ideas, he never published them. Some have incorrectly claimed Lobachevsky was influenced by Gauss, but there is no evidence of personal correspondence before Lobachevsky's publication.
- **Initial Reception:** His work was initially rejected by major academic institutions, highlighting the resistance to challenging long-held mathematical axioms.

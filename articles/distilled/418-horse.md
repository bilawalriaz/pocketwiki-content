# Horse

## Overview
The horse (*Equus ferus caballus*) is a domesticated, one-toed ungulate descended from the small, multi-toed *Eohippus* over 45–55 million years, originally in North America. Humans domesticated horses around 4000 BCE in Central Asia (Botai culture), with widespread domestication by 3000 BCE; all modern domestic horses carry the DOM2 gene that spread rapidly c. 2200 BCE alongside spoke-wheeled chariots. Horses are adapted for flight—possessing a 350° field of vision, the ability to sleep standing via a "stay apparatus," and a digestive system specialized for continuous forage fermentation. They serve humans in sport, agriculture, transport, therapy, and historically warfare, with over 300 breeds categorized by temperament as hot-blooded (speed/endurance), cold-blooded (heavy work), or warmblooded (crosses for riding).

## Timeline
- **45–55 Mya** — *Hyracotherium* (Eohippus), earliest Equidae, appears in Eocene North America; 4 toes front, 3 back.
- **32–37 Mya** — *Mesohippus* loses extra front toe; side toes shrink toward vestigial splint bones.
- **~5 Mya** — Modern *Equus* evolves; spreads from North America to Old World during Pleistocene.
- **~15,000 ya** — *Equus ferus* widespread across Holarctic (Europe, Eurasia, Beringia, North America).
- **10,000–7,600 ya** — Horses go extinct in North America; causes debated (human arrival vs. climate/vegetation shift).
- **c. 4000–3500 BCE** — Earliest archaeological evidence for domestication attempts in Western Eurasian Steppes (lower Volga-Don); Botai culture (Kazakhstan) domesticates Przewalski's horses, not modern ancestors.
- **c. 3000 BCE** — Horse fully domesticated; DOM2 gene emerges in West Eurasia.
- **c. 2100 BCE** — Irrefutable domestication evidence: horse remains interred with chariots in Sintashta/Petrovka graves.
- **c. 2200 BCE** — Rapid DOM2 spread coincides with Sintashta culture and spoke-wheeled chariots.
- **1791** — General Stud Book for Thoroughbreds founded, early formal breed registry.
- **1909** — Tarpan (*Equus ferus ferus*) extinct; last captive dies in Russian zoo.
- **1969–1992** — Przewalski's horse (*E. f. przewalskii*) extinct in wild; survives in zoos.
- **1992** — Przewalski's horse reintroduced to Mongolia via zoo conservation efforts.
- **2007** — Horse genome sequenced (2.7 Gb, 64 chromosomes).
- **2008** — FAO estimates ~59 million horses worldwide.

## Body

### Taxonomy and Evolution
Horses belong to order Perissodactyla (odd-toed ungulates), once diverse (14 families) but now reduced to Equidae, Tapiridae, and Rhinocerotidae. The lineage traces from *Hyracotherium* (Eocene, 45–55 Mya) through *Mesohippus* (Oligocene) to *Equus* (~5 Mya), with toes reducing to a single hoof and teeth adapting from browsing soft tropical plants to grazing tough plains grasses. This shifted proto-horses from forest dwellers to inhabitants of semi-arid steppes worldwide. *Equus ferus* became a holarctic species by ~15,000 ya but vanished from North America 10,000–7,600 ya; theories cite human arrival or climate-driven replacement of steppe grasses by unpalatable shrub tundra.

### Wild and Feral Distinctions
A truly wild horse has no domesticated ancestors. Only two subspecies survived into recorded history: the tarpan (extinct 1909) and Przewalski's horse (*E. f. przewalskii*). Przewalski's horse was extinct in the wild 1969–1992 but reintroduced to Mongolia from zoo stock. Genetic studies complicate its status: Botai culture horses (c. 3500 BCE) were Przewalski's horses, raising the possibility that modern Przewalski's horses are feral descendants of a failed domestication attempt, though only one of seven modern individuals shared this ancestry. Most "wild" horses today (e.g., North American mustangs, Australian brumbies) are feral—descended from domestic stock. Semi-feral populations (e.g., Dartmoor, New Forest) are privately owned but live on public lands.

### Domestication and Breeds
Domestication likely began in Central Asia pre-3500 BCE. Archaeological (Volga-Don, Botai) and genetic (modern vs. ancient DNA) evidence converge: Botai horses were Przewalski's, not modern ancestors. By 3000 BCE horses were fully domesticated; by 2000 BCE horse bones surge in northwestern Europe. The DOM2 gene, originating in West Eurasia late 3rd millennium BCE, spread rapidly c. 2200 BCE with Sintashta chariots, indicating strong selection for riding traits. Genetic data show few wild stallions but many mares contributed to domestic stock (low Y-chromosome diversity, high mitochondrial diversity), and coat color variation exploded 5000–3000 BCE. Over 300 breeds exist today, developed for "form to function" (e.g., Andalusian for dressage, draft breeds for farm work). Registries (Thoroughbred Stud Book, 1791) track pedigrees; "purebred" ≠ "Thoroughbred" (a specific breed).

### Anatomy and Physiology
**Skeleton:** 205 bones; no collarbone—forelimbs attach via muscles/tendons/ligaments. "Knee" = carpal bones (human wrist); "hock" = ankle/heel; fetlock = proximal sesamoids (human knuckles). No muscles below knees/hocks—only bone, tendons, ligaments, hoof tissues.  
**Hooves:** Distal phalanx (fingertip equivalent) surrounded by laminae; wall/sole of keratin. Horse (~500 kg) effectively walks on tiptoe. Hooves grow continuously; domesticated horses need trimming/shoeing every 5–8 weeks.  
**Teeth:** 12 incisors (biting), 24 premolars/molars (chewing). Stallions/geldings have 4 canines ("tushes"); some develop vestigial "wolf teeth" (removed if bit interference). Interdental space holds bit. Teeth erupt lifelong; wear patterns give rough age estimates.  
**Digestion:** Herbivore, non-ruminant (one stomach). Small stomach, long intestines for steady forage fermentation. 450 kg horse eats 7–11 kg/day, drinks 38–45 L. Hindgut fermentation (cecum/large intestine) digests cellulose via symbiotic microbes. Cannot vomit → colic risk. No gallbladder but tolerates dietary fat.  
**Senses:** Lateral eyes → 350° vision (65° binocular, 285° monocular); dichromatic (red-green blind). Smell better than humans, less than dogs; two olfactory systems (nasal + vomeronasal/Jacobson's for pheromones). Ears rotate 180° → 360° hearing. Noise affects behavior: UK study (2013) found jazz/rock increased nervousness vs. quiet/classical/country; Australian study linked talk radio to higher gastric ulcer rates in racehorses. Touch highly sensitive (eyes, ears, nose); taste sorts fodder. Proprioception and balance highly developed.

### Movement
Four natural gaits: walk (4-beat, 6.4 km/h), trot/jog (2-beat, 13–19 km/h), canter/lope (3-beat, 19–24 km/h), gallop (40–48 km/h; sprint record 70.76 km/h). Some breeds are "gaited," genetically replacing trot with smoother 4-beat ambling gaits (rack, running walk, tölt, fox trot) at similar speeds. Pace (2-beat lateral) occurs in some horses instead of trot.

### Behavior and Cognition
Prey animals with strong fight-or-flight; first reaction is startle/flee, but defend if cornered or young threatened. Curious—hesitate to assess threats. Herd hierarchy led by dominant mare; social, form attachments across species. Isolated horses develop "stable vices" (stereotypies: weaving, cribbing, wall kicking) from insufficient companionship/exercise. Cognitive studies show spatial discrimination, categorization, concept learning, quantity discrimination (<4), and learning via habituation, classical/operant conditioning, and reinforcement. Horses thrive on routine; "intelligent" horses often reflect skilled trainers using positive reinforcement.

### Temperament Classification
"Hot-blooded" (oriental: Akhal-Teke, Arabian, Barb, Thoroughbred): spirited, bold, quick learners, refined, bred for speed/agility. "Cold-blooded" (draft: Belgian, Clydesdale, Shire, some ponies): muscular, calm, patient, bred for heavy work. "Warmblooded" (Trakehner, Hanoverian, Irish Draught): crosses of hot/cold bloods for riding—refined yet substantial, milder temperament. Modern "Warmblood" often refers specifically to sport horse breeds for dressage/show jumping.

### Sleep
Polyphasic: many short bouts. Stand via "stay apparatus" (light sleep); lie down for REM (1–2 hours every few days). Total sleep ~2.9 hrs/day (range: minutes to hours). Group sleep safer (sentinels). Sleep deprivation if never allowed to lie down → involuntary REM collapse (distinct from narcolepsy).

### Reproduction and Development
Gestation ~340 days (320–370); usually one foal (twins rare). Foals precocial—stand/run soon after birth; born spring. Mare estrous cycle 19–22 days (spring–autumn); winter anestrus. Weaning 4–6 months. Sexual maturity ~18 months but rarely bred before 3 (females especially). Skeletal maturity at 4–6 years (epiphyseal plates fuse later in larger horses). Training under saddle 2–4 years (Thoroughbreds as early as 2; dressage/endurance later: 3–4 or 5 years for endurance).

### Size, Measurement, and Ponies
Height at withers (stable anatomical point). English system: hands (4 in / 101.6 mm); e.g., 15.2 h = 62 in (157.5 cm). Light riding: 14–16 h (380–550 kg). Large riding: 15.2–17 h (500–600 kg). Draft: 16–18 h (700–1000 kg). Record: Shire "Mammoth" 21.2¼ h (219 cm, 1524 kg); smallest: Thumbelina 4.1 h (43 cm, 26 kg, dwarfism).  
Ponies: taxonomically horses; distinction primarily height (traditional 14.2 h / 147 cm cutoff), but phenotype matters (thicker mane/tail/coat, shorter legs, wider barrel, calmer temperament). Exceptions: breed registries may classify all members as horses regardless of height; miniature horses (Falabella ≤ 7.2 h) are horses, not ponies. Competition cutoffs vary: Australia <14 h, USEF Western <14.1 h, FEI <148 cm (no shoes) / 149 cm (with shoes).

### Colors and Markings
Specialized vocabulary; color often primary classification. Base colors: chestnut (recessive "red factor") and black (dominant) via *MC1R* (extension gene). Modifiers: bay (black restricted to points), spotting (pinto, leopard), dilution (palomino, dun), greying. "White" horses usually aged grays (born dark, lighten, black skin); true whites (born white hair, pink skin) rare. Multiple alleles (dominant white, sabino-1) produce white coats; no albinos (pink skin + red eyes). ≥13 alleles identified for coat color; white markings/spotting inherited separately from base color.

### Genetics
64 chromosomes. Genome sequenced 2007: 2.7 Gb base pairs (larger than dog, smaller than human/bovine). Map available to researchers.

### Human Interaction
**Numbers/Economy:** FAO 2008: ~59M worldwide (33.5M Americas, 13.8M Asia, 6.3M Europe); US ~9.5M. US horse industry direct impact >$39B (1.4M jobs); indirect >$102B.  
**Sport:** Origins in military training (dressage, eventing, jumping) or ranch work (rodeo). Disciplines: show jumping, dressage, eventing, driving, endurance, gymkhana, rodeo, fox hunting, polo, jousting, buzkashi. Horse shows (from medieval fairs) judge conformation/performance. Racing: flat, steeplechase, harness (sulky); gambling drives economics.  
**Work:** Mounted police, cattle ranching (remote terrain), search/rescue, nature reserves (low soil disruption), wilderness transport, logging (selective, less damage), agriculture (100M equids still working in less developed areas; 27M in Africa).  
**Warfare:** Archaeological evidence 4000–3000 BCE; widespread by late Bronze Age. Modern limited use: ceremonial, reconnaissance/transport in rough terrain (e.g., Janjaweed, Darfur 21st century).  
**Entertainment/Culture:** Historical reenactments, ceremonies (UK royal carriages), Budweiser Clydesdales, film/TV/literature, advertising, heraldry, mythology (winged horses, solar/lunar chariots), Chinese zodiac. Inspiration for auto names/logos (Mustang, Bronco, Ferrari, Porsche, etc.).  
**Therapy:** Therapeutic riding (balance, coordination, confidence); hippotherapy (therapist uses horse movement for cognitive/motor skills); equine-facilitated psychotherapy (anxiety, mood disorders, behavioral issues, life transitions); prison programs reduce recidivism. Paralympic equestrian events recognized.  
**Products:** Meat (~5M slaughtered/year; taboo in some cultures), hide (leather), hooves (glue), bone (tools: Italian *spinto* for ham curing), mare's milk (fermented to kumis), blood (historical Mongol nutrition), Premarin (estrogens from pregnant mare urine), tail hair (violin bows), saba (horsehide kumis vessel in Asia).  
**Care:** Forage-based diet (2–2.5% body weight dry feed); grain supplement if active but ≥50% forage by weight. Water 38–45 L/day. Shelter from wind/precipitation. Routine farrier (hooves), vaccinations, dental exams. Daily exercise if stalled. Sturdy fencing. Regular grooming.

## Terms
- ****Colt**** — Male horse under four years old (racing definitions vary).
- ****Filly**** — Female horse under four years old.
- ****Foal**** — Horse of either sex less than one year old; *suckling* if nursing, *weanling* if weaned.
- ****Gelding**** — Castrated male horse of any age.
- ****Hand**** — Unit of height = 4 inches (101.6 mm); measured at withers.
- ****Hot-blooded**** — Temperament type: spirited, energetic, refined (e.g., Arabian, Thoroughbred); not body temperature.
- ****Cold-blooded**** — Temperament type: calm, heavy, muscular (e.g., draft breeds); not body temperature.
- ****Warmblood**** — Cross between hot- and cold-blooded breeds; modern sense: sport horses for dressage/jumping.
- ****Feral horse**** — Free-living horse descended from domesticated ancestors; not truly wild.
- ****Stay apparatus**** — Ligament/tendon mechanism locking legs, allowing horse to doze standing without collapsing.
- ****Hindgut fermentation**** — Digestion of cellulose by symbiotic microbes in cecum and large intestine (non-ruminant).
- ****DOM2 gene**** — Genetic lineage in all modern domestic horses, originating West Eurasia late 3rd millennium BCE, spread c. 2200 BCE with chariots.
- ****Przewalski's horse**** — *Equus ferus przewalskii*; only surviving truly wild subspecies; reintroduced to Mongolia from zoo stock.
- ****Gaited horse**** — Breed genetically predisposed to perform smooth 4-beat ambling gaits (rack, tölt, running walk) instead of trot.

## Debates and open questions
- **North American extinction cause:** Human arrival vs. climate-driven vegetation change (steppe → shrub tundra) 10,000–7,600 ya.  
- **Przewalski's horse ancestry:** Whether modern population descends from feral Botai domesticates (c. 3500 BCE) or represents a separate wild lineage; genetic data show only 1 of 7 modern individuals shares Botai ancestry.  
- **Domestication center:** Botai culture domesticated Przewalski's horses, not modern horse ancestors; the exact population giving rise to DOM2 modern horses remains under study (West Eurasian steppes favored).  
- **Pony vs. horse classification:** Height cutoffs vary by region/registry (14.0–14.2 hands); phenotype and breed registry rules create exceptions (miniature horses = horses; some pony breeds exceed height cutoff).  
- **Cognitive limits:** Quantity discrimination demonstrated only for <4; full extent of concept learning and problem-solving under investigation.  
- **Music/environmental stress:** Conflicting studies on auditory stimuli (music genre, talk radio) effects on stress/ulcers; optimal acoustic environment not settled.

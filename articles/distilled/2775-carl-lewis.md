# Carl Lewis

## Overview
Carl Lewis is one of the most decorated track and field athletes in history, renowned for his dominance in the 100 m, 200 m, and long jump from the early 1980s through the mid-1990s. His career is defined by his Olympic success, including four consecutive long jump gold medals, and his role in some of the sport's most dramatic competitions and controversies.

## Timeline
- **July 1, 1961** — Born in Birmingham, Alabama.
- **1979** — Sets high school long jump record (8.13 m).
- **1980** — Misses Moscow Olympics due to U.S. boycott; competes in Liberty Bell Classic.
- **1981** — Becomes world #1 in 100 m and long jump; wins James E. Sullivan Award.
- **1983** — Wins inaugural World Championships in 100 m, long jump, and 4×100 m relay (world record).
- **1984** — Wins four gold medals (100 m, long jump, 200 m, 4×100 m) at Los Angeles Olympics, matching Jesse Owens.
- **1987** — Loses 100 m at World Championships to Ben Johnson, who sets a world record.
- **1988** — After Johnson's disqualification for doping, Lewis is awarded 100 m Olympic gold in Seoul.
- **1991** — Sets 100 m world record (9.86 s) at Tokyo World Championships; loses long jump to Mike Powell's world record (8.95 m).
- **1992** — Wins long jump gold at Barcelona Olympics; anchors 4×100 m relay to world record (37.40 s).
- **1996** — Wins fourth consecutive long jump gold at Atlanta Olympics.
- **2003** — Documents reveal he tested positive for banned stimulants at 1988 trials; cleared for inadvertent use.
- **2023** — Elevated to head track and field coach at the University of Houston.

## Body

### Rise to Dominance (1979-1984)
Born to an athletic family, Lewis was coached by his father and later by Tom Tellez at the University of Houston. He emerged as a world-class long jumper and sprinter by 1981, frequently topping world rankings. He was determined to set records at sea level, not at altitude. His breakthrough came in 1983, where he won three golds at the first World Championships. At the 1984 Los Angeles Olympics, he matched Jesse Owens by winning gold in the 100 m, long jump, 200 m, and 4×100 m relay. His tactical decision to take minimal jumps in the long jump final to preserve energy for other events drew public criticism.

### Rivalry with Ben Johnson and Doping Scandal (1985-1988)
Lewis's dominance in the 100 m was challenged by Canadian Ben Johnson. After Johnson beat him in 1987 and set a world record (9.83 s), Lewis publicly questioned the use of performance-enhancing drugs. At the 1988 Seoul Olympics, Johnson won the 100 m final in a world record time but was later disqualified for steroid use. Lewis was awarded the gold medal and credited with the Olympic record (9.92 s). In 2003, documents revealed Lewis had tested positive for banned stimulants (ephedrine-related compounds) at the 1988 U.S. trials. The U.S. Olympic Committee had cleared him, accepting his claim of inadvertent ingestion from a dietary supplement. The IAAF affirmed the proper procedures were followed.

### The 1991 World Championships and Later Career
The 1991 Tokyo World Championships are considered Lewis's greatest performance. He set the 100 m world record (9.86 s) in a historic race. The long jump final became an epic duel with Mike Powell. Lewis jumped a wind-aided 8.91 m (beyond Bob Beamon's record), but Powell responded with a legal world record of 8.95 m, ending Lewis's 65-meet, ten-year undefeated streak in the event. Lewis continued to win Olympic long jump gold in 1992 and 1996, becoming one of only a few athletes to win the same individual event four times. He retired in 1997.

## Terms
- **World Record (WR):** The best performance ever officially ratified in an event.
- **Wind-aided:** A jump or run performed with a tailwind exceeding the legal limit (2.0 m/s), making it ineligible for record purposes.
- **Sea-level record:** A record set at an altitude below 1,000 meters, often considered more prestigious than records set at high altitude where thinner air aids performance.
- **Ephedrine/Ma Huang:** A stimulant banned in sport; found in some over-the-counter herbal supplements.
- **Ancillary jumps:** In a long jump series, jumps that are valid but not the athlete's best of that competition.
- **Congressional Gold Medal:** A high civilian honor; 461 were created for the boycotted 1980 U.S. Olympic team.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **1984 Long Jump Tactics:** Lewis was booed for not attempting to break Bob Beamon's world record after securing the gold with his first jump. He defended his strategy as necessary to win four gold medals.
- **Public Perception and Marketability:** Despite his achievements, Lewis struggled with endorsements after 1984. Factors included the long jump controversy, rumors about his sexuality, and a public image perceived as aloof.
- **Drug Testing Exoneration:** The 2003 revelation of his 1988 positive tests for stimulants sparked debate. While officially cleared for inadvertent use, the incident remains a point of discussion regarding his legacy and the era's anti-doping practices.

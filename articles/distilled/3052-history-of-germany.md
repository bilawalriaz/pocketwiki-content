# History of Germany

## Overview
Germany's history is a complex narrative of fragmentation and unification, shaped by its central European location. From the Roman frontier and medieval empire to its pivotal roles in the World Wars and the Cold War, its development has profoundly influenced European and global affairs. The modern Federal Republic emerged from division, becoming a leading economic power and a cornerstone of European integration.

## Timeline
- **843** — Treaty of Verdun divides the Frankish Empire, creating East Francia (the precursor to Germany).
- **962** — Otto I is crowned the first Holy Roman Emperor.
- **1517** — Martin Luther's 95 Theses spark the Protestant Reformation.
- **1618–1648** — Thirty Years' War devastates the Holy Roman Empire; Peace of Westphalia increases state autonomy.
- **1806** — Holy Roman Empire dissolved by Napoleon.
- **1871** — Unification of Germany under Prussian leadership as the German Empire.
- **1918–1919** — German Revolution ends the monarchy; Weimar Republic established.
- **1933** — Adolf Hitler appointed Chancellor; Nazi dictatorship established.
- **1939–1945** — World War II; Germany defeated and occupied.
- **1949** — Germany divided into West Germany (FRG) and East Germany (GDR).
- **1989–1990** — Fall of the Berlin Wall; German reunification.

## Body

### From Tribes to Empire
The region was first named *Germania* by Julius Caesar. Germanic tribes resisted full Roman annexation, though Rome established provinces along the Rhine. Following the Western Roman Empire's fall, the Franks conquered other Germanic peoples. The Frankish Empire was divided in 843, with the eastern portion becoming East Francia. In 962, Otto I was crowned emperor, founding the Holy Roman Empire, a medieval Germanic kingdom.

### The Holy Roman Empire and Reformation
During the High Middle Ages, the Hanseatic League dominated Baltic and North Sea trade, and the Teutonic Order established a state in Prussia. Regional rulers gained power at the expense of emperors. Martin Luther's Reformation after 1517 divided the empire religiously, with northern states becoming Protestant and southern states remaining Catholic. The Thirty Years' War (1618–48) ended with the Peace of Westphalia, granting significant autonomy to the imperial states, notably Austria and Prussia.

### Unification and Empire
The Holy Roman Empire dissolved in 1806 during the Napoleonic Wars. After a period of reorganization, Prussia, under Chancellor Otto von Bismarck, engineered unification through wars with Denmark (1864), Austria (1866), and France (1870–71). The German Empire was proclaimed in 1871. It rapidly industrialized, becoming a leading world power, but its aggressive foreign policy and naval arms race with Britain contributed to the outbreak of World War I.

### World Wars and Division
Germany's defeat in World War I led to the abdication of the Kaiser and the establishment of the Weimar Republic. The republic was weakened by the Treaty of Versailles, economic crisis, and political extremism. Adolf Hitler and the Nazi Party rose to power in 1933, establishing a totalitarian regime that initiated World War II and perpetrated the Holocaust. Germany's defeat in 1945 resulted in its division into four occupation zones. The western zones became the democratic Federal Republic of Germany (West Germany), while the Soviet zone became the communist German Democratic Republic (East Germany).

### Reunification and Modern Germany
The Berlin Wall, a symbol of the Cold War division, was opened in 1989 amid popular revolution. Germany was officially reunified on October 3, 1990. The Federal Republic, with Berlin as its capital, became a founding member of the eurozone in 1999. It has since played a leading role in the European Union, navigated the 2015 migrant crisis, and, following the 2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine, initiated a major rearmament program.

## Terms
- **Holy Roman Empire:** A multi-ethnic complex of territories in Western and Central Europe that existed from 962 to 1806, with the German king as its emperor.
- **Reformation:** The 16th-century religious movement initiated by Martin Luther that challenged the Catholic Church and led to the establishment of Protestant churches.
- **Thirty Years' War:** A devastating European conflict (1618–1648) fought largely in the Holy Roman Empire, ending with increased autonomy for its states.
- **Zollverein:** A customs union of German states, led by Prussia, that facilitated economic integration before political unification.
- **Kulturkampf:** The "culture struggle" (1871–1878) under Bismarck to reduce the influence of the Catholic Church in the new German Empire.
- **Weimar Republic:** The democratic government of Germany from 1919 to 1933, established after World War I.
- **Nazi Party (NSDAP):** The far-right National Socialist German Workers' Party, which established a totalitarian dictatorship under Adolf Hitler.
- **Holocaust:** The state-sponsored systematic murder of approximately six million Jews and millions of others by the Nazi regime.
- **Ostpolitik:** The normalization of relations between West Germany and Eastern Europe, including East Germany, pursued by Chancellor Willy Brandt in the 1970s.
- **Wirtschaftswunder:** The "economic miracle" of rapid growth and industrial expansion in West Germany during the 1950s and 1960s.

## Debates and open questions
- The extent to which the Holy Roman Empire's decentralized structure constituted a form of nation-building is debated.
- Historians discuss the links between the Herero and Nama genocide in German South West Africa (1906–07) and the later Holocaust.
- The causes and human cost of the flight and expulsion of Germans after World War II remain contested, with death toll estimates ranging from 600,000 to 2 million.
- Scholars debate the nature of East Germany's communist regime, with some emphasizing its commitment to social progress and others highlighting its repressive control.

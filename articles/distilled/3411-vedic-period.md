# Vedic period

## Overview
The Vedic period (c. 1500–500 BCE) is a foundational era in Indian history, marking the composition of the Vedas and the development of early Indo-Aryan culture in the northern Indian subcontinent. It bridges the collapse of the Indus Valley Civilisation and the rise of second urbanisation, laying the groundwork for later Hindu traditions and social structures.

## Timeline
- **c. 1900 BCE** — Collapse of the Indus Valley Civilisation.
- **c. 1500 BCE** — Indo-Aryan migration into northwestern India begins; early Vedic period starts.
- **c. 1400–1000 BCE** — Composition of the Rigveda, the oldest Vedic text.
- **c. 1200–1000 BCE** — Aryan culture spreads east to the Ganges Plain; iron tools adopted.
- **c. 1000–600 BCE** — Later Vedic period; Kuru kingdom emerges and codifies rituals.
- **c. 900–750 BCE** — Political center shifts to Panchala kingdom under King Keśin Dālbhya.
- **c. 600 BCE** — Second urbanisation begins; rise of Mahajanapadas (large kingdoms).
- **Early 6th century BCE** — Achaemenid invasion of the Indus Valley by Cyrus and Darius I.

## Body

### Origins and Migration
The Vedic period began after the Indus Valley Civilisation's collapse (c. 1900 BCE). Indo-Aryan groups, part of the broader Indo-Iranian migration from the Andronovo horizon, moved into northwestern India. They brought Vedic religious traditions linked to Proto-Indo-European and Indo-Iranian practices, such as fire worship and the Soma cult. Some Indian scholars argue for an indigenous origin of the Indo-Aryans, but this view is outside the academic mainstream.

### Early Vedic Period (c. 1500–1000 BCE)
Early Vedic society was semi-nomadic, pastoral, and tribal, centered in the Punjab. The Rigveda describes conflicts between Aryas and non-Vedic groups like the Dasas and Dasyus. A key event was the Battle of the Ten Kings, where the Bharata tribe, led by Sudas, defeated a confederation of ten tribes on the river Parushni (modern Ravi). This conflict possibly arose over water resources and led to the formation of the Kuru tribe.

### Later Vedic Period (c. 1000–600 BCE)
Iron tools enabled forest clearing and agricultural expansion into the Ganges Plain. Society transitioned to settled farming, and tribes consolidated into kingdoms. The Kuru kingdom systematized Vedic literature and developed the Śrauta ritual. The varna system emerged as a social hierarchy: Brahmins (priests), Kshatriyas (warriors), Vaishyas (peasants/traders), and Shudras (servants). The Kuru kingdom declined after defeat by the Salva tribe, shifting political power to Panchala and later Videha.

### Society and Politics
Early Vedic society was relatively egalitarian, with tribal councils (sabha and samiti) limiting the chief's (rajan) power. Over time, hereditary kingship and social stratification increased. The household became a central unit, and women's status gradually declined. By the later period, political systems included monarchies and oligarchies. The Kuru kingdom is considered an early "state," though its development was limited by ritual expenditure over state-building.

### Economy and Religion
The economy combined pastoralism and agriculture, with trade using cattle as currency. Iron use expanded in the later period. Vedic religion centered on sacrifices (yajna) to deities like Indra, Agni, and Soma, with ethics based on Satya (truth) and Ṛta (cosmic order). The Vedas were orally transmitted and later codified. This tradition influenced the "Hindu synthesis" around the Common Era.

### Literature and Archaeology
Vedic literature includes the four Vedas, Brahmanas, and early Upanishads. Archaeological cultures linked to the period are the Ochre Coloured Pottery, Gandhara grave, Black and Red Ware, and Painted Grey Ware cultures. These show transitions from Bronze to Iron Age technologies.

## Terms
- **Vedas**: Ancient liturgical texts forming the basis of Brahmanical ideology, composed c. 1500–900 BCE.
- **Śrauta**: Orthodox Vedic ritual tradition systematized in the later Vedic period.
- **Varna**: Social class system (Brahmin, Kshatriya, Vaishya, Shudra) that emerged in the later Vedic period.
- **Rajan**: Tribal chief or king in Vedic society.
- **Yajna**: Vedic sacrifice involving fire offerings and chanting.
- **Mahajanapadas**: Large kingdoms that emerged around 600 BCE, marking the end of the Vedic period.
- **Painted Grey Ware (PGW)**: Archaeological pottery culture (c. 1200–600 BCE) associated with the later Vedic period.
- **Upanishads**: Philosophical texts composed in the later Vedic period, exploring concepts like Brahman and Atman.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Indigenous vs. Migration Origin**: The academic consensus supports an Indo-Aryan migration into India, but some Indian scholars argue for an indigenous origin, a view outside mainstream academia.
- **Nature of the Kuru Kingdom**: Scholars debate whether the Kuru kingdom was a true "state" or a complex chiefdom, as its kings used the title "rajan" (chief) rather than higher royal titles.
- **Social Mobility in Vedic Society**: While the varna system became rigid, some Rigvedic hymns suggest possible social mobility, indicating a less fixed hierarchy in earlier times.
- **Chronology**: The Puranic chronology presents a much older timeline for Vedic events (e.g., Kurukshetra War at c. 3100 BCE), which contrasts with mainstream historical dating.

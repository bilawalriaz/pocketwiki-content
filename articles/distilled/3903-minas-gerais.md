# Minas Gerais

## Overview
Minas Gerais is a large, populous state in southeastern Brazil, historically defined by its mineral wealth. Its discovery of gold in the late 17th century triggered a massive population boom, shifted Brazil's economic center southward, and fostered a unique colonial culture. Today, it remains a major economic powerhouse, leading the nation in mineral production and agriculture, particularly coffee and milk.

## Timeline
- **c. 11,400–12,000 years ago** — Inhabitation by indigenous peoples, evidenced by the Luzia fossil.
- **1692–1693** — First gold deposits discovered by bandeirante Antônio Rodrigues Arzão.
- **1709** — Creation of the Captaincy of São Paulo and Minas do Ouro.
- **1720** — Creation of the Captaincy of Minas Gerais after the Vila Rica Revolt.
- **1789** — The Minas Gerais Conspiracy (Inconfidência Mineira) for independence fails.
- **18th century** — Peak of gold mining; Vila Rica (Ouro Preto) becomes Brazil's largest city.
- **19th century** — Coffee cultivation becomes the new economic driver.
- **1960** — Inauguration of Brasília, built by Minas-born President Juscelino Kubitschek.
- **2010** — Inauguration of the Administrative City of Minas Gerais in Belo Horizonte.
- **2022** — State population reaches 20,539,989 according to the census.

## Body

### Etymology and Geography
The name "Minas Gerais" likely means "General Mines," referring to the numerous mining sites in the region. The state's terrain is rugged, containing some of Brazil's highest peaks and the sources of major rivers like the São Francisco and Grande. Its tropical climate varies from humid in the south to semi-arid in the north, supporting rich biodiversity in biomes like the Cerrado and Atlantic Forest.

### Prehistory and Indigenous Peoples
The region shows evidence of human habitation dating back over 11,000 years. Over a hundred indigenous groups lived there when the Portuguese arrived, including the Cataguás, Kiriris, and various "botocudo" groups. Colonization and the subsequent gold rush led to the enslavement and decimation of these populations; today, only five indigenous groups remain in the state.

### Colonial Era and the Gold Rush
The discovery of gold in the 1690s sparked a massive influx of settlers, leading to conflicts like the War of the Emboabas (1707–1709). The Portuguese Crown imposed strict control, including a 20% tax on gold ("the fifth"). This period saw the rise of major cities like Ouro Preto, the construction of the Estrada Real (Royal Road), and a flourishing of Baroque art and music. The discovery of diamonds in the 1720s further boosted the economy. The wealth was built on enslaved African labor, leading to the formation of numerous quilombos (maroon communities).

### Political History and Modern Era
Minas Gerais played a pivotal role in Brazilian independence movements, most notably the failed 1789 Inconfidência Mineira conspiracy. In the 20th century, the state produced influential presidents like Juscelino Kubitschek and Tancredo Neves. It has historically shared political power with São Paulo in a dynamic known as "coffee with milk politics."

### Economy
Minas Gerais has the third-largest GDP in Brazil. Its economy is diversified: the service sector (47.1%) and industry (44.1%) are the largest components. The state is the national leader in mineral extraction, producing the most iron, gold, and zinc. In agriculture, it is the largest producer of coffee (74% of Brazil's Arabica) and milk, and a major producer of sugarcane, soy, and oranges. The automotive and steel industries are also significant.

### Demographics and Culture
The population is a mix of European, African, and Native American ancestry. Roman Catholicism is the dominant religion, though evangelical and Spiritist communities are growing. The culture is marked by traditional cuisine featuring cheese bread ("pão de queijo") and cachaça, colonial architecture in historic cities, and a reserved, family-oriented populace known as "Mineiros."

## Terms
- **Bandeirantes**: 17th-century explorers from São Paulo who ventured into the interior of Brazil in search of gold, gems, and indigenous people to enslave.
- **Captaincy**: A colonial administrative division of Brazil under Portuguese rule.
- **Cerrado**: A vast tropical savanna ecoregion of Brazil, characterized by its biodiversity.
- **Inconfidência Mineira**: The 1789 separatist conspiracy in Minas Gerais against Portuguese rule, led by intellectuals and military officers.
- **Quilombo**: A settlement founded by escaped enslaved Africans in colonial Brazil.
- **The Fifth (O Quinto)**: The 20% tax on all gold and diamonds extracted in colonial Minas Gerais, paid to the Portuguese Crown.
- **Estrada Real**: A network of colonial roads built to transport gold and diamonds from the mines to the ports.
- **Mineiro**: A person born and raised in Minas Gerais; also refers to the local dialect and culture.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The exact origin of the name "Minas Gerais" is debated, with interpretations ranging from "General Mines" to "Mines of the General Woods."
- The ancestral composition of the population is complex; genetic studies show a majority European heritage (59.20%), but the historical social dynamics and racial classifications of the colonial period are subjects of ongoing scholarly analysis.

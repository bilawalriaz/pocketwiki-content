# Zurich

## Overview
Zurich is Switzerland's largest city and a global financial hub, located at the northwestern tip of Lake Zurich. With a history spanning over 2,000 years from a Roman tax post to a modern metropolis, it is a center for banking, education, and culture, significantly influencing Switzerland's economy and politics.

- **1990s** — Pioneering harm reduction strategies dismantle the open drug scene.

## Body

### Early History and Roman Foundations
Evidence of human settlement near Lake Zurich dates back over 6,400 years. The Romans established a castellum and later a customs post named *Turicum* on the Lindenhof hill around 15 BC. After the Roman era, the Alemanni tribe settled the area. In 853, Louis the German founded the Fraumünster abbey, which was granted significant rights, including market and minting rights in 1045, effectively making its abbess the city's ruler.

### Medieval Autonomy and the Reformation
Zurich gained the status of an Imperial free city in 1218. Political power shifted from the abbey to the citizens with the establishment of guild laws in 1336. The city joined the Swiss Confederacy in 1351 but was temporarily expelled from 1440 to 1450 due to the Old Zurich War. In 1519, Huldrych Zwingli launched the Protestant Reformation from the Grossmünster, making Zurich a key Protestant center and leading to religious conflicts within the Confederacy.

### Modern Development and Expansion
The 19th century brought transformative change. The first Swiss railway opened in 1847, and the city's main station became the hub of the national network. Major territorial expansions in 1893 and 1934 incorporated surrounding municipalities. The construction of the Quaianlagen lakefront reshaped the city's geography. In the late 20th century, Zurich addressed a severe drug crisis with innovative harm reduction policies. The canton of Zurich did not legally recognize Jewish communities until 2005.

### Economy and Education
Zurich is Switzerland's economic powerhouse, contributing about 10% to the national GDP. It is a leading global financial center, home to major institutions like UBS and the SIX Swiss Exchange. The city's strength is bolstered by high quality of life and a strong education sector, including the world-renowned ETH Zurich (founded 1854) and the University of Zurich (founded 1833).

### Culture and Society
The official language is Standard German, but the spoken language is Zurich German. Historically Protestant, the city is now religiously diverse, with about 30% Roman Catholic, 26% Swiss Reformed, and over 25% non-affiliated residents. Zurich is known for its high quality of life, extensive public transport, and cultural events like the Street Parade and Zürifäscht festival. It is also a major sports hub, hosting FIFA and the IIHF.

## Terms
- **Imperial immediacy (Reichsunmittelbarkeit):** A status in the Holy Roman Empire where a city was subject only to the emperor, not to a local lord, granting it significant autonomy.
- **Zunftordnung:** The guild laws established in 1336 that restructured Zurich's political system, giving citizens more power.
- **Protestant Reformation:** The 16th-century religious movement against the Catholic Church, led in Zurich by Huldrych Zwingli.
- **Sonderbund War:** A brief civil war in 1847 between Catholic and Protestant cantons, which preceded the formation of the modern Swiss federal state.
- **Harm reduction:** Public health strategies aimed at reducing the negative consequences of drug use, pioneered by Zurich in the 1990s.
- **Temperate oceanic climate (Köppen: Cfb):** A climate characterized by warm summers, cool winters, and precipitation throughout the year.
- **ETH Zurich:** The Swiss Federal Institute of Technology, a world-leading university for science and technology founded in 1854.
- **S-Bahn:** A suburban commuter rail system that forms a key part of Zurich's public transport network.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The origin of the name "Zurich" is interpreted as deriving from a Gaulish personal name, possibly *Tūros*, but this remains a scholarly interpretation.
- The exact nature and extent of the first Jewish community in Zurich, which existed in the 13th century before being persecuted in 1349, is a subject of historical study.
- The effectiveness and implementation of Zurich's "Masterplan Velo" to promote cycling have faced public critique regarding delays and infrastructure goals.

# Ramesses III

## Overview
Ramesses III was the second pharaoh of Egypt's Twentieth Dynasty, reigning during the Late Bronze Age collapse (c. 1185–1154 BC). He is considered the last New Kingdom pharaoh to wield substantial power, successfully defending Egypt against major invasions by the "Sea Peoples" and Libyans. However, the immense cost of these wars, combined with internal strife and economic decline, weakened the state. His reign ended with his assassination in a harem conspiracy, which further accelerated Egypt's decline.

## Timeline
- **c. 1185 BC** — Ramesses III ascends to the throne.
- **Year 5 of reign** — First major campaign against invading Libyan tribesmen in the Western Delta.
- **Year 8 of reign** — Defeats the Sea Peoples in the Battle of Djahy (land) and Battle of the Delta (sea).
- **Year 11 of reign** — Second major campaign against Libyan tribesmen.
- **Year 29 of reign** — First recorded labour strike occurs at Deir el-Medina due to food shortages.
- **1155 BC** — Harem conspiracy led by Queen Tiye and her son Pentawere is uncovered.
- **Year 32 of reign** — Ramesses III is assassinated; succeeded by his designated heir, Ramesses IV.

## Body

### Military Campaigns and Foreign Policy
Ramesses III's reign was defined by constant warfare against foreign invaders. In Year 8, he decisively defeated the "Sea Peoples"—a confederation including groups like the Peleset and Shardana—in two major battles. The naval Battle of the Delta was a strategic victory where Egyptian archers and navy annihilated the enemy fleet. He also fought two significant campaigns against Libyan tribesmen in Years 5 and 11. These victories saved Egypt from immediate collapse but exhausted its treasury. He incorporated some Sea Peoples as subject peoples in southern Canaan, possibly contributing to the later formation of Philistia.

### Economic and Internal Strain
The heavy cost of continuous warfare severely strained Egypt's economy. This culminated in the first recorded labour strike in history during Year 29, when workers at the royal tomb village of Deir el-Medina did not receive their food rations. A climatic event, possibly the Hekla 3 volcanic eruption, may have reduced sunlight and crop yields, exacerbating shortages. Despite these crises, Ramesses III's official monuments project an image of stability and continuity, emulating his predecessor Ramesses II. His massive mortuary temple at Medinet Habu, however, was built with unprecedented fortifications, signaling the era's insecurity.

### Assassination and Succession
Ramesses III was murdered in a harem conspiracy instigated by his secondary wife, Tiye, who sought to place her son Pentawere on the throne instead of the designated heir, Ramesses IV. Trial transcripts reveal a wide plot involving nobles and officials; 38 people were sentenced to death. Forensic analysis of Ramesses III's mummy confirmed he was killed by a deep throat wound, likely from multiple assailants. The conspiracy failed politically—Ramesses IV succeeded him—but the turmoil weakened the dynasty. Genetic testing strongly suggests an unidentified mummy ("Unknown Man E") is Pentawere, who was likely punished with a dishonorable burial.

### Legacy and Construction
Ramesses III commissioned the Great Harris Papyrus, which records his vast donations to temples and his building projects. He constructed one of Egypt's largest and best-preserved mortuary temples at Medinet Habu, decorating it with scenes of his victories. He also began reconstructing the Temple of Khonsu at Karnak. His tomb (KV11) in the Valley of the Kings is among the largest. His mummy, discovered in 1886, is a prototypical example used in popular culture.

## Terms
*   **Sea Peoples:** A confederation of naval raiders who attacked the Eastern Mediterranean civilizations during the Late Bronze Age collapse.
*   **Late Bronze Age collapse:** A widespread societal crisis c. 1200–1150 BC that saw the fall of several major empires.
*   **Ma'at:** The ancient Egyptian concept of truth, balance, order, and justice, central to pharaonic authority.
*   **Harem conspiracy:** The plot to assassinate Ramesses III, led by his wife Tiye to alter the succession.
*   **Deir el-Medina:** The village of artisans and tomb-builders who constructed the royal tombs in the Valley of the Kings.
*   **Hekla 3 eruption:** A major volcanic eruption in Iceland, dated variously, which may have caused climatic cooling affecting Egypt.
*   **Great Harris Papyrus:** A document commissioned by Ramesses IV detailing the reign and achievements of Ramesses III.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Exact Reign Dates:** Scholars debate the precise dates of Ramesses III's reign (e.g., 1185–1154 BC vs. 1187–1156 BC), which affects the chronology of the entire Late Bronze/Iron Age transition in the Levant.
*   **Hekla 3 Eruption Timing:** The date of the Hekla 3 eruption is contested. If it occurred c. 1159 BC, it could explain the economic troubles in Ramesses III's Year 29. However, alternative dates place it well after his reign, disconnecting it from his rule.
*   **Identity of "Unknown Man E":** While genetic and contextual evidence strongly suggests the mummy "Unknown Man E" is the conspirator prince Pentawere, the precise cause of his death remains undetermined.

# Cayman Islands

## Overview
The Cayman Islands is a self-governing British Overseas Territory in the western Caribbean, comprising three islands: Grand Cayman, Cayman Brac, and Little Cayman. It is a major international offshore financial centre and tourism destination, known for its high standard of living and lack of direct taxation.

## Timeline
- **10 May 1503** — Christopher Columbus sights the islands, naming them "Las Tortugas".
- **1670** — England takes formal control via the Treaty of Madrid.
- **1734** — First royal land grant; permanent English-speaking settlement and slavery begin.
- **1794** — The "Wreck of the Ten Sail" incident occurs.
- **1802** — First census records a population of 933, including 545 slaves.
- **1833** — Slavery is abolished in the Cayman Islands.
- **1863** — The islands are officially declared a dependency of Jamaica.
- **1962** — The Cayman Islands become a separate Crown colony after Jamaican independence.
- **1972** — A new constitution grants a large degree of internal autonomy.
- **2004** — Hurricane Ivan causes catastrophic damage to Grand Cayman.
- **2009** — A new constitution incorporating a Bill of Rights is ordained.

## Body

### History and Governance
The islands were likely first sighted by Christopher Columbus in 1503. No evidence of pre-European occupation has been found. Permanent settlement began in the 1730s, following England's formal acquisition of the islands with Jamaica in 1670. The economy was initially based on turtle hunting, seafaring, and slavery. The Cayman Islands were administered as a dependency of Jamaica from 1863 until 1962, when they became a separate British Overseas Territory. The territory has a 19-seat Parliament and a Governor appointed by the British monarch, who retains significant executive and legislative authority.

### Economy and Taxation
The economy is dominated by financial services and tourism, which together account for 50–60% of GDP. The territory is a major offshore financial centre, with over 100,000 companies registered. It has no income, capital gains, or corporation tax, relying instead on indirect taxes like import duties, licensing fees, and a tourist accommodation tax. This tax structure has led to allegations of money laundering, though the government has implemented measures to increase transparency. The GDP per capita was $97,750 in 2023, one of the highest globally.

### Demographics and Society
The population was estimated at 81,546 in 2022, making it the most populous British Overseas Territory. The society is a "melting pot" with immigrants from over 140 countries; 52.5% of the population is non-Caymanian. Jamaicans form the largest immigrant community (24.7%). The population is aging, with low birth rates driving a need for foreign workers. The official language is English, with Cayman Creole also spoken. Christianity is the predominant religion (67% in 2021).

### Geography and Climate
The three islands are the peaks of the submerged Cayman Ridge, mostly flat limestone with coral reefs. The highest point is The Bluff on Cayman Brac at 43 m. The climate is tropical with a wet season from May to October. The islands are highly vulnerable to hurricanes, as demonstrated by the devastation from Hurricane Ivan in 2004.

## Terms
- **British Overseas Territory**: A territory under the jurisdiction and sovereignty of the United Kingdom, but not part of it.
- **Offshore financial centre**: A jurisdiction providing financial services to nonresidents, often with light regulation and low taxation.
- **Tax haven**: A jurisdiction with a low or zero tax regime, used to minimize tax liabilities.
- **Crown colony**: A type of British colonial territory administered by a governor appointed by the monarch.
- **Ironshore**: The prehistoric coral reef lining the coastline that protrudes from the water.
- **Rollover policy**: Immigration policy limiting non-Caymanian workers to a maximum of nine years of residency and work.
- **CARICOM**: The Caribbean Community, a regional organization promoting economic integration among its members.

## Debates and open questions
- The "Wreck of the Ten Sail" legend, which claims King George III promised the islands would never be taxed as a reward for rescuing shipwrecked crews, is not supported by documented evidence.
- The "rollover" immigration policy is controversial, with concerns that it may damage the financial sector's ability to recruit experienced staff, balanced against arguments it protects Caymanian identity and jobs.
- The territory's status as a tax haven has drawn international criticism, including from former U.S. President Barack Obama, who described a specific building as a "tax scam."

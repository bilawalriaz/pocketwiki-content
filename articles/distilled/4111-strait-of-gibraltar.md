# Strait of Gibraltar

## Overview
The Strait of Gibraltar is a narrow waterway connecting the Atlantic Ocean and the Mediterranean Sea, separating the continents of Europe and Africa. It is a critical maritime passage, a site of unique oceanographic phenomena, and a historical and cultural boundary whose formation and ongoing dynamics have shaped regional geology, ecology, and human history.

## Timeline
- **c. 125,000 years ago** — First known human habitation by Neanderthals in the area.
- **c. 24,000 years ago** — Evidence suggests the Rock of Gibraltar was one of the last Neanderthal outposts.
- **c. 40,000 years ago** — Archaeological evidence of *Homo sapiens* habitation.
- **c. 5.97 million years ago** — The connection to the Atlantic closes, triggering the Messinian salinity crisis.
- **c. 5.33 million years ago** — The Zanclean flood reopens the connection, refilling the Mediterranean.
- **8th–11th centuries** — Moors and Berbers cross the strait.
- **1492** — The last Muslim government north of the strait is overthrown, solidifying a cultural divide.
- **1713** — The Treaty of Utrecht cedes Gibraltar to Britain in perpetuity.
- **1928** — Mercedes Gleitze becomes the first known person to swim across the strait.
- **1936** — The "Convoy de la Victoria" breaks a Republican naval blockade during the Spanish Civil War.
- **2003** — Spain and Morocco agree to explore an undersea rail tunnel.

## Body

### Geology and Formation
The strait's seabed is composed of specific clay and sediment layers. Its current depth is primarily due to erosion from the Zanclean flood, which reconnected the Mediterranean to the Atlantic approximately 5.33 million years ago. This flood ended the Messinian salinity crisis, a period beginning around 5.97 million years ago when the strait closed, causing the Mediterranean to partially evaporate and deposit vast salt layers. The strait is expected to close again on geological timescales as the African Plate moves northward.

### Oceanography and Flow
The strait features a continuous two-layer flow: a larger, eastward surface current of less saline Atlantic water and a smaller, deeper westward current of denser, saltier Mediterranean water. This balance exists because Mediterranean evaporation exceeds freshwater input. The Camarinal Sill, the strait's shallowest point, limits mixing and generates powerful internal waves at the density boundary, which can be traced for up to 100 km. During World War II, German U-boats used the currents to enter the Mediterranean undetected.

### History and Culture
The strait has been a crossing point for civilizations since antiquity, including Carthaginians, Romans, Vandals, and Moors. After 1492, it became a barrier that fostered distinct cultures: Christian-European on the northern side and Muslim-Arabic/Mediterranean on the southern side. The British enclave of Gibraltar, ceded in 1713, serves as a strategic naval base. The strait is also a common route for illegal immigration from Africa to Europe.

### Territorial Waters and Law
The strait lies within the territorial waters of Spain, Morocco, and the British Overseas Territory of Gibraltar. Sovereignty over Gibraltar and the Spanish city of Ceuta is disputed. Under the United Nations Convention on the Law of the Sea, the strait is governed by the regime of **transit passage**, granting foreign vessels and aircraft freedom of navigation and overflight for continuous transit.

### Communications and Crossings
Ferries regularly cross the strait. Proposals for a fixed link, such as a bridge or tunnel, have been discussed since the 1980s, with Spain and Morocco agreeing to explore an undersea rail tunnel in 2003. The first known swim was by Mercedes Gleitze in 1928. Other firsts include a powered paraglider crossing in 1995 and a stand-up paddleboard crossing in 2010.

## Terms
- **Transit Passage**: A legal regime under UNCLOS allowing vessels and aircraft continuous and expeditious transit through an international strait.
- **Messinian Salinity Crisis**: A geological event c. 5.97–5.33 million years ago when the Mediterranean's connection to the Atlantic closed, causing massive evaporation and salt deposition.
- **Zanclean Flood**: The catastrophic event c. 5.33 million years ago that refilled the Mediterranean Sea via the Strait of Gibraltar.
- **Camarinal Sill**: The shallowest point in the strait, which limits water mixing and generates internal waves.
- **Thermohaline**: Relating to the combined effects of temperature (thermo) and salinity (haline) on water density.
- **Internal Waves**: Waves that occur within the ocean at the boundary between water layers of different density, rather than at the surface.
- **Flysch**: A sequence of sedimentary rock layers deposited in a deep marine environment.

## Debates and Open Questions
The ownership of Gibraltar and its territorial waters is disputed by Spain. Similarly, Morocco disputes Spanish sovereignty over Ceuta. The feasibility and environmental impact of proposed fixed links, like an undersea tunnel, remain subjects of discussion. The long-term survival of the resident orca pod is threatened by pollution, raising conservation concerns.

# Denver

## Overview
Denver is the capital and most populous city of Colorado, located at the intersection of the Great Plains and the Rocky Mountains. Founded during the 1858 Pikes Peak Gold Rush, it evolved from a frontier mining camp into a major commercial, transportation, and cultural hub for the Mountain West. Its strategic location, high elevation, and role as a government and economic center have shaped its growth and identity.

- **2005** — Denver votes to legalize private marijuana possession for adults, preceding state law.

## Body

### Founding and Early Growth
Denver was founded in 1858 during the Pike's Peak Gold Rush by prospectors from Kansas. The townsite was named for Kansas Territorial Governor James W. Denver. Early settlements like Montana City and Auraria faded as Denver City, staked at the confluence of Cherry Creek and the South Platte River, grew as a supply center for miners. The city's prosperity was secured in 1870 when citizens funded a railroad to connect to the transcontinental line, bypassing Cheyenne, Wyoming. This spurred rapid growth, attracting wealth from mining and leading to the construction of grand hotels and mansions.

### Displacement of Indigenous Peoples
The Denver area was originally inhabited by Arapaho, Cheyenne, and other tribes. The 1851 Treaty of Fort Laramie defined their territory, but the gold rush brought a flood of settlers. The 1861 Treaty of Fort Wise, signed by some chiefs, ceded over 90% of their lands, including Denver. Disagreements over the treaty's validity contributed to the Colorado War (1864-65) and the Sand Creek massacre. Subsequent treaties, like the Medicine Lodge Treaty, relocated the Cheyenne and Arapaho, effectively clearing the Denver area of its indigenous inhabitants by the late 1860s.

### 20th Century to Present
Denver's economy diversified beyond mining into energy, telecommunications, and transportation. The city hosted the 1908 and 2008 Democratic National Conventions. A major turning point was the 1995 opening of Denver International Airport (DIA), one of the world's busiest. The city has a strong outdoor culture and a growing tech sector. Politically, it is a Democratic stronghold. Denver was the first major U.S. city to vote to legalize recreational marijuana (2005) and later decriminalized psilocybin mushrooms (2019).

## Terms
- **Consolidated city-county**: A single government that administers both a city and a county, as Denver does.
- **Front Range Urban Corridor**: The populated region along the eastern slope of the Rocky Mountains in Colorado, with Denver as its economic center.
- **High Plains**: The eastern part of the Great Plains, where Denver is located.
- **Mile High City**: Denver's nickname, referencing its official elevation of exactly one mile (5,280 ft) above sea level.
- **Pikes Peak Gold Rush**: The 1858-1861 gold rush in the Rocky Mountains that led to Denver's founding.
- **Treaty of Fort Wise (1861)**: An agreement where Cheyenne and Arapaho chiefs ceded most of their territory in eastern Colorado, including the Denver area.
- **Sand Creek massacre (1864)**: A U.S. Army attack on a Cheyenne and Arapaho camp, a pivotal event in the Colorado War.
- **Silver Crash of 1893**: A collapse in silver prices that caused a severe depression in Denver and other mining regions.
- **SCFD (Scientific and Cultural Facilities Tax)**: A 0.1% sales tax approved by Denver metro voters in 1988 to fund cultural and scientific organizations.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Validity of the Treaty of Fort Wise**: The source notes that some Cheyenne chiefs opposed the treaty, claiming it was signed by a minority without consent and that signatories were bribed. The territorial government, however, considered it a "solemn obligation."
- **Olympic Bid Legacy**: Denver's withdrawal from hosting the 1976 Winter Olympics, due to voter rejection of public funding, remains a unique historical event. The source states this has made subsequent Olympic bids difficult for the city.
- **Environmental Contamination**: The Rocky Flats Plant (1953-1989) produced plutonium pits for nuclear weapons. A 1981 study linked contamination to increased birth defects and cancer in Denver. The source confirms plutonium contamination was still present outside the plant site as of 2010, posing risks for development.

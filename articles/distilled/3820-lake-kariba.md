# Lake Kariba

## Overview
Lake Kariba is the world's largest artificial lake by volume, created by damming the Zambezi River between Zambia and Zimbabwe. Its creation in the 1960s was a major engineering feat that reshaped the region's geography, ecology, and human settlement, but also caused significant displacement and environmental change.

## Timeline

## Body

### Creation and Human Impact
Lake Kariba was formed by the construction of the Kariba Dam at the northeastern end of the Kariba Gorge on the Zambezi River. The lake was filled between 1958 and 1963. The dam's construction led to the creation of the Zimbabwean town of Kariba for workers. It also caused the displacement of communities, leading to the expansion of settlements such as Binga and Mlibizi in Zimbabwe, and Siavonga and Sinazongwe in Zambia, to house those relocated by the flooding.

### Physical Characteristics
Lake Kariba is over 223 kilometres long and up to 40 kilometres wide, covering an area of 5,580 square kilometres. Its storage capacity is 185 cubic kilometres, making it four times larger by volume than the Three Gorges Dam. The lake has a mean depth of 29 metres and a maximum depth of 97 metres. The immense weight of the water is believed to have induced seismicity in the region, causing over 20 earthquakes greater than magnitude 5. The lake contains several islands, including Maaze, Chete, and Fothergill.

### Ecology
The lake's ecology is vibrant, fueled by nutrients from vegetation inundated during its filling. Key introduced species include the kapenta, a sardine-like fish from Lake Tanganyika, which supports a commercial fishery. Native species like the tigerfish prey on the kapenta, attracting tourism. The shores are home to Nile crocodiles, hippopotamuses, elephants, and other big game. The southern Matusadona National Park was historically a refuge for black and white rhinoceros, but poaching has severely reduced their numbers.

### Protected Status
The portion of Lake Kariba within Zimbabwe is designated a recreational park within the Zimbabwe Parks and Wildlife Estate.

## Terms
- **Artificial lake/reservoir**: A large, human-made body of water, typically created by damming a river.
- **Induced seismicity**: Earthquakes triggered by human activities, such as the filling of a large reservoir.
- **Kapenta**: A small, sardine-like fish introduced to Lake Kariba from Lake Tanganyika, now central to its fishery.
- **Tigerfish**: A predatory native fish species of the Zambezi system that thrives in the lake.
- **Inundated vegetation**: Plant life submerged and decomposing under the lake's waters.

## Debates and Open questions
The source notes that the enormous mass of water in Lake Kariba is *believed* to have caused induced seismicity in the seismically active region, indicating this is a proposed but not definitively proven causal link.

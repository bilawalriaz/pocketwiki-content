# Non-fiction

## Overview
Non-fiction is a category of prose writing that, in good faith, conveys information about the real world rather than imaginary events, aiming to present topics objectively based on historical, scientific, and empirical evidence. While some non-fiction ventures into subjective territory through sincerely held opinions, it remains distinct from fiction by its commitment to truthfulness at the time of composition. The genre matters because it underpins education, journalism, scholarship, and public discourse, serving as the primary means by which factual knowledge is recorded, analyzed, and transmitted across societies.

## Timeline
- **Ancient times** — Emergence of historical writing (e.g., Herodotus) and life writing (e.g., autobiographies)
- **Medieval–Renaissance** — Development of reference works (encyclopedias, dictionaries) and scholarly treatises
- **17th–18th centuries** — Rise of the essay (Montaigne, Addison) and periodical literature
- **19th century** — Expansion of journalism, popular science, and self-help literature
- **Early 20th century** — Formal distinction between fiction and non-fiction in publishing
- **Mid-20th century** — Growth of academic publishing and textbook production
- **Late 20th century** — Coinage and popularization of "creative nonfiction" as a literary category

## Body

### Descriptions and Characteristics
Non-fiction writers employ techniques such as comparison, classification, summarization, and logical or chronological ordering to convey information. They may use graphic and structural aids—charts, diagrams, glossaries, timelines, headings, and captions—to help readers navigate content. Simplicity, clarity, and directness are paramount, as the goal is the direct provision of information rather than indirect thematic interpretation. Audience awareness is critical: authors must account for readers' purposes and prior knowledge. Despite claims to truth, persuasion remains essential, requiring balanced, coherent, and informed argumentation. The boundaries between fiction and non-fiction are frequently debated, particularly in biography, where the challenge lies in reconciling factual accuracy with narrative coherence.

### Blurring Boundaries and Ethical Considerations
Including knowingly false information is generally considered dishonest in non-fiction. However, some works—such as personal journals, letters, and magazine articles—can legitimately function as either fiction or non-fiction. Semi-fiction incorporates substantial non-fictional elements into fictional frameworks, while some non-fiction may include unverified supposition or imaginative reconstruction to smooth narrative flow. The inclusion of open falsehoods, however, discredits a work as non-fiction. The term "creative nonfiction" distinguishes works with literary or intellectual merit from more utilitarian non-fiction subjects.

### Types and Genres
Non-fiction genres are classified by authorial intention or content purpose: instructional, explanatory, discussion-based, report-based (non-chronological), opinion-based (persuasive), and relating (chronological recounting). These genres manifest across formats including academic texts (scholarly papers, monographs, journals), history books, life writings (autobiographies, memoirs, diaries), literary and media criticism, news stories and documentaries, persuasive essays and manifestos, reference works (encyclopedias, dictionaries, atlases), self-help and popular science books, and practical guides (textbooks, manuals, recipes, travelogues).

## Terms
- **Creative nonfiction**: Literary non-fiction distinguished by stylistic or intellectual merit, often employing narrative techniques while maintaining factual accuracy.
- **Semi-fiction**: Fiction that incorporates a significant amount of non-fictional content, such as stories based on true events.
- **Life writing**: A genre encompassing autobiographies, biographies, memoirs, diaries, letters, and other personal narratives.
- **Expository writing**: Non-fiction prose that explains, describes, or informs about factual topics.
- **Instructional non-fiction**: Works designed to teach or guide the reader in performing tasks or understanding processes.
- **Report-based non-fiction**: Non-chronological presentation of information, such as news reports or academic studies.
- **Opinion-based non-fiction**: Persuasive writing that presents the author's viewpoint on real-world topics, including essays and polemics.

## Debates and Open Questions
The boundary between fiction and non-fiction remains contested, particularly in biography, where the tension between factual fidelity and narrative artistry raises questions about representation and truth. The legitimacy of blending imaginative reconstruction with factual content in non-fiction narratives is debated, especially regarding the use of unverified supposition to enhance readability. The classification of hybrid works—those that could be either fiction or non-fiction—challenges rigid genre distinctions. Additionally, the role of persuasion in ostensibly objective non-fiction raises questions about the extent to which advocacy compromises truth claims. The definition and scope of "creative nonfiction" continues to evolve, reflecting ongoing negotiations between literary craft and factual integrity.

# South Pole

## Overview
The South Pole is the southernmost point on Earth, where the planet's axis of rotation meets its surface in the continent of Antarctica. It is a site of extreme climate, significant scientific research, and historic exploration, marked by the permanent Amundsen–Scott South Pole Station. Its unique geography, with all directions pointing north and a constantly moving ice sheet, presents distinct challenges for navigation and habitation.

## Timeline
- **14 December 1911** — Roald Amundsen and his Norwegian party become the first to reach the Geographic South Pole.
- **17 January 1912** — Robert Falcon Scott and his British party reach the Pole, 34 days after Amundsen.
- **29 November 1929** — Richard Evelyn Byrd becomes the first person to fly over the South Pole.
- **31 October 1956** — Admiral George J. Dufek's US Navy party lands at the Pole, the first humans there since 1912.
- **1956–1957** — The US Amundsen–Scott South Pole Station is established and has been continuously staffed since.
- **4 January 1958** — Edmund Hillary reaches the Pole overland during the Commonwealth Trans-Antarctic Expedition.
- **1969** — The first group of women reaches the South Pole.
- **30 December 1989** — Arved Fuchs and Reinhold Messner make the first traverse of Antarctica via the Pole without animal or motorized help.
- **2024** — Vincent Colliard sets the record for the fastest unsupported journey to the Pole from the ocean (22 days, 6 hours, 8 minutes).

## Body

### Geography and Location
The Geographic South Pole is defined as the southern point where Earth's axis intersects its surface, located at 90°S latitude. Its longitude is undefined, as all lines of longitude converge there. It sits on a high, barren ice plateau in Antarctica at an altitude of about 2,835 meters (9,301 ft). The ice sheet, roughly 2,700–3,200 meters thick, is slowly moving about 10 meters per year, requiring the geographic marker to be repositioned annually. A separate Ceremonial South Pole, marked with a metallic sphere and flags, exists for photographs.

### Exploration
Early 20th-century exploration was driven by the race to the Pole. After several attempts by British expeditions led by Scott and Shackleton, the Norwegian Roald Amundsen's party first reached it in December 1911. Scott's party arrived in January 1912 but perished on the return journey. After a gap, the Pole was reached by air in 1929 and by land again in 1956. Subsequent decades saw numerous expeditions, including the first women in 1969, first unsupported traverses, and ongoing records for speed and endurance.

### Climate and Environment
The South Pole has an ice cap climate (Köppen EF), characterized by extreme cold, low precipitation, and near-zero humidity. It experiences six months of continuous darkness in winter and six months of daylight in summer, with the sun never rising high in the sky. The mean annual temperature is –49.5 °C (–57.1 °F). The highest recorded temperature was –12.3 °C (9.9 °F) in 2011, and the lowest was –82.8 °C (–117.0 °F) in 1982. Due to the harsh conditions, there are no native plants or animals, though microbes have been found in the ice.

### Time and Logistics
No time zone is defined for the South Pole. For practical reasons, the Amundsen–Scott Station keeps New Zealand Time (UTC+12/13), aligning with its supply chain from Christchurch. The station's buildings are raised on stilts to prevent burial by accumulating snow, which averages about 7 cm per year.

## Terms
- **Geographic South Pole**: The precise point where Earth's rotational axis meets the surface, at 90°S latitude.
- **Ceremonial South Pole**: A marked area near the geographic pole for photographs, featuring a sphere and flags of Antarctic Treaty signatories.
- **Antarctic Treaty**: An international agreement designating Antarctica as a scientific preserve and banning military activity.
- **Polar motion**: Small wobbles in Earth's axis of rotation, affecting precise definitions of the poles.
- **Grid north**: A directional reference at the Pole, pointing north along the prime meridian.
- **Ice sheet**: A persistent body of dense ice covering a landmass, in this case, Antarctica.
- **Köppen climate classification EF**: A climate type for ice cap regions, where the mean temperature of the warmest month is below 0 °C.

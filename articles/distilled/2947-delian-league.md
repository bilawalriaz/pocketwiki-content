# Delian League

## Overview
The Delian League was a confederacy of Greek city-states founded in 478 BC under Athenian leadership to continue the war against the Persian Empire. It began as a voluntary alliance of autonomous states but gradually transformed into an Athenian-dominated empire, a shift that fundamentally altered the balance of power in Greece and contributed to the outbreak of the Peloponnesian War.

## Timeline
- **478 BC** — Formation of the Delian League on the island of Delos after Sparta's withdrawal from the Persian Wars.
- **c. 471 BC** — Naxos becomes the first member to attempt secession; is defeated and forced back into the League.
- **465 BC** — Thasos revolts over Athenian colonial expansion; after a siege, it is forcibly rejoined, marking the League's shift to a hegemony.
- **461 BC** — Ostracism of Cimon signals a shift in Athenian policy towards democratic imperialism and conflict with Sparta.
- **454 BC** — Pericles moves the League treasury from Delos to Athens, a key step in its transformation into an empire.
- **460-454 BC** — Failed Athenian-led intervention in the Egyptian revolt against Persia results in heavy losses.
- **451 BC** — Cimon dies during a campaign against Persia in Cyprus; a major victory is won, leading to a de facto peace with Persia.
- **447 BC** — Battle of Coronea ends Athenian control of Boeotia; the Thirty Years' Peace is signed with the Peloponnesian League.
- **431 BC** — Outbreak of the Peloponnesian War, triggered in part by the threat of the Delian League to Spartan hegemony.
- **404 BC** — Defeat of Athens in the Peloponnesian War leads to the dissolution of the League.
- **377 BC** — Athens founds the Second Athenian League to counter Spartan power.

## Body

### Formation and Purpose
The League was founded in 478 BC after the Greek victory in the Persian Wars. Its stated aim was to "avenge the wrongs" suffered by ravaging Persian territory. Members could contribute armed forces or pay a monetary tribute to a common treasury; most chose the latter. An oath of loyalty bound the allies, who swore to have the same friends and enemies as Athens.

### Transformation into Empire
From its inception, Athens was the dominant military power. Over time, it used League funds for its own purposes, such as building the Parthenon, and forced members to pay tribute in cash rather than provide ships. This led to conflicts and rebellions, such as those of Naxos and Thasos, which Athens suppressed. The moving of the treasury to Athens in 454 BC symbolized the League's evolution from a voluntary alliance into the Athenian Empire, where allies lost autonomy and were referred to as "all the cities Athens rules."

### Military Campaigns and Conflicts
The League successfully expelled Persian forces from the Aegean. However, Athens's imperial policies led to internal wars. The First Peloponnesian War (c. 460-445 BC) saw Athens gain and then lose control of Boeotia, culminating in the Thirty Years' Peace. The Peloponnesian War (431-404 BC) erupted due to the threat the League posed to Spartan hegemony and Athens's heavy-handed rule. The war ended with the League's dissolution after Athens's defeat.

### Administration and Control
Athens maintained control through several mechanisms. It established *kleruchiai* (quasi-colonies that served as garrisons), appointed officials like *proxenoi* (to foster relations) and *episkopoi* (to oversee tribute), and used its courts to adjudicate disputes between member states. Revolts were brutally punished; for example, after the revolt of Mytilene, Athens executed the ringleaders and redistributed the land.

## Terms
- **Symmachia**: A defensive and offensive alliance of autonomous states, the formal structure of the Delian League.
- **Hegemony**: Leadership or dominance of one state over others, which Athens gradually exercised over the League.
- **Tribute**: The monetary payment most League members contributed to the common treasury instead of military forces.
- **Kleruchiai**: Quasi-colonies established by Athens in allied territory, remaining tied to the mother city and serving as military garrisons.
- **Proxenoi**: Officials appointed by Athens to maintain good relations with League members.
- **Episkopoi**: Athenian officials who oversaw the collection of tribute from League members.
- **Hellenotamiai**: Treasurers who received tribute on Athens' behalf.
- **Ostracism**: A democratic procedure in Athens where a citizen could be exiled for ten years, used to remove political rivals like Cimon.

## Debates and Open Questions
The exact nature and date of the "Peace of Callias," a treaty supposedly formalizing peace with Persia around 450 BC, is debated; some historians consider it a later myth. The role of the "Delian Proclamation," a possible founding document discovered at Delphi, is also contested, with some scholars linking it to religious rather than purely political foundations. The extent to which the League's transformation into an empire was a deliberate Athenian policy from the start, rather than a gradual evolution, remains a subject of scholarly analysis.

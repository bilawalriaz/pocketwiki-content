# Stephen the Great

## Overview
Stephen the Great (r. 1457–1504) was the Voivode of Moldavia whose 47-year reign marked a period of stability, military prowess, and cultural flourishing. He is celebrated for his astute diplomacy and military campaigns that preserved Moldavian autonomy against the Ottoman Empire, Poland, and Hungary, making him a national hero in Romania and Moldova and a canonized saint of the Romanian Orthodox Church.

## Timeline
- **1451** — Stephen's father, Bogdan II, is assassinated; Stephen flees to Transylvania.
- **1457** — Stephen defeats his rival Peter Aaron at the Battle of Orbic and becomes Voivode of Moldavia.
- **1465** — Stephen captures the important Danube port of Chilia from Hungary.
- **1475** — Stephen inflicts a major defeat on a large Ottoman army at the Battle of Vaslui.
- **1476** — Stephen is routed by Ottoman Sultan Mehmed II at the Battle of Valea Albă.
- **1484** — The Ottomans capture Moldavia's key ports, Chilia and Cetatea Albă.
- **1497** — Stephen and his allies defeat a Polish army at the Battle of the Cosmin Forest.
- **1503** — Stephen formally acknowledges the loss of his ports to the Ottomans.
- **1504** — Stephen dies on 2 July and is buried at Putna Monastery.
- **1992** — Stephen is canonized by the Romanian Orthodox Church.

## Body

### Early Life and Accession
Stephen was the son of Bogdan II, who seized the Moldavian throne in 1449. After his father was murdered in 1451, Stephen took refuge in Transylvania. With the support of Vlad the Impaler, he invaded Moldavia in 1457, defeated his rival Peter Aaron, and was acclaimed voivode. He immediately established his authority by attacking Poland to prevent it from supporting Aaron, though he later acknowledged Polish suzerainty in 1459 to secure peace.

### Military Conflicts and Diplomacy
Stephen's reign was defined by constant warfare to defend Moldavia's autonomy. His primary strategic goal was to secure the Danube ports of Chilia and Cetatea Albă, which he captured from Hungary in 1465. This brought him into conflict with multiple powers. He defeated a Hungarian invasion at the Battle of Baia in 1467. In the 1470s, he stopped paying tribute to the Ottoman Empire and launched campaigns in Wallachia to install friendly rulers. This provoked a major Ottoman invasion, which Stephen decisively defeated at the Battle of Vaslui in 1475, earning him the title *Athleta Christi* ("Champion of Christ") from the Pope. However, he was later routed by Mehmed II in 1476. The Ottomans ultimately captured Chilia and Cetatea Albă in 1484, dealing a severe blow to Moldavian commerce. Stephen's later years saw conflicts with Poland, culminating in his victory at the Battle of the Cosmin Forest in 1497.

### Internal Policy and Cultural Legacy
Stephen strengthened central authority by suppressing rebellious boyars and building a network of fortresses. He employed mercenaries and reformed the military, enabling him to field large armies. Culturally, his reign saw a flourishing of architecture and literature. He commissioned dozens of stone churches and monasteries, developing a distinctive "Moldavian style" that blended Byzantine and Gothic elements. He also sponsored the creation of chronicles and religious texts, contributing to the development of historiography and Church Slavonic literature in Moldavia.

### Family and Succession
Stephen married several times to secure political alliances, including with Evdochia of Kiev and Maria of Mangup. He had numerous children, both legitimate and illegitimate. His designated successor, Alexandru, died in 1496. Stephen was eventually succeeded by his son Bogdan III, who had served as co-ruler in his final years.

## Terms
- **Voivode**: A title for a military commander or ruler, used for the princes of Moldavia and Wallachia.
- **Suzerainty**: A relationship where a weaker state acknowledges the overlordship of a stronger one, often in exchange for protection, while retaining internal autonomy.
- **Haraç**: The annual tribute paid by vassal states to the Ottoman Empire.
- **Boyars**: The landed aristocracy and nobility in medieval Moldavia and Wallachia.
- **Pârcălab**: The commander of a fortress, who also held administrative and judicial powers.
- **Armaș**: A central office established to oversee the work of the fortress commanders.
- **Moldovenism**: A cultural and political concept emphasizing a distinct Moldovan identity separate from Romanian identity.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The exact circumstances of Stephen's election or acclamation as voivode in 1457 are debated, with some historians considering the story of a boyar assembly at Direptate to be a later legend.
- The nature of the relationship between Stephen and the Ecumenical Patriarchate of Constantinople is disputed, particularly whether his metropolitan was a loyalist or a dissenter.
- The precise number and identity of Stephen's wives and children, especially his early sons, remains uncertain due to conflicting historical sources.

# Altai Mountains

## Overview
The Altai Mountains are a major range in Central Asia where Russia, China, Mongolia, and Kazakhstan meet. They are a globally significant region for biodiversity, geological history, and human prehistory, hosting a complete sequence of ecological zones and evidence of ancient hominid habitation.

## Timeline
- **c. 3700–3300 BCE** — Afanasievo culture, early herders, migrates to the region from the Eurasian Steppe.
- **c. 2nd millennium BC** — The Seima-Turbino Phenomenon, a rapid migration of peoples, originates in the Altai.
- **40,000 years ago** — Denisovan hominins, a branch of hominids, inhabit the Denisova Cave.
- **33,000 years ago** — A dog-like canid, a potential early dog ancestor, is found in the Razboinichya Cave.
- **Late Bronze Age** — The Afanasievo culture is replaced by peoples from the Andronovo culture.
- **Western Han Dynasty (206 BC–24 AD)** — The oldest known text describing skiing refers to skiers in the Altai Mountains.
- **2008** — The Denisova hominin is discovered in the Denisova Cave.
- **2003** — A magnitude 7.3 earthquake devastates the Chuya Basin area.
- **2009** — The "Altaigate Scandal" involves allegations of poaching and corruption.

## Body

### Geography and Geology
The Altai system covers 845,000 km² and stretches 2,525 km. Its highest peak is Belukha at 4,506 m. The range is geologically active, representing the northernmost region affected by the tectonic collision of India into Asia. It features extensive fault systems and was the site of cataclysmic glacial lake outburst floods. The region is a UNESCO World Heritage Site, the "Golden Mountains of Altai," recognized for its complete altitudinal vegetation zones and endangered fauna.

### Fauna and Climate
The Altai-Sayan ecoregion is an intersection of Central Asian and Siberian fauna, with habitats ranging from steppes to alpine zones. It has retained a remarkably stable climate and an ice age fauna, including species like the snow leopard, argali, and Siberian ibex. Historically, it was also home to the Caspian tiger and wisent. The region's low average temperatures have preserved ancient DNA exceptionally well.

### Human History and Culture
The Altai is a key site for human prehistory. The Denisova Cave yielded remains of Denisovans, Neanderthals, and *Homo sapiens*, making it the only known location where all three hominids lived. The area was later home to the Afanasievo culture, early Indo-European migrants, and Eastern Scythians. It was part of successive empires, including the Xiongnu and Turkic Khaganates, leading to the cultural and linguistic Turkicization of the local population. The region is also a candidate for the birthplace of skiing, based on ancient petroglyphs and historical texts.

## Terms
- **Denisovan**: An extinct branch of hominids, contemporaries of Neanderthals and modern humans, known primarily from DNA and artifacts found in the Denisova Cave.
- **Afanasievo culture**: The earliest known herders in East Asia, descended from migrants who crossed the Eurasian Steppe c. 3700–3300 BCE.
- **Seima-Turbino Phenomenon**: A Bronze Age cultural enigma involving a rapid, massive migration of peoples from the Altai region into Europe and Asia.
- **Turkicization**: The process by which the local population of the Altai became culturally and linguistically Turkic.
- **Altitudinal vegetation zones**: The complete sequence of ecological zones found in the mountains, from steppe to alpine vegetation.
- **Glacial lake outburst flood**: A catastrophic release of water from a glacier-dammed lake, with evidence of such events found in the Altai.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Origin of skiing**: Some historians believe the Altai may be where skiing was born, based on ancient petroglyphs and a Western Han Dynasty text. This claim remains disputed due to the difficulty of accurately dating rock art.
- **Denisovan legacy**: Knowledge of Denisovans comes mainly from DNA and artifacts, as no complete skeletons have been found. Their exact relationship to other hominids and their full range are still being researched.
- **Altaigate Scandal**: The 2009 helicopter crash that killed Russian VIPs led to allegations of poaching and corruption, raising questions about the enforcement of protected area status in the region.

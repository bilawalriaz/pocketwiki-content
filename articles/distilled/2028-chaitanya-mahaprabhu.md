# Chaitanya Mahaprabhu

## Overview
Chaitanya Mahaprabhu (1486–1534) was a Bengali Hindu saint and the founder of Gaudiya Vaishnavism. He is revered as an incarnation of Krishna, sometimes as the combined avatar of Radha and Krishna. His life and teachings popularized the congregational chanting of the Hare Krishna mantra and established a devotional (bhakti) movement that profoundly influenced Vaishnavism in Eastern India and beyond.

## Timeline
- **18 February 1486** — Born as Vishvambhara Mishra in Nabadwip, Bengal, during a lunar eclipse.
- **c. 1508–1509** — Visits Gaya; initiated by Īśvara Purī; experiences spiritual awakening.
- **1510** — Takes sannyāsa (monastic vows) at Katwa; given the name Śrī Kṛṣṇa Caitanya.
- **1510s** — Travels to Puri, Odisha, which becomes his headquarters.
- **c. 1512–1514** — Extensive travels through southern India; meets Rāmānanda Rāya.
- **c. 1514** — Meets future disciples Rūpa and Sanātana Gosvāmī at Ramakeli.
- **c. 1515** — Spends two months in Vrindavan, instructing key disciples.
- **c. 1528–1534** — Dies in Puri; accounts of his passing vary between natural death and miraculous disappearance.

## Body

### Early Life and Education
Born to Jagannātha Miśra and Śacidevī, Chaitanya was the tenth child and was nicknamed Nimai (for being born under a neem tree) and Gauranga (for his fair complexion). He was a prodigious scholar, mastering Sanskrit grammar and Navya-nyāya (neo-logic) and establishing his own seminary by age sixteen. He married Lakṣmīpriyā, and later, after her death, Viṣṇupriyā. His early life was marked by intellectual pride, which he later renounced.

### Spiritual Awakening and the Sankirtan Movement
Around 1508–1509, after a spiritual awakening in Gaya, Chaitanya abandoned his scholarly life for ecstatic devotion to Krishna. He closed his school and began leading public congregational chanting (saṃkīrtana) with associates like Nityānanda and Advaita Ācārya. This movement faced opposition from the local Muslim ruler, Chand Kazi, but Chaitanya's peaceful protest led to the ruler's conversion to the cause. To spread his message more widely and overcome social barriers, he entered the renounced order of life (sannyāsa) in 1510.

### Teachings and Later Life
Chaitanya's core teaching was that kirtana (chanting God's name) is the most effective spiritual practice in the current age (Kali Yuga). He revealed bhakti (devotion) as the primary path to Krishna, emphasizing prema (divine love) that transcends social distinctions. He composed the Śikṣāṣṭaka, eight devotional prayers. After settling in Puri, he spent his final decades teaching, engaging in kirtana, and experiencing intense devotional ecstasies. He instructed key disciples like Rūpa and Sanātana Gosvāmī, who later systematized Gaudiya Vaishnava theology.

### Biographies and Legacy
The primary source for Chaitanya's life is the hagiographical tradition of Gaudiya Vaishnavism. The canonical narrative was established in the early 1600s by Kṛṣṇadāsa Kavirāja's *Caitanya Caritāmṛta*. Earlier biographies include works by Vrindavana Dasa Thakura and Murari Gupta. His legacy is a socio-religious revival that produced a vast philosophical and literary tradition, and he remains a central figure in Bengali culture, inspiring films and ongoing devotional practice.

## Terms
- **Gaudiya Vaishnavism**: The Vaishnava tradition founded by Chaitanya Mahaprabhu, centered on devotion to Krishna.
- **Saṃkīrtana**: Congregational chanting of the names of God, especially the Hare Krishna mantra.
- **Bhakti**: Devotional love and surrender to a personal God, in this context, Krishna.
- **Sannyāsa**: The Hindu monastic stage of life, involving renunciation of worldly ties.
- **Navya-nyāya**: A school of Hindu logic and epistemology that flourished in Bengal.
- **Prema**: Divine, selfless love for God, considered the highest stage of devotion.
- **Achintya Bheda Abheda**: The Vedantic philosophy of "inconceivable simultaneous difference and non-difference" between the individual soul and God, which Chaitanya is considered the chief proponent of.
- **Hare Krishna Maha-mantra**: The sixteen-word mantra central to Gaudiya Vaishnava practice: "Hare Krishna, Hare Krishna, Krishna Krishna, Hare Hare / Hare Rama, Hare Rama, Rama Rama, Hare Hare."

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Nature of his passing**: Biographies differ; some, like Jayananda's *Caitanya-Mangala*, mention a natural death, while others, like the *Caitanya Caritāmṛta*, are silent or suggest a miraculous disappearance into the Jagannath deity.
- **Authenticity of early biographies**: The date and authenticity of Murari Gupta's *Śrī Kṛṣṇa Caitanya Caritāmṛtam* (c. 1513) are disputed due to its inclusion of later events, suggesting possible interpolation.
- **Theological identity**: Hagiographies portray him variously as an avatāra of Krishna, Svayaṁ Bhagavān (God Himself), or the combined form of Radha-Krishna, reflecting ongoing theological interpretation within the tradition.

# Astana

## Overview
Astana is the capital city of Kazakhstan, a planned urban center that became the seat of government in 1997. Its rapid transformation from a regional town into a modern metropolis reflects Kazakhstan's post-Soviet development and its strategic positioning as a hub in Central Asia.

## Timeline
- **1830** — Settlement of Akmoly founded on the Ishim River.
- **1832** — Granted town status and renamed Akmolinsk.
- **1961** — Renamed Tselinograd ("City of Virgin Lands") under the Soviet Virgin Lands Campaign.
- **1991** — Renamed Akmola after Kazakhstan's independence.
- **Dec 1997** — Replaces Almaty as the capital of Kazakhstan.
- **May 1998** — Renamed Astana ("capital" in Kazakh).
- **2019** — Renamed Nur-Sultan in honor of former President Nursultan Nazarbayev.
- **Sep 2022** — Name reverted to Astana by constitutional amendment.
- **Jun 2017** — Hosts Expo 2017 with the theme "Future Energy."
- **May 2026** — First line of the Astana Light Metro (Tarlan Astana) opens.

## Body

### Names and History
The city was founded in 1830 as Akmoly, a military outpost. Its name evolved through Akmolinsk, Tselinograd (1961), and Akmola (1991) before becoming Astana in 1998. In 2019, it was renamed Nur-Sultan, but reverted to Astana in 2022. The city holds a Guinness World Record for the most name changes for a modern capital.

The territory has ancient roots, with the Bozok settlement, a Silk Road fortress, located on its site. The modern city grew from a small Russian colonial town. During the Soviet era, it became the administrative center for the Virgin Lands Campaign in the 1950s, leading to major development and a population influx. After independence, the government decided to move the capital from Almaty to Akmola in 1997, citing its central location, space for growth, and strategic advantages. The city was then master-planned by Japanese architect Kisho Kurokawa.

### Geography and Climate
Astana lies on the Ishim River in a flat, semi-arid steppe. It is one of the world's coldest capitals, with an extreme continental climate. Winters are long and severe, with temperatures commonly reaching −30 to −35 °C, while summers can be warm. The city's elevation is 347 meters above sea level.

### Demographics
As of January 2025, the population is 1,528,703, having more than tripled since 2002. The ethnic composition has shifted dramatically; as of 2026, ethnic Kazakhs make up 83% of the population, up from about 18% in 1989. The city is a magnet for migrants from across Kazakhstan and neighboring states.

### Economy
Astana's economy is driven by trade, construction, transport, and finance. It is a major hub for the Belt and Road Initiative and hosts the Astana International Financial Centre (AIFC). The city is the headquarters for major state-owned corporations. The shift of the capital spurred massive economic growth, with investments and industrial output increasing many times over. Tourism is also a growing sector.

### Cityscape and Architecture
The city is divided into four districts. Its modern core, south of the Ishim River, was designed by Kisho Kurokawa to blend history and future. Key landmarks include the presidential residence Akorda, the 105-meter Baiterek observation tower, the Khan Shatyr entertainment center (the world's largest tent), and the Palace of Peace and Reconciliation. The Astana Grand Mosque, completed in 2022, is the largest in Central Asia.

### Transportation
Public transport includes buses and taxis. A light rail transit system, the Tarlan Astana, opened its first line in 2026. Nursultan Nazarbayev International Airport is the city's main air gateway. The city is a key node in national rail and road networks.

### Education and Sport
Astana is home to several universities, including the L.N. Gumilyov Eurasian National University and the autonomous Nazarbayev University. The city hosts professional sports teams in football, ice hockey, basketball, and cycling, and has a presidential sports club. It has hosted major events like the 2011 Asian Winter Games.

## Terms
- **Okrug/Uyezd:** Administrative divisions of the Russian Empire.
- **Virgin Lands Campaign:** A Soviet agricultural project in the 1950s to cultivate steppe regions for grain production.
- **Oblast:** A region or province in some Slavic countries.
- **Akimat:** The local executive body of a city in Kazakhstan.
- **Belt and Road Initiative:** A global infrastructure development strategy adopted by the Chinese government.
- **Astana International Financial Centre (AIFC):** A financial hub in Astana established to attract international investment.
- **Köppen climate classification:** A system for classifying the world's climates.

## Debates and open questions
- The etymology of the city's original name, Akmola ("white grave" or "white tomb"), is noted as a theory that is "not universally accepted."
- The decision to move the capital from Almaty was based on several stated advantages, but the long-term socio-economic impacts of this massive relocation continue to unfold.

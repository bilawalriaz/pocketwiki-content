# Guangdong

## Overview
Guangdong is a coastal province in South China, the country's most populous and economically powerful provincial-level division. Its strategic South China Sea location, proximity to Hong Kong, and historical role as a trade and migration gateway have made it a global manufacturing hub and cultural center for Cantonese, Hakka, and Teochew communities worldwide.

## Timeline
- **c. 5050–3050 BC** — Neolithic era begins in the Pearl River Delta.
- **226 CE** — Guangdong is made its own province (Guang Province) under the Wu Kingdom.
- **627** — Guangdong and Guangxi become part of Lingnan Circuit during the Tang dynasty.
- **971** — The Guangdong part of Lingnan Circuit is renamed Guangnan East Circuit, the source of the name "Guangdong."
- **1279** — The Battle of Yamen in Guangdong marks the end of the Southern Song dynasty.
- **1557** — Macau, on Guangdong's coast, becomes the first European settlement.
- **1839–1842** — The First Opium War is triggered by opium trade through Guangzhou.
- **1920s** — Guangdong is the staging area for the Kuomintang's Northern Expedition.
- **1978** — Deng Xiaoping's "Reform and Opening Up" policy begins, radically transforming Guangdong's economy.
- **1988** — Hainan Island is separated from Guangdong to become its own province.
- **2005** — Guangdong officially becomes China's most populous province.

## Body

### Name and Etymology
The name "Guangdong" (廣東) means "East of Guang." "Guang" (廣) means "wide" or "vast," associated with the region since the creation of Guang Prefecture in AD 226. The name derives from Guangxin, a Han dynasty outpost. During the Song dynasty, the region was formally divided into "Guangnan East Circuit" (Guangdong) and "Guangnan West Circuit" (Guangxi). The term "Canton," derived from the Portuguese transliteration of "Guangdong," often refers specifically to the capital, Guangzhou.

### History
Originally inhabited by the Baiyue ("Hundred Yue") tribes, the region first became part of China during the Qin dynasty. It was later the independent Nanyue kingdom before being administered by the Han dynasty. The area was a prosperous port trading with Persians, Arabs, and others, though its importance fluctuated due to raids and corruption. Massive migrations from northern China during periods of turmoil gradually shifted the demographics toward Han Chinese dominance.

From the 16th century, Guangdong had extensive trade links with European merchants. The opium trade through Guangzhou triggered the First Opium War, leading to the cession of Hong Kong to Britain and the establishment of a French colony at Kouang-Tchéou-Wan (modern Zhanjiang). Due to large-scale emigration, many overseas Chinese communities, particularly in Southeast Asia and the West, have origins in Guangdong. The province was also the birthplace of Sun Yat-sen, the founder of the Republic of China.

In the 20th century, Guangdong was a base for the Kuomintang's Northern Expedition and later became a final Nationalist stronghold before the Communist takeover. After 1978, Deng Xiaoping's reforms allowed Guangdong to leverage its coastal access and links to Hong Kong and overseas Chinese, leading to explosive economic growth and the creation of Special Economic Zones like Shenzhen.

### Geography and Climate
Guangdong faces the South China Sea with a 4,300 km coastline. The Pearl River Delta, formed by the East, North, and West Rivers, is a densely populated megalopolis. The province is separated from the north by the Nan Mountains, with its highest peak being Shikengkong at 1,900 meters. It has a humid subtropical climate with short, mild winters and long, hot, wet summers.

### Economy
Guangdong has China's largest provincial GDP (CN¥14.16 trillion in 2024), contributing about 10.5% of mainland China's output. Its economy transformed from an "economic backwater" under autarky to a global manufacturing and trade powerhouse after the 1978 reforms. It is China's largest exporter and importer, with a diversified economy centered on the Pearl River Delta Economic Zone. The province hosts the Canton Fair, the largest import and export fair in China, and is home to numerous Special Economic and Technological Development Zones.

### Demographics and Languages
Guangdong is China's most populous province, with a population of 126,012,510 in the 2020 census. This includes a massive "floating population" of migrants drawn by economic opportunities. The province is a "treasure trove of regional languages." The majority are Han Chinese, with major linguistic groups including:
*   **Yue Chinese (Cantonese):** Spoken by over 35 million in the province, it is the dominant language in the Pearl River Delta and has global influence.
*   **Hakka Chinese:** Spoken by about 20 million, primarily in the northeastern highlands.
*   **Min Chinese:** Includes Teoswa (Chaoshan Min) in the east and Leizhou Min on the Leizhou Peninsula.
Mandarin is used in education and government, but Cantonese remains dominant in daily life and media.

### Culture
Guangdong's culture is diverse, with distinct regional spheres. The central Pearl River Delta is the heartland of Cantonese culture, known for its cuisine (e.g., dim sum), opera, and tea ceremonies. The eastern Chaoshan area has its own Teochew culture, cuisine, and opera. The Hakka people, spread across the northeastern and northern regions, have their own cuisine, music, and folk songs. The province is also the ancestral home of vast numbers of overseas Chinese.

### Education and Research
Guangdong hosts 160 institutions of higher education, ranking second among Chinese provinces. As of 2025, its major cities, Guangzhou (6th) and Shenzhen (18th), are among the world's top 20 cities for scientific research output.

## Terms
*   **Baiyue:** The "Hundred Yue," a collective name for the tribal groups originally inhabiting southern China before Sinicization.
*   **Cantonese:** A variety of Yue Chinese, the prestige dialect of Guangdong, widely spoken in the province and globally.
*   **Hakka:** A Han Chinese subgroup with a distinct language and culture, traditionally living in the highlands of Guangdong, Fujian, and Jiangxi.
*   **Teochew (Chaoshan):** A cultural and linguistic group in eastern Guangdong speaking a variety of Southern Min Chinese.
*   **Pearl River Delta:** A megalopolis and economic zone in Guangdong, including major cities like Guangzhou and Shenzhen.
*   **Special Economic Zone (SEZ):** Designated areas in China with more market-oriented economic policies and foreign investment incentives, such as Shenzhen.
*   **Floating Population:** Migrants who live in a province for at least six months of the year but are not permanent residents.
*   **Lingnan:** The historical name for the region south of the Nanling Mountains, encompassing modern Guangdong and Guangxi.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   The precise origins and migration patterns of the Hakka, Cantonese, and Teochew peoples are subjects of ongoing historical and linguistic research.
*   The economic disparity between the highly developed Pearl River Delta and the less-developed eastern, western, and northern parts of the province remains a significant challenge.
*   The balance between promoting Mandarin for national unity and preserving the dominant status of Cantonese and other local languages in daily life and media is a recurring cultural issue.

# Emiliano Zapata

## Overview
Emiliano Zapata (1879–1919) was a central figure of the Mexican Revolution, leading the peasant revolt in the state of Morelos. He is remembered as a champion of agrarian reform, whose Plan of Ayala demanded land redistribution for peasants and whose legacy continues to inspire social movements in Mexico and beyond.

## Timeline
- **8 August 1879** — Born in Anenecuilco, Morelos.
- **1909** — Elected president of the Anenecuilco village council.
- **1910** — Joins Francisco I. Madero's revolution against dictator Porfirio Díaz.
- **May 1911** — Captures Cuautla, contributing to the fall of Díaz.
- **November 1911** — Promulgates the Plan of Ayala, calling for radical land reform and denouncing Madero.
- **1913** — Victoriano Huerta seizes power in a coup; Zapata fights against him.
- **July 1914** — Zapata's forces help oust Huerta.
- **October–November 1914** — Convention of Aguascalientes; Zapata and Pancho Villa break with Venustiano Carranza.
- **1915** — Implements land reforms in Morelos under the Plan of Ayala.
- **1917** — Re-takes control of most of Morelos from Carrancista forces.
- **10 April 1919** — Assassinated in an ambush at the Hacienda de San Juan, Chinameca.
- **1920** — After Carranza's fall, Zapatista generals secure land reforms in Morelos.

## Body

### Early Life and the Porfiriato
Zapata was born into a well-known family in the village of Anenecuilco, Morelos. He grew up during the Porfiriato, the long dictatorship of Porfirio Díaz, when peasant communities faced increasing repression from large landowners (*hacendados*) who monopolized land and water for sugarcane production. Zapata became active in his village's long struggle to regain lost lands, using colonial-era documents to prove their claims. In 1909, he was elected president of the Anenecuilco village council, a position from which he could advocate for land rights.

### Revolution and the Plan of Ayala
Zapata joined Madero's 1910 revolution, hoping for land reform. After helping defeat Díaz, he became disillusioned when President Madero failed to deliver on promises of land redistribution. In November 1911, Zapata broke with Madero and issued the **Plan of Ayala**. This radical document declared Madero a traitor and called for the immediate return of lands stolen under Díaz, the nationalization of one-third of large plantations for distribution to peasants, and the confiscation of two-thirds from any owner who resisted. This plan became the core of Zapata's political vision.

### Civil War and the Conventionists
After Huerta's coup against Madero in 1913, Zapata fought against him. Following Huerta's defeat in 1914, revolutionary factions met at the Convention of Aguascalientes. Zapata and Villa rejected Carranza's authority, leading to a civil war among the victors. Zapata focused on rebuilding Morelos, instituting the Plan of Ayala's land reforms. When Carranza's forces invaded Morelos with scorched-earth tactics, Zapata waged guerrilla warfare against them, re-taking most of the state by 1917.

### Assassination and Legacy
President Carranza made eliminating Zapata a priority. In April 1919, Zapata was lured to a meeting and ambushed by forces under General Pablo González, led by the double-agent Jesús Guajardo. Zapata's death weakened but did not end the Zapatista movement. After Carranza was overthrown in 1920, Zapatista generals helped form the new government and implemented significant land reforms in Morelos. Zapata became a martyr for agrarian reform; his Plan of Ayala influenced Article 27 of the 1917 Mexican Constitution. His image remains a powerful symbol of peasant struggle, inspiring later movements like the Zapatista Army of National Liberation (EZLN).

## Terms
- **Porfiriato**: The period of Porfirio Díaz's dictatorship (1876–1911), characterized by economic growth but severe social inequality and repression of peasants.
- **Hacendados**: Owners of large estates (*haciendas*), who accumulated vast landholdings during the Porfiriato.
- **Plan of Ayala**: Zapata's 1911 manifesto calling for radical land reform, including the redistribution of hacienda lands to peasants.
- **Liberation Army of the South**: The peasant revolutionary force led by Zapata in the state of Morelos.
- **Zapatismo**: The agrarian movement and political philosophy inspired by Zapata, emphasizing land rights and peasant autonomy.
- **Convention of Aguascalientes**: A 1914 meeting of revolutionary leaders that attempted to establish a provisional government but instead deepened factional divides.
- **Scorched-earth policy**: A military strategy of destroying resources and villages to deny them to the enemy, used against Zapatista communities.
- **Article 27**: A key part of the 1917 Mexican Constitution that codified agrarian reform, influenced by Zapata's demands.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Bandit or Revolutionary?**: Contemporary and historical portrayals debate whether Zapata and his followers were social bandits or legitimate revolutionaries fighting for justice.
- **Scale of Land Reform**: While Zapata's vision was radical, the full-scale land redistribution he sought was not enacted until the presidency of Lázaro Cárdenas (1934–1940).
- **Personal Life**: Historical scrutiny exists regarding Zapata's close relationships with men, including Ignacio de la Torre y Mier and Manuel Palafox, and their potential romantic nature.
- **Myth vs. History**: Zapata's posthumous transformation into a national icon and symbol of indigenous and peasant resistance continues to be shaped by political and cultural forces.

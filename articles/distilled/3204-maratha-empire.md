# Maratha Empire

## Overview
The Maratha Empire, also called the Maratha Confederacy, was an early modern Indian polity that rose to prominence in the 17th century. Founded by Shivaji, it expanded to control much of the Indian subcontinent by the 18th century, becoming the primary rival to the British East India Company before its dissolution in 1818. Its history illustrates the transition from a centralized kingdom to a decentralized confederacy and its ultimate defeat by colonial power.

## Timeline
- **1645** — Shivaji begins resistance against the Bijapur Sultanate, capturing Fort Torna.
- **1674** — Shivaji is crowned Chhatrapati (sovereign) of the Maratha Kingdom.
- **1689** — Sambhaji, Shivaji's son, is captured and executed by the Mughals.
- **1707** — Aurangzeb dies; Shahu is released by the Mughals and claims the Maratha throne.
- **1713** — Balaji Vishwanath is appointed Peshwa (prime minister) by Shahu.
- **1720** — Baji Rao I becomes Peshwa, initiating major territorial expansion.
- **1737** — Marathas raid the suburbs of Delhi in the Battle of Delhi.
- **1761** — The Maratha army is decisively defeated by the Durrani Empire at the Third Battle of Panipat.
- **1771** — Mahadaji Shinde recaptures Delhi and restores the Mughal emperor as a puppet ruler.
- **1795** — The Marathas jointly defeat the Nizam of Hyderabad at the Battle of Kharda.
- **1802** — Peshwa Baji Rao II signs the Treaty of Bassein, seeking British protection.
- **1818** — The Third Anglo-Maratha War ends with the dissolution of the Confederacy.

## Body

### Origins and Early Kingdom
The Marathas were a Marathi-speaking warrior group from the western Deccan Plateau. Under Shivaji (1630–1680), they revolted against the Bijapur Sultanate and the Mughal Empire to establish "Hindavi Swarajya" (self-rule of Hindus). Shivaji was crowned Chhatrapati in 1674, creating an independent state with a modern administration. At his death, his realm comprised about 4.1% of the subcontinent, defended by 40,000 cavalry and 50,000 soldiers.

### Succession Struggles and the Peshwa Era
After Shivaji, his sons Sambhaji and Rajaram fought the Mughals. Sambhaji was executed by Aurangzeb in 1689. Following Aurangzeb's death in 1707, Shivaji's grandson Shahu was released by the Mughals and claimed the throne, leading to a civil war with his aunt Tarabai. Shahu appointed Balaji Vishwanath as Peshwa in 1713, a role that became hereditary. Under Peshwa Baji Rao I (1720–1740), the Maratha Kingdom expanded tenfold, with major victories at Palkhed (1728) and Bhopal (1737). The Peshwas became the de facto rulers after Shahu's death in 1749.

### Expansion and the Confederacy
The Maratha state evolved into a confederacy of four major states—Scindia, Holkar, Gaekwad, and Bhonsle—under the nominal leadership of the Peshwa. They expanded into northern and central India, extracting tribute (chauth) from defeated rulers. Their zenith came around 1760, with territory spanning about 2.5 million km². However, their expansion was halted by a catastrophic defeat at the Third Battle of Panipat (1761) against the Durrani Empire. Maratha power was revived in the 1770s under Peshwa Madhavrao I and generals like Mahadaji Shinde, who recaptured Delhi in 1771.

### Decline and British Intervention
Internal divisions weakened the Confederacy. The British East India Company intervened in Maratha succession disputes, leading to the First Anglo-Maratha War (1775–1782), which ended in a stalemate. After the Second Anglo-Maratha War (1803–1805), the Marathas lost control of Delhi and significant territories. The Third Anglo-Maratha War (1817–1818) saw the Maratha chiefs defeated individually. The Peshwa was exiled, and the British annexed the Maratha heartland, establishing paramountcy over the remaining princely states.

## Terms
- **Chhatrapati**: The sovereign title held by the Maratha rulers, beginning with Shivaji.
- **Peshwa**: The prime minister and head of the council of eight ministers; the office became hereditary and the de facto center of power after 1749.
- **Hindavi Swarajya**: A concept meaning "self-rule of Hindus," the ideological foundation of Shivaji's state.
- **Chauth**: A tax, typically one-fourth of revenue, collected by the Marathas from defeated or tributary states.
- **Sardeshmukhi**: An additional tax of 10% levied by the Marathas as the nominal head of the Deccan.
- **Ashtapradhan**: The council of eight ministers that administered the Maratha kingdom.
- **Confederacy**: A political system where semi-autonomous states (like the Scindias and Holkars) operate under a nominal central authority.

## Debates and Open Questions
Historians debate the accurate terminology for the polity. Some argue "empire" implies too much centralization, while "confederacy" suggests a stability of power-sharing that did not exist, as internal dynamics shifted frequently. The Maratha treatment of other Hindu groups, such as the Jats and Rajputs, is criticized for contributing to their defeat at Panipat, where these groups did not support them. The causes of the Panipat defeat itself—including strategic errors, lack of allies, and supply issues—remain a subject of scholarly analysis.

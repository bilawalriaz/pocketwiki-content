# Gulf of Guinea

## Overview
The Gulf of Guinea is the northeasternmost part of the tropical Atlantic Ocean, defined by a line from Cape Lopez in Gabon to Cape Palmas in Liberia. It is a region of significant geographical, economic, and security importance, hosting major rivers like the Niger, key shipping lanes, and numerous islands. The gulf faces severe challenges from maritime crimes, including piracy, illegal fishing, and human trafficking, which are interconnected and threaten regional stability.

## Body

### Name and Geography
The name "Guinea" has disputed origins, possibly deriving from the Berber word *aguinaou* (Negro) or the city of Jenne. Its modern application to the coast dates to 1481. The gulf's main river is the Niger. Its geographic limits are defined by the International Hydrographic Organization as a line from Cape Lopez, Gabon, to Ilhéu das Rôlas, and then to Cape Palmas, Liberia. The gulf contains several islands, including Annobón, Bioko, and the nation of São Tomé and Príncipe, which are part of the Cameroon volcanic line.

### Maritime Security and Piracy
Maritime security in the gulf involves 18 sovereign states and institutions like ECOWAS, ECCAS, and the Gulf of Guinea Commission (GGC). A 60% population growth since 2000 has contributed to crimes like hijacking, kidnapping, and robbery. While piracy has declined from its 2020 peak, kidnapping for ransom remains a critical issue. Pirate tactics have evolved to target high-value individuals, with attacks often carried out quickly at night. The region accounts for a vast majority of global maritime kidnappings and hostage-takings.

### Illegal, Unreported, and Unregulated (IUU) Fishing
IUU fishing is a severe problem, with reported catches rising from 212,800 tons in 2000 to 232,200 tons in 2012. It depletes fish stocks, threatens food security, and is linked to organized crimes like arms and drug trafficking. IUU fishing creates "blue crime spillover," directly contributing to piracy and human trafficking. Depleted resources and poor economic conditions lure unemployed fishermen into piracy.

### Human Costs and Forced Labor
IUU fishing operations are linked to forced labor. Reports indicate workers are forced to stay at sea for months without land access, paid in low-value "trash fish," and endure poor safety, hygiene, and living conditions. The economic desperation from depleting fish stocks and poor wages fuels the cycle of maritime crime, as individuals are recruited into piracy for quick money.

## Terms
- **Gulf of Guinea Commission (GGC):** An institution addressing maritime security in the gulf.
- **Null Island:** The point at 0° latitude and 0° longitude, located in the Gulf of Guinea.
- **Bight of Benin / Bight of Bonny:** Coastal indentations within the Gulf of Guinea.
- **Cameroon line of volcanoes:** A chain of extinct volcanoes forming islands in the gulf.
- **Illegal, Unreported, and Unregulated (IUU) fishing:** Fishing activity that violates national or international laws, is not reported, or occurs in unregulated areas.
- **Blue crime:** A term for maritime crimes, including piracy, IUU fishing, drug trafficking, and human trafficking.
- **Citadel:** A secure, fortified room on a ship where crew can shelter during a pirate attack.

## Debates and open questions
- The precise etymological origin of the name "Guinea" is debated, with theories linking it to the name Ghana, the city of Jenne, or the Berber word for Negro.
- The exact geographic boundaries of the Gulf of Guinea are defined differently by various sources, though the International Hydrographic Organization provides a specific definition.

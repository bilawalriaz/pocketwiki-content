# Durban

## Overview
Durban is South Africa's third-largest city and the busiest port in sub-Saharan Africa. Located on the Indian Ocean coast, it is the economic and cultural hub of KwaZulu-Natal province, with a diverse population shaped by Zulu, Indian, British, and other influences. Its history as a trading post and colonial settlement has evolved into a modern metropolis facing challenges like crime and climate change, while hosting major events like the 2010 FIFA World Cup.

## Timeline
- **1497** — Portuguese explorer Vasco da Gama sights the coast and names it "Natal" (Christmas).
- **1824** — British traders establish a settlement at Port Natal, granted land by Zulu King Shaka.
- **1835** — The settlement is named "D'Urban" after the Cape Colony governor.
- **1839** — Voortrekkers establish the Natalia Republic with Pietermaritzburg as capital.
- **1843** — Britain annexes the Natalia Republic, making Natal a British colony.
- **1860** — Indentured labourers from British India begin arriving to work on sugar plantations.
- **1910** — Natal becomes a province of the Union of South Africa.
- **1935** — Durban is granted city status.
- **1999** — The eThekwini Metropolitan Municipality is formed, incorporating Durban and surrounding towns.
- **2010** — King Shaka International Airport opens; Durban hosts FIFA World Cup matches.

## Body

### History and Settlement
The area was inhabited by hunter-gatherers for millennia before the Nguni people settled. European contact began with Vasco da Gama in 1497. In 1824, British traders led by Francis Farewell and Henry Fynn established a trading post, receiving a land grant from Zulu King Shaka. The settlement was named D'Urban in 1835. After the brief Voortrekker-led Natalia Republic (1839), Britain annexed the area in 1843. From 1860, indentured labourers from India were brought to work in sugar agriculture, significantly shaping the city's demographics.

### Geography and Climate
Durban lies on the east coast at the mouth of the Umgeni River, featuring a natural harbour. The city proper is part of the larger eThekwini Metropolitan Municipality (2,556 km²). It has a humid subtropical climate with hot, wet summers and mild, dry winters. Climate change projections suggest Durban will face significant sea-level rise and temperature increases, with potential damages in the billions of dollars by 2050.

### Demographics and Society
Durban is ethnically diverse, with Zulu people forming the largest group, followed by significant Indian, White, and Coloured populations. Post-apartheid population growth led to the formation of shanty towns, though government housing programs have since reduced their prevalence. The city has a high murder rate compared to other South African cities, with significant variation between suburbs. It is also a major drug trafficking route.

### Economy and Infrastructure
The port is a critical economic asset, handling cargo and hosting a cruise terminal. Key industries include finance, manufacturing, trade, transport, and tourism. Sugar refining is a major industry, with most of South Africa's cane grown in KwaZulu-Natal. The city has an extensive transport network, including King Shaka International Airport, the N3 and N2 freeways, and commuter rail. The informal sector, including street vendors at Warwick Junction, is significant.

### Culture and Tourism
Durban's cultural attractions include the Phansi Museum, Playhouse Theatre, and the African Art Centre. The city is known for its beaches, particularly the Golden Mile, and was named a UNESCO City of Literature. It hosted matches for the 2010 FIFA World Cup, for which the Moses Mabhida Stadium was built. Major annual sporting events include the Comrades Marathon and Dusi Canoe Marathon.

## Terms
- **eThekwini Metropolitan Municipality**: The local government structure formed in 1999, encompassing Durban and over 120 surrounding towns.
- **Indentured labourers**: Workers from British India brought to South Africa from 1860 under contract to work on sugar plantations.
- **Humid subtropical climate**: A climate classification (Köppen Cfa) characterized by hot, humid summers and mild, dry winters.
- **Port of Durban**: The busiest port in sub-Saharan Africa and a natural harbour.
- **N3 Western Freeway**: The major highway linking Durban to the economic hinterland of Gauteng.
- **Minibus taxis**: The standard form of public transport for many residents, often privately owned and operated in fleets.
- **Xenophobic attacks**: Episodes of violence directed at foreign nationals and asylum seekers, recorded in 2008 and 2015.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Street Renaming**: The late 2000s renaming of 107 streets to honor anti-apartheid figures, mostly affiliated with the ANC, sparked controversy. Opposition parties and some residents criticized the process as partisan and lacking local connection.
- **Climate Change Vulnerability**: Durban is identified as one of 12 major African cities most severely threatened by future sea-level rise, with projected damages running into tens of billions of dollars. The long-term costs and necessary adaptation measures are significant open questions.
- **Crime and Safety**: While murder rates dropped from a peak in the 1990s, they have risen again in the 2010s. The effectiveness of policing and social interventions in addressing this trend remains a critical issue.

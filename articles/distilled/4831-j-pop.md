# J-pop

## Overview
J-pop (Japanese popular music) is a broad genre of Japanese popular music that entered the mainstream in the 1990s, replacing the earlier term *kayōkyoku*. It encompasses a wide range of styles, from rock and electronic to idol music and hip hop, and is a major component of Japan's cultural exports and soft power. Its evolution reflects Japan's post-war engagement with Western music and the development of a distinct domestic music industry.

## Timeline
- **1920s–1980s** — *Kayōkyoku* ("Lyric Singing Music") is the dominant term for Japanese popular music.
- **1960s–1970s** — Bands like Happy End fuse Western rock with Japanese music, proving rock can be sung in Japanese.
- **1978** — Yellow Magic Orchestra (YMO) debuts, pioneering electropop/technopop.
- **1980s** — "City pop" and "visual kei" emerge; idol singers like Seiko Matsuda achieve massive popularity.
- **1990** — The term "J-pop" is popularized by radio station J-Wave and record stores.
- **1990–1997** — The "Being System" and producer Tetsuya Komuro dominate the charts with commercial pop.
- **1998** — The Japanese physical music market peaks at ¥607 billion.
- **1999** — Hikaru Utada's album *First Love* becomes the best-selling album in Oricon history.
- **2002** — The digital ringtone (*chaku-uta*) market is created, shifting consumption.
- **2007** — Vocaloid software Hatsune Miku is released, spawning a new music scene.
- **2010s** — The "Warring Idols Period" sees a boom in idol groups like AKB48 and Morning Musume.
- **2020s** — J-pop gains unprecedented global chart success, led by artists like Yoasobi and Ado.

## Body

### Origins and Definition
Modern J-pop has roots in traditional Japanese music and 1960s Western pop/rock. It replaced *kayōkyoku* and is characterized by a special English-influenced pronunciation and the use of the major second interval, adopted from Western rock. The term "J-pop" was initially used for Western-style artists like Pizzicato Five but became a blanket term for most Japanese popular music, excluding *enka* and independent music. Its definition remains fluid, with some artists like Greeeen embracing the label, while many rock musicians dislike its association with "pop."

### Historical Development
The genre evolved from pre-war *ryūkōka* and post-war *kayōkyoku*. The 1960s saw the "rockabilly boom" and the Beatles-inspired "group sounds" movement. The 1970s "new music" era focused on singer-songwriters, while the late 1970s saw the rise of electronic music with YMO and rock with Southern All Stars. The 1980s fused these elements, producing city pop and the visual kei rock movement. The 1990s commercialized J-pop with the "Being System" and Komuro-produced dance acts, leading to a market peak in 1998.

### 2000s–Present: Diversification and Global Reach
The 2000s saw diversification with the rise of hip hop, R&B, and digital downloads. Johnny & Associates' boy bands dominated physical single sales. The 2010s were the "Warring Idols Period," marked by intense competition among idol groups. In the 2020s, J-pop achieved global chart success, with artists like Yoasobi and Ado topping international charts. The industry also saw the rise of Vocaloid music and anime-related songs as major commercial forces.

## Terms
- **Kayōkyoku**: A term for Japanese popular music from the 1920s to the 1980s, which J-pop largely replaced.
- **Ryūkōka**: The precursor to *kayōkyoku*, blending Western instruments with traditional Japanese melodies.
- **Group Sounds**: A 1960s Japanese rock genre inspired by the Beatles and other Western bands.
- **New Music**: A 1970s genre focusing on singer-songwriters with more complex arrangements than folk.
- **City Pop**: A 1980s genre with a big-city theme, influenced by album-oriented rock and jazz fusion.
- **Visual Kei**: A 1980s rock movement characterized by androgynous costumes and makeup.
- **Chaku-uta**: A digital ringtone song format that became a major market in the 2000s.
- **Vocaloid**: A singing voice synthesizer software, with Hatsune Miku being its most famous virtual idol.
- **Idol**: A manufactured pop star, often in groups, who is marketed for their personality and image as much as their music.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The precise boundaries of "J-pop" are debated; it is sometimes used as a blanket term, while other times it is contrasted with rock, hip hop, and other genres.
- The commercial peak of the physical music market in 1998 and its subsequent decline due to digitalization is a key point of industry analysis.
- The long-term impact of digital downloads, streaming, and Vocaloid music on traditional artist development and copyright systems is ongoing.

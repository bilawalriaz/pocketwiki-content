# Khanate of Bukhara

## Overview
The Khanate of Bukhara was an Uzbek monarchy in Central Asia that existed from 1501 to 1785. It was a major political and cultural power in the region, founded by the Shaybanid dynasty and later ruled by the Janid dynasty. Its history reflects the complex interplay of nomadic and settled traditions, dynastic succession, and regional power struggles, ultimately transitioning into the Emirate of Bukhara under the non-Chinggisid Manghit dynasty.

## Timeline
- **1501** — Muhammad Shaybani conquers major cities of Mawarannahr, establishing the Khanate.
- **1510** — Muhammad Shaybani is killed by Shah Ismail I of the Safavid Empire.
- **1562** — Abdullah Khan II establishes Bukhara as the permanent capital.
- **1599** — Baqi Muhammad Khan founds the Janid (Ashtarkhanid) dynasty.
- **1711** — Assassination of Ubaydullah Khan leads to political disintegration.
- **1740** — Nader Shah of Iran conquers the Khanate.
- **1747** — After Nader Shah's death, real power passes to the Manghit ataliqs.
- **1756** — Muhammad Rahim, a Manghit, takes the title of Khan.
- **1785** — Shah Murad formalizes Manghit rule, transforming the Khanate into the Emirate of Bukhara.

## Body

### Founding and the Shaybanid Dynasty (1501–1598)
The Khanate was founded by Muhammad Shaybani, grandson of the steppe ruler Abu'l Khayr Khan. In the 1490s, Shaybani conquered the settled cities of Transoxiana, including Samarkand, Bukhara, and Tashkent, defeating his rival, the Timurid prince Babur. After Shaybani's death in 1510, the lands were divided among Abu'l-Khayrid lineages in a decentralized appanage system. The capital shifted between cities until Abdullah Khan II centralized power and made Bukhara the permanent capital by 1562.

### The Janid Dynasty and Cultural Flourishing (1599–1747)
The Janids, descendants of Astrakhanid rulers who fled Russian conquest, replaced the Shaybanids in 1599. This period saw cultural development, including the work of poets like Turdy and Mashrab, and historians writing in Persian. However, political instability grew, culminating in the disintegration of central authority after the 1711 assassination of Ubaydullah Khan. The Khanate was further weakened by the 1740 invasion and conquest by Nader Shah of Iran.

### The Manghit Takeover and Transition to Emirate (1740–1785)
The Manghit family rose to power through the position of *ataliq* (prime minister). After Nader Shah's death in 1747, Manghit ataliqs like Muhammad Hakim-biy and his son Muhammad Rahim became the de facto rulers. Muhammad Rahim expanded the state's territory and, though not a descendant of Genghis Khan, took the title of Khan in 1756. His descendant Shah Murad formalized this dynastic rule in 1785, establishing the Emirate of Bukhara and using the Islamic title *emir* instead of *khan* to legitimize his non-Chinggisid rule.

### Government and Administration
The Khanate was initially decentralized, governed through a system of appanages (territories ruled by nobles). The khan's power depended on his relationship with these appanage holders, who controlled revenue and military levies. A key measure of central authority was the power to appoint *ataliqs* to supervise these regions. Centralization increased under Abdullah Khan II, making Bukhara the permanent capital.

## Terms
- **Appanage**: A territory granted to a noble for governance, a key feature of the Khanate's decentralized administration.
- **Ataliq**: A high-ranking official, often a prime minister, who supervised administration and military affairs in an appanage.
- **Chinggisid**: A ruler claiming descent from Genghis Khan, which was a source of political legitimacy.
- **Kurultai**: A traditional Mongol/Turkic assembly of nobles used to elect a new khan and divide territories.
- **Mawarannahr**: The Arabic name for Transoxiana, the region between the Amu Darya and Syr Darya rivers.
- **Shaybanid**: The first ruling dynasty of the Khanate, a branch of the Abu'l-Khayrids claiming descent from Genghis Khan.
- **Janid (Ashtarkhanid)**: The second ruling dynasty, descendants of Astrakhanid rulers who were the last Chinggisid dynasty to rule Bukhara.
- **Manghit**: The Uzbek dynasty that replaced the Janids, taking the title *emir* as they were not Chinggisid.

## Debates and Open Questions
The sources note that the expression "92 Uzbek tribes" is used in 17th-century sources regarding the population, but the exact composition and political significance of these tribes is not detailed. The precise extent of the khan's authority versus that of the appanage holders fluctuated significantly over time, making the balance of power a central dynamic of the state's history.

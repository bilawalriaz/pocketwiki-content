# Lisbon

## Overview
Lisbon is the capital and largest city of Portugal, situated on the northern shore of the River Tagus. As one of the oldest cities in the world and the second-oldest European capital, its history spans from pre-Celtic tribes to its current status as a major global city and political, economic, and cultural centre.

## Body

### History and Etymology
Lisbon's name has uncertain origins, possibly deriving from Proto-Celtic *Olisippo* or a Phoenician term. The city was founded by pre-Celtic tribes and later developed by Phoenicians and Greeks. The Romans made it a municipality named *Felicitas Julia*. After the fall of Rome, it was ruled by Germanic tribes, including the Visigoths, before being captured by the Moors in 711. The Christian Reconquista of the city in 1147 was a pivotal event. Lisbon became Portugal's capital in 1255 and flourished as the hub of the Age of Discovery in the 15th and 16th centuries. A catastrophic earthquake in 1755 led to a major reconstruction under the Marquis of Pombal, creating the modern city grid. The 20th century saw the end of the monarchy (1910), the authoritarian Estado Novo regime (1926-1974), and its overthrow in the Carnation Revolution.

### Geography and Climate
Lisbon is located at the mouth of the River Tagus and is the westernmost capital of mainland Europe. The city covers 100.05 km², with its boundaries matching the municipality. The Monsanto Forest Park occupies 10% of this area. The climate is Mediterranean, with mild, rainy winters and warm, dry summers. The average annual temperature is 17.6 °C. The city receives about 793 mm of precipitation annually, mostly in autumn and winter, and enjoys approximately 2,875 hours of sunshine per year.

### Demographics and Economy
The city proper has an estimated population of 658,236 (2025), while the metropolitan area houses about 3.35 million people. The largest religion is Roman Catholicism. The economy is primarily service-based, with the Lisbon region producing over 31% of Portugal's GDP. It is a major financial centre, home to the Euronext Lisbon stock exchange, and a significant tourist destination, with 7.7 million visitors to the region in 2022. Despite its wealth, the city faces challenges with low wages and high living costs relative to the EU average.

### Transport and Infrastructure
Lisbon is served by the Humberto Delgado Airport within the city limits. Its public transport includes a four-line metro, a historic tram network, commuter trains, and buses. The city is connected to the south bank of the Tagus by the 25 de Abril Bridge and the Vasco da Gama Bridge, the latter being the longest bridge in Europe at 17.2 km. A new international airport in Alcochete was announced in 2024.

### Culture and Landmarks
Lisbon's architecture spans Romanesque, Gothic, Manueline, and modern styles. Key landmarks include the Belém Tower and Jerónimos Monastery (UNESCO World Heritage Sites), the Castle of São Jorge, and the Pombaline Baixa district. The city is known for its *azulejo* tile art and *calçada portuguesa* pavement. It hosts numerous museums, including the Gulbenkian Museum and the National Museum of Ancient Art. Major events include the Rock in Rio Lisboa festival and World Youth Day 2023.

## Terms
- **Municipium**: A Roman administrative unit for a town or city, granting its citizens certain rights.
- **Reconquista**: The centuries-long period of Christian reconquest of the Iberian Peninsula from Muslim rule.
- **Manueline**: An ornate Portuguese architectural style from the early 16th century, incorporating maritime and Gothic elements.
- **Pombaline**: The style of urban planning and architecture used to rebuild Lisbon after the 1755 earthquake, characterized by grid layouts and earthquake-resistant construction.
- **Estado Novo**: The authoritarian, right-wing regime that ruled Portugal from 1926 to 1974.
- **Azulejo**: A form of Portuguese painted tin-glazed ceramic tilework.
- **Calçada Portuguesa**: A traditional Portuguese pavement made of small, flat stones arranged in patterns.
- **Freguesia**: A civil parish, the smallest administrative division in Portugal.
- **Fado**: A Portuguese music genre characterized by mournful tunes and lyrics, often about the sea or the life of the poor.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The precise etymology of "Lisbon" is unknown, with theories linking it to Celtic, Phoenician, or legendary Greek origins.
- The exact population of the city at various historical points is often an estimate.
- The need for and location of a new international airport to replace Humberto Delgado Airport has been a subject of long-standing debate and planning.

# Hyderabad

## Overview
Hyderabad is the capital and largest city of the Indian state of Telangana, situated on the Deccan Plateau. Founded in 1591, it has served as the capital of successive dynasties and states, evolving into a major economic and cultural hub. Its significance lies in its rich history, its role as a joint capital for Telangana and Andhra Pradesh, and its emergence as a global center for information technology and pharmaceuticals.

## Timeline
- **1591** — Muhammad Quli Qutb Shah establishes Hyderabad to extend the capital from Golconda.
- **1687** — The city is annexed by the Mughal Empire.
- **1724** — Asaf Jah I defeats the Mughal governor, establishing the autonomous Asaf Jahi (Nizam) dynasty.
- **1769** — Hyderabad becomes the formal capital of the Asaf Jahi Nizams.
- **1798** — The Nizam signs a subsidiary alliance with the British East India Company.
- **1948** — Hyderabad State is annexed by the Indian Union after Operation Polo.
- **1956** — The States Reorganisation Act makes Hyderabad the capital of the new Andhra Pradesh.
- **2014** — Telangana state is formed, with Hyderabad as its capital and the joint capital of Andhra Pradesh until 2024.

## Body

### History and Founding
The region has been inhabited since the Stone Age, with evidence of Iron Age sites from c. 500 BCE. It was ruled by the Chalukya and Kakatiya dynasties before coming under the Bahmani Sultanate. In 1518, Sultan Quli established the independent Qutb Shahi dynasty. His descendant, Muhammad Quli Qutb Shah, founded Hyderabad in 1591 on the banks of the Musi River to alleviate water shortages at Golconda. The city was annexed by the Mughals in 1687. In 1724, the Mughal viceroy Asaf Jah I declared sovereignty, founding the Asaf Jahi dynasty (the Nizams), who ruled Hyderabad as a princely state under British paramountcy until Indian independence.

### Post-Independence and Statehood
After India's independence in 1947, the Nizam initially sought to remain independent. In 1948, the Indian Army annexed Hyderabad State. Following the States Reorganisation Act of 1956, Hyderabad became the capital of Andhra Pradesh. Decades of the Telangana movement advocating for a separate state culminated in 2014 with the formation of Telangana. Hyderabad was designated the joint capital of Telangana and Andhra Pradesh for a period not exceeding ten years.

### Geography and Climate
Hyderabad covers 650 km² on the Deccan Plateau with an average altitude of 536 m. The terrain is hilly, dotted with artificial lakes like Hussain Sagar (built 1562). The climate is tropical wet and dry, with hot summers (March–June) and a monsoon season (June–October). The annual mean temperature is 26.6 °C.

### Economy
Hyderabad's economy has transitioned from a historic pearl and diamond trading center to a modern industrial and service hub. Industrialization began in the late 19th century. Since the 1990s, it has emerged as a global center for information technology (IT) and pharmaceuticals, earning nicknames like "Cyberabad" and "India's pharmaceutical capital." The IT sector is a major employer, with numerous multinational companies operating in special economic zones like HITEC City.

### Culture and Demographics
Hyderabad's culture is a blend of North and South Indian influences, shaped by the patronage of the Qutb Shahi and Nizam dynasties. Telugu and Urdu are official languages. The city is renowned for its distinctive Hyderabadi cuisine, recognized by UNESCO as a creative city of gastronomy. According to the 2011 census, the population within city limits was 6.9 million, with Hindus forming the majority (64.9%) and Muslims a large minority (30.1%).

## Terms
- **Deccan Plateau:** A large plateau in southern India, forming the peninsular region.
- **Subsidiary Alliance:** A treaty system imposed by the British East India Company on Indian princely states, requiring them to pay for the maintenance of British troops.
- **Instrument of Accession:** A legal document executed by Indian princely states to accede to the Dominion of India.
- **States Reorganisation Act (1956):** An act that reorganized the boundaries of Indian states along linguistic lines.
- **Cyberabad:** A colloquial name for Hyderabad, referencing its status as a major IT hub.
- **HITEC City:** A technology township in Hyderabad that houses many IT and multinational companies.
- **Geographical Indication (GI):** A sign used on products that have a specific geographical origin and possess qualities or a reputation due to that origin.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The exact origin of the city's name is debated between the historical "Haydar's City" and the legendary "Bhagya-nagar" named after Bhagmati.
- The proposal by B. R. Ambedkar in 1955 to designate Hyderabad as India's second capital was not implemented.
- The implementation of the CURE (Core Urban Region Economy) framework represents a current administrative approach to managing the city's rapid urbanization.

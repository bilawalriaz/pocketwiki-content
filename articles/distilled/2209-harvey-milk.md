# Harvey Milk

## Overview
Harvey Milk (1930–1978) was an American politician and gay rights activist who became the first openly gay man elected to public office in California. His brief career on the San Francisco Board of Supervisors, marked by landmark civil rights legislation and a powerful message of hope, made him an enduring icon and martyr for the LGBTQ community. His assassination, and the lenient sentence given to his killer, galvanized a national movement for equality.

## Timeline
- **May 22, 1930** — Born in Woodmere, New York.
- **1951** — Graduated from New York State College for Teachers.
- **1955** — Resigned from the U.S. Navy with an "other than honorable" discharge due to his homosexuality.
- **1972** — Moved to San Francisco and settled in the Castro District.
- **1973** — Ran for city supervisor for the first time, finishing 10th.
- **1976** — Appointed to the Board of Permit Appeals, becoming the first openly gay city commissioner in the U.S.
- **November 8, 1977** — Elected to the San Francisco Board of Supervisors.
- **1978** — Sponsored and passed a landmark gay rights ordinance banning discrimination.
- **November 27, 1978** — Assassinated at City Hall, along with Mayor George Moscone, by former supervisor Dan White.
- **May 21, 1979** — Dan White convicted of voluntary manslaughter, not murder.
- **August 7, 2009** — Posthumously awarded the Presidential Medal of Freedom.

## Body

### Early Life and Political Awakening
Born to a Jewish family in New York, Milk acknowledged his homosexuality in adolescence but kept it secret for years. After a series of jobs and relationships, he moved to San Francisco in 1972, opening a camera store in the Castro, a neighborhood experiencing a gay migration. Frustrations with government bureaucracy and the political climate, particularly during the Watergate era, spurred his entry into politics. He ran for city supervisor in 1973, 1975, and for the State Assembly in 1976, building a grassroots following and the moniker "Mayor of Castro Street" but losing each race.

### The Briggs Initiative and Election Victory
The late 1970s saw a national backlash against gay rights, led by figures like Anita Bryant. In California, State Senator John Briggs proposed Proposition 6, the "Briggs Initiative," which would ban gay people from teaching in public schools. Milk campaigned vigorously against it, debating Briggs and delivering his famous "Hope Speech." In 1977, San Francisco switched to district-based supervisor elections, allowing Milk to win his seat. He became a national symbol of gay political power.

### Supervisor and Assassination
During his nearly eleven months in office, Milk sponsored a civil rights bill banning discrimination based on sexual orientation in housing, employment, and public accommodations. It passed 11–1, with only Supervisor Dan White voting against it. Milk also championed neighborhood issues, like a "pooper scooper" law. On November 27, 1978, White, a disgruntled former supervisor, assassinated Mayor Moscone and then Milk at City Hall. White was convicted of voluntary manslaughter and served just over five years, a verdict that sparked the White Night riots.

### Legacy and Impact
Despite his short career, Milk became a martyr for LGBTQ rights. His assassination and the subsequent trial highlighted deep social and political divisions. He is remembered for his philosophy that government should solve people's basic problems, his belief in the power of visibility, and his enduring message of hope for marginalized people. His life and death are credited with accelerating the gay rights movement and ensuring the political participation of LGBTQ individuals.

## Terms
- **Openly gay**: Publicly acknowledging one's homosexual orientation, a significant and risky act in Milk's era.
- **Castro District**: A neighborhood in San Francisco that became a cultural and political hub for the gay community in the 1970s.
- **Alice B. Toklas Memorial Democratic Club**: The established gay political organization in San Francisco that initially opposed Milk's candidacy.
- **Briggs Initiative (Proposition 6)**: A 1978 California ballot proposition that would have banned gay people from teaching in public schools.
- **Hope Speech**: Harvey Milk's signature oration, urging marginalized people to come out and fight for their rights to inspire hope.
- **Diminished capacity**: A legal defense arguing a defendant's mental state was impaired, used by Dan White's lawyers.
- **White Night riots**: Violent protests in San Francisco following the lenient verdict in Dan White's trial.
- **Tikkun Olam**: A Jewish concept meaning "repairing the world," which influenced Milk's social justice philosophy.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The source notes Milk's legacy is "ambiguous," with historian Frances FitzGerald suggesting his martyrdom was seen as "an end rather than a beginning" for the Castro's optimism.
- The trial of Dan White raised profound questions about the influence of homophobia and police bias within the justice system, as evidenced by officers' behavior and the prosecutor's oversights.
- The long-term impact of Milk's assassination versus his life is debated; activist Cleve Jones argues the murder and response did more to ensure LGBTQ political participation than his life did.

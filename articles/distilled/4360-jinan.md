# Jinan

## Overview
Jinan is the capital of Shandong province in Eastern China, a major national administrative, economic, and transportation hub with a population of 9.2 million. Historically significant for over 4,000 years, it is famed as the "City of Springs" for its numerous artesian springs and has evolved from an ancient settlement into a modern metropolis with a strong industrial and educational base.

## Body

### Etymology and Early History
The name "Jinan" means "south of the Ji," referring to the now-vanished Ji River. The area has been inhabited for over 4,000 years, with the Neolithic Longshan culture known for its intricate "egg-shell pottery." During the Spring and Autumn and Warring States periods, the region was split between the states of Lu and Qi. Notable figures like the legendary physician Bian Que and the philosopher Zou Yan, who developed concepts of Yin-Yang and the Five Elements, are associated with the area.

### Imperial Era
After China's unification under the Qin dynasty, Jinan became a commandery and later the capital of the Kingdom of Jibei during the Han dynasty, growing into a cultural and economic hub. Buddhism flourished from the 5th century CE, leading to the construction of major temples and cave sites. The city remained a cultural center during the Song dynasty, home to renowned poets Li Qingzhao and Xin Qiji. Despite conflicts, such as the 1262 rebellion against Mongol rule, culture thrived during the Jin and Yuan dynasties.

### Ming, Qing, and Modern Transformation
Jinan became the capital of Shandong province under the Ming dynasty. The 1852 shift of the Yellow River's course connected the city to the Grand Canal, spurring modern growth. German influence grew after 1897, leading to the construction of the Jiaoji railway, which was completed in 1904 and cemented Jinan's role as a transportation and trade hub. The city became a major center for agricultural goods and industry.

### Republican Era and War
After World War I, Japan took over German interests in Shandong. The 1928 Jinan incident saw violent clashes between Japanese and Kuomintang forces. During the Nanjing decade, military governor Han Fuju brought stability but later abandoned Jinan to the Japanese in 1937, leading to his execution. Japanese control lasted until 1945, followed by a brief Kuomintang period before the People's Liberation Army captured the city in September 1948.

### Post-1949 and Cultural Revolution
Jinan experienced significant upheaval during the Cultural Revolution (1966-1976), with schools closed, cultural heritage damaged, and factional fighting. Government institutions were severely disrupted. The period saw radical changes to the educational system, including the relocation of university departments, though many policies were reversed by the early 1970s. The city began to recover and open to foreign visitors in the mid-1970s.

### Economy and Industry
Jinan's economy grew from its role as an agricultural market to include textile, flour, and paper industries. Major iron, steel, and chemical works were established in the 1950s. The city's GDP reached 1,352.76 billion yuan in 2024. Key industrial enterprises include Shandong Energy Group, China National Heavy Duty Truck Group, and Inspur Group. The Jinan High-tech Industrial Development Zone, founded in 1991, has attracted international companies.

### Transportation and Infrastructure
Jinan is a major railway intersection, served by the Jinghu (Beijing-Shanghai) and Jiaoji railways. The Beijing–Shanghai high-speed railway calls at Jinan West station. The Jinan Metro opened in 2019 and now operates several lines. The city is also connected by expressways and has an international airport with domestic and international flights.

### Culture and Tourism
Jinan is famed for its springs, particularly Baotu Spring, and Daming Lake, which together with Thousand-Buddha Mountain form the city's "Three Greatest Attractions." The city has a distinct dialect and cuisine, part of the Shandong culinary tradition. Major cultural institutions include the Shandong Provincial Museum and Library. Tourism is centered on its natural springs, lakes, and historical sites.

## Terms
- **Artesian Spring:** A natural spring where water rises under its own pressure from a confined aquifer.
- **Longshan Culture:** A Neolithic culture in eastern China known for its black, thin-walled "egg-shell pottery."
- **Commandery:** A historical administrative division in China, similar to a province or prefecture.
- **Double First-Class Construction:** A Chinese government initiative to develop world-class universities and disciplines.
- **Jiaoji Railway:** A railway line connecting Jinan to the port city of Qingdao, originally built by Germany.
- **Cultural Revolution:** A sociopolitical movement in China from 1966 to 1976 that caused widespread upheaval.

# Blackbeard

## Overview
Edward Teach, better known as Blackbeard, was an English pirate who operated around the West Indies and the North American colonies in the early 18th century. He is one of history's most famous pirates, renowned for his fearsome appearance and calculated use of intimidation rather than violence. His career and dramatic death have made him an enduring archetype of the "Golden Age of Piracy."

## Timeline
- **c. 1680** — Edward Teach is born, likely in Bristol, England.
- **c. 1716** — Joins the crew of pirate Captain Benjamin Hornigold at New Providence.
- **Late 1717** — Hornigold retires; Teach takes command of his own fleet.
- **28 Nov 1717** — Captures the French slave ship *La Concorde*, renames it *Queen Anne's Revenge*.
- **May 1718** — Blockades the port of Charles Town, South Carolina, to ransom for medical supplies.
- **10 Jun 1718** — *Queen Anne's Revenge* runs aground at Beaufort Inlet, North Carolina.
- **Jun 1718** — Accepts a royal pardon from Governor Charles Eden in Bath, North Carolina.
- **1718** — Briefly returns to piracy; entertains other pirates like Charles Vane at Ocracoke.
- **22 Nov 1718** — Killed in battle with Lieutenant Robert Maynard's forces at Ocracoke Inlet.

## Body

### Early Life and Pirate Beginnings
Little is confirmed about Teach's early life. He was likely born around 1680 and may have been a sailor on privateering ships during Queen Anne's War. By about 1716, he had settled on New Providence in the Bahamas, a known pirate haven, and joined Captain Benjamin Hornigold's crew. Hornigold placed Teach in command of a captured sloop, and they raided shipping together. In late 1717, Hornigold retired from piracy, leaving Teach in control of a small fleet, which included a ship commanded by Stede Bonnet.

### Rise to Infamy
Teach's fame grew after he captured the large French slave ship *La Concorde* in November 1717. He renamed it *Queen Anne's Revenge*, equipped it with 40 guns, and crewed it with over 300 men. His nickname, "Blackbeard," derived from his thick black beard and deliberate, terrifying appearance, which reportedly included tying lit fuses under his hat during battle. Despite his fearsome reputation, there are no verified accounts of him murdering captives. He blockaded Charles Town in May 1718 to secure medical supplies, then sailed north. In June 1718, his flagship ran aground at Beaufort Inlet, an event some historians speculate was intentional to reduce his crew and increase shares of plunder.

### Pardon, Return to Piracy, and Death
After the shipwreck, Teach accepted a royal pardon from North Carolina Governor Charles Eden in June 1718 and settled in Bath. He soon returned to piracy, capturing French ships and using his connection with Governor Eden to legitimize the plunder. His activities alarmed Virginia's governor, Alexander Spotswood, who organized an unauthorized expedition to capture him. On 22 November 1718, Lieutenant Robert Maynard's forces engaged Teach's ship, *Adventure*, at Ocracoke Inlet. After a fierce battle, Teach and several of his crew were killed. Maynard's men beheaded Teach, and his head was displayed as a warning.

### Legacy
Teach was not the most successful pirate in terms of plunder, but his calculated persona made him legendary. Much of his story comes from Charles Johnson's 1724 book, *A General Historie of the Pyrates*, which is considered generally reliable but possibly embellished. His flagship, *Queen Anne's Revenge*, was discovered in 1996, yielding over 280,000 artifacts. The modern romanticized view of pirates owes much to his image, inspiring countless books, films, and the archetype of the fearsome pirate captain.

## Terms
- **Privateer**: A privately owned ship authorized by a government (via a "letter of marque") to attack enemy shipping during wartime.
- **Royal Pardon**: An official forgiveness for crimes, offered by the British Crown to pirates who surrendered by a set date (5 September 1718 for this period).
- **Sloop**: A small, single-masted sailing ship, commonly used by pirates for its speed and shallow draft.
- **Broadside**: An attack where all guns on one side of a ship are fired simultaneously.
- **Quartermaster**: On a pirate ship, the officer responsible for discipline, food, and distribution of plunder.
- **Careen**: To beach a ship to clean or repair its hull.
- **Gibbet**: A framework for displaying the bodies of executed criminals as a warning.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **The Shipwreck of *Queen Anne's Revenge***: Historian Robert Lee and others speculate Teach intentionally ran the ship aground to reduce his crew and increase his share of loot. Others view it as an accident during careening.
- **The Pardon's Legitimacy**: The pardon offered immunity only for crimes before 5 January 1718, potentially leaving Teach liable for later acts like the Charles Town blockade. Authorities often waived this condition.
- **Governor Eden's Complicity**: Governor Eden was accused of being Teach's accomplice for legitimizing his plunder. He was investigated but defended by his council.
- **Charles Johnson's Identity**: The author of the seminal 1724 pirate history is anonymous; candidates include playwright Charles Johnson, publisher Charles Rivington, or writer Daniel Defoe.
- **Buried Treasure**: Despite legends, there is no historical evidence Teach buried treasure. The only known pirate to do so was William Kidd.

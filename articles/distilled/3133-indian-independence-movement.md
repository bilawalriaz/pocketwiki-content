# Indian independence movement

## Overview
The Indian independence movement was a decades-long political struggle (c. 1885–1947) to end British colonial rule in the Indian subcontinent. It evolved from moderate petitions into mass civil disobedience and revolutionary activity, ultimately leading to the partition of British India into the independent nations of India and Pakistan.

## Timeline
- **1885** — Indian National Congress (INC) founded.
- **1905** — Partition of Bengal sparks Swadeshi movement and nationalist protests.
- **1919** — Rowlatt Act passed; Jallianwala Bagh massacre occurs.
- **1920–22** — First Non-Cooperation Movement led by Gandhi.
- **1929** — Lahore session: Congress adopts Purna Swaraj (complete independence) goal.
- **1930–31** — Civil Disobedience Movement, including the Salt March.
- **1937** — Provincial elections; Congress wins majorities in several provinces.
- **1940** — Lahore Resolution: Muslim League demands separate Muslim state.
- **1942** — Quit India Movement launched; mass arrests follow.
- **1946** — Royal Indian Navy Mutiny in Bombay.
- **1947** — Indian Independence Act passed; India and Pakistan partitioned and gain independence (15 August).
- **1950** — Constitution of India comes into effect, establishing the Republic.

## Body

### Early Resistance and the 1857 Rebellion
Before organized nationalism, regional rulers and communities resisted British East India Company rule. The Indian Rebellion of 1857, sparked by sepoys' grievances over religious insensitivity and political annexations, was a major uprising. Its suppression led to the end of Company rule; the British Crown assumed direct control via the Government of India Act 1858.

### Rise of Organized Nationalism
The Indian National Congress (INC), founded in 1885, initially functioned as a moderate debating society seeking greater Indian representation. The 1905 Partition of Bengal radicalized opinion, fueling the Swadeshi (self-reliance) movement. The INC later split into moderates and extremists (led by Bal Gangadhar Tilak). The All-India Muslim League was founded in 1906 to safeguard Muslim interests.

### Gandhi and Mass Movements
Mahatma Gandhi returned from South Africa in 1915, championing non-violent civil disobedience (*satyagraha*). The Rowlatt Act (1919) and the Jallianwala Bagh massacre (1919) destroyed hopes for reform within the Empire. Gandhi launched the Non-Cooperation Movement (1920–22), which he called off after violence at Chauri Chaura. The Civil Disobedience Movement (1929–31) followed the Congress's demand for *Purna Swaraj* (complete independence). The Quit India Movement (1942), demanding an immediate British exit, was met with mass arrests but demonstrated the movement's strength.

### Revolutionary and Alternative Movements
Alongside non-violence, revolutionary groups like the Anushilan Samiti and Jugantar in Bengal, and the Hindustan Socialist Republican Association (HSRA) in the north, pursued armed struggle. Figures like Bhagat Singh became iconic. During World War II, Subhas Chandra Bose formed the Indian National Army (INA) with Axis support to fight the British. The Royal Indian Navy Mutiny (1946) further strained British control.

### Partition and Independence
The Muslim League, led by Muhammad Ali Jinnah, demanded a separate Muslim state, culminating in the 1940 Lahore Resolution. Communal tensions and political deadlock led to the 1947 partition of British India into the dominions of India and Pakistan, accompanied by massive violence and displacement. India adopted its constitution in 1950, becoming a republic.

## Terms
- **Swaraj**: Self-rule or self-governance; a key goal of the independence movement.
- **Satyagraha**: Gandhi's philosophy of non-violent civil disobedience and resistance.
- **Swadeshi**: The promotion of indigenous Indian goods and boycott of British imports.
- **Purna Swaraj**: Complete independence, formally demanded by the Congress in 1929.
- **Rowlatt Act (1919)**: British law allowing detention without trial, which sparked widespread protests.
- **Quit India Movement (1942)**: A mass civil disobedience campaign demanding an immediate end to British rule.
- **Indian National Army (INA)**: An armed force formed by Subhas Chandra Bose with Axis support to fight the British.
- **Two-Nation Theory**: The idea, promoted by the Muslim League, that Hindus and Muslims were distinct nations requiring separate states.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The relative importance of non-violent mass movements versus revolutionary armed struggle in achieving independence.
- The causes and responsibility for the partition of India and the ensuing communal violence.
- The impact of World War II on the British decision to withdraw and the trajectory of the independence movement.
- The extent to which the movement represented all sections of Indian society, including peasants, workers, and lower castes.

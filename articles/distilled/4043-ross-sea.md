# Ross Sea

## Overview
The Ross Sea is a deep bay of the Southern Ocean in Antarctica, located between Victoria Land and Marie Byrd Land. It is the southernmost sea on Earth and is named after British explorer James Clark Ross, who visited in 1841. The sea is ecologically significant due to its high biodiversity and relative isolation from human impact, making it a major focus for scientific research and conservation efforts, culminating in the establishment of a large marine protected area in 2016.

## Timeline
- **1841** — The Ross Sea is discovered by the Ross expedition.
- **1911** — Roald Amundsen begins his South Pole expedition from the Bay of Whales on the Ross Ice Shelf.
- **Late 20th C.** — Seismic studies define the major geological features of the Ross Sea.
- **2005** — CCAMLR begins commissioning scientific analysis for Antarctic Marine Protected Areas (MPAs).
- **2010** — The Ross Sea Antarctic toothfish fishery is certified by the Marine Stewardship Council.
- **2012** — The US submits a formal proposal for a Ross Sea MPA to CCAMLR.
- **2013** — An MPA deal fails at a CCAMLR meeting after Russia votes against it.
- **2014** — An MPA proposal is defeated by votes from China and Russia.
- **2016** — An international agreement establishes the Ross Sea as a marine park.
- **2017** — New Zealand scientists discover the breeding ground of the Antarctic toothfish in the northern Ross Sea.
- **2018** — IODP Expedition 374 drills holes in the central Ross Sea to study ice sheet history.

## Body

### Geography and Oceanography
The Ross Sea is a deep bay within the Ross Embayment, with its southern part covered by the Ross Ice Shelf, approximately 200 miles (320 km) from the South Pole. Its circulation is dominated by a slow-moving, wind-driven gyre influenced by three submarine ridges. A key oceanographic feature is the Circumpolar Deep Water (CDW), a relatively warm, salty, and nutrient-rich water mass that flows onto the continental shelf, moderating ice cover and fueling primary production. The sea is ice-covered for most of the year, with ice concentrations heavily influenced by winds.

### Geology
The Ross Sea overlies a deep continental shelf, averaging about 500 meters in depth—much deeper than the global average due to erosion and sediment deposition from past ice sheet cycles. The underlying geology consists of basement rocks faulted into four major north-trending graben systems (basins), formed during the Cretaceous rifting of the Zealandia microcontinent from Antarctica. These basins are filled with sediments. A major regional unconformity, RSU-6, is proposed to mark the first appearance of the Antarctic Ice Sheet in the Oligocene. Drilling projects like DSDP Leg 28, the Cape Roberts Project, and IODP Expedition 374 have recovered cores that help define the region's glacial and climate history.

### Ecology and Conservation
The nutrient-rich waters support a highly diverse ecosystem, including at least 10 mammal species (e.g., minke whales, seals), 6 bird species (e.g., Adélie and emperor penguins), and 95 fish species (e.g., Antarctic toothfish). The sea remains relatively unaffected by human activities. However, the industrial Antarctic toothfish fishery, which began in 1996, has raised concerns about population declines and impacts on predators like orcas. After years of negotiation and failed proposals blocked by nations including Russia and China, the Commission for the Conservation of Antarctic Marine Living Resources (CCAMLR) declared the Ross Sea a marine park in 2016. This protected over 1.5 million square kilometers but includes a controversial 35-year sunset provision.

## Terms
- **Polynya:** An area of open water surrounded by sea ice, often maintained by winds or ocean currents.
- **Circumpolar Deep Water (CDW):** A relatively warm, salty, and nutrient-rich water mass that flows around Antarctica and onto continental shelves.
- **Graben:** A depressed block of the Earth's crust bordered by parallel faults, forming a basin.
- **Unconformity (RSU-6):** A gap in the geological record, here marking a major erosion event linked to the onset of the Antarctic Ice Sheet.
- **Marine Protected Area (MPA):** A designated area of ocean where human activities are restricted to protect biodiversity and ecosystems.
- **Sunset provision:** A clause in an agreement that causes it to expire after a set period unless renewed.
- **CCAMLR:** The Commission for the Conservation of Antarctic Marine Living Resources, an international body managing Antarctic marine resources.

## Debates and open questions
- **MPA Effectiveness:** The 35-year sunset provision in the Ross Sea MPA is controversial, as conservation groups argue it undermines the goal of permanent protection.
- **Fishery Impacts:** There is debate over the sustainability of the Antarctic toothfish fishery, with reports of population declines in some areas conflicting with its sustainability certification.
- **Geopolitical Authority:** Past failures to establish the MPA highlighted disputes over CCAMLR's authority and the geopolitical interests of member nations like Russia and China.

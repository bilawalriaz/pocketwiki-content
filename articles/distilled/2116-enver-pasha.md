# Enver Pasha

## Overview
İsmâil Enver Pasha (1881–1922) was a pivotal Ottoman military officer and revolutionary who rose to become one of the "Three Pashas" ruling the empire during World War I. As a key leader of the Young Turk Revolution and later Minister of War, he was instrumental in bringing the Ottoman Empire into WWI and was a principal perpetrator of the Armenian, Greek, and Assyrian genocides. His career embodies the empire's final, turbulent decades, marked by revolutionary idealism, military disaster, and the pursuit of pan-Turkic ambitions.

## Body

### Early Life and the Young Turk Revolution
Enver was born in Constantinople and educated at the Ottoman Military Academy, where he was a classmate and rival of Mustafa Kemal. Stationed in Macedonia, he joined the Committee of Union and Progress (CUP), the political arm of the Young Turk movement. On 3 July 1908, he and Ahmed Niyazi Bey launched the Young Turk Revolution, forcing Sultan Abdul Hamid II to restore the constitution. Enver was hailed as a "hero of the revolution," which established a constitutional government.

### Rise to Power and the 1913 Coup
Following crises like the 31 March Incident and the Balkan Wars, the CUP grew disillusioned with liberal politics. On 23 January 1913, Enver led a coup, seizing power for the CUP. He became Minister of War in January 1914, reorganizing the army by purging older officers and promoting young, educated ones. He also married Princess Naciye Sultan, entering the royal family.

### World War I and the Genocides
Enver was a chief architect of the Ottoman-German alliance. He authorized German warships to enter the Dardanelles and, without full cabinet approval, allowed them to attack Russian ports, precipitating Ottoman entry into WWI. His only field command was the disastrous Battle of Sarikamish (Jan 1915), where a large portion of his army perished. He subsequently blamed Armenians for the defeat, initiating their deportation and massacre, which culminated in the Armenian genocide. He later commanded forces around the capital and oversaw the "Army of Islam" campaign in the Caucasus.

### Exile and Death
After the Ottoman defeat, Enver escaped in November 1918. He was tried in absentia and sentenced to death. He traveled through Europe, attempting to reach Moscow and support the Turkish nationalist movement led by his rival, Mustafa Kemal, who rejected his involvement. In 1921, Enver went to Central Asia, where he initially worked for the Bolsheviks but defected to lead the Basmachi Revolt against them, aiming to realize pan-Turkic dreams. He was killed in combat near Dushanbe on 4 August 1922. His remains were repatriated to Turkey in 1996.

## Terms
*   **Committee of Union and Progress (CUP):** The political organization behind the Young Turk Revolution, which later ruled the Ottoman Empire as a one-party state.
*   **Young Turk Revolution:** The 1908 uprising that restored the Ottoman Constitution and parliamentary rule, initially led by CUP officers like Enver.
*   **Three Pashas:** The triumvirate of Enver (War), Talaat (Interior/Grand Vizier), and Djemal (Navy) who ruled the Ottoman Empire during WWI.
*   **Mektebli:** An officer educated in modern military academies, contrasting with the older *alaylı* tradition of promotion from the ranks.
*   **Basmachi Revolt:** A major anti-Soviet insurgency in Central Asia in the early 1920s, which Enver Pasha joined and briefly led.
*   **Pan-Turkism:** An ideology advocating for the political, cultural, and ethnic unity of all Turkic peoples, a goal Enver pursued in his final years.
*   **Armistice of Mudros:** The agreement signed on 30 October 1918 that ended Ottoman participation in WWI and led to Allied occupation.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Responsibility for Genocide:** While the source states Enver was a "principal perpetrator" of the Armenian, Greek, and Assyrian genocides, the precise extent of his personal responsibility versus that of the broader CUP leadership is a subject of historical analysis.
*   **Military Competence:** Enver's reputation as a military leader is highly contested. He was celebrated as a hero after the Young Turk Revolution and the recapture of Edirne, but his command at Sarikamish is universally regarded as a catastrophic failure.
*   **Legacy in Modern Turkey:** Enver's image remains controversial. He was rehabilitated in the 1990s by President Süleyman Demirel and has been praised by later leaders like President Erdoğan, contrasting with his historical rivalry with Atatürk, the founder of the Turkish Republic.

# Sheikh Mujibur Rahman

## Overview
Sheikh Mujibur Rahman (1920–1975) was the founding father and first president of Bangladesh. He led the Bengali nationalist movement that culminated in the country's independence from Pakistan in 1971, but his post-independence rule was marked by economic crisis and authoritarianism, ending in his assassination. His legacy remains deeply revered and contested in Bangladesh.

## Timeline
- **17 March 1920** — Born in Tungipara, Bengal, British India.
- **1949** — Co-founded the Awami Muslim League.
- **1966** — Presented the Six-Point autonomy plan for East Pakistan.
- **7 March 1971** — Delivered the historic 7 March speech, calling for resistance.
- **26 March 1971** — Issued the Proclamation of Bangladeshi Independence; arrested by Pakistan Army.
- **10 January 1972** — Returned to independent Bangladesh as its leader.
- **4 November 1972** — Constitution of Bangladesh adopted, establishing a secular, parliamentary republic.
- **25 January 1975** — Fourth Amendment to the constitution established a one-party state (BAKSAL).
- **15 August 1975** — Assassinated in a military coup in Dhaka.

## Body

### Early Life and Political Rise
Born into an aristocratic Bengali Muslim family, Mujib became a student activist in Calcutta. He initially supported the Muslim League and the Pakistan movement. After partition, he studied law at the University of Dhaka and was expelled for political activities. In 1949, he became a joint secretary of the newly formed Awami Muslim League, a liberal, secular, and left-wing faction. He emerged as a key defender of Bengali rights, playing a central role in the Bengali Language Movement of 1952, which demanded Bengali be recognized as a national language of Pakistan.

### Struggle for Bengali Autonomy
Elected to Pakistan's parliament in 1954, Mujib spent a total of 13 years in prison under British and Pakistani rule for his political activism. By the 1960s, he championed Bengali nationalism and became the undisputed leader of East Pakistan. His 1966 Six-Point plan, demanding significant provincial autonomy, was a turning point. The Agartala Conspiracy Case (1967–69), in which he was accused of sedition, further galvanized public support. Following a mass uprising, he was released in 1969 and conferred the title "Bangabandhu" (Friend of Bengal).

### The Liberation War
Mujib's Awami League won a landslide victory in Pakistan's 1970 general election. When the military junta refused to transfer power, he delivered his famous 7 March 1971 speech, which is recognized by UNESCO. After the Pakistan Army launched a crackdown on 25 March 1971, Mujib was arrested. He formally declared Bangladesh's independence via telegram from jail. The ensuing Bangladesh Liberation War ended with Pakistani surrender in December 1971, following Indian intervention.

### Post-Independence Rule and Crisis
Mujib returned to lead a war-devastated country in January 1972. He oversaw the creation of a secular constitution and the normalization of diplomatic relations. However, his government struggled with corruption, political anarchy, and economic mismanagement, which led to a famine in 1974. To combat a left-wing insurgency, he formed the Jatiya Rakkhi Bahini, a paramilitary force implicated in human rights abuses. In 1975, he declared a one-party state (BAKSAL), abolishing democratic institutions and civil liberties. He was assassinated in a coup on 15 August 1975.

## Terms
- **Bangabandhu**: An honorary title meaning "Friend of Bengal," conferred on Sheikh Mujibur Rahman in 1969.
- **Six-Point Movement**: A 1966 political program demanding provincial autonomy for East Pakistan, considered a catalyst for the independence movement.
- **Mukti Bahini**: The Bengali guerrilla resistance force during the 1971 Bangladesh Liberation War.
- **Jatiya Rakkhi Bahini**: A special paramilitary force formed by Mujib in 1972, loyal to him and accused of human rights violations.
- **BAKSAL**: The Bangladesh Krishak Sramik Awami League, the sole legal political party established under the 1975 one-party state system.
- **Operation Searchlight**: The codename for the Pakistan Army's crackdown on Bengali civilians and political leaders on 25 March 1971.
- **Secularism**: A state principle enshrined in Bangladesh's 1972 constitution, later removed and reinstated.

## Debates and open questions
Mujib's legacy is deeply divisive. He is universally credited as the "Father of the Nation" for leading Bangladesh to independence, a title constitutionally recognized in 2011 but later contested. His post-independence record is criticized for economic failure, the 1974 famine, and the authoritarian turn with the BAKSAL system. Scholars and political analysts continue to debate the extent of his responsibility for the economic crisis versus the challenges of post-war reconstruction, and the motivations behind his shift to one-party rule.

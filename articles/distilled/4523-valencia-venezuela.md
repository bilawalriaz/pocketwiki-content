# Valencia, Venezuela

## Overview
Valencia is the capital of Carabobo State and Venezuela's third-largest city, serving as a major economic and industrial hub. It is the core of the Valencia-Maracay metropolitan region, the country's second most populous after Caracas. Its history spans from pre-Columbian settlements to its founding in 1555, making it one of Venezuela's oldest Spanish colonial cities.

- **c. 1000** — Migration from Orinoco area leads to Valencioide culture.

- **1561** — Entered by Spanish conquistador Lope de Aguirre.
- **1677** — Raided by French pirates, destroying early colonial documents.
- **June 24, 1821** — Battle of Carabobo fought nearby, securing Venezuelan independence.
- **1830** — Becomes capital of Venezuela after separation from Gran Colombia.
- **1858** — Becomes seat of national government again after the March Revolution.
- **November 15, 1892** — University of Valencia (future University of Carabobo) founded.
- **1935** — Population explosion begins after dictator Juan Vicente Gómez's death, fueled by oil and immigration.
- **1988** — First direct local elections held.

## Body

### History and Foundation
The Valencia region has been inhabited since the fourth millennium BC. Around AD 200-1000, a significant settlement developed near Lake Valencia. Later migrations from the Orinoco area, likely via the Pao River, around 1000 AD fused with existing populations to create the Valencioide culture. These Arawak groups were hunter-gatherers, fishers, and cultivators of maize and cotton, living on artificial mounds in flood-prone valleys. The Spanish city was founded in 1555, eight years before Caracas, and became the first Spanish settlement in north-central Venezuela. The encomienda system placed indigenous populations under Spanish control, leading to displacement and intermarriage. Key historical events include a 1561 visit by Lope de Aguirre, a destructive 1677 pirate raid, and the nearby 1821 Battle of Carabobo, which sealed independence from Spain. Valencia served as Venezuela's capital in 1830 and again briefly in 1858. A major population boom occurred after 1935 due to oil revenues and immigration.

### Geography and Climate
Valencia sits in a central valley at an elevation of 520 metres (1,710 ft), surrounded by the Coastal Range (Cordillera de la Costa) mountains. Lake Valencia, Venezuela's second-largest lake, lies on its eastern outskirts, while the Llanos grasslands extend to the south. The city has a tropical savanna climate (Köppen: Aw) with a wet season from May to November and a dry season from December to April. The average annual temperature is 26 °C (79 °F). Environmental concerns include pollution of the Cabriales River and waste disposal issues at the La Guásima site southwest of the city.

### Government and Administration
The city comprises five municipalities: Valencia, Libertador, Los Guayos, Naguanagua, and San Diego. Each municipal government has four main functions: executive (led by the mayor), legislative (Municipal Council), comptroller (municipal comptroller's office), and planning (Local Public Planning Council).

### Education and Media
The University of Carabobo, founded in 1892, is the main higher education institution and one of Venezuela's most important public universities. Other universities include UNITEC, UAM, and UJAP. Major regional newspapers are *El Carabobeño* and *Notitarde*. Key television stations are Ecovisión and DAT TV.

### Transportation and Infrastructure
Valencia is connected by a national highway network. The Metro de Valencia, inaugurated in 2007, has 9 operational stations. Mass transit includes traditional buses (bus, buseta, microbus) and the VALBUS system. The Arturo Michelena International Airport is the nation's third busiest.

### Culture and Landmarks
Notable sites include the colonial Plaza Bolívar, the Valencia Atheneum cultural center, and the Municipal Theater (finished 1895). Museums feature the Páez Mansion (independence-era history), the Celis Mansion (1765 colonial house), and the Museum of History and Anthropology. Parks include Negra Hipólita, Valencia Metropolitan, and the Valencia Aquarium, which houses trained Amazon river dolphins. The Plaza Monumental de Valencia is the world's second-largest bullring.

## Terms
- **Encomienda:** A Spanish colonial system granting settlers control over indigenous labor and tribute.
- **Valencioide culture:** An archaeological culture formed by the fusion of earlier settlements and later migrations around Lake Valencia c. 1000 AD.
- **Tropical savanna climate (Köppen: Aw):** A climate characterized by distinct wet and dry seasons and warm temperatures year-round.
- **Cordillera de la Costa:** The Coastal Range, a mountain system running along Venezuela's northern coast.
- **Llanos:** The vast tropical grassland plains of northern South America, extending into Venezuela south of Valencia.
- **Metro de Valencia:** The city's rapid transit system, which began operations in 2007.

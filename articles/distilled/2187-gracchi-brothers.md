# Gracchi brothers

## Overview
The Gracchi brothers, Tiberius and Gaius, were Roman tribunes in the late 2nd century BC who proposed sweeping social and political reforms. Their efforts to address land inequality and empower the common people were met with violent opposition, setting a dangerous precedent for political violence that contributed to the collapse of the Roman Republic. Their legacy is debated, seen alternately as martyrs for the people or demagogues who undermined the state.

## Timeline
- **c. 163 BC** — Tiberius Gracchus born.
- **c. 154 BC** — Gaius Gracchus born.
- **133 BC** — Tiberius, as tribune, passes agrarian law to redistribute public land; is killed by a senatorial mob.
- **129 BC** — Senate limits the power of the Gracchan land commission.
- **123–122 BC** — Gaius, as tribune, enacts broad reforms including grain subsidies, jury reform, and colonial projects.
- **121 BC** — Gaius is killed after the Senate passes the first *senatus consultum ultimum* against him.
- **111 BC** — A new agrarian law overhauls Roman public land policy, ending the Gracchan commission.

## Body

### Background and Rural Conditions
Ancient sources claimed a severe decline in Italy's rural population and the rise of slave-staffed estates (*latifundia*) displaced small farmers. Modern archaeology, however, suggests this decline was overstated and localized, not a general crisis. Census data showing fewer citizens likely reflects draft evasion, not actual depopulation. The Gracchan reforms were thus a reaction to a perceived, not necessarily actual, crisis, possibly generalized from local conditions in coastal Etruria.

### Tiberius Gracchus's Reforms and Death
As tribune in 133 BC, Tiberius proposed a law to survey public land (*ager publicus*), limit holdings to 500 *jugera* (about 125 hectares), and redistribute the excess to poor Roman citizens. This aimed to boost the military recruitment pool. He unprecedentedly deposed a vetoing tribune to pass the law. His later proposal to use the inheritance of Attalus III of Pergamon for the land commission was seen as a threat to senatorial authority. He was beaten to death by senators led by Publius Cornelius Scipio Nasica Serapio, setting a precedent for political violence.

### Gaius Gracchus's Reforms and Death
Gaius, tribune in 123–122 BC, expanded his brother's program with broader reforms. He established subsidized grain for citizens, reformed juries for corruption courts to include equestrians (wealthy non-senators), and proposed colonies abroad, including at Carthage. He also proposed extending Roman citizenship to Italian allies, a measure that was vetoed. In 121 BC, when the Senate moved to repeal some of his laws, Gaius resisted. The Senate passed the first *senatus consultum ultimum* ("final decree"), authorizing the consul to use force. Gaius and his ally Marcus Fulvius Flaccus were killed.

### Aftermath and Impact
Most of the Gracchan legislation survived their deaths. The land commission operated until 111 BC, distributing over 3,268 square kilometers, primarily in southern Italy. However, the reforms strained relations with Italian allies, as redistribution often meant evicting them from ancestral lands they occupied. The violent suppression of the brothers established that political violence was an acceptable tool, a key factor in the Republic's later crises. The *senatus consultum ultimum* became a controversial tool for the Senate to bypass legal norms.

## Terms
- **Ager publicus:** Public land conquered by Rome, legally state-owned but often occupied by citizens and allies.
- **Tribune of the plebs:** A magistrate elected to protect the rights of the common people (*plebs*), with the power to veto laws and officials.
- **Senatus consultum ultimum:** An emergency decree of the Senate, urging a magistrate to take whatever action necessary to preserve the state, used to justify extralegal violence.
- **Equestrians:** A wealthy social class below the senators, often involved in tax collection and business.
- **Jugera:** A Roman unit of land area, approximately 0.25 hectares (0.62 acres).
- **Latifundia:** Large, commercial agricultural estates, often worked by slaves.
- **Provocatio:** The right of a Roman citizen to appeal a magistrate's capital sentence to the people.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Cause of Rural Crisis:** Scholars debate whether the Gracchan reforms addressed a real, widespread decline of the Italian peasantry or a localized problem exaggerated for political purposes.
- **Motives of the Gracchi:** Ancient sources differ on whether the brothers were motivated by genuine concern for the poor or personal ambition and a desire for political glory.
- **Impact of Reforms:** The actual economic impact of the land redistribution is debated; some evidence suggests the allotted plots may have been too small or infertile to be viable for families.
- **Role in the Social War:** Modern reassessments increasingly view the Gracchan land law as a major contributing factor to the Social War (91–88 BC), as it reasserted Roman claims to land long occupied by Italian allies.

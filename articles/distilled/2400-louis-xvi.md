# Louis XVI

## Overview
Louis XVI was the last king of France, reigning from 1774 until the monarchy was abolished in 1792. His reign was defined by financial crisis, failed reforms, and the French Revolution, which ultimately led to his execution. His story illustrates the collapse of the *ancien régime* and the violent birth of modern French republicanism.

## Timeline
- **23 August 1754** — Born Louis-Auguste at the Palace of Versailles.
- **10 May 1774** — Becomes King of France and Navarre after the death of Louis XV.
- **7 November 1787** — Signs the Edict of Versailles, granting tolerance to non-Catholics.
- **5 May 1789** — Convenes the Estates General, which had not met since 1614.
- **14 July 1789** — The storming of the Bastille marks the start of the French Revolution.
- **6 October 1789** — Forced by a Parisian mob to move from Versailles to the Tuileries Palace.
- **21 June 1791** — The royal family's failed Flight to Varennes destroys his legitimacy.
- **20 April 1792** — France declares war on Austria, beginning the French Revolutionary Wars.
- **10 August 1792** — The Tuileries Palace is stormed; the royal family is arrested.
- **21 September 1792** — The monarchy is abolished; the French First Republic is proclaimed.
- **21 January 1793** — Louis XVI is executed by guillotine in Paris.

## Body

### Early Life and Accession
Louis-Auguste was the second son of the Dauphin Louis and Maria Josepha of Saxony. He became heir apparent in 1765 after his father's death. His strict, conservative education did not prepare him for the throne. He married Marie Antoinette of Austria in 1770, a union initially unpopular due to France's recent defeat by Austria in the Seven Years' War. The marriage was not consummated until 1777, a delay that fueled public scandal. They eventually had four children, though their failure to produce an heir for years was a source of great strain.

### Reign and Financial Crisis
Louis XVI inherited a monarchy in deep debt. His early reign saw attempts at Enlightenment-inspired reform, such as increasing religious tolerance, but these were blocked by the nobility. Economic policies, including deregulation of the grain market, led to food scarcity and riots. Massive French spending to support the American Revolution (1775–1783) worsened the financial crisis. Attempts at fiscal reform by ministers like Turgot, Necker, and Calonne failed, leading Louis to convene the Estates General in 1789 to approve new taxes.

### The French Revolution
The Estates General quickly evolved into the National Assembly, challenging royal authority. Louis's indecisiveness and perceived conservatism turned public opinion against him. The storming of the Bastille (14 July 1789) forced him to recognize the Assembly's authority. His attempted escape to Varennes in June 1791 was a catastrophic failure that confirmed suspicions of his collusion with foreign powers and destroyed his remaining legitimacy. A constitutional monarchy was declared, but Louis was effectively a prisoner.

### Trial and Execution
With the outbreak of civil and international war, Louis was arrested in August 1792. The monarchy was abolished, and he was tried by the National Convention for high treason. He was found guilty and executed by guillotine on 21 January 1793. His death ended over a thousand years of continuous French monarchy and radicalized the Revolution.

## Terms
*   **Ancien Régime:** The political and social system of the Kingdom of France before the Revolution of 1789.
*   **Estates General:** A representative assembly of the three classes of French society (clergy, nobility, and commoners), convened by the king in times of crisis.
*   **National Assembly:** The revolutionary body formed by members of the Third Estate in 1789, which declared itself the true representative of the French people.
*   **Flight to Varennes:** The royal family's failed attempt to escape Paris in June 1791, which fatally undermined Louis XVI's credibility.
*   **Civil Constitution of the Clergy:** A 1790 law that subordinated the Roman Catholic Church in France to the state, causing a major schism.
*   **Girondins:** A moderate political faction during the Revolution that initially sought to preserve the monarchy under a constitution.
*   **The Mountain (Montagnards):** The radical, left-wing faction in the National Convention that demanded the king's execution.
*   **Guillotine:** The device used for Louis XVI's execution, which became a symbol of the Reign of Terror.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **The King's Guilt:** Historians debate the extent of Louis XVI's active conspiracy with foreign powers versus his passive resistance to the Revolution.
*   **The Vote for Execution:** The narrow margin (361 to 360) for immediate execution has led to analysis of the political pressures and personal convictions of the deputies.
*   **The Queen's Influence:** The role of Marie Antoinette in encouraging Louis's resistance and seeking foreign intervention remains a subject of scholarly discussion.
*   **Legacy:** Historians like Jules Michelet argued that the execution was a tragic mistake that radicalized the Revolution, while others see it as a necessary, if brutal, step in establishing a republic.

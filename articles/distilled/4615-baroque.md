# Baroque

## Overview
The Baroque was a major Western artistic style that flourished from the early 1600s to the mid-18th century, characterized by grandeur, dramatic movement, rich detail, and emotional intensity. It emerged in Rome as a tool of the Catholic Counter-Reformation to inspire awe and devotion, and it spread across Europe and its colonies, evolving into regional variations before giving way to the more delicate Rococo and eventually Neoclassicism.

## Timeline
- **c. 1545–1563** — Council of Trent encourages art that communicates religious themes with emotional involvement.
- **1584** — Church of the Gesù in Rome, with the first Baroque façade, is completed.
- **Early 1600s** — Baroque style begins in Rome and spreads to Italy, France, Spain, and Portugal.
- **1605–1619** — Façade and new nave of St. Peter's Basilica in Rome are built, creating dramatic contrasts.
- **1623–1634** — Gian Lorenzo Bernini creates St. Peter's Baldachin in Rome.
- **1634–1646** — Francesco Borromini builds the Church of San Carlo alle Quattro Fontane in Rome.
- **1661–1710** — Expansion of the Palace of Versailles under Louis XIV defines French Baroque.
- **1680–1750** — High period of ornate Baroque and Rococo architecture in Central Europe.
- **1730s** — The more flamboyant Rococo style emerges from the Baroque in France and Central Europe.
- **1745–1747** — Sanssouci palace in Potsdam, Prussia, is built in the Baroque style.
- **1888** — Heinrich Wölfflin publishes *Renaissance und Barock*, the first serious academic study of the style.

## Body

### Origins and Characteristics
The Baroque style originated in Rome in the early 17th century, encouraged by the Catholic Church following the Council of Trent (1545–1563). The Church sought to counter Protestant austerity with art that was emotionally engaging and popular. Baroque architecture is defined by contrast, movement, exuberant detail, and grandeur. Key features include large central spaces in churches, dramatic domes, *quadratura* (trompe-l'œil ceiling paintings), twisted columns, and ornate decoration like cartouches. The style aimed to create a sense of awe and theatricality, often using optical illusions.

### Regional Variations in Europe
The Baroque adapted to local cultures across Europe. In Italy, architects like Bernini and Borromini pioneered dramatic forms. Spanish Baroque, driven by the Church, featured extreme ornamentation, exemplified by the Churrigueresque style. French Baroque, associated with Louis XIV, was more restrained and classical, as seen at Versailles. In the Dutch Republic, a sober, civic Baroque emerged, reflecting Protestant values. Central European Baroque, especially in Austria and Bohemia, became highly ornate, often blending into Rococo. Portuguese Baroque was influenced by Jesuit "plain style" architecture, which was practical and easily decorated.

### Spread to the Colonies
Baroque art and architecture spread to the Spanish and Portuguese colonies in the Americas and Asia. In the Americas, it blended with indigenous and local traditions, creating unique hybrid styles. Notable examples include the Churrigueresque façades in Mexico and the ornate churches of Peru and Brazil. In Asia, Portuguese colonies like Goa developed a Baroque mixed with Hindu elements, while Spanish Baroque appeared in the Philippines. This colonial Baroque was a key tool for religious conversion and cultural integration.

### Painting and Sculpture
Baroque painting emphasized intense color, dramatic light and shadow (*chiaroscuro*), movement, and emotional expression. Caravaggio pioneered realistic figures spotlit against dark backgrounds. Other major painters include Rubens (Flemish), Velázquez (Spanish), and Poussin (French, more classical). Sculpture was dominated by Gian Lorenzo Bernini, whose works expressed vivid emotion and movement. Baroque art was often allegorical, telling stories with symbolic messages for an educated audience.

### Music and Dance
The Baroque period (c. 1600–1750) was a time of musical innovation, producing new forms like opera, the concerto, and the sonata. Composers such as Monteverdi, Vivaldi, Bach, and Handel explored rich harmonies, ornamentation, and dramatic expression. The piano was invented during this era. Classical ballet also originated in the Baroque court, with Louis XIV founding the first professional dance academy in 1662.

### Transition to Rococo and Legacy
By the 1730s, the Baroque evolved into the Rococo, a lighter, more playful, and asymmetrical style focused on secular pleasure and domestic interiors. After falling out of favor in the 18th and 19th centuries, the Baroque was rehabilitated by art historians like Heinrich Wölfflin in the late 19th century. Its influence resurfaced in 19th-century revivals and eclecticism, and it saw renewed appreciation in the postmodern era.

## Terms
- **Counter-Reformation:** The Catholic Church's response to the Protestant Reformation, using art and architecture to inspire faith.
- **Chiaroscuro:** A painting technique using strong contrasts between light and dark to model forms and create drama.
- **Quadratura:** Trompe-l'œil ceiling paintings that create an illusion of extended architectural space.
- **Cartouche:** An ornamental plaque, often oval, used in architecture for decoration or inscriptions.
- **Churrigueresque:** An extremely ornate Spanish Baroque style named after the Churriguera family of architects.
- **Rococo:** A later, more delicate and asymmetrical style that evolved from the Baroque, emphasizing lightness and ornament.
- **Trompe-l'œil:** An artistic technique that uses realistic imagery to create the optical illusion that the depicted objects exist in three dimensions.
- **Solomonic column:** A spiraling, twisted column, often associated with Baroque architecture.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The exact origin of the word "baroque" is debated, with theories linking it to the Portuguese for "flawed pearl," a term in medieval logic, or the painter Federico Barocci.
- The boundaries between the late Baroque and the early Rococo are often blurred, with some scholars considering Rococo simply a "late Baroque" phase.
- The degree to which Baroque art was a deliberate tool of the Counter-Reformation versus a broader cultural movement is a subject of ongoing scholarly discussion.

# Hudson Bay

## Overview
Hudson Bay is a large, shallow inland sea in northeastern Canada, forming a significant part of the Arctic Ocean. It is the world's second-largest body of water called a "bay" and is central to the geography, history, and ecology of northern North America. Its vast drainage basin covers much of central Canada and parts of the northern United States.

## Timeline
- **1610** — Henry Hudson explores the bay, becoming trapped in ice for the winter.
- **1611** — Hudson's crew mutinies, leaving him and others adrift; their fate is unknown.
- **1668** — The ship *Nonsuch* reaches the bay, leading to the founding of the Hudson's Bay Company (HBC).
- **1670** — The English Crown grants the HBC a charter for a fur-trading monopoly over the Hudson Bay watershed (Rupert's Land).
- **1713** — The Treaty of Utrecht ends French claims to the Hudson Bay region.
- **1870** — The HBC's monopoly is abolished; Rupert's Land is ceded to Canada.
- **1912** — Western and eastern shores are transferred to adjacent provinces, but the bay remains part of the Northwest Territories.
- **1929** — The Port of Churchill, Manitoba, is established as a deep-sea port for wheat exports.
- **2016** — The Port of Churchill is shut down by its owner, OmniTRAX.
- **2018** — The port and railway are sold to the Arctic Gateway Group consortium.
- **2019** — The Port of Churchill resumes grain shipping and cargo services.

## Body

### Geography and Climate
Hudson Bay is an epicontinental sea with an average depth of about 100 m (330 ft). It is largely frozen from mid-December to mid-June. The bay has a lower salinity than ocean water due to low evaporation, limited ocean connection, and massive freshwater runoff from its 3,861,400 km² (1,490,900 sq mi) drainage basin. This lower salinity raises the water's freezing point, extending ice cover. The climate ranges from polar (ET) in the north to subarctic (Dfc) and humid continental (Dfb) in the south. The western shores are the low-lying Hudson Bay Lowlands, Canada's largest peatland, while the eastern shores are rocky and part of the Canadian Shield.

### Geology
The bay sits within the Hudson Bay Basin, a structural depression in the Canadian Shield filled with up to 2,500 m (8,200 ft) of sedimentary rocks. The basin formed as the Canadian Shield subsided during the Ordovician and Silurian periods. The Precambrian Shield beneath is composed of ancient cratons that collided about 1.9–1.8 Ga (billion years ago) in the Trans-Hudson orogeny. The bay's center lies within a large free-air gravity anomaly, partly attributed to ongoing glacial isostatic adjustment from the melting of the Laurentide Ice Sheet. The distinctive Nastapoka arc on the eastern shore is debated as either a tectonic feature or a possible ancient impact structure, though the latter lacks conclusive evidence.

### Economy and Communities
The coast is sparsely populated, with about a dozen communities, many founded as HBC trading posts. The Port of Churchill is Canada's only Arctic deep-water port, connected by rail to the national network. After a closure from 2016–2019, it was revived by a consortium including First Nations. The potential "Arctic Bridge" trade route between Churchill and Murmansk, Russia, has gained interest due to reduced Arctic ice. Historically, the HBC built coastal forts for the fur trade and later for Cold War defense.

## Terms
- **Epicontinental sea**: A shallow sea that lies on top of a continental shelf.
- **Rupert's Land**: The territory granted to the Hudson's Bay Company in 1670, covering the Hudson Bay drainage basin.
- **Glacial isostatic adjustment**: The ongoing rise of land masses that were depressed by the weight of ice sheets during the last glacial period.
- **Free-air gravity anomaly**: A variation in the Earth's gravitational pull, often indicating subsurface density differences.
- **Nastapoka arc**: A 155-degree curved shoreline feature on eastern Hudson Bay, of debated origin.
- **Trans-Hudson orogeny**: A major mountain-building event ~1.9–1.8 Ga when ancient continental plates collided.
- **Permafrost**: Ground that remains frozen for two or more consecutive years.
- **Peatland**: A wetland characterized by the accumulation of peat, formed from partially decayed vegetation.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Origin of the Nastapoka arc**: Scientists disagree on whether this semicircular shoreline is a tectonic feature from ancient continental collisions or a rare Precambrian impact structure. No definitive evidence for an impact has been found.
- **Cause of the gravity anomaly**: While glacial isostatic adjustment explains 25–45% of the free-air gravity anomaly over Hudson Bay, the remainder is attributed to deeper mantle convection processes, the details of which are still studied.
- **Classification**: Authorities debate whether Hudson Bay is part of the Arctic Ocean or the Atlantic Ocean, based on its water budget and hydrographic connections.

# Emile Berliner

## Overview
Emile Berliner (1851–1929) was a German-American inventor and businessman whose most significant contribution was the invention of the lateral-cut flat disc record and the gramophone, which revolutionized the recording and playback of sound. His work laid the foundation for the modern music industry, and he also made pioneering contributions to early helicopter technology.

## Timeline
- **May 20, 1851** — Born in Hanover, Germany.
- **1870** — Migrated to the United States to avoid the Franco-Prussian War draft.
- **1877** — Moved to Boston; began working for Bell Telephone.
- **1883** — Returned to Washington, D.C., as a private researcher.
- **1887** — Received U.S. patent for the gramophone (horizontal recording).
- **1894** — Founded the United States Gramophone Company.
- **1901** — U.S. Court of Appeals declared his telephone transmitter patent void, awarding rights to Edison.
- **1909** — Founded the Gyro Motor Company to develop helicopter technology.
- **June 16, 1922** — Demonstrated a helicopter for the U.S. Navy with his son, Henry.
- **August 3, 1929** — Died of a heart attack in Washington, D.C.

## Body

### Early Life and Telephone Work
Born into a Jewish merchant family in Hanover, Berliner apprenticed as a merchant but pursued invention. He emigrated to the U.S. in 1870, working various jobs while studying physics at Cooper Union. His interest in audio technology led him to invent an improved telephone transmitter, an early microphone. The Bell Telephone Company acquired his patent, but it was contested by Thomas Edison. A 1901 court ruling voided Berliner's patent, stating Edison preceded him in the invention of the carbon transmitter.

### The Gramophone and Flat Disc Record
Berliner's most famous invention was the gramophone, which used a lateral-cut flat disc record instead of the cylinders used by Edison's phonograph. Early toy gramophones with hard rubber discs were manufactured in Germany by 1890, but U.S. patents were pending. In 1894, Berliner secured $25,000 in investment to found the United States Gramophone Company, aiming to commercialize the device beyond a toy. This technology became the standard for recorded music.

### Helicopter Development and Later Work
From 1907 to 1926, Berliner focused on vertical flight. He developed a lightweight rotary engine, improving upon heavier inline engines. In 1909, he founded the Gyro Motor Company. By 1910, he was experimenting with a vertically mounted tail rotor to counteract torque, a key configuration for practical helicopters. In 1922, he and his son Henry demonstrated a helicopter for the U.S. Navy. Henry later founded the Berliner Aircraft Company, which merged into Berliner-Joyce Aircraft in 1929. Berliner also invented a loom for mass cloth production and acoustic tile, and he advocated for public health and women's equality.

## Terms
- **Lateral-cut flat disc record**: A sound recording medium where the groove is cut laterally (side-to-side) on a flat disc, the format Berliner pioneered.
- **Gramophone**: The device invented by Berliner to record and reproduce sound using his flat disc records.
- **Telephone transmitter**: An early type of microphone that converts sound into electrical signals for transmission.
- **Rotary engine**: An internal combustion engine where the cylinders rotate around a central crankshaft, which Berliner adapted for lighter weight in aircraft.
- **Torque**: The rotational force that, in a single-main-rotor helicopter, must be counteracted to prevent the body from spinning; Berliner's tail rotor design addressed this.

## Debates and Open Questions
The primary historical debate concerns the invention of the carbon telephone transmitter. While Berliner received a patent, a 1901 U.S. Court of Appeals ruling voided it, declaring that "Edison preceded Berliner in the transmission of speech" and that the use of carbon in a transmitter was Edison's invention. This legal and historical dispute over priority and credit remains a noted point in the history of telecommunications.

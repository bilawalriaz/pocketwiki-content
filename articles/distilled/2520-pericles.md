# Pericles

## Overview
Pericles (c. 495–429 BC) was the preeminent Athenian statesman, general, and orator of the 5th century BC. He led Athens during its Golden Age, transforming the Delian League into an Athenian empire, championing radical democracy, and overseeing a massive building program that defined classical Athens. His leadership during the first years of the Peloponnesian War and his death from plague marked a turning point for the city-state.

## Timeline
- **c. 495 BC** — Born in Athens to Xanthippus and Agariste of the Alcmaeonid family.
- **472 BC** — Presents Aeschylus's *The Persians*, signaling his entry into public life.
- **461 BC** — Ostracizes his political rival Cimon; consolidates democratic leadership.
- **454–453 BC** — Treasury of the Delian League transferred from Delos to Athens.
- **451 BC** — Proposes citizenship law restricting Athenian citizenship to those with two Athenian parents.
- **446–445 BC** — Negotiates the Thirty Years' Peace with Sparta.
- **444 BC** — Defeats political challenge from Thucydides (son of Melesias), who is ostracized.
- **440–439 BC** — Leads naval campaign to suppress the revolt of Samos.
- **433 BC** — Proposes the Megarian Decree, a trade embargo that strains peace with Sparta.
- **431 BC** — Peloponnesian War begins; implements defensive strategy, evacuating rural Attica.
- **430 BC** — Delivers Funeral Oration; Athens is devastated by plague.
- **429 BC** — Dies of the plague in Athens.

## Body

### Early Life and Political Rise
Pericles was born into Athenian aristocracy. His mother, Agariste, connected him to the powerful Alcmaeonid family, which included the reformer Cleisthenes. He was educated in music and philosophy, becoming a close associate of the philosopher Anaxagoras, whose skepticism and emphasis on emotional calm influenced Pericles's renowned self-control. He entered politics around 472 BC and rose to prominence by the early 460s BC. In 461 BC, he helped orchestrate the reduction of the aristocratic Areopagus council's power, a key step in Athens's shift to "radical democracy." He then used ostracism to remove his conservative rival, Cimon.

### Democratic Reforms and Populist Policies
As the leader of the democratic faction, Pericles enacted policies that expanded political access for poorer citizens. These included state-funded theater attendance, reduced property requirements for office, and pay for jury service. His most controversial reform was the citizenship law of 451 BC, which limited citizenship to those with two Athenian parents. Critics like the historian Paparrigopoulos later argued these populist measures led to demagoguery and Athens's decline, while supporters like Donald Kagan contend they created unassailable political strength by empowering the lower classes who manned the fleet.

### Imperial Leadership and the Acropolis
Pericles solidified Athenian dominance over the Delian League, effectively turning it into an empire. He transferred the league's treasury to Athens in 454–453 BC and used its funds for a monumental building program on the Acropolis, including the Parthenon. This project, financed by allied tribute, beautified Athens, provided employment, and showcased its glory. He also established Athenian colonies (cleruchies) and defended the empire through military campaigns, such as the suppression of the Samian revolt (440–439 BC).

### The Peloponnesian War and Death
Convinced war with Sparta was inevitable, Pericles refused to rescind the Megarian Decree. When war began in 431 BC, he implemented a defensive strategy: evacuating the rural population inside Athens's Long Walls and relying on naval power to harass the Peloponnese. This strategy avoided a decisive land battle but was deeply unpopular as it required Athenians to watch their farms be plundered. In 430 BC, a devastating plague struck the crowded city, killing a large portion of the population, including Pericles's sons. He was temporarily stripped of his generalship but was soon re-elected. He died of the plague in 429 BC, which Thucydides considered a disaster for Athens, as his successors lacked his skill and restraint.

## Terms
*   **Strategos:** The elected position of general in Athens, which Pericles held almost continuously from 461 to 429 BC.
*   **Delian League:** An alliance of Greek city-states led by Athens, originally formed to fight Persia, which Pericles transformed into an Athenian empire.
*   **Ecclesia:** The principal assembly of ancient Athens, where citizens voted on laws and policies.
*   **Areopagus:** A traditional council of former archons (magistrates) with aristocratic roots; its powers were reduced by Pericles's mentor Ephialtes.
*   **Ostracism:** A procedure in Athenian democracy where citizens could vote to exile a person for ten years without loss of property.
*   **Phoros:** The tribute or tax paid by members of the Delian League to Athens.
*   **Cleruchy:** An Athenian colony where settlers retained their Athenian citizenship, used to secure strategic locations.
*   **Long Walls:** Fortified walls connecting Athens to its port of Piraeus, central to Pericles's defensive strategy.
*   **Funeral Oration:** A speech Pericles delivered in 431 BC to honor Athenian war dead, as recorded by Thucydides, which became a classic defense of Athenian democracy.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **The Peace of Callias:** Historians debate whether a formal peace treaty between Athens and Persia existed, and if so, when it was signed (c. 463 BC or 450–449 BC).
*   **Pericles's Strategy:** Scholars disagree on whether his defensive strategy in the Peloponnesian War was brilliant or a failure. Critics like Donald Kagan argue it was wishful thinking that ignored Athens's financial limits, while defenders like Platias and Koliopoulos state Athens only lost when it abandoned this strategy.
*   **Thucydides's Speeches:** It is uncertain how accurately the historian Thucydides recorded Pericles's actual words, especially in the Funeral Oration, as he likely paraphrased or embellished them.
*   **Pericles's Role:** Some modern historians question whether Pericles was an all-powerful "first citizen" or a charismatic figurehead guided by advisors or the will of the people.

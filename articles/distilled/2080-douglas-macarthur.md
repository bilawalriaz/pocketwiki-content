# Douglas MacArthur

## Overview
Douglas MacArthur was one of the most prominent and controversial American military figures of the 20th century. He served with distinction in World War I, was the Army Chief of Staff during the interwar period, and commanded Allied forces in the Pacific during World War II, where he accepted Japan's surrender. He later oversaw the post-war occupation of Japan and commanded UN forces in the Korean War before his contentious removal by President Harry S. Truman. His career highlights the complex relationship between military leadership and civilian control.

## Body

### Early Life and World War I
MacArthur was a military brat, the son of a Medal of Honor recipient. He graduated first in his class from West Point in 1903. During World War I, he served as chief of staff of the 42nd ("Rainbow") Division and later commanded its 84th Infantry Brigade. He was wounded twice and decorated extensively, including two Distinguished Service Crosses and seven Silver Stars. He became the youngest general in the American Expeditionary Forces.

### Interwar Years and Rise to Prominence
From 1919 to 1922, MacArthur was Superintendent of West Point, where he modernized the curriculum and formalized the Cadet Honor Code. He became the Army's youngest major general in 1925. As Chief of Staff (1930-1935), he oversaw budget cuts during the Great Depression and was involved in the controversial expulsion of the "Bonus Army" of veterans from Washington, D.C. in 1932. He later served as Military Advisor to the Commonwealth of the Philippines, where he was given the rank of Field Marshal.

### World War II in the Pacific
Recalled to active duty in 1941, MacArthur commanded U.S. forces in the Philippines. After the Japanese invasion, his forces retreated to Bataan. He was ordered to evacuate to Australia in March 1942, famously vowing, "I shall return." As Supreme Commander, Southwest Pacific Area, he directed the New Guinea campaign, employing a strategy of bypassing Japanese strongholds. He fulfilled his promise by returning to lead the liberation of the Philippines in 1944-45. He accepted Japan's surrender in 1945.

### Occupation of Japan and the Korean War
As Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers (SCAP), MacArthur oversaw the occupation of Japan (1945-1951). He protected Emperor Hirohito from prosecution, oversaw war crimes tribunals, and implemented sweeping democratic and economic reforms, including a new pacifist constitution. During the Korean War, he commanded UN forces. His amphibious landing at Inchon was a major success, but after China entered the war, UN forces suffered heavy defeats. MacArthur's public criticism of President Truman's limited-war strategy led to his removal from command in April 1951.

### Later Life
After his removal, MacArthur delivered a famous farewell address to Congress. He later served as chairman of the board of Remington Rand and advised Presidents Kennedy and Johnson. He died in 1964.

## Terms
*   **General of the Army:** A five-star general officer rank, one of the highest possible in the U.S. Army, held by only five people in history.
*   **Supreme Commander, Southwest Pacific Area (SWPA):** MacArthur's title as the top Allied commander in the Pacific theater from 1942 to 1945.
*   **Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers (SCAP):** The title held by MacArthur as the effective ruler of occupied Japan from 1945 to 1951.
*   **United Nations Command (UNC):** The multinational military force that fought in the Korean War under U.S. leadership.
*   **"Island Hopping":** A military strategy of bypassing heavily fortified Japanese positions to capture strategically important but lightly defended islands. MacArthur preferred a strategy of maneuver and bypassing strongholds on larger landmasses.
*   **Zaibatsu:** The large, family-controlled industrial and financial business conglomerates in the Empire of Japan, whose power MacArthur's occupation government sought to break up.
*   **Article 9:** A clause in the post-war Japanese Constitution, drafted under MacArthur's authority, that renounces war as an instrument of state policy.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **The Medal of Honor (1942):** MacArthur's award for the defense of the Philippines is debated. He had not performed a specific act of "conspicuous gallantry" as required by law; the award was justified by General Marshall as a propaganda tool to counter Japanese claims he had fled.
*   **The Bonus Army (1932):** His use of troops to disperse protesting veterans is viewed by some as a necessary action to prevent a communist-inspired insurrection, and by others as a brutal overreaction that damaged civil-military relations.
*   **Removal from Command (1951):** The firing of MacArthur by President Truman is a seminal case in American civil-military relations. It raised questions about whether a popular military commander can publicly challenge the President's war policy, and whether the military or civilian leadership should control war strategy.
*   **Legacy in Japan:** His role as occupier is highly regarded in Japan for overseeing a peaceful transition to democracy and economic recovery, though his decision to shield Emperor Hirohito from war crimes prosecution remains controversial.

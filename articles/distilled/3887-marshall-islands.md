# Marshall Islands

## Overview
The Marshall Islands is an archipelagic nation in the central Pacific Ocean, consisting of 29 coral atolls and five islands. It is one of only four atoll-based sovereign states in the world. The nation's history is marked by Austronesian settlement, European exploration, colonial rule by Germany, Japan, and the United States, and significant U.S. nuclear testing during the Cold War. Today, it is a sovereign state in free association with the United States, facing existential threats from climate change and sea-level rise.

## Timeline
- **c. 2nd millennium BC** — Austronesian settlers arrive, introducing crops and chickens.
- **1526** — Spanish explorer Alonso de Salazar is the first European to sight the islands.
- **1788** — British captains John Marshall and Thomas Gilbert visit; islands later named for Marshall.
- **1885** — German Empire annexes the Marshall Islands as a protectorate.
- **1914** — Empire of Japan occupies the islands at the start of World War I.
- **1944** — United States invades and captures the Marshall Islands during World War II.
- **1946-1958** — U.S. conducts 67 nuclear tests at Bikini and Enewetak Atolls.
- **1979** — U.S. recognizes the constitution and independence of the Republic of the Marshall Islands.
- **1986** — Compact of Free Association with the United States enters into force.
- **1991** — Marshall Islands becomes a member of the United Nations.
- **2011** — Government declares the world's largest shark sanctuary.
- **2023** — U.S. financial aid under the Compact is extended for another 20 years.

## Body

### Prehistory and Early Society
Austronesian settlers reached the Marshall Islands as early as the 2nd millennium BC, introducing Southeast Asian crops like coconuts, giant swamp taro, and breadfruit, as well as domesticated chickens. This made the islands permanently habitable. The Marshallese developed sophisticated navigation techniques, using stars and interpreting ocean swell patterns to locate atolls. They used stick charts as teaching tools for navigation. Society was hierarchical, with paramount chiefs (*iroijlaplap*) holding authority over individual island chiefs (*iroij*).

### European Contact and Colonial Rule
The first European contact was by the Spanish in 1526. The islands were later named for British Captain John Marshall, who visited in 1788. American Protestant missionaries arrived in the 1850s, followed by German copra traders who dominated the economy. The German Empire formally annexed the islands as a protectorate in 1885. After World War I, Japan received the islands as part of the South Seas Mandate. Japan's administration was primarily strategic, building military airfields in the late 1930s.

### World War II and the Nuclear Age
The United States invaded the Marshall Islands in 1944 during World War II. After the war, the U.S. administered the islands as part of the Trust Territory of the Pacific Islands. From 1946 to 1958, the U.S. conducted 67 nuclear tests at Bikini and Enewetak Atolls, including the first hydrogen bomb test in 1952. The tests caused significant fallout, displaced populations, and left lasting contamination. The Runit Dome was built to store radioactive debris.

### Independence and Modern Politics
The U.S. recognized the constitution of the Republic of the Marshall Islands in 1979, granting independence. Full sovereignty was achieved through a Compact of Free Association with the U.S., which provides defense and financial aid. The government is a presidential parliamentary republic. The Marshall Islands has been a UN member since 1991. The nation's economy relies heavily on U.S. aid, fishing license fees, and its role as a major flag of convenience for international shipping.

### Geography and Climate Change
The country consists of 29 atolls and five islands with a total land area of only about 70 square miles, but an ocean territory of 750,000 square miles. The average elevation is just 7 feet above sea level. Climate change is an existential threat, with sea levels rising at more than twice the global average. Projections indicate that a 3.3-foot (1 m) rise would permanently flood 37% of buildings in Kwajalein and 50% in Ebeye.

## Terms
- **Atoll:** A ring-shaped coral reef, island, or series of islets, enclosing a lagoon.
- **Copra:** The dried meat of the coconut, from which coconut oil is extracted.
- **Flag of convenience:** A business practice whereby a ship's owner registers a ship in a foreign country to avoid regulations and taxes of the owner's country.
- **Iroij:** A traditional Marshallese chief.
- **Iroijlaplap:** A paramount chief in the traditional Marshallese social hierarchy.
- **Nitijela:** The unicameral legislature of the Marshall Islands.
- **Compact of Free Association:** An agreement between the U.S. and the Marshall Islands that grants the U.S. responsibility for defense while allowing Marshallese to live and work in the U.S.
- **Stick chart:** A traditional Marshallese navigational tool made of sticks and shells that maps ocean swell patterns.
- **South Seas Mandate:** A League of Nations mandate administered by Japan after World War I, comprising former German territories north of the equator.

## Debates and open questions
- The source notes uncertainty about the exact islands sighted by early Spanish explorers due to imprecise 16th-century logs.
- There is an ongoing territorial dispute over Wake Island, which the Marshall Islands claims based on oral legends but which has been administered by the U.S. since 1899.
- The ability of the Marshall Islands to enforce its large shark sanctuary has been questioned.
- The long-term health and environmental impacts of U.S. nuclear testing remain a significant issue, with compensation from the Nuclear Claims Tribunal unpaid.

# Mieszko I

## Overview
Mieszko I (c. 930–992) was the Duke of Poland and the founder of the first unified Polish state, the Civitas Schinesghe. A member of the Piast dynasty, his reign was pivotal in establishing Poland as a Christian realm within Western Europe through strategic alliances, military expansion, and his conversion to Roman Catholicism in 966.

## Timeline
- **c. 930** — Mieszko I is born.
- **c. 960** — Mieszko I becomes Duke of Poland, inheriting core territories of the Polans and Goplans.
- **963** — Margrave Gero of Meissen conquers Polabian tribes, coming into contact with Poland.
- **965** — Mieszko marries Dobrawa of Bohemia, forming a political alliance.
- **966** — Mieszko I is baptized, placing Poland in the sphere of Western Christianity.
- **967** — Polish-Bohemian forces defeat the Wolinians, giving Mieszko control of the mouth of the Oder River.
- **972** — Mieszko's forces defeat Margrave Odo I of Ostmark at the Battle of Cedynia.
- **977** — Mieszko's first wife, Dobrawa, dies.
- **978/979** — Mieszko marries Oda of Haldensleben, a Saxon noblewoman.
- **980s** — Mieszko allies with Sweden against Denmark.
- **983** — A major uprising of Polabian Slavs breaks out against German rule.
- **986** — Mieszko meets Emperor Otto III, cementing an alliance with a gift of a camel.
- **990** — Mieszko fights a war against Bohemia, annexing Silesia and likely Lesser Poland.
- **c. 991–992** — Mieszko issues the *Dagome iudex* document, placing his lands under papal protection.
- **25 May 992** — Mieszko I dies, leaving an expanded state to his son Bolesław I the Brave.

## Body

### Early Reign and Expansion
Mieszko I took power after his father's death (c. 950–960), inheriting a tribal federation centered on the Polans. He continued the process of unification, likely conquering Masovia and parts of Gdańsk Pomerania early in his reign. His primary military focus was on the Polabian Slavs to the west, leading to conflicts with the Veleti tribe and their Saxon ally, Wichmann the Younger, who defeated Mieszko twice around 963.

### Conversion to Christianity and Foreign Policy
Mieszko's marriage to the Bohemian princess Dobrawa in 965 was a key political alliance. In 966, he accepted baptism, a decision most historians view as a strategic move to align with Bohemia, reduce the threat of German holy wars, and centralize power by diminishing the pagan priest class. His conversion integrated Poland into Latin Christendom. He skillfully navigated foreign relations, paying tribute to the Holy Roman Emperor to avoid invasion while later allying with Sweden against Denmark.

### Military Conflicts and Territorial Growth
Mieszko's victory at the Battle of Cedynia (972) against Margrave Odo I secured his control over Western Pomerania. In the 970s, he likely incorporated the Sandomierz region. After the death of Emperor Otto I, Mieszko joined a German opposition faction but later realigned with Emperor Otto II, possibly to counter the Polabian Slav uprising of 983. In his final years, he fought Bohemia, annexing Silesia and, according to most historians, Lesser Poland (including Kraków), significantly expanding the state he left to his son.

### Legacy and Succession
Mieszko I is credited as the founder of the Polish state. His acceptance of Christianity was foundational for Poland's future in European civilization. He sponsored the construction of major churches, including at Gniezno and Poznań. Before his death, he issued the *Dagome iudex* document, which placed his realm under papal protection. He died in 992, leaving a territory at least twice as large as what he inherited to his eldest son, Bolesław I the Brave.

## Terms
*   **Piast dynasty:** The ruling dynasty of Poland, founded by Mieszko I.
*   **Civitas Schinesghe:** The name for the first unified Polish state established by Mieszko I.
*   **Polans:** The West Slavic tribe whose territory formed the core of Mieszko's state.
*   **Polabian Slavs:** A collective term for Slavic tribes living along the Elbe and Oder rivers, often in conflict with Mieszko and the German Empire.
*   **Dagome iudex:** A late 10th-century papal document summarizing a dedication of Mieszko I's lands to the Pope.
*   **Peter's Pence:** An annual tax paid by England and other areas to the Papacy, possibly instituted in Poland after the *Dagome iudex*.
*   **Denarius:** A silver coin; Poland may have begun minting its own during Mieszko's reign.
*   **Margrave:** A title for a military commander responsible for defending a border territory (march) of the Holy Roman Empire.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Motives for Baptism:** Scholars debate whether Mieszko's conversion was primarily a political decision to ally with Bohemia and the Empire, a forced submission after a German military defeat (Gero's war), or influenced by his wife Dobrawa.
*   **Extent of Conquests:** The exact timing and nature of the incorporation of Lesser Poland and parts of Pomerania are uncertain due to limited sources. Some historians argue Bohemian rule in Lesser Poland was only nominal.
*   **The *Dagome iudex*:** Its purpose is debated: was it to establish a regular church organization and pay Peter's Pence, or to protect the inheritance rights of Mieszko's second wife, Oda, and their sons?
*   **Mieszko's Daughter:** The identity and marriages of Mieszko's daughter (possibly named Gunhild) are confused in sources. She was long identified as Sigrid the Haughty, but modern research suggests she married only Sweyn Forkbeard of Denmark.

# Stephen I of Hungary

## Overview
Stephen I (c. 975–1038) was the first King of Hungary, whose reign established the Christian Kingdom of Hungary in the Carpathian Basin. His rule unified semi-independent tribes, created a state administrative system, and secured international recognition, ensuring the survival of the Hungarian nation in a region previously controlled by transient powers like the Huns and Avars. He is venerated as a saint and is a central figure in Hungarian national identity.

## Timeline
- **c. 975** — Born as Vajk in Esztergom, son of Grand Prince Géza.
- **997** — Succeeds his father as Grand Prince; defeats rival Koppány.
- **1000/1001** — Crowned King with a crown from Pope Sylvester II.
- **c. 1002–1003** — Conquers the realm of his uncle, Gyula the Younger, in Transylvania.
- **1009** — Establishes the Bishopric of Pécs; papal legate Cardinal Azo arrives.
- **c. 1014–1018** — Wars with Poland over the Morava valley.
- **1018** — Allied military campaign with Byzantium against Bulgaria.
- **1020s** — Begins regular coinage; establishes new capital at Székesfehérvár.
- **1030** — Defends Hungary against invasion by Emperor Conrad II.
- **1031** — His son and heir, Emeric, dies in a hunting accident.
- **15 August 1038** — Dies; buried in the basilica at Székesfehérvár.
- **1083** — Canonized by Pope Gregory VII along with his son Emeric and Bishop Gerard.

## Body

### Early Years and Accession
Stephen was born c. 975 to Grand Prince Géza and Sarolt. Though his parents were baptized, he was the first devout Christian in his family. In 997, he succeeded his father, but his claim was challenged by his pagan relative Koppány, who invoked the traditional principle of seniority. Stephen, supported by foreign knights (including Vecelin, Hont, and Pázmány) and native lords, defeated and killed Koppány, securing his rule over the northwestern Carpathian Basin.

### Coronation and Consolidation
To legitimize his rule internationally, Stephen received a crown from Pope Sylvester II and was crowned on 25 December 1000 or 1 January 1001. This act established Hungary as a Christian kingdom independent of the Holy Roman Empire. He immediately began consolidating power by establishing an archbishopric at Esztergom and conquering the realms of semi-independent chieftains, such as his uncle Gyula in Transylvania and the "Black Hungarians." He implemented a county-based administrative system organized around royal fortresses.

### Wars and Foreign Policy
Stephen's reign involved conflicts to secure his borders. He fought wars with Poland (c. 1014–1018) over the Morava valley and allied with the Byzantine Empire in its conquest of Bulgaria in 1018. His most significant conflict was with the Holy Roman Empire. After Emperor Conrad II invaded in 1030, Stephen's scorched-earth tactics forced a withdrawal, and Conrad ceded lands between the Lajta and Fischa rivers to Hungary in 1031.

### Domestic Policy and Legacy
Stephen focused on Christianizing his realm, issuing laws that enforced Christian customs and protected the Church. He established at least one archbishopric, six bishoprics, and three Benedictine monasteries. His reign brought a period of peace, making Hungary a safe route for pilgrims and merchants between Western Europe and the Holy Land. He died in 1038, having outlived his children. His death triggered decades of civil wars until stability was restored under his grandson, Ladislaus I. He was canonized in 1083.

## Terms
- **Primogeniture**: The principle where a father is succeeded by his son, which Stephen followed.
- **Seniority**: The traditional Hungarian custom where the throne passed to the most senior male member of the ruling dynasty, which Stephen's rival Koppány claimed.
- **Ispán**: The royal official who administered a county, the basic unit of Stephen's administrative system.
- **Levirate marriage**: A pagan custom where a man marries his deceased brother's widow, which Koppány proposed to Stephen's mother.
- **Admonitions (De institutione morum)**: A work attributed to Stephen, advising his son on moral and kingly duties, emphasizing cooperation with the Church and nobility.
- **Holy Dexter (Szent Jobb)**: Stephen's intact right hand, a major relic and object of veneration in Hungary.
- **Gyula**: A high-ranking chieftain title; Stephen's mother was from a family of gyulas.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Coronation Date and Sovereignty**: The exact date (25 Dec 1000 or 1 Jan 1001) is uncertain. Scholars debate whether Stephen accepted suzerainty from the Pope or Emperor; evidence suggests he always asserted his sovereignty.
- **Identity of Gyula**: Historians disagree on whether Stephen's uncle Gyula was identical to "Gylas," a chieftain baptized in Constantinople.
- **Location of the Black Hungarians**: Their territory is debated, with proposals placing them east of the Tisza or in southern Transdanubia.
- **Date of Conflict with Ajtony**: The clash with the chieftain Ajtony is dated by some to the 1020s, while others place it a decade earlier.
- **Authenticity of Laws**: The authenticity and dating of some of Stephen's law books, like the Second Book of Laws and the decree on tithes, are debated among scholars.

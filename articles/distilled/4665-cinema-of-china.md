# Cinema of China

## Overview
The cinema of mainland China is one of three distinct threads of Chinese-language film, alongside Hong Kong and Taiwan. It has evolved from early foreign introductions to becoming the world's largest film market by box office in 2020, reflecting the nation's social, political, and economic transformations. Its history is marked by state involvement, generational artistic movements, and a growing global commercial presence.

## Timeline
- **1896** — First recorded motion picture screening in Shanghai.
- **1905** — First Chinese film, a recording of the Peking opera *Dingjun Mountain*, is made.
- **1921** — First financially successful domestic films, like *Yan Ruisheng*, spur industry investment.
- **1930s** — The Left-Wing Film Movement emerges, emphasizing realism and class struggle.
- **1937** — Wan brothers release *Princess Iron Fan*, the first Chinese animated feature.
- **1987** — Ministry of Radio, Film and Television promotes the "main melody" genre to invigorate national spirit.
- **1990s** — The "Sixth Generation" underground film movement begins, focusing on contemporary urban life.
- **2000** — *Crouching Tiger, Hidden Dragon* wins the Academy Award for Best Foreign Language Film.
- **2002** — Zhang Yimou's *Hero* becomes a major international box office success.
- **2012** — China becomes the world's second-largest film market by box office receipts.
- **2015** — *Monkey King: Hero Is Back* breaks records for Chinese domestic animation.
- **2019** — *Ne Zha* becomes the highest-grossing animated non-English film ever.
- **2020** — China's film market surpasses the U.S. to become the world's largest.

## Body

### Early History and Pre-Revolutionary Cinema
Motion pictures arrived in China in 1896 via foreign exhibitors. The first Chinese film was made in 1905. The early industry was centered in Shanghai and dominated by foreign companies. The first successful domestic films in 1921 led to the establishment of Chinese studios. Following the May Thirtieth Movement, a "revive national cinema" campaign promoted films based on Chinese culture and genres like *wuxia*. In the 1930s, the industry split politically. The Left-Wing Film Movement, influenced by Communists, promoted realistic, narrative-driven films about class struggle. In opposition, the "soft film" movement emphasized aesthetics and modernist techniques.

### State-Endorsed and Independent Generations
In the late 20th century, the government promoted the "main melody" genre to foster national pride. These state-subsidized films often depict military history or CCP leaders and use Hollywood-style techniques and "red songs" for propaganda. In contrast, the Fifth Generation directors (e.g., Chen Kaige, Zhang Yimou) created critically acclaimed works exploring personal identity and history, such as *Farewell My Concubine* (1993). The post-1990 "Sixth Generation" emerged as an underground movement with low-budget, documentary-style films focusing on contemporary urban life and social marginalization, often facing state censorship. A subsequent "dGeneration" of independent filmmakers uses digital equipment for extremely low-budget productions.

### Animation
Chinese animation began in the 1920s, inspired by Disney. The Wan brothers' *Princess Iron Fan* (1941) was the first Chinese animated feature. A "golden era" from the 1950s-1980s produced distinctive styles like ink and puppet animation. After a period of decline relative to foreign imports, the 2015 hit *Monkey King: Hero Is Back* revitalized the industry. The 2019 film *Ne Zha* became a massive commercial and cultural breakthrough, establishing animation as entertainment for all ages.

### Commercial Success and International Co-productions
Post-1980s liberalization led to commercial successes like *Romance on the Lu Mountain* (1980) and *Shaolin Temple* (1982). Director Feng Xiaogang pioneered the commercially viable "hesui pian" (New Year film) with *The Dream Factory* (1997). The 2000s saw Chinese films achieve major international success, notably *Crouching Tiger, Hidden Dragon* and *Hero*. This era blurred lines between mainland and international Chinese-language cinema through co-productions featuring pan-Chinese casts and crews. Domestically, films like *Lost in Thailand* (2012) and *Monster Hunt* (2015) broke box office records. By 2021, 9 of the top 10 highest-grossing films in China were domestic productions.

### Industry and Regulation
China's film infrastructure has expanded dramatically, from 600 projection units in 1949 to over 31,000 screens by 2015. The market became the world's largest in 2020. Major companies include state-owned China Film Group and private firms like Huayi Brothers and Alibaba Pictures. The 2016 Film Industry Promotion Act regulates content, banning material harmful to the "dignity, honor and interests" of the PRC. Since the early 2020s, the industry faces competition from *duanju* (short vertical dramas), whose market surpassed the film box office in 2024.

## Terms
- **Main melody (主旋律):** An official genre of state-endorsed films promoting national spirit and socialist values, often using Hollywood-style production and "red songs."
- **Left-Wing Film Movement:** A 1930s movement emphasizing realistic, narrative films about class struggle, opposed to the aesthetic-focused "soft film" movement.
- **Fifth Generation:** Filmmakers who graduated from the Beijing Film Academy in 1982, known for visually striking films exploring history and identity (e.g., Chen Kaige, Zhang Yimou).
- **Sixth Generation:** An underground film movement from the 1990s characterized by low-budget, documentary-style films focusing on contemporary urban life and social issues.
- **dGeneration:** Independent filmmakers using digital equipment for extremely low-budget productions, often outside the state film system.
- **Hesui pian (贺岁片):** A commercially successful "New Year film" genre, pioneered by Feng Xiaogang.
- **Wuxia:** A genre of Chinese fiction concerning the adventures of martial artists.
- **Duanju (短剧):** Short vertical dramas, a digital format that has become a major competitor to traditional cinema since the early 2020s.
- **New Documentary Movement (NDM):** A movement since the 1990s producing independent, often digital, documentaries reflecting social change.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The boundary between "mainland Chinese" cinema and international "Chinese-language" cinema is blurred by co-productions with pan-Chinese casts and crews.
- Sixth Generation filmmakers have often faced harsh treatment from state censorship despite international acclaim.
- The rise of *duanju* (short vertical dramas) presents a significant challenge to the traditional film industry's business model and viewing habits.

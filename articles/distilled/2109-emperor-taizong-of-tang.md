# Emperor Taizong of Tang

## Overview
Emperor Taizong of Tang (r. 626–649) was the second emperor of the Tang dynasty and is traditionally regarded as a co-founder for his role in the dynasty's establishment. His reign, known as the "Reign of Zhenguan," is considered a golden age in Chinese history, marked by political stability, economic prosperity, and military expansion that laid the foundation for the Tang dynasty's peak.

## Timeline
- **598** — Li Shimin (future Taizong) is born.
- **617** — Li Shimin plays a key role in encouraging his father Li Yuan to rebel against the Sui dynasty at Jinyang.
- **618** — Li Yuan establishes the Tang dynasty as Emperor Gaozu; Li Shimin becomes Prince of Qin.
- **626** — Li Shimin kills his brothers Li Jiancheng and Li Yuanji in the Xuanwu Gate Incident; he becomes emperor.
- **630** — Tang general Li Jing defeats the Eastern Turkic Khaganate; Taizong takes the title "Khan of Heaven."
- **635** — Tang forces defeat Tuyuhun.
- **640** — Tang annexes the kingdom of Gaochang (Karakhoja).
- **641** — Princess Wencheng marries Tibetan king Songtsän Gampo.
- **644** — Tang general Guo Xiaoke captures the king of Karasahr (Yanqi).
- **645** — Taizong's campaign against Goguryeo ends in withdrawal at the siege of Ansi.
- **648** — Tang general Ashina She'er conquers Kucha (Qiuzi).
- **649** — Emperor Taizong dies; he is succeeded by his son Li Zhi (Emperor Gaozong).

## Body

### Rise to Power and Unification
Li Shimin was born in 598 to Li Yuan, a Sui general. In 617, he secretly plotted with his father's associates to rebel against the Sui, forcing Li Yuan's hand. After the Tang was established in 618, Li Shimin served as a principal general, defeating key rivals like Xue Ju, Liu Wuzhou, Wang Shichong, and Dou Jiande, thereby reunifying China under Tang rule by 623. His military success created a power struggle with his older brother, Crown Prince Li Jiancheng. In 626, fearing for his life, Li Shimin ambushed and killed Li Jiancheng and their brother Li Yuanji at the Xuanwu Gate. He then forced his father, Emperor Gaozu, to abdicate, becoming Emperor Taizong.

### Governance and the Zhenguan Era
Taizong's reign is famed for its effective governance. He actively solicited criticism from his officials, most notably the former rival advisor Wei Zheng, to avoid the mistakes of past rulers. He promoted officials based on merit through the imperial examination system and emphasized loyalty to policy over personal allegiance. His court, including chancellors like Fang Xuanling and Du Ruhui, is celebrated for its competence. This period of wise rule, known as the "Reign of Zhenguan," saw economic recovery, population growth, and the consolidation of a vast empire.

### Military Campaigns and Foreign Policy
Taizong pursued an aggressive foreign policy to secure Tang's borders. In 630, he destroyed the power of the Eastern Turkic Khaganate, resettling some Turks within the empire. He launched a series of campaigns against the oasis states of the Tarim Basin and their Western Turkic allies, annexing Gaochang (640), Karasahr (644), and Kucha (648). He also defeated Tuyuhun (635) and managed relations with the rising Tibetan Empire, eventually agreeing to the marriage of Princess Wencheng in 641. His later campaign against Goguryeo (645) was unsuccessful, ending in a strategic withdrawal.

### Succession and Death
Taizong's later years were marred by a bitter succession dispute. His eldest son and crown prince, Li Chengqian, was deposed in 643 after plotting a rebellion, partly due to Taizong's favoritism toward another son, Li Tai. Taizong ultimately chose his younger, gentler son Li Zhi as heir to avoid further conflict. He died in 649, having secured the succession and prepared the empire for continued stability under his successor, Emperor Gaozong.

## Terms
- **Reign of Zhenguan**: The era name (627–649) of Emperor Taizong, synonymous with a golden age of wise and effective governance in Chinese history.
- **Xuanwu Gate Incident**: The 626 coup in which Li Shimin killed his brothers Li Jiancheng and Li Yuanji, leading to his succession as emperor.
- **Khan of Heaven**: A title taken by Emperor Taizong after his victory over the Eastern Turks, asserting his authority over Turkic peoples.
- **Imperial Examination System**: A civil service recruitment system based on meritocratic exams, which Taizong continued to develop.
- **Heqin**: A Chinese diplomatic practice of marrying a princess to a foreign leader to secure peace, which Taizong considered but ultimately avoided with the Xueyantuo.
- **Circuit (Dao)**: A high-level administrative division created by Taizong to improve governance over the empire's many prefectures.
- **Records of Clans (Shizu Zhi)**: A work commissioned by Taizong to rank noble families based on merit and recent service, challenging traditional aristocratic prestige.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Motives for the Xuanwu Gate Incident**: Traditional accounts portray Li Shimin as acting in self-defense against plots by Li Jiancheng and Li Yuanji. Modern historians debate the extent to which these accounts were shaped by Taizong's later control of historical records to legitimize his usurpation.
- **Succession and Character of Li Zhi**: Taizong's choice of the "gentler" Li Zhi as heir, despite doubts about his strength, is seen as a pivotal decision. Scholars debate whether this choice, aimed at avoiding fratricide, inadvertently paved the way for the later dominance of Empress Wu Zetian.
- **Legacy of the Zhenguan Era**: While celebrated as a model reign, some historians note that Taizong's later years saw increased autocracy, costly campaigns, and the succession crisis, complicating the image of a flawless golden age.

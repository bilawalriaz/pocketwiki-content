# Leon Trotsky

## Overview
Leon Trotsky (1879–1940) was a Marxist theorist, revolutionary, and key leader of the Russian Revolution and Civil War. He was a central figure in the early Soviet state, second only to Lenin, before being outmaneuvered by Joseph Stalin and exiled. His ideas, particularly the theory of permanent revolution, formed the basis of Trotskyism, a major school of Marxist thought that opposed Stalinism.

## Timeline
- **1898** — Joins the Russian Social Democratic Labour Party (RSDLP); arrested and exiled to Siberia.
- **1903** — Sides with Mensheviks in the RSDLP split, later declares himself non-factional.
- **1905** — Elected chairman of the Saint Petersburg Soviet during the 1905 Revolution; exiled again.
- **1917** — Joins the Bolsheviks; elected chairman of the Petrograd Soviet; helps lead the October Revolution.
- **1918** — Negotiates the Treaty of Brest-Litovsk; becomes People's Commissar for Military Affairs.
- **1918–1920** — Builds and leads the Red Army to victory in the Russian Civil War.
- **1923** — Leads the Left Opposition against growing party bureaucracy.
- **1927** — Expelled from the Communist Party.
- **1929** — Deported from the Soviet Union.
- **1936** — Sentenced to death in absentia at the Moscow show trials.
- **1938** — Founds the Fourth International.
- **1940** — Assassinated in Mexico City by NKVD agent Ramón Mercader.

## Body

### Early Life and Revolutionary Activity
Born Lev Bronstein into a Jewish farming family in Ukraine, Trotsky became involved in revolutionary activities in 1898. He was arrested, exiled to Siberia, and escaped in 1902, adopting the pseudonym "Trotsky." In London, he worked with Lenin on the newspaper *Iskra*. During the 1905 Revolution, he became chairman of the Saint Petersburg Soviet, developing his theory of permanent revolution. After further exile, he lived abroad, working as a journalist and developing his Marxist ideas.

### Role in the Revolution and Civil War
Returning to Russia after the February Revolution of 1917, Trotsky joined the Bolsheviks. He was elected chairman of the Petrograd Soviet and was instrumental in planning the October Revolution. As People's Commissar for Foreign Affairs, he negotiated Russia's exit from World War I with the Treaty of Brest-Litovsk. From 1918 to 1925, as Commissar for Military Affairs, he built the Red Army from scratch and led it to victory in the Civil War, employing strict discipline, conscription, and the use of former Tsarist military specialists.

### Power Struggle with Stalin and Exile
After Lenin's death in 1924, Trotsky emerged as a leading critic of Joseph Stalin. He led the Left Opposition, advocating for rapid industrialisation and greater party democracy. Stalin, controlling the party apparatus, politically outmaneuvered him. Trotsky was expelled from the Politburo in 1926, from the party in 1927, and deported in 1929. In exile, he wrote extensively, critiquing Stalinism as a "degenerated workers' state" and advocating for proletarian internationalism. He founded the Fourth International in 1938 as an alternative to Stalin's Comintern.

### Assassination and Legacy
Sentenced to death in absentia during the Moscow show trials, Trotsky lived in Turkey, France, Norway, and finally Mexico. He was assassinated in 1940 by Ramón Mercader, a Stalinist agent. Trotsky was never rehabilitated by the Soviet Union. Historically, he is credited with the Red Army's victory and major contributions to Soviet development, but also criticized for his role in the Red Terror and the suppression of the Kronstadt rebellion. His ideas inspired the international Trotskyist movement.

## Terms
*   **Permanent Revolution:** Trotsky's theory that in "backward" countries, the bourgeoisie cannot complete democratic tasks, so the proletariat must lead a revolution that transitions directly into socialist tasks, requiring international spread to survive.
*   **Trotskyism:** A Marxist school of thought based on Trotsky's ideas, emphasizing permanent revolution, proletarian internationalism, and opposition to Stalinism.
*   **Left Opposition:** A faction within the Soviet Communist Party (1923–1927) led by Trotsky, advocating for rapid industrialisation, voluntary collectivisation, and party democratisation.
*   **Red Army:** The military force of Soviet Russia, built and commanded by Trotsky during the Civil War.
*   **Brest-Litovsk:** The 1918 treaty that ended Russia's participation in World War I, ceding significant territory to the Central Powers.
*   **Degenerated Workers' State:** Trotsky's term for the Soviet Union under Stalin, where the working class had lost political power to a bureaucratic caste, but state ownership of the means of production remained.
*   **Fourth International:** An international communist organization founded by Trotsky in 1938 to oppose the Stalin-dominated Third International (Comintern).
*   **United Front:** A tactic advocated by Trotsky for revolutionaries to form alliances with reformist parties (like Social Democrats) in common struggle against fascism, while maintaining political independence.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Lenin's Successor:** Historians debate whether Lenin intended Trotsky as his successor. Evidence includes Lenin's proposal for Trotsky to become his deputy and their 1923 alliance against Stalin, but Trotsky declined the position.
*   **Role in Revolutionary Violence:** Trotsky's defense of the Red Terror and the violent suppression of the Kronstadt rebellion remain controversial, with critics arguing these actions laid groundwork for Stalinist totalitarianism.
*   **Economic Proposals:** Scholars debate the extent to which Stalin's later industrialisation and collectivisation policies appropriated the Left Opposition's earlier proposals, and how Trotsky's planned economy would have differed in practice.
*   **Political Acumen:** A central debate concerns why Trotsky, despite his prominence, lost the power struggle to Stalin. Explanations range from his alleged lack of political skill and failure to build a power base to objective factors like the party bureaucracy's desire for stability.

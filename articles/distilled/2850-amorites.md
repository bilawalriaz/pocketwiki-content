# Amorites

## Overview
The Amorites were a Northwest Semitic-speaking people who emerged from western Mesopotamia around 2500 BC. They expanded to rule much of the Levant and Mesopotamia from the 21st to the 16th centuries BC, establishing major states like Babylon and influencing regions as far as Egypt. Their history illustrates the transition from the Bronze Age urban civilizations to the political landscape of the early second millennium BC.

## Timeline
*   c. 2500 BC | First sparse mentions in records from the kingdom of Ebla.
*   c. 2240 BC | Akkadian emperor Naram-Sin records defeating a coalition including Amorites near Jebel Bishri.
*   c. 2100 BC | The Third Dynasty of Ur constructs a wall to hold off Amorite migrations.
*   21st century BC | Amorite rulers gain power in Mesopotamian city-states, beginning the Isin-Larsa period.
*   19th century BC | An Amorite presence is attested in Egypt; rulers of the Fourteenth Dynasty bear Amorite names.
*   18th century BC | Shamshi-Adad I forms the Kingdom of Upper Mesopotamia; Hammurabi rules Babylon.
*   1650 BC | The Hyksos, likely including Amorites, establish the Fifteenth Dynasty in Egypt.
*   16th century BC | The Amorite era in Mesopotamia ends with the fall of Babylon to the Hittites and Kassites.
*   c. 1200 BC | Amorites disappear from historical records, displaced or absorbed by other groups like the Arameans.

## Body

### Early History and Expansion
The Amorites, referred to as *mar.tu* in early texts, were initially a rural, nomadic group in the middle and upper Euphrates region. Sumerian and Akkadian sources from the third millennium BC often depicted them with contempt, describing them as primitive, tent-dwelling herders. As the Third Dynasty of Ur weakened, Amorite migrations intensified, forcing Ur to build a defensive wall. Their infiltration contributed to the collapse of Ur's centralized control, leading to the independent Isin-Larsa period.

### Political Dominance in the Second Millennium BC
Amorite leaders founded or took control of key city-states. In the north, Shamshi-Adad I created a short-lived empire. In the south, the Amorite dynasty of Babylon, culminating under Hammurabi, became the dominant power. Other significant Amorite states included Mari and Yamhad (centered at Aleppo). In Egypt, Amorite names appear among rulers of the Fourteenth Dynasty, and the later Hyksos rulers are considered to have included Amorite populations, linking their cultural practices to northern Syria.

### Language, Religion, and Decline
The Amorite language is a Northwest Semitic language, known primarily from personal names and loanwords in Akkadian texts. Their chief god was Dagan, identified with the Mesopotamian Enlil, and also known as Amurru. A bilingual list from the Old Babylonian period names ten Amorite deities. The Amorite political era ended in the 16th century BC with the Kassite takeover of Babylon and the rise of the Mitanni in the north. They were later absorbed by groups like the Arameans.

### Biblical and Scholarly Perspectives
The Hebrew Bible refers to the Amorites as powerful, tall inhabitants of Canaan, sometimes described as giants. They are portrayed as a pre-Israelite population defeated by Joshua. Modern scholarship debates their exact homeland, with Jebel Bishri in Syria being a common proposal. Genetic analysis of remains from the Amorite city of Alalakh shows the inhabitants were a mixture of Levantine and Mesopotamian ancestry.

## Terms
*   **Amorites (Amurru):** An ancient Northwest Semitic-speaking people and the name for their kingdom and chief deity.
*   **Northwest Semitic:** A branch of the Semitic language family that includes Amorite, Canaanite, and Aramaic.
*   **Third Dynasty of Ur (Ur III):** A Sumerian empire that collapsed c. 2004 BC, partly due to Amorite pressure.
*   **Isin-Larsa period:** A period in southern Mesopotamia (c. 2004–1763 BC) characterized by competing city-states, many ruled by Amorite dynasties.
*   **Old Babylonian period:** The era of Babylonian dominance under Amorite rulers, most famously Hammurabi (c. 1894–1595 BC).
*   **Hyksos:** A people of mixed origin, including Amorites, who ruled Lower Egypt during the Second Intermediate Period (c. 1650–1550 BC).
*   **Dagan:** The supreme Amorite deity, often identified with the Mesopotamian god Enlil.
*   **Jebel Bishri:** A mountain range in Syria, often identified as the Amorite homeland.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Homeland:** The precise geographic origin of the Amorites is debated, with theories ranging from a broad area between the Euphrates and the Mediterranean to the specific region of Jebel Bishri.
*   **Biblical Use:** Scholars debate why biblical writers used the "Amorite" ethnonym, with suggestions including a polemical use of stereotypes or a historical interest in the pre-Israelite past.
*   **Relationship to Hyksos:** The exact nature and proportion of Amorite involvement in the Hyksos confederation that ruled Egypt is still being clarified by evidence.
*   **Genetic Identity:** While ancient DNA shows a mixed Levantine-Mesopotamian profile for Amorite-era populations, the genetic distinctiveness of the Amorites as a specific group remains an area of study.

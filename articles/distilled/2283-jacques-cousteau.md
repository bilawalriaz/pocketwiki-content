# Jacques Cousteau

## Overview
Jacques-Yves Cousteau (1910–1997) was a French naval officer, oceanographer, filmmaker, and author who co-invented the Aqua-Lung, the first successful open-circuit self-contained underwater breathing apparatus (SCUBA). His work, through books, documentaries, and expeditions, pioneered underwater exploration and cinematography, significantly advancing public awareness and scientific understanding of the ocean.

- **28 June 1979** — His son Philippe dies in a plane crash.
- **1985** — Receives the U.S. Presidential Medal of Freedom.
- **25 June 1997** — Dies in Paris, France.

## Body

### Early Life and Innovation
Born in 1910, Cousteau graduated from the École navale as a gunnery officer. A car accident that broke both his arms ended his naval aviation career, redirecting his focus to the ocean. In the 1930s, he began underwater experiments using early goggles. During World War II, he produced the first French underwater films. The war years were decisive for diving innovation; in 1943, Cousteau and engineer Émile Gagnan created the first functional Aqua-Lung prototype, combining a demand regulator with a compressed-air tank. This invention made extended, independent underwater exploration possible.

### Exploration and Media Career
After leaving the Navy in 1949, Cousteau founded the French Oceanographic Campaigns and acquired the *Calypso*, refitting it as a mobile sea laboratory. He conducted underwater archaeology, such as at the Roman wreck of Mahdia (1948), and led the rescue of a bathyscaphe in 1949. His 1953 book *The Silent World* correctly predicted porpoise echolocation. He won the Palme d'Or for the film adaptation in 1956. From 1957, he directed the Oceanographic Museum of Monaco and developed the "diving saucer" SP-350 for deep-sea exploration. His television series, *The Undersea World of Jacques Cousteau* (1966–1976) and *The Cousteau Odyssey* (1977–1982), brought oceanography to a mass audience.

### Environmental Advocacy and Later Life
In the 1960s, Cousteau led the Conshelf projects, creating experimental underwater habitats. He became a prominent environmental advocate, notably campaigning successfully in 1960 against the dumping of radioactive waste in the Mediterranean. He co-founded The Cousteau Society in 1973. His later work included locating the wreck of the *Britannic* (1976) and producing numerous documentaries. He received major honors, including the Presidential Medal of Freedom (1985) and election to the Académie française (1988). He died in 1997, leaving a legacy of over 120 documentaries and 50 books.

## Terms
*   **Aqua-Lung**: The first successful open-circuit SCUBA system, co-invented by Cousteau and Émile Gagnan in 1943, allowing divers to breathe compressed air from a tank.
*   **SCUBA**: Self-Contained Underwater Breathing Apparatus; a portable unit enabling independent underwater breathing.
*   **Open-circuit**: A breathing system where exhaled air is released into the water, not recycled.
*   **Demand regulator**: A device (invented by Gagnan) that supplies air from a tank only when the diver inhales, conserving air.
*   **Calypso**: Cousteau's iconic research vessel, converted from a minesweeper, used for expeditions and filming from 1950.
*   **Conshelf (Precontinent)**: A series of experimental underwater habitats (1962-1965) designed for humans to live and work on the seafloor.
*   **Diving saucer (SP-350)**: A small, maneuverable submersible vehicle developed by Cousteau for deep-sea exploration.
*   **Oceanographic Museum of Monaco**: An institution Cousteau directed from 1957, dedicated to marine science.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Priority for SCUBA**: In 1994, Hans Hass publicly claimed priority for the first use of a mobile, self-contained diving apparatus, a claim directed at Cousteau.
*   **Ecological Impact**: Cousteau and his Oceanographic Museum in Monaco have been identified as possibly introducing the invasive "Killer Algae" (*Caulerpa taxifolia*) into the Mediterranean ecosystem.
*   **Population Control Views**: A 1991 interview quoted Cousteau advocating for drastic human population control measures, a stance that remains controversial.

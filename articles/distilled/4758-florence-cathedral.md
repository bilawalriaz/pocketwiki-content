# Florence Cathedral

## Overview
The Florence Cathedral, formally the Cathedral of Saint Mary of the Flower, is the mother church of the Catholic Archdiocese of Florence and a masterpiece of Italian Gothic architecture. Its construction spanned over 140 years, but it is most famous for its revolutionary dome, engineered by Filippo Brunelleschi, which remains the largest masonry dome ever built. The cathedral complex is a UNESCO World Heritage Site and a defining symbol of the Renaissance.

## Timeline
- **1296** — Construction begins to a design by Arnolfo di Cambio.
- **1334** — Giotto appointed to oversee work; designs the campanile.
- **1348** — Work halts due to the Black Death.
- **1367** — Neri di Fioravanti's model for an unbuttressed dome is chosen.
- **1418** — Competition held for the dome's design; Brunelleschi wins.
- **1420** — Construction of the dome begins.
- **1436** — Dome completed; cathedral consecrated by Pope Eugene IV.
- **1587-88** — Original medieval façade dismantled.
- **1887** — Current neo-Gothic façade by Emilio De Fabris completed.

## Body

### Construction and Design
The cathedral was built on the site of the earlier, crumbling church of Santa Reparata. Arnolfo di Cambio's original 1296 design called for a vast basilica with an octagonal dome. After his death, work slowed until the 1330s, when the Arte della Lana (wool merchants' guild) took patronage. Architects like Giotto and Francesco Talenti expanded the project, but by the early 15th century, the main challenge remained: how to build the enormous, unbuttressed dome specified in Neri di Fioravanti's 1367 model.

### Brunelleschi's Dome
Filippo Brunelleschi won the 1418 competition to build the dome, defeating his rival Lorenzo Ghiberti. His ingenious solution was a double-shell dome made of brick, constructed without temporary wooden centering (scaffolding from the ground). To prevent the dome from spreading outward under its own weight, he embedded four internal horizontal stone chains, like barrel hoops, within the inner shell. He also used a herringbone brick pattern to transfer weight to vertical ribs during construction. The dome was crowned by a lantern, also designed by Brunelleschi, which was completed after his death.

### Exterior and Façade
The cathedral's exterior is clad in polychrome marble panels from Carrara (white), Prato (green), and Siena (red). The original medieval façade was left incomplete and was eventually dismantled in the 16th century. The current elaborate neo-Gothic façade was designed by Emilio De Fabris and completed in 1887, harmonizing with the older structures of the complex.

### Interior and Art
The vast interior has a Latin cross plan and a relatively bare, austere appearance. It is notable for its 44 large stained-glass windows, designed by artists including Donatello and Ghiberti. The interior houses important artworks, including frescoes of Florentine military leaders by Paolo Uccello and Andrea del Castagno, and a colossal clock face by Uccello. The dome's interior is painted with a massive fresco of *The Last Judgment*, begun by Giorgio Vasari and completed by Federico Zuccari.

### The Dome's Cracking and Monitoring
The dome has exhibited cracks since its construction, likely due to its immense weight (estimated at 25,000 tons) and thermal expansion. A symmetrical pattern of major "Type A" cracks divides the dome into four sections. Modern monitoring systems, installed since the 1970s, show the cracks widen by approximately 3 mm per century. Analysis confirms the primary cause is the dome's dead weight, not the filling of historical scaffolding holes in the 1980s.

## Terms
*   **Polychrome marble:** Facing of multi-colored marble panels, a characteristic of Tuscan church architecture.
*   **Gothic style:** Medieval architectural style featuring pointed arches, ribbed vaults, and flying buttresses.
*   **Octagonal dome:** An eight-sided dome, a challenging form to construct without external supports.
*   **Centering:** Temporary wooden scaffolding and forms used to support an arch or dome during construction.
*   **Hoop stress:** The outward force exerted by a dome's weight, which must be contained to prevent collapse.
*   **Double-shell dome:** A dome construction with two concentric layers, an inner structural shell and an outer protective shell.
*   **Lantern:** A decorative structure atop a dome that admits light and crowns the building.
*   **Arte della Lana:** The powerful guild of wool merchants that patronized the cathedral's construction.
*   **Mendicant orders:** Religious orders (like Franciscans and Dominicans) that relied on alms, influencing the austerity of their churches.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Original Façade Appearance:** The exact design of the original medieval façade, dismantled in 1587, is debated, with knowledge based only on later drawings.
*   **Cause of Cracking:** While the primary cause is identified as the dome's dead weight, the precise contribution of historical earthquakes, thermal cycles, and material fatigue remains a subject of ongoing study.
*   **Impact of Modern Interventions:** The 1980s decision to fill 48 historical scaffolding holes with concrete was initially theorized to have worsened cracking, but later analysis concluded it had no significant impact on the dome's expansion and contraction.

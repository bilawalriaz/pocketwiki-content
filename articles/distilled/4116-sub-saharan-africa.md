# Sub-Saharan Africa

## Overview
Sub-Saharan Africa is the region of the African continent south of the Sahara Desert, encompassing Central, East, Southern, and West Africa. It is a non-standardized geographical and geopolitical term, with the number of included countries varying from 46 to 48 depending on the organization. The region is defined by its distinct history, diverse cultures, and significant economic and demographic challenges, including widespread poverty and rapid population growth.

## Timeline
- **c. 3000 BCE** — Iron-smelting evidence found in Central African Republic and Cameroon.
- **c. 2500 BCE** — Bantu expansion begins from West Central Africa.
- **c. 1000 BCE** — Bantu expansion reaches East and Central Africa.
- **1st millennium BCE** — Trans-Saharan trade in full motion; Nok culture flourishes in Nigeria.
- **1st century CE** — Aksumite Empire rises to prominence in the Horn of Africa.
- **14th-15th centuries** — Large medieval kingdoms emerge in Southeast Africa (e.g., Buganda).
- **1652** — Dutch establish a settlement at the Cape of Good Hope.
- **1960** — Nigeria gains independence, part of a wider decolonization wave.
- **2010** — South Africa hosts the FIFA World Cup, the first in a sub-Saharan country.
- **2022** — An estimated 460 million people in the region live in extreme poverty.

## Body

### Nomenclature and Criticism
The term "sub-Saharan Africa" is a geographical grouping that has been criticized as a racist construction intended to separate the continent. Critics argue it overlooks historical, cultural, and genetic connections between North and sub-Saharan Africa. Historically, medieval Arabic commentators used terms like *bilâd as-sûdân* ("Land of the Blacks") for the Sudan region and *Zanj* for the Swahili coast.

### History and Prehistory
Early hominids developed bipedal locomotion in Africa between 10 and 5 million years ago. *Homo sapiens* emerged in East and Southern Africa by around 350,000 to 260,000 years ago and began migrating out of the continent between 50,000 and 60,000 years ago. The Sahara became a desert barrier around 3900 BCE, though trade routes using oases and later camels connected the regions. Major historical developments include the Bantu migration, the rise of empires like Ghana, Mali, and Songhai in West Africa, the Luba and Lunda kingdoms in Central Africa, and the Aksumite Empire in the Horn of Africa.

### Geography and Climate
The region features diverse climate zones, from the semi-arid Sahel belt to tropical rainforests in the Congo Basin and savannas in East and Southern Africa. It includes deserts like the Namib and Kalahari, and the Cape Floristic Region at its southern tip. Countries like South Africa and the Democratic Republic of the Congo are considered megadiverse.

### Economy and Resources
Sub-Saharan Africa is a major global exporter of minerals, including gold, uranium, chromium, and diamonds, and holds significant oil reserves. Agriculture is vital, employing 50-90% of the labor force in some countries, but productivity is low due to factors like poor-quality inputs and limited credit access. The region faces severe infrastructure deficits, particularly in energy and transport, with less than 40% of rural Africans living near an all-season road.

### Demographics and Society
The population was 1.1 billion in 2019 with a growth rate of 2.3%, and is projected to reach 2-2.5 billion by 2050. The region has the world's highest fertility rates and a very young population, with over 40% under age 15. It is linguistically diverse, with over 1,500 languages from families like Niger-Congo, Afroasiatic, and Nilo-Saharan. Christianity and Islam are the principal religions, alongside traditional African religions.

### Health and Education
Major health challenges include HIV/AIDS, malaria, and maternal mortality, though life expectancy increased from 40 years in 1960 to 61 in 2017. Significant progress has been made in primary school enrollment since 1999, but literacy rates remain low, averaging 65% in 2017, and 29.6 million children were still out of school in 2012.

### Culture
Sub-Saharan African cultures are communal, with strong extended family ties. Art is often three-dimensional, functional, and visually abstract, using fractal patterns. Traditional music is frequently polyrhythmic and drum-centered. Cuisine varies regionally but often features starchy staples like fufu, ugali, and cassava.

## Terms
- **Bantu expansion:** A major migration of Bantu-speaking peoples from West Central Africa that spread agriculture and iron-working across central, eastern, and southern Africa.
- **Sahel:** A semi-arid belt south of the Sahara Desert, acting as a transition zone between the desert and the savanna.
- **Trans-Saharan trade:** Historic trade routes crossing the Sahara Desert, connecting sub-Saharan Africa with North Africa and the Mediterranean, trading goods like gold, salt, and slaves.
- **Subsistence farming:** Agriculture where crops are grown primarily to feed the farmer and their family, with little surplus for sale.
- **Polyrhythmic:** A musical texture involving the simultaneous use of two or more conflicting rhythms.
- **Henotheism:** The belief in one's own gods while accepting the existence of other gods.
- **Brain drain:** The emigration of highly trained or intelligent people from a particular country.
- **Megadiverse country:** A country that harbors the majority of Earth's species and high numbers of endemic species.

## Debates and Open questions
- The term "sub-Saharan Africa" is debated as a potentially artificial and racist construct that ignores deep historical and cultural connections across the entire African continent.
- The exact number of countries considered part of the region varies between international organizations like the UN, WHO, and World Bank.
- The causes of persistently low agricultural productivity are debated, with recent research focusing on the role of poor-quality inputs and market failures rather than just access to credit.

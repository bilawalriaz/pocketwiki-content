# Greater Antilles

## Overview
The Greater Antilles is a grouping of the largest islands in the Caribbean Sea, including Cuba, Hispaniola, Puerto Rico, Jamaica, Navassa Island, and the Cayman Islands. This region is geographically and historically significant, containing nearly 90% of the land mass and over 90% of the population of the entire West Indies. It is home to seven island states and territories, with a complex colonial history that has shaped its modern political and cultural landscape.

## Timeline
- **c. 1500s** — Spanish colonization begins on Cuba, Hispaniola, and Puerto Rico.
- **1625** — France begins exerting influence over Haiti, dividing Hispaniola.
- **1804** — Haiti declares independence from France, becoming the first black-led republic in the Americas.
- **1821** — The Dominican Republic declares independence from Spain.
- **1844** — The Dominican Republic gains independence from Haitian rule.
- **1898** — Spain cedes Cuba and Puerto Rico to the United States after the Spanish-American War.
- **1902** — Cuba is granted formal independence from U.S. military rule.
- **1917** — The Jones–Shafroth Act grants U.S. citizenship to Puerto Ricans.
- **1959** — The Cuban Revolution establishes a communist state in Cuba.
- **1962** — Jamaica gains independence from the United Kingdom.

## Body

### Etymology and Geography
The term "Antilles" originated on medieval European charts as a mythical land called "Antillia." Geographically, the Greater Antilles comprises four major islands and numerous smaller ones. Cuba is the largest island in the region, followed by Hispaniola. The islands were formed by the relative motion of the Caribbean plate and surrounding plates, involving processes like volcanism and crustal deformation. Together with the Lesser Antilles and the Lucayan Archipelago, they form the West Indies.

### Colonial History and Independence
The first European contact came from Christopher Columbus's first voyage. Spain established permanent settlements, and the Atlantic slave trade brought many Africans to the islands. France and Britain later exerted colonial influence, dividing Hispaniola and taking Jamaica. The Haitian Revolution (1791–1804) was the first successful anti-slavery and anti-colonial insurrection by self-liberated slaves, establishing Haiti as an independent nation. The Dominican Republic later gained independence from both Spain and Haiti. Most of the Greater Antilles remained under colonial rule until the 20th century, with the Spanish-American War (1898) transferring Cuba and Puerto Rico to U.S. control. Jamaica was the last to achieve independence in 1962.

### Demographics and Languages
The Greater Antilles is part of Latin America, with a population of 38 million. The largest city is Santo Domingo. Languages are primarily colonial in origin: Spanish is dominant in Cuba, the Dominican Republic, and Puerto Rico. Haiti has Haitian Creole and French as official languages. English is the main language in Jamaica and the Cayman Islands. Human Development Index (HDI) levels vary, with Haiti having the lowest in the Americas.

### Political Status
Most of the Greater Antilles consists of independent countries. However, Puerto Rico and Navassa Island are unincorporated U.S. territories, and the Cayman Islands are a British Overseas Territory. The Cuban Revolution of 1959 established Cuba as the only communist state in the region.

## Terms
- **Greater Antilles**: A grouping of the larger islands in the Caribbean Sea.
- **West Indies**: A region comprising the Greater Antilles, Lesser Antilles, and Lucayan Archipelago.
- **Encomienda**: A Spanish colonial system of forced labor imposed on Indigenous populations.
- **Haitian Revolution**: The first successful anti-slavery and anti-colonial insurrection by self-liberated slaves, leading to Haiti's independence.
- **Human Development Index (HDI)**: A composite statistic of life expectancy, education, and per capita income indicators, used to rank countries.
- **Unincorporated territory**: A territory under the jurisdiction of the United States but not fully incorporated into it.
- **British Overseas Territory**: A territory under the jurisdiction and sovereignty of the United Kingdom.

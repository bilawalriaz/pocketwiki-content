# Harry S. Truman

## Overview
Harry S. Truman was the 33rd U.S. president (1945–1953), assuming office after Franklin D. Roosevelt's death. He presided over the end of World War II, the start of the Cold War, and the Korean War, making pivotal decisions on atomic weapons, containment of communism, and civil rights that shaped the modern world.

## Timeline
- **May 8, 1884** — Born in Lamar, Missouri.
- **1917–1918** — Served as a captain in World War I, commanding Battery D, 129th Field Artillery.
- **1922** — Elected as a Jackson County judge with support from the Pendergast political machine.
- **1934** — Elected to the U.S. Senate from Missouri.
- **1941–1944** — Chaired the Truman Committee, investigating waste in wartime contracts.
- **January 20, 1945** — Sworn in as Vice President.
- **April 12, 1945** — Became President upon Roosevelt's death.
- **August 6 & 9, 1945** — Authorized atomic bombings of Hiroshima and Nagasaki.
- **1947** — Announced the Truman Doctrine to contain communism; signed the National Security Act creating the CIA and NSC.
- **1948** — Signed the Marshall Plan; recognized the State of Israel; issued Executive Order 9981 desegregating the armed forces.
- **November 2, 1948** — Won a surprise re-election victory.
- **June 25, 1950** — Korean War began; U.S. intervened under UN mandate.
- **April 11, 1951** — Fired General Douglas MacArthur from command in Korea.
- **January 31, 1950** — Ordered development of the hydrogen bomb.
- **November 1, 1950** — Survived an assassination attempt at Blair House.
- **April 1952** — Seized steel mills to prevent a strike; Supreme Court ruled the action unconstitutional.
- **March 1952** — Withdrew from the presidential race after poor primary results.
- **December 26, 1972** — Died in Kansas City, Missouri.

## Body

### Early Life and Military Service
Born in rural Missouri, Truman was raised in Independence. He failed the West Point eye exam and joined the Missouri National Guard in 1905. During World War I, he served as an artillery captain in France, earning a reputation for leadership. After the war, he ran a failed haberdashery and entered politics with the support of Kansas City's Pendergast machine, serving as a county judge and then a U.S. Senator.

### Rise to the Presidency
As a Senator, Truman gained national prominence chairing the Truman Committee, which saved billions by exposing wartime waste. He was elected Vice President in 1944 and became President in April 1945, unaware of the Manhattan Project until after taking office. He authorized the atomic bombings of Japan in August 1945, arguing it saved lives that would be lost in an invasion.

### Cold War and Foreign Policy
Truman's administration defined early Cold War policy. The Truman Doctrine (1947) committed the U.S. to containing Soviet expansion, while the Marshall Plan provided $13 billion to rebuild Western Europe. He oversaw the Berlin Airlift (1948–49) and helped found NATO (1949). His recognition of Israel in 1948 over State Department objections was influenced by personal conviction and advice from friend Eddie Jacobson. The Korean War (1950–53) became a major crisis; Truman fired General MacArthur for insubordination to prevent wider conflict with China, a decision that severely damaged his popularity.

### Domestic Policy and Civil Rights
Truman proposed the "Fair Deal," a liberal domestic agenda including national health insurance, but a conservative coalition in Congress blocked most of it. He made civil rights a moral priority, creating a presidential committee and issuing Executive Order 9981 to desegregate the armed forces. His administration also faced postwar economic challenges, including strikes and inflation, and corruption scandals that became a major issue in the 1952 election.

### Post-Presidency and Legacy
Truman left office deeply unpopular but later regained stature. He established the model for presidential libraries and secured passage of the Former Presidents Act, which provided a pension. Historians now consistently rank him in the first quartile of U.S. presidents for his decisive leadership in establishing the postwar international order and advancing civil rights.

## Terms
- **Truman Doctrine**: 1947 policy pledging U.S. support for nations resisting communist expansion.
- **Marshall Plan**: U.S. program providing over $13 billion in economic aid to rebuild Western Europe after WWII.
- **Containment**: U.S. foreign policy goal of preventing the spread of Soviet communism.
- **NATO**: North Atlantic Treaty Organization, a 1949 military alliance between the U.S., Canada, and Western European nations.
- **Fair Deal**: Truman's domestic policy agenda, which proposed expansions of Social Security, national health insurance, and civil rights.
- **Executive Order 9981**: 1948 order directing the desegregation of the U.S. Armed Forces.
- **Manhattan Project**: Top-secret U.S. program to develop the atomic bomb during WWII.
- **McCarthyism**: Widespread anti-communist suspicion and accusations in the late 1940s and 1950s, named after Senator Joseph McCarthy.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Atomic Bombings**: Historians debate whether using atomic weapons on Hiroshima and Nagasaki was militarily necessary to end WWII or constituted an unnecessary act.
- **Korean War**: Truman's decision to fire General MacArthur is debated as either a crucial stand for civilian control of the military or a politically damaging mistake that prolonged the war.
- **"Losing" China**: Truman faced intense criticism from conservatives for not preventing Mao Zedong's communist victory in the Chinese Civil War.
- **Post-Presidency Finances**: While long believed to have been nearly destitute, evidence shows Truman accumulated considerable wealth from his salary, memoirs, and investments, complicating the narrative that his financial struggles led to the Former Presidents Act.

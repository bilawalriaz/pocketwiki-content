# Honshu

## Overview
Honshu is the largest and most populous island of Japan, serving as the nation's historical, cultural, and economic core. It is the seventh-largest island globally and is home to over 80% of Japan's population, including the capital, Tokyo. The island's geography is defined by its mountainous, volcanic terrain and its position on the Pacific Ring of Fire, making it seismically active.

## Timeline
- **c. 37,000 years ago** — First humans, Stone Age hunter-gatherers, arrive in Honshu.
- **c. 14,000–300 BCE** — Jōmon period; emergence of cord-marked pottery and sedentary communities.
- **1603–1867** — Edo (Tokugawa) Period; Tokugawa shogunate rules Japan.
- **1868** — Meiji Restoration; end of the shogunate and return to imperial rule.
- **1923** — Great Kantō earthquake heavily damages Tokyo.
- **1964** — Tokaido Shinkansen, the world's first high-speed rail line, opens.
- **2011** — Magnitude 9.0–9.1 earthquake and tsunami cause the Fukushima nuclear disaster.

## Body

### Geography and Climate
Honshu is roughly 1,300 km long and 227,960 km² in area, slightly larger than Britain. It is mountainous and volcanic, with the Japanese Alps spanning its width. Its highest peak is the active volcano Mount Fuji (3,776 m). The island experiences frequent earthquakes and has 40 active volcanoes. Its climate varies: the south has long, hot summers and mild winters, while the north has four distinct seasons. Central Honshu, especially around the Japanese Alps, receives heavy snowfall in winter.

### Population and Economy
As of 2025, Honshu's population is 100.7 million, constituting 81.9% of Japan's total. About 30% live in the Greater Tokyo Area. The island is the economic heart of Japan, generating over 80% of the nation's GDP. Most industry is concentrated in a southern coastal belt (the Taiheiyō Belt megalopolis) from Tokyo to Hiroshima. Agriculture includes rice, fruit, and vegetables, with the Tōhoku region being a major rice producer. Honshu holds most of Japan's mineral reserves, including copper, lead, zinc, and small oil and coal deposits.

### Transportation and Connections
Honshu is linked to Japan's other main islands by bridges and tunnels, including the Seikan Tunnel to Hokkaido and the Akashi Kaikyo Bridge to Shikoku. Its high-speed rail network includes the Tokaido Shinkansen (opened 1964) and the San'yō Shinkansen. Major airports serving the island include Haneda, Narita, Chubu Centrair, and Kansai International.

### Administrative Structure
The island is divided into five nominal regions and contains 34 prefectures, including metropolitan Tokyo. Some smaller islands, like the Ogasawara and Sado Islands, are administratively part of these prefectures.

## Terms
- **Jōmon period**: A prehistoric era in Japan (c. 14,000–300 BCE) characterized by cord-marked pottery and early sedentary communities.
- **Meiji Restoration**: The 1868 political revolution that ended the Tokugawa shogunate and restored direct imperial rule.
- **Taiheiyō Belt**: A megalopolis along Honshu's southern coast, containing most of Japan's industry and population.
- **Shinkansen**: Japan's high-speed rail network; the Tokaido line was the world's first.
- **Ring of Fire**: A zone of frequent earthquakes and volcanic eruptions encircling the Pacific Ocean, on which Honshu sits.
- **Seismic activity**: The occurrence of earthquakes and related geological events, common on Honshu due to its tectonic setting.

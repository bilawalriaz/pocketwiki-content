# Poyang Lake

## Overview
Poyang Lake is China's largest freshwater lake, located in Jiangxi Province. It is a vital ecological habitat, particularly for migratory birds and endangered species like the finless porpoise, but faces severe environmental pressures from human activity and climate factors, leading to significant shrinkage and biodiversity loss.

## Timeline
- **c. AD 400** — The Yangtze River's course shifts, causing the Gan River to back up and form Poyang Lake.
- **Tang dynasty** — The lake reaches its greatest historical size of 6,000 square kilometers.
- **1363** — The Battle of Lake Poyang, claimed to be the largest naval battle in history, occurs.
- **2002** — A fishing ban is implemented on the lake.
- **2007** — Population of the Yangtze finless porpoise is reported to be less than half of 1997 levels.
- **2012** — Severe drought and other factors shrink the lake to about 200 square kilometers.
- **2019** — Conservation International China begins applying the Freshwater Health Index in the basin.
- **January 2020** — China imposes a 10-year fishing moratorium on the Yangtze, including Poyang Lake.

## Body

### Formation and History
Poyang Lake formed around AD 400 due to a shift in the Yangtze River's course, which caused the Gan River to back up. The lake's creation flooded ancient counties, leading to a mass migration that gave rise to the idiom "Drowning Haihun County gives rise to Wucheng Township." It reached its maximum size during the Tang dynasty. The lake is the site of the 1363 Battle of Lake Poyang, a major naval conflict.

### Ecological Significance and Threats
The lake is a critical habitat for half a million migratory birds, hosting up to 90% of the world's critically endangered Siberian cranes in winter. It is also a primary refuge for the Yangtze finless porpoise, whose population is declining at 7.3% per year. Major threats include sand dredging, which muddies the water and disrupts the porpoise's sonar, and heavy ship traffic. A proposed Poyang Lake Dam to control water levels is criticized by scientists and groups like the World Wide Fund for Nature for potentially devastating wildlife diversity.

### Shrinkage and Conservation
Poyang Lake's area fluctuates dramatically, averaging 3,500 square kilometers in a normal year. In 2012, drought and water storage at the upstream Three Gorges Dam reduced it to about 200 square kilometers. To combat ecological decline, a fishing ban has been in place since 2002, and a 10-year moratorium was imposed in 2020. Conservation efforts, such as the 2019 Freshwater Health Index project, aim to identify challenges for wetland protection and sustainable use.

## Terms
*   **Finless porpoise (jiangzhu):** A critically endangered freshwater cetacean native to the Yangtze River system, including Poyang Lake.
*   **Siberian crane:** A critically endangered bird species, of which up to 90% overwinter in Poyang Lake.
*   **Three Gorges Dam:** A major hydroelectric dam on the Yangtze River upstream from Poyang Lake; its water storage practices affect the lake's levels.
*   **Sand dredging:** The extraction of sand from the lakebed, a major local industry that harms wildlife by increasing water turbidity and ship traffic.
*   **Freshwater Health Index:** A framework used to assess the ecological health of freshwater ecosystems and guide conservation.
*   **Sluice wall:** A proposed engineering structure to be built across the lake's connection to the Yangtze to artificially control water levels.

## Debates and open questions
The primary debate concerns the proposed Poyang Lake Dam. The Jiangxi local government supports it to maintain water levels, while scientists and environmental groups argue it would cause devastating effects on wildlife diversity. The long-term effectiveness of fishing moratoriums and other conservation measures in reversing the decline of species like the finless porpoise remains an open question.

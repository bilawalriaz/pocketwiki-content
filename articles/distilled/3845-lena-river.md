# Lena River

## Overview
The Lena River is the easternmost of Siberia's three great Arctic rivers, flowing entirely within Russia. It is the world's eleventh-longest river and the longest river system located wholly within a single country. Its vast basin, underlain by continuous permafrost, is a remote and ecologically significant region of the Russian Far East.

## Timeline
- **1620–1623** — Russian fur hunters under Demid Pyanda discover the Lena River.
- **1628** — Vasily Bugor reaches the Lena and founds Kirinsk.
- **1631–1632** — Pyotr Beketov founds the fortress of Yakutsk on the Lena.
- **1655** — The Lena Delta is reached by Russian explorers.
- **1881** — Survivors of the Jeannette expedition reach the Lena Delta.
- **1885–1886** — Baron Eduard Von Toll's expedition explores the Lena Delta and New Siberian Islands.
- **1912** — The Lena massacre occurs near Bodaybo, sparking revolutionary sentiment.

## Body

### Course and Basin
The Lena originates in the Baikal Mountains, 7 km west of Lake Baikal, and flows north-east to the Arctic Ocean. Its 4,294 km course is joined by major tributaries including the Kirenga, Vitim, Olyokma, Aldan, and Vilyuy. The river is navigable for 3,540 km, with an ice-free navigation period of about 70 days in the estuary and 125 days elsewhere. The basin covers 2,490,000 km², with a mean annual discharge of 489 km³. Permafrost underlies the entire catchment and is continuous in over 75% of the basin.

### Delta and Ecology
The Lena Delta, covering 30,000 km², extends 100 km into the Laptev Sea. It is frozen tundra for about seven months of the year, transforming into a wetland in summer. The delta is a protected wildlife reserve. Its fish resources, including Siberian taimen and sturgeon, are well-preserved due to the region's remoteness.

### Human History and Exploration
The river's name is believed to derive from the Even-Evenk name *Elyu-Ene* ("the Large River"). Russian exploration began in the 1620s, leading to the establishment of trading posts like Yakutsk. The Lena Delta was mapped by Fyodor Matisen. The 1912 Lena massacre, where striking miners were shot, was reported in the Duma and is credited with stimulating revolutionary feeling. Vladimir Lenin may have taken his alias from the river during his exile.

## Terms
- **Permafrost**: Ground that remains frozen for two or more consecutive years.
- **Drainage basin**: The area of land where all water drains into a common outlet.
- **Tributary**: A river that flows into a larger river.
- **Delta**: A landform created by sediment deposition at the mouth of a river.
- **Yasak**: A tribute, often in furs, collected from indigenous peoples in Siberia.
- **Voyevoda**: A historical Russian military governor or commander.

# ABC islands

## Overview
The ABC islands—Aruba, Bonaire, and Curaçao—are a trio of westernmost Caribbean islands that share a Dutch colonial history and current political ties to the Kingdom of the Netherlands. Their strategic location near Venezuela has shaped their economic and cultural development, making them a unique case of European political entities in the Caribbean with distinct autonomous statuses.

## Timeline
- **1499** — First European exploration, possibly by Alonso de Ojeda.
- **1527** — Spanish establish government and Catholicism.
- **1634** — The Dutch win control from Spain.
- **1814** — Anglo-Dutch Treaty cedes the islands to the Netherlands.
- **1863** — Abolition of slavery devastates plantation economies.
- **Early 20th c.** — Oil discovery in Venezuela leads to major refinery construction.
- **1954** — Islands become part of the Netherlands Antilles, gaining autonomy.
- **1986** — Aruba secedes from the Netherlands Antilles, becoming a separate country within the Kingdom.
- **10 October 2010** — Dissolution of the Netherlands Antilles; Curaçao becomes a constituent country, Bonaire a special municipality.

## Body

### History and Political Status
The islands were originally inhabited by the Arawakan Caquetio people. Spanish control was established by 1527, but the Dutch West India Company seized Curaçao in 1634, later taking Bonaire and Aruba. The Dutch used Curaçao and Bonaire for slave-based plantations, while Aruba's dry climate limited large-scale agriculture. The islands were administered as the Colony of Curaçao and Dependencies from 1815. In 1954, they gained autonomy within the Kingdom of the Netherlands as part of the Netherlands Antilles. Political evolution continued: Aruba became a separate constituent country in 1986, and in 2010, the Netherlands Antilles dissolved. Today, Aruba and Curaçao are autonomous countries within the Kingdom, while Bonaire is a special municipality of the Netherlands proper. All are outside the EU but are special member state territories, granting citizens Dutch/EU nationality and benefits.

### Geography and Climate
Located just north of Venezuela, the islands are geographically on the South American continental shelf. From west to east, they are Aruba, Curaçao, and Bonaire. Aruba is flat and exposed to storm surges, while Bonaire and Curaçao are sheltered by reefs. They have a hot desert climate (Köppen BWh/BWk) with high evaporation and sparse rainfall, primarily from October to January. Rainfall is highly variable, influenced by the El Niño-Southern Oscillation; severe droughts can occur during El Niño years, while La Niña can bring heavy rains. Temperatures are consistently hot, averaging 28–37°C (82–99°F).

### Demographics and Language
The population of Curaçao and Bonaire is predominantly of African descent, while Aruba's majority is Mestizo and European. Significant immigration from South America, the Caribbean, and elsewhere has occurred. Multilingualism is common. Dutch is the official language of government and education, but Papiamentu (or Papiamento on Aruba), a Portuguese-based creole, is the most widely spoken daily language and has been co-official since 2003 (Aruba) and 2007 (Curaçao and Bonaire). Spanish and English are also widely spoken due to regional ties and tourism.

### Environment and Economy
The islands have a unique ecology with cactus scrub, flamingos, and sea turtles. Bonaire is renowned for ecotourism and diving. Aruba has designated 20% of its land as Arikok National Park. Economically, the islands shifted from agriculture to oil refining in the 20th century, which supported the Allied effort in WWII. Today, tourism and offshore finance are key sectors, with the Netherlands benefiting from preferential trade and resource rights.

## Terms
- **Constituent country**: An autonomous country within the Kingdom of the Netherlands (e.g., Aruba, Curaçao).
- **Special municipality**: A municipality of the Netherlands proper located outside Europe (e.g., Bonaire).
- **Papiamentu/Papiamento**: A Portuguese-based creole language, the most widely spoken language on the islands.
- **Leeward Antilles**: The westernmost island chain of the Lesser Antilles, including the ABC islands.
- **Köppen climate classification BWh/BWk**: Codes for hot and cold desert climates, respectively.
- **Southern Oscillation**: A climate pattern (El Niño/La Niña) that significantly influences the islands' rainfall variability.

## Debates and Open Questions
The source notes that the first European explorer is disputed, with letters of Amerigo Vespucci claiming Alonso de Ojeda landed in 1499, but Vespucci's own exploration is also cited. The political and economic implications of the islands' special status within the EU and the Kingdom of the Netherlands continue to evolve.

# Blood

## Overview
Blood is a specialized connective tissue and body fluid in vertebrates that circulates through a closed vascular system, delivering oxygen, nutrients, and hormones to cells while removing metabolic wastes like carbon dioxide and urea. It consists of formed elements—red blood cells (erythrocytes), white blood cells (leukocytes), and platelets (thrombocytes)—suspended in plasma, a straw-yellow fluid that is 92% water. Hemoglobin within erythrocytes binds oxygen reversibly, increasing blood’s oxygen-carrying capacity roughly seventyfold compared to simple dissolution. Beyond transport, blood mediates immunity, coagulation, thermoregulation, and hydraulic functions (e.g., erectile tissue). Disorders range from anemia and leukemia to coagulation defects and blood-borne infections; treatments include transfusion, plasma expanders, and targeted phlebotomy.

## Timeline
- **c. 1628** — William Harvey describes systemic blood circulation.
- **1900** — Karl Landsteiner discovers the ABO blood group system.
- **1907** — Jan Janský classifies blood into four types (A, B, AB, O); first ABO-matched transfusion performed.
- **1914 (27 Mar)** — First non-direct (indirect) blood transfusion performed.
- **1937** — Rhesus (Rh) factor discovered.

## Body

### Composition and Constituents
In humans, blood accounts for ~7% of body weight (~5 L in an average adult) with a density of ~1060 kg/m³. By volume, erythrocytes constitute ~45% (hematocrit), plasma ~54.3%, and leukocytes ~0.7%. Plasma (55% of whole blood) is 92% water, 8% proteins (albumin, clotting factors, immunoglobulins), plus electrolytes, nutrients, hormones, and waste. Serum is plasma minus clotting factors. Erythrocytes (4.2–6.1 million/µL) lack nuclei in mammals and carry hemoglobin; their combined surface area is ~2,000× the body’s exterior. Leukocytes (4,000–11,000/µL) mediate immunity; their malignancy is leukemia. Thrombocytes (200,000–500,000/µL) form platelet plugs; fibrin mesh stabilizes clots. Non-mammalian vertebrates have nucleated, ovoid erythrocytes and use nucleated thrombocytes (not platelets) for clotting.

### Physiology: Circulation, Production, and Gas Transport
The heart pumps deoxygenated blood from the right ventricle → pulmonary artery → lungs → pulmonary veins → left atrium → left ventricle → aorta → systemic arteries → capillaries → systemic veins → right atrium. Skeletal muscle compression aids venous return. Hematopoiesis occurs in bone marrow (erythropoiesis, myelopoiesis); in adults, active marrow is limited to axial skeleton and proximal long bones. The liver produces most plasma proteins; the kidney regulates water fraction and excretes waste. Erythrocytes live ~120 days before splenic/hepatic removal.

Oxygen transport: 98.5% bound to hemoglobin (capacity 1.36–1.40 mL O₂/g Hb), 1.5% dissolved. Arterial hemoglobin is 98–99% saturated at rest, delivering 950–1150 mL O₂/min; resting consumption is 200–250 mL/min. Venous saturation ~75% at rest, dropping <15% in elite athletes during exercise. Fetal hemoglobin (HbF) has higher O₂ affinity for placental transfer. Carbon dioxide transport: ~70% as bicarbonate (HCO₃⁻) via carbonic anhydrase in erythrocytes, ~23% as carbamino-hemoglobin, ~7% dissolved. The Haldane effect (O₂ binding reduces CO₂ affinity) and Bohr effect (↑CO₂/↓pH reduces O₂ affinity) coordinate gas exchange. Deoxyhemoglobin buffers H⁺ ions.

### Homeostasis and Specialized Functions
Blood pH is tightly held at 7.35–7.45; <6.9 or >7.8 is usually lethal. Respiratory and renal systems compensate via bicarbonate buffering. Lymph forms by capillary ultrafiltration, drains via thoracic duct into left subclavian vein. Thermoregulation: cutaneous vasodilation increases heat loss; vasoconstriction conserves core heat. Flow rates vary: liver ~1350 mL/min, kidney ~1100 mL/min, brain ~700 mL/min; per 100 g tissue, kidney, adrenal, and thyroid are highest. Hydraulic functions include erectile tissue engorgement and spider leg extension via hemolymph pressure.

### Color
Oxygenated arterial/capillary blood is bright red; deoxygenated venous blood is dark red. Carboxyhemoglobin (CO poisoning) is bright red; cyanide poisoning leaves venous blood oxygenated/red. Methemoglobin (oxidized heme) is brownish; sulfhemoglobinemia yields dark red with bluish hue. Cyanosis (blue skin) reflects altered light scattering, not blue blood. *Prasinohaema* skinks have green blood from biliverdin accumulation.

### Disorders
**Volume:** Healthy adults tolerate ~20% loss (1 L) before symptoms, ~40% (2 L) before shock. Dehydration reduces volume, causing orthostatic hypotension.  
**Circulation:** Shock = inadequate perfusion. Atherosclerosis narrows arteries (age, smoking, hypertension, hyperlipidemia, diabetes). Thrombosis obstructs vessels. Ischemia = inadequate perfusion; infarction = necrosis from blocked supply.  
**Hematological:** Anemia (Hb <13.5 g/dL men, <12.0 g/dL women) from blood loss, thalassemia, nutritional deficiency, or genetic defects; treated with typed transfusions. Sickle-cell anemia alters erythrocyte function. Leukemia = malignancy of blood-forming tissue. Polycythemia vera (excess erythrocytes) and essential thrombocytosis (excess platelets) may be premalignant. Myelodysplastic syndromes = ineffective cell production. Hemophilia = genetic clotting factor defect → hemarthrosis. Thrombocytopenia → coagulopathy. Thrombophilia → thrombosis.  
**Infectious:** Blood transmits HIV, Hepatitis B/C, malaria, trypanosomes. Bacteremia/sepsis (bacterial), viremia (viral). Blood-stained objects are biohazards.  
**Carbon monoxide poisoning:** CO binds hemoglobin irreversibly → carboxyhemoglobin → functional anemia/suffocation.

### Treatments
Transfusion uses typed, crossmatched donor blood (ABO and Rh systems critical). Products: packed red cells, platelets, plasma, cryoprecipitate, factor concentrates. Intravenous drugs bypass gut absorption. Plasma expanders (crystalloids: saline; colloids: albumin, dextran, fresh frozen plasma) are preferred over transfusion in acute hemorrhagic shock because transfused erythrocytes do not metabolize immediately. Therapeutic phlebotomy treats hemochromatosis and polycythemia; historical bloodletting (pre-19th century) was based on humoral theory.

### History and Culture
Greek humorism (four humors) may derive from observing layered clotted blood: dark clot (black bile), erythrocytes (blood), leukocytes (phlegm), serum (yellow bile). Plato/Aristotle held blood was made from food in the heart. ABO system (Landsteiner 1900, Janský 1907), first indirect transfusion 1914, Rh factor 1937. Culturally, blood symbolizes kinship ("bloodline," "blood is thicker than water"). Leviticus 17:11 ("life…is in the blood") underpins Jewish (kashrut: no blood consumption, salting meat), Islamic (Qur’an 5:3 forbids blood; ritual slaughter drains blood), and Christian (Eucharistic theology: transubstantiation vs. symbolism) dietary and ritual laws. Jehovah’s Witnesses refuse transfusions per Acts 15:28–29. Indigenous Australians use blood/ochre in rituals to connect with Dreamtime. Germanic Blóts sprinkled blood for blessing (blóedsian → bless). Cruentation (corpse bleeding at murderer’s presence) used until early 17th century. Vampire myths exist globally; only hematophagous bats are linked to European folklore.

### Invertebrates and Other Uses
Invertebrates use hemolymph (open system); crustaceans/mollusks use hemocyanin (copper-based); most insects lack respiratory pigment, relying on tracheae. Forensics: bloodstain pattern analysis reconstructs events; residue links suspects. Archaeology: blood residue analysis. Art: performance (Nitsch, Kantor, etc.), photography (Serrano), sculpture (Quinn’s frozen blood casts). Genealogy: "bloodline," "blue-blood," "royal blood."

## Terms
- ****Erythrocyte**** — Red blood cell; in mammals, anucleate, packed with hemoglobin for O₂ transport.
- ****Leukocyte**** — White blood cell; nucleated immune cell defending against pathogens and clearing debris.
- ****Thrombocyte (Platelet)**** — Anucleate cell fragment (mammals) or nucleated spindle cell (non-mammals) essential for clot formation.
- ****Plasma**** — Liquid component of blood (55% volume), 92% water, carrying proteins, nutrients, hormones, electrolytes.
- ****Serum**** — Plasma minus clotting factors (fibrinogen, etc.); mostly albumin and immunoglobulins.
- ****Hematocrit**** — Volume fraction of whole blood occupied by erythrocytes (~45% normal).
- ****Hemoglobin**** — Iron-containing protein in erythrocytes that reversibly binds O₂ (and CO₂, CO, H⁺).
- ****Hematopoiesis**** — Formation of all blood cellular components in bone marrow (erythropoiesis + myelopoiesis).
- ****Bohr effect**** — Decreased hemoglobin O₂ affinity caused by ↑pCO₂ or ↓pH, promoting O₂ release in tissues.
- ****Haldane effect**** — Decreased hemoglobin CO₂ affinity caused by O₂ binding, promoting CO₂ release in lungs.

## Debates and open questions
- **Erythrocyte maturation:** Why mammals uniquely evolved anucleate erythrocytes while other vertebrates retain nuclei.  
- **Oxygen affinity modulation:** Precise in vivo integration of Bohr/Haldane effects with 2,3-BPG and temperature across species.  
- **Transfusion thresholds:** Optimal hemoglobin triggers for transfusion in critical illness remain contested.  
- **Blood substitutes:** Development of safe, effective hemoglobin-based or perfluorocarbon oxygen carriers.  
- **Hemolymph evolution:** Whether open vs. closed circulatory systems represent convergent solutions or deep homology.

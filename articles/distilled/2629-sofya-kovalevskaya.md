# Sofya Kovalevskaya

## Overview
Sofya Kovalevskaya (1850–1891) was a pioneering Russian mathematician who made fundamental contributions to analysis, partial differential equations, and mechanics. She was the first woman to earn a modern doctorate in mathematics and the first woman in modern Europe appointed to a full professorship in mathematics, breaking significant gender barriers in science.

## Timeline
- **15 January 1850** — Born in Moscow, Russia.
- **1868** — Entered a "fictitious marriage" with Vladimir Kovalevsky to study abroad.
- **1869–1870** — Audited classes at the University of Heidelberg.
- **1870–1874** — Studied privately with Karl Weierstrass in Berlin.
- **1874** — Awarded a doctorate *summa cum laude* from the University of Göttingen for three papers.
- **1874–1883** — Lived in Russia; experienced financial difficulties and personal turmoil.
- **1883** — Secured a position as privat-docent at Stockholm University.
- **1884** — Appointed Extraordinary Professor at Stockholm University.
- **1888** — Won the Prix Bordin for her work on rigid body rotation.
- **1889** — Appointed Ordinary (full) Professor at Stockholm University.
- **10 February 1891** — Died in Stockholm, Sweden, of influenza complicated by pneumonia.

## Body

### Early Life and Education
Kovalevskaya was born into a minor noble family with a strong educational background. Her early interest in mathematics was sparked by lecture notes on calculus used as wallpaper in her childhood room. Despite her aptitude, she could not attend university in Russia, where women were barred. In 1868, she entered a "fictitious marriage" with Vladimir Kovalevsky, a palaeontology student, which allowed her to travel to Germany for advanced studies. She audited classes at the University of Heidelberg and then studied privately for three years under the renowned mathematician Karl Weierstrass in Berlin.

### Doctoral Work and Key Mathematical Contributions
In 1874, Kovalevskaya presented three papers to the University of Göttingen, earning her a doctorate *summa cum laude*. Her paper on partial differential equations contains the Cauchy–Kovalevskaya theorem, which proves the existence and analyticity of local solutions to such equations under specific initial conditions. This theorem remains a fundamental result in the field.

### Career in Sweden and Later Work
After returning to Russia and facing limited professional opportunities, Kovalevskaya moved to Sweden in 1883 with the help of mathematician Gösta Mittag-Leffler. She became an editor of the journal *Acta Mathematica* and was appointed a professor at Stockholm University. In 1888, she won the prestigious Prix Bordin for her work on the rotation of a rigid body, discovering the "Kovalevskaya top," a new integrable case in mechanics. In 1889, she became the first woman in modern Europe to hold a full professorship in mathematics.

### Personal Life and Legacy
Kovalevskaya's personal life was complex. Her marriage to Vladimir Kovalevsky was initially fictitious but later became a true partnership; they had a daughter, Sofia. Vladimir's mental health declined, and he died by suicide in 1883. In Sweden, Kovalevskaya formed a close friendship with writer Anne Charlotte Edgren-Leffler. She also wrote non-mathematical works, including a memoir and a novel. She died of pneumonia in 1891 at age 41. Her achievements paved the way for future women in mathematics and science.

## Terms
- **Doctorate (in the modern sense)**: An advanced academic degree awarded for original research, which Kovalevskaya was the first woman to earn in mathematics.
- **Cauchy–Kovalevskaya theorem**: A fundamental result proving the existence and analyticity of local solutions to certain partial differential equations.
- **Privat-docent**: A university teaching position, often for a lecturer not yet a full professor, which Kovalevskaya held in Stockholm.
- **Extraordinary Professor**: A position equivalent to an assistant professor, which Kovalevskaya held before becoming a full professor.
- **Ordinary Professor**: A full professorship, the highest academic rank, which Kovalevskaya achieved in 1889.
- **Prix Bordin**: A prestigious prize awarded by the French Academy of Science, which Kovalevskaya won in 1888.
- **Kovalevskaya top**: A specific case of rigid body motion around a fixed point that is completely integrable, discovered by Kovalevskaya.
- **Fictitious marriage**: A marriage of convenience, which Kovalevskaya used to gain permission to study abroad.

## Debates and Open Questions
The source notes that Kovalevskaya's decision to pursue a fictitious marriage was partly a dramatic choice fashionable among radicals of her generation, rather than a strict necessity. The exact details of her 1870 debate with Herbert Spencer on "woman's capacity for abstract thought" are not recorded, though it may have involved the Euler equations for rigid body motion.

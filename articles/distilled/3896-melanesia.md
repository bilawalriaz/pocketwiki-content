# Melanesia

## Overview
Melanesia is a subregion of Oceania in the southwestern Pacific Ocean, encompassing New Guinea and numerous island chains. It is defined by its extraordinary linguistic diversity, complex ancient migrations, and a modern identity that has evolved from a term of racial classification to one of cultural affirmation and political cooperation.

## Timeline
- **c. 40,000+ years ago** — First human migration into the ancient continent of Sahul (Australia-New Guinea).
- **c. 4000–3000 BC** — Austronesian migrants from Taiwan arrive in Melanesia, settling along coasts.
- **1756** — Charles de Brosses theorizes an "old black race" in the Pacific.
- **1832** — French navigator Jules Dumont d'Urville coins the term "Melanesia."
- **1885–1891** — Robert Henry Codrington publishes key monographs defining Melanesia and introducing the concept of *mana*.
- **2008** — A genetic study challenges the "Polynesian theory" of extensive Melanesian-Polynesian mixing.

## Body

### Etymology and Concept
The name "Melanesia" derives from Ancient Greek for "black islands," referencing the dark skin of its inhabitants. The concept of Melanesia as a distinct region evolved through European exploration. Early explorers noted physical differences among Pacific Islanders, leading to racial theories like Charles de Brosses's 1756 idea of an "old black race." In the 19th century, Jules Dumont d'Urville formally coined the term alongside "Micronesia" and "Polynesia." Scholarly definitions have been fluid; missionary Robert Henry Codrington initially excluded New Guinea, but modern consensus includes it. The term's meaning has shifted from a racial classification to a cultural and geographical category, now embraced by many inhabitants as a source of identity and empowerment, as seen in the Melanesian Spearhead Group trade agreement.

### Ancient History and Genetics
Melanesia's population has a distinctive ancestry from two major migrations. The first, over 40,000 years ago, involved hominids from Africa who settled in Sahul (the combined Australia-New Guinea landmass). A later wave of Austronesian migrants arrived from Taiwan between 4000 and 3000 BC. A late-20th-century "Polynesian theory" suggested extensive mixing between these groups, but a 2008 genetic study found little evidence for it, indicating Polynesians moved quickly through the region. Genetic studies also reveal that Melanesians carry DNA from archaic human species: 3–4% Denisovan and 2% Neanderthal. Their frequent blond hair is due to a unique genetic mutation, independent from the one in European populations.

### Languages and Demographics
Melanesia is the world's most linguistically dense region, with 1,319 languages across a small area—averaging one language per 716 km². Vanuatu alone has 138 languages for a population of 0.3 million. Languages belong to two main groups: the Austronesian family and numerous, unrelated "Papuan" language families (a geographical term). Contact and trade have produced distinct creole languages, most notably Tok Pisin and Hiri Motu in Papua New Guinea, which are now primary languages for many.

### Geography and Political Divisions
Geographically, Melanesia is divided between the large island of New Guinea (part of the Australian continent) and "Island Melanesia," a chain of archipelagos including the Bismarck and Louisiade groups (Papua New Guinea), Vanuatu, New Caledonia (geologically part of Zealandia), and Fiji. The region includes four independent countries: Fiji, Papua New Guinea, Solomon Islands, and Vanuatu. It also encompasses territories like New Caledonia (France) and several Indonesian provinces on New Guinea. Political borders often cut across geographical and cultural zones, such as the island of Bougainville.

## Terms
*   **Melanesia:** A subregion of Oceania; the name means "black islands" from Greek, referring to inhabitants' skin color.
*   **Austronesian:** A large language family originating from Taiwan, whose speakers migrated into Melanesia around 4000–3000 BC.
*   **Papuan languages:** A term for the non-Austronesian languages of Melanesia, comprising many unrelated language families.
*   **Sahul:** The ancient continent that combined Australia and New Guinea during periods of lower sea level.
*   **Mana:** A concept of spiritual power or efficacy in Melanesian cultures, introduced to the West by Robert Henry Codrington.
*   **Creole language:** A stable language that develops from a mixture of different languages, such as Tok Pisin in Papua New Guinea.
*   **Melanesian Spearhead Group:** A regional trade and political alliance between Fiji, Papua New Guinea, Solomon Islands, and Vanuatu.
*   **Denisovan:** An archaic human species whose DNA is found in modern Melanesian populations.

## Debates and Open Questions
The precise geographical and cultural boundaries of Melanesia remain debated. Scholars disagree on whether to include all of New Guinea, and Fiji is sometimes considered a border region or even part of Polynesia. The extent of genetic and cultural interaction between ancient Austronesian migrants and indigenous Papuan peoples is also contested, with recent genetic evidence suggesting less mixing than previously theorized.

# Hürrem Sultan

## Overview
Hürrem Sultan (c. 1505–1558), also known as Roxelana, was the chief consort and legal wife of Ottoman Sultan Suleiman the Magnificent. She was the first *haseki sultan* (a title for the chief consort) and the mother of his successor, Selim II. Her unprecedented rise from a slave in the imperial harem to the sultan's legal wife and a powerful political figure marked the beginning of the "Sultanate of Women," a period of significant female influence in the Ottoman court.

## Timeline
- **c. 1505** — Born in Ruthenia (likely modern Ukraine) to an Orthodox family.
- **1512–1520** — Captured by Crimean Tatars and taken to Constantinople.
- **c. 1520** — Enters the imperial harem and becomes Suleiman's favorite concubine.
- **1521** — Gives birth to her first son, Şehzade Mehmed.
- **c. 1534** — Suleiman legally marries Hürrem in an unprecedented ceremony.
- **1534** — After the death of Suleiman's mother, Hafsa Sultan, Hürrem's influence increases.
- **1538–1541** — Her first major public work, the Haseki Sultan Complex, is built in Constantinople.
- **1548** — Writes a letter to King Sigismund II Augustus of Poland, a rare diplomatic act for a woman.
- **1553** — Her rival, Şehzade Mustafa, is executed by Suleiman.
- **15 April 1558** — Dies in Constantinople and is buried near the Süleymaniye Mosque.

## Body

### Origin and Rise in the Harem
Presumably born in Ruthenia, Hürrem was captured by Crimean Tatars and sold into slavery in Constantinople. She entered the imperial harem and, around 1520, became the favorite concubine of Sultan Suleiman. She broke Ottoman tradition by bearing him multiple sons, violating the "one concubine, one son" custom. Suleiman's deep devotion led him to free and legally marry her around 1534, making her the first former slave to become the sultan's legal wife and granting her the title *Haseki Sultan*.

### Political Influence and State Affairs
Hürrem became Suleiman's most trusted confidant and adviser, especially during his military campaigns. She corresponded with him, providing political intelligence and warnings. She wielded significant power, influencing appointments of officials like Grand Vizier Rüstem Pasha (her son-in-law) and engaging in foreign diplomacy, notably with Poland. Her influence was so great that rumors claimed she had bewitched the sultan. She was a controversial figure, often blamed for political intrigues, including the execution of Grand Vizier Pargalı Ibrahim Pasha (1536) and Suleiman's son Mustafa (1553), though these claims are based on speculative sources.

### Patronage and Legacy
Hürrem was a major patron of public works, funding complexes across the empire. Her most famous project was the Haseki Sultan Complex in Constantinople (built 1538–1551), which included a mosque, schools, a hospital for women (the first of its kind), and a soup kitchen. She also built the Hürrem Sultan Hamam (bathhouse) near Hagia Sophia and a soup kitchen in Jerusalem. She died in 1558 and was buried in an elegant mausoleum adjacent to Suleiman's within the Süleymaniye Mosque complex.

## Terms
*   **Haseki Sultan:** A title created for Hürrem, designating the chief consort of the sultan and elevating her status above other imperial women.
*   **Sultanate of Women:** A period (c. 1533–1656) in the Ottoman Empire when powerful women of the imperial harem, like Hürrem, exerted significant political influence.
*   **Kardeş katliamı:** The Ottoman practice of fratricide, where a new sultan would execute his brothers to prevent succession struggles.
*   **Waqf:** An Islamic endowment, typically of property, to be held in trust and used for charitable or religious purposes.
*   **Mimar Sinan:** The chief Ottoman architect who designed many of Hürrem's commissioned buildings, including the Haseki Sultan Complex.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Origin:** Her exact birth name and place are unknown. European sources called her "Roxelana" (from Ruthenia), while later Ukrainian and Polish traditions name her Anastasia or Aleksandra Lisowska, but these names appear in literature centuries after her death.
*   **Role in Political Intrigues:** Hürrem is traditionally blamed for the executions of Ibrahim Pasha and Şehzade Mustafa. However, historians note these accounts are based on hearsay and later chronicles, not first-hand sources from within the secluded harem.
*   **Date of Marriage:** The exact year of her legal marriage to Suleiman is debated, with scholarly estimates ranging from before 1534 to around 1534.

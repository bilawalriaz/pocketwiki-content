# Mount Pelée

## Overview
Mount Pelée is an active stratovolcano (a conical volcano built from layers of hardened lava and ash) on the Caribbean island of Martinique. It is globally significant for its catastrophic 1902 eruption, which destroyed the city of Saint-Pierre and killed approximately 30,000 people, making it the deadliest volcanic disaster of the 20th century. The volcano remains active and under continuous monitoring.

## Timeline
- **c. 100,000 years ago** — Intermediate phase begins, forming the Morne Macouba lava dome and later caldera.
- **c. 25,000 years ago** — Large southwest sector collapse (landslide) occurs.
- **c. 3,000 years ago** — Étang Sec caldera forms after a large pumice eruption.
- **1792, 1851** — Minor phreatic (steam) eruptions occur.
- **8 May 1902** — Main eruption destroys Saint-Pierre, killing 29,000–30,000 people.
- **4 July 1905** — Eruption ends; volcano enters a dormant period.
- **16 September 1929** — New eruption begins, leading to evacuation of danger area.
- **Late 1932** — Eruption ends.
- **6 December 2020** — Alert level raised to Yellow (Restless) due to increased seismicity.
- **16 September 2023** — Volcanoes and forests of Mount Pelée listed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

## Body
### Geological Setting and History
Mount Pelée is part of the Lesser Antilles Volcanic Arc, formed by the subduction of the Caribbean Plate beneath the Atlantic oceanic crust. Its evolution is divided into three phases: the initial "Paléo-Pelée" stratovolcano, an intermediate phase starting ~100,000 years ago that created a lava dome and caldera, and the modern phase which built the current cone. The 1902 eruption occurred within the Étang Sec caldera, formed ~3,000 years ago.

### The 1902 Eruption and Aftermath
The catastrophic eruption on 8 May 1902 produced pyroclastic flows (fast-moving currents of hot gas and volcanic matter) that obliterated Saint-Pierre in minutes. Only three known survivors were recorded: Ludger Sylbaris (in a jail cell), Léon Compère-Léandre (on the city's edge), and Havivra Da Ifrile (in a sea cave). The city was never fully rebuilt.

### Subsequent Activity and Current Status
After dormancy until 1929, a new eruption began, prompting immediate evacuation. This activity, which ended in 1932, was less violent but formed a new lava dome. The volcano is currently active, with ongoing seismic monitoring. In 2020, its alert level was raised to Yellow due to increased shallow and deep seismicity, indicating a change in volcanic system behavior. As of May 2025, hundreds of earthquakes were recorded, interpreted as magma movement at depth, though not signaling an imminent eruption.

### Ecology and Protection
The volcano hosts endemic species like the Martinique volcano frog (*Allobates chalcopis*) and is an Important Bird Area for numerous bird species. In 2023, its volcanic landscapes were inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site, recognized as the 7th natural site in France to receive this status.

## Terms
- **Stratovolcano:** A tall, conical volcano composed of alternating layers of hardened lava, ash, and other volcanic debris.
- **Subduction zone:** A geological area where one tectonic plate sinks beneath another, often creating volcanic arcs.
- **Pyroclastic flow:** A fast-moving, deadly current of hot gas and volcanic matter that flows down the side of a volcano.
- **Caldera:** A large, basin-shaped depression formed by the collapse of a volcano following a major eruption.
- **Lava dome:** A steep-sided mound formed by the slow extrusion of viscous lava from a volcano.
- **Phreatic eruption:** A steam-driven explosion that occurs when groundwater is heated by magma.
- **Fumarole:** A vent in the Earth's surface that emits steam and volcanic gases.
- **Seismicity:** The frequency and magnitude of earthquakes in a given area.

# Nice

## Overview
Nice is a major city on the French Riviera, serving as the prefecture of the Alpes-Maritimes department. Its strategic location on the Mediterranean coast, at the foot of the Alps, has made it a historically contested territory and a premier tourist destination. The city's identity is shaped by its complex history of changing sovereignty, its role as a winter resort for European aristocracy, and its vibrant cultural scene.

## Timeline
- **c. 350 BC** — Founded by Greek colonists from Phocaea as Nikaia.
- **1388** — Places itself under the protection of the Counts of Savoy, becoming the County of Nice.
- **1792** — Annexed by the French First Republic.
- **1815** — Returned to the Kingdom of Piedmont-Sardinia after the Napoleonic Wars.
- **1860** — Ceded to France via the Treaty of Turin, confirmed by a controversial plebiscite.
- **1931** — The Promenade des Anglais is inaugurated after refurbishment.
- **1944** — Liberated by American paratroopers after German occupation.
- **2001** — European leaders sign the Treaty of Nice.
- **2016** — A terrorist attack on the Promenade des Anglais kills 87 people.
- **2021** — Proclaimed a UNESCO World Heritage Site as "Nice, Winter Resort Town of the Riviera."

## Body

### Ancient and Medieval History
The Nice area shows evidence of early human settlement, including the use of fire at the Terra Amata site around 380,000 years ago. The city was founded around 350 BC by Greeks from Phocaea and named Nikaia. It became a busy trading port but was rivalled by the Roman town of Cemenelum. In the Middle Ages, Nice participated in Italian wars and conflicts between Pisa and Genoa. In 1388, it entered the dominion of the Counts of Savoy, which began a long period of Savoyard rule.

### Under Savoy and the Kingdom of Sardinia
As part of the Duchy of Savoy, Nice's maritime strength grew, and Italian became its official administrative language in 1561. The city suffered during wars between Francis I and Charles V and was attacked by Franco-Ottoman forces in 1543. After the Treaty of Utrecht (1713), it became part of the Kingdom of Sicily, then the Kingdom of Sardinia. The "new town" was built during a period of peace, though French and Spanish forces again occupied it from 1744 to 1748.

### Annexation by France and the "Niçard Exodus"
The County of Nice was annexed by the French First Republic in 1792 and remained part of France until 1815, when it reverted to Piedmont-Sardinia. In 1860, the Treaty of Turin ceded Nice to France as a reward for French assistance in the Second Italian War of Independence. A plebiscite approved the change, but with high abstention and allegations of irregularities. This led to the "Niçard exodus," where about a quarter of the population (around 11,000 people) emigrated to Italy. The French government implemented a policy of Francization, abolishing the Italian language and changing surnames. Pro-Italian sentiment persisted, culminating in the "Niçard Vespers" revolt of 1871, which was suppressed.

### 20th Century to Present
In the 20th century, Nice became a refuge for displaced people during World War II, including Jews fleeing Nazi persecution. The city was liberated in 1944. Post-war economic growth was driven by tourism and construction under the long-serving mayors Jean and Jacques Médecin. The latter was later convicted of corruption. The city hosted the signing of the Treaty of Nice in 2001. It suffered major terrorist attacks in 2016 and 2020. In 2021, its historical importance as a winter resort town was recognized by UNESCO.

## Terms
- **Prefecture**: The administrative capital of a French department.
- **Plebiscite**: A vote in which an entire population is asked to accept or reject a proposal, often used to legitimize a political change.
- **Francization**: The policy of imposing the French language and culture on a region.
- **Niçard exodus**: The voluntary emigration of about 11,000 Italian-speaking Niçois to Italy after the 1860 annexation.
- **Irredentism**: A political movement advocating for the return of a territory to its "mother country."
- **Pieds-Noirs**: French citizens who repatriated from Algeria after its independence in 1962.
- **Mediterranean climate (Köppen: Csa)**: A climate characterized by hot, dry summers and mild, wet winters.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The legitimacy of the 1860 plebiscite that approved annexation to France is debated, with evidence of irregularities and information control by French and Piedmontese governments.
- The classification of the local Niçard language is contested; while generally considered an Occitan dialect, some Italian scholars argue it is a Ligurian dialect.
- The extent of the cultural and demographic impact of the 1860 annexation and subsequent Francization policies on Nice's identity remains a subject of historical discussion.

# Cesare Borgia

## Overview
Cesare Borgia (1475–1507) was an Italian cardinal, condottiero (mercenary captain), and nobleman of the Spanish House of Borgia. As the illegitimate son of Pope Alexander VI, he leveraged his father's power to carve out a state in Central Italy, becoming a key figure in the Italian Wars. His ruthless political maneuvering and dramatic fall from power made him a primary inspiration for Niccolò Machiavelli's political treatise, *The Prince*.

## Timeline
- **13 September 1475** — Born in Subiaco, Papal States, the illegitimate son of Cardinal Rodrigo Borgia (later Pope Alexander VI).
- **1492** — Made Cardinal at age 18 upon his father's election as Pope Alexander VI.
- **1497** — His brother Giovanni is murdered; Cesare is a suspect.
- **17 August 1498** — Resigns from the cardinalate to pursue a military career; created Duke of Valentinois by Louis XII of France.
- **1499** — Accompanies Louis XII's invasion of Italy into Milan.
- **1500** — Created Duke of Romagna after conquering Imola, Forlì, Pesaro, Rimini, and Faenza.
- **1501** — Commands French troops in the sieges of Naples and Capua.
- **31 December 1502** — Entraps and executes his rebellious condottieri at Senigallia.
- **1503** — Conquers the Republic of San Marino.
- **August 1503** — His father, Pope Alexander VI, dies, fatally undermining Cesare's power base.
- **1504** — Captured by Spanish forces in Naples and imprisoned.
- **12 March 1507** — Killed in an ambush while besieging Viana, Navarre.

## Body

### Early Life and Church Career
Cesare Borgia was the son of Cardinal Rodrigo Borgia and his mistress Vannozza dei Cattanei. Groomed for the Church, he studied law and was appointed Bishop of Pamplona at 15 and Archbishop of Valencia at 17. In 1492, he was made Cardinal at 18. The assassination of his brother Giovanni in 1497, for which Cesare was suspected, provided the opportunity to leave the clergy. He resigned his cardinalate in 1498 to pursue a secular and military career, a move facilitated by his father and an alliance with France.

### Military Conquests and the State of Romagna
Cesare's military career was founded on his father's patronage and an alliance with King Louis XII of France, reinforced by his marriage to Charlotte d'Albret. He served as a condottiero for France during the Italian Wars, participating in the conquest of Milan. His primary goal was to establish a personal state in Central Italy. With papal backing and hired mercenaries, he conquered the Romagna, taking cities like Imola, Forlì, and Faenza. He was created Duke of Romagna in 1501 and expanded his domain to include Piombino and parts of the Marche. His rule was marked by both effective administration and brutal tactics, most notably the 1502 massacre of his own rebellious captains at Senigallia.

### Fall from Power and Death
Cesare's power was entirely dependent on his father's papacy. Upon Alexander VI's death in 1503, Cesare's position collapsed. Though briefly supported by the short-lived Pope Pius III, he was outmaneuvered by his enemy, the new Pope Julius II. Forced to surrender his lands and titles, Cesare was captured by Spanish forces in 1504 and imprisoned in Spain. He escaped in 1506 and sought refuge with his brother-in-law, King John III of Navarre. While fighting for Navarre, he was killed in an ambush at Viana in 1507.

### Legacy and Machiavelli
Cesare Borgia's career was famously analyzed by Niccolò Machiavelli, who met him during a diplomatic mission in 1502-1503. In *The Prince*, Machiavelli uses Cesare as a case study in acquiring power through another's might (his father's papacy) and the dangers of failing to secure an independent power base. He admired Cesare's skill in pacifying the Romagna and his decisive, if ruthless, handling of the Senigallia conspiracy. Cesare's story illustrates the precariousness of power built on patronage rather than personal strength or popular support.

## Terms
- **Condottiero**: A professional military commander who led mercenary companies, often hired by Italian city-states or foreign powers during the Renaissance.
- **Cardinal**: A senior official of the Catholic Church, ranking just below the Pope. Cesare resigned this position to become a secular ruler.
- **Papal Gonfalonier**: A military title meaning "standard-bearer," granting command of the papal armies.
- **Italian Wars**: A series of conflicts (1494–1559) fought primarily in the Italian peninsula, involving major European powers like France and Spain.
- **Romagna**: A historical region in northeastern Italy, which Cesare conquered and ruled as Duke.
- **Senigallia conspiracy**: A 1502 plot by Cesare's own mercenary captains against him, which he crushed by luring them to Senigallia and executing them.
- **Laicisation**: The process of a cleric returning to the status of a layperson, as Cesare did to leave the Church.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **The murder of Giovanni Borgia**: Cesare has long been suspected of orchestrating his brother's death in 1497 to secure his own path to a secular career, but definitive proof is lacking.
- **Machiavelli's interpretation**: Scholars debate whether Machiavelli's portrayal of Cesare in *The Prince* is a straightforward political analysis or a more nuanced critique of amoral statecraft. Some see Cesare as a precursor to modern state crimes, while others view his actions as typical of the era's corruption.
- **The fate of his remains**: Cesare's tomb was destroyed and his bones desecrated after his death. Ongoing petitions to return his remains to the Church of Santa María in Viana have been rejected, as he had renounced his cardinalate.

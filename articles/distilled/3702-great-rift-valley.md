# Great Rift Valley

## Overview
The Great Rift Valley (GRV) is a massive, contiguous series of geographic depressions stretching approximately 6,500 km from northern Syria to Mozambique. It is a globally significant geological and ecological feature, forming a key landscape for biodiversity, human evolution, and ongoing tectonic activity. The term is widely used in geography and ecology, though geologists prefer "Afro-Arabian Rift System" to describe the underlying structures.

## Timeline
- **1896** — British geologist John Walter Gregory introduces the term "Great Rift Valley."
- **c. 3000 BC** — Lake Lisan, a precursor to the Dead Sea, existed during the Pleistocene.
- **Ongoing** — The African Plate is splitting into the Nubian and Somali plates along the East African Rift System.

## Body

### Geology and Formation
The GRV is the surface expression of the Afro-Arabian Rift System (AAR), a series of major geological structures. The northern section is the Dead Sea Transform (DST), a left-lateral strike-slip boundary between the Arabian and African (Nubian) plates. This transform creates pull-apart basins like the Dead Sea and Sea of Galilee. South of the DST, the Red Sea Rift is a mid-ocean ridge separating the plates. It connects to the East African Rift System (EARS) at the Afar triple junction. The EARS splits into two main branches around the Victoria microplate: the eastern Gregory Rift and the western Albertine Rift. The eastern branch is characterized by volcanism, while the western branch features deep, freshwater lakes.

### Geographic Features: Asia to the Red Sea
The Asian section begins with the Ghab Plain in Syria, followed by the Beqaa Valley. The Jordan Rift Valley continues south, containing the Hula Valley, the Sea of Galilee, and the Dead Sea depression—the lowest point on Earth's land surface. The Arabah valley leads to the Gulf of Aqaba, which connects to the Red Sea. The Red Sea itself is a deep central valley flanked by shallow shelves, linking the Asian and African sections.

### Geographic Features: Africa
South of the Red Sea, the GRV begins with the Danakil Depression in the Afar Triangle, an area of thinned continental crust. The Main Ethiopian Rift (MER) follows, bordered by high plateaus. South of the MER, the rift splits. The eastern Gregory Rift runs through Kenya and Tanzania, featuring saline lakes, volcanoes, and major peaks like Mount Kenya and Kilimanjaro. The western Albertine Rift contains a chain of deep, freshwater lakes, including Lake Tanganyika (the world's second-deepest), bordered by high mountain ranges. The rift continues south through Lake Malawi (the world's third-deepest freshwater lake) and extends into Mozambique's Urema Valley.

### Ecology and Other Disciplines
The Great Rift Valley/Red Sea flyway is the world's second-most important route for migratory soaring birds, including species like the White Stork and Great White Pelican. The term "Great Rift Valley" is also used in fields like paleontology, archaeology, and epidemiology (e.g., Rift Valley fever) to refer to the relevant local section of the system.

## Terms
*   **Afro-Arabian Rift System (AAR):** The series of geological structures (faults, basins) that create the topographic feature known as the Great Rift Valley.
*   **Dead Sea Transform (DST):** A left-lateral strike-slip fault forming the northern boundary of the Arabian Plate.
*   **Pull-apart basin:** A depression formed by extensional forces along a strike-slip fault, such as the Dead Sea.
*   **East African Rift System (EARS):** The active continental rift zone in eastern Africa, part of the larger AAR.
*   **Triple junction:** A point where three tectonic plates meet; the Afar triple junction is where the Red Sea Rift, Gulf of Aden Rift, and EARS converge.
*   **Half-graben:** A valley formed by the displacement of rock along a single fault, common in the western Albertine Rift.
*   **Endorheic:** A closed drainage basin that retains water and allows no outflow to other external bodies of water (e.g., Lake Rukwa).
*   **Flyway:** A generally used route followed by birds during migration (e.g., the Asian–East African Flyway).

# Usain Bolt

## Overview
Usain Bolt is a Jamaican retired sprinter widely regarded as the greatest of all time. He is the only sprinter to win Olympic 100 m and 200 m titles at three consecutive Games (2008, 2012, 2016) and holds the world records in the 100 m (9.58 s), 200 m (19.19 s), and 4 × 100 m relay (36.84 s). His dominance, charismatic personality, and record-breaking performances transformed the global profile of track and field.

## Timeline
- **21 Aug 1986** — Born in Sherwood Content, Jamaica.
- **2002** — Wins 200 m gold at World Junior Championships in Kingston, becoming youngest world-junior gold medallist.
- **2004** — Turns professional; sets world junior 200 m record (19.93 s).
- **2008** — Sets 100 m world record (9.72 s) in New York. Wins 100 m (9.69 s), 200 m (19.30 s), and 4 × 100 m relay gold at Beijing Olympics, breaking world records in all.
- **2009** — Sets current 100 m world record (9.58 s) and 200 m world record (19.19 s) at Berlin World Championships.
- **2011** — False-starts and is disqualified from 100 m final at Daegu World Championships; wins 200 m and 4 × 100 m relay gold.
- **2012** — Wins 100 m (9.63 s, Olympic record), 200 m (19.32 s), and 4 × 100 m relay (36.84 s, world record) at London Olympics.
- **2015** — Wins 100 m, 200 m, and 4 × 100 m relay gold at Beijing World Championships.
- **2016** — Wins 100 m, 200 m, and 4 × 100 m relay gold at Rio Olympics, completing the "triple-triple."
- **2017** — Finishes third in 100 m final at London World Championships; pulls up injured in 4 × 100 m relay final, ending his career.
- **Jan 2023** — Reports over $12 million missing from his retirement savings due to alleged fraud. |

## Body

### Early Life and Junior Career
Born to parents Wellesley and Jennifer Bolt, Usain showed early speed at Waldensia Primary and William Knibb Memorial High School. Coached by former Olympian Pablo McNeil, he won silver in the 200 m at the 2001 CARIFTA Games and set records at the 2002 World Junior Championships in Kingston. His talent was recognized by Prime Minister P. J. Patterson, who facilitated his move to Kingston for better training. Despite his potential, Bolt was initially unfocused, preferring fast food and partying.

### Rise to Professionalism and Early Setbacks
Bolt turned professional in 2004 under coach Fitz Coleman, setting a world junior 200 m record of 19.93 s. However, a hamstring injury hampered his 2004 Olympics, and he was eliminated in the first round. In 2005, he began working with coach Glen Mills, who aimed to improve his professionalism. Injuries and a car accident disrupted his progress, but he showed improvement, winning his first major world medal (bronze) at the 2006 IAAF World Athletics Final. In 2007, he broke the Jamaican 200 m record (19.75 s) and won silver at the Osaka World Championships.

### 2008 Beijing Olympics: Global Stardom
Bolt's career exploded in 2008. He set a 100 m world record of 9.72 s in May. At the Beijing Olympics, he won the 100 m final in 9.69 s, slowing to celebrate before the finish. He then won the 200 m in 19.30 s, breaking Michael Johnson's 12-year-old record. He also anchored Jamaica to 4 × 100 m relay gold in a world record of 37.10 s. This performance made him the first person to hold both 100 m and 200 m world records since the advent of electronic timing.

### World Record Dominance (2009–2016)
At the 2009 Berlin World Championships, Bolt set his enduring world records: 9.58 s in the 100 m (the largest improvement in the event's history) and 19.19 s in the 200 m. He continued his dominance, winning consecutive World Championship 100 m, 200 m, and 4 × 100 m relay titles from 2009 to 2015 (except for the 2011 100 m false start). He successfully defended his Olympic titles at London 2012 (setting an Olympic 100 m record of 9.63 s) and Rio 2016, becoming the first athlete to win the 100 m and 200 m at three Olympics.

### Retirement and Later Life
Bolt retired after the 2017 World Championships, where he won 100 m bronze and suffered a hamstring injury in his final relay race. His 2008 relay gold was later stripped after teammate Nesta Carter tested positive for a banned substance. Post-retirement, Bolt has been involved in various ventures, including co-founding the electric scooter company Bolt Mobility (which ceased operations in 2022) and producing dancehall music. In 2023, he was the victim of a fraud that cost him over $12 million in retirement savings.

## Terms
*   **Sprinter:** An athlete who competes in short-distance running races, typically up to 400 metres.
*   **World Record (WR):** The best performance in an event ever ratified by World Athletics.
*   **Olympic Record (OR):** The best performance in an event at the Olympic Games.
*   **False Start:** When an athlete leaves the starting blocks before the starting signal, resulting in disqualification under current IAAF rules.
*   **Diamond League:** The top-tier annual series of track and field meetings.
*   **4 × 100 m relay:** A team event where four runners each complete one lap of the track, passing a baton.
*   **Sub-10/9.6:** Running the 100 m in under 10 or 9.6 seconds, a benchmark of elite speed.
*   **IAAF/World Athletics:** The international governing body for the sport of athletics (track and field).

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **The 2008 Relay Disqualification:** Bolt's "triple-triple" was compromised when the 2008 relay gold was stripped due to Nesta Carter's doping violation, raising questions about team legacy.
*   **Impact of Scoliosis:** Bolt has scoliosis, causing a ½-inch leg length discrepancy. Biomechanics researchers have studied whether this asymmetry aided or hindered his sprinting, with no firm conclusions.
*   **Performance in the Context of Doping:** Bolt's career occurred amid high-profile drug scandals in sprinting. While he vehemently denied doping and passed all tests, his dominance inevitably invites scrutiny and debate within the sport's history.

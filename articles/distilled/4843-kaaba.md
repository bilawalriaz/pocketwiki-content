# Kaaba

## Overview
The Kaaba is a cuboid stone building at the center of Islam's holiest site, the Masjid al-Haram in Mecca, Saudi Arabia. It is considered the "House of God" and serves as the qibla, or direction of prayer, for Muslims worldwide. Its significance is central to Islamic pilgrimage rites, particularly the Hajj and Umrah, and its history reflects the religious and political evolution of the Arabian Peninsula.

## Timeline
- **c. 2100 BCE (Islamic tradition)** — Rebuilt by the prophets Ibrahim (Abraham) and Ismail (Ishmael).
- **Pre-6th century CE** — A sanctuary for polytheistic Arabian tribes, housing 360 idols.
- **c. 600 CE** — Rebuilt by the Quraysh tribe; Muhammad participated and settled a dispute over the Black Stone.
- **624 CE** — The qibla (prayer direction) for Muslims was changed from Jerusalem to the Kaaba.
- **630 CE** — Conquered by Muhammad, who removed all idols from the Kaaba.
- **683 CE** — Severely damaged by fire during the first siege of Mecca.
- **692 CE** — Bombarded during the second siege of Mecca.
- **693 CE** — Rebuilt by the Umayyads, returning to its pre-Islamic cubic shape.
- **930 CE** — Sacked by the Qarmatians, who stole the Black Stone.
- **1626 CE** — Walls collapsed due to flooding.
- **1627 CE** — Rebuilt with granite stones during the Ottoman era.
- **1996 CE** — Most recent major restoration completed.

## Body

### Etymology and Pre-Islamic Usage
The name "Kaaba" likely derives from the Arabic word for "cube." In pre-Islamic Arabia, cubic stone structures and aniconic carvings were common abodes for deities. The Black Stone of the Kaaba has been compared to pre-Islamic sacred stones called baetyls. The site was a major pilgrimage center, and a sanctuary zone around it prohibited violence, allowing Mecca to thrive as a trading hub.

### History and Construction
Islamic tradition holds the Kaaba was originally built by Ibrahim and Ismail as the first house of worship for monotheism. It was rebuilt multiple times. The Quraysh reconstructed it in the early 7th century, during which Muhammad mediated a dispute over placing the Black Stone. After Muhammad's conquest of Mecca in 630 CE, he cleansed the Kaaba of idols. The structure has since been damaged and rebuilt several times, notably after fires in 683 CE and floods in 1626 CE. Its basic cubic form has remained unchanged since its reconstruction in 693 CE.

### Architecture and Interior
The Kaaba is approximately 15 meters high, with sides measuring 12 m by 10.5 m. Its entrance is a gold door set 2.13 m above ground. The interior features marble-clad walls, three pillars, and inscribed tablets. Key external features include the Black Stone (Ḥajar al-Aswad) at the eastern corner, the semi-circular Hatīm wall, and the gold rain spout (Mīzab ar-Raḥmah). The Kiswah, a black silk and gold embroidered covering, is replaced annually.

### Significance and Rituals
The Kaaba is the holiest site in Islam. The Tawaf, a compulsory rite of pilgrimage, involves circling it seven times counterclockwise. It is the focal point for the qibla, the direction Muslims face during prayer. The building is cleaned biannually in a ceremony involving Zamzam water and perfumes.

## Terms
- **Qibla**: The fixed direction toward the Kaaba that Muslims face during prayer.
- **Tawaf**: The ritual of circumambulating the Kaaba seven times counterclockwise, a compulsory rite of Hajj and Umrah.
- **Kiswah**: The black silk and gold-embroidered cloth covering the Kaaba, replaced annually.
- **Hajj**: The annual Islamic pilgrimage to Mecca, obligatory for able-bodied Muslims at least once in their lifetime.
- **Umrah**: A non-obligatory pilgrimage to Mecca that can be performed at any time of the year.
- **Mataaf**: The area around the Kaaba where pilgrims perform Tawaf.
- **Black Stone (Ḥajar al-Aswad)**: A stone set in the eastern corner of the Kaaba, the starting point for Tawaf.
- **Hatīm (Hijr Ismail)**: A low, semi-circular wall adjacent to the Kaaba, considered part of its original foundation.
- **Maqam Ibrahim**: A stone near the Kaaba bearing what is believed to be the footprint of Ibrahim (Abraham).

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Pre-Islamic Prominence**: Scholars debate whether the Kaaba was a major pan-Arabian pilgrimage site before Islam or primarily a local sanctuary. Peter Webb argues it played a local role, while others cite its importance.
- **Qibla Direction**: The significance of early Islamic mosques facing different directions is a subject of debate between revisionist historians and conservatives.
- **Historical Identification**: The identification of the ancient town "Macoraba," mentioned by Ptolemy, with Mecca is disputed. Patricia Crone argued it was a different town in southern Arabia.
- **Origin of Tawaf**: The ritual of circumambulation is believed to have pre-Islamic origins, possibly adopted from Najranite pagan practices.

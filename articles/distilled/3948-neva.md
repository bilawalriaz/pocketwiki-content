# Neva

## Overview
The Neva is a short but voluminous river in northwestern Russia, flowing from Lake Ladoga to the Gulf of Finland. Despite its modest length of 74 km (46 mi), it is Europe's fourth-largest river by discharge and has been a vital trade route and strategic site for centuries. It flows through Saint Petersburg, the city founded at its mouth, and its history is intertwined with major events from medieval battles to the Siege of Leningrad.

## Timeline
- **c. 1410–1250 BC** — The Neva river begins its flow after a glacial lake overspills.
- **1240** — Battle of the Neva; Prince Alexander Yaroslavich defeats the Swedes and gains the name "Nevsky."
- **1617** — Treaty of Stolbovo makes the Neva region part of Swedish Ingria.
- **1703** — Founding of Saint Petersburg at the Neva's mouth by Peter the Great.
- **1712** — Saint Petersburg becomes the capital of the Russian Empire.
- **1850** — Blagoveshchensky Bridge, the first permanent bridge across the Neva, opens.
- **1924** — A catastrophic flood reaches 369 cm (145 in) on the city gauge.
- **1941–1944** — Siege of Leningrad; German forces capture the Neva's source, cutting off the city.
- **1963** — A Soviet Tu-124 airliner makes an emergency water landing on the Neva.
- **2005** — South-West Wastewater Treatment Plant is constructed to improve river pollution.

## Body

### Formation and Geography
The Neva is geologically young, formed when a glacial lake overspilled between 1410 and 1250 BC, breaking into the valley of the Tosna river. Its delta is a "pseudodelta" formed by scouring, not sediment accumulation. The river is 74 km long with a very uniform flow from Lake Ladoga, resulting in few natural floods. It freezes from early December to early April. The basin, including Lake Ladoga and Onega, covers 281,000 km² and contains over 48,300 rivers, but only 26 flow directly into the Neva.

### Ecology and Pollution
The Neva's rapid flow, cold water, and lack of aquatic vegetation support limited fish diversity, with permanent residents like perch and transitory species of commercial value like smelt and salmon. The river is classified as "heavily polluted" by Russian authorities, with annual pollutant influx of 80,000 tonnes from hundreds of factories and regular oil spills. Wastewater treatment in Saint Petersburg has improved, rising from 74% purification in 1997 to 91.7% by 2008.

### Historical Significance
The Neva provided a key waterway between Scandinavia and Byzantium from the 8th to 13th centuries. The 1240 Battle of the Neva was a major victory for Novgorod against Sweden. After the Great Northern War (1700–21), the river became part of the Russian Empire. Peter the Great founded Saint Petersburg at its mouth in 1703, making the Neva central to the city's design, with canals, embankments, and bridges. During World War II, the German capture of the Neva's source in 1941 was a critical step in the Siege of Leningrad.

### Commercial and Modern Use
The Neva is navigable and part of the Volga–Baltic Waterway and White Sea–Baltic Canal, though its capacity is limited by width, depth, and bridges. It transports timber, minerals, coal, and oil. An oil pipeline runs under the river west of Shlisselburg, and an underwater tunnel hosts the Nord Stream 1 gas pipeline. The Neva is the primary source (96%) of drinking water for Saint Petersburg.

## Terms
- **Discharge**: The volume of water flowing through a river channel per unit of time.
- **Pseudodelta**: A delta-like formation created by the scouring of existing sediments rather than the accumulation of new river-borne material.
- **Endorheic lake**: A lake with no outflow to the sea or other rivers.
- **Glacial rebound**: The rise of land masses after the retreat of heavy ice sheets from the last Ice Age.
- **Littorina Sea**: A stage in the development of the Baltic Sea, with a higher water level than the present.
- **Slash and burn agriculture**: A farming method where vegetation is cut and burned to create fertile land.
- **Ingria**: A historical region around the Neva River, which was under Swedish control from 1617 to 1703.
- **Waterway**: A navigable river, canal, or other body of water used for transportation.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The exact date of the Neva's formation is debated, with early sources suggesting c. 2000 BC and more recent research pointing to 1410–1250 BC.
- The etymology of "Neva" is uncertain; it may derive from Finnic languages meaning "poor fen" or "watercourse," or from an Indo-European adjective meaning "new."

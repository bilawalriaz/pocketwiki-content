# Trần dynasty

## Overview
The Trần dynasty ruled Đại Việt (Vietnam) from 1225 to 1400, a period marked by successful resistance against Mongol invasions, significant cultural and linguistic development, and eventual decline due to internal strife and Champa attacks. It is considered a golden age for Vietnamese arts, literature, and the solidification of a distinct national identity through the use of the Vietnamese language (chữ Nôm) alongside Literary Chinese.

## Timeline
- **1225** — Trần Thủ Độ orchestrates the overthrow of the Lý dynasty; Trần Cảnh (Trần Thái Tông) becomes emperor.
- **1258** — First Mongol invasion ends with Đại Việt submitting to a tributary relationship with the Mongol court.
- **1285** — Second Yuan (Mongol) invasion is repelled, culminating in a decisive victory at the Battle of Chương Dương.
- **1288** — Third Yuan invasion ends with a decisive naval victory for the Trần at the Battle of Bạch Đằng River.
- **1306** — Champa cedes the provinces of Ô and Lý (renamed Thuận and Hóa) to Đại Việt in a marriage treaty.
- **1376** — Emperor Trần Duệ Tông is killed in a disastrous campaign against Champa at the Battle of Đồ Bàn.
- **1390** — General Trần Khát Chân defeats Champa, killing King Chế Bồng Nga and stabilizing the south.
- **1396** — Hồ Quý Ly introduces paper money, replacing copper coins for the first time in Vietnam.
- **1398** — Emperor Trần Thuận Tông is forced to abdicate in favor of his three-year-old son, Trần Thiếu Đế.
- **1400** — Hồ Quý Ly overthrows the Trần dynasty, establishing the Hồ dynasty.

## Body

### Foundation and Early Rule
The Trần clan, originally migrants from Fujian, rose to power through intermarriage with the ruling Lý dynasty. In 1225, the powerful official Trần Thủ Độ orchestrated the overthrow of the child empress Lý Chiêu Hoàng, marrying her to his nephew Trần Cảnh and establishing the Trần dynasty. The early period was focused on consolidating power, which involved eliminating Lý family members and suppressing rebellions. A major succession crisis occurred in 1237 when Trần Thủ Độ forced the emperor's brother to give up his pregnant wife to ensure an heir, leading to a brief revolt.

### Resistance to Mongol Invasions
The dynasty faced three major invasions from the Mongol-led Yuan dynasty (1258, 1285, 1288). After initial defeats and a tributary agreement, Đại Việt successfully repelled the larger and better-equipped Yuan forces through guerrilla tactics, strategic retreats, and decisive battles. Key victories at Chương Dương (1285) and Bạch Đằng (1288) preserved Đại Việt's independence. The wars fostered a strong sense of patriotism, reflected in literature, and were aided by refugees from the fallen Song dynasty.

### Expansion, Culture, and Decline
Following the Mongol wars, the Trần expanded southward, annexing territories from Champa. The dynasty experienced a cultural golden age, marked by the development of chữ Nôm (Vietnamese written in modified Chinese characters) for literature and court use, alongside Literary Chinese. Confucian education and imperial examinations were systematized. However, the dynasty weakened in its final decades due to extravagant rulers, succession disputes, and devastating invasions by Champa under King Chế Bồng Nga. The powerful minister Hồ Quý Ly exploited this instability, eventually usurping the throne in 1400.

### Economy and Technology
The Trần economy was based on agriculture, supported by a system of levees along the Red River. The state implemented personal and land taxes. Manufacturing, shipbuilding, and trade expanded, with markets and state-run inns established. Technologically, the Trần improved Chinese gunpowder and firearms, which proved decisive against Champa. In 1396, Hồ Quý Ly introduced paper money to replace copper coins, partly to conserve copper for weapons. Advances were also made in medicine and calendar calculation.

## Terms
- **Đại Việt**: The historical name for Vietnam used during this period.
- **Chữ Nôm**: A logographic writing system using modified Chinese characters to represent Vietnamese words; its use for literature and court documents expanded significantly under the Trần.
- **Mandarin**: A scholar-official in the imperial bureaucracy.
- **Tributary state**: A state that acknowledges the nominal supremacy of a more powerful neighbor (like the Yuan dynasty) through regular tribute missions in exchange for peace and trade.
- **Tam khôi**: The three top laureates of the imperial examination: trạng nguyên (first), bảng nhãn (second), and thám hoa (third).
- **Thể thân**: A personal tax levied on individuals based on their cultivated land area.
- **Hát tuồng**: A form of traditional Vietnamese theatre that developed during the late Trần period.
- **Trúc Lâm School**: A Vietnamese school of Zen Buddhism founded by Emperor Trần Nhân Tông.

## Debates and Open questions
- The role and agency of the child empress Lý Chiêu Hoàng in the dynasty's founding is often overshadowed by the actions of Trần Thủ Độ.
- The extent to which the Trần dynasty's cultural achievements, particularly the use of chữ Nôm, represented a deliberate policy of "Sinicization" versus the development of a distinct Vietnamese identity is a subject of scholarly discussion.
- The precise causes of the famines that ravaged Đại Việt from 1290 to 1292 are not recorded, though war neglect, labor mobilization, and climate events are proposed factors.

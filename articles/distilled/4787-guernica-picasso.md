# Guernica (Picasso)

## Overview
Pablo Picasso's *Guernica* (1937) is a monumental oil painting created in response to the bombing of the Basque town of Guernica during the Spanish Civil War. It is widely regarded as one of the most powerful anti-war statements in art history, using a stark, monochromatic style to depict the suffering of civilians. The painting became a universal symbol of the horrors of war and a focal point for political and artistic debate.

## Timeline
- **Jan 1937** — Picasso commissioned by Spanish Republic for Paris Exposition.
- **26 Apr 1937** — German Condor Legion bombs Guernica, Spain.
- **28 Apr 1937** — George Steer's eyewitness account published in *The Times* and *The New York Times*.
- **1 May 1937** — Picasso reads Steer's account and abandons his initial studio theme.
- **4 Jun 1937** — Picasso completes *Guernica*.
- **Jul 1937** — Painting unveiled at Spanish Pavilion, 1937 Paris International Exposition.
- **1938-39** — Painting tours Scandinavia and the UK.
- **May 1939** — Painting first shown in the US at Valentine Gallery, New York.
- **1939-81** — Painting housed at Museum of Modern Art (MoMA), New York, per Picasso's wishes.
- **1981** — Painting arrives in Spain, displayed in Madrid.
- **1992** — Painting moved to its current home at the Museo Reina Sofía, Madrid.

## Body

### Commission and Creation
In January 1937, the Spanish Republican government commissioned Picasso for the Paris International Exposition. He initially worked on sketches of an artist's studio. After the 26 April 1937 bombing of Guernica, and upon reading journalist George Steer's eyewitness account, Picasso abandoned his initial idea. At the urging of poet Juan Larrea, he began sketching the bombing. Photographer Dora Maar found a large studio for the work and documented its creation over 35 days, finishing on 4 June 1937. Her photographic practice may have influenced Picasso's choice of a stark black-and-white palette.

### Composition and Symbolism
The large painting (3.49m x 7.76m) depicts a scene of chaos and suffering within a room. Key figures include a gored horse, a bull, screaming women, a dead baby, and a dismembered soldier. A bare light bulb blazes overhead. Interpretations vary widely, especially regarding the bull and horse. Picasso resisted assigning specific meanings, stating, "I make the painting for the painting." Scholarly consensus notes that the composition expresses protest, uses monochrome to convey pain and chaos, and includes symbols like a broken sword (defeat) and a flower (hope). Some analysts see hidden images, such as a skull within the horse's form.

### Exhibition and Journey
*Guernica* was first shown at the 1937 Paris Exposition, where it received mixed reviews. It then toured Europe and the United States to raise funds for Spanish war relief. After Franco's victory, Picasso refused to let the painting return to Spain until democracy was restored. It was housed at MoMA in New York from 1939 until 1981. During the Vietnam War, it became a site for anti-war protests and was vandalized in 1974. After Franco's death and Spain's transition to democracy, the painting was sent to Madrid in 1981. It was moved to the Museo Reina Sofía in 1992.

### Legacy and Reproductions
*Guernica* is a universal symbol against the suffering of war. A full-size tapestry copy hangs at the United Nations in New York. In 2003, it was controversially covered during press conferences arguing for the Iraq War. The painting has inspired numerous artworks and remains a touchstone for political and artistic expression. Basque nationalists have advocated for its display in the Basque Country, but it is considered too fragile to move.

## Terms
- **Condor Legion**: The German air force unit that bombed Guernica in support of Franco's Nationalists.
- **Spanish Civil War (1936-1939)**: Conflict between the Republican government and Nationalist forces led by Francisco Franco.
- **Cubism**: An early 20th-century art movement that uses geometric shapes and multiple perspectives, which Picasso helped pioneer.
- **Museo Reina Sofía**: The museum in Madrid, Spain, where *Guernica* is permanently displayed.
- **Museum of Modern Art (MoMA)**: The New York museum that housed *Guernica* from 1939 to 1981.
- **Dora Maar**: A photographer and artist who documented the creation of *Guernica* and influenced its style.
- **Tapestry**: A woven textile reproduction; a famous copy of *Guernica* hangs at the UN.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Symbolism**: The specific meanings of the bull, horse, and other elements are debated; Picasso himself denied intentional symbolism.
- **Political Efficacy**: Some Marxist groups initially criticized the painting for lacking a clear political vision for the future.
- **Location**: There is ongoing debate about whether the painting should be displayed in the Basque Country, its historical subject, rather than Madrid.
- **UN Tapestry Covering**: The 2003 covering of the UN tapestry sparked debate about whether it was done to avoid an anti-war backdrop during the Iraq War buildup.

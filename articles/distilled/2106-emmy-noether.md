# Emmy Noether

## Overview
Emmy Noether (1882–1935) was a German mathematician whose work fundamentally reshaped abstract algebra and mathematical physics. She is best known for Noether's theorem, which links symmetry in physical systems to conservation laws, and for her pioneering theories of rings, fields, and algebras. Despite facing significant gender discrimination, she became a leading figure in mathematics, influencing generations of students and colleagues.

## Timeline
- **23 March 1882** — Born in Erlangen, Bavaria, to mathematician Max Noether.
- **1907** — Earns doctorate in mathematics from the University of Erlangen under Paul Gordan.
- **1915** — Invited to the University of Göttingen by Hilbert and Klein; begins lecturing under Hilbert's name.
- **1918** — Proves Noether's theorems on invariants and conservation laws.
- **1919** — Gains habilitation, becoming a Privatdozent (unpaid lecturer).
- **1921** — Publishes *Idealtheorie in Ringbereichen*, foundational for commutative ring theory.
- **1924** — B. L. van der Waerden joins her circle; later publishes *Moderne Algebra* based on her work.
- **1932** — Delivers plenary address at the International Congress of Mathematicians in Zürich; receives Ackermann–Teubner Memorial Award.
- **1933** — Dismissed from Göttingen by the Nazi government; emigrates to the United States.
- **14 April 1935** — Dies at age 53 in Bryn Mawr, Pennsylvania, following surgery.

## Body

### Early Life and Education
Noether initially qualified to teach languages but chose to study mathematics at the University of Erlangen–Nuremberg, where women were largely excluded. She completed her doctorate in 1907 with a dissertation on algebraic invariants, a computational approach she later dismissed. From 1908 to 1915, she taught at Erlangen without pay, a common situation for women in academia at the time.

### Göttingen and Noether's Theorem
In 1915, David Hilbert and Felix Klein recruited Noether to Göttingen, but the philosophical faculty blocked her habilitation due to her gender. She lectured under Hilbert's name until 1919, when she finally obtained the rank of Privatdozent. During this period, she proved Noether's theorem, which demonstrates that every differentiable symmetry of a physical system's action corresponds to a conserved quantity. This work, initially presented to resolve a paradox in general relativity, became a cornerstone of modern theoretical physics.

### Development of Abstract Algebra
Noether's most influential work began in 1920. In her 1921 paper, she developed the theory of ideals in commutative rings using the ascending chain condition, leading to the concept of Noetherian rings. She generalized earlier results and introduced a new, axiomatic approach to algebra. Her ideas were disseminated widely, particularly through B. L. van der Waerden's textbook *Moderne Algebra*. In her third epoch (1927–1935), she unified the representation theory of groups with the theory of modules and advanced noncommutative algebra.

### The Noether School and Teaching
At Göttingen, Noether mentored a large group of students, known as the "Noether Boys," and fostered a collaborative environment. Her lectures were spontaneous and discussion-based, often serving as incubators for new research. She was known for her generosity with ideas, which were frequently published by her students and colleagues. Her teaching style was energetic but could be challenging for outsiders to follow.

### Exile and Final Years
In 1933, the Nazi government dismissed Noether from her position. She emigrated to the United States, taking a position at Bryn Mawr College and lecturing at the Institute for Advanced Study in Princeton. She continued to teach and collaborate until her death from a tumor in 1935.

## Terms
- **Abstract Algebra**: A branch of mathematics that studies algebraic structures such as rings, fields, and groups using axiomatic methods.
- **Noether's Theorem**: A fundamental result in mathematical physics stating that every continuous symmetry of a physical system corresponds to a conserved quantity.
- **Ideal**: A subset of a ring that is closed under addition and multiplication by any element of the ring, used to generalize the concept of divisibility.
- **Noetherian Ring**: A ring in which every ascending chain of ideals eventually stabilizes (the ascending chain condition).
- **Ascending Chain Condition**: A finiteness condition where any increasing sequence of subsets (e.g., ideals, subgroups) becomes constant after a finite number of steps.
- **Privatdozent**: An academic title in German universities allowing a scholar to lecture, often without pay or a formal position.
- **Commutative Ring**: A ring where the multiplication operation is commutative (i.e., a·b = b·a).
- **Representation Theory**: A field of mathematics that studies abstract algebraic structures by representing their elements as linear transformations of vector spaces.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Noether's Problem**: Noether asked whether the fixed field of a subgroup of a permutation group acting on a field of rational functions is always a pure transcendental extension. This was later shown to be false in general by counterexamples (e.g., Swan, 1969), but the inverse Galois problem, to which it is related, remains unsolved.
- **Noether's Gap**: For years, it was uncertain whether Noether's bound for the degrees of generators of invariant rings of finite groups held when the characteristic of the field divides the group order. This was resolved affirmatively by Fleischmann (2000) and Fogarty (2001).
- **Recognition**: Despite her profound influence, Noether was never promoted to a full professorship in Germany and was not elected to the Göttingen Academy of Sciences, reflecting the persistent gender and political biases of her time.

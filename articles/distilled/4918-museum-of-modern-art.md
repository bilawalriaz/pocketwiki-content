# Museum of Modern Art

## Overview
The Museum of Modern Art (MoMA) is a leading art museum in New York City, instrumental in shaping the history of modern art, particularly from Europe. Founded in 1929, its collection spans the late 19th century to the present, encompassing painting, sculpture, film, photography, architecture, design, and more. MoMA is renowned for its influential exhibitions, its role in establishing modern art as a serious discipline, and its ongoing evolution in response to cultural and institutional debates.

## Body

### Founding and Early Years (1929–1939)
MoMA was conceived in 1929 by Abby Aldrich Rockefeller, Lillie P. Bliss, and Mary Quinn Sullivan. It opened just days after the Wall Street Crash in rented quarters. Alfred H. Barr Jr. was its first director, under whose leadership the collection rapidly expanded, beginning with European modernists. Despite initial opposition from John D. Rockefeller Jr., the museum moved through several temporary locations before he donated the land for its permanent site. The current building, designed by Philip L. Goodwin and Edward Durell Stone, opened in 1939.

### Growth and Influence (1930s–1960s)
Under Barr, MoMA pioneered multidisciplinary collecting, establishing departments for photography and film. It hosted landmark exhibitions, including a 1939–1940 Picasso retrospective that redefined the artist's legacy. Nelson Rockefeller, president from 1939, drove publicity and expansion. The museum's influence extended into cultural politics; it was among institutions that aided the CIA in Cold War cultural propaganda. A 1958 fire destroyed a major Monet but also highlighted the museum's emergency protocols.

### Expansion and Controversy (1980s–Present)
Major expansions in 1983 and 2004 (by Yoshio Taniguchi) nearly doubled exhibition space. The 2019 renovation by Diller Scofidio + Renfro added 47,000 square feet, aiming to integrate the collection chronologically and highlight underrepresented artists. MoMA has faced controversies, including protests over labor practices (its union was founded in 1971) and the 2014 demolition of the adjacent American Folk Art Museum building for expansion. The museum has also been involved in art repatriation claims for works lost during the Holocaust.

### Collections and Departments
MoMA's collection includes over 200,000 works. Its six curatorial departments are Architecture and Design, Drawings and Prints, Film, Media and Performance, Painting and Sculpture, and Photography. The photography collection, founded in 1940, is world-renowned. The film archive, established in 1932, holds over 25,000 titles. The library contains about 300,000 books and extensive artist files. The Architecture and Design department, founded in 1932, holds 28,000 works, including the Mies van der Rohe Archive.

## Terms
- **Modern Art**: Art produced from the late 19th century through the mid-20th century, characterized by a break from traditional techniques and subjects.
- **International Style**: A major architectural style of the 1920s and 1930s, emphasizing volume over mass, regularity, and the use of modern materials like glass and steel.
- **Curatorial Department**: A specialized division within a museum responsible for the care, study, and exhibition of a specific category of the collection.
- **Provenance**: The documented history of an artwork's ownership, crucial for verifying authenticity and legal title.
- **Repatriation**: The return of cultural property, such as art, to its country or people of origin, often following disputes over looted or stolen items.
- **Cultural Propaganda**: The use of cultural institutions and events to promote a particular political ideology or national image, notably during the Cold War.
- **Chronological Presentation**: Organizing artworks by the time period of their creation, rather than by medium or style.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Labor Practices**: MoMA has faced ongoing criticism and protests, including the 2021 "Strike MoMA" movement, over what critics call "toxic philanthropy" and its treatment of staff.
- **Art Repatriation**: The museum continues to be involved in claims for artworks lost during the Holocaust, with cases sometimes settled confidentially, raising questions about transparency and justice.
- **Architectural Impact**: The demolition of the American Folk Art Museum building in 2014 sparked debate about the balance between institutional expansion and preserving recent architectural heritage.
- **Collection Integration**: The 2019 renovation's shift from a medium-based to a chronological presentation is a significant curatorial experiment, the long-term impact of which is still being assessed.

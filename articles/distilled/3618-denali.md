# Denali

## Overview
Denali, also known as Mount McKinley, is the highest mountain peak in North America, with a summit elevation of 20,310 feet (6,190 m). Located in the Alaska Range, it is the centerpiece of Denali National Park and Preserve. The mountain is notable for its immense vertical relief and has been the subject of a long-standing naming controversy between its traditional Indigenous name and the name honoring President William McKinley.

## Timeline
- **c. 1794** — First European sighting by British explorer George Vancouver.
- **1896** — Gold prospector William Dickey names the mountain "Mount McKinley" for presidential candidate William McKinley.
- **1903** — First recorded climbing attempt by James Wickersham; unsuccessful.
- **1906** — Frederick Cook claims the first ascent, but the claim is later discredited.
- **1913** — First verified ascent by Hudson Stuck, Harry Karstens, Walter Harper, and Robert Tatum via the South Summit.
- **1917** — U.S. federal government officially recognizes the name "Mount McKinley."
- **1951** — Bradford Washburn pioneers the West Buttress route, now the most popular.
- **1975** — Alaska state government officially changes the name to Denali.
- **2015** — U.S. Department of the Interior changes the federal name to Denali.
- **2025** — U.S. Department of the Interior reverts the federal name to Mount McKinley.

## Body

### Geology and Features
Denali is a granitic pluton, primarily pink quartz monzonite, uplifted by tectonic pressure from the subduction of the Pacific Plate beneath the North American Plate. Its high topography is linked to the Denali Fault, a right-lateral fault system. A restraining bend in the fault creates immense compressional forces that push the crust upward. The mountain has two significant summits: the higher South Summit (20,310 ft) and the North Summit (19,470 ft). Five large glaciers flow from its slopes, including the Kahiltna Glacier, the longest in the Alaska Range.

### Naming History
The Koyukon Athabaskan people have called the peak "Denali" (meaning "the high one") for centuries. During Russian ownership, it was known as "Bolshaya Gora" ("big mountain"). In 1896, prospector William Dickey named it "Mount McKinley" in support of William McKinley. The federal government recognized "Mount McKinley" from 1917. In 1975, Alaska changed the state name to Denali, but a federal name change was blocked by an Ohio congressman. In 2015, the Obama administration changed the federal name to Denali. In January 2025, the Trump administration reverted the federal name to Mount McKinley via executive order, though the name of Denali National Park remains unchanged.

### Climbing History
Early attempts included James Wickersham's 1903 expedition and Frederick Cook's disputed 1906 claim. The first uncontroversial ascent of the main (South) summit was achieved on June 7, 1913, by the Stuck-Karstens expedition. The West Buttress route, pioneered by Bradford Washburn in 1951, is now the standard and most popular route. The mountain is one of the Seven Summits and has claimed the lives of nearly 100 mountaineers. Notable milestones include the first winter ascent (1967), first solo ascent (1970), and first ascent by dog team (1979).

### Weather and Records
A weather station at 19,000 feet (5,800 m) is the third-highest in the world. It recorded a temperature of −75.5 °F (−59.7 °C) in 2003 and a North American record windchill of −118.1 °F (−83.4 °C). Historical thermometers placed on the mountain have recorded temperatures as low as −100 °F (−73 °C).

## Terms
- **Topographic Prominence**: The height of a mountain's summit relative to the lowest contour line encircling it but containing no higher summit within it.
- **Topographic Isolation**: The distance to the nearest point of equal elevation.
- **Granitic Pluton**: A body of intrusive igneous rock (granite) that crystallized from magma below Earth's surface.
- **Denali Fault**: A major active right-lateral strike-slip fault in southern Alaska.
- **Restraining Bend**: A curve in a strike-slip fault where movement is impeded, causing compression and uplift.
- **West Buttress Route**: The most popular climbing route on Denali, pioneered in 1951.
- **Seven Summits**: The highest mountains on each of the seven continents.

## Debates and Open Questions
The primary ongoing debate concerns the mountain's official name. While the federal name was changed to Denali in 2015, it was reverted to Mount McKinley in 2025. This decision has been criticized by Alaska's political leaders, who prefer Denali. The historical climbing record also contains controversies, most notably the unverified 1906 ascent claim by Frederick Cook and the disputed 1910 "Sourdough Expedition" claim to have reached the North Summit.

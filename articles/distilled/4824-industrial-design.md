# Industrial design

## Overview
Industrial design is the creative process of defining a product's form and features before mass production, focusing on aesthetics, usability, and problem-solving. It emerged with the Industrial Revolution, separating design from manufacturing to address the needs of a growing consumer market and complex production systems. The field bridges art, engineering, and business to create products that are functional, manufacturable, and appealing.

## Timeline
- **c. 1750** — School of St. Peter founded in Lyons for training draftsmen in silk pattern design.
- **1667** — Gobelins Manufactory opens in Paris, a large government-operated workshop under Charles Le Brun.
- **1709** — Meissen porcelain workshops established in Saxony, using patterns from court artists.
- **Mid-18th C.** — Industrial Revolution begins in Great Britain, mechanizing production and creating demand for designed consumer goods.
- **1840** — Earliest published use of the term "industrial design" possibly appears in *The Art-Union*.
- **1853** — Jacques-Eugène Armengaud publishes *The Practical Draughtsman's Book of Industrial Design*.
- **1907** — Deutscher Werkbund founded in Germany to integrate crafts with industrial mass-production.
- **1919** — Joseph Claude Sinel is often (but disputedly) first to use the term "industrial design."
- **1950s** — Henry Dreyfuss pioneers an analytical, user-focused design approach using ergonomics.
- **2015** — International Council of Societies of Industrial Design (ICSID) unveils a renewed definition of industrial design.

## Body

### Precursors and Early Development
Before industrialization, design and manufacturing were unified in craft-based work, where a product's form was determined by the individual creator during production. Precedents for the division of labor emerged in medieval workshops and through 16th-century pattern books. Large-scale royal patronage, like the Gobelins Manufactory (1667) and Meissen porcelain (1709), organized teams of specialists but still relied on craft-based reproduction, where quality often declined as production scale increased.

### Birth of the Discipline
Industrial design emerged directly from the Industrial Revolution's mechanization and urbanization, which created mass markets and a heterogeneous middle class. The discipline's formal origins are linked to efforts like the German Deutscher Werkbund (1907), which aimed to merge traditional crafts with industrial techniques. While the term "industrial design" was first used in 1840, its modern practice was established by pioneers like Christopher Dresser and solidified through publications like Armengaud's 1853 book, which expanded technical drawing education.

### Definition and Process
Industrial design is defined as a strategic, human-centered problem-solving process that drives innovation and improves quality of life. It focuses on a product's form, usability, and relationship with the user and environment, distinct from engineering's focus on technical functionality. The design process involves research, prototyping (using methods like 3D printing or clay modeling), and testing, often employing 3D software. Designers collaborate with engineers and marketers to ensure functionality, manufacturability, and market appeal.

### Education and Professional Practice
Education in industrial design overlaps with graphic, interior, and engineering design, but is distinguished as an applied art focused on aesthetics and usability. Programs are accredited by art and design associations (e.g., NASAD in the U.S.) and lead to degrees like a Bachelor of Industrial Design (B.I.D.) or Bachelor of Fine Arts (B.F.A.). The field's socio-technical impact is demonstrated by designers like Raymond Loewy, Kenneth Grange, and Jonathan Ive, whose work on everyday objects—from locomotives to smartphones—has shaped culture and daily life.

## Terms
- **Industrial Design:** The creative process of defining a product's form and features before mass production, focusing on aesthetics, usability, and problem-solving.
- **Mass Production:** Manufacturing of goods in large quantities using standardized, often automated, processes.
- **Craft-based Design:** An approach where a product's form is determined by its creator largely during the act of production, based on personal skill and client requirements.
- **Ergonomics:** The study of people's efficiency in their working environment; in design, it involves optimizing products for human use and comfort.
- **Anthropometrics:** The comparative study of human body measurements and properties.
- **Prototyping:** Creating an early sample or model of a product to test concepts and production processes.
- **CAD (Computer-Aided Design):** Software used by designers to create, modify, and optimize a design.
- **Functionalist Approach:** A design philosophy emphasizing that an object's form should be primarily based upon its intended function or purpose.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The precise first use of the term "industrial design" is debated, with attributions to Joseph Claude Sinel in 1919 (which he denied) and an earlier possible publication in 1840.
- The boundaries between industrial design and engineering design vary by country and are often defined by professional credentials and licensure.
- The field continually evolves in response to new technologies, sustainability concerns, and changing definitions of user experience and service design.

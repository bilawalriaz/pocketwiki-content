# Henry IV, Holy Roman Emperor

## Overview
Henry IV (1050–1106) was Holy Roman Emperor and King of Germany whose reign was defined by the Investiture Controversy, a power struggle with the papacy over the appointment of church officials. His conflict with Pope Gregory VII, culminating in the famous "Walk to Canossa," became a defining moment in medieval history, symbolizing the clash between secular and ecclesiastical authority. His reign was marked by internal rebellions, civil wars, and a contested legacy.

## Timeline
- **1056** — Becomes King of Germany under the regency of his mother, Agnes of Poitou.
- **1062** — Kidnapped by Archbishop Anno II of Cologne in the Coup of Kaiserswerth.
- **1065** — Comes of age; begins personal rule.
- **1073** — Pope Alexander II excommunicates Henry's advisers; Saxon rebellion begins.
- **1076** — Excommunicated by Pope Gregory VII; subjects released from allegiance.
- **1077** — Performs penance at Canossa, securing absolution.
- **1080** — Renewed excommunication; Henry's supporters elect Antipope Clement III.
- **1084** — Crowned Holy Roman Emperor by Clement III in Rome.
- **1093** — His son Conrad rebels against him.
- **1103** — Proclaims the first *Reichsfriede* (imperial peace) across Germany.
- **1105** — Forced to abdicate by his younger son, Henry V.
- **1106** — Dies without absolution, excommunicated.

## Body

### Early Life and Regency
Henry IV was the son of Emperor Henry III and became king at age six upon his father's death in 1056. His mother, Agnes, served as regent but struggled to control the growing "liberty of the Church" movement and aristocratic factions. In 1062, Archbishop Anno II of Cologne kidnapped Henry to seize power. After Henry came of age in 1065, he sought to recover royal estates lost during his minority, relying on lower-ranking officials (*ministeriales*) which alienated the nobility, particularly in Saxony.

### The Investiture Controversy and Saxon Rebellions
Henry's insistence on his royal prerogative to appoint bishops and abbots (lay investiture) brought him into direct conflict with the reformist papacy. Pope Gregory VII condemned the practice as simony (the sale of church offices). After crushing a Saxon rebellion in 1075, Henry challenged Gregory's authority. In 1076, German bishops declared Gregory's election invalid, and Gregory responded by excommunicating Henry and releasing his subjects from loyalty. Facing opposition at home, Henry performed public penance at Canossa in 1077, a humiliating act that temporarily restored him to communion but did not end the conflict.

### Civil War and Imperial Coronation
The controversy evolved into a civil war. Henry's opponents elected a rival king, Rudolf of Rheinfelden, in 1077. After Rudolf's death in 1080, Henry's supporters elected the antipope Clement III. In 1084, Clement crowned Henry emperor in Rome, though the city was sacked by Norman allies of the pope. Henry spent the following years campaigning in Italy against his enemies, including Matilda of Tuscany, and dealing with continued rebellion in Germany.

### Later Reign and Abdication
Henry's authority was undermined by the rebellion of his son Conrad in 1093 and later by his second wife, Eupraxia, who publicly accused him of abuse. After a period of restoration, Henry proclaimed the *Reichsfriede* (imperial peace) in 1103. In 1105, his younger son, Henry V, rebelled and forced his abdication. Henry IV died in 1106, still excommunicated, and was initially buried in unconsecrated ground.

## Terms
*   **Investiture Controversy:** The conflict between secular rulers and the papacy over the appointment of church officials.
*   **Simony:** The buying or selling of ecclesiastical privileges, such as church offices.
*   **Reichsfriede:** An imperial peace decree banning private feuds and violence across the empire.
*   **Ministeriales:** Unfree liegemen or officials who served the king, often in administrative or military roles.
*   **Antipope:** A claimant to the papacy in opposition to the legitimately elected pope.
*   **Patrician:** A title held by the Holy Roman Emperor, granting certain rights in papal elections.
*   **Liberty of the Church:** The principle that the Church should be free from secular control, especially in appointments.

## Debates and open questions
*   The primary sources offer starkly contrasting portraits of Henry, depicting him either as a pious defender of the poor or a wicked tyrant who robbed churches and enslaved freemen.
*   The authenticity of the accusations of sexual immorality and debauchery leveled against him by opponents is debated by historians, often seen as polemical propaganda.
*   The motivations behind his son Conrad's rebellion in 1093 are unclear, with sources offering different explanations ranging from personal grievance to political maneuvering.

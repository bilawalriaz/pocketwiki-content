# Central business district

## Overview
A central business district (CBD) is the commercial and business core of a city, characterized by a high concentration of offices, specialized services, and commercial space. It is often highly accessible and may function as a financial district. While frequently synonymous with a city's geographical center or downtown, a CBD can be located away from the historic core, especially in cities with strong preservation laws. The shape and location of a CBD are shaped by a city's history, transportation development, and urban planning policies.

## Body

### Definition and Characteristics
A CBD is the commercial heart of a city, containing a high density of offices and specialized goods and services. It is typically highly accessible. Geographically, it often coincides with the "city centre" or "downtown," but these terms are not synonymous; many cities have a CBD located away from their traditional historic core. The shape and type of a CBD closely reflect a city's history, with preservation laws often pushing modern CBDs to the periphery.

### Global Patterns and Examples
In many European cities (e.g., London, Paris, Moscow), historic preservation has led to CBDs being distinct from the old city center. In contrast, many "New World" cities developed a single center that combined commercial and cultural functions. In the 21st century, urbanization has led to megacities often having multiple, scattered CBDs.

*   **Europe:** London's largest CBDs are the City of London and Canary Wharf. Paris's is La Défense. Berlin has three CBDs due to its Cold War division.
*   **Asia:** Hong Kong's traditional CBD is Central, with other major districts in Quarry Bay and Tsim Sha Tsui. Singapore's CBD is in the Downtown Core, with plans for a second in Jurong East. Seoul has three main CBDs: Downtown Seoul, Gangnam, and Yeouido.
*   **The Americas:** São Paulo, Brazil, is multipolar with several business districts. In the U.S., Chicago's Loop is the second-largest CBD. Mexico City has two major CBDs: Paseo de la Reforma and Santa Fe.
*   **India:** India's office market shows a structural shift away from historic CBDs. As of mid-2025, only 19% of national office stock is in traditional CBDs, with 45% in suburban business districts (SBDs) and 36% in peripheral business districts (PBDs). Major cities exemplify this: Mumbai's primary CBD has shifted from Nariman Point to the Bandra Kurla Complex (BKC); Delhi's NCR retains a high CBD share (44%) split between Connaught Place and Gurugram's DLF Cyber City; Bengaluru's CBD accounts for only 9% of office stock, with most activity in peripheral IT corridors like Whitefield; Hyderabad's center has moved to HITEC City; and Kolkata's market is the most peripherally weighted, with 71% of stock outside CBD/SBD zones.

## Terms
*   **Central Business District (CBD):** The commercial and business center of a city, containing a high concentration of offices and specialized services.
*   **City Centre / Downtown:** The geographical or traditional heart of a city, which may or may not be the same as the CBD.
*   **Suburban Business District (SBD):** A major commercial area located on the periphery of a city's historic core, often developed to decongest the CBD.
*   **Peripheral Business District (PBD):** A commercial zone located even further from the city center than an SBD, often in newly developed corridors.
*   **Purpose-built business district:** A CBD planned and constructed as a dedicated commercial zone, often separate from the historic city center (e.g., La Défense, Paris).
*   **Micro central business district:** A smaller, secondary business hub serving a specific area outside the main CBD (e.g., Parramatta in Sydney).
*   **Grade-A office space:** High-quality office space with modern amenities, typically commanding the highest rents in a market.

## Debates and Open Questions
The source highlights an ongoing structural shift in many global cities, particularly in India, where commercial activity is migrating from historic CBDs to newer suburban and peripheral corridors. This raises questions about the future economic and cultural role of traditional city centers and the long-term viability of policies that restrict development in historic cores. The emergence of multiple CBDs in megacities also prompts debate about urban planning, transportation infrastructure, and the definition of a city's "center."

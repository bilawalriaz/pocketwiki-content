# Bay of Biscay

## Overview
The Bay of Biscay is a gulf of the northeast Atlantic Ocean, bordered by the northern coast of Spain and the western coast of France. It is known for its rough seas, severe winter storms, and significant historical naval engagements. The bay is a major habitat for marine mammals, including several whale species.

- **Prior to 1850s** — Basque whaling drives the eastern North Atlantic right whale population to near extinction.
- **1592** — Spanish defeat an English fleet in the Battle of the Bay of Biscay.
- **June 1795** — British and French fleets fight the Biscay campaign during the French Revolutionary Wars.
- **22 June 1918** — USS *Californian* sinks after striking a naval mine.
- **1920** — SS *Afrique* sinks in a storm, killing 575 people.
- **28 December 1943** — Battle of the Bay of Biscay occurs between British and German naval forces during World War II.

- **25 August 1944** — German submarine U-667 sinks after hitting a mine.
- **12 April 1970** — Soviet submarine *K-8* sinks due to a fire, killing 40 sailors and losing four nuclear torpedoes.

## Body

### Geography and Climate
The bay is defined by the International Hydrographic Organization as the line joining Cap Ortegal in Spain to Penmarch Point in France. Its average depth is 1,744 m (5,722 ft), with a maximum depth of 4,735 m (15,535 ft). The continental shelf extends far into the bay, creating shallow waters that contribute to its notoriously rough seas. The Gulf Stream moderates temperatures year-round. In late spring and early summer, a large fog triangle often fills the southwestern half. Winter brings severe weather, with depressions from the west causing powerful windstorms (*galernas*) and constant, light rain (*orbayu*).

### Etymology and Names
The bay has multiple names reflecting its bordering regions. In Spain, it is the Gulf of Biscay (*Golfo de Vizcaya*); in France, the Gulf of Gascony (*Golfe de Gascogne*). The English name derives from Biscay on the northern Spanish coast. The southern portion, washing over Spain's Cantabria, is locally called the Cantabrian Sea (*Mar Cantábrico*), a name originating with the Romans. Medieval maps sometimes labeled it *El Mar del los Vascos* (the Basque Sea).

### History and Naval Significance
The bay has been a site of major naval conflicts for centuries. Notable engagements include the 1592 battle between Spain and England, the 1795 Biscay campaign between Britain and France, and a 1943 World War II battle between British ships and German destroyers. During WWII, the bay became infamous as the "Valley of Death" for German U-boats due to sustained Allied air attacks. The area also saw significant maritime disasters, such as the sinking of the SS *Afrique* in 1920 and the loss of the Soviet submarine *K-8* in 1970 due to a fire that crippled its nuclear reactors.

### Wildlife
The bay is a critical habitat for cetaceans (whales and dolphins). Ferry routes provide opportunities to observe species, including the relatively frequently seen Cuvier's beaked whale. The North Atlantic right whale, once common for feeding and calving, was nearly wiped out by Basque whaling and is now almost extinct in the region, with only a few sightings recorded in the late 20th century. The best areas to see larger whales are over deep-water canyons like the Santander Canyon. The coastal Plaiaundi Ecological Park, where the Bidasoa River meets the sea, supports diverse flora and migratory birds.

## Terms
- **Cetacean**: A marine mammal of the order Cetacea, which includes whales, dolphins, and porpoises.
- **Continental Shelf**: The extended perimeter of each continent and associated coastal plain, which is relatively shallow compared to the deep ocean basin.
- **Galerna**: A severe, sudden windstorm in the Bay of Biscay, characterized by a rapid fall in atmospheric pressure.
- **Gulf Stream**: A powerful, warm ocean current that flows from the Gulf of Mexico into the Atlantic, moderating the climate of Western Europe.
- **Orbayu**: A term for the light, constant rain that falls on the shores of the Bay of Biscay, often associated with winter depressions.
- **Rennell Current**: A cold ocean current that flows anti-clockwise along the continental shelf border of the Bay of Biscay.
- **Sinus Cantabrorum**: The Latin name for the bay, meaning "Bay of the Cantabri," used by the Romans in the 1st century BC.

# Davao City

## Overview
Davao City is a highly urbanized city in the Davao Region of the Philippines. It is the country's largest city by land area and the most populous city in Mindanao, serving as the region's primary trade, commerce, and industrial hub. The city's history is marked by indigenous settlement, colonial struggles, rapid economic growth, and periods of significant social unrest.

## Timeline

- **1800** — Datu Bago moves to the Davao Gulf area and begins consolidating power.
- **1848** — José Cruz de Oyanguren founds the town of Nueva Vergara after defeating Datu Bago.
- **1867** — Nueva Vergara is officially renamed Davao.
- **1936** — Davao is inaugurated as a charter city.
- **1941-1945** — Japanese occupation of Davao City during World War II.
- **1967** — The Province of Davao is divided; Davao City becomes part of Davao del Sur.
- **1988** — Rodrigo Duterte first elected Mayor of Davao City.
- **2024** — City population reaches 1,848,947 according to the census.

## Body

### Etymology and Precolonial Era
The name "Davao" is derived from the phonetic blending of three Bagobo words for the Davao River: *Davah*, *Dawaw*, and *Dabo*. The area was originally inhabited by Lumad peoples like the Bagobos and Matigsalugs. Spanish explorers in 1543 avoided the region due to the presence of Moro warships, leaving it largely untouched by European influence for centuries.

### Maguindanao and Spanish Eras
In the late 1700s, Datu Bago was granted the Davao Gulf territory by the Sultan of Maguindanao. He established a fortified capital, Pinagurasan, which grew into a major trade center. Spanish colonization began in earnest in 1848 under José Cruz de Oyanguren, who defeated Datu Bago and founded the town of Nueva Vergara. Spanish control was often unstable, facing resistance from Lumad and Moro natives. The town was renamed Davao in 1867.

### American Period and World War II
American administration beginning in 1900 spurred economic growth through agricultural investment, attracting many Japanese entrepreneurs. Davao was incorporated into Moro Province (1903-1914) and later became the capital of Davao Province. It was chartered as a city in 1936. During World War II, the Japanese occupied the city from 1941 to 1945, subjecting it to brutal rule and extensive combat that caused tremendous destruction.

### Postwar Growth and Social Unrest
After the war, Davao regained its status as an economic hub. In 1967, the province was divided, and the city became part of Davao del Sur. The late 1960s and 1970s saw rising ethnic tensions and the growth of the Communist insurgency (NPA) following the Jabidah massacre and the declaration of martial law. The 1980s were marked by extreme violence, with the city earning labels like "Murder Capital" due to clashes between the NPA, vigilante groups like Alsa Masa, and state forces. This period ended with the People Power Revolution in 1986.

### Modern Governance and Economy
Rodrigo Duterte served as mayor for over 20 years, implementing strict policies on crime, smoking, and firecrackers. The city's economy is driven by agriculture (bananas, pineapples, coconuts), with major multinational corporations maintaining regional headquarters there. It is also a financial hub, home to the largest rural bank in the Philippines. The city is part of the East Asian Growth Area.

### Demographics and Culture
The population is predominantly Roman Catholic (78%), with a significant Muslim minority (4%). Cebuano is the most widely spoken language, often mixed with Tagalog. The city is a melting pot of Visayan, Luzon, Moro, and indigenous cultures, with historical Chinese and Japanese communities. The annual Kadayawan Festival celebrates its indigenous heritage and harvest. The city is known as the "Durian Capital of the Philippines."

## Terms
- **Highly Urbanized City:** A city in the Philippines that is independent of the province it is geographically located in, with its own government.
- **Lumad:** A collective term for the indigenous, non-Muslim, non-Christian peoples of Mindanao.
- **Moro:** A term for the Muslim ethnic groups of the southern Philippines.
- **Barangay:** The smallest administrative division in the Philippines, similar to a village or district.
- **Datu:** A chief or leader in pre-colonial Philippine societies.
- **New People's Army (NPA):** The armed wing of the Communist Party of the Philippines.
- **Alsa Masa:** A right-wing vigilante group formed in Davao in the 1980s to counter the NPA.
- **Kadayawan Festival:** An annual week-long festival in Davao celebrating a bountiful harvest and indigenous cultures.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Safety and Human Rights:** The city's reputation as one of the "safest" is contested by human rights groups and crime statistics, which have cited high murder and rape rates. The existence and actions of the alleged "Davao Death Squad" remain a subject of investigation and debate.
- **Political Dynasty:** The Duterte family has held the mayoral office for most of the period since 1988, raising discussions about political dynasties in the city.
- **Infrastructure Development:** Major projects like the Mindanao Railway and the Davao Public Transport Modernization Project face funding and implementation delays, impacting the city's growth.

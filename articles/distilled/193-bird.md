# Bird

## Overview
Birds (class Aves) are warm-blooded, feathered vertebrates and the only living descendants of dinosaurs, specifically theropods. They are defined by unique adaptations for flight—including lightweight pneumatic bones, a four-chambered heart, a high metabolic rate, and a specialized respiratory system with unidirectional airflow—though many lineages have secondarily lost flight. With over 11,000 species across 44 orders, birds inhabit every continent and play critical ecological roles as predators, pollinators, seed dispersers, and ecosystem engineers. Their evolutionary history bridges the Mesozoic and Cenozoic eras, making them central to understanding vertebrate evolution, while their visibility and diversity make them culturally and economically significant to humans.

## Timeline
- **Late Jurassic (~160 Ma)** — Earliest avialans (*Anchiornis*, *Xiaotingia*, *Aurornis*) appear in Tiaojishan Formation; *Archaeopteryx* appears ~155 Ma in Germany.
- **Early Cretaceous (~130–120 Ma)** — Diversification of Enantiornithes (opposite birds) and Euornithes (true birds); pygostyle and keeled sternum evolve.
- **Late Cretaceous (~100–66 Ma)** — Ancestors of modern birds (Neornithes) evolve open pelvis (~100 Ma), improved olfaction (~95 Ma); *Vegavis* (Maastrichtian) proves modern bird diversification began before Cenozoic; *Asteriornis* (Maastrichtian) close to Galloanserae.
- **66 Ma (K–Pg boundary)** — Cretaceous–Paleogene extinction eliminates non-avian dinosaurs, pterosaurs, and Enantiornithes; Neornithes survive.
- **Paleogene (66–23 Ma)** — Explosive adaptive radiation of modern birds (Neoaves, Galloanserae) via Antarctic interchange and South American land bridges.
- **1676** — Francis Willughby and John Ray develop the first classification of birds in their volume *Ornithologiae*.
- **1758** — Carl Linnaeus modifies that work to devise the taxonomic classification system currently in use.
- **1990** — Sibley and Ahlquist publish *Phylogeny and Classification of Birds*, pioneering DNA-DNA hybridization phylogeny.
- **2010–2022** — Avian genomics expands from 2 to 542 sequenced species, covering all orders and ~90% of families.
- **2009** — IUCN lists 1,227 bird species as threatened; estimated 2.9 billion breeding adults lost in North America since 1970.

## Body

### Evolution and Classification
Birds are nested within Theropoda (Maniraptora), making them living dinosaurs; their closest living relatives are crocodilians (clade Archosauria). The definition of "Aves" is debated: Gauthier’s crown-group definition (Neornithes only) excludes *Archaeopteryx* and fossil taxa, placing them in Avialae, while alternative definitions include all feathered dinosaurs (Avifilopluma) or all flying theropods (Avialae). Early avialans from the Tiaojishan Formation (~160 Ma) show four-winged hindlimb feathers and enlarged second toe claws. **Over 40% of key traits found in modern birds evolved during the 60 million year transition from the earliest bird-line archosaurs to the first maniraptoromorphs.** After the appearance of Maniraptoromorpha, the next 40 million years marked continuous body size reduction, neoteny, brain expansion, and pennaceous feather development. The Cretaceous saw two major radiations: Enantiornithes (dominant, toothed, reversed shoulder joint, extinct at K–Pg) and Euornithes (semi-aquatic ancestors of modern birds, evolving toothless beaks, pygostyle, and keeled sternum). Modern birds (Neornithes) split into Palaeognathae (ratites, tinamous) and Neognathae (all others). Molecular clocks suggest a Cretaceous origin in West Gondwana, but fossils indicate a post-K–Pg radiation ("rocks vs. clocks"); genomic data confirm a pulse of diversification at the K–Pg boundary with dispersal from Gondwana via Antarctica and South America.

### Distribution and Habitat
Birds occupy all seven continents and most terrestrial habitats, with highest diversity in the tropics. Tropical diversity results from lower extinction rates rather than higher speciation (which is higher at high latitudes). Many species migrate annually across hemispheres; seabirds and penguins are adapted for marine life (diving to 300 m). Human introductions have established populations globally (e.g., ring-necked pheasant, monk parakeet), while agricultural expansion has facilitated natural range expansions (cattle egret) but intensive farming reduces farmland populations.

### Anatomy and Physiology
**Skeletal:** Lightweight bones with pneumatic cavities connected to respiratory system; fused skull; variable cervical vertebrae; synsacrum (fused pelvic vertebrae); keeled sternum for flight muscle attachment (absent in flightless orders); forelimbs modified as wings.
**Excretory:** Primarily uricotelic (excrete uric acid); no urinary bladder (except ostrich); cloaca serves for waste, reproduction, and egg-laying; altricial nestlings often produce fecal sacs for parental removal.
**Reproductive:** Most males lack intromittent penis (present in Palaeognathae, Anseriformes, some Galliformes); females typically have single left ovary/oviduct (likely adaptation to avoid simultaneous dual-egg shell formation); ZW sex determination (female heterogametic); no temperature-dependent sex determination.
**Respiratory/Circulatory:** Unique flow-through lung with posterior air sacs ensuring fresh air during both inhalation and exhalation; syrinx for sound production; right aortic arch (vs. left in mammals); nucleated red blood cells; four-chambered myogenic heart larger relative to body mass than mammals; 10× greater gas-exchange surface area per volume than mammals.
**Nervous/Sensory:** Large brain relative to size; cerebellum coordinates flight, cerebrum controls behavior; generally poor smell (exceptions: kiwis, vultures, tubenoses); tetrachromatic vision (UV, blue, green, red cones) with double cones for achromatic vision; UV plumage patterns used in signaling and foraging; nictitating membrane lubricates eye; ears lack pinnae, cochlea not spiral; some species hear infrasound or use ultrasound/echolocation (swifts, oilbirds).
**Defense:** Chemical defenses rare (stomach oil in Procellariiformes, neurotoxin in pitohuis); weaponized spurs on wing (screamers, jacanas, lapwing) or bony alular knobs (steamer ducks, geese, swans); *Xenicibis* used elongated forelimb as jointed club.
**Integument:** Feathers (epidermal growths on pterylae tracts) for flight, insulation, display, camouflage; moult patterns vary (annual, centrifugal/centripetal sequences); preening with uropygial gland secretions (antimicrobial, flexibility); anting supplements parasite control; scales on toes/metatarsus (keratin, homologous to reptiles).

### Flight
Most birds fly; 60 extant species are flightless. Adaptations: lightweight skeleton, large pectoralis (15% body mass) and supracoracoideus muscles, wing as aerofoil. Wing shape determines flight style (flapping vs. soaring). Flightlessness correlates with island isolation, limited resources, absent mammalian predators, and gigantism. Penguins "fly" underwater using homologous musculature.

### Behaviour
**Diet/Feeding:** Varied (nectar, fruit, seeds, carrion, animals); crop stores food, gizzard grinds with gastroliths (no teeth); rapid digestion; insectivores consume 400–500 million metric tons arthropods annually. Specialists (nectarivores with brush tongues, probers, divers, filter feeders, grazers) vs. generalists. Kleptoparasitism supplements diet (frigatebirds steal ~5% average).
**Water:** Excretion and lack of sweat glands reduce needs; desert birds obtain water from food or allow hyperthermia; seabirds excrete salt via nasal glands; drinking methods include scooping, sucking (pigeons, finches), belly-feather transport (sandgrouse), crop milk (pigeons, flamingos, penguins).
**Migration:** Driven by seasonal resource optimization; triggered by photoperiod/weather; pre-migration fat deposition and organ reduction. Ranges: landbirds ~2,500 km, shorebirds ~4,000 km, bar-tailed godwit 10,200 km non-stop; Arctic tern ~70,900 km annually. Navigation via sun compass (internal clock), stellar compass (Polaris), and geomagnetic photoreceptors. Partial migration common (44% non-passerines, 32% passerines in Australia); altitudinal and nomadic movements also occur.
**Communication:** Visual (plumage, ritualized displays from preening/pecking, courtship dances) and auditory (syrinx songs/calls for mate attraction, territory, alarm, individual recognition; mechanical sounds: snipe feather drumming, woodpecker drumming, palm cockatoo tool use).
**Sociality:** Flocking for safety and foraging efficiency; mixed-species flocks in forests; associations with non-avians (seabirds/dolphins, hornbills/mongooses). Resting involves vigilant sleep (peeking), unihemispheric slow-wave sleep (predator vigilance), communal roosting (thermoregulation), tendon-locking perching, torpor/hibernation (hummingbirds, poorwill).
**Breeding:** 95% socially monogamous (seasonal to lifelong); extra-pair copulation common (genetic benefits, infertility insurance). Other systems: polygyny, polyandry, promiscuity; cooperative breeding (helpers at nest, common in Corvida). Territories defended for food; colonial breeding where defense impossible. Amniotic eggs (calcium carbonate shells); nest types vary (scrape to elaborate); incubation shared (monogamous) or single-parent (polygamous); brood patches transfer heat; periods 10–80+ days. Chicks altricial (helpless) to precocial (independent); parental care ranges from none (megapodes) to 14+ months post-fledging (frigatebirds); biparental care common, male care more frequent than in other vertebrates. Brood parasitism (obligate: cuckoos, honeyguides; non-obligate) exploits host care. Sexual selection drives dimorphism (Fisherian runaway, honest signaling). Inbreeding depression documented (zebra finch, Darwin’s finch); avoidance via dispersal, extra-pair mating, kin recognition.

### Ecology
Birds occupy diverse niches: canopy/insectivores, understory/frugivores, forest floor/nectarivores; aquatic (fishing, piracy); grassland granivores; raptors (mammal/bird hunters); vultures (scavengers). Preyed on by mammals, avivorous bats. Parasites (endo/ecto) show host-specific coevolution. Pollination (nectarivores) and seed dispersal (frugivores) are key mutualisms; plant-pollinator coevolution can be exclusive. On islands, birds fill megafauna roles (moa browsers in NZ; kererū/kōkako today); plants retain anti-moa defenses. Nest construction engineers invertebrate microhabitats; seabird guano enriches terrestrial and marine ecosystems.

### Relationship with Humans
**Economic:** Poultry (chickens, turkeys, ducks, geese) = largest animal protein source (76 Mt meat, 61 Mt eggs, 2003). Hunting (waterfowl, gamebirds) recreational/commercial; muttonbirding in Australasia. Feathers (down) for insulation; guano (phosphorus/nitrogen) historically strategic (Guano War). Falconry, cormorant fishing, messenger pigeons (used until WWII). Birdwatching/feeding = multi-million dollar industry (75% UK households feed birds).
**Cultural:** Prominent in religion/mythology (messengers, augury, symbols, deities: dove, owl, peacock). Art from cave paintings to Audubon; poetry (Coleridge, Catullus); metaphors (albatross burden, vulture funds); aircraft names (F-16 Falcon, V-22 Osprey). Perceptions vary culturally (owls: bad luck Africa, wise Europe).
**Threats/Conservation:** 120–130 extinctions since 1600s; 750–1800 lost during Pacific colonization. 1,227 threatened (2009); 2.9 billion lost in N. America since 1970. Threats: habitat loss, overhunting, collisions, bycatch, pollution, invasives (cats), climate change. Conservation successes: 16 species saved 1994–2004 (California condor, Norfolk parakeet). Some temperate species expand with humans (barn swallow, starling); tropical crop expansion benefits some (64+ species from rice in S. Asia) but harms others. Disease vectors: psittacosis, avian influenza, salmonellosis (zoonotic).

## Terms
- ****Aves**** — The biological class comprising all living birds; in crown-group definition, the last common ancestor of all extant birds and all its descendants (synonymous with Neornithes).
- ****Avialae**** — Clade containing all dinosaurs closer to birds than to *Deinonychus* (or *Troodon*); includes *Archaeopteryx* and all birds; often used synonymously with "bird" by palaeontologists.
- ****Neornithes**** — Crown-group birds: the most recent common ancestor of all living birds and all its descendants; split into Palaeognathae and Neognathae.
- ****Palaeognathae**** — Basal subdivision of Neornithes including flightless ratites (ostrich, emu) and weak-flying tinamous; retain primitive palate structure.
- ****Neognathae**** — Diverse subdivision of Neornithes containing all other living birds; characterized by derived palate and skeletal features.
- ****Pygostyle**** — Fused terminal caudal vertebrae supporting tail feathers (rectrices); replaces long bony tail in bird evolution.
- ****Alula**** — Small, movable feathered digit on the wing leading edge; functions as a leading-edge slot to prevent stall at low speeds.
- ****Uricotelic**** — Excreting nitrogenous waste primarily as uric acid (semi-solid), conserving water; characteristic of birds and reptiles.
- ****Syrinx**** — Vocal organ at the base of the trachea; contains vibrating tympanic membranes allowing complex sound production, sometimes two simultaneous songs.
- ****Altricial / Precocial**** — Developmental spectrum: altricial chicks hatch helpless, naked, blind; precocial chicks hatch mobile, feathered, eyes open.
- ****Brood parasitism**** — Reproductive strategy where a parasite lays eggs in a host's nest, leaving incubation and rearing to the host (e.g., cuckoos, honeyguides).
- ****Kleptoparasitism**** — Stealing food from other animals; in birds, a supplement to hunting (e.g., frigatebirds, skuas).
- ****ZW sex determination**** — System where females are heterogametic (ZW) and males homogametic (ZZ); opposite of mammalian XY system.

## Debates and open questions
- **Definition of Aves:** Whether to restrict Aves to the crown group (Neornithes) or include stem-group fossils like *Archaeopteryx*; impacts classification of Avialae and Avifilopluma.
- **Timing of modern bird radiation:** "Rocks vs. clocks" conflict—molecular clocks place Neornithes origin in Early–Late Cretaceous with Gondwanan vicariance, while fossil record suggests explosive post-K–Pg Cenozoic radiation; genomic calibrations support Cretaceous origin but K–Pg diversification pulse.
- **Flight origin:** Whether flight evolved from trees-down (arboreal gliding) or ground-up (cursorial flapping); basal paravians like *Microraptor* show four-winged gliding, but earliest avialans may have been omnivorous ground-foragers.
- **Phylogeny of Neoaves:** Relationships among the ~40 neoavian orders remain contentious despite genomic-scale data; rapid radiation creates short internodes and incomplete lineage sorting.
- **Inbreeding avoidance mechanisms:** Relative roles of dispersal, extra-pair mating, and kin recognition across species; demographic constraints on extra-pair mating as inbreeding avoidance.
- **Conservation prioritization:** Balancing habitat protection, invasive species control (especially cats), climate adaptation, and disease management for 1,200+ threatened species.

# Sakhalin

## Overview
Sakhalin is a large, resource-rich island in Northeast Asia, administered by Russia. Its history is defined by centuries of contested sovereignty between China, Japan, and Russia, culminating in its current status as Russian territory. The island's strategic location and natural resources, particularly oil and gas, have made it a focal point of geopolitical and economic interest.

## Timeline
- **1263–1308** — Mongol Yuan dynasty subdues the Ainu on Sakhalin, establishing a tributary relationship.
- **1409–1430s** — Ming dynasty establishes an administrative outpost on the mainland to manage Sakhalin tributaries.
- **1689** — Treaty of Nerchinsk defines the Stanovoy Mountains as the Qing-Russian border, leading to Qing enforcement of tribute on Sakhalin.
- **1679** — Japanese settlement established at Ōtomari on southern Sakhalin.
- **1855** — Treaty of Shimoda declares shared Russo-Japanese sovereignty over Sakhalin.
- **1875** — Treaty of Saint Petersburg: Japan cedes all claims to Sakhalin to Russia in exchange for the Kuril Islands.
- **1905** — Treaty of Portsmouth: Southern Sakhalin (below 50th parallel) is granted to Japan after the Russo-Japanese War.
- **1945** — Soviet Union invades and seizes all of Sakhalin from Japan in the final days of World War II.
- **1951** — Japan renounces claims to southern Sakhalin in the Treaty of San Francisco.
- **1983** — Korean Air Flight 007 is shot down by the Soviet Union after flying over Sakhalin.
- **1995** — The 7.0 Mw Neftegorsk earthquake devastates a settlement, killing 1,989 people.

## Body

### Etymology and Indigenous Peoples
The name "Sakhalin" derives from the Manchu *Sahaliyan*, meaning "black," referring to the Amur River. The Qing dynasty called it "Kuyedao" (Island of the Ainu). The island's indigenous peoples are the Ainu (south), Oroks (central), and Nivkhs (north). These groups paid tribute to the Yuan, Ming, and Qing dynasties, though control from China was generally loose.

### Early History and Tributary Systems
Humans inhabited Sakhalin since the Neolithic. Following Mongol conquests, the Yuan dynasty subdued the Ainu by 1308. The Ming dynasty (1368–1644) incorporated Sakhalin into its "system for subjugated peoples," appointing local headmen who paid tribute. The subsequent Qing dynasty (1644–1912) imposed a formal fur-tribute system, registering households and rewarding chiefs with silk and official titles, which fostered regional stability and trade.

### Japanese and Russian Exploration and Rivalry
Japanese exploration began in the 17th century, with the Matsumae clan establishing a settlement in 1679. By the early 19th century, Japanese economic influence extended up the east coast. Russia began asserting claims in the 19th century, establishing a penal colony in 1857. The 1855 Treaty of Shimoda created a shared sovereignty arrangement. After the 1875 Treaty of Saint Petersburg, Russia gained full control in exchange for the Kuril Islands.

### Division and World War II
Following the Russo-Japanese War, the 1905 Treaty of Portsmouth divided the island along the 50th parallel, with Japan administering the south as Karafuto Prefecture. After a brief Japanese occupation of the north during the Siberian Intervention (1920–1925), the division held until 1945. In August 1945, the Soviet Union invaded southern Sakhalin, completing its conquest by August 25. Most of the approximately 400,000 Japanese and Korean residents were repatriated to Japan between 1946 and 1950.

### Modern Era and Economy
Since 1945, Sakhalin has been entirely under Russian administration. The economy is dominated by oil and gas exports, developed through international consortia like Sakhalin-I and Sakhalin-II. The island's population, about 500,000, is predominantly ethnic Russian, with a significant Sakhalin Korean minority. Transport relies on sea links, railways (converted from Japanese to Russian gauge by 2019), and air connections.

## Terms
- **Tributary system**: A pre-modern political relationship where peripheral peoples or states acknowledged a central power's superiority through periodic gift-giving, in return for recognition and trade rights.
- **Karafuto**: The Japanese name for the southern part of Sakhalin when it was a prefecture of Japan (1905–1945).
- **Katorga**: A system of penal labor in the Russian Empire, notably used on Sakhalin.
- **Production-sharing agreement (PSA)**: A contract where a foreign company finances and operates a resource project, sharing production with the host government after cost recovery.
- **Regauging**: The process of converting railway tracks from one gauge (width between rails) to another, as Sakhalin's railways were changed from Japanese 1,067 mm to Russian 1,520 mm gauge.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The precise nature and extent of Qing dynasty control over Sakhalin is debated, with some scholars emphasizing its loose, tributary character versus more formalized administration.
- The environmental and economic trade-offs of large-scale oil and gas development on Sakhalin, particularly regarding projects like Sakhalin-II, remain contentious.
- While Japan no longer claims Sakhalin, the unresolved status of the southern Kuril Islands (Northern Territories) continues to affect Russo-Japanese relations.

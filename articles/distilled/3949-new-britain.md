# New Britain

## Overview
New Britain is the largest island in the Bismarck Archipelago, part of Papua New Guinea. It is a crescent-shaped, volcanically active island of significant size, roughly comparable to Taiwan. Its modern history is defined by successive colonial rule, strategic importance in two world wars, and a diverse indigenous population with complex cultural traditions.

- **11 September 1914** — Australian forces land, beginning the Battle of Rabaul and occupying the island for WWI.
- **1920** — League of Nations mandates the island to Australia as part of the Territory of New Guinea.
- **23 January 1942** — Japanese forces invade and capture Rabaul, using it as a major base until 1944.
- **1942-1944** — Allied forces, including U.S. Marines at Cape Gloucester, invade parts of the island to bypass Rabaul.
- **1994** — A major eruption of Tavurvur volcano buries the capital Rabaul under ash, leading to the capital's relocation to Kokopo.

## Body

### Geography and Administration
New Britain is a crescent-shaped island, approximately 520 km long and 35,140 km² in area. Its terrain features steep coastal cliffs, flat coral reef-bordered coasts, and a mountainous interior. The highest point is the active stratovolcano Mount Ulawun (2,334 m). The island is divided into two provinces: East New Britain (capital: Kokopo) and West New Britain (capital: Kimbe). A notable geological feature is the Nakanai Range, a limestone karst region with extensive caves and sinkholes nominated for World Heritage consideration.

### History and Colonial Era
Indigenous Papuan habitation dates back to at least 21,000 BC. The first recorded European contact was by William Dampier in 1700. In 1884, Germany established a protectorate, renaming the island Neupommern and making it part of German New Guinea. By 1909, the population was estimated at 190,000, with plantations established on the Gazelle Peninsula. Australia captured the island in 1914 during World War I and administered it as a mandate territory after 1920.

### World War II and Aftermath
Japan invaded New Britain in January 1942, capturing Rabaul and using it as a key base. Allied strategy focused on bypassing and isolating Rabaul rather than direct assault, involving invasions at points like Cape Gloucester. After the war, the island remained under Australian administration until Papua New Guinea's independence. A significant modern event was the 1994 volcanic eruption that buried Rabaul, forcing the relocation of the provincial capital to Kokopo.

### People, Culture, and Languages
The population consists of two main indigenous groups: Papuans, who have been there for millennia, and Austronesians, who arrived about 3,000 years ago. The Austronesians form the majority. The island is linguistically diverse, with about ten Papuan and forty Austronesian languages, alongside Tok Pisin and English. Cultural structures vary; for example, the Tolai people have a matrilineal society and maintain the active *dukduk* secret society.

### Ecology
New Britain hosts two ecoregions: lowland and montane rainforests. These forests face severe deforestation, primarily for oil palm plantations. Lowland forest loss has been particularly rapid, with nearly a quarter below 100 m disappearing between 1989 and 2000. While most forest bird species remain widespread, some forest-dependent species like the New Britain kingfisher are at risk if deforestation continues.

## Terms
- **Stratovolcano**: A conical volcano built up by many layers of hardened lava and tephra.
- **Karst**: A landscape formed from the dissolution of soluble rocks like limestone, characterized by sinkholes and caves.
- **Doline**: A closed depression or sinkhole in karst terrain.
- **Matrilineal**: A social system in which descent and inheritance are traced through the mother's line.
- **Patrilineal**: A social system in which descent and inheritance are traced through the father's line.
- **Tok Pisin**: An English-based creole language and one of the official languages of Papua New Guinea.
- **Mandated territory**: A territory administered by another country under a League of Nations mandate.

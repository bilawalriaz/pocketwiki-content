# Ernst Mach

## Overview
Ernst Mach (1838–1916) was an Austrian physicist and philosopher whose work bridged experimental science and philosophical critique. He is best known for describing shock waves, leading to the "Mach number" in fluid dynamics, and for developing a radical empiricist philosophy that influenced logical positivism and foreshadowed aspects of Einstein's relativity.

## Timeline
- **1838** — Born in Chrlice, Moravia (now part of Brno, Czech Republic).
- **1860** — Received doctorate in physics from the University of Vienna.
- **1864** — Became professor of mathematics at the University of Graz.
- **1867** — Appointed chair of experimental physics at Charles-Ferdinand University, Prague.
- **1887** — Published paper with Peter Salcher describing and photographing shock waves from supersonic projectiles.
- **1895–1901** — Held the chair for "history and philosophy of the inductive sciences" at the University of Vienna.
- **1897** — Publicly rejected the existence of atoms in a lecture.
- **1901** — Retired from Vienna; appointed to the Austrian House of Lords.
- **1913** — Moved to Vaterstetten, near Munich.
- **1916** — Died one day after his 78th birthday.

## Body

### Physics and Physiology
Mach's early work focused on optics and acoustics. His most famous physical contribution was the experimental confirmation of shock waves. In 1887, he and Peter Salcher used schlieren photography to visualize the conical shock wave formed by a projectile moving faster than sound. The ratio of an object's speed to the local speed of sound is named the Mach number. Independently of Josef Breuer, Mach also discovered in 1873 that the inner ear's semicircular canals control the sense of balance, a finding that may have influenced his later critique of absolute space.

### Philosophy of Science
Mach developed a philosophy called empirio-criticism, which holds that all we can know are our sensations. He saw scientific laws not as descriptions of an external reality, but as the "simplest and most economical abstract expression of facts." This led him to oppose atomic theory, as atoms were not directly observable. His insistence on describing phenomena in terms of relative motion, rather than absolute space and time, directly influenced Albert Einstein, who called Mach a precursor to general relativity. Mach's principle—that inertia arises from the distribution of matter in the universe—was cited by Einstein as a foundational idea, though Mach himself apparently rejected relativity theory before his death.

### Influence and Criticism
Mach's positivism was a major precursor to the Vienna Circle and logical positivism. However, his philosophy was also heavily criticized. Vladimir Lenin, in *Materialism and Empirio-criticism*, argued that Mach's view that bodies are "complexes of sensations" leads to solipsism (the idea that only one's own mind is sure to exist). Lenin accused Mach of plagiarizing George Berkeley and creating a philosophy that denies the objective existence of the external world.

## Terms
- **Mach number**: The ratio of the speed of a flow or object to the local speed of sound.
- **Empirio-criticism**: A philosophy developed by Mach and Richard Avenarius asserting that all knowledge is derived from sensory experience.
- **Phenomenalism**: The view that physical objects are permanent possibilities of sensation.
- **Mach's principle**: The hypothesis that inertia originates from the relationship between a body and all other matter in the universe.
- **Schlieren photography**: An optical technique used to visualize density variations in fluids, such as shock waves.
- **Mach bands**: An optical illusion where contrast at the edges of slightly different shades is exaggerated.
- **Solipsism**: The philosophical idea that only one's own mind is sure to exist.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Mach vs. Atomism**: Mach rejected atoms as unobservable and uneconomical, a position contradicted by the later success of atomic theory.
- **Mach's Principle and Relativity**: While Einstein was inspired by Mach's critique of absolute space, it is debated whether Mach's principle is fully incorporated into general relativity, and Mach himself did not accept Einstein's theory.
- **Philosophical Legacy**: Scholars debate whether Mach is best understood as a precursor to logical positivism, a radical constructivist, or simply a rigorous empiricist.

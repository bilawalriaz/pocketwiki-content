# Linus Pauling

## Overview
Linus Pauling (1901–1994) was an American chemist and peace activist whose work fundamentally shaped modern chemistry and molecular biology. He is one of only four individuals to win two Nobel Prizes and the only person to receive two unshared Nobel Prizes, in Chemistry (1954) and Peace (1962). His scientific contributions, particularly on the chemical bond and protein structure, provided the foundation for fields like quantum chemistry and molecular genetics.

- **August 19, 1994** — Died of prostate cancer in Big Sur, California.

## Body

### Early Life and Education
Pauling was born in Portland, Oregon, and developed an early interest in chemistry through a friend's lab kit. He attended Oregon State University (then Oregon Agricultural College), where he studied chemical engineering and was influenced by the work of Gilbert N. Lewis on chemical bonding. He completed his PhD at the California Institute of Technology (Caltech) in 1925, using X-ray diffraction to study crystal structures.

### Founding Quantum Chemistry
After a Guggenheim Fellowship in Europe studying quantum mechanics, Pauling returned to Caltech and applied quantum theory to chemical bonding. He introduced the concept of **orbital hybridization** (e.g., sp³ orbitals in methane) and the **Pauling Electronegativity Scale** (1932), which predicts bond character based on the electronegativity difference between atoms. His 1939 book, *The Nature of the Chemical Bond*, synthesized this work and became a foundational text in chemistry.

### Biological Molecules and Molecular Biology
Influenced by funding priorities, Pauling shifted to biological molecules. Using X-ray crystallography and model building, he and colleagues correctly proposed the **alpha helix** and **beta sheet** as the secondary structures of proteins in 1951. His earlier work on hemoglobin led to the 1949 discovery that sickle cell anemia is caused by an abnormal hemoglobin protein, making it the first "molecular disease" and a cornerstone of molecular genetics. His incorrect triple-helix model for DNA (1953) highlighted the competitive race to determine its structure, which was ultimately solved by Watson and Crick.

### Peace Activism and Political Controversy
Deeply affected by the atomic bomb, Pauling became a prominent peace activist. He campaigned against nuclear weapons testing, organizing a petition of scientists presented to the United Nations in 1958. His activism led to political persecution, including a 1952 passport denial by the U.S. State Department. He was awarded the 1962 Nobel Peace Prize for his efforts, which contributed to the Partial Test Ban Treaty of 1963. His political views drew criticism, and he was labeled a "fellow traveler" by some conservative publications.

### Later Years: Orthomolecular Medicine
In his later career, Pauling advocated for **orthomolecular medicine**, the theory that optimal concentrations of normal body substances (like vitamins) can treat disease. He promoted high-dose vitamin C for preventing colds and treating cancer, based on his own research and collaboration with Ewan Cameron. These claims were largely rejected by the mainstream medical community after clinical trials, including those by the Mayo Clinic, failed to confirm their efficacy.

## Terms
- **Orbital Hybridization**: The concept that atomic orbitals (like s and p) can mathematically combine to form new hybrid orbitals (e.g., sp³) that better describe bonding in molecules.
- **Electronegativity**: A measure of an atom's ability to attract shared electrons in a chemical bond. The Pauling scale provides numerical values for elements.
- **Alpha Helix & Beta Sheet**: Common secondary structures of proteins, stabilized by hydrogen bonds between atoms of the polypeptide backbone.
- **Molecular Disease**: A disease caused by an abnormal molecule, such as a mutant protein; sickle cell anemia was the first example.
- **Orthomolecular Medicine**: A controversial medical theory proposing that disease can be treated by varying the concentrations of substances normally present in the body, such as vitamins.
- **Resonance**: A quantum mechanical description where the true structure of a molecule (e.g., benzene) is a blend or superposition of multiple hypothetical structures.
- **Valence Bond Theory**: A model of chemical bonding where electrons are localized between atoms, with concepts like hybridization and resonance used to describe molecular structure.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Vitamin C Therapy**: The efficacy of high-dose vitamin C for treating cancer and the common cold remains a subject of debate, with most clinical trials showing no benefit, contrary to Pauling's claims.
- **Spheron Model of the Nucleus**: Pauling's alternative model for nuclear structure, based on clusters of nucleons, was not accepted by the mainstream physics community, which favors the independent-particle shell model.
- **Political Legacy**: The extent to which political persecution (e.g., passport denial) impacted his scientific work, particularly the DNA race, is debated; evidence suggests it was not the primary factor in his failure to determine the correct structure.

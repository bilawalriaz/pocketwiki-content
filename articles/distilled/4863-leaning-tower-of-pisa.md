# Leaning Tower of Pisa

## Overview
The Leaning Tower of Pisa is the freestanding bell tower (campanile) of Pisa Cathedral in Italy. It is a globally recognized architectural icon, famous for its unintended tilt caused by an unstable foundation. Its survival through centuries of leaning and earthquakes, along with modern stabilization efforts, makes it a significant case study in engineering and historical preservation.

## Timeline
- **1173** — Foundation laid on 9 August; construction begins on 14 August.
- **1178** — Tower begins to sink after the second floor is completed.
- **c. 1178–1272** — Construction halted for nearly a century due to Pisan wars.
- **1272** — Construction resumes under Giovanni di Simone; upper floors built with a curve to compensate for tilt.
- **1278** — Construction halted again after Pisan defeat by Genoa.
- **1319** — Seventh floor completed.
- **1372** — Bell-chamber added by Tommaso di Andrea Pisano.
- **1987** — Piazza del Duomo, including the tower, declared a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
- **1990** — Tower closed to the public; stabilization work begins.
- **1993–2001** — Remedial work using lead counterweights and soil extraction reduces tilt.
- **2001** — Tower reopened to the public.
- **2008** — Engineers announce the tower has been stabilized and stopped moving.

## Body
### Construction and Early Lean
Construction began in 1173. The tower started to lean after the second floor was completed in 1178 due to a shallow three-metre foundation on weak, unstable subsoil. A long construction pause (c. 1178–1272) allowed the soil to settle, preventing collapse. When work resumed in 1272, engineers built upper floors with one side taller than the other to compensate for the tilt, giving the tower its curved shape. The bell-chamber was finally added in 1372.

### Architectural Identity and Design
The architect is debated. The design was long attributed to Guglielmo and Bonanno Pisano, but a 2001 study suggests Diotisalvi was the original architect. The tower's circular shape and height were unusual for its time. The belfry, added last, is stylistically distinct and incorporates a 14 cm correction for the inclined axis below.

### Stabilization and Modern History
By 1990, the tilt had reached 5.5 degrees. The tower was closed, and stabilization efforts began, including adding 870 tonnes of lead counterweights and removing 70 metric tons of soil from beneath the raised end. This reduced the tilt to 3.97 degrees. The tower was reopened in 2001 and declared stable for at least 300 years. In 2008, it was announced that the tower had stopped moving for the first time in its history.

### Earthquake Survival and Records
The tower has survived at least four strong earthquakes since 1280. A 2018 study concluded its survival is due to dynamic soil-structure interaction, where the soft soil that caused the lean also prevents destructive resonance during quakes. Guinness World Records certified its tilt at 3.97 degrees, though other structures like the Capital Gate in Abu Dhabi have a greater, deliberate lean.

## Terms
- **Campanile**: A freestanding bell tower, especially one attached to a church.
- **Dynamic soil-structure interaction**: A phenomenon where the properties of the foundation soil influence a building's vibrational response to earthquakes, preventing resonance.
- **Blind arcade**: A decorative wall feature consisting of arches supported by columns that are attached to the wall.
- **Engaged column**: A column that is attached to a wall, projecting only slightly from it.
- **Corinthian capitals**: The ornate, leaf-decorated tops of columns in the classical Corinthian order.
- **UNESCO World Heritage Site**: A location with legal protection for its cultural or natural heritage, deemed of outstanding value to humanity.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The identity of the original architect remains controversial, with attributions to Guglielmo, Bonanno Pisano, and Diotisalvi.
- The primary source for Galileo's famous experiment dropping cannonballs from the tower is a biography written long after his death, leaving the story's historicity open to question.

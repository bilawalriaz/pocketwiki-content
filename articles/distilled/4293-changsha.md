# Changsha

## Overview
Changsha is the capital and largest city of Hunan Province, China, located in the lower Xiang River valley. With a history spanning over 2,400 years, it has evolved from an ancient Chu settlement into a modern national hub for transportation, education, and industry. Its significance lies in its role as a historical cultural center, a revolutionary base in modern Chinese history, and a rapidly growing economic powerhouse in central China.

## Timeline
- **c. 3000 BC** — Development begins with the proliferation of Longshan culture.
- **11th century BC** — The name "Changsha" first appears in historical records.
- **221–206 BC** — Under Qin dynasty, Changsha Commandery is established.
- **202 BC** — City walls built for the Han dynasty capital, Linxiang.
- **186–165 BC** — Mawangdui tombs constructed, containing well-preserved artifacts and texts.
- **907–951** — Changsha serves as the capital of the Southern Chu Kingdom.
- **976** — Yuelu Academy is founded, later becoming one of China's great academies.
- **1904** — City is opened to foreign trade following the Treaty of Shanghai.
- **1938** — The Wenxi Fire deliberately destroys much of the city before Japanese occupation.
- **1939–1942** — Three Battles of Changsha are fought between Chinese and Japanese forces.
- **2015** — Xiangjiang New Area, the first state-level new area in Central China, is established.

## Body

### Historical Development
Changsha's urban history dates to around 3000 BC. It was a key southern outpost of the Chu state during the Warring States period. Under the Han dynasty, it was the capital of the Changsha Kingdom, famous for the Mawangdui tombs which yielded exceptionally preserved remains and early copies of the *Tao Te Ching*. The city prospered as a trade center under the Tang dynasty, producing the world's earliest underglaze porcelain at the Tongguan Kiln. It served as the capital of the Southern Chu Kingdom in the 10th century. The Yuelu Academy, founded in 976, became a major intellectual center, with alumni like Zeng Guofan influencing national history.

### Modern History and Revolution
Changsha opened to foreign trade in 1904, leading to the construction of factories, churches, and schools. It became a cradle of revolution, with figures like Tan Sitong and Huang Xing establishing influential societies. Mao Zedong began his political career here as a student and teacher. The city suffered greatly during the Second Sino-Japanese War, enduring the deliberate 1938 Wenxi Fire and three major battles. After the Chinese Civil War, it recovered and accelerated development from the 1990s onward, becoming a core city in the Yangtze River Economic Belt.

### Economy and Industry
Changsha is a major economic center, classified as a new first-tier city and a Beta- (global second-tier) city. Its GDP per capita exceeded CN¥144,525 in 2024, indicating high-income status. Known as the "Construction Machinery Capital of the World," its key industries include construction machinery, new materials, automobiles, and electronics. The Xiangjiang New Area was established in 2015. The city has a prominent media industry, is home to the influential Hunan Broadcasting System, and was named the first "UNESCO City of Media Arts" in China.

### Education and Research
Changsha is a major educational hub, hosting 62 institutions of higher education as of 2025. It is home to four Double First-Class universities: Hunan University, Central South University, the National University of Defense Technology, and Hunan Normal University. The city is a significant center for research and innovation, ranking 23rd globally in scientific research outputs in 2024. It is the birthplace of super hybrid rice, the Tianhe-1 supercomputer, and China's first domestic medium-low speed maglev line.

### Geography and Climate
Located in northeastern Hunan, Changsha covers an area of 11,819 km². The Xiang River is its main waterway, dividing the city into commercial east and cultural-west banks. The climate is humid subtropical, with hot, rainy summers and chilly, overcast winters. The annual average temperature is 17.8°C.

## Terms
- **Changzhutan City Cluster**: The metropolitan region comprising Changsha, Zhuzhou, and Xiangtan.
- **Double First-Class**: A Chinese government initiative to develop world-class universities and disciplines.
- **Mawangdui**: A Han dynasty tomb site near Changsha famous for its well-preserved artifacts and texts.
- **New First-Tier City**: A classification for major Chinese cities with high economic potential and influence.
- **Tongguan Kiln**: A Tang dynasty kiln in Changsha that produced the world's earliest underglaze porcelain.
- **Xiang River**: The major river flowing through Changsha and Hunan Province.
- **Yuelu Academy**: One of China's four great ancient academies, founded in 976, now part of Hunan University.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The precise origin of the name "Changsha" is unknown, though it originally referred to the region rather than the settlement.
- The link between the city's early development around 3000 BC and the Longshan culture is noted as having "no firm evidence."

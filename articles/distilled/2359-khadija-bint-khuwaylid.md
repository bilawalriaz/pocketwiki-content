# Khadija bint Khuwaylid

## Overview
Khadija bint Khuwaylid (c. 554–619 CE) was the first wife of the Islamic prophet Muhammad and the first person to accept Islam. A wealthy and respected merchant from the Quraysh tribe, she played a crucial role in the early establishment of the Muslim community through her financial support and personal encouragement of Muhammad's prophetic mission.

## Timeline
- **c. 554** — Born into the aristocratic Quraysh clan in Mecca.
- **Before 595** — Marries three times and becomes a successful merchant.
- **c. 595** — Hires Muhammad to manage a trade caravan to Syria; impressed, she proposes marriage.
- **610** — Becomes the first convert to Islam after Muhammad's first revelation.
- **616–619** — Supports the Muslim community during the Quraysh's trade boycott of the Hashim clan.
- **November 619** — Dies in the "Year of Sorrow," buried in Mecca's al-Mu'alla cemetery.

## Body

### Early Life and Profession
Khadija was born into the Quraysh tribe; her father was a merchant and leader, and her mother was a third cousin of Muhammad's mother. She became a highly successful merchant, with her trade caravans reportedly equaling those of all other Quraysh traders combined. Known for her nobility, she did not worship idols, which was unusual for the time. She employed others to trade on her behalf, including the young Muhammad, whose honest conduct on a Syrian caravan earned him great respect.

### Marriage to Muhammad
After Muhammad's successful trade mission, Khadija, through a friend, proposed marriage to him. Muhammad, lacking financial resources, agreed, and the marriage was formalized with the consent of their uncles. The couple had six children: two sons, Qasim and Abd Allah (both died young), and four daughters—Zaynab, Ruqayya, Umm Kulthum, and Fatima. Two other children, Ali ibn Abi Talib and Zayd ibn Harithah, also lived in their household.

### Conversion and Support for Islam
Khadija was the first person to accept Islam after Muhammad reported his first revelation from the Angel Gabriel. She comforted him and affirmed his prophethood, possibly with confirmation from her Christian cousin, Waraqah ibn Nawfal. She used her wealth to support the early Muslim community, ransoming slaves and providing food during persecution. Her steadfast support was vital during the Quraysh's boycott of the Hashim clan (616–619).

### Death and Legacy
Khadija died in November 619, a year Muhammad called the "Year of Sorrow" due to the loss of both her and his uncle Abu Talib. She is buried in Mecca's al-Mu'alla cemetery. Muslims honor her as "Mother of the Believers" and one of the four "Ladies of Heaven," alongside her daughter Fatima, Mary, and Asiya.

## Terms
- **Quraysh:** The dominant Arab tribe in Mecca during Muhammad's time.
- **Revelation:** The communication of God's message to a prophet; Muhammad's first occurred in the Cave of Hira.
- **Year of Sorrow:** The Islamic year (c. 619 CE) marking the deaths of both Khadija and Abu Talib.
- **Al-Mu'alla:** A historic cemetery in Mecca where Khadija is buried.
- **Mother of the Believers:** An honorific title for Muhammad's wives, especially Khadija.
- **Jahiliyyah:** The pre-Islamic period in Arabia characterized by polytheism and tribal customs.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Number of Marriages:** Sunnis generally accept Khadija had three marriages before Muhammad, while some Shia sources argue she was a virgin at the time of her marriage to him.
- **Age at Marriage:** Sources conflict on whether Khadija was about 40 or approximately 28 when she married Muhammad.
- **Number of Children:** Historical sources disagree on whether Muhammad and Khadija had six, seven, or eight children, with the identity of some daughters being debated between Sunni and Shia traditions.

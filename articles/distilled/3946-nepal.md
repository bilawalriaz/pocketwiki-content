# Nepal

## Overview
Nepal is a landlocked, multi-ethnic Himalayan nation between India and China. Its strategic location and dramatic geography have shaped its history as a buffer state, its diverse culture, and its modern challenges as a federal democratic republic.

## Timeline
- **c. 563–483 BC** — Gautama Buddha is traditionally born in Lumbini.
- **c. 250 BC** — Mauryan Emperor Ashoka visits Lumbini, erecting a pillar.
- **c. 400 AD** — The Licchavi dynasty rises to power in the Kathmandu Valley.
- **1769** — Prithvi Narayan Shah of Gorkha conquers the Kathmandu Valley, beginning unification.
- **1815–16** — The Anglo-Nepali War ends with the Sugauli Treaty, ceding territory.
- **1846** — The Kot massacre leads to the rise of the Rana dynasty.
- **1951** — The Rana regime is overthrown; parliamentary democracy is introduced.
- **1960** — King Mahendra suspends democracy, establishing a partyless Panchayat system.
- **1990** — The People's Movement forces the restoration of multiparty democracy.
- **1996–2006** — The Nepalese Civil War is fought between Maoist insurgents and the state.
- **2008** — Nepal is declared a federal republic, ending the monarchy.
- **2015** — A new constitution establishes Nepal as a federal parliamentary republic with seven provinces.

## Body

### Etymology and Early History
The origin of "Nepal" is uncertain, with theories linking it to Hindu or Buddhist mythology or Tibeto-Burman roots. The earliest modern human remains in South Asia date to about 30,000 years ago, with similar evidence in Nepal. The Kathmandu Valley was the seat of the Newar confederacy, Nepal Mandala, a prosperous hub on the Himalayan Silk Road, intertwined with Tibetan and Indo-Aryan cultures.

### Unification and the Kingdom
In the 18th century, the Gorkha Kingdom unified Nepal. The Shah dynasty allied with the British Empire, with Nepal serving as a buffer between Imperial China and British India. Internal factionalism led to the 1846 Kot massacre and the Rana dynasty's rule as hereditary prime ministers until 1951. Parliamentary democracy was introduced that year but twice suspended by monarchs. A Maoist-led civil war ended with the establishment of a secular republic in 2008.

### Government and Politics
Nepal is a federal parliamentary republic with a multi-party system. The government has executive, legislative, and judicial branches. The Federal Parliament includes a House of Representatives and a National Assembly. The country is divided into seven provinces, each with a unicameral legislature. Major parties include the Communist Party of Nepal (Unified Marxist–Leninist) and the Nepali Congress. Foreign policy emphasizes neutrality and non-alignment, balancing relations with India and China. Nepal is a founding member of SAARC and a UN peacekeeping contributor.

### Geography and Biodiversity
Nepal has three physiographic belts: the high Himal, middle hills (Pahad), and southern lowland plains (Terai). It contains eight of the world's ten highest mountains, including Mount Everest. The country lies in a seismically active zone from the Indian-Eurasian plate collision. Nepal has disproportionate biodiversity, hosting 4.0% of mammal and 8.9% of bird species. National parks and protected areas cover 19.67% of its land.

### Economy and Infrastructure
Nepal is one of the least developed countries, with an economy reliant on agriculture, services, and remittances. Tourism contributes 7.9% of GDP. The country has significant hydroelectric potential but has exploited only a fraction. Infrastructure is limited; roads are often inoperable during monsoons, and the national airline is in poor condition. Nepal depends heavily on foreign aid and imports, with India as its largest trading partner.

### Demographics and Culture
Nepal is a multi-ethnic state with 125 ethnic groups and 123 mother tongues. Nepali is the official language. The population is predominantly Hindu (81.3%), with Buddhist (9%) and Muslim (4.4%) minorities. Traditional society is hierarchical with a caste system, though untouchability is illegal. Culture is rich in festivals, art, and architecture, with pagoda-style temples and woodcraft. Traditional clothing includes the sari for women and daura-suruwal for men.

## Terms
- **Buffer State**: A country between rival powers, whose existence prevents conflict.
- **Licchavi Dynasty**: A ruling dynasty in the Kathmandu Valley from c. 400 AD.
- **Rana Dynasty**: Hereditary prime ministers who ruled Nepal from 1846 to 1951.
- **Panchayat System**: A partyless political system established in 1962 by King Mahendra.
- **Maoist Insurgency**: A violent campaign (1996–2006) to overthrow the monarchy.
- **SAARC**: The South Asian Association for Regional Cooperation, headquartered in Kathmandu.
- **Terai**: Nepal's southern lowland plains, part of the Indo-Gangetic Plain.
- **Newar**: An ethnolinguistic group indigenous to the Kathmandu Valley.
- **Gurkha**: A term for Nepali soldiers, renowned for service in British and Indian armies.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The precise etymology of "Nepal" remains uncertain.
- The origins of the earliest inhabitants of the Kathmandu Valley are debated.
- The effectiveness of Nepal's federal structure and power balance are ongoing challenges.
- Economic development debates focus on hydropower, tourism, and managing relations with India and China.

# Volgograd

## Overview
Volgograd is a major Russian city on the Volga River, historically known as Tsaritsyn and Stalingrad. Its significance stems from its role as a strategic fortress, a commercial hub, and, most notably, the site of the pivotal Battle of Stalingrad during World War II, a turning point in the conflict. The city's identity is deeply intertwined with its turbulent history and the political regimes that have shaped it.

- **10 Apr 1925** — City renamed Stalingrad in honor of Joseph Stalin.

- **2 Feb 1943** — German forces surrender, ending the Battle of Stalingrad.

- **2018** — Host city for the FIFA World Cup.
- **2025** — Airport renamed Stalingrad International Airport by presidential decree.

## Body

### Tsaritsyn and Early Development
The city was founded as the fortress of Tsaritsyn in 1589 at the confluence of the Tsaritsa and Volga rivers. It served as a military outpost and trading settlement, enduring revolts and raids. By the 19th century, its role as a river-port and commercial center drove rapid population growth, from under 3,000 in 1807 to about 84,000 in 1900. This growth outpaced infrastructure, leading to severe public health crises, including cholera epidemics (1907-1910) that killed over 1,000 people. The city's social and economic tensions made it a hotbed of revolutionary activity.

### Stalingrad and World War II
Renamed Stalingrad in 1925, the city became a major industrial center under Soviet rule. During World War II, it was the site of the Battle of Stalingrad (August 23, 1942 – February 2, 1943), the deadliest single battle in history with estimated casualties between 1.25 and 2.5 million. The battle began with massive aerial bombardment and devolved into brutal urban combat. By early November, German forces controlled 90% of the city, but a Soviet counteroffensive on November 19 encircled the German Sixth Army. The German surrender on January 31, 1943, marked a major turning point in the war. The battle destroyed 99% of the city and left only 1,515 of its pre-war population of over half a million.

### Volgograd and Modern Era
In 1961, the city was renamed Volgograd under Nikita Khrushchev's de-Stalinization policy, a move that remains controversial due to the city's WWII symbolism. Proposals to revert the name have surfaced periodically, with strong local support. In 2013, the city council allowed the ceremonial use of "Hero City Stalingrad" on specific historical dates. The city was rebuilt after the war and now features major memorials like "The Motherland Calls" statue. It hosted 2018 FIFA World Cup matches and remains an important industrial, transportation, and educational hub.

## Terms
- **De-Stalinization:** The political reform process initiated by Nikita Khrushchev after Stalin's death, aimed at reducing the cult of personality and reversing Stalinist policies.
- **Hero City:** An honorary title awarded to Soviet cities for outstanding heroism during World War II.
- **Battle of Stalingrad:** The pivotal WWII battle (1942-43) between Nazi Germany and the Soviet Union for control of Stalingrad, resulting in a decisive Soviet victory.
- **Volga-Don Canal:** A shipping canal opened in 1952 connecting the Volga and Don rivers in southern Russia.
- **Mamayev Kurgan:** A dominant hill in Volgograd, site of intense fighting during the Battle of Stalingrad, now home to a major memorial complex.
- **Floating Church:** A self-propelled or towed boat converted into a mobile chapel, used to serve remote communities along Russia's rivers.

## Debates and Open Questions
The most prominent debate concerns the city's name. While officially Volgograd since 1961, there is significant local and political support for reverting to Stalingrad to honor its WWII legacy. A 2013 petition to President Putin for a permanent name change received 50,000 signatures; Putin suggested a local referendum should precede any change. The 2025 renaming of the airport to Stalingrad International Airport indicates the ongoing symbolic importance of the historical name.

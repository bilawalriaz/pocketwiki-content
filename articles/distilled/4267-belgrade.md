# Belgrade

## Overview
Belgrade is the capital and largest city of Serbia, strategically located at the confluence of the Sava and Danube rivers. As one of Europe's oldest continuously inhabited cities, its history reflects the turbulent political and cultural shifts of the Balkans, from prehistoric settlements to its role as a modern capital and regional hub.

## Timeline
- **6th millennium BC** — Vinča culture, a major prehistoric farming society, develops in the Belgrade area.
- **279 BC** — Celtic tribe Scordisci capture the settlement, naming it Singidūn.
- **Mid-2nd century AD** — Roman city is granted *municipium* status.
- **1284** — King Stefan Dragutin receives Belgrade, making it the first Serbian king to rule the city.
- **1427** — Đurađ Branković returns Belgrade to Hungary, making Smederevo the Serbian capital.
- **1456** — Ottoman siege of Belgrade is successfully defended by a Christian army led by John Hunyadi.
- **1521** — Ottomans under Suleiman the Magnificent conquer Belgrade.
- **1841** — Prince Mihailo Obrenović moves the capital of the Principality of Serbia from Kragujevac to Belgrade.
- **1918** — Belgrade becomes the capital of the newly formed Kingdom of Serbs, Croats and Slovenes.
- **1941** — City is heavily bombed by the Luftwaffe; Yugoslavia is invaded and occupied.
- **1944** — Belgrade is liberated by the Red Army and Yugoslav Partisans.
- **1961** — Belgrade hosts the first Non-Aligned Movement Summit.
- **1999** — NATO bombing campaign targets buildings in Belgrade during the Kosovo War.
- **2000** — Massive protests lead to the ousting of President Slobodan Milošević.

## Body

### Ancient and Medieval Foundations
Belgrade's area was inhabited in the Palaeolithic era. The Neolithic Starčevo culture (c. 6200–5200 BC) was succeeded by the sophisticated Vinča culture (c. 5500–4500 BC), known for large settlements and early copper metallurgy. In antiquity, the region was home to Thracian and Dacian tribes. After a Celtic invasion in 279 BC, the settlement was named Singidūn. Conquered by Romans, it became the romanized *Singidunum*, a *colonia* by the late 2nd century AD. Slavs settled the area in the 520s, and the city's name evolved into Belograd (first recorded in 878). For centuries, it was a contested prize between the Byzantine Empire, the Bulgarian Empire, and the Kingdom of Hungary. It became a Serbian royal seat in 1284 and the capital of the Serbian Despotate in the early 15th century, flourishing under Despot Stefan Lazarević.

### Ottoman and Habsburg Rule
The Ottomans captured Belgrade in 1521, making it the seat of the Sanjak of Smederevo and a major European city. Ottoman rule introduced new architecture and demographics. The city was occupied and substantially razed by the Habsburgs three times (1688–1690, 1717–1739, 1789–1791) before being recaptured by the Ottomans. This period saw the Great Serbian Migrations, where Serbs retreated into the Habsburg Empire.

### Modern Capital and Yugoslav Era
Following the Serbian Revolution, Belgrade was re-established as Serbia's capital in 1841. After full independence in 1878, it grew rapidly. It became the capital of the Kingdom of Serbs, Croats and Slovenes (later Yugoslavia) after World War I. During World War II, the city was bombed by both the Axis and Allies and suffered under Nazi occupation. Liberated in 1944, it became the capital of socialist Yugoslavia under Josip Broz Tito. The post-war period saw rapid industrialization, the construction of New Belgrade, and the city's role as a host for major international events like the 1961 Non-Aligned Movement summit. The breakup of Yugoslavia in the 1990s brought protests, international sanctions, and the 1999 NATO bombing. After the 2000 revolution, Belgrade has undergone significant urban renewal and economic development.

## Terms
- **Vinča culture**: A major Neolithic archaeological culture in Southeast Europe, centered near Belgrade, known for large settlements and early metallurgy.
- **Singidūn**: The Celtic and later Roman name for the settlement that became Belgrade.
- **Sanjak of Smederevo**: An Ottoman administrative unit, with Belgrade as its seat after 1521.
- **Despotate**: The final medieval Serbian state, with Belgrade as its capital in the early 15th century.
- **Primate city**: A city that is disproportionately larger and more influential than others in its country, serving as its economic, political, and cultural center.
- **Banovina**: A province of the Kingdom of Yugoslavia (1929–1941).
- **Non-Aligned Movement**: A forum of states not formally aligned with any major power bloc, founded in Belgrade in 1961.
- **Splav**: A floating river barge, often used as a nightclub or restaurant, characteristic of Belgrade's nightlife.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The exact population of Belgrade's metropolitan area is debated, as it has not been formally defined statistically or administratively and sprawls into neighboring municipalities.
- The city's strategic location at a river confluence and crossroads of plains and mountains is cited as the reason it has been "battled over in 115 wars and razed 44 times," making its historical resilience a key scholarly and cultural point of discussion.

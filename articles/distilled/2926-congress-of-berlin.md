# Congress of Berlin

## Overview
The Congress of Berlin (June–July 1878) was a diplomatic conference where the major European powers revised the Treaty of San Stefano, which had ended the Russo-Turkish War of 1877–78. It was convened to prevent a wider European war by redrawing the map of the Balkans, primarily to counter Russian influence and preserve the Ottoman Empire as a regional counterbalance. The resulting Treaty of Berlin averted immediate conflict but sowed seeds of future instability by frustrating nationalist aspirations and deepening great power rivalries.

## Timeline
- **March 1878** — Treaty of San Stefano ends the Russo-Turkish War, creating a large, Russian-aligned "Greater Bulgaria."
- **4 June 1878** — Britain and the Ottoman Empire secretly sign the Cyprus Convention, allowing British occupation of Cyprus.
- **13 June – 13 July 1878** — Congress of Berlin is held, mediated by German Chancellor Otto von Bismarck.
- **13 July 1878** — Treaty of Berlin is signed, dismantling Greater Bulgaria and reorganizing southeastern Europe.
- **1878** — Romania, Serbia, and Montenegro gain formal independence; Austria-Hungary occupies Bosnia and Herzegovina.
- **1881** — Greece gains Thessaly and the Arta Prefecture from the Ottoman Empire after further negotiations.
- **1908** — Austria-Hungary formally annexes Bosnia and Herzegovina.
- **1912–1913** — Balkan Wars erupt, fueled by unresolved tensions from the Congress.
- **1914** — Assassination of Archduke Franz Ferdinand in Sarajevo triggers World War I.

## Body

### Background and Rivalries
In the decades before the Congress, Pan-Slavism—a movement to unite Balkan Slavs under Russian leadership—threatened the Ottoman Empire and alarmed other powers. Russia sought to expand its influence southward, while Britain aimed to prevent Russian dominance in the Mediterranean. Austria-Hungary, blocked from expanding southwest by Italian and German unifications, also sought Balkan influence. Germany, the strongest continental power after 1871, had little direct interest and was positioned to mediate.

### The Treaty of San Stefano and the Call for a Congress
Following the Russo-Turkish War, the Treaty of San Stefano created a large, autonomous Bulgarian principality under Russian protection, vastly expanding Russia's sphere of influence. This alarmed Britain, France, and Austria-Hungary. To avoid war, particularly from Britain, which threatened conflict if Russia seized Constantinople, German Chancellor Otto von Bismarck was asked to mediate. He called the Congress to revise San Stefano and preserve the balance of power.

### The Congress and Its Outcomes
Delegates from the six great powers (Britain, France, Austria-Hungary, Germany, Italy, Russia), the Ottoman Empire, and representatives from Greece, Serbia, Romania, and Montenegro attended. Bismarck, hosting the Congress, aimed to maintain European peace and Germany's alliances. The Treaty of Berlin dismantled Greater Bulgaria, dividing it into a smaller Principality of Bulgaria and the autonomous province of Eastern Rumelia, both under Ottoman suzerainty. It recognized the full independence of Romania, Serbia, and Montenegro, though with territorial adjustments. Austria-Hungary gained the right to occupy and administer Bosnia and Herzegovina and garrison the Sanjak of Novi Pazar. Britain acquired Cyprus. The settlement was a diplomatic victory for Britain and Austria-Hungary, which had not fought in the war.

### Reactions and Long-Term Consequences
The results were widely resented. Russia felt humiliated despite winning the war. The new Balkan states—Bulgaria, Serbia, and Greece—received far less territory than they believed they deserved. The settlement deepened the Anglo-Russian "Great Game" rivalry and damaged the League of Three Emperors (Germany, Austria-Hungary, Russia). Austria-Hungary's administration of Bosnia-Herzegovina angered South Slavs and contributed to the assassination of Archduke Franz Ferdinand in 1914. The unresolved nationalist grievances and great power competition in the Balkans directly contributed to the Balkan Wars (1912–1913) and World War I.

## Terms
*   **Pan-Slavism:** A political movement advocating the unity of all Slavic peoples, often under Russian leadership.
*   **Treaty of San Stefano (1878):** The peace treaty ending the Russo-Turkish War, which created a large Bulgarian state and was later revised by the Congress of Berlin.
*   **Greater Bulgaria:** The large, autonomous Bulgarian principality envisioned by the Treaty of San Stefano, which was dismantled at the Congress of Berlin.
*   **League of Three Emperors:** An 1873 alliance between Germany, Austria-Hungary, and Russia to preserve monarchies, which was undermined by the Congress.
*   **Cyprus Convention (1878):** A secret agreement allowing Britain to occupy and administer the island of Cyprus in exchange for protecting the Ottoman Empire against Russia.
*   **Sanjak of Novi Pazar:** A region between Serbia and Montenegro that Austria-Hungary was allowed to garrison, preventing the two Slavic states from uniting.
*   **Eastern Rumelia:** An autonomous Ottoman province created from part of the former Bulgarian state at the Congress.
*   **Realpolitik:** A political system based on practical and material factors rather than theoretical or ethical objectives, associated with Bismarck's diplomacy.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Bismarck's Role:** Historians debate whether Bismarck's mediation was truly impartial or if it primarily served German interests by aligning Germany more closely with Austria-Hungary.
*   **Long-Term Impact:** Scholars analyze whether the Congress's settlement was a necessary, if flawed, compromise to prevent immediate war, or a short-sighted arrangement that guaranteed future conflict by inflaming nationalist tensions.
*   **Russian Humiliation:** The degree to which the Congress's outcome directly caused the breakdown of the League of Three Emperors and shaped subsequent Russian foreign policy is a subject of ongoing historical analysis.

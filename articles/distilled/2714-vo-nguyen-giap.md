# Võ Nguyên Giáp

## Overview
Võ Nguyên Giáp (1911–2013) was a Vietnamese general and communist revolutionary who served as the military commander of the Việt Minh and the People's Army of Vietnam (PAVN) from 1941 to 1972. He is renowned for leading Vietnamese forces to victory in the First Indochina War against France and for his central role in the Vietnam War against South Vietnam and the United States. His career exemplifies the application of protracted "revolutionary war" strategy against technologically superior opponents.

## Timeline
- **25 August 1911** — Born in Quảng Bình province, French Indochina.
- **1925** — Began anti-colonial political activity.
- **1940** — Exiled to China; joined Ho Chi Minh.
- **December 1944** — Formed the first Armed Propaganda Brigade, the nucleus of the PAVN.
- **2 September 1945** — Ho Chi Minh declares Vietnamese independence; Giáp becomes Minister of the Interior.
- **19 December 1946** — First Indochina War begins with Vietnamese declaration of war on France.
- **7 May 1954** — French surrender at Battle of Điện Biên Phủ, ending the First Indochina War.
- **1955–1991** — Served as Deputy Prime Minister of North Vietnam and later unified Vietnam.
- **1968** — Tet Offensive launched; Giáp opposed its implementation.
- **1972** — Replaced as field commander after the Easter Offensive; remained Defense Minister.
- **30 April 1975** — Fall of Saigon; end of the Vietnam War.
- **4 October 2013** — Died in Hanoi at age 102.

## Body

### Early Life and Political Activism
Born to an affluent peasant family, Giáp's father was a nationalist who died in French prison. Giáp was educated at the French-run Quốc Học in Huế, where he met future South Vietnamese President Ngô Đình Diệm and future North Vietnamese leader Ho Chi Minh (then Nguyễn Sinh Cung). He joined anti-colonial movements, was imprisoned for protests, and studied law and political economy at the Indochinese University in Hanoi. He worked as a history teacher and journalist while studying military theory, particularly the works of Sun Tzu and T.E. Lawrence. In 1940, he went into exile in China, where he joined Ho Chi Minh and studied Chinese Communist strategy.

### Building the Viet Minh and First Indochina War
In 1942, Giáp returned to Vietnam to build the Việt Minh resistance. In December 1944, he formed its first military unit, the Armed Propaganda Brigade. After the Japanese surrender in 1945, he led forces into Hanoi. Following the French return and the outbreak of the First Indochina War in 1946, Giáp transformed the Việt Minh from a guerrilla force into a conventional army. His masterpiece was the 1954 Battle of Điện Biên Phủ. Defying convention, he placed artillery on forward mountain slopes and used a trench system to neutralize French advantages, leading to a decisive French surrender that ended the war.

### Vietnam War and Later Career
As Defense Minister and PAVN commander, Giáp oversaw the army's expansion during the Vietnam War. He opposed the 1968 Tet Offensive, favoring protracted guerrilla warfare, but was overruled by other Politburo leaders. After the failed 1972 Easter Offensive, he was replaced as field commander by Văn Tiến Dũng but remained Defense Minister until 1980. He oversaw the 1978 Vietnamese invasion of Cambodia and the 1979 Sino-Vietnamese War. He retired from the Politburo in 1982 and as Deputy Prime Minister in 1991, later becoming a critic of bauxite mining.

## Terms
- **Việt Minh**: The Vietnam Independence League, a nationalist coalition led by the Indochinese Communist Party, which Giáp helped build into a military force.
- **PAVN**: People's Army of Vietnam, the regular army of North Vietnam, which Giáp commanded and transformed.
- **Protracted War**: A military strategy of avoiding decisive battles, wearing down a stronger enemy over time through guerrilla tactics and political mobilization.
- **Revolutionary War**: A concept of warfare where military struggle is integrated with political and diplomatic efforts, aiming to break the enemy's will rather than achieve purely military victory.
- **Ho Chi Minh Trail**: The logistical network from North to South Vietnam through Laos and Cambodia, which Giáp was the principal architect of.
- **Tet Offensive**: A major 1968 military campaign by North Vietnamese and Viet Cong forces during the Vietnam War, which Giáp opposed.
- **Easter Offensive**: A 1972 conventional military offensive by North Vietnam against the South, which was repelled with high casualties.
- **Điện Biên Phủ**: The site of the decisive 1954 battle where Giáp's forces defeated the French, leading to the end of the First Indochina War.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Credit for Victory**: While often credited with North Vietnam's victory over the U.S. and South Vietnam, recent scholarship suggests other leaders like Văn Tiến Dũng and Lê Duẩn had more direct military responsibility for the final campaigns.
- **Tet Offensive**: Giáp's role is debated; he opposed the offensive and left for medical treatment, but later defended it as a successful political and psychological strategy.
- **Military Genius vs. Recklessness**: Critics like William Westmoreland argued Giáp showed disregard for human life, while defenders like Derek Frisby and Henry Kissinger contend he masterfully applied protracted war strategy, understanding that political will, not battlefield attrition, was decisive.

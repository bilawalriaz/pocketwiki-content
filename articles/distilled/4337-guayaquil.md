# Guayaquil

## Overview
Guayaquil, officially Santiago de Guayaquil, is Ecuador's largest city, economic capital, and principal port. Located on the Guayas River near the Pacific Ocean, it is a major hub for trade, culture, and finance, handling about 70% of the country's private exports and 83% of its imports. Its history as a colonial shipyard and its role in independence movements have shaped its modern identity as a dynamic, densely populated metropolis.

## Timeline
- **1538** — Founded by Francisco de Orellana as "Muy Noble y Muy Leal Ciudad de Santiago de Guayaquil."
- **1547** — Definitively established as a shipyard and port for the Spanish Empire.
- **1687** — Attacked and looted by English and French pirates.
- **1709** — Looted again by English captains; an epidemic of yellow fever caused them to depart without ransom.
- **9 October 1820** — Declared independence from Spain, becoming the "Provincia Libre de Guayaquil."
- **1822** — Site of the Guayaquil Conference between Generals José de San Martín and Simón Bolívar.
- **1829** — Invaded and occupied by the Peruvian Army for seven months.
- **1860** — Site of the Battle of Guayaquil, countering Peruvian influence.
- **1896** — Large portions of the city destroyed by a fire.
- **1922** — General strike ends after military and police kill at least 300 people.
- **2006** — First rapid transit bus system, Metrovía, opens.
- **2020** — Hit hard by the COVID-19 pandemic, with overwhelmed services.

## Body

### Nomenclature and Founding
The name "Guayaquil" is pre-Hispanic. A romantic legend attributes it to a leader named Guayas and his wife Quil, who resisted the Spanish. Historical research points to a pre-Hispanic town ruled by a man named Guayaquile. The Spanish founded the city in 1538, naming it for their patron saint, Santiago. After independence in 1820, the honorific "Muy Noble y Muy Leal" was dropped.

### Colonial Era and Economy
Guayaquil was the chief Spanish shipyard in the Pacific and a key stopover in the Manila galleon trade between Asia and Latin America. In the late 18th century, it had a large enslaved population. Many enslaved people, like María Chiquinquirá, fought for freedom through the courts. By the 19th century, freed slaves formed a social group known as the *plebe*.

### Independence and Political Role
Guayaquil was the first Ecuadorian city to achieve definitive independence from Spain in 1820. It served as the capital of the Free Province of Guayaquil before being annexed to Gran Colombia. The city was the starting point for the campaign that led to the Battle of Pichincha, confirming the independence of Gran Colombia and the future Ecuador. It has since played a significant economic and political role within the Republic of Ecuador.

### Modern Economy and Port
Guayaquil is Ecuador's commercial heart, with an economy based on trade, business, agriculture, and aquaculture. The Port of Guayaquil is the nation's most important commercial port. In recent decades, urban regeneration projects like the Malecón 2000 waterfront and the Metrovía transit system have modernized the city. The port has also become a significant location in the Latin American drug trade following the dissolution of FARC in Colombia.

### Geography and Climate
The city is located on the west bank of the Guayas River, about 60 km from the Gulf of Guayaquil. It faces major earthquake and tsunami threats due to its location on the ring of fire and its weak, compressible soil. The climate is tropical savanna (Köppen: Aw), with a hot, rainy season from January to April and a drier period from May to December.

## Terms
- **Jornal**: A practice where enslaved people had considerable independence in exchange for paying their owners a daily fee.
- **Plebe**: A social group formed by enslaved people who had fought for and won their freedom in the late 18th and early 19th centuries.
- **Malecón**: A waterfront promenade; the renovated "Malecón 2000" is a major tourist attraction.
- **Metrovía**: Guayaquil's bus rapid transit system, opened in 2006.
- **El Clásico del Astillero**: The name for the football rivalry match between Barcelona Sporting Club and Club Sport Emelec.
- **Encebollado**: A traditional seafood soup, a staple of Guayaquil cuisine.
- **Chifa**: Chinese-Ecuadorian fusion cuisine, common in Guayaquil.
- **Tsunami detachment systems**: Major east-west trending geological fault systems (e.g., Posorja, Jambeli) in the Gulf of Guayaquil that contribute to seismic risk.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The origin of the name "Guayaquil" is debated, with theories ranging from a romantic legend about a leader and his wife to historical references to a pre-Hispanic ruler named Guayaquile.
- The relative importance of Guayaquil versus Valdivia (now in Chile) as the chief Spanish shipyard in the Pacific was noted by some contemporary navigators.

# Great Pyramid of Giza

## Overview
The Great Pyramid of Giza is the largest and oldest of the Seven Wonders of the Ancient World, built as the tomb for Pharaoh Khufu during Egypt's Old Kingdom. It remained the tallest human-made structure for over 3,700 years and is the only ancient wonder still largely intact, making it a monumental achievement in engineering and a key source for understanding ancient Egyptian civilization.

## Timeline
- **c. 2600 BC** — Construction of the Great Pyramid begins under Pharaoh Khufu.
- **c. 2570 BC** — Construction completed after about 26 years.
- **381–384 AD** — Christian pilgrim Egeria identifies pyramids as "Joseph's granaries."
- **c. 820 AD** — Caliph al-Ma'mun forces an entry into the pyramid.
- **1435 AD** — Cyriacus of Ancona rediscoveries the pyramid's true nature.
- **1837** — Howard Vyse discovers relieving chambers with Khufu's name in graffiti.
- **1954** — The Khufu ship is discovered in a sealed boat pit.
- **2013** — The Diary of Merer papyri, documenting limestone transport, are found.
- **2017** — The ScanPyramids project detects a large void above the Grand Gallery.

## Body

### Purpose and Attribution
The Great Pyramid served as Khufu's tomb, designed to house his body, demonstrate his status, and facilitate his afterlife cult. Attribution to Khufu is confirmed by worker graffiti in the relieving chambers above the King's Chamber, which include his name over a dozen times. The surrounding necropolis contains tombs of Khufu's family and officials, and the mortuary cult operated for centuries. The Diary of Merer papyri (c. 27th year of Khufu's reign) explicitly name the pyramid "Akhet Khufu" and detail limestone deliveries.

### Age and Dating
The pyramid is approximately 4,600 years old. Historical chronology, based on king lists and succession, places Khufu's reign between 2700 and 2500 BC. Radiocarbon dating of organic material in the original mortar calibrated to 2871–2604 BC, though the "old wood problem" may offset dates by 100–300 years. A cedar plank found in a shaft was dated to 3341–3094 BC, likely due to the use of old or recycled wood.

### Construction and Materials
Built with an estimated 2.3 million blocks, the pyramid's core is local limestone, with white limestone casing from Tura and granite from Aswan for internal chambers. The base is level to within 21 mm. The workforce likely consisted of thousands of conscript laborers, not slaves. Experiments suggest about 3,500 quarrymen could produce the needed blocks daily. The design uses a seked (slope ratio) of 5½ palms, and its perimeter-to-height ratio approximates 2π.

### Interior Structure
The interior features three main chambers: the unfinished Subterranean Chamber in the bedrock, the Queen's Chamber with mysterious shafts, and the King's Chamber containing a granite sarcophagus. They are connected by the Descending Passage, Ascending Passage, and the corbelled Grand Gallery. Above the King's Chamber are five relieving chambers to distribute weight. A large void, the "ScanPyramids Big Void," was detected above the Gallery in 2017. The original entrance is on the north face; tourists now use a "Robbers' Tunnel."

### Casing and Exterior
Originally cased in smooth, white limestone, the pyramid's height was reduced from 146.6 m to 138.5 m after most casing stones were removed in the Middle Ages. The casing was precisely fitted with mortar. The pyramid was topped by a pyramidion (capstone), likely of limestone, not gold. The four sides show a slight concavity, now attributed to damage from casing removal.

### Pyramid Complex
The complex included a Pyramid Temple (east side), a Valley Temple (near the Nile), a causeway, subsidiary pyramids for queens, and boat pits. Two sealed boat pits south of the pyramid contained the Khufu ship (a 43.6 m cedar vessel) and a second, unopened boat. A worker's town and cemeteries are located nearby.

### Historiography and Looting
Classical authors like Herodotus (5th century BC) and Diodorus Siculus (1st century BC) provided early, often inaccurate, accounts. In late antiquity, the pyramids were misidentified as Joseph's granaries. The pyramid was likely looted by the First Intermediate Period and entered by al-Ma'mun around 820 AD. Strabo (c. 25 BC) described a movable stone that could be raised to enter.

## Terms
- **Seked**: An ancient Egyptian measure of slope, defined as the horizontal run for a vertical rise of one cubit (7 palms). The Great Pyramid's seked is 5½ palms.
- **Relieving chambers**: Five stacked compartments above the King's Chamber designed to distribute the weight of the masonry above and prevent the chamber's roof from collapsing.
- **Pyramidion**: The capstone or topmost stone of a pyramid, often gilded in later periods.
- **Mortuary temple**: A temple adjacent to a pyramid where rituals for the deceased pharaoh were performed.
- **Valley temple**: A temple near the Nile connected to the mortuary temple by a causeway, part of the funerary complex.
- **Royal cubit**: The standard unit of length in ancient Egypt, approximately 52.4 cm, used in the pyramid's design.
- **Nummulitic limestone**: A type of limestone composed of the fossils of prehistoric shell creatures, used for the pyramid's core blocks.
- **Diary of Merer**: A collection of papyrus logs from c. 27th year of Khufu's reign, documenting the transport of limestone from Tura to Giza.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Construction techniques**: The exact methods for quarrying, transporting, and lifting the massive blocks remain debated, with theories involving ramps, levers, and waterways.
- **Purpose of shafts**: The function of the narrow shafts in the King's and Queen's Chambers is unknown; theories range from ventilation to symbolic passages for the pharaoh's spirit.
- **The ScanPyramids Big Void**: The purpose and nature of the large cavity detected above the Grand Gallery in 2017 are not yet understood.
- **Original entrance and looting**: The exact sequence of early entries and resealing of the pyramid before al-Ma'mun is uncertain.
- **Alignment methods**: How the Egyptians achieved the precise cardinal alignment of the base (within 3'38" of arc) is still explored, with proposed methods including solar and stellar observations.

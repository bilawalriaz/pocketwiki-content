# Mumbai

## Overview
Mumbai, formerly Bombay until 1995, is the capital of Maharashtra and India’s financial, commercial, and entertainment hub. Built on an archipelago of seven islands originally inhabited by Koli fishing communities, the city was ceded to the Portuguese in 1535 and transferred to the English East India Company in 1661 as part of a royal dowry. Large-scale land reclamation, notably the Hornby Vellard project (1782–1845), merged the islands into a single peninsula, enabling Mumbai’s rise as a major Arabian Sea port. The city drove India’s independence movement, absorbed massive post-independence migration, and became the capital of the linguistically defined state of Maharashtra in 1960. Today, Mumbai generates 6.16% of India’s GDP, handles 70% of its maritime trade, and houses the Bombay Stock Exchange and Bollywood, while grappling with extreme density, slum housing (41.8% of residents), and recurrent flooding and terrorism.

## Body

### Etymology and Identity
The name **Mumbai** derives from *Mumbā* or *Mahā-Ambā*, the patron goddess (Kula Devata) of the native Koli community, combined with *ā'ī* ("mother" in Marathi). The Portuguese recorded *Bombaim* post-1512, later anglicised to *Bombay* by the English. A popular but unsubstantiated theory links *Bombaim* to *bom baim* ("good little bay"). The Government of India officially restored the name Mumbai in November 1995 at the insistence of the Marathi nationalist Shiv Sena party, arguing "Bombay" symbolised colonial rule. A resident is termed a **Mumbaikar** (Marathi suffix *-kar* = resident of).

### Early History and Dynastic Rule
The seven islands (Isle of Bombay, Parel, Mazagaon, Mahim, Colaba, Worli, Old Woman’s Island) show Pleistocene sediments indicating Stone Age habitation. In the 3rd century BCE, they fell under the Maurya Empire. Between the 2nd century BCE and 9th century CE, control passed through successive indigenous dynasties—Satavahanas, Western Satraps, Abhira, Vakataka, Kalachuris, Konkan Mauryas, Chalukyas, and Rashtrakutas—followed by the Shilaharas (810–1260 CE). Key ancient structures include the Kanheri Caves (1st century CE Buddhist centre), Jogeshwari Caves (520–525 CE), Elephanta Caves (6th–7th century CE), Walkeshwar Temple (10th century), and Banganga Tank (12th century). King Bhimdev established a capital at Mahikawati (Mahim) in the late 13th century. The Delhi Sultanate annexed the islands in 1347–48, succeeded by the independent Gujarat Sultanate (1407), which built the Haji Ali Dargah (1431).

### Portuguese and British Colonial Era
Fearing Mughal emperor Humayun, Sultan Bahadur Shah of Gujarat ceded the islands to the Portuguese via the Treaty of Bassein (1534), formalised 1535. The Portuguese built churches (St. Michael’s Mahim, 1534), forts (Bombay Castle, Castella de Aguada), and leased lands to officers. In 1661, the marriage treaty of Charles II and Catherine of Braganza transferred the islands to England as dowry. The East India Company leased them in 1668 (£10/annum); population surged from 10,000 (1661) to 60,000 (1675). The Company moved its headquarters from Surat to Bombay in 1687, making it the seat of the Bombay Presidency.

The Marathas captured Salsette (1737) and Bassein (1739), ending Portuguese presence. The British secured Salsette via the Treaty of Purandar (1776) and Treaty of Salbai (1782) after the First Anglo-Maratha War. From 1782, the **Hornby Vellard** causeway project began large-scale reclamation, completed by 1845, physically uniting the islands. The 1817 Battle of Khadki ended Maratha resistance, bringing the Deccan under British suzerainty. The 1853 railway to Thane, the 1861–65 American Civil War cotton boom, and the 1869 Suez Canal opening cemented Bombay’s status as a global port. The 1896 bubonic plague killed over 20,000 and triggered mass flight. The city hosted the 1942 Quit India Movement and the 1946 Royal Indian Navy mutiny.

### Post-Independence Reorganisation and Growth
After 1947, Bombay Presidency became Bombay State, expanded by integrating princely states. The **Samyukta Maharashtra Movement** (1950s) demanded a Marathi-speaking state; 105 died in protests. Despite proposals for a bilingual state or autonomous city-state, Bombay State was bifurcated on linguistic lines on 1 May 1960, creating Maharashtra (capital Bombay) and Gujarat. Flora Fountain was renamed Hutatma Chowk (Martyr’s Square) to honour protesters.

Subsequent decades saw massive suburban expansion: Nariman Point and Cuffe Parade reclaimed in the 1960s; petrochemical refineries (Hindustan Petroleum, Bharat Petroleum) established at Trombay in the 1950s; Bombay Metropolitan Region Development Authority formed 1975; Navi Mumbai township created 1979 across Thane/Raigad. The 1982 Great Bombay Textile Strike (250,000 workers) collapsed the mill industry, leaving land for 21st-century redevelopment. Jawaharlal Nehru Port commissioned 1989 at Nhava Sheva to decongest Mumbai Harbour.

### Violence and Terrorism (1990s–2010s)
Post-1992 Babri Masjid demolition, Hindu-Muslim riots (1992–93) killed >1,000. The 1993 serial bombings (13 blasts) by Islamic extremists and the underworld killed 257. Subsequent attacks: 2006 train bombings (209 dead), 2008 coordinated attacks (26/11, 173 dead, heritage sites damaged), 2011 triple blasts (26 dead).

### Geography and Climate
Mumbai spans 603 km² (233 sq mi) across two districts: Mumbai City (157 km², "Island City"/South Mumbai) and Mumbai Suburban (446 km²). It sits on Salsette Island, bounded by the Arabian Sea (west), Thane Creek (east), and Vasai Creek (north). Elevation averages 14 m (46 ft); highest point 450 m (Powai–Kanheri). The eastern coast has mangroves; the western coast is sandy/rocky. Underlying rock is black Deccan basalt (late Cretaceous/early Eocene). The city sits on 23 fault lines in Seismic Zone III (max magnitude 6.5). Sanjay Gandhi National Park (103 km²) lies partly within city limits. Water supply comes from six lakes (Tulsi, Vihar, Tansa, Bhatsa, Vaitarna dams); Mithi River is highly polluted.

**Climate:** Tropical wet/dry (Aw); suburbs tropical monsoon (Am). Rainless October–May; peak rain July. Annual avg 27°C (81°F); avg rainfall 2,213 mm (city), 2,502 mm (suburbs). Record 24-hour rain: 944 mm (26 July 2005). Prone to monsoon flooding exacerbated by unplanned drainage and informal settlements; 2005 floods caused 500–1,000 deaths and US$1.2B losses. Cyclone risk: worst 1948 (151 km/h gusts, 38 dead).

### Demographics
2011 Census: 12.44 million (density 20,482/km²); Mumbai Metropolitan Region 18.39 million. Sex ratio 853 females/1,000 males (national 914), skewed by male migrants. Literacy 89.73% (national 86.7%). 41.8% live in slums (7% of land). Dharavi (2.39 km²) houses ~1 million. 54.8% of 1991–2001 net population growth from migrants outside Maharashtra.

**Religion (2011):** Hindu 65.99%, Muslim 20.65%, Buddhist 4.85%, Jain 4.10%, Christian 3.27%, Sikh 0.49%. Oldest Muslim groups: Dawoodi Bohras, Ismaili Khojas, Konkani Muslims. Native Christians: East Indian Catholics (Portuguese converts). Bene Israeli Jews (Konkan coast, claimed descent from 175 BCE shipwreck). Largest global Parsi Zoroastrian population (~60,000, declining). **Ethnicity:** Maharashtrians 32%, Gujaratis 20%, others from across India. **Languages:** Marathi (official, most spoken), Hindi, Urdu, Gujarati; English (white-collar); *Bombay Hindi* colloquial.

### Economy
Mumbai generates 6.16% of India’s GDP; 19.8% of Maharashtra’s GDP (2023–24). Mumbai Metropolitan Region GDP $368–400B PPP (2017–18), top two in India. Key shares (2006): 25% industrial output, 70% maritime trade (Mumbai Port, JNPT, Dharamtar), 70% capital transactions, 33% income tax, 60% customs duty. Post-1970s diversification from textiles/port to finance, engineering, diamond-polishing, healthcare, IT. Post-1991 liberalisation: finance boom (mid-1990s), IT/exports/services/outsourcing (2000s). Current key sectors: finance, gems/jewellery, leather, IT/ITES, textiles, petrochemicals, electronics, autos, entertainment. Dharavi hosts 15,000 single-room recycling factories.

Houses Reserve Bank of India, Bombay Stock Exchange (oldest in Asia, 1875), National Stock Exchange, SEBI. Financial centres: Nariman Point, Bandra Kurla Complex. Five Fortune Global 500 HQs. 2019: top 10 globally by billionaire count; total wealth ~$960B. Alpha world city (2008). Extreme inequality: 2010 median income ₹20,000 vs avg ₹40,000; per capita residential space 8.3 m² (slums 2.73 m²). Median 1BR rent ₹30,000 (2019). Long commutes from affordable suburbs to central business districts.

### Government and Politics
**Civic Administration:** Brihanmumbai Municipal Corporation (BMC) governs 603 km². Richest civic body in India; 2022–23 revenue ₹378.91B (US$3.9B), expenditure ₹338.25B. Headed by Mayor (2.5-year term, indirect election) and Municipal Commissioner (IAS officer, state-appointed, executive head). Mumbai Police (Police Commissioner, IPS) under state Home Ministry. Seat of Bombay High Court (jurisdiction: Maharashtra, Goa, Dadra & Nagar Haveli, Daman & Diu).

**Politics:** Indian National Congress founded in Mumbai (1885); hosted six early sessions. 1966: Shiv Sena formed by Bal Thackeray for Marathi rights; shifted to Hindutva (1985), allied with BJP. Congress dominated until Shiv Sena won 1985 municipal polls. 1989: BJP–Shiv Sena alliance dislodged Congress in state. 1999: NCP split from Congress, later allied (Democratic Front). 2019 Lok Sabha: BJP–Shiv Sena won all 6 Mumbai seats (3 each). 2024 Maharashtra Assembly: BJP 15, Shiv Sena (UBT) 10, Shiv Sena 6, Congress 3, NCP 1, Independent 1 (36 seats). 2026 BMC (projected): BJP–Shiv Sena alliance 118 of 227 seats (BJP 89).

### Transport
**Road:** Golden Quadrilateral terminus. NH-48 to Chennai/Delhi. Mumbai–Pune Expressway (2000, India’s first). Mumbai–Nagpur Expressway (June 2025). Delhi–Mumbai Expressway (under construction, target 2027). 1,900 km public roads. Eastern Freeway (2013), Bandra-Worli Sea Link, Mumbai Trans Harbour Link (21.8 km, inaugurated Jan 2024). 721,000 private vehicles (2014). Taxis (CNG) citywide; auto-rickshaws (CNG) suburbs only. Ride-hailing: Ola, Rapido, Uber.

**Bus:** BEST (BMC undertaking) carried 5.5M/day (2008), dropped to 2.8M (2015). 4,608 buses, 390 routes (2011). Fleet: single/double-decker, low-floor, AC; diesel/CNG/electric. MSRTC, NMMT, TMT provide intercity/suburban links. BRT lanes planned.

**Rail:** **Mumbai Suburban Railway** ("locals") is backbone: 319 km, 2,226 daily services (2010), 6.3M passengers/day (2007). Extreme overcrowding: 12-car trains (capacity 1,700) carry ~4,500. HQ of Central Railway (CSMT) and Western Railway (Churchgate). **Mumbai Metro:** Line 1 opened June 2014; 4 lines operational, 8 planned (as of 2026). **Monorail:** Single route, opened Feb 2014. Long-distance terminals: CSMT, Dadar, LTT, Mumbai Central, Bandra Terminus.

**Air:** Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj International Airport (CSMIA): 52.8M passengers (2023–24), 2nd busiest in India. T2 terminal opened 2014. Navi Mumbai International Airport (Ulwe, Raigad) opened Dec 2025. Juhu Aerodrome: heliport, flying club.

**Water:** Mumbai Port (natural harbour, wet/dry docks) and Jawaharlal Nehru Port (JNPT, commissioned 1989, busiest container port, 50% India’s container cargo). Ferries/catamarans for local transport. Western Naval Command HQ.

### Utility Services
Water: 3.5B litres/day required (2009); 700M lost to theft/leakage. Tansa Lake supplies western suburbs; filtered at Bhandup (Asia’s largest filtration plant). Refuse: 7,800 t/day (2005), 40 t plastic; dumping at Gorai, Mulund, Deonar. Sewage treated at Worli/Bandra, discharged via 3.4 km and 3.7 km marine outfalls. Electricity: BEST (City district), Mahavitaran/Adani/Tata Power (suburbs); mostly underground cables. Cooking gas: LPG cylinders, PNG (Mahanagar Gas). Telecom: MTNL (former monopoly); private operators Airtel, Jio, Vodafone Idea. 16.4M internet users (2014, highest in India).

### Education
**Schools:** BMC runs 1,135 schools (2026), free education to ~293,000 students in 8 languages (Marathi, Hindi, Gujarati, Urdu, English, Tamil, Telugu, Kannada). Aided and private schools also operate.

**Higher Education:** 10+2+3 system (10 years school, 2 years junior college: arts/commerce/science, then degree/professional). University of Mumbai (1857) affiliates most colleges; ranked 41 globally for engineering (Business Insider 2012). Historic colleges: Wilson (1832), Grant Medical (1845), Government Law (1855), Elphinstone (1856), VJTI (1887, first engineering). Premier institutes: IIT Bombay, Institute of Chemical Technology, IIM Mumbai, JBIMS, NMIMS, SPJIMR, TISS. Research: Tata Institute of Fundamental Research, Bhabha Atomic Research Centre (CIRUS reactor, Trombay).

### Culture
Birthplace of Indian cinema. Bollywood (Hindi, 150–200 films/year) centred in Goregaon (Film City). Mumbai International Film Festival, Filmfare Awards hosted here. Thriving theatre movement (Marathi, Hindi, English). Art: Jehangir Art Gallery, National Gallery of Modern Art. Asiatic Society (1833, oldest public library). Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj Vastu Sangrahalaya (Prince of Wales Museum). Jijamata Udyaan (zoo). Literary legacy: Salman Rushdie, Aravind Adiga (Booker winners); Marathi modernised by Mohan Apte, Anant Kanekar, Gangadhar Gadgil.

**Festivals:** Ganesh Chaturthi (largest, ~5,000 pandals), Diwali, Holi, Navratri, Christmas, Eid, Durga Puja, Gudi Padwa (Marathi New Year), Maharashtra Day (1 May). Kala Ghoda Arts Festival, Banganga Festival (Jan, music at Banganga Tank), Elephanta Festival (Feb, classical dance/music). Beaches: Girgaum Chowpatty, Juhu (swimmable), Dadar, Gorai, Versova, Madh, Aksa, Manori. Essel World/Water Kingdom (Gorai), Adlabs Imagica (Khopoli).

### Architecture and Cityscape
Blend of Gothic Revival (Victoria Terminus/CSMT, University), Indo-Saracenic (Gateway of India), Art Deco (Marine Drive, Oval Maidan—2nd highest count globally after Miami), and contemporary. 956 skyscrapers, 272 under construction (2009); 237 >100m (vs 327 Shanghai, 855 NYC). Mumbai Heritage Conservation Committee (1995) protects heritage. Three UNESCO World Heritage Sites: CSMT, Elephanta Caves, Victorian & Art Deco Ensemble. Vertical growth driven by land scarcity and population; 77% of India’s tallest buildings in Mumbai (2023). ~250 tall buildings (100+ completed, 90+ under construction, June 2023). Premium real estate prices.

# Li Ka-shing

## Overview
Sir Ka-shing Li (born 29 July 1928) is a Hong Kong business magnate, investor, and philanthropist who built a conglomerate spanning ports, telecommunications, retail, energy, and real estate. Starting as a refugee and factory worker, he founded Cheung Kong Industries in 1950, eventually acquiring Hutchison Whampoa (1979) and Hongkong Electric Holdings (1985) to create a business empire that at times represented a significant portion of the Hong Kong Stock Exchange's market capitalization. He retired as chairman of CK Hutchison Holdings and CK Asset Holdings in May 2018, passing control to his son Victor Li, while remaining a senior advisor. Through the Li Ka Shing Foundation (established 1980), he has donated over HK$30 billion, primarily to education and medical research in Hong Kong and mainland China, owning the second largest private foundation in the world after Bill & Melinda Gates Foundation.

## Body

### Early Life and Entry into Business
Li was born in Chao'an, Guangdong, to Teochew parents Li Yun-ching and Cheung Bik-chin. In 1940, the family fled to Hong Kong as refugees from the Sino-Japanese War. His father died of tuberculosis in 1943, forcing 15-year-old Li to leave school and work 16-hour days at a plastics trading company. In 1950, using personal savings and borrowed funds, he founded Cheung Kong Industries. He mastered the technique of mixing colour into plastics to resemble real flowers, securing a major foreign buyer and becoming Asia's largest supplier of plastic flowers.

### Real Estate Expansion and Corporate Strategy
Li entered real estate in 1958, purchasing a site for his own factory. The 1967 Hong Kong riots caused a property crash; Li bought land from fleeing residents, betting the crisis was temporary. Cheung Kong Holdings listed in 1972. A successful 1977 bid for development sites above the Central and Admiralty MTR stations positioned Cheung Kong to challenge Hongkong Land (Jardines) as Hong Kong's premier developer. Jardines responded in the 1980s with a cross-shareholding structure and moved its legal domicile to Bermuda in 1984 ahead of the 1997 handover. Li later divested major assets, including The Center (HK$40.2 billion, Asia-Pacific's largest office sale) and Century Link in Shanghai (US$2.95 billion).

### Conglomerate Diversification
**Retail:** Through CK Hutchison subsidiary AS Watson (ASW), Li operates over 15,000 stores globally, including Superdrug (UK), Marionnaud (France), Kruidvat (Benelux), Watson's (Asia), PARKnSHOP supermarkets, and Fortress appliance stores.  
**Asset Trading:** CK Hutchison builds and sells businesses for shareholder value. Notable exits include Orange to Mannesmann (1999, US$15.12B profit), 20% of ports business to PSA Corporation (2006, US$3.12B profit), and 67% of Hutchison Essar (India) to Vodafone (US$11.1B).  
**Technology:** Horizons Ventures (investment arm) and Li Ka Shing Foundation backed early-stage tech firms: Facebook (0.8%, US$120M), Spotify (~US$50M), Siri Inc. (US$15.5M Series B), Summly, Wibbitz, BitPay, Zoom (US$30M Series C), Impossible Foods (US$108M Series D), Blockstream (US$55M Series A), and Expa. In 2017, Li partnered with Jack Ma to launch AlipayHK.  
**Water & Energy:** CK Infrastructure owns 75% of UK water supplier Northumbrian Water (25% KKR). Despite ~£22M losses (March 2022–July 2023), CK and KKR extracted £159M in dividends; the firm was fined £240,000 in 2022 for sewage discharge. In energy, Li held a majority stake in Canada's Husky Energy (Alberta), merged into Cenovus in 2021 (Li retains 27.2%).  
**Other Holdings:** Li was the largest shareholder of Canadian Imperial Bank of Commerce (CIBC) until selling his CA$1.2B stake in 2005 (proceeds donated). He also invested personally in Singapore and Canada real estate. He was the non-executive director of the Hongkong & Shanghai Banking Corporation since 1980 and became deputy chairman of the bank in 1985. He was also Deputy Chairman of HSBC in 1991–1992.

### Retirement and Succession
After nearly 70 years leading the conglomerate, Li announced retirement on 16 March 2018, retiring from those roles in May 2018 and passing chairmanship of CK Hutchison Holdings and CK Asset Holdings to his elder son, Victor Li (Canadian citizen). Li remains senior advisor. Younger son Richard Li chairs PCCW, Hong Kong's largest telecom company.

### Personal Life
Li married his first cousin, Chong Yuet-ming (daughter of maternal uncle Cheung Jing-on), in 1962; she died in 1990 (suspected drug overdose or cardiac arrest). He is a Buddhist known for frugality: wearing simple black dress shoes, inexpensive watches (Citizen HK$50, later Seiko, recently Apple Watch), and living decades in the same Deep Water Bay house. In 1996, Victor Li was kidnapped by gangster Cheung Tze-keung ("Big Spender"); Li paid HK$1 billion ransom directly. No Hong Kong police report was filed; mainland authorities pursued Cheung, who was executed in 1998—an outcome impossible under Hong Kong law—fueling rumors of a deal between Li and mainland authorities.

### Politics and Mainland Relations
Long viewed as pro-Beijing, tensions grew in the 2010s. During the 2019–20 Hong Kong protests, Li refused to condemn protesters, urging peace and humane government response. In 2011, he endorsed Henry Tang for Chief Executive and remarked "one-person-one-vote," later walking it back. He was criticized by Xinhua for ambiguity on the Umbrella Movement. In 2015, Global Times attacked Li as "ungrateful" and "immoral" for considering sales of prime Shanghai/Beijing properties, interpreting it as capital flight amid China's economic slowdown. Security Times estimated Li sold ≥CN¥73.8B in China assets since 2014. His companies denied divestment, citing ordinary business. Attacks ceased after People's Daily and Beijing Youth Daily published neutral editorials.

### Philanthropy
The Li Ka Shing Foundation (1980) focuses on education, medical services, and research. Li has invested over HK$30 billion, ~80% in mainland China and Hong Kong.  
**Education:** Founded Shantou University (STU) and Medical College near his hometown (1981); granted >HK$12B to STU. Established Guangdong Technion–Israel Institute of Technology (2013, US$130M). Funded Cheung Kong Graduate School of Business (2002). Named facilities at Hong Kong Polytechnic University (HK$100M, 2001), Cambridge (£5.3M, 2002; £2M oncology professorship, 2007), Singapore Management University (US$11.5M, 2002), Stanford (US$90M Center for Learning and Knowledge, opened 2010), Lee Kuan Yew School of Public Policy (SGD$100M, 2007), McGill (CA$6.6M, 2013), and University of Ottawa (CA$1M+).  
**Medical Research:** Li Ka Shing Centre at Cambridge (Cancer Research UK). Li Ka Shing Center for Biomedical and Health Sciences at UC Berkeley (US$40M, 2005; opened 2011). Innovative Genomics Initiative at UC Berkeley/UCSF (US$10M, 2014). Stanford biomedical data (US$3M, 2014). St. Michael's Hospital Toronto Knowledge Institute (CA$25M). University of Alberta Institute of Virology (CA$8M). UCSF precision medicine (US$2M, 2013). Oxford Big Data Institute (£20M, 2017). University of Melbourne Centre for Cancer Research (US$3M, 2017). University of Michigan hepatology/cancer research (US$4M, 2024). Histotripsy systems donated to HKU and CUHK medical faculties (2024).  
**Disaster Relief & Welfare:** 2004 Indian Ocean tsunami (US$3M); 2008 Sichuan earthquake (HK$160M); 2015 Nepal earthquake (US$1M); 2018 Palu-Donggala tsunami (US$5M); 2020 COVID-19 (HK$180M, including HK$100M for Wuhan medics); 2022 Gansu earthquake (HK$30M); 2022 Beijing-Tianjin/Northeast floods (HK$30M).  
**Hong Kong Community:** "Love Can Help" programmes (HK$200M+ 2019–20) for patients outside safety net and NGOs serving disabled, elderly, underprivileged. Crunch Time Instant Relief Fund for SMEs (HK$1B, 2019). HK$170M to four universities (2020). Construction Industry Casualty Assistance Programme (2022). Tsz Shan Monastery (HK$3B+, opened 2015) for Buddhist practice/education. Antarctic circumnavigation by research drone (2019) for climate data.

## Terms
- **CK Hutchison Holdings**: Multinational conglomerate (ports, retail, telecommunications, infrastructure) formed from Cheung Kong Holdings and Hutchison Whampoa; Li served as chairman until 2018.
- **CK Asset Holdings**: Real estate-focused spin-off from CK Hutchison (2015); Li chaired until 2018.
- **Hutchison Whampoa**: British-owned Hong Kong conglomerate acquired by Li in 1979; core of his ports and telecom empire.
- **AS Watson (ASW)**: CK Hutchison retail subsidiary operating >15,000 health/beauty, supermarket, and appliance stores globally.
- **Horizons Ventures**: Li's venture capital firm targeting internet/technology startups (e.g., Facebook, Spotify, Zoom, Blockstream).
- **Li Ka Shing Foundation**: Private philanthropic foundation (est. 1980); owning the second largest private foundation in the world after Bill & Melinda Gates Foundation; focus on education, medical research, disaster relief.
- **Shantou University (STU)**: Public university in Guangdong founded via Li's 1981 donation; received >HK$12B in subsequent grants.
- **Northumbrian Water**: UK water utility 75%-owned by CK Infrastructure; fined £240,000 (2022) for sewage discharge.
- **Husky Energy**: Alberta-based energy company; Li was majority shareholder until 2021 merger with Cenovus (Li retains 27.2%).
- **Tsz Shan Monastery**: Buddhist institute in Hong Kong funded by Li (HK$3B+); opened 2015; rumored designated burial site.

## Debates and open questions
- **Mainland asset sales (2015)**: Whether Li's disposal of Shanghai/Beijing properties represented strategic rebalancing or political signaling/loss of confidence in China's economy remains debated; state media attacks ceased abruptly after neutral editorials in *People's Daily* and *Beijing Youth Daily*.
- **Victor Li kidnapping (1996)**: The decision not to report to Hong Kong police, direct ransom payment, and mainland execution of the kidnapper (illegal in HK) fueled persistent rumors of a covert deal between Li and mainland authorities; no evidence confirmed.
- **Northumbrian Water dividends**: Critics question the extraction of £159M in dividends (March 2022–July 2023) while the utility posted ~£22M losses and was fined for environmental violations.
- **Political stance**: Li's 2011 "one-person-one-vote" remark and 2019 refusal to condemn protesters created ambiguity about his alignment with Beijing versus Hong Kong civil society; the degree of his current influence on Hong Kong politics is unclear.

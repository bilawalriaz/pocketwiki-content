# Mount Kenya

## Overview
Mount Kenya is the second-highest mountain in Africa and the namesake of the country Kenya. It is a massive, extinct stratovolcano with significant ecological, hydrological, and cultural importance, serving as a critical water source and a sacred site for multiple ethnic groups. Its rapidly retreating glaciers and unique alpine ecosystems make it a key site for studying climate change and biodiversity.

## Timeline
- **c. 3 million years ago** — Mount Kenya volcano becomes active after the opening of the East African Rift.
- **Prehistoric** — The volcano becomes extinct; its original crater may have been over 7,000 m high.
- **Prehistoric** — The mountain is covered by an ice cap for thousands of years, eroding its slopes.
- **3 December 1849** — German missionary Johann Ludwig Krapf becomes the first European to report seeing Mount Kenya.
- **1883** — Joseph Thomson scientifically describes the mountain as an extinct volcano.
- **1887** — Count Sámuel Teleki makes the first European exploration high onto the mountain.
- **13 September 1899** — Sir Halford John Mackinder's expedition makes the first ascent of the highest peak, Batian.
- **1949** — The area is gazetted as Mount Kenya National Park.
- **1978** — The area is designated a UNESCO Biosphere Reserve.
- **1997** — Mount Kenya National Park and forest reserve become a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
- **2024** — A study shows only 10% of the 1980s glacier surface area remains.

## Body

### Geology and Glaciers
Mount Kenya is an extinct stratovolcano that was active in the Plio-Pleistocene. Its original ice cap, which may have covered a peak over 7,000 m high, eroded the mountain into its current form with radiating valleys. Today, 11 small glaciers are shrinking rapidly and may disappear by 2050. The mountain's rocks include basalts, phonolites, and kenytes, a rock type first described from here in 1900.

### Ecology and Climate
The mountain features distinct altitudinal ecological zones, from savanna at the base to a nival zone near the glaciers. The Afro-alpine zone is home to endemic giant flora like senecios and lobelias, adapted to extreme daily temperature fluctuations ("winter every night and summer every day"). The climate is equatorial, with two wet and two dry seasons. The mountain is a major water tower, feeding the Tana and Ewaso Nyiro rivers, which supply water and hydroelectric power for millions.

### Cultural Significance
Mount Kenya is sacred to the surrounding ethnic groups. The Kikuyu believe it is the earthly throne of God, Ngai. The Embu and Ameru also view it as God's home. The Maasai, who graze cattle on its northern slopes, believe their ancestors descended from it. The name "Kenya" derives from the Kamba name for the mountain, *Ki nyaa*, recorded by Ludwig Krapf.

### European Exploration and Mountaineering
After Krapf's 1849 sighting, early European expeditions faced skepticism and difficulty. The first ascent of Batian was in 1899 by Mackinder's expedition. The first ascent of Nelion followed in 1929. Point Lenana, the highest peak reachable without technical climbing, is summited by most of the park's 16,000 annual visitors. The mountain has eight main walking routes, with Chogoria, Naro Moru, and Sirimon being the most popular.

### Conservation
The national park was established in 1949 to protect the area's scenic beauty, biodiversity, and water catchment. An electric fence, partially completed by 2021, is being built to keep animals from straying into farmland. The park and surrounding forest reserve are managed by the Kenya Wildlife Service and the Kenya Forest Service.

## Terms
- **Stratovolcano**: A large, steep-sided volcano built up by many layers of hardened lava and ash.
- **Afro-alpine zone**: A high-altitude ecological belt characterized by extreme daily temperature swings and specialized giant flora.
- **Endemic**: A species found only in a specific, limited geographic area.
- **Periglacial**: Relating to a cold climate region adjacent to a glacier, characterized by freeze-thaw processes.
- **Nival zone**: The highest ecological zone, where plant life is absent due to permanent snow and ice.
- **Gendarme**: A sharp, rocky pinnacle on a mountain ridge.
- **Anabatic wind**: A valley wind that blows uphill during the day, caused by the heating of the slope.
- **Katabatic wind**: A drainage wind that blows downhill from a high elevation, typically at night.

## Debates and Open Questions
The exact etymology of the name "Kenya" is debated. While derived from the Kamba *Ki nyaa* ("mountain of whiteness" or "place of the ostrich"), some claim it was a corruption of the name, while others argue it was a precise notation of the African pronunciation. The future of the mountain's glaciers is a major concern, with predictions they could disappear by 2050 due to climate change.

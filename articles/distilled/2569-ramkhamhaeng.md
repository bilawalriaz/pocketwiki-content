# Ramkhamhaeng

## Overview
Ramkhamhaeng the Great was the third king of the Phra Ruang dynasty, ruling the Sukhothai Kingdom in what is now Thailand from 1279 to 1298. His reign is considered the kingdom's most prosperous era, and he is a foundational figure in Thai history, credited with creating the Thai alphabet and establishing Theravada Buddhism as the state religion.

## Timeline
- **c. 1279** — Ramkhamhaeng ascends the throne, possibly marked by planting a sugar palm tree.
- **1282–1323** — Sends embassies to Yuan China, importing ceramic-making techniques.
- **1292** — Composes the Ramkhamhaeng Inscription, a key historical source.
- **1298** — Dies, according to the Chinese *History of Yuan*; succeeded by his son Loe Thai.

## Body

### Early Life and Accession
Ramkhamhaeng was the son of Pho Khun Si Inthrathit, who established Sukhothai's independence from the Khmer Empire. At age 19, Ramkhamhaeng participated in the campaign to capture the city of Sukhothai. After his father's death, his elder brother Ban Mueang became king and appointed Ramkhamhaeng as ruler of Si Satchanalai. Ramkhamhaeng succeeded Ban Mueang upon the latter's death. Historian Tri Amattayakun suggests his accession in 1279 is indicated by the planting of a sugar palm tree, a tradition for Tai Ahom monarchs.

### Reign and Expansion
Ramkhamhaeng's reign saw significant cultural and political developments. He is credited with creating the Thai alphabet (Lai Nangsue Thai) from Khmer, Sanskrit, Pali, and Grantha scripts. He established close ties with neighboring rulers, including Mangrai of Chiang Mai, and his campaigns against Cambodia left the Khmer country "utterly devastated." His kingdom is traditionally described as vast, extending from the Bay of Bengal to the Malay Peninsula, though this is interpreted within the "mandala" political model, where power radiated from the capital rather than defining strict borders. A notable early event was the elopement of his daughter, Lady Soidao, with a commoner who later founded the Burmese Hanthawaddy kingdom.

### Economic and Cultural Legacy
Ramkhamhaeng fostered trade with Yuan China, which led to the importation of ceramic-making techniques. This laid the foundation for the Sukhothai Kingdom's renowned "Sangkhalok ware" industry, which exported ceramics across Asia for centuries. His primary historical source is the Ramkhamhaeng Inscription of 1292, now in the Bangkok National Museum and a UNESCO Memory of the World document. His legacy is commemorated in modern Thailand, including on the 20 Baht banknote and through Ramkhamhaeng University.

## Terms
- **Phra Ruang dynasty**: The royal dynasty of the Sukhothai Kingdom.
- **Sukhothai Kingdom**: A historical polity in what is now Thailand, considered the first capital of Siam.
- **Theravada Buddhism**: A branch of Buddhism that became the state religion under Ramkhamhaeng.
- **Mandala political model**: A political system where a kingdom's influence radiates from a powerful center, lacking fixed borders.
- **Sangkhalok ware**: A type of ceramic ware produced in Sukhothai and exported widely after techniques were imported from China.
- **Ramkhamhaeng Inscription**: A stone inscription from 1292, a primary source for early Thai history.
- **Lai Nangsue Thai**: The Thai script, credited to Ramkhamhaeng.

## Debates and Open questions
- The exact date of Ramkhamhaeng's death is debated. The Chinese *History of Yuan* records 1298, but historian George Cœdès suggests it was "more probable" it occurred "shortly before 1318."
- The traditional narrative of his vast empire is interpreted by scholars within the flexible "mandala" model, questioning the extent of direct control versus spheres of influence.

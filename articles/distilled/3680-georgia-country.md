# Georgia (country)

## Overview
Georgia is a country in the Caucasus region at the intersection of Eastern Europe and West Asia. It is defined by its ancient Christian heritage, a unique language unrelated to any other family, and a history of unification, golden age, and foreign domination. Its modern identity is shaped by a pro-Western orientation and ongoing conflicts with Russia over the occupied territories of Abkhazia and South Ossetia.

## Body

### History and Statehood
Georgia's history begins with prehistoric settlements, including the Dmanisi hominins (c. 1.8 million years ago). The classical era saw the kingdoms of Colchis (west) and Iberia (east). Christianity was adopted in 337, which later aided unification under the Bagrationi dynasty. The Kingdom of Georgia reached its Golden Age in the 12th–13th centuries under Kings David IV and Tamar. Following Mongol invasions and internal fragmentation, the kingdom collapsed in 1466. Weakened by Ottoman and Persian conflicts, eastern Georgia became a Russian protectorate in 1783 (Treaty of Georgievsk) before being fully annexed by the Russian Empire in 1801.

After a brief independence as a democratic republic (1918–1921), Georgia was forcibly incorporated into the Soviet Union. Independence was re-established in 1991, followed by civil war and secessionist conflicts in Abkhazia and South Ossetia. The 2003 Rose Revolution led to a pro-Western government, which pursued EU and NATO integration. This orientation contributed to the 2008 Russo-Georgian War, after which Russia recognized the independence of Abkhazia and South Ossetia, a move rejected by most of the international community. The Georgian Dream party has governed since 2012, and the country's EU accession process was paused by the government in 2024.

### Government and Politics
Georgia is a unitary parliamentary republic. The President is the largely ceremonial head of state, while the Prime Minister is head of government. The Parliament is unicameral with 150 members. Following the 2024 parliamentary elections, which were marred by allegations of fraud, a political crisis ensued. The ruling Georgian Dream party suspended the EU accession process until 2028 and inaugurated its candidate as president, a move contested by the outgoing president and opposition. International assessments, such as the V-Dem Institute's 2025 report, classify Georgia as an electoral autocracy, noting a sharp democratic decline.

### Economy and Society
Georgia has transitioned from a Soviet command economy to a free-market system. Post-2003 reforms led to rapid growth, a dramatic reduction in corruption, and a very high ranking for economic freedom and ease of doing business. Key sectors include tourism, agriculture (especially wine), and transport corridors linking Europe and Asia. The population is approximately 3.9 million, with ethnic Georgians comprising about 87%. The Georgian language, written in its own unique script, is the primary language. The Georgian Orthodox Church is the dominant religion, with special constitutional status. Georgia is a member of the Council of Europe and an official EU candidate.

## Terms
*   **Kartvelian:** The language family to which Georgian belongs, unrelated to any other language family.
*   **Bagrationi dynasty:** The royal dynasty that ruled Georgia for centuries, including during its Golden Age.
*   **Rose Revolution:** The 2003 peaceful change of government that ousted President Eduard Shevardnadze and brought pro-Western reformers to power.
*   **Georgian Dream:** The ruling political party in Georgia since 2012, founded by Bidzina Ivanishvili.
*   **Abkhazia and South Ossetia:** Two regions within Georgia's internationally recognized borders that have declared independence and are supported by Russia, which has maintained a military presence there since the 2008 war.
*   **EU Candidate Status:** A formal step in the European Union accession process, granted to Georgia in December 2023.
*   **Supra:** A traditional Georgian feast and social gathering, led by a tamada (toastmaster).
*   **Kvevri:** Large clay jars used for the traditional Georgian winemaking method, recognized by UNESCO.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Democratic Backsliding:** Scholars and international organizations debate the extent to which Georgia has transitioned from a democracy to an autocracy under Georgian Dream rule, with the V-Dem Institute's 2025 classification marking a significant point in this assessment.
*   **EU Integration:** The future of Georgia's EU membership bid is uncertain following the government's 2024 decision to pause the accession process until 2028, citing "blackmail" from the EU.
*   **Occupied Territories:** The status of Abkhazia and South Ossetia remains a core unresolved conflict. Georgia and most of the international community consider them occupied by Russia, while Russia and a few other UN member states recognize them as independent states.
*   **Name Origin:** While the modern name "Georgia" is generally derived from the Persian term *Gurj*, the ultimate origin of that term is debated, with theories connecting it to "land of wolves."

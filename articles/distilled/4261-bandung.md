# Bandung

## Overview
Bandung, capital of West Java, Indonesia, is a highland city on a volcanic plateau. Its cooler climate and strategic location made it a planned colonial capital and a major cultural, educational, and economic hub. It is historically significant as the host of the 1955 Bandung Conference, a landmark in decolonization.

## Timeline
- **1488** — Earliest reference to the Bandung area.
- **1786** — Supply road connecting Batavia (Jakarta) to Bandung constructed.
- **1809** — Governor Daendels orders the Great Post Road built through Bandung.
- **1880** — First major railroad between Batavia and Bandung completed.
- **1906** — Bandung granted municipality (*gemeente*) status.
- **1920s** — Dutch plan to move colonial capital to Bandung begins.
- **1945** — Indonesia declares independence; Bandung becomes West Java capital.
- **1946** — Indonesian forces burn southern Bandung while withdrawing from a British ultimatum.
- **1955** — First Asian-African Conference (Bandung Conference) hosted.
- **2005** — Cipularang Toll Road connecting Jakarta and Bandung completed.
- **2015** — Bandung joins UNESCO Creative Cities Network.
- **2016** — Redevelopment of Husein Sastranegara International Airport completed.
- **2018** — Kertajati International Airport opens as a new regional airport.

## Body

### Colonial Development and the "Paris of Java"
The Dutch East Indies Company (VOC) established plantations in the fertile Bandung area in the 17th-18th centuries. Governor-General Daendels ordered the Great Post Road (1809) through Bandung for Java's defense. The city became the capital of the Preanger Regencies Residency in 1856. The 1880 railroad from Batavia boosted light industry. In the early 20th century, Bandung developed into an exclusive European resort due to its climate. It received municipal status in 1906. The Dutch planned to move the colonial capital from Batavia to Bandung in the 1920s, constructing buildings like Gedung Sate, but World War II halted the project.

### Post-Independence and the Bandung Conference
After Indonesian independence in 1945, Bandung became the West Java capital. During the Indonesian National Revolution, Indonesian forces deliberately burned parts of the city on March 24, 1946, in response to a British ultimatum. In 1955, President Sukarno hosted the first Asian-African Conference in Bandung, attended by 29 nations. Held at Gedung Merdeka, it produced the Declaration of Bandung, promoting peace and opposing colonialism, and is a foundational event for the Non-Aligned Movement.

### Geography, Climate, and Environment
Bandung lies in a river basin surrounded by volcanic mountains, about 145 km southeast of Jakarta at 768 meters elevation, giving it a cooler climate. The Bandung Basin, formed by ancient volcanic activity, is a crucial water source. The northern area is hillier and home to the Tangkuban Perahu volcano. The city faces environmental challenges, including regular flooding and waste management issues, though it has received awards for having the cleanest air among major ASEAN cities.

### Architecture and Urban Development
Bandung is renowned for its Dutch colonial architecture, particularly the tropical Art Deco "New Indies Style." Architects like Henri Maclaine Pont and Charles Prosper Wolff Schoemaker blended modern designs with local traditions, creating landmarks such as the Bandung Institute of Technology campus and Gedung Merdeka. The city experienced rapid urbanization after independence, transforming into a dense metropolitan area with an ongoing high-rise boom of over 100 buildings.

### Economy and Tourism
Bandung's economy is driven by tourism, creative industries, high-tech, manufacturing, and education. It is a popular weekend destination for Jakarta residents, known for its climate, food, and shopping. The city is famous for its "factory outlets" and "distros" (shops selling local designer products). Key industrial centers specialize in textiles, shoes, and food. Tourist attractions include the Tangkuban Prahu volcano, Kawah Putih lake, and numerous museums.

### Transportation and Infrastructure
Bandung is connected to Jakarta by the Cipularang Toll Road. It has two main railway stations and is served by regional trains. Public transport includes *angkot* minibuses and bus rapid transit (BRT) systems like Trans Metro Bandung and Trans Metro Pasundan. The primary airport was Husein Sastranegara International Airport (BDO). A new, larger airport, Kertajati International Airport (KJT), opened in 2018. In 2023, commercial flights moved to Kertajati, but plans were announced to reactivate Husein Sastranegara for commercial jets by late 2026.

### Education and Culture
Bandung is a major educational center with nearly 50 higher education institutions, including the prestigious Bandung Institute of Technology (ITB), founded in 1920. The city is a cultural hub, known for its Sundanese heritage and as a center for Indonesian music, with many notable bands originating there. It is home to professional football (Persib Bandung) and basketball teams.

## Terms
- **Gemeente**: A Dutch term for a municipality, a status granted to Bandung in 1906.
- **New Indies Style**: A tropical Art Deco architectural style developed in the Dutch East Indies, blending modern designs with local cultural elements.
- **Angkot**: A privately operated minibus that serves as a primary, low-cost means of public transportation in Indonesian cities.
- **Distro**: Small retail shops in Bandung that sell non-trademarked products, such as fashion and music, created by local independent designers.
- **Bandung Conference**: The first Asian-African Conference held in 1955, a key event in the decolonization movement and the formation of the Non-Aligned Movement.
- **Tangkuban Perahu**: A volcano with a distinctive truncated flat peak located north of Bandung, a major tourist attraction.
- **Bandung Basin**: The geological depression surrounding the city, formed by ancient volcanic activity and serving as a vital water reservoir.
- **Trans Metro Pasundan**: A bus rapid transit system in the Bandung metropolitan area with an integrated fare system.

## Debates and open questions
- The exact reasons for the drainage of the prehistoric "Great Lake of Bandung" are debated among geologists.
- The future of Bandung's airports is in flux, with plans to reactivate Husein Sastranegara for commercial flights by 2026 while also developing Kertajati as an aviation hub.
- The city struggles to balance rapid urban development and population density against environmental protection, particularly in the northern water reservoir area.

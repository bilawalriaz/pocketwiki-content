# Joseph Goebbels

## Overview
Paul Joseph Goebbels (1897–1945) was a German Nazi politician and the regime's chief propagandist. As Reich Minister of Propaganda from 1933, he masterminded the Nazi Party's messaging, controlled all German media, and was a fervent advocate for the persecution and extermination of Jews. He was one of Adolf Hitler's most loyal followers, serving as his final Chancellor for one day before their joint suicides in 1945.

## Timeline
- **29 October 1897** — Born in Rheydt, Germany.
- **21 April 1922** — Receives PhD in philology from the University of Heidelberg.
- **c. 1924** — Joins the Nazi Party.
- **August 1926** — Appointed Gauleiter (district leader) of Berlin.
- **30 January 1933** — Hitler becomes Chancellor; Goebbels is not initially in the cabinet.
- **March 1933** — Appointed Reich Minister of Public Enlightenment and Propaganda.
- **8–9 November 1938** — Orchestrates the Kristallnacht pogrom.
- **18 February 1943** — Delivers the "Sportpalast speech" calling for "total war."
- **23 July 1944** — Appointed Reich Plenipotentiary for Total War.
- **30 April 1945** — Hitler commits suicide; Goebbels succeeds him as Chancellor.
- **1 May 1945** — Goebbels and his wife Magda commit suicide after poisoning their six children.

## Body

### Early Life and Nazi Activism
Goebbels was born into a Catholic family and had a deformed right foot, which prevented him from serving in World War I. He earned a doctorate in philology in 1922. Initially aspiring to be an author, he joined the Nazi Party around 1924, drawn by Hitler's charisma. He worked for Gregor Strasser's more socialist-leaning northern branch of the party. After Hitler repudiated Strasser's program at the 1926 Bamberg conference, Goebbels switched his loyalty entirely to Hitler, writing in his diary, "I love him... He has thought through everything."

### Propagandist in Berlin
Appointed Gauleiter of Berlin in 1926, Goebbels revitalized the party's presence through provocative tactics, street brawls, and modern advertising techniques. He founded the antisemitic newspaper *Der Angriff* in 1927. His skillful propaganda, including the creation of martyrs like Horst Wessel, helped grow the party's urban support. By 1930, he was appointed Reich leader of Nazi Party propaganda, replacing Gregor Strasser.

### Reich Minister of Propaganda
After the Nazis seized power in 1933, Goebbels' newly created Propaganda Ministry centralized control over all German cultural and intellectual life. He used radio and film extensively, promoting the mass-produced *Volksempfänger* (people's receiver) radio. His ministry, organized into seven departments, enforced Nazi ideology through the Reich Chamber of Culture, which controlled all artistic professions. He staged events like the 1934 Nuremberg Rally and the 1937 "Degenerate Art Exhibition." His leadership style was described as "tempestuous and unpredictable," but his department was noted for its efficiency.

### Antisemitism and the Holocaust
Goebbels was antisemitic from a young age, and his views radicalized after joining the Nazis. He actively promoted persecution, instituting discriminatory measures in Berlin and orchestrating the Kristallnacht pogrom in November 1938. He was fully aware of and supported the "Final Solution." After receiving minutes from the 1942 Wannsee Conference, he wrote in his diary that 60% of Jews would have to be "liquidated," calling the judgment "barbaric but thoroughly deserved." He publicly advocated for the extermination, a stance few other top Nazi officials took.

### World War II and Total War
Goebbels was an enthusiastic supporter of Hitler's expansionist policies. During the war, he controlled domestic media and increasingly became the regime's public voice. As the war turned against Germany, he was appointed Reich Plenipotentiary for Total War in July 1944, tasked with mobilizing all remaining manpower. His efforts, including the unpopular Volkssturm militia, were largely unsuccessful. His influence waned as military and economic concerns overshadowed propaganda.

### Defeat and Death
In the final days of the war, Goebbels remained with Hitler in the Berlin bunker, opposing any surrender or escape. After Hitler's suicide on 30 April, Goebbels succeeded him as Chancellor. His sole official act was a failed request for a ceasefire. On 1 May, he and his wife Magda had their six children poisoned with cyanide before committing suicide themselves. Their bodies were burned in the Chancellery garden.

## Terms
*   **Gauleiter:** The regional leader of the Nazi Party, a powerful political position.
*   **Reich Minister of Public Enlightenment and Propaganda:** The cabinet position Goebbels held, responsible for controlling all German media, arts, and public information.
*   **Reich Chamber of Culture:** An organization under Goebbels' control that regulated all artistic and intellectual professions, excluding Jews and opponents.
*   **Volksempfänger:** "People's receiver," a cheap radio set mass-produced to ensure Nazi propaganda reached all German households.
*   **Kristallnacht:** The "Night of Broken Glass," a state-sponsored pogrom against Jews on 9–10 November 1938, orchestrated by Goebbels.
*   **Total War:** A concept promoted by Goebbels from 1943, demanding the complete mobilization of all national resources, including civilians and industry, for the war effort.
*   **Volkssturm:** The Nazi Party militia formed in late 1944, consisting of men previously exempt from military service, which Goebbels helped administer in Berlin.
*   **Gleichschaltung:** The Nazi process of "coordination," bringing all aspects of German society under party control.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   Historians speculate whether Goebbels' lifelong pursuit of women was a compensation for his physical disability.
*   The exact circumstances of the suicides of Joseph and Magda Goebbels on 1 May 1945 are subject to conflicting eyewitness accounts.
*   The extent to which Goebbels' propaganda successfully shaped German public opinion, especially regarding the war's progress and the Holocaust, is analyzed through his diaries and other sources.

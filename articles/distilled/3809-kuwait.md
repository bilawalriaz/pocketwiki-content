# Kuwait

## Overview
Kuwait is a small, oil-rich emirate in West Asia at the head of the Persian Gulf, bordering Iraq and Saudi Arabia. Its strategic location has made it a historical trade hub and a focal point of regional conflict, most notably the 1990 Iraqi invasion and the ongoing 2026 Iran war. The country is a high-income economy with the world's sixth-largest oil reserves and most valuable currency, but its political system is dominated by the ruling Al Sabah family, and it faces significant demographic and human rights challenges due to its large expatriate population and stateless Bedoon community.

## Timeline
- **c. 5500–3700 BC** — Kuwait is a central borderland during the Ubaid period.
- **2000 BC** — Mesopotamians settle on Failaka island; Dilmun civilization controls Gulf trade routes.
- **1752** — Sabah I bin Jaber is elected ruler, establishing the Sheikdom of Kuwait.
- **1899** — Sheikh Mubarak Al Sabah signs the Anglo-Kuwaiti Agreement, making Kuwait a British protectorate.
- **1938** — Oil is first discovered in the Burgan field.
- **1961** — Kuwait gains independence from the UK; Iraq initially refuses recognition.
- **1990** — Iraq invades and annexes Kuwait.
- **1991** — A U.S.-led coalition liberates Kuwait on 26 February.
- **2005** — Women gain the right to vote and run in elections.
- **2024** — The Emir begins revoking citizenships of hundreds of thousands; the National Assembly is suspended.
- **2026** — Kuwait is subjected to aerial attacks during the wider Iran war.

## Body

### History and Statehood
The territory of modern Kuwait has been inhabited since antiquity due to its strategic location near the Tigris-Euphrates delta. It became a sheikhdom under the Bani Khalid clan in the early 18th century and was established as an independent state in 1752. In 1899, it became a British protectorate, a status that lasted until independence in 1961. The discovery of oil in 1938 transformed the economy, leading to a "golden era" of modernization from 1946 to 1982. Kuwait's history is marked by regional conflicts, including the Kuwait–Najd War (1919–20), the Iran–Iraq War (during which it supported Iraq and suffered pro-Iranian attacks), and the 1990 Iraqi invasion and subsequent Gulf War. The post-war period has seen political deadlock between the government and parliament, economic dependence on oil, and, since 2024, mass citizenship revocations and the suspension of the National Assembly.

### Government and Politics
Kuwait is an emirate where the Al Sabah family holds hereditary rule. The Emir is the head of state, appoints the government, and has the power to dissolve the National Assembly. The political system is categorized as an autocracy, with a history of constitutional suspensions. A significant political issue is the stateless Bedoon population, who lack basic rights and documentation. Kuwaiti society is relatively liberal in the Gulf region, with women having been enfranchised in 2005 and participating actively in the workforce. The country has a secular legal system based on French civil law, with Sharia governing only family law for Muslims.

### Economy and Resources
Kuwait has a wealthy, petroleum-based economy with proven crude oil reserves of 104 billion barrels, about 10% of the world's total. The Kuwait Investment Authority (KIA), established in 1953, is the world's oldest sovereign wealth fund. The economy is heavily dependent on oil, which accounts for 89.1% of exports. Political instability has hampered economic diversification and infrastructure projects. The country has a state-funded healthcare system and a modern highway network but lacks a railway system. Kuwait Vision 2035 aims to develop the country into a financial and cultural hub.

### Demographics and Society
As of 2024, Kuwait has a population of 4.82 million, with citizens making up less than 30% of the total. The majority are foreign nationals from over 100 countries, with Indians and Egyptians being the largest expatriate communities. The official state religion is Maliki Sunni Islam, but there are significant Shia Muslim and Christian minorities. The official language is Modern Standard Arabic, with Kuwaiti Arabic used in daily life and English widely understood. Kuwait is considered a pioneer in the arts and popular culture in the Gulf, with a thriving television drama industry and a strong tradition of theatre and music.

## Terms
- **Emirate:** A state ruled by an emir, a title of high office in an Arab country.
- **Bedoon:** A term for stateless people in Kuwait, many of whom lack citizenship and basic rights.
- **Rentier State:** A state that derives a substantial portion of its national revenues from the rent of indigenous resources to external clients.
- **Kafala System:** A sponsorship system governing the employment of foreign workers in some Gulf countries, often criticized for enabling exploitation.
- **Shamal:** A northwesterly wind common in June and July that causes dramatic sandstorms.
- **Souk Al-Manakh:** An alternative stock market in Kuwait that peaked in the early 1980s before a crash.
- **Kuwait Vision 2035:** A national development plan aiming to diversify the economy and improve infrastructure.
- **Gulf War (1990-1991):** The conflict following Iraq's invasion of Kuwait, ending with a U.S.-led coalition liberating the country.

## Debates and open questions
- The status and rights of the stateless Bedoon population remain a major human rights issue, with organizations accusing the government of ethnic cleansing.
- The mass revocation of citizenships since 2024 is described as one of the largest such policies since World War II, raising questions about its political and social motives.
- Kuwait's chronic political deadlock between the appointed government and the elected parliament is seen as a key factor hampering economic reform and development.
- The country's heavy dependence on oil revenues and the effectiveness of Kuwait Vision 2035 in achieving economic diversification are ongoing concerns.

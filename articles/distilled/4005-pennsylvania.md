# Pennsylvania

## Overview
Pennsylvania is a U.S. state in the Mid-Atlantic and Northeastern regions, founded in 1681 as a haven for religious tolerance. It played a pivotal role in American history, hosting the signing of the Declaration of Independence and the U.S. Constitution. Today, it is the fifth-most populous state, with a diverse economy transitioning from its industrial past to sectors like healthcare, technology, and agriculture.

## Timeline
- **1638–1655** — A southeast portion of present-day Pennsylvania is part of the Swedish colony New Sweden.
- **1681** — King Charles II grants a land charter to William Penn, establishing the Province of Pennsylvania.
- **1776** — The Second Continental Congress, meeting in Philadelphia, unanimously adopts the Declaration of Independence.
- **1787** — Pennsylvania becomes the second state to ratify the U.S. Constitution.
- **1863** — The Battle of Gettysburg, the Civil War's bloodiest battle, is fought in Pennsylvania.
- **1907** — The current Pennsylvania State Capitol building in Harrisburg is dedicated.
- **1979** — The Three Mile Island nuclear accident occurs, the most significant in U.S. commercial nuclear history.
- **2001** — United Airlines Flight 93 crashes near Shanksville during the September 11 terrorist attacks.
- **2024** — An assassination attempt on former President Donald Trump occurs near Butler, Pennsylvania.

## Body

### History
Pennsylvania's human habitation dates back over 10,000 years. Before European colonization, the Lenape and Susquehannock tribes were the primary inhabitants. The Dutch and Swedish established early settlements in the 17th century before the English took control. In 1681, King Charles II granted the land to William Penn to repay a debt, naming it "Penn's Woods." Penn established a government with innovations like freedom of religion and maintained peaceful relations with the Lenape.

Pennsylvania was central to the American Revolution, hosting the First and Second Continental Congresses in Philadelphia. It was the second state to ratify the U.S. Constitution in 1787. The 19th century saw industrial growth, with the state becoming a leader in steel, coal, and oil production. The Civil War's pivotal Battle of Gettysburg was fought in Pennsylvania in 1863. The 20th century brought further industrialization, labor movements, and later, economic restructuring as manufacturing declined.

### Geography and Climate
Pennsylvania is the 33rd-largest state by land area, with a diverse geography including the Appalachian Mountains, the Allegheny and Pocono mountains, and extensive forests covering nearly 60% of the state. It has no oceanic shoreline but features waterfront along Lake Erie and the tidal Delaware River. The state experiences a humid continental climate with cool to cold winters and warm, humid summers, with significant regional variation.

### Demographics
As of the 2020 census, Pennsylvania had a population of over 13 million, making it the fifth-most populous state. The largest ancestry groups are German, Irish, and Italian. The Hispanic/Latino population has grown significantly, primarily from Puerto Rican migration. English is the primary language, with Spanish and Pennsylvania German (often called Pennsylvania Dutch) also spoken. The state is religiously diverse, with Christianity as the dominant faith, including large Catholic, Protestant, and Anabaptist (e.g., Amish, Mennonite) communities.

### Economy
Pennsylvania's 2025 gross state product was $1.056 trillion, the sixth-largest among U.S. states. The economy is diverse, with major sectors including healthcare, education, manufacturing, agriculture, and finance. The state is a leading producer of mushrooms, apples, and dairy, and is the nation's top producer of pretzels and potato chips. It is home to 23 Fortune 500 companies, including Comcast and U.S. Steel. The Lehigh Valley is a major logistics hub.

### Government and Politics
Pennsylvania has a bicameral legislature and a governor-led executive branch. The state is a key presidential swing vote. Its political landscape is often described as having two strong Democratic urban centers (Philadelphia and Pittsburgh) surrounded by more conservative rural areas. The state retains the death penalty but has a gubernatorial hold on executions.

### Culture and Recreation
Pennsylvania's culture reflects its history and diversity, with notable foods like cheesesteaks (Philadelphia), pierogies (Pittsburgh), and Pennsylvania Dutch dishes. The state is home to eight major professional sports teams, the Little League World Series, and numerous museums and amusement parks. Hunting and fishing are popular recreational activities.

## Terms
- **Commonwealth:** A formal title used by Pennsylvania (and three other states) in its official name, signifying it is a state with a republican form of government.
- **Frame of Government:** The series of constitutions (1682, 1683, 1696, 1701) that governed the Province of Pennsylvania before U.S. independence.
- **Pennsylvania Dutch (German):** A West Central German dialect still spoken by Old Order Amish and Mennonite communities, not related to the Dutch language.
- **Anabaptist:** A Christian movement (including Amish, Mennonites, and Brethren) that practices believer's baptism and often maintains separation from mainstream society.
- **Gross State Product (GSP):** The total value of goods and services produced within a state, equivalent to a state's GDP.
- **Keystone State:** Pennsylvania's primary nickname, referencing its central geographic and political role among the original thirteen colonies.
- **Cellicon State:** A 21st-century nickname referencing the role of Philadelphia and Pittsburgh in developing cancer immunotherapies.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The exact timing of the first human settlement in present-day Pennsylvania is unclear, though the Meadowcroft Rockshelter site suggests activity over 10,000 years ago, possibly pre-dating the Clovis culture.
- The political alignment of Pennsylvania's suburbs and rural areas continues to evolve, influencing its status as a critical swing state in national elections.
- The long-term economic and environmental remediation of the state's extensive abandoned mine lands remains a significant challenge.

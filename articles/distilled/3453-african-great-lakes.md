# African Great Lakes

## Overview
The African Great Lakes are a series of major lakes in and around the East African Rift, containing about 25% of the planet's unfrozen surface fresh water. They are a region of immense biodiversity, historical significance as the source of the Nile, and complex human geography, having been home to ancient hominins and later organized into powerful kingdoms before facing modern challenges of conflict and refugee crises.

## Timeline
- **c. 12 million years ago** — Formation of the Great Rift Valley begins, altering regional drainage patterns.
- **c. 2–3 million years ago** — Lake Turkana is larger and more fertile, becoming a center for early hominid activity.
- **1972** — Richard Leakey's team discovers the 2-million-year-old Skull 1470 at Lake Turkana.
- **1984** — The nearly complete "Turkana Boy" skeleton (Homo erectus) is discovered at Lake Turkana.
- **1999** — A 3.5-million-year-old skull, named Kenyanthropus platyops, is found at Lake Turkana.
- **Pre-colonial era** — The region's high population and agricultural surplus lead to the formation of powerful monarchies like Buganda and Rwanda.
- **19th century** — Christian missionaries become the first Europeans to arrive in numbers, opening the region to later colonization.
- **c. 1980–2020** — The region suffers from decades of civil war and conflict.
- **2020** — The population of the Great Lakes region is estimated to be over 50 million people.
- **2022** — The UNHCR commends Tanzania for consistently aiding refugees from the region.

## Body

### Geography and Hydrology
The African Great Lakes include Lake Victoria (world's 2nd largest freshwater lake by area), Lake Tanganyika (2nd by volume and depth), Lake Malawi (4th by volume), and Lake Turkana (world's largest permanent desert and alkaline lake). Collectively, they hold 31,000 km³ of water. The lakes are grouped by drainage basin: those draining into the White Nile (e.g., Victoria, Albert), the Congo River (e.g., Tanganyika, Kivu), the Zambezi (Malawi), and closed basins (Turkana). The region is defined by ten riparian countries.

### Geology and Climate
The lakes were formed by the Great Rift Valley, a weak point in Earth's crust where tectonic plates separate, creating troughs that fill with water. This rift began as East Africa separated from the rest of the continent. Lake Victoria is an exception, occupying a depression between the Eastern and Western Rifts. The climate varies: highlands are cool (17–19°C) with abundant rainfall, while lowlands like the Congo Basin are hot (avg. 35°C). The area around Lake Turkana is hot and very dry.

### Biodiversity and Ecology
The freshwater lakes of the Western Rift are biodiversity hotspots, hosting over 1,500 endemic cichlid fish species and about 10% of the world's fish species. Habitats support amphibians, Nile crocodiles, elephants, gorillas, and hippos. Lake Turkana is a key site for endemic birds and a flyway for migrants, supported by plankton. Vegetation ranges from rainforest to savanna. Invasive plants like water hyacinth are a problem, notably on Lake Victoria.

### Human History and Economy
The region's high population and fertile volcanic soils supported early agriculture and the rise of powerful kingdoms. European interest, driven by the search for the Nile's source, led to missionary activity and colonization, which brought devastating epidemics. Today, fishing (tilapia, Nile perch) is a primary livelihood, with Uganda being a major producer. Economies vary, with GDP per capita ranging from $600 in Burundi and DRC to $800 in Uganda.

### Archaeological Significance
The Lake Turkana basin was a crucial center for early hominin evolution due to its ancient fertile conditions. Excavations led by Richard Leakey have yielded major discoveries, including the 2-million-year-old Skull 1470 (possibly *Homo rudolfensis*), the "Turkana Boy" (*Homo erectus*), and the 3.5-million-year-old *Kenyanthropus platyops*.

## Terms
- **Rift Valley**: A low area between hills or mountain ranges, often formed by tectonic plate separation.
- **Endemic species**: Species native to and found only in a specific geographic area.
- **Cichlid**: A diverse family of fish, highly prevalent in the African Great Lakes.
- **Drainage basin**: An area of land where all water drains to a common outlet, like a river or lake.
- **Interlacustrine**: An adjective meaning "between lakes," referring to the region or its nations.
- **Hominid**: A primate of the family Hominidae, including humans and their extinct ancestors.
- **Tectonic plates**: Massive, irregularly shaped slabs of solid rock that make up Earth's lithosphere.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The exact number of lakes considered part of the "African Great Lakes" varies by list.
- The taxonomic classification of Skull 1470 is debated; it was originally thought to be *Homo habilis* but some anthropologists assign it to *Homo rudolfensis*.
- The region's post-independence potential has been undermined by decades of conflict, presenting ongoing challenges for stability and development.

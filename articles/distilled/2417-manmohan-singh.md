# Manmohan Singh

## Overview
Manmohan Singh (1932–2024) was an Indian economist and politician who served as the country's Prime Minister from 2004 to 2014. As the architect of India's 1991 economic liberalization and the first (and only) Sikh to hold the office, his tenure is defined by significant economic growth, landmark social welfare legislation, and a pragmatic foreign policy, though later marred by corruption scandals.

## Timeline
- **26 Sep 1932** — Born in Gah, Punjab, British India (now Pakistan).
- **1947** — Family migrates to India during Partition.
- **1962** — Completes D.Phil. in Economics at the University of Oxford.
- **1991** — Appointed Finance Minister under P. V. Narasimha Rao; begins economic liberalization.
- **2004** — Becomes Prime Minister after Sonia Gandhi declines the post.
- **2005** — Key legislation passed: National Rural Employment Guarantee Act (NREGA) and Right to Information Act.
- **2008** — Indo-US Civil Nuclear Agreement signed.
- **2009** — Re-elected as Prime Minister, becoming the first since Nehru to win re-election after a full term.
- **2014** — UPA loses general election; Singh's premiership ends.
- **26 Dec 2024** — Dies in New Delhi at age 92.

## Body

### Early Life and Academic Career
Born into a Sikh family in what is now Pakistan, Singh migrated to India after the 1947 Partition. He excelled academically, earning degrees from Panjab University, the University of Cambridge, and a doctorate from Oxford. His early career was in academia and international organizations, including a stint at the United Nations Conference on Trade and Development (UNCTAD). He held several senior bureaucratic roles in India, including Chief Economic Advisor (1972–1976), Governor of the Reserve Bank of India (1982–1985), and head of the Planning Commission (1985–1987).

### 1991 Economic Liberalization
In 1991, facing a severe balance-of-payments crisis with foreign reserves at just US$1 billion, Prime Minister P. V. Narasimha Rao appointed Singh as Finance Minister. Despite internal party resistance, Singh implemented sweeping structural reforms. He dismantled the "License Raj" (a system of bureaucratic controls on industry), reduced state control, lowered import taxes, and opened the economy to foreign direct investment (FDI) and privatization. These reforms shifted India from a socialist-oriented economy to a more market-based one, earning him a global reputation as a reform-minded economist.

### First Term as Prime Minister (2004–2009)
After the 2004 elections, Congress leader Sonia Gandhi unexpectedly nominated Singh, a technocrat who had never won a direct election, as Prime Minister. His government launched major social welfare programs, including the National Rural Health Mission (NHRM) and the Unique Identification Authority of India. It passed the Right to Information Act (2005) and the National Rural Employment Guarantee Act (NREGA). A major foreign policy achievement was the 2008 Indo-US Civil Nuclear Agreement, which nearly collapsed his government when Left Front allies withdrew support. His first term saw high economic growth, peaking at 9% GDP growth in 2007.

### Second Term and Legacy (2009–2014)
Singh won re-election in 2009, but his second term was dominated by political scandals, notably the 2G spectrum case and the Commonwealth Games corruption allegations. The government's approval rating fell to 30% by 2013. Key legislation included the Right to Education Act (2009) and the Land Acquisition Act (2013). He did not contest the 2014 election, which the BJP-led NDA won decisively. Singh remained a Rajya Sabha member until 2024. He was widely praised for his personal integrity and economic vision but criticized by opponents as a "weak" leader beholden to the Gandhi family.

## Terms
- **License Raj**: The system of licenses, regulations, and red tape that governed Indian industry and business from 1947 until the 1991 liberalization.
- **Rajya Sabha**: The upper house of India's bicameral Parliament.
- **United Progressive Alliance (UPA)**: A coalition of center-left political parties in India led by the Indian National Congress, which governed from 2004 to 2014.
- **National Rural Employment Guarantee Act (NREGA)**: A 2005 law guaranteeing 100 days of wage employment per year to rural households whose adult members volunteer for unskilled manual work.
- **Right to Information Act (RTI)**: A 2005 law that empowers Indian citizens to request information from public authorities, promoting transparency and accountability.
- **Foreign Direct Investment (FDI)**: An investment made by a firm or individual in one country into business interests located in another country.
- **Balance of Payments**: A statement of all transactions made between entities in one country and the rest of the world over a defined period, such as a quarter or a year.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Economic Legacy vs. Scandals**: Scholars debate whether the high growth and social welfare achievements of Singh's tenure are overshadowed by the corruption scandals of his second term.
- **Leadership Style**: A persistent debate concerns whether Singh was a decisive, independent leader or a "weak" figurehead controlled by Congress President Sonia Gandhi (referred to as "10 Janpath").
- **Effectiveness of Reforms**: The long-term impact and inclusivity of the 1991 liberalization and his government's welfare schemes continue to be analyzed by economists.

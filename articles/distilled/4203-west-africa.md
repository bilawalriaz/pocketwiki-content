# West Africa

## Overview
West Africa is the westernmost region of the African continent, defined by the United Nations as 16 sovereign nations and one British Overseas Territory. Historically, it was home to powerful empires that controlled trans-Saharan trade and later became a focal point for the Atlantic slave trade and European colonialism. Today, it is one of Africa's fastest-growing regions demographically and economically, but faces significant environmental and developmental challenges.

## Timeline
- **c. 780,000–126,000 BP** — Acheulean tool-using archaic humans may have dwelled in West Africa.
- **By 9400 BCE** — Niger-Congo speakers create the earliest pottery in Africa in Ounjougou, Mali.
- **c. 1600 BCE** — Dhar Tichitt, an early centralized state, begins in modern Mauritania.
- **2nd–12th centuries CE** — The Ghana Empire flourishes, controlling regional trade.
- **c. 1240** — Sundiata Keita defeats the Sosso Empire, founding the Mali Empire.
- **15th century** — The Songhai Empire becomes the dominant state in the region.
- **1445 onward** — Portuguese traders establish coastal settlements, initiating European contact and the Atlantic slave trade.
- **Early 19th century** — Fulani reformist jihads sweep the region, establishing states like the Fulani Empire.
- **1897** — The British Empire annexs the Benin Empire.
- **1957** — Ghana becomes the first West African colony to achieve independence.
- **1974** — All West African nations are autonomous from colonial rule.

## Body

### Prehistory and Early Civilizations
West Africa has a deep history of human settlement. Archaic humans may have been present from the Middle Pleistocene. During the Holocene, independent innovations occurred, including the creation of pottery by Niger-Congo speakers and the domestication of crops like yams, rice, and pearl millet. The region saw the rise of early centralized states, beginning with Dhar Tichitt (c. 1600 BCE) and Djenné-Djenno (c. 300 BCE).

### Empires and Kingdoms
The region's economy supported powerful empires. The Ghana Empire (2nd–12th centuries CE) was succeeded by the Mali Empire, which flourished under rulers like Musa I before collapsing. The Songhai Empire then became dominant in the 15th century. South of the Sudan, city-states like the Kingdom of Nri (10th century), the Oyo Empire (14th century), and the Benin Empire (c. 11th century CE) developed complex political and artistic traditions.

### European Contact, Slave Trade, and Colonialism
Portuguese traders arrived in 1445, followed by other European powers. The ensuing Atlantic slave trade debilitated the region's economy and population for centuries. In the 19th century, European powers, primarily France and Britain, advanced during the Scramble for Africa, subjugating local kingdoms. Britain controlled The Gambia, Sierra Leone, Ghana, and Nigeria, while France unified its territories into French West Africa. Only Liberia retained its independence.

### Postcolonial Era and Modern Challenges
Ghana was the first to gain independence in 1957, with full regional autonomy achieved by 1974. The postcolonial era has been marked by political instability, civil wars (e.g., in Nigeria, Sierra Leone, Liberia, and Ivory Coast), and military coups. Economically, nations like Ivory Coast, Ghana, Nigeria, and Senegal are active regionally and globally. The region faces severe environmental threats, including one of the world's worst deforestation rates, overfishing, and biodiversity loss.

## Terms
- **Sahel:** A semi-arid transitional zone between the Sahara Desert to the north and the Sudanian Savanna to the south.
- **Griots:** Hereditary caste of musicians and oral historians in West African cultures (e.g., Mande, Wolof) who preserve and perform histories and praise songs.
- **ECOWAS:** Economic Community of West African States, a regional political and economic union.
- **CFA franc:** A currency used by eight mostly Francophone West African countries in the West African Monetary Union (UEMOA).
- **Nsibidi:** An ancient system of symbols indigenous to southeastern Nigeria, possibly dating to 2000 BCE.
- **Harmattan:** A dry, dusty wind from the Sahara that affects West Africa.
- **Boubou:** A flowing, wide-sleeved robe that is a traditional garment across West Africa.
- **Jollof rice:** A popular one-pot rice dish with origins in the Kingdom of Jolof (modern Senegal).

## Debates and Open Questions
- The exact timeline and nature of the transition from hunter-gatherer societies to agricultural communities in West Africa are subjects of ongoing archaeological research.
- The precise origins and development dates of cultural elements like Nsibidi script and the "Venus of Thiaroye" statuette are debated, with estimates ranging from 5000 BCE to 2000 BCE.
- The historical impact of the Atlantic slave trade versus later colonial exploitation on the region's long-term economic development is a complex scholarly discussion.

# Bering Sea

## Overview
The Bering Sea is a marginal sea of the Northern Pacific Ocean that separates the continents of Eurasia and the Americas. It is a highly productive ecosystem supporting major commercial fisheries and diverse marine life, but its future is uncertain due to climate change.

## Timeline
- **c. Last Glacial Period** — Low sea levels created the "Bering land bridge," allowing human and animal migration between Asia and North America.
- **1728** — Danish-born Russian navigator Vitus Bering becomes the first European to systematically explore the sea.
- **18 December 2018** — A large meteor explodes above the Bering Sea at an altitude of 25.6 km, releasing 49 kilotons of energy.

## Body

### Geography and Extent
The Bering Sea covers over 2,000,000 square kilometers (770,000 sq mi). It is bordered by Alaska to the east, the Russian Far East and Kamchatka Peninsula to the west, the Alaska Peninsula and Aleutian Islands to the south, and the Bering Strait to the north. The International Hydrographic Organization defines its southern limit as a line from Kabuch Point in Alaska through the Aleutian and Komandorski Islands to Cape Kamchatka. Key regions include Bristol Bay, the Gulf of Anadyr, and Norton Sound. The sea contains 16 submarine canyons, including the world's largest, Zhemchug Canyon.

### Ecosystem and Productivity
The Bering Sea ecosystem is driven by two main factors. First, the shelf break, where the continental shelf drops into the deeper North Aleutians Basin, creates a "Greenbelt" of nutrient upwelling that fuels constant phytoplankton production. Second, seasonal sea ice triggers spring phytoplankton blooms; its melting causes water stratification and provides substrate for algae growth. Evidence suggests significant ecosystem change, including a 30–40% decline in average seasonal primary productivity over the last 50 years, implying a lower carrying capacity.

### Biodiversity
The sea supports numerous whale species, including beluga, humpback, bowhead, gray, blue, sperm, fin, sei, and the rare North Pacific right whale. Other marine mammals include walrus, Steller sea lion, northern fur seal, orca, and polar bear. It is a critical habitat for over 30 seabird species and approximately 20 million breeding individuals. At least 419 fish species have been reported. Two species, the Steller's sea cow and spectacled cormorant, are extinct due to human overexploitation.

### Fisheries and Economy
The Bering Sea is renowned for its productive fisheries, including king crab, snow crab, Bristol Bay salmon, pollock, and other groundfish. These fisheries are vital to the economies of the United States and Russia, with U.S. commercial catches worth approximately $1 billion annually and Russian catches worth about $600 million. Landings from Alaskan waters represent half the U.S. catch of fish and shellfish.

### Climate and Change
The future evolution of the Bering Sea's climate and ecosystem is uncertain due to Arctic changes. Between 1979 and 2012, the region experienced a small growth in sea ice extent, contrasting with the substantial loss of summer sea ice in the Arctic Ocean to the north.

## Terms
- **Marginal sea:** A sea that is partially enclosed by islands, archipelagos, or peninsulas.
- **Continental shelf:** The extended perimeter of each continent and associated coastal plain, covered by relatively shallow water.
- **Bering land bridge:** The prehistoric land connection between Asia and North America, exposed during glacial periods of low sea level.
- **Shelf break:** The point where the relatively flat continental shelf drops off into the steeper continental slope.
- **Phytoplankton:** Microscopic marine plants that form the base of the ocean food web.
- **Upwelling:** The process where cold, nutrient-rich water rises from the deep ocean to the surface, stimulating biological productivity.
- **Sea ice:** Frozen ocean water that forms, grows, and melts in the ocean.
- **Primary productivity:** The rate at which energy is converted by photosynthetic and chemosynthetic autotrophs to organic substances.

## Debates and Open questions
- The acceptance of the Bering land bridge as the first point of human entry into the Americas is noted as "accepted by most, though not all scientists."
- The future evolution of the Bering Sea climate and ecosystem is explicitly stated as "uncertain" due to ongoing Arctic changes.

# Aleksandr Karelin

## Overview
Aleksandr Karelin is a Russian politician and retired Greco-Roman wrestler, widely considered the greatest and most dominant in the history of the sport. His career, spanning from 1986 to 2000, was marked by unprecedented physical dominance, a near-perfect record, and multiple Olympic and World Championship titles, making him a legendary figure in combat sports.

## Timeline
- **19 September 1967** — Born in Novosibirsk, Russian SFSR, Soviet Union.
- **1981** — Begins training in wrestling under coach Viktor Kuznetsov.
- **1986** — Wins first senior-level tournaments; begins international career.
- **1988** — Wins first Olympic gold medal (Seoul) for the Soviet Union at age 21.
- **1992** — Wins second Olympic gold medal (Barcelona) for the Unified Team.
- **1996** — Wins third Olympic gold medal (Atlanta) for Russia; named Hero of the Russian Federation.
- **1999** — Enters politics, elected to the Russian State Duma; defeats Akira Maeda in an MMA bout.
- **2000** — Loses 0-1 to Rulon Gardner in the Sydney Olympic final, his only international defeat; retires from competition.
- **2003** — Inducted into the inaugural FILA International Wrestling Hall of Fame.
- **2020** — Appointed as a senator in the Russian Federation Council.

## Body

### Wrestling Career and Dominance
Karelin competed in the super heavyweight division (130 kg) from 1986 to 2000. He won three Olympic gold medals (1988, 1992, 1996) and a silver (2000), nine World Championship gold medals, and twelve European Championship gold medals. His senior-level record was 887 wins and only two losses, both by a single point and both considered controversial. He went undefeated in World Championship matches and held the record for most world-level gold medals (12) and tied for most world-level medals (13) in men's wrestling. He was named the greatest Greco-Roman wrestler of the 20th century by FILA.

### Training and Physical Prowess
Karelin was renowned for his extraordinary strength, agility, and conditioning. His daily training included rowing, long runs through the Taiga forest with a log, and heavy weightlifting, including bench presses over 204 kg (450 lb) and clean and presses over 190 kg (420 lb). He could perform up to 50 chin-ups and was known for his flexibility, performing backflips and splits despite his size. He attributed his success to intense training, stating, "The most important drug is to train like a madman."

### Signature Technique and Style
Karelin's signature move was the "Karelin Lift," a reverse body lift where he would hoist a prone opponent into the air and slam them to the mat for a maximum 5-point throw. This required immense strength to execute against elite heavyweights. He possessed an exceptionally long reach (213-220 cm) and grip strength compared to an "anaconda." His combination of strength, explosiveness, and agility led commentator Joe Rogan to describe him as a "human panther."

### Injuries and Resilience
Throughout his career, Karelin overcame severe injuries, including a broken leg at 15, a concussion, broken ribs, and a torn pectoral muscle. He often competed and won major championships while injured, crediting his fast recoveries to team physician Valery Okhapkin.

### Later Career and Legacy
After his sole international loss to Rulon Gardner at the 2000 Olympics, Karelin retired. He transitioned to politics in 1999, serving in the State Duma and later the Federation Council. He is celebrated as one of the most dominant and intimidating athletes of all time, with his loss to Gardner considered one of the biggest upsets in sports history.

## Terms
- **Greco-Roman Wrestling**: A style of wrestling where holds below the waist are forbidden.
- **Super Heavyweight**: The heaviest weight class in wrestling; at the time, 130 kg (286 lb).
- **Karelin Lift**: Karelin's signature move, a reverse body lift and slam worth 5 points.
- **FILA**: International Federation of Associated Wrestling Styles, the sport's former global governing body.
- **State Duma**: The lower house of the Russian parliament.
- **Federation Council**: The upper house of the Russian parliament.
- **Sambo**: A Soviet/Russian martial art and combat sport.
- **Hero of the Russian Federation**: A high state decoration of the Russian Federation.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Controversial Losses**: Both of Karelin's senior-level losses (1987 USSR Championships to Igor Rostorotsky and 2000 Olympics to Rulon Gardner) are considered controversial. Some, like Finnish wrestler Tuomo Karila, argue Gardner should not have been awarded the decisive point.
- **Gardner Upset**: The magnitude of Karelin's loss to Gardner is debated as one of the greatest upsets in sports history, given Karelin's prior dominance and his previous 5-0 victory over Gardner.
- **"The Experiment" Nickname**: The origin of this nickname is debated; it is linked to speculation about performance-enhancing drugs, which Karelin denied, attributing his physique to extreme training.

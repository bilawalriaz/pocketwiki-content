# Sapporo

## Overview
Sapporo is the largest city in Hokkaido, Japan, and the fifth-most populous in the country. It serves as the prefecture's cultural, economic, and political center. Founded as a planned administrative capital in 1869, it grew rapidly in the 20th century, hosting the 1972 Winter Olympics and becoming a major hub for tourism, industry, and education.

## Timeline
- **1866** — Construction begins on a canal, encouraging settlement of Sapporo village.
- **1868** — Meiji government decides to establish a new capital on the Ishikari Plain.
- **1869** — Hokkaido Development Commission headquarters established in Sapporo.
- **1876** — William S. Clark helps found Sapporo Agricultural College (now Hokkaido University).
- **1922** — Sapporo-ku is officially renamed Sapporo City.
- **1937** — Sapporo chosen to host the 1940 Winter Olympics (later cancelled).
- **1950** — First Sapporo Snow Festival is held.
- **1971** — Sapporo Municipal Subway system inaugurated.
- **1972** — Hosts the 1972 Winter Olympics, the first in Asia.
- **2001** — Sapporo Dome construction completed.
- **2002** — Sapporo Dome hosts matches for the FIFA World Cup.
- **2039** — Planned completion of the Hokkaido Shinkansen line to Sapporo.

## Body
### Founding and Early Development
Before its establishment, the area was inhabited by the indigenous Ainu people. The city began in 1868 when the Meiji government selected the Ishikari Plain for a new capital, as the existing center at Hakodate was deemed unsuitable. Construction started around Odori Park, following a grid plan inspired by Kyoto. American advisors, including Horace Capron, assisted in development. The Hokkaido Development Commission was abolished in 1882 as the region prospered.

### 20th Century Growth and Modernization
Sapporo was officially renamed a city in 1922. It was selected for the 1940 Winter Olympics, but the event was cancelled due to war. After World War II, the city expanded through mergers with neighboring towns. It replaced Otaru as Hokkaido's commercial hub, surpassing a population of one million by 1970. The subway opened in 1971, and the city hosted the 1972 Winter Olympics. It was designated a government-ordinance city in 1972, establishing its current ten wards.

### 21st Century and International Events
The Sapporo Dome was completed in 2001, hosting FIFA World Cup matches in 2002. The city has hosted the Asian Winter Games three times (1986, 1990, 2017) and matches for the 2019 Rugby World Cup. The Hokkaido Shinkansen high-speed rail line is planned to reach Sapporo by 2039. The economy is dominated by the tertiary sector, with major industries in IT, retail, tourism, and manufacturing, including the Sapporo Breweries.

### Geography and Climate
Sapporo lies on the alluvial fan of the Toyohira River in southwestern Hokkaido. The urban district has a grid layout, while mountains occupy the west and south. It has a humid continental climate with warm, humid summers and cold, very snowy winters, averaging 4.79 meters of snowfall annually. This heavy snowfall enables major winter festivals.

### Culture and Sightseeing
The annual Sapporo Snow Festival draws over 2 million tourists. The city is the birthplace of miso ramen and home to Sapporo Brewery. Notable sites include Odori Park, the Sapporo Beer Museum, the Sapporo TV Tower, and the Hokkaido University campus. It is a member of the UNESCO Creative Cities Network for Media Arts.

## Terms
- **Ainu**: The indigenous people of Hokkaido and northern Japan.
- **Alluvial fan**: A triangle-shaped deposit of gravel, sand, and even smaller pieces of sediment, such as silt.
- **Hokkaido Development Commission (Kaitaku-shi)**: The Meiji government body established to develop Hokkaido.
- **Government-ordinance city**: A major Japanese city with a population over 500,000, granted greater administrative autonomy from the prefecture.
- **Humid continental climate (Köppen: Dfa)**: A climate characterized by large seasonal temperature differences, with warm to hot summers and cold winters.
- **Oyatoi gaikokujin**: Foreign experts hired by the Japanese government during the Meiji era to help modernize the country.
- **Shinkansen**: Japan's network of high-speed rail lines.
- **Tertiary sector**: The segment of the economy providing services rather than producing goods.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The city has considered bids for the 2026 and 2030 Winter Olympics but is now reportedly considering a bid for the 2034 Winter Olympics, with estimated costs up to 456.5 billion yen.
- The planned extension of the Hokkaido Shinkansen to Sapporo is a major ongoing infrastructure project with a target completion date of March 2039.

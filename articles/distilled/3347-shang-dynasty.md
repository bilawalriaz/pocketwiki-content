# Shang dynasty

## Overview
The Shang dynasty (c. 1600–1046 BC) was the earliest Chinese royal dynasty for which there is both a traditional historical account and firm archaeological evidence. It ruled the Yellow River valley and is crucial for understanding the origins of Chinese civilization, as its oracle bone inscriptions provide the first direct written records of Chinese history, politics, and religion.

## Timeline
- **c. 1600 BC** — Traditional start of the dynasty; possibly linked to the foundation of the Erligang culture walled city at Yanshi.
- **c. 1500 BC** — Large-scale production of ritual bronze vessels and weapons begins.
- **c. 1250 BC** — Earliest extant oracle bone inscriptions date from the reign of King Wu Ding at Yinxu.
- **c. 1046 BC** — Most widely accepted end date; the Shang are defeated by the Zhou at the Battle of Muye.
- **1899** — Scholars identify "dragon bones" sold by pharmacists as ancient oracle bones.
- **1928–1937** — First major scientific excavations at Yinxu (Anyang) uncover royal tombs, palaces, and tens of thousands of artefacts.
- **1950s** — Discovery of the earlier walled Zhengzhou Shang City.
- **1976** — Tomb of Fu Hao, a consort of King Wu Ding, is found undisturbed and richly furnished.

## Body

### Traditional Accounts and Archaeology
The classic history of the Shang comes from texts like the *Book of Documents* and Sima Qian's *Shiji*. For centuries, the Shang were considered legendary until the 20th century, when archaeologists traced oracle bones sold as medicine to the site of Yinxu near Anyang. Excavations there confirmed the final Shang capital of Yin, revealing royal tombs, palace foundations, and sacrificial remains. This made the Shang the earliest Chinese dynasty verified by both text and archaeology.

### Chronology and Succession
The dynasty's traditional dates (1766–1122 BC) are debated. Modern projects like the Xia–Shang–Zhou Chronology Project propose a start c. 1600 BC and an end in 1046 BC, based on radiocarbon dating and astronomical records. The Shang kings, whose names are reconstructed from oracle bones, formed a hereditary line. The dynasty was founded by Tang, who overthrew the last Xia ruler, and ended when the final king, Di Xin, was defeated by the Zhou.

### Society, Religion, and Writing
Shang society was agricultural, with a complex bureaucracy and a warrior aristocracy. The king served as both political and religious leader, performing divination and sacrifices to ancestors and the high god Di. The most significant cultural achievement was a fully developed writing system, preserved primarily on oracle bones (turtle shells and ox bones) used for divination. These inscriptions detail royal activities, warfare, and rituals.

### Bronze Age and Material Culture
The Shang are renowned for their advanced bronze casting, used for ritual vessels and weapons, not everyday tools. This required large-scale organization for mining and production. Archaeological sites like Zhengzhou and Yinxu show massive rammed-earth walls and palaces. The discovery of the tomb of Fu Hao, filled with bronzes and jades, illustrates the wealth and ritual importance of the elite.

### Military and Expansion
The Shang state engaged in frequent warfare with neighboring settlements and northern tribes like the Guifang. The army, equipped with bronze weapons and chariots (appearing c. 1200 BC), could mobilize thousands of conscripts. The king was the supreme military commander, and victories were recorded in oracle bones.

## Terms
- **Oracle Bones**: Turtle shells or ox scapulae used for divination, inscribed with the earliest known Chinese writing.
- **Yinxu**: The archaeological site of the final Shang capital near Anyang, where most oracle bones and royal tombs were found.
- **Rammed Earth**: A construction technique using compacted soil, used for Shang city walls and palace foundations.
- **Bronze Ritual Vessels**: Ceremonial containers made of bronze, used in ancestor worship and status display.
- **Divination**: The practice of seeking knowledge of the future or divine will, central to Shang religion.
- **Erligang Culture**: An early Bronze Age culture (c. 1600 BC) associated with the early Shang period, centered at Zhengzhou.
- **Di**: The high god in Shang religion, believed to control natural forces.
- **Fu Hao**: A consort of King Wu Ding and military general, whose intact tomb was discovered in 1976.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Chronology**: The exact start and end dates of the dynasty remain debated, with proposals for the end ranging from 1130 to 1018 BC.
- **Relationship with Xia**: Whether the preceding Xia dynasty was a concurrent political entity or a later myth is uncertain.
- **Shamanism**: The degree to which shamanism was central to Shang religion is debated among scholars.
- **Gija Joseon**: Legends claim a Shang prince founded a state in Korea, but its historical accuracy is disputed.

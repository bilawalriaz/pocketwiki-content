# Su Song

## Overview
Su Song (1020–1101) was a Chinese polymath and statesman of the Song dynasty whose work in horology, astronomy, and pharmacology represents a pinnacle of medieval Chinese science. He is best known for designing a monumental water-powered astronomical clock tower, which featured the world's first known mechanical clock drive and an endless chain drive, and for compiling major scientific treatises that preserved and advanced knowledge across multiple disciplines.

## Timeline
- **1020** — Su Song is born in modern-day Fujian.
- **1063** — Edits the Han-dynasty classic *Huainanzi*.
- **1070** — Co-compiles the *Bencao Tujing* (Illustrated Pharmacopoeia).
- **1077** — Diplomatic mission to the Liao dynasty; acknowledges Liao calendar's superior accuracy.
- **1086** — Emperor Zhezong commissions Su Song to reconstruct an armillary clock for a new tower.
- **1088** — Wooden pilot model of the astronomical clock tower is completed.
- **1092** — Writes the horological treatise *Xinyi Xiangfayao*.
- **1094** — The full-scale bronze astronomical clock tower is completed.
- **1127** — Capital moves south; clock tower is dismantled and later cannot be reassembled.

## Body

### Career as a Scholar-Official
Su Song rose through the imperial examination system, mastering calendrical science. He held high-ranking posts, including Minister of Justice and Vice President of the Chancellery Secretariat, while navigating the political factionalism between Reformists and Conservatives. In 1077, a diplomatic mission to the Liao dynasty revealed their calendar was slightly more accurate than the Song's, an admission that led to official punishments. Historians suggest Emperor Zhezong later sponsored Su's clock tower project in 1086 to compete with the Liao for scientific prestige.

### Astronomy and Cartography
Su Song created a celestial atlas with star maps using an equidistant cylindrical projection, making them the oldest extant printed star maps. His work incorporated the more accurate pole star position calculated by his contemporary Shen Kuo. He also compiled terrestrial maps and used his cartographic expertise to help settle a Song-Liao border dispute.

### Pharmacology and Natural History
In 1070, Su Song and a team of scholars compiled the *Bencao Tujing*, a groundbreaking illustrated pharmacopoeia. It systematically documented pharmaceutical botany, zoology, and mineralogy, including the use of ephedrine, descriptions of mineral properties like realgar and cinnabar, and the habitats of various animals. This work preserved earlier knowledge and was later incorporated into the Ming-dynasty *Bencao Gangmu*.

### Horology and the Astronomical Clock Tower
Su Song's greatest achievement was the 40-foot-tall water-powered astronomical clock tower in Kaifeng. Its core innovation was a hydro-mechanical escapement mechanism, which used the periodic filling and emptying of containers to regulate the flow of water and drive the clockwork. This united the clepsydra (water clock) with mechanical gearing. The tower featured a rotating armillary sphere and celestial globe, and 133 clock jacks that announced the hours. The escapement evolved from an earlier design by Yi Xing and Liang Lingzan (725 AD), but Su's was the first to use a mechanical clock drive. The tower also employed the world's oldest known endless power-transmitting chain drive, called the "celestial ladder," to transfer motion from the waterwheel to the astronomical instruments.

### Legacy and Transmission
Su Song's treatise *Xinyi Xiangfayao* (1092) detailed the clock's mechanics with 47 illustrations. After the capital moved in 1127, the dismantled tower could not be reassembled, and attempts to build a new one failed. However, the treatise was printed and preserved, influencing later Chinese horology. While the clock tower was a government-sponsored project for court astronomers and did not lead to mass-produced clocks, the mechanical principles continued in later dynasties. Modern reconstructions by Joseph Needham, John Christiansen, and Wang Zhenduo have brought renewed attention to Su Song's engineering.

## Terms
- **Polymath**: A person with expertise in a wide range of different fields.
- **Escapement mechanism**: A device in a clock that regulates the transfer of energy, allowing the gear train to advance at a periodic rate.
- **Armillary sphere**: A model of objects in the sky, consisting of a spherical framework of rings representing lines of celestial longitude and latitude.
- **Clepsydra**: A water clock, a device for measuring time by the regulated flow of water.
- **Endless chain drive**: A power-transmitting mechanism using a continuous chain loop to transfer motion between shafts.
- **Bencao Tujing**: An 11th-century Chinese illustrated pharmacopoeia on botany, zoology, and mineralogy.
- **Xinyi Xiangfayao**: Su Song's 1092 horological treatise detailing the design of his astronomical clock tower.
- **Diurnal motion gear ring**: A toothed ring gear on the armillary sphere that rotated to simulate the daily motion of the heavens.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The precise accuracy of Su Song's clock compared to contemporary European mechanical clocks is debated, as it relied on continuous water power rather than a fully self-contained weight-driven mechanism.
- The extent to which Su Song's work was influenced by or isolated from external technological traditions remains a subject of scholarly discussion.
- The reason for the failure to reassemble or replicate the clock tower after 1127 is not definitively known; one theory suggests Su Song may have intentionally omitted key details in his writings to protect his ideas.

# David Ben-Gurion

## Overview
David Ben-Gurion (1886–1973) was the primary founder and first Prime Minister of the State of Israel. As the de facto leader of the Jewish community in Mandatory Palestine from 1935, he was the central figure in the movement for an independent Jewish state, overseeing its declaration, the 1948 Arab–Israeli War, and the establishment of its core institutions.

## Timeline
- **16 October 1886** — Born David Grün in Płońsk, Congress Poland.
- **1906** — Immigrates to Ottoman Palestine; adopts the name Ben-Gurion in 1909.
- **1920** — Helps found the Histadrut (Zionist Labor Federation) and serves as its general secretary until 1935.
- **1935** — Becomes chairman of the Jewish Agency Executive, the de facto leader of the Yishuv (Jewish community in Palestine).
- **1946** — Becomes executive head of the World Zionist Organization.
- **14 May 1948** — Proclaims the establishment of the State of Israel and signs the Declaration of Independence.
- **1948–1949** — Leads Israel during the 1948 Arab–Israeli War; unifies Jewish militias into the Israel Defense Forces (IDF).
- **1949** — Elected Israel's first Prime Minister after Mapai wins the first national election.
- **1953** — Resigns as Prime Minister; returns to the post in 1955.
- **1956** — Leads Israel in the Suez Crisis alongside Britain and France.
- **1963** — Steps down as Prime Minister; retires from political life in 1970.
- **1 December 1973** — Dies at Sde Boker kibbutz in the Negev desert.

## Body

### Early Life and Zionist Activism
Born to a Polish Jewish family, Ben-Gurion's Zionist convictions developed early. He immigrated to Palestine in 1906, where he worked as a laborer and became a leader in the Poalei Zion party. He advocated for Hebrew as the language of the movement and for a separate Jewish economy. After World War I, he rose through the Zionist labor movement, helping to found the Histadrut in 1920 and later leading the Mapai party.

### Leadership in Mandatory Palestine
As head of the Jewish Agency from 1935, Ben-Gurion was the paramount leader of the Yishuv. He navigated the 1936–1939 Arab revolt with a policy of restraint ("Havlagah") and supported the 1937 Peel Commission's partition plan. During World War II, he encouraged Jewish enlistment in the British Army while facilitating illegal Jewish immigration. After the war, he led the Jewish Resistance Movement against British restrictions, culminating in the 1947 UN Partition Plan, which he accepted.

### Founding of Israel and the 1948 War
On 14 May 1948, Ben-Gurion declared Israeli independence. He immediately unified disparate Jewish militias into the IDF, using force during the Altalena Affair to ensure state monopoly on violence. He oversaw Israel's victory in the ensuing 1948 Arab–Israeli War, which resulted in the expulsion and flight of a majority of the Palestinian Arab population. His strategy, outlined in Plan Dalet, remains debated as either defensive or a blueprint for territorial conquest and population transfer.

### Prime Ministership and State-Building
As Prime Minister (1948–1953, 1955–1963), Ben-Gurion focused on institution-building, mass immigration absorption, and national development projects like the National Water Carrier. His foreign policy included a reparations agreement with West Germany. Domestically, he secured a "status quo agreement" with religious parties, guaranteeing state support for kosher food and the Sabbath. Militarily, he authorized reprisal operations against Palestinian fedayeen and led Israel in the 1956 Suez Crisis. He was deeply committed to developing the Negev, eventually retiring to Kibbutz Sde Boker.

### Later Years and Legacy
Ben-Gurion resigned in 1963, partly due to political isolation and the unresolved Lavon Affair. He formed the Rafi party in 1965 but failed to unseat his successor, Levi Eshkol. He criticized the government's handling of the lead-up to the Six-Day War (1967) but supported the war's outcome, advocating for withdrawal from most captured territories in exchange for peace. He retired from politics in 1970 and died in 1973. He is remembered as "Israel's founding father."

## Terms
*   **Yishuv:** The Jewish community in Palestine prior to the establishment of the State of Israel.
*   **Histadrut:** The General Organization of Workers in the Land of Israel, a powerful Zionist labor federation and political party.
*   **Mapai:** The Workers' Party of the Land of Israel, the dominant center-left Zionist party led by Ben-Gurion from 1930.
*   **Haganah:** The main Jewish paramilitary organization in Mandatory Palestine, which became the core of the IDF.
*   **Plan Dalet (Plan D):** A 1948 Haganah plan for securing the territory of the proposed Jewish state, whose intent regarding population transfer is debated.
*   **Altalena Affair:** A 1948 incident where Ben-Gurion ordered the IDF to fire on a ship carrying arms for the Irgun militia, asserting state authority.
*   **Status Quo Agreement:** A 1947 understanding between Ben-Gurion and Orthodox parties that established state accommodation of Jewish religious law in public life.
*   **Fedayeen:** Palestinian guerrilla fighters who infiltrated into Israel from neighboring Arab states in the 1950s.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Role in the 1948 Palestinian Exodus:** Historians debate whether Ben-Gurion endorsed a policy of "transfer" (expulsion) or if the exodus was a byproduct of war. Benny Morris argues an "atmosphere of transfer" existed, while others note the absence of explicit written orders.
*   **Intent of Plan Dalet:** Scholars are divided on whether Plan Dalet was a defensive military strategy or a deliberate plan for ethnic cleansing and territorial expansion.
*   **1937 Letter:** A letter Ben-Gurion wrote to his son contains scribbled-out text that scholars interpret differently as either evidence of an intention to "expel the Arabs" or the opposite.
*   **Conquest of the West Bank:** There is controversy over whether Ben-Gurion actively sought to conquer the West Bank during the 1948 war or if his proposals were more limited, with the Cabinet rejecting a broader offensive.

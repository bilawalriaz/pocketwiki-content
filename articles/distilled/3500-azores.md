# Azores

## Overview
The Azores are a volcanic archipelago of nine islands in the North Atlantic Ocean, forming an autonomous region of Portugal. Located at a tectonic triple junction, their geology, climate, and history of settlement make them a unique European territory with a distinct culture and strategic importance.

## Timeline
- **c. 8 million years ago** — First island (Santa Maria) begins to form.
- **1427** — Possible discovery by a Portuguese captain sailing for Prince Henry the Navigator.
- **1439** — Settlement begins with migrants from mainland Portugal.
- **1522** — Earthquake and landslide devastate Vila Franca do Campo, the capital of São Miguel.
- **1580–1642** — Azores are under Spanish rule during the Iberian Union.
- **1828–1834** — Liberal Wars; Terceira becomes the base for the liberal regime.
- **1943** — Portugal leases air and naval bases to the UK during WWII (Operation Alacrity).
- **1957–1958** — Last major volcanic eruption at Capelinhos, Faial.
- **1976** — Azores gain status as an Autonomous Region of Portugal.

## Body

### Geography and Geology
The Azores are a volcanic archipelago of nine islands, divided into Eastern, Central, and Western groups. They lie at the Azores triple junction, where the North American, Eurasian, and African tectonic plates meet. This active setting creates frequent seismic activity and volcanism. The islands are geologically young, with Pico being the most recently formed (c. 0.27 million years ago). Mount Pico, at 2,351 m, is Portugal's highest point. The climate is mild and oceanic, influenced by the Gulf Stream, with temperatures typically between 16–25 °C and high humidity.

### History and Settlement
The islands were uninhabited until Portuguese navigators arrived in the 15th century. Settlement began in 1439, primarily with migrants from mainland Portugal, but also included Flemings, who settled in large numbers by 1490. The Azores were the last part of the Portuguese Empire to resist Spanish rule during the Iberian Union (1580–1642). In the 20th century, the islands became a key refueling point for transatlantic flights. Following the 1974 Carnation Revolution in Portugal, the Azores became an autonomous region in 1976.

### Economy and Demographics
The economy is based on services, agriculture (including dairy and cheese), fishing, and tourism. The government is a major employer. The population was 242,796 in 2019. Since the 17th century, emigration has been significant, with major diaspora communities in the United States (especially Rhode Island and Massachusetts), Canada, and Brazil. The islands are divided into 19 municipalities, with Ponta Delgada as the largest city and regional government seat.

### Culture and Governance
The culture reflects its settlement history, with strong Roman Catholic traditions. The most important festivals are the Holy Spirit festivals (Festas do Espírito Santo) and the Festival of Senhor Santo Cristo dos Milagres. Politically, the Azores have their own regional government and legislative assembly but are integrated into the Portuguese Republic. Foreign affairs and defence are handled by the national government. The Lajes Air Base on Terceira is a joint Portuguese-American military facility.

## Terms
- **Autonomous Region:** A political division of Portugal with its own elected government and legislative assembly, but not a sovereign state.
- **Triple Junction:** A point where three tectonic plates meet; the Azores sit at the junction of the North American, Eurasian, and African plates.
- **Macaronesia:** A collection of five volcanic archipelagos in the North Atlantic, including the Azores, Madeira, and the Canary Islands.
- **Laurissilva:** A type of subtropical forest found in Macaronesia, characterized by laurel trees.
- **Império:** A small building or shrine used to host Holy Spirit festivals.
- **Comarca:** A judicial district in the Portuguese legal system.
- **Freguesia:** A civil administrative parish, the smallest local government unit in Portugal.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Pre-Portuguese Settlement:** Archaeological structures (hypogea) and biological evidence (mouse DNA, pollen from lake sediments) suggest possible human presence before the 15th century, possibly by Norse or other groups, but this is not conclusively proven.
- **Discovery Date:** The exact date and discoverer of the Azores in 1427 are uncertain, with multiple historical accounts offering different names.

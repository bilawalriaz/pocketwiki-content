# Rothschild family

## Overview
The Rothschild family is an Ashkenazi Jewish banking dynasty originating in 16th-century Frankfurt, Germany. Their documented history begins with Isaak Elchanan Bacharach, who built the house "Rothschild" (German: *zum rothen Schild*, "at the red shield") in 1567. The family rose to global prominence through Mayer Amschel Rothschild (1744–1812), a court factor to the Landgraves of Hesse-Kassel who established a banking business in the 1760s. Uniquely for court factors of that era, Mayer Amschel bequeathed his wealth and established an international enterprise by stationing his five sons in the five major European financial centres: Frankfurt, London, Paris, Vienna, and Naples. This structure allowed them to move capital as financial instruments (stocks, bonds, debts) across borders, insulating their assets from local violence and expropriation. During the 19th century, the family possessed the largest private fortune in modern history and dominated European finance until the rise of joint-stock banks in the 1870s. Their wealth declined and fragmented in the 20th century due to taxation, the Nazi seizure of Austrian and French assets, and the nationalisation of their French bank in 1982. Today, the French and British branches survive, operating in financial services, real estate, mining, energy, agriculture, and winemaking, alongside significant philanthropic activity. The family has been a persistent target of antisemitic conspiracy theories alleging covert global control.

## Body

### Origins and Structural Innovation
The family name derives from the house *zum rothen Schild* ("at the red shield"), built in Frankfurt in 1567 by Isaak Elchanan Bacharach; the first known user of the surname was Isaak Elchanan Rothschild (born 1577). Mayer Amschel Rothschild (1744–1812), son of a money changer, founded the dynasty's fortune in the 1760s. His critical innovation was dispersing his five sons across Europe’s financial capitals—Amschel Mayer (Frankfurt), Salomon Mayer (Vienna), Nathan Mayer (London), Kalman Mayer (Naples), and Jakob Mayer (Paris)—creating the first truly international banking network. Unlike earlier court factors whose wealth was vulnerable to local rulers, the Rothschilds held assets in portable financial instruments, rendering them "impervious to local attacks" (per Paul Johnson). They maintained secrecy and control through family intermarriage (often first- or second-cousins) until the late 19th century, when they began marrying into aristocracy and other financial dynasties. The five brothers were elevated to the Austrian nobility as *Freiherr* (barons) in 1822; the British branch received a baronetcy in 1847 and a peerage (Baron Rothschild) in 1885.

### Napoleonic Wars and Financial Hegemony
By 1803, the Rothschilds dominated the bullion trade. From London, Nathan Mayer Rothschild (1777–1836) became the primary financier of the British war effort (1813–15), organising bullion shipments to the Duke of Wellington and arranging subsidy payments to allies. In 1815 alone, they provided £9.8 million (≈£910 million in 2024) in subsidy loans. Their private courier network delivered news of Wellington’s victory at Waterloo a full day before official messengers; Nathan immediately informed the government rather than exploiting the information for trade. His most famous profit came afterward: anticipating a post-war bond bounce, he bought heavily at high prices in 1815 and sold in 1817 for a 40% gain, a massive sum given their leverage. By 1818, he arranged a £5 million loan to Prussia, establishing government bond issuance as a core business. In 1825–26, N M Rothschild & Sons averted a Bank of England liquidity crisis by supplying sufficient coin.

### International High Finance and Industrialisation
The family pioneered international high finance during European industrialisation. They financed railway systems globally and complex government projects, notably the Suez Canal. From 1895–1907, they loaned nearly $450 million (≈$15.5 billion in 2025) to European governments. They were instrumental in Brazilian independence (1825), raising £2 million for compensation to Portugal and assuming a £1.5 million Portuguese debt. Major enterprises founded with Rothschild capital include Alliance Assurance (1824), Chemin de Fer du Nord (1845), Rio Tinto mining (1873, under full family control from the 1880s), Eramet (1880), Imerys (1880), and De Beers (1888). They funded Cecil Rhodes’s Rhodesia and the Rhodes Scholarship. By the late 19th century, they owned at least 41 palaces across Europe. In 1909, Chancellor David Lloyd George called Nathan, Lord Rothschild "the most powerful man in Britain." Their influence was such that *Niles' Weekly Register* (1836) described them as holding "a whole continent in the hollow of their hands."

### Decline and 20th-Century Ruptures
The Neapolitan branch collapsed first: Garibaldi’s capture of Naples (1860) and Italian unification (1861) ended their primary clientele (the aristocracy), closing the bank in 1863. The Frankfurt branch closed in 1901 for lack of male heirs. National taxation systems ended their unified accounting, fragmenting the five-house system by World War I. The Nazi annexation of Austria (1938) forced the sale of the Viennese bank at a fraction of value; Louis Rothschild was imprisoned for a year until ransomed. Nazi officers looted Viennese palaces and art collections (250 pieces returned by Austria in 1999). In France, German occupation seized assets; though the bank was restored post-war, it was nationalised in 1982 by President Mitterrand. The family acknowledged a strategic failure in never establishing a major foothold in the United States.

### Branches: English, French, Austrian, Neapolitan
**English Branch:** Founded 1798 by Nathan Mayer in Manchester, moving to London (N M Rothschild & Sons, 1811). Financed Napoleonic subsidies, 1875 Suez Canal purchase (Lionel de Rothschild), Cecil Rhodes, and Rio Tinto/De Beers. Managed by Lionel Nathan, Anthony Gustav, and Sir Evelyn de Rothschild (1931–2022). Merged with French firm in 2003 under David René de Rothschild.
**French Branches:** (1) James Mayer de Rothschild (1792–1868) founded de Rothschild Frères in Paris, financing French railways and industry. Nationalised in 1982 as Compagnie Européenne de Banque; David de Rothschild rebuilt as Rothschild & Cie Banque. (2) Nathaniel de Rothschild (1812–1870), fourth child of Nathan Mayer, moved to Paris in 1850, acquired Château Brane Mouton (renamed Château Mouton Rothschild) in 1853; uncle James acquired neighbouring Château Lafite in 1868.
**Austrian Branch:** Salomon Mayer established a Vienna bank in the 1820s. Rivals included the Sina family. Baron Louis von Schwartz Rothschild tried to save Creditanstalt in 1929. Bank surrendered to Nazis in 1938; palaces confiscated and destroyed. Family escaped Holocaust; some returned post-war.
**Neapolitan Branch:** C M de Rothschild & Figli lent to Papal States, Kings of Naples, Parma, and Tuscany. Shift from bond issues in 1830s hurt growth. 1832 loan to Pope Gregory XVI (£400,000) noted for Carl von Rothschild not kissing the Pope’s feet. Closed 1863 after Italian unification. Described by *Jewish Encyclopedia* (1906) as "guardians of the papal treasure."

### Jewish Identity and Zionism
Support for Zionism varied. Alphonse de Rothschild (French branch) supported *Eretz Israel* after 1868. His brother, **Baron Edmond James de Rothschild** (1845–1934), known as *HaNadiv* ("the Benefactor"), was pivotal: he funded early Jewish agricultural colonies (Rishon LeZion 1882, Petah Tikva 1883), purchased 2–3% of Ottoman Palestine land, and transferred holdings plus 15 million francs to the Jewish Colonisation Association (ICA) in 1896, reorganising it as the Palestine Jewish Colonisation Association (PICA) in 1924 (125,000+ acres). Numerous Israeli towns bear family names (Zichron Ya'akov, Mazkeret Batya, Binyamina, etc.). In Britain, **Walter Rothschild, 2nd Baron Rothschild** (1868–1937), led the Zionist Federation and was addressee of the 1917 Balfour Declaration. However, his nephew Victor, 3rd Baron Rothschild, opposed aiding Jewish refugees in 1938. The family funded the Knesset (James A. de Rothschild) and Supreme Court (Dorothy de Rothschild) buildings in Israel, and established the Yad Hanadiv foundation (1958). Baron Benjamin de Rothschild (d. 2021) supported the peace process but refused to visit Israel due to a tax dispute; his widow Ariane manages the Caesarea Foundation.

### Modern Business, Investments, and Philanthropy
Since the late 19th century, the family maintains a low public profile, donating estates and art (often anonymously). Current operations are smaller in scale but diverse: real estate, financial services, farming, energy, mining, winemaking, nonprofits.
**Rothschild & Co:** Since 2003, controlled by Rothschild Continuation Holdings (Swiss, chaired by David René de Rothschild) → Concordia BV (Dutch) → Paris Orléans S.A. (French, listed on Euronext) → Rothschild Concordia SAS (family holding). Controls N M Rothschild & Sons (London, M&A advisory) and Rothschild & Cie Banque (France/Europe). 20% sold to Jardine Strategic (2005); 7.5% to Rabobank (2008) for East Asian access. N M Rothschild withdrew from gold market (2004) after two centuries; ranked 2nd in UK M&A (2006, $104.9bn deals); £83.2m pre-tax profit (2006).
**Edmond de Rothschild Group:** Founded 1953 in Geneva by Edmond Adolphe de Rothschild (1926–1997); 15 countries; asset management, private banking, mixed farming, luxury hotels, yacht racing. Chaired by Ariane de Rothschild. Benjamin de Rothschild (d. 2021) claimed conservative practices insulated them from the 2008 crisis.
**RIT Capital Partners:** Jacob Rothschild, 4th Baron Rothschild (1936–2024), resigned from N M Rothschild in 1980 to run this investment trust ($3.4bn assets, 2008; LSE listed). Major investor in BullionVault (gold platform). Held significant physical gold, oil, energy (2010). Bought 37% stake in Rockefeller wealth advisory group (2012).
**Investments:** Jacob Rothschild founded J. Rothschild Assurance Group (now St. James's Place, LSE listed) with Sir Mark Weinberg (1991). Nathaniel Rothschild (5th Baron, b. 1971) invested in North Sea oil (2009), Glencore, and United Company RUSAL (2010). Close ties remain with Rio Tinto.
**Wine:** 150-year history. Nathaniel acquired Château Mouton Rothschild (1853); James acquired Château Lafite Rothschild (1868). Both are *Premier Cru Classé* (First Growth, Bordeaux). Current estates span France, Americas, South Africa, Australia. Saskia de Rothschild chairs Lafite (since 2018); Philippe Sereys de Rothschild directs Mouton.
**Art and Charity:** Once held one of the world’s largest private collections; significant donations to public museums (often anonymous). Hannah Rothschild chaired the National Gallery, London (2014).

## Terms
- ****Court Factor**** — A Jewish banker or merchant who managed finances, supplied credit, and provisioned armies for European nobility in the 17th–18th centuries; often vulnerable to expropriation.
- ****Freiherr**** — Austrian hereditary title of nobility, equivalent to Baron; granted to the five Rothschild brothers in 1822.
- ****Bullion Trade**** — The business of buying, transporting, and selling precious metals (gold/silver) in bulk; the Rothschilds dominated this during the Napoleonic Wars.
- ****Subsidy Loans**** — Payments made by one government (Britain) to allies to fund their war efforts; Nathan Mayer Rothschild organised the transfer of £9.8m in 1815 alone.
- ****Joint-Stock Banks**** — Corporations owned by shareholders rather than partners/families; their rise in the 1870s broke the Rothschilds' hegemony over European finance.
- ****HaNadiv**** — Hebrew for "the Benefactor"; honorific for Baron Edmond James de Rothschild (1845–1934) for his funding of early Zionist settlements in Palestine.
- ****PICA**** — Palestine Jewish Colonisation Association (1924), successor to ICA, funded by Edmond de Rothschild; acquired >125,000 acres and established business ventures.
- ****Balfour Declaration**** — 1917 British government letter (addressed to Walter Rothschild, 2nd Baron Rothschild) supporting a "national home for the Jewish people" in Palestine.
- ****Premier Cru Classé**** — "First Growth"; the highest classification for Bordeaux wines. Both Château Lafite Rothschild and Château Mouton Rothschild hold this status.
- ****Nationalisation**** — State takeover of private assets. The French Rothschild bank was nationalised in 1982 by President Mitterrand; the Austrian bank was seized by Nazis in 1938.

## Debates and open questions
- **Source of 19th-century profits:** Paul Johnson (cited in source) argued Nathan Mayer Rothschild in London generated the greatest fortune; Niall Ferguson’s later research contends profits were "greater and equal" across the Paris, Naples, and Frankfurt branches.
- **Waterloo legend:** Popular accounts (e.g., Morton) claim Nathan used early news of Waterloo for market speculation; the source states this is a "fiction," noting he took the news directly to the government and his profitable bond trade occurred *after* the public announcement.
- **Zionism stance:** Not uniform. Edmond James de Rothschild and Walter Rothschild were leading supporters; Victor Rothschild opposed refugee aid in 1938. The family archives show consistent funding for Eastern European Jewry (Alliance Israélite Universelle) and Israeli state infrastructure.
- **American "missed opportunity":** *The New York Times* and Evelyn de Rothschild acknowledged the family "grossly misjudged" and "never seized the initiative in America," viewing it as a major strategic error.
- **Conspiracy theories:** The source explicitly attributes the persistence of theories (world wealth control, war manipulation) to "antisemitic prejudice and various antisemitic tropes," citing historian Niall Ferguson’s rebuttal that as bondholders, the Rothschilds stood to *lose* from war by the mid-19th century.
- **Art restitution:** Austrian government returned 250 looted art treasures in 1999; the extent of unrecovered assets from Nazi seizures (Austria, France) remains an open historical ledger.

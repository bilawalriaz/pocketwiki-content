# Pretoria

## Overview
Pretoria is the executive capital of South Africa, serving as the seat of the national government and hosting all foreign embassies. Founded in 1855 by the Voortrekkers, it evolved from the capital of the Boer South African Republic to the administrative capital of the modern nation. The city is a major academic and research hub, known for its jacaranda trees and its role as a diplomatic centre.

- **1860** — Officially becomes the capital of the ZAR.

- **1900** — Surrenders to British forces during the Second Boer War.

- **1961** — Remains the administrative capital when South Africa becomes a republic.
- **2005** — South African Geographical Names Council approves renaming the city to Tshwane.

## Body

### History and Government
Pretoria was founded in 1855 by Marthinus Pretorius, who named it after his father, Voortrekker hero Andries Pretorius. It became the capital of the South African Republic (ZAR) in 1860. The city was central to the Boer Wars, being besieged in the First Boer War and surrendering to the British in the Second Boer War, which ended with the 1902 Peace of Vereeniging. With the 1910 formation of the Union of South Africa, Pretoria became the administrative capital, a role it retained when the country became a republic in 1961. It hosts the headquarters of all national government departments and 134 foreign embassies, the second-largest concentration globally after Washington, D.C.

### Geography and Climate
Pretoria is located in a fertile valley at an altitude of about 1,339 m (4,393 ft), straddling the Apies River. It has a humid subtropical climate with hot, rainy summers and mild, dry winters. Snowfall is extremely rare. The city has experienced record-breaking heat events, including temperatures of 43 °C (109 °F) in November 2015 and a record high of 42.7 °C (108.9 °F) in January 2016.

### Demographics and Society
The population varies from 700,000 to 2.95 million depending on the area defined. Main languages include Sepedi, Setswana, Xitsonga, Tshivenda, Afrikaans, and English. Pretoria has historically been a major Afrikaner population centre. The city's urban lingua franca is SePitori, a creole language blending Tswana, Northern Sotho, and other languages that developed during apartheid.

### Cityscape and Culture
Known as the "Jacaranda City," Pretoria has approximately 60,000-70,000 jacaranda trees, first planted in 1888. Architecture ranges from 19th-century colonial styles to modern skyscrapers. Notable structures include the Union Buildings, Voortrekker Monument, and Palace of Justice. The city is a major sports hub, with Loftus Versfeld stadium hosting rugby and football. It has a vibrant music scene, being the birthplace of genres like Bacardi and a key centre for Amapiano.

### Education and Research
Pretoria is a leading academic city, home to the University of South Africa (Unisa), the largest distance education university in Africa; the University of Pretoria (UP), a major research institution; and the Tshwane University of Technology (TUT). It also hosts the Council for Scientific and Industrial Research (CSIR), the largest R&D organisation in Africa.

### Proposed Name Change
In 2005, the South African Geographical Names Council approved changing the city's name to Tshwane, the name of the metropolitan municipality. The change has been highly controversial, opposed by various groups, and has not been implemented as of 2026 due to political and legal hurdles.

## Terms
- **Voortrekkers**: Boer pioneers who migrated inland from the Cape Colony in the 1830s-40s.
- **South African Republic (ZAR)**: The Boer republic, also known as the Transvaal, for which Pretoria served as capital.
- **Boer Wars**: Two wars (1880-81, 1899-1902) between the British Empire and the Boer republics.
- **SePitori**: The urban lingua franca of Pretoria, a creole language combining Tswana, Northern Sotho, and other languages.
- **Jacaranda**: A genus of flowering trees, with *Jacaranda mimosifolia* being iconic to Pretoria's landscape.
- **Amapiano**: A South African house music genre blending kwaito, deep house, and jazz, with strong roots in Pretoria.
- **Bacardi**: A style of percussive, bass-heavy music that originated in Pretoria's townships.
- **CSIR**: The Council for Scientific and Industrial Research, South Africa's central scientific R&D organisation.

## Debates and Open Questions
The most significant ongoing debate is the proposed official name change from Pretoria to Tshwane. Approved by the South African Geographical Names Council in 2005, it has faced strong opposition, legal challenges, and political delays, remaining unresolved. The controversy highlights tensions between historical Afrikaner heritage and post-apartheid transformation.

# Nader Shah

## Overview
Nader Shah Afshar (r. 1736–1747) was the founder of the Afsharid dynasty and one of the most powerful rulers in Iranian history. Rising from humble origins during a period of national crisis, he reunited the Iranian realm, expelled foreign invaders, and built a vast empire through brilliant military campaigns. His reign marked the last great era of Iranian imperial expansion before a period of decline and foreign encroachment.

## Timeline
- **1698/1688** — Born in Khorasan to the Afshar tribe.
- **1722** — Safavid dynasty overthrown by Hotaki Afghans; Iran descends into chaos.
- **1726** — Recaptures Mashhad, joining the cause of Safavid claimant Tahmasp II.
- **1729** — Defeats the Hotaki Afghans at Damghan and Murchakhort, recapturing Isfahan.
- **1736** — Deposes the Safavid puppet shah and is crowned Shah of Iran at the Moghan Plain.
- **1738** — Conquers Kandahar, ending the Hotaki dynasty.
- **1739** — Invades India, defeats the Mughal Empire at Karnal, and sacks Delhi.
- **1740** — Conquers the Khanate of Khiva in Central Asia.
- **1743** — Begins a second war against the Ottoman Empire.
- **1747** — Assassinated by his personal guard; his empire quickly disintegrates.

## Body

### Rise to Power
Nader Shah emerged from the chaos following the 1722 Hotaki Afghan overthrow of the weak Safavid ruler Soltan Hoseyn. With the Ottoman and Russian Empires seizing Iranian territory, Nader initially served local powers before joining the Safavid claimant Tahmasp II. Through a series of decisive victories against the Afghans, he became the de facto ruler, eventually deposing Tahmasp in 1732 and having himself crowned Shah in 1736, ending the 200-year Safavid dynasty.

### Military Campaigns and Empire
Nader's military genius created a vast empire. After securing Iran's core, he reconquered territories lost to the Ottomans and Russians in the Caucasus. His 1739 invasion of the Mughal Empire was a masterstroke, culminating in the sack of Delhi and the acquisition of immense wealth, including the Peacock Throne and Koh-i-Noor diamond. This wealth allowed him to suspend taxation in Iran for three years. He also campaigned in Central Asia and Arabia, but his later wars, particularly the second Ottoman campaign, showed diminished brilliance.

### Religious and Domestic Policy
To unify his diverse, multi-ethnic empire and improve relations with Sunni powers like the Ottomans, Nader promoted a syncretic "Ja'fari" school of Islam, compatible with Sunni law, and banned anti-Sunni Shia practices. This was a political move to weaken the Safavid religious legacy and gain legitimacy. Domestically, he reformed the coinage, increased the central army's size at the expense of tribal levies, and was known for his austere lifestyle and strict military discipline.

### Decline and Assassination
Following his Indian campaign, Nader's health declined and his rule became increasingly despotic and paranoid. He blinded his own son, Reza Qoli Mirza, suspecting him of plotting an assassination. His brutal campaigns in Dagestan and heavy taxation to fund his military caused widespread discontent. In June 1747, he was assassinated by members of his own guard. His empire immediately fractured into civil war, leading to the rise of the Zand and Qajar dynasties.

## Terms
*   **Afsharid dynasty:** The ruling dynasty of Iran founded by Nader Shah, succeeding the Safavids.
*   **Qizilbash:** A coalition of Turkic tribes who were the military backbone of the Safavid dynasty.
*   **Hotaki dynasty:** A Pashtun dynasty from Afghanistan that overthrew the Safavids in 1722.
*   **Ja'fari school:** A version of Shia Islam promoted by Nader Shah that was designed to be more compatible with Sunni Islam, named after the sixth Shia imam.
*   **Peacock Throne:** A legendary jeweled throne from the Mughal Empire, looted by Nader Shah after his invasion of India.
*   **Qoroltai:** A grand traditional Mongol/Turkic assembly, which Nader convened to legitimize his seizure of the throne.
*   **Koh-i-Noor:** A famous diamond ("Mountain of Light") looted from the Mughals, later becoming part of the British Crown Jewels.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Motivations for the Indian Campaign:** Historians debate whether Nader's primary goal was to secure Iran's eastern frontier by removing Afghan rebels, to replenish the state treasury with Mughal wealth, or simply for personal conquest.
*   **Religious Sincerity:** Scholars question whether Nader's promotion of the Ja'fari school was a genuine attempt at religious reform or a purely political tool to consolidate power and challenge Ottoman and Safavid religious authority.
*   **Historical Legacy:** His reputation is contested; he is celebrated as a national hero and unifier by Iranian nationalists but condemned as a brutal tyrant whose military spending ruined the economy and whose death triggered a century of instability.

# Brunei

## Overview
Brunei Darussalam is a small, oil-rich absolute monarchy on the island of Borneo. Its modern wealth and political stability are derived from petroleum and natural gas reserves, which fund a comprehensive welfare state. The country's history is marked by a powerful medieval sultanate, a period of decline and British protectorate status, and its eventual independence in 1984.

## Timeline
- **c. 1400** — Founding of the Sultanate by Awang Alak Betatar (Sultan Muhammad Shah).
- **1485–1528** — Reign of Sultan Bolkiah; peak of the Bruneian Empire's territorial control.
- **1521** — Brunei is visited by the surviving crew of the Magellan Expedition.
- **1578** — The Castilian War; Spain briefly captures Brunei's capital before withdrawing.
- **1888** — Brunei becomes a British protectorate.
- **1929** — Petroleum is discovered at Seria, beginning the foundation of the modern economy.
- **1941–1945** — Japanese occupation during World War II.
- **1959** — A new constitution is written, making Brunei self-governing in internal affairs.
- **1962** — The Brunei Revolt is suppressed with British help, influencing the Sultan's decision not to join Malaysia.
- **1 January 1984** — Brunei gains full independence from the United Kingdom.
- **2014** — The first phase of a controversial Sharia Penal Code is implemented.

## Body

### Early History and the Sultanate
The area now known as Brunei participated in the ancient Maritime Jade Road trading network. Early settlements, such as Vijayapura, were vassal states of regional empires like Srivijaya and Majapahit. By the 15th century, the region had converted to Islam and formed the independent Sultanate of Brunei. Under Sultan Bolkiah (1485–1528), the empire reached its peak, claiming control over most of Borneo and parts of the Philippines. The state's power was bolstered by trade, and it was visited by the Magellan Expedition in 1521.

### Decline, Conflict, and British Influence
Brunei's regional power waned due to European expansion and internal strife. In 1578, Spain fought the Castilian War, briefly capturing the capital before a disease outbreak forced their withdrawal. The 19th century saw significant territorial losses; the Sultanate ceded land to James Brooke, who established the Raj of Sarawak, and to the British North Borneo Chartered Company. To halt further encroachment, Brunei became a British protectorate in 1888. A British Resident was appointed in 1906 to advise on administration, a system that lasted until 1959.

### Independence and Modern Governance
After a new constitution in 1959 and the suppression of the 1962 Brunei Revolt, the country moved toward full sovereignty. Britain's protectorate ended on 1 January 1984. The government is an absolute monarchy under Sultan Hassanal Bolkiah, who has ruled since 1967. The political system is based on the Malay Islamic Monarchy (MIB) ideology. The Legislative Council is consultative, with all members appointed by the Sultan. The legal system is a fusion of English common law and Islamic jurisprudence, with sharia law superseding common law in some cases.

### Economy and Society
Brunei's economy is dominated by extensive petroleum and natural gas fields, which account for about 90% of GDP. This wealth funds a welfare state with free healthcare, education, and housing subsidies, contributing to a high Human Development Index. The country imports most of its food. The population is approximately 466,330 (2025), with Malay (65.7%) as the largest ethnic group. Islam is the state religion, practiced by over 82% of the population. The official language is Malay, but English is widely used in business and education.

## Terms
- **Absolute Monarchy:** A form of monarchy where the ruler holds supreme, unchecked authority. In Brunei, the Sultan is the head of state with full executive power.
- **MIB (Melayu Islam Beraja):** The national ideology of Brunei, meaning "Malay Islamic Monarchy," which integrates Malay culture, Islam, and the political framework of the monarchy.
- **Sharia:** Islamic law derived from religious precepts. Brunei has incorporated a Sharia Penal Code into its legal system.
- **British Protectorate:** A state that is protected and controlled by a foreign power (Britain) in exchange for the protection of its sovereignty, particularly in external affairs.
- **White Rajahs:** The dynasty of British rulers (the Brooke family) who governed the Raj of Sarawak, a territory ceded by the Sultanate of Brunei.
- **Legislative Council:** Brunei's unicameral legislature, which is consultative and whose members are appointed by the Sultan.
- **Purchasing Power Parity (PPP):** An economic metric used to compare economic productivity and standards of living between countries by adjusting for price differences.
- **Human Development Index (HDI):** A composite statistic of life expectancy, education, and per capita income indicators, used to rank countries by level of human development.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Historical Extent of the Empire:** The precise territorial control of the Bruneian Empire at its peak, including claims over parts of the Philippines and Borneo, is a subject of scholarly debate.
- **Impact of the Sharia Penal Code:** The implementation of the Sharia Penal Code, particularly its later phases, has been a source of significant international controversy and debate regarding human rights and religious law.
- **Economic Diversification:** A key ongoing challenge is Brunei's efforts to diversify its economy away from heavy reliance on oil and gas to ensure long-term sustainability.

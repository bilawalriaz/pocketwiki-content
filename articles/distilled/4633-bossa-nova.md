# Bossa nova

## Overview
Bossa nova is a relaxed style of samba that emerged in Rio de Janeiro, Brazil, in the late 1950s and early 1960s. It is characterized by a calm, syncopated rhythm played on the classical guitar and complex, often "ambiguous" harmonies. The style modernized Brazilian music, influenced global jazz, and remains a significant cultural export.

## Timeline
- **c. 1957** — The term "bossa nova" is first publicly used for a concert by the Grupo Universitário Hebraico do Brasil.
- **1959** — João Gilberto's album *Chega de Saudade* is released, considered the landmark birth of bossa nova.
- **1959** — The soundtrack to *Black Orpheus* is released, featuring future bossa nova standard "Manhã de Carnaval".
- **1962** — Bossa nova is presented at Carnegie Hall in New York.
- **1963** — Jorge Ben writes "Mas que nada".
- **1964** — The album *Getz/Gilberto* by João Gilberto, Stan Getz, and Tom Jobim wins a Grammy Award.
- **1966** — Sérgio Mendes & Brazil 66 have a hit with "Mas que nada"; Walter Wanderley's "Summer Samba" becomes a major U.S. seller.
- **1967** — Frank Sinatra and Tom Jobim record their collaborative album.
- **1980s** — "New Bossa" emerges in Europe with artists like Sade.
- **2020s** — Bossa nova rhythms influence contemporary pop artists like Billie Eilish and Laufey.

## Body

### Origins and Etymology
Bossa nova emerged from Rio de Janeiro's artistic beach culture in the late 1950s. The word "bossa" is old-fashioned Brazilian slang for a natural flair or knack. "Bossa nova" literally means "new trend" or "new wave." The term was first used publicly in 1957 by journalist Moyses Fuks to promote a concert. The style developed as a simplification and stylization of samba rhythms for the guitar, pioneered by João Gilberto.

### Musical Innovation and Structure
The core innovation of bossa nova was synthesizing the rhythm of a samba percussion ensemble onto the classical guitar. Gilberto's thumb played bass notes (stylizing the surdo drum), while his fingers plucked chords in a pattern mimicking the tamborim drum. This created a syncopated, "swaying" rhythm distinct from jazz's swing feel. The style is typically performed on a nylon-string guitar with vocals. Harmonically, bossa nova uses complex chords, which some attribute to jazz influence, though similar structures existed in earlier samba guitar playing.

### Key Figures and Global Spread
João Gilberto is the central pioneer, whose guitar style and soft, nasal vocal delivery defined the genre. He collaborated with composer Antônio Carlos "Tom" Jobim and lyricist Vinicius de Moraes. The 1964 album *Getz/Gilberto* brought bossa nova international fame. The style influenced American jazz artists like Stan Getz and Quincy Jones. Outside Brazil, Japan has a particularly strong affinity for bossa nova, with a boom ignited by Sadao Watanabe's 1967 album.

### Themes and Legacy
Early bossa nova lyrics often depicted the leisurely life of the middle and upper classes. The style was largely apolitical. Its popularity was later eclipsed in Brazil by the more politically charged Música Popular Brasileira (MPB) following the 1964 military coup. Despite this, bossa nova's influence persists in global popular music, from 1960s rock to 21st-century pop.

## Terms
- **Samba:** A Brazilian musical genre with rhythmic patterns originating in Afro-Brazilian communities, emphasizing the second beat.
- **Syncopation:** A rhythm where emphasis is placed on normally weak beats or off-beats, creating a "swaying" feel.
- **Classical guitar:** A nylon-string guitar played with the fingers, the primary instrument of bossa nova.
- **Surdo:** A large bass drum in a samba ensemble, providing the foundational beat.
- **Tamborim:** A small, high-pitched frame drum in a samba ensemble, whose pattern João Gilberto adapted for guitar.
- **Bossa nova:** Literally "new trend"; a relaxed, syncopated style of samba developed in late 1950s Rio.
- **Samba-canção:** A slower, more romantic style of samba that was dominant in Brazil before bossa nova.
- **Música Popular Brasileira (MPB):** A genre of Brazilian popular music that emerged in the mid-1960s, often with politically charged lyrics.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Jazz Influence:** A common misconception is that bossa nova's complex harmonies were derived from jazz. However, samba guitarists used similar structures since the 1920s, suggesting parallel evolution rather than direct transference.
- **Authorship of the Term:** The exact origin of the phrase "bossa nova" was unclear for decades, though it is now attributed to journalist Moyses Fuks in 1957.

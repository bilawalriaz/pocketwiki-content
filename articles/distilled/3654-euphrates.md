# Euphrates

## Overview
The Euphrates is the longest river in West Asia, stretching approximately 2,780 km (1,730 mi) from its sources in Turkey through Syria and Iraq. Together with the Tigris, it defines the region of Mesopotamia ("the land between the rivers"), a cradle of civilization. Its waters have been vital for agriculture, settlement, and conflict for millennia, but modern damming and climate change now threaten its flow and ecological health.

## Timeline
- **Mid-3rd millennium BCE** — Earliest cuneiform references to the river (as *Buranuna*) found in Sumerian texts.
- **11th millennium BCE** — Early permanent villages (e.g., Abu Hureyra) established in the upper Euphrates basin.
- **6th millennium BCE** — Occupation of lower Mesopotamia begins, associated with the introduction of irrigation.
- **4th millennium BCE** — Uruk period sees the emergence of urban settlements across Mesopotamia.
- **2335–2154 BCE** — The Akkadian Empire unites large parts of the Euphrates basin for the first time.
- **1923** — Treaty of Lausanne stipulates riparian states must agree on water use.
- **1973–1974** — Syria and Turkey complete their first major dams (Tabqa and Keban).
- **1975** — Filling of new dams reduces flow to Iraq, sparking an international crisis.
- **1987** — Turkey-Syria treaty guarantees a minimum flow of 500 m³/s to Syria.
- **1992** — Turkey completes the massive Atatürk Dam as part of the GAP project.
- **2014** — Turkey temporarily cuts off the Euphrates flow to Syria and Iraq.
- **2014–2017** — The Islamic State controls large stretches of the river in Syria and Iraq.

## Body

### Etymology and Course
The name derives from Old Persian *hUfrātuš*, ultimately from Akkadian *Purattu*. The river forms in Turkey from the confluence of the Kara Su and Murat Su. It flows south through Syria and Iraq, joining the Tigris to form the Shatt al-Arab before emptying into the Persian Gulf. Its discharge is naturally highest in spring (April-May) due to snowmelt and rain, but this regime has been drastically altered by dams since the 1970s, reducing average flow and seasonal peaks.

### Drainage Basin and Climate
The Euphrates basin covers an estimated 440,000 km² across six countries, with Iraq (40-47%), Turkey (28-33%), and Syria (17-20%) holding the largest shares. The river flows through distinct vegetation zones: mountain woodlands in Turkey, steppe in Syria, and desert in Iraq. The Iraqi Ministry of Water Resources reported in 2021 that the river could dry up by 2040 due to climate change and droughts.

### Human Intervention and Conflict
The 20th century saw massive hydraulic engineering. Iraq built the first modern barrage (Hindiya, 1913). In the 1970s, Turkey and Syria built the Keban and Tabqa dams, leading to water-sharing crises. Turkey's Southeastern Anatolia Project (GAP) is a vast system of 22 dams and 19 power plants for irrigation and electricity, displacing nearly 200,000 people. These projects have reduced flow, increased evaporation, degraded water quality, and flooded archaeological sites like Zeugma. International agreements (e.g., 1987, 1989) attempt to manage water sharing, but tensions persist, as seen when Turkey cut the flow in 2014.

### Historical and Religious Significance
The river's valley was a core area for the Neolithic Revolution and the rise of early cities like Uruk. It served as a frontier and prize for empires from the Akkadians and Babylonians to the Assyrians, Persians, Romans, and Ottomans. In religion, it is mentioned in the Bible as one of the rivers of Paradise and in Islamic tradition as a site of a future apocalyptic battle over gold.

## Terms
- **Mesopotamia**: "The land between the rivers," referring to the region between the Tigris and Euphrates.
- **Riparian**: Relating to the banks of a river; riparian states are countries through which the river flows.
- **Drainage basin**: The area of land where all water drains into a common outlet, like a river.
- **Discharge**: The volume of water flowing through a river channel per unit of time.
- **Barrage**: A type of dam with gates that regulate water flow.
- **GAP (Southeastern Anatolia Project)**: Turkey's large-scale regional development project for irrigation and hydropower.
- **Salinization**: The increase in salt content of soil, often due to irrigation and poor drainage.
- **Substrate language**: A language that influences another language but is not directly attested.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The exact area of the Euphrates drainage basin is debated, with estimates ranging from 233,000 to 766,000 km².
- The impact of upstream dams on the ecologically fragile Mesopotamian Marshes in Iraq is a major environmental concern.
- The effectiveness and fairness of international water-sharing agreements among Turkey, Syria, and Iraq remain contentious, especially amid climate change and regional instability.

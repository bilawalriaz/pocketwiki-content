# Wilma Rudolph

## Overview
Wilma Rudolph (1940–1994) was an American sprinter who overcame childhood polio to become a world-record-holding Olympic champion. Her triple gold medal victory at the 1960 Rome Olympics made her an international sports icon and a pioneering role model for Black and female athletes, significantly elevating women's track and field in the United States.

## Timeline
- **June 23, 1940** — Born prematurely in Saint Bethlehem, Tennessee.
- **c. 1945** — Contracts polio, loses strength in left leg.
- **1956** — Wins bronze medal in 4 × 100 m relay at Melbourne Olympics.
- **1959** — Wins silver in 100 m and gold in 4 × 100 m relay at Pan American Games.
- **1960** — Sets world record in 200 m at U.S. Olympic trials.
- **Sept. 1960** — Wins three gold medals (100 m, 200 m, 4 × 100 m) at Rome Olympics.
- **Oct. 4, 1960** — "Welcome Wilma Day" parade in Clarksville is desegregated at her insistence.
- **1962** — Retires from competition at age 22, still a world record-holder.
- **1963** — Graduates from Tennessee State University; marries Robert Eldridge.
- **1981** — Founds the Wilma Rudolph Foundation.
- **Nov. 12, 1994** — Dies of brain and throat cancer in Brentwood, Tennessee.

## Body

### Early Life and Overcoming Disability
Wilma Rudolph was the 20th of 22 children. She contracted infantile paralysis (polio) at age five, which weakened her left leg. Due to limited medical care for African Americans in Clarksville, her parents took her for treatment at Meharry Medical College in Nashville. For two years, she and her mother made weekly bus trips for treatment, and family members gave her daily massages. By age 12, she had regained full use of her leg without a brace or orthopedic shoe.

### Rise to Olympic Stardom
Rudolph began organized sports in basketball at Burt High School, where her speed earned her the nickname "Skeeter." She was spotted by Ed Temple, Tennessee State University's track coach, who invited her to his summer training program. At 16, she qualified for the 1956 Olympics, winning a bronze medal in the 4 × 100 m relay. After enrolling at Tennessee State, she won a silver and gold medal at the 1959 Pan American Games.

### 1960 Rome Olympics and Global Fame
At the 1960 Rome Olympics, Rudolph became the first American woman to win three gold medals in a single Olympiad, taking the 100 m, 200 m, and 4 × 100 m relay. Her victories, broadcast worldwide on television, made her an international star. She was hailed as "the fastest woman in the world" and given nicknames like "La Gazzella Nera" (The Black Gazelle). Her success provided a major boost to women's track and field in the U.S.

### Civil Rights and Post-Athletic Career
Rudolph used her platform for civil rights. In 1960, she refused to attend her own segregated hometown parade until it was integrated, marking Clarksville's first integrated public event. In 1963, she participated in a protest to desegregate a local restaurant. After retiring from track in 1962 at the peak of her career, she earned a degree in education, worked as a teacher and coach, and founded the Wilma Rudolph Foundation to train youth athletes. She also worked in television and as a publicist.

## Terms
- **Polio (Infantile Paralysis):** A viral disease that can cause muscle weakness and paralysis; Rudolph recovered but had a weakened leg.
- **World Record:** The best performance ever officially recorded in a sport; Rudolph held records in the 100 m, 200 m, and 4 × 100 m relay.
- **Olympiad:** A four-year interval between Olympic Games; the 1960 Games in Rome was the Olympiad where Rudolph won three golds.
- **AAU (Amateur Athletic Union):** A U.S. organization that governed amateur sports and competitions; Rudolph won multiple AAU titles.
- **Tigerbelles:** The nickname for the Tennessee State University women's track team, coached by Ed Temple.
- **Cinder Track:** A running surface made of cinders, common in mid-20th century stadiums like Rome's Stadio Olimpico.
- **Wind-aided time:** A race time recorded with a tailwind exceeding the legal limit (2.0 m/s), making it ineligible for official world record status.
- **Desegregation:** The process of ending the separation of racial groups in public facilities; Rudolph actively protested for this in her hometown.

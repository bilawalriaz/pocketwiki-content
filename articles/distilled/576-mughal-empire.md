# Mughal Empire

## Overview
The Mughal Empire (1526–1857) was an early modern South Asian empire founded by Babur, a Timurid prince from Central Asia. At its zenith under Aurangzeb (r. 1658–1707), it controlled nearly the entire subcontinent, deriving immense wealth from agricultural taxes paid in standardized silver currency. The empire is noted for a highly centralized bureaucracy (the *mansabdari* system), a syncretic culture blending Persian, Indian, and Central Asian traditions, and a proto-industrial economy that produced roughly 25% of global manufacturing output until 1750. Military success relied on gunpowder artillery and cavalry tactics. After Aurangzeb’s death, fiscal crises, succession wars, Maratha expansion, and the invasion of Nader Shah (1739) caused rapid fragmentation. The British East India Company gradually assumed control, formally dissolving the empire after the 1857 Rebellion.

## Timeline
- **1526** — Babur defeats Ibrahim Lodi at the **First Battle of Panipat**, founding the empire.
- **1530–1556** — Reign of **Humayun**; interrupted by exile in Persia (1540–1555) after defeat by Sher Shah Suri.
- **1556–1605** — Reign of **Akbar**; consolidates administration, expands territory, institutes *zabt* revenue system and *Din-i-Ilahi*.
- **1605–1627** — Reign of **Jahangir**; increases *madad-i-ma'ash* grants, executes Sikh Guru Arjan.
- **1628–1658** — Reign of **Shah Jahan**; architectural zenith (Taj Mahal), Deccan expansion, court costs exceed revenue.
- **1658–1707** — Reign of **Aurangzeb**; maximum territorial extent, compiles *Fatawa 'Alamgiri*, reinstates *jizya*, prolonged Deccan wars drain treasury.
- **1707–1720** — Rapid succession crises; four emperors in 1719; Sayyid Brothers become de facto rulers.
- **1739** — **Nader Shah sacks Delhi**, looting the imperial treasury (including the Peacock Throne).
- **1757** — British East India Company seizes control of **Bengal Subah** after Battle of Plassey.
- **1761** — **Third Battle of Panipat**; Afghans defeat Marathas; Mughal emperor becomes British pensioner.
- **1857** — **Indian Rebellion**; last emperor Bahadur Shah Zafar deposed and exiled to Rangoon (1858).

## Body

### Origins and Dynastic Identity
The dynasty’s founders were **Chagatai Turks** descended from **Timur** (paternal) and **Genghis Khan** (maternal). The term *Mughal* (Persian for *Mongol*) was applied in India to distinguish them from the Afghan Lodi Sultans. Their self-designation was **Gurkani** ("sons-in-law"), referencing Timur’s marriage to a Chinggisid princess. Official documents used *Hindustan* ("Dominion of Hindustan"), while Chinese chronicles recorded *Hindustan*. Historians debate the label; Marshall Hodgson preferred *Timurid* or *Indo-Timurid*.

### Political History: Expansion, Zenith, and Collapse
**Babur and Humayun (1526–1556):** Babur used Ottoman-style wagon-fort artillery formations to win at Panipat (1526) and Khanwa (1527). He died before consolidating administration. Humayun lost the empire to Sher Shah Suri (1540), fled to Safavid Persia (gaining Persian cultural influence), and returned in 1555, dying soon after.

**Akbar to Aurangzeb (1556–1707):** **Akbar** (r. 1556–1605) built the imperial structure: a merit-based bureaucracy (*mansabdari*), the *zabt* cash-revenue system, and a policy of *sulh-i-kul* (universal tolerance), culminating in the syncretic *Din-i-Ilahi*. He secured the subcontinent north of the Godavari. **Jahangir** (r. 1605–1627) leaned on Islamic orthodoxy, expanding tax-free *madad-i-ma'ash* grants, and executed Sikh Guru Arjan, initiating Mughal-Sikh conflict. **Shah Jahan** (r. 1628–1658) presided over architectural splendor (Taj Mahal) but saw costs outpace revenue. **Aurangzeb** (r. 1658–1707) seized the throne from liberal heir Dara Shikoh. He expanded to the deep south (annexing Bijapur, Golconda), enforced Islamic law (*Fatawa 'Alamgiri*), reinstated the *jizya* (poll tax on non-Muslims), and executed Sikh Guru Tegh Bahadur (sparking Sikh militarization). His 25-year Deccan campaign ruined the treasury and governance.

**Decline (1707–1857):** Post-Aurangzeb, weak rulers and civil wars (four emperors in 1719) empowered the **Sayyid Brothers** as kingmakers. **Muhammad Shah** (r. 1719–1748) lost central India to the **Marathas**. **Nader Shah’s** 1739 invasion sacked Delhi, stripping the treasury. The empire fragmented into successor states (Bengal, Awadh, Hyderabad) while paying ceremonial allegiance to the emperor. The British East India Company became the emperor’s "protector" after the Second Anglo-Maratha War (1803), annexed Bengal (1793), and dissolved the dynasty after the 1857 Rebellion.

### Administration and State
**Central Government:** Headed by the emperor; four ministries: Finance (*Diwan* / *Wazir*), Military (*Mir Bakhshi*, managing *mansabdari*), Law/Religion (*Sadr as-Sudr*), and Household/Works (*Mir Saman*). The *Diwan* was paramount.

**Provincial Structure:** Empire divided into **Subahs** (provinces) under *Subadars*. Subahs mirrored central ministries but reported directly to the center, checking the governor. Subahs → **Sarkars** (districts) → **Parganas** (village clusters, basic unit). *Pargana* administration: a *qadi* (judge) and tax collector. Boundaries were fluid, rearranged for revenue optimization; detailed land surveys (*cadastral*) replaced formal maps.

**Capitals:** Multiple simultaneous capitals: Agra, Delhi, Lahore, Fatehpur Sikri (Akbar’s ideological city). Kabul served as summer capital (1526–1681). The **imperial camp** functioned as a mobile capital during campaigns. Post-Aurangzeb, **Shahjahanabad (Old Delhi)** became fixed capital.

**Law:** A hybrid system. As a Muslim state, it used **fiqh** (Islamic jurisprudence, Sunni Hanafi school) with *qadi* (judge), *mufti* (jurist), *muhtasib* (market inspector). *Fatawa 'Alamgiri* (Aurangzeb) codified Hanafi law for South Asia. Persian kingship theory made the emperor supreme legal authority. Courts ranged from *pargana qadi* to *qadi al-quddat* (imperial camp) to provincial governors (*subadars*). Jahangir’s "chain of justice" allowed direct petition. Village *panchayats* operated but are poorly documented. Corruption among local judges was systemic.

### Economy
**Structure:** Proto-industrial; 24.5% of world manufacturing output (pre-1750). **Agriculture** was the tax base: peasants paid >50% of output in silver rupees (standardized >96% purity, minted from imported bullion via export surplus). *Zabt* system (Akbar) replaced tribute with cash taxes based on cadastral surveys, incentivizing cash crops (cotton, indigo, opium). **Industry:** Textiles (cotton, silk) dominated; Bengal produced ~25% of global textile trade (early 18th c.). Spinning wheel diffusion and worm-gear cotton gins boosted productivity. **Shipbuilding:** Bengal output ~223,000 tons/year (16th–17th c.), far exceeding colonial North America. **Labor:** 82% rural, 18% urban (15% urbanization rate—higher than contemporary Europe). Per-capita wheat income (late 16th c.) slightly exceeded early 20th c. British India, though elite hoarding depressed wages. Slavery limited to household servants.

**Bengal Subah:** Wealthiest province (1590–1757). Major exporter of rice, silk, cotton, opium, saltpetre. Gained de facto independence under Nawab Murshid Quli Khan (1717).

### Demographics
**Population:** Grew 60–253% (1500–1700), unprecedented for pre-Mughal India. **Urbanization:** ~15% (Irfan Habib), higher than Europe (reached 15% only in 19th c.) or 19th c. British India. Akbar (1600): 17M urban (15%); 1700: 23M urban. Major cities: Dhaka (>1M), Agra (800k), Lahore (700k), Delhi (600k). *Critique:* Tim Dyson estimates <9% urbanization.

### Culture
**Synthesis:** Persian + Indian + Central Asian. **Language:** Persian became court/official language (vehicle for Indo-Persian political culture); Chagatai Turkish faded. **Hindustani** (Urdu) emerged in late Mughal Delhi, replacing Persian as Muslim elite vernacular by Shah Jahan’s reign. **Architecture:** Bulbous domes, ogive arches, red sandstone/marble. Synthesis of Indo-Islamic, Timurid, Iranian, Hindu styles. UNESCO sites: Taj Mahal, Agra Fort, Fatehpur Sikri, Red Fort, Humayun’s Tomb, Lahore Fort, Shalamar Gardens. **Art:** Miniatures (historical, portraiture, natural history—Jahangir’s *Ustad Mansur*). Illustrated manuscripts: *Baburnama*, *Akbarnama*, *Razmnama* (Mahabharata). Aurangzeb ended painting patronage (~1668). **Cuisine:** Mughlai (South Asian/Iranian/Central Asian). **Music:** Hindustani classical; sitar development. **Wrestling:** *Pehlwani* (Indian *malla-yuddha* + Persian *varzesh-e bastani*). **Education:** *Maktab* schools (Quran, *Fatawa 'Alamgiri* in vernaculars). **Gardens/Irrigation:** Iranian-style waterworks, *hammams*.

### Military
**Gunpowder Empire:** One of three Islamic gunpowder empires (with Ottomans, Safavids). Babur used Ottoman formation (artillery/wagons center, cavalry wings) at Panipat. **Artillery:** Bronze guns; Fathullah Shirazi (Akbar’s engineer) developed a multi-barrel volley gun. 17th c.: diverse firearms manufactured (Tanjore, Dacca, Bijapur, Murshidabad). **Rocketry:** Akbar first used metal-cylinder *bans* vs. war elephants (Battle of Sanbal). 1657 Siege of Bidar: rockets/grenades used; rocket hit powder magazine, deciding siege. Indian rockets (bamboo-guided, iron-tipped) preceded European use; trajectory erratic. Mines/counter-mines used under Akbar/Jahangir.

### Science and Technology
**Education:** Madrasa curriculum reformed to emphasize *uloom-i-muqalat* (Rational Sciences): geometry, medicine, philosophy, mathematics. Produced scholars, engineers, architects. **Astronomy:** Observational focus; *Zij* treatises. Humayun built observatory near Delhi. 17th c. synthesis: Islamic instruments + Hindu computational methods. Jai Singh II (early 18th c.) built *Yantra Mandirs* (large observatories) to refine *Siddhantas* and *Zij-i-Sultani*. **Metallurgy:** *Karkhanas* (state workshops) produced arms, luxury goods. **Celestial Globes:** Lost-wax cast, hollow, seamless globes invented in Kashmir by Ali Kashmiri ibn Luqman (1589–90 CE); 20+ produced in Lahore/Kashmir. Thought technically impossible until rediscovery in 1980s. Diya' ad-din Muhammad made globe in Lahore (1668).

## Terms
- ****Mansabdari**** — A combined civil-military ranking system where officials (*mansabdars*) held a numerical rank (*zat* for status, *sawar* for cavalry obligation) determining salary and troop quota; not hereditary.
- ****Zabt**** — Akbar’s land revenue system: cash taxes assessed via cadastral survey of cultivated area, replacing crop-sharing/tribute; biased toward high-value cash crops.
- ****Subah**** — Top-level provincial administrative division, governed by a *Subadar*; mirrored central ministries but reported directly to the emperor.
- ****Pargana**** — Basic local administrative unit (cluster of villages) with a *qadi* (judge) and tax collector.
- ****Jizya**** — Poll tax on non-Muslims; abolished by Akbar, reinstated by Aurangzeb (1679).
- ****Madad-i-ma'ash**** — Tax-free land revenue grants to religious scholars or spiritual figures; expanded significantly by Jahangir.
- ****Fatawa 'Alamgiri**** — Comprehensive compendium of Hanafi Islamic law commissioned by Aurangzeb (compiled 1660s–70s) to serve as the empire’s central legal reference.
- ****Gurkani**** — The dynasty’s self-designation ("sons-in-law"), referencing Timur’s marriage to a Chinggisid princess.
- ****Karkhanas**** — State-run workshops producing arms, textiles, luxury goods, and scientific instruments for the imperial household and army.
- ****Proto-industrialisation**** — Pre-factory, export-oriented manufacturing growth (textiles, shipbuilding) resembling 18th-century Western Europe; Mughal India produced ~25% of world industrial output until 1750.

## Debates and open questions
- **Dynastic Label:** Whether "Mughal" (Mongol) is appropriate given Chagatai Turkic origins; Hodgson’s "Timurid/Indo-Timurid" vs. standard usage.
- **Decline Causality:** Relative weight of *internal* factors (fiscal collapse, jagirdari crisis, succession wars, Aurangzeb’s Deccan overreach/religious policy) vs. *external* shocks (Maratha expansion, Nader Shah, European companies).
- **Aurangzeb’s Legacy:** Whether his Islamic orthodoxy (*jizya*, temple destruction, Sikh executions) fatally alienated subjects, or whether he patronized Hindu temples, employed more Hindus in bureaucracy than predecessors, and opposed anti-Hindu/Shia bigotry.
- **Urbanization Rate:** Irfan Habib’s ~15% (higher than contemporary Europe) vs. Tim Dyson’s <9%.
- **18th-Century Sovereignty:** Whether the post-1707 emperor was a mere figurehead or retained legitimate sovereign authority acknowledged by Marathas, Sikhs, and regional Nawabs.
- **Economic Trajectory:** Whether the economy was stagnating before British rule (per "deindustrialization" thesis) or remained dynamic until colonial disruption.
- **Legal Pluralism:** Extent to which *fiqh* governed non-Muslims vs. reliance on custom (*riwaj*), administrative decree, and village *panchayats* (poorly documented).

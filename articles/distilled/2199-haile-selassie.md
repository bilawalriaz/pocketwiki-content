# Haile Selassie

## Overview
Haile Selassie I (1892–1975) was Emperor of Ethiopia from 1930 to 1974, a central figure in modern Ethiopian history and a global symbol of anti-colonial resistance. He is also venerated as a divine figure in the Rastafari religion, which emerged in the 1930s. His reign saw Ethiopia's struggle against Italian invasion, its entry into the modern international system, and internal tensions between modernization and traditional feudal structures, culminating in his overthrow by a military junta.

- **27 Sept 1916** — Deposed Emperor Lij Iyasu; Tafari becomes Crown Prince and Regent Plenipotentiary under Empress Zewditu.
- **2 Nov 1930** — Crowned Emperor Haile Selassie I following Zewditu's death.
- **16 July 1931** — Promulgates Ethiopia's first written constitution.
- **3 Oct 1935** — Issues mobilization order as Italy invades Ethiopia.
- **2 May 1936** — Departs for exile in the United Kingdom after Italian forces advance.
- **12 May 1936** — Delivers historic speech to the League of Nations, denouncing Italian aggression.
- **5 May 1941** — Returns triumphantly to Addis Ababa after the liberation of Ethiopia in WWII.
- **2 Dec 1950** — UN Resolution 390 (V) federates Eritrea with Ethiopia.
- **12 Sept 1974** — Deposed by the Derg military junta and placed under house arrest.
- **27 Aug 1975** — Murdered on the orders of the Derg regime (fact revealed publicly in 1994).

## Body

### Rise to Power and Regency
Born into the Solomonic dynasty, Tafari Makonnen was the son of Ras Makonnen, a general at the Battle of Adwa. He was appointed governor of Harar in 1910/1911. In 1916, he was instrumental in deposing the designated heir, Lij Iyasu, for his pro-Islamic stance. Tafari was made Crown Prince and Regent Plenipotentiary (*Enderase*) under Empress Zewditu, becoming the de facto ruler. He consolidated power, defeated a rebellion by the Empress's husband in 1930, and succeeded her as Emperor upon her death.

### Modernization and Conflict with Italy
As Emperor, Selassie introduced reforms, including a 1931 constitution that kept power with the nobility but envisioned a future transition to democracy. He secured Ethiopia's admission to the League of Nations in 1923 by promising to eradicate slavery, though the practice persisted. His modernization efforts were interrupted by the Second Italo-Ethiopian War (1935–1941). Despite Ethiopian resistance, Italy's superior military and use of chemical weapons led to defeat. Selassie's 1936 League of Nations speech made him an international anti-fascist icon but failed to secure effective intervention. He spent the war in exile in Bath, England.

### Restoration and Post-War Leadership
Selassie was restored to power in 1941 with British assistance. He led Ethiopia into the United Nations and in 1950 oversaw the UN-mandated federation with Eritrea, which he later dissolved, annexing Eritrea as a province in 1962 and sparking a 30-year independence war. Internationally, he was a leading voice for African decolonization, presiding over the formation of the Organisation of African Unity (OAU) in 1963. Domestically, his reforms were slow and compromised by the entrenched aristocracy, leading to resistance and student unrest.

### Overthrow and Death
In the early 1970s, a devastating famine in Wollo and Tigray, combined with economic crisis and military mutinies, eroded support for his regime. In 1974, the Derg, a military junta, deposed the 82-year-old emperor. He was imprisoned and, on 27 August 1975, murdered on the Derg's orders—a fact concealed until 1994. His remains were interred at Holy Trinity Cathedral in 2000.

### Rastafari Veneration
The Rastafari movement, which emerged in Jamaica in the 1930s, regards Selassie as the returned messiah, based on his titles (Conquering Lion of the Tribe of Judah) and Solomonic lineage. Selassie visited Jamaica in 1966, a pivotal event for the faith. While he never affirmed his divinity in a 1967 interview, he respected the movement's beliefs and donated land at Shashamane for the African diaspora.

## Terms
- **Solomonic dynasty**: The imperial dynasty of Ethiopia, claiming descent from King Solomon and the Queen of Sheba.
- **Enderase**: The title for Regent Plenipotentiary, the de facto ruler under a nominal monarch.
- **Ras**: A high noble title in Ethiopia, equivalent to a duke or prince.
- **Negusa Nagast**: The Amharic title for "King of Kings," the formal title for the Emperor of Ethiopia.
- **Derg**: The military junta that overthrew Haile Selassie in 1974 and ruled Ethiopia until 1991.
- **OAU (Organisation of African Unity)**: A continental organization founded in 1963 to promote African unity and decolonization, precursor to the African Union.
- **Autocephaly**: The status of a Christian church that is self-governing and independent of other churches.
- **Mesafint**: The landed aristocracy or nobility of Ethiopia, whose power Selassie sought to centralize.

## Debates and open questions
- The extent of Selassie's personal responsibility for and awareness of the 1972–74 Wollo famine is debated, with accounts varying on whether he was deliberately kept uninformed or willfully ignored it.
- Scholars debate the effectiveness of his modernization policies, noting that reforms were often compromised by the need to appease the traditional nobility and clergy, slowing social and economic change.
- Within the Rastafari movement, there is ongoing debate about whether Selassie actually died in 1975, with some rejecting the official account of his murder.

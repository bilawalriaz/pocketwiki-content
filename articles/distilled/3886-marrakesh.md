# Marrakesh

## Overview
Marrakesh is Morocco's fourth-largest city and one of its four imperial capitals, founded circa 1070 as the seat of the Almoravid dynasty. Its historic medina, a UNESCO World Heritage Site, and its role as a cultural, religious, and trading hub for the Maghreb have made it a major global tourist destination and a symbol of Moroccan identity.

## Timeline
- **c. 1070** — Founded by Abu Bakr ibn Umar as the Almoravid capital.
- **1122–1123** — City walls built by Ali ibn Yusuf, giving Marrakesh its "Red City" nickname.
- **1147** — Almohads conquer the city; Abd al-Mu'min founds the Kutubiyya Mosque.
- **1558–16th c.** — Saadian dynasty makes Marrakesh capital again; El Badi Palace begun (1578).
- **1912** — French Protectorate established; T'hami El Glaoui becomes Pasha.
- **1956** — Moroccan independence; end of Glaoui's rule.
- **1985** — Medina declared a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
- **1994** — Marrakesh Agreement signed, establishing the World Trade Organization.
- **2011** — Bomb attack in Jemaa el-Fnaa square kills 15.
- **2016** — Hosts the UN Climate Change Conference (COP22).
- **2023** — Deadly earthquake affects the city; hosts IMF/World Bank Annual Meetings.

## Body

### Founding and Imperial Capitals
The area was inhabited by Berber farmers from Neolithic times. Marrakesh was founded c. 1070 by the Almoravid dynasty, who established its first structures and urban layout. The city grew into a major cultural and trading center. After the Almohad conquest in 1147, it became their capital, witnessing further architectural development like the Kutubiyya Mosque and Menara Gardens. Following Almohad decline, the Marinids conquered the city in 1269, and it lost capital status to Fez.

### Saadian Revival and Modern Era
Marrakesh regained its status as capital under the Saadian dynasty in the early 16th century, experiencing a golden age with monuments like the El Badi Palace. From the 17th century, it became a center for Sufi pilgrimage to its seven patron saints. In the 20th century, under the French Protectorate (1912–1956), T'hami El Glaoui dominated the city. After independence in 1956, Marrakesh thrived as a tourist destination, attracting international visitors and investment, though the historic medina's residential population has declined.

### Geography and Climate
Marrakesh lies west of the Atlas Mountains in the Tensift River valley. It has a hot semi-arid climate (Köppen BSh) with long, hot summers and mild winters. The city's water supply relies on groundwater, which has been declining significantly since the early 2000s.

### Economy and Tourism
Tourism is a vital economic driver, with over two million annual visitors. The city is popular with French tourists and investors. The medina's souks (markets) are a major attraction, employing over 40,000 people in crafts. Despite tourism growth, poverty persists, and some households lack basic utilities. Real estate and hotel development have boomed in the 21st century.

### Landmarks and Culture
The Jemaa el-Fnaa square is the city's iconic center. The medina contains numerous souks, historic gates, and the Kutubiyya Mosque with its influential minaret. Notable sites include the Saadian Tombs, Bahia Palace, and the Ben Youssef Madrasa. The city is known for its gardens (Menara, Majorelle), traditional crafts (carpets, metalwork), and cuisine featuring dishes like tanjia and tajine. It hosts major cultural events like the International Film Festival.

## Terms
- **Medina**: The old, fortified city quarter, a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
- **Souk**: A traditional market or marketplace in North African cities.
- **Almoravid**: A Berber Muslim dynasty that ruled parts of North Africa and Iberia (c. 1040–1147).
- **Almohad**: A Berber Muslim reform movement and empire that succeeded the Almoravids (12th–13th centuries).
- **Saadian**: A Moroccan dynasty that ruled in the 16th–17th centuries.
- **Khettara**: An ancient underground water channel system used for irrigation.
- **Riad**: A traditional Moroccan house or palace with an interior garden or courtyard.
- **Zellij**: Mosaic tilework used in Islamic architecture.
- **Mellah**: A historic Jewish quarter in a North African city.
- **Ziyara**: A pilgrimage to visit a holy person's tomb.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The exact founding date of Marrakesh is debated, with sources citing 1062, 1070, or 1078.
- The etymology of the name "Marrakesh" is uncertain, with proposed origins from Berber words meaning "Land of God" or "country of the sons of Kush."
- The long-term sustainability of tourism-driven urban policy and the commodification of heritage are subjects of scholarly critique.

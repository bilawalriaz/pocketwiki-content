# J. Edgar Hoover

## Overview
J. Edgar Hoover was the first and longest-serving director of the FBI, leading the agency for 48 years under eight presidents. He transformed it into a powerful, modern crime-fighting and intelligence organization, but his legacy is deeply controversial due to extensive abuses of power, including illegal surveillance and harassment of political dissidents and civil rights leaders.

## Timeline
- **Jan 1, 1895** — Born in Washington, D.C.
- **1917** — Hired by the Justice Department's War Emergency Division.
- **Aug 1919** — Becomes head of the Bureau of Investigation's Radical Division.
- **May 10, 1924** — Appointed Director of the Bureau of Investigation by President Coolidge.
- **1930s** — Leads FBI pursuit of Depression-era gangsters like John Dillinger.
- **Jun 1935** — Bureau of Investigation renamed the Federal Bureau of Investigation (FBI).
- **1956** — Formalizes the covert COINTELPRO program to disrupt domestic groups.
- **1964** — FBI investigates Martin Luther King Jr.; sends him an anonymous letter.
- **1971** — COINTELPRO is exposed to the public.
- **May 2, 1972** — Dies of a heart attack in Washington, D.C.

## Body

### Early Career and Building the FBI
Born in 1895, Hoover worked at the Library of Congress before earning law degrees from George Washington University. He joined the Justice Department in 1917, quickly rising to head the Alien Enemy Bureau during WWI. In 1919, he led the General Intelligence Division during the First Red Scare, overseeing the Palmer Raids. Appointed Director of the Bureau of Investigation in 1924, he professionalized the agency, firing female agents and emphasizing scientific policing. He built the FBI's reputation in the 1930s by pursuing high-profile gangsters, though he privately denied the existence of organized crime for decades.

### Domestic Intelligence and COINTELPRO
Hoover's primary focus was always subversion. He directed extensive investigations into suspected communists and radicals, often exaggerating threats. During WWII, the FBI handled counterespionage, including the capture of Nazi saboteurs. In the Cold War, Hoover kept the top-secret Venona project (decoding Soviet communications) from top officials. Frustrated by legal limits, he created COINTELPRO in 1956, a covert program using infiltration, wiretapping, and disinformation to disrupt groups like the Communist Party USA and, later, civil rights and anti-war organizations.

### Civil Rights and Abuses of Power
Hoover vehemently opposed the Civil Rights Movement, falsely linking leaders like Martin Luther King Jr. to communism. The FBI conducted illegal surveillance of King, sent him a letter interpreted as urging suicide, and spread rumors to discredit him. COINTELPRO tactics were used against the Nation of Islam, the Black Panther Party, and others. Hoover amassed secret files on politicians and citizens, using them for intimidation. His actions were later condemned by the Church Committee as illegal and unconstitutional.

### Legacy and Personal Life
Hoover died in 1972 after 48 years as director. His legacy is mixed: he built the FBI into a formidable institution but left a record of civil liberties violations. The FBI headquarters is named after him, though there have been repeated attempts to rename it. His personal life was private; he never married and had a lifelong close relationship with FBI Deputy Director Clyde Tolson. Rumors about his sexuality and cross-dressing persist, though their validity is debated by historians.

## Terms
- **Bureau of Investigation (BOI):** The predecessor to the FBI, which Hoover directed from 1924.
- **COINTELPRO:** A series of covert and illegal FBI programs to disrupt and discredit domestic political organizations.
- **Palmer Raids:** A series of raids conducted in 1919-1920 under Hoover's direction to arrest and deport suspected radical leftists.
- **Venona project:** A secret US-UK program to decrypt Soviet intelligence messages, which confirmed Soviet espionage.
- **Habeas corpus:** A legal recourse to challenge unlawful detention; Hoover proposed suspending it in 1950.
- **G-Man:** A slang term for a government agent, particularly an FBI agent, popularized during Hoover's tenure.
- **Church Committee:** A 1975 US Senate committee that investigated abuses by intelligence agencies, including the FBI.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Hoover's Sexuality:** Historians debate whether Hoover was homosexual, bisexual, or asexual, and the nature of his relationship with Clyde Tolson.
- **Extent of Blackmail:** The degree to which Hoover used secret files to maintain his power and influence over presidents is contested.
- **Handling of Organized Crime:** Scholars analyze why Hoover long denied the Mafia's existence, with theories ranging from fear of corruption to possible blackmail.
- **Assassination of JFK:** Critics argue Hoover prematurely concluded Lee Harvey Oswald acted alone and resisted investigating a possible conspiracy.

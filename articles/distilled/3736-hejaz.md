# Hejaz

## Overview
The Hejaz is a historical region along the western Arabian Peninsula, now part of Saudi Arabia. Its global significance stems from containing Islam's two holiest cities, Mecca and Medina, making it the spiritual heart of the Muslim world and a focal point of Arabo-Islamic history and politics.

## Timeline
- **c. 2500–3000 BCE** — A river system in the Hejaz was active.
- **5th–1st century BCE** — The Kingdom of Lihyan, with its capital at Dedan, ruled northwestern Hejaz.
- **1st century BCE–106 CE** — The Nabataean Kingdom occupied northwestern Hejaz until annexed by Rome.
- **8th century BCE–5th century CE** — The Thamud tribal confederation was based in northwestern Hejaz.
- **3rd–4th century CE** — The Ghassanids were the main tribal confederation in the Hejaz.
- **5th–7th century CE** — The Mudar confederation and Jewish tribes were major powers in the region.
- **622 CE** — Muhammad migrated to Medina; the Constitution of Medina was brokered.
- **622–632 CE** — The First Islamic state, with Medina as its capital, ruled the Hejaz.
- **632–967 CE** — The Hejaz was ruled by the Rashidun, Umayyad, and Abbasid Caliphates.
- **967–1916 CE** — The semi-sovereign Sharifate of Mecca governed the region under various suzerains.
- **1916–1925 CE** — The independent Kingdom of Hejaz existed after Ottoman rule ended.
- **1925–1932 CE** — The Kingdom of Hejaz and Nejd was formed after Saudi conquest.
- **23 September 1932** — The Kingdom of Saudi Arabia was proclaimed, unifying the Hejaz and Nejd.

## Body

### Etymology and Geography
The name "Hejaz" derives from the Arabic root meaning "to separate," as the region geographically separates the Najd in the east from the Tihāmah coastal plain in the west. It is bordered by the Red Sea, Jordan, the Najd, and South Arabia. The landscape includes the volcanic lava fields known as *ḥarrāt*, which form one of Earth's largest alkali basalt regions.

### Ancient and Pre-Islamic History
The Hejaz's history centers on its oasis cities, which became major trading hubs due to the incense trade and the domestication of the dromedary camel. Successive powers controlled the region, including the Kingdom of Lihyan, the Nabataean Kingdom, and various tribal confederations like the Thamud, Ghassanids, and Mudar. The 5th–7th centuries saw the rise of Jewish tribes alongside the Mudar confederation, which included the Quraysh of Mecca.

### Islamic and Medieval History
According to Islamic tradition, the Hejaz is where the prophet Muhammad was born and founded the first Muslim community. It was the core of the early Islamic state and the Rashidun Caliphate, with Medina as its capital until 656 CE. Following the early caliphates, the region was ruled by empires like the Umayyads and Abbasids. From 967 to 1916, the local Sharifs of Mecca governed with considerable autonomy under the suzerainty of various dynasties, culminating in Ottoman control.

### Modern History and Integration into Saudi Arabia
After Ottoman control ended, the independent Kingdom of Hejaz was proclaimed in 1916. In 1925, the Saudi Sultanate of Nejd conquered the Hejaz. The two kingdoms were united as the Kingdom of Hejaz and Nejd in 1926, and on 23 September 1932, this entity, along with other Saudi dominions, was renamed the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia.

### Demographics and Culture
The Hejaz is Saudi Arabia's most populated region. Its people, predominantly Sunni Arab, often have a cosmopolitan identity due to centuries of connection with the Islamic world via the holy cities. Hejazi cuisine reflects this history, featuring native dishes like Saleeg alongside imports from Central Asia, Turkey, and South Asia.

## Terms
*   **Hejaz**: A historical region in western Saudi Arabia, meaning "the barrier" in Arabic.
*   **Tihāmah**: The coastal plain along the Red Sea, west of the Hejaz mountains.
*   **Najd**: The central plateau region of the Arabian Peninsula, east of the Hejaz.
*   **Sharifate of Mecca**: A semi-sovereign state ruled by the Sharifs of Mecca, who were guardians of Islam's holy sites.
*   **Ummah**: The global community of Muslims.
*   **Constitution of Medina**: A 622 CE treaty brokered by Muhammad between the Arab and Jewish tribes of Medina.
*   **Ḥarrāt**: Arabic term for the vast, dark volcanic lava fields found in the Hejaz.
*   **Dromedary**: A single-humped camel, crucial for long-distance trade in ancient Arabia.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   The exact cause of the fall of the Kingdom of Lihyan is unknown; it is debated whether it was conquered by the Nabataeans.
*   The historical extent of Jewish political sovereignty in northern Hejaz before Islam is a subject of scholarly discussion, with sources like Al-Masudi and Ferdinand Wüstenfeld suggesting it existed.

# Issyk-Kul

## Overview
Issyk-Kul is a large, saline, endorheic lake (a closed basin with no outflow) in the Tian Shan mountains of Kyrgyzstan. It is the world's eighth-deepest and second-largest saline lake, notable for rarely freezing despite its high altitude and for its significant biodiversity, historical role on the Silk Road, and modern use as a naval testing site.

## Timeline
- **7th century** — The Buddhist monk Xuanzang passes the lake, noting it in his travelogue.
- **c. 1338-1339** — Genetic evidence indicates the Black Death was present in a settlement near the lake, possibly its origin point for the later pandemic.
- **15th century** — Earthquakes submerge the medieval settlement of Toru-Aygyr.
- **19th century** — The lake and surrounding territory are ceded from China's Qing dynasty to Russia via the Treaty of Tarbagatai.
- **1916** — Kyrgyz rebels attack a monastery on the lake, killing seven monks.
- **1948** — The Issyk-Kul State Reserve is established as Kyrgyzstan's first nature reserve.
- **1975** — The lake is designated a Ramsar site of global biodiversity importance.
- **1970s** — The endangered Sevan trout is introduced into the lake.
- **2000** — The Biosphere Reserve Issyk-Kul is established within the UNESCO World Network.
- **2008** — Kyrgyzstan leases land to the Russian Navy for torpedo and submarine testing.
- **2011** — India announces plans to invest in and use the Russian naval test facility.
- **2022** — Researchers confirm the presence of the Black Death pathogen in 14th-century burials near the lake.

## Body

### Geography and Hydrology
Issyk-Kul is 182 km long and up to 60 km wide, with a surface area of 6,236 km². It sits at an elevation of 1,607 m and reaches a depth of 668 m. Fed by 118 rivers and streams, including the Jyrgalang and Tüp, and by snowmelt and hot springs, it has no surface outlet. Its water salinity is approximately 0.6%, compared to 3.5% for seawater. The lake's level is currently about 8 m higher than in medieval times but is dropping by roughly 5 cm per year due to water diversion. The bottom contains the mineral monohydrocalcite, a rare lacustrine deposit.

### History and Archaeology
The lake was a stopover on the Silk Road. In 2022, researchers analyzing genetic material from 14th-century burials near the lake confirmed the presence of the Black Death pathogen in 1338 or 1339, suggesting it may have been an early epicenter of the pandemic that later devastated Eurasia. Submerged settlements, like the 13th-century Toru-Aygyr, have been found in shallow areas, along with artifacts including ancient coins and a sophisticated bronze cauldron.

### Environment and Ecology
The lake is a protected area, including a Ramsar site and a UNESCO Biosphere Reserve. It supports large populations of wintering and migratory birds. Its fish community is highly endemic but threatened; four endemic species, including Schmidt's dace and the sheer osman, are endangered due to overfishing, invasive predators like pike perch, and the cessation of restocking. The introduced Sevan trout, endangered in its native Armenia, has established itself in Issyk-Kul.

### Modern Use and Tourism
During the Soviet era, the northern shore became a major resort area. Tourism has since revived with refurbished hotels and private rentals. The city of Karakol is a regional administrative center and excursion base. Since the Soviet period, the lake's eastern end has been used for naval testing; Russia leases a facility there, and India has also invested in it for torpedo and underwater vehicle testing.

## Terms
- **Endorheic lake**: A closed drainage basin that retains water and allows no outflow to other external bodies of water, such as rivers or oceans.
- **Saline lake**: A lake with a high concentration of dissolved salts, higher than freshwater but typically lower than seawater.
- **Ramsar site**: A wetland site designated to be of international importance under the Ramsar Convention, especially as a waterfowl habitat.
- **Biosphere Reserve**: An international conservation designation by UNESCO, part of its Man and the Biosphere Programme, promoting a balanced relationship between people and nature.
- **Endemic (species)**: A species native and restricted to a certain place or area.
- **Alluvial cone**: A triangle-shaped deposit of gravel, sand, and even smaller pieces of sediment, such as silt, that is created as flowing water interacts with rocks.
- **Monohydrocalcite**: A rare carbonate mineral that is one of the few known to form in lake (lacustrine) environments.

## Debates and open questions
The source presents a key hypothesis: some hydrologists propose that Issyk-Kul's water filters deep underground into the Chu River, as it has no surface outlet. The exact origin and early spread of the Black Death from the Issyk-Kul region remains a subject of ongoing research, with the 2022 genetic study providing strong evidence for its presence there in the 14th century.

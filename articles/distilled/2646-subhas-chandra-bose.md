# Subhas Chandra Bose

## Overview
Subhas Chandra Bose was a prominent Indian anti-colonial nationalist who defied British rule, becoming a hero to many Indians. His legacy is complex and controversial due to his wartime alliances with Nazi Germany and Imperial Japan, which involved authoritarian methods and raised serious ethical questions, particularly regarding anti-Semitism. He remains a significant, if debated, figure in India's independence movement.

## Timeline
- **23 January 1897** — Born in Cuttack, Bengal Presidency (now Odisha), British India.
- **1921** — Abandoned Indian Civil Service career in England to join India's independence movement.
- **1938** — Elected President of the Indian National Congress (INC).
- **1939** — Re-elected Congress President but forced to resign after conflict with Gandhi and the INC leadership.
- **17 January 1941** — Escaped British surveillance in Calcutta, traveling via Afghanistan to Nazi Germany.
- **Late May 1942** — Met Adolf Hitler in Berlin; arranged passage to Japanese-held Southeast Asia.
- **February 1943** — Departed Germany by submarine, transferring to a Japanese submarine en route to Asia.
- **July 1943** — Took command of the Indian National Army (INA) in Singapore from Rash Behari Bose.
- **21 October 1943** — Proclaimed the Provisional Government of Free India (Azad Hind) in Singapore.
- **18 August 1945** — Died from burns sustained in a plane crash in Taihoku, Japanese-ruled Formosa (now Taiwan).

## Body

### Early Life and Nationalist Politics
Born into a privileged Bengali family, Bose received an Anglo-centric education. He passed the first Indian Civil Service exam in England but abandoned the career path in 1921 to join the Indian independence movement led by Mahatma Gandhi. He quickly aligned with more radical nationalists like Chittaranjan Das and Jawaharlal Nehru, advocating for complete independence (Swaraj) and open to socialist ideas. His confrontational style and differing views on non-violence led to a major rift with Gandhi. After being elected Congress President in 1938 and 1939, he was forced to resign and was ousted from the party.

### Alliance with Nazi Germany
Facing imprisonment, Bose escaped British India in January 1941 and reached Nazi Germany. The German leadership offered equivocal support for Indian independence. He established a Free India Centre in Berlin and recruited an Indian Legion from prisoners of war. However, the Germans were unwilling to form a full alliance, viewing Bose as less popular than Gandhi. Disappointed by Germany's stalled military progress and lack of commitment, Bose sought to move to Southeast Asia, where Japan was achieving victories. He met Hitler only once, in May 1942, before departing for Japan by submarine in February 1943.

### Leadership of the INA and Death
In Japanese-controlled Southeast Asia, Bose reorganized the Indian National Army (INA), composed largely of Indian prisoners of war. He established the Provisional Government of Free India (Azad Hind), which was recognized by Axis powers. The INA, alongside Japanese forces, launched an invasion of India (Operation U-Go) in 1944, but was ultimately defeated at the battles of Imphal and Kohima. With Japan's surrender imminent, Bose died on 18 August 1945 from burns after a plane crash in Taiwan. His death fueled persistent conspiracy theories among his supporters.

### Ideology and Controversial Legacy
Bose's ideology was a synthesis of anti-colonial nationalism, socialism, and authoritarianism. He admired the mobilization methods of fascist regimes and believed a strong, dictatorial state was necessary for post-independence India. His alliance with the Axis powers is the most controversial aspect of his legacy. While fighting British colonialism, he did not publicly criticize Nazi anti-Semitism or offer refuge to its victims, and in 1942 published an article urging anti-Semitism as part of the Indian liberation movement. His legacy is thus a mix of heroic defiance and troubling ethical compromises.

## Terms
*   **Netaji**: An honorific meaning "Respected Leader," first applied to Bose in Germany in 1942 and now used throughout India.
*   **Indian National Congress (INC)**: The principal political party leading India's independence movement, which Bose briefly led before being ousted.
*   **Indian National Army (INA)**: An armed force formed by Indian nationalists in exile during WWII, primarily from prisoners of war, to fight for India's independence with Japanese support.
*   **Azad Hind**: The Provisional Government of Free India, established by Bose in 1943 to administer Indian territories captured by the INA and Japanese forces.
*   **Swaraj**: A Sanskrit term meaning "self-rule" or "independence," a central goal of the Indian independence movement.
*   **Forward Bloc**: A faction Bose formed within the INC in 1939 to consolidate the political left after his resignation from the presidency.
*   **Operation U-Go**: The codename for the Japanese-INA offensive against British India in 1944, which ended in defeat at Imphal and Kohima.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Nature of his ideology**: Scholars debate whether Bose was a pragmatic militant nationalist using fascist methods for anti-colonial ends or a true believer in authoritarian, fascist-inspired governance.
*   **Ethical legacy**: His alliance with Nazi Germany and Imperial Japan poses a major ethical dilemma. Critics highlight his failure to condemn the Holocaust and his 1942 article promoting anti-Semitism, while supporters often emphasize his anti-colonial struggle.
*   **Circumstances of death**: The official account of his death in a 1945 plane crash is rejected by many supporters, who believe he survived and that various conspiracy theories about his fate are true.
*   **Impact on independence**: Historians assess whether the INA's military campaigns and the subsequent British trials of its officers significantly weakened British resolve to hold India, thereby accelerating independence.

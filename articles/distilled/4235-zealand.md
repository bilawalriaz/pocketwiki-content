# Zealand

## Overview
Zealand (Danish: Sjælland) is the largest and most populous island in Denmark proper, hosting 40% of the country's population, including the capital, Copenhagen. It serves as the nation's economic and political core, connected to neighboring islands and Sweden via major bridges and tunnels.

## Timeline
- **Pre-13th c.** — Tribal Danes settle Zealand and Scania, displacing earlier Jutish inhabitants.
- **13th c.** — Valdemar's Zealandic Law is enacted, one of Denmark's first regional civil codes.
- **Late 16th c.** — Most of North Zealand becomes a royal hunting domain.
- **1847** — First railway section opens between Copenhagen and Roskilde.
- **2007** — Kobanke is confirmed as Zealand's highest natural point (122.9 m).

## Body

### Etymology and Name
The origin of the Danish name *Sjælland* is uncertain. A leading hypothesis derives it from Old Danish *Siâland*, based on a word meaning "seal" or "deep bay, fjord," possibly referencing the island's geography near Roskilde Fjord. The English name "Zealand" likely comes from the German *Seeland* or an older Danish form, *Sælland*. The island has no etymological link to New Zealand, which is named after the Dutch province of Zeeland, though Danish immigration to New Zealand occurred in the 19th century.

### History and Settlement
The tribal Danes, speakers of an early North Germanic language, originated from Zealand and Scania. Before their arrival, Jutland and nearby islands were settled by the Jutes, who later migrated to Britain. On Zealand, early political and religious centers included Ringsted and Roskilde, later supplanted by Copenhagen. Helsingør became a key seaport due to its strategic location and the Sound Dues. The island's economy was historically based on manors, with early industrial centers like the Mølleåen watermills. The first railway, built by Det Sjællandske Jernbaneselskab, opened in 1847.

### Geography and Connections
Zealand is irregularly shaped and lies north of Lolland, Falster, and Møn. Its highest natural point is Kobanke (122.9 m). The island is connected to Funen by the Great Belt Fixed Link and to Amager by bridges. Through Amager and the Øresund Bridge, it is linked to Scania, Sweden. The Storstrøm and Farø Bridges connect it south to Falster and Lolland. The Fehmarnbelt Tunnel, under construction, will link Lolland to the German island of Fehmarn and mainland Europe.

### Mythology
In Norse mythology, the island was created by the goddess Gefjun, who tricked the Swedish king Gylfi. She carved out a piece of land and transported it to Denmark, forming Zealand. The resulting hole in Sweden filled with water to become Lake Mälaren, though some scholars identify the similar-shaped Lake Vänern as the true origin.

## Terms
- **Denmark proper**: The mainland and islands of Denmark, excluding Greenland and Disko Island.
- **Sound Dues**: Tolls collected by Denmark for passage through the Øresund strait.
- **Great Belt Fixed Link**: A combined bridge and tunnel system connecting Zealand to Funen.
- **Øresund Bridge**: A bridge-tunnel linking Copenhagen, Zealand, to Malmö, Sweden.
- **Paleocene**: The first epoch of the Paleogene Period; Zealand gives its name to the Selandian stage within it.
- **Valdemar's Zealandic Law**: A 13th-century civil code for the island, part of early Danish legal unification.

## Debates and Open Questions
The exact etymology of *Sjælland* remains debated, with competing theories linking it to "seal," "deep bay," or other roots. In Norse mythology, there is scholarly debate over whether the Swedish lake left by Gefjun was Vänern or Mälaren, based on geographical similarities to Zealand's shape.

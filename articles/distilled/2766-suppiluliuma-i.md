# Šuppiluliuma I

## Overview
Šuppiluliuma I (r. c. 1350–1322 BC) was a Hittite king who transformed his kingdom into the dominant power of the Late Bronze Age Near East. His military campaigns dismantled the rival kingdom of Mitanni and extended Hittite authority across Syria and into Mesopotamia. His reign is also marked by a disastrous diplomatic incident with Egypt, the Zannanza Affair, which led to a devastating plague that ravaged the Hittite state for decades.

## Timeline
- **c. 1350 BC** — Šuppiluliuma I becomes Hittite king after the death of Tudḫaliya III.
- **Early Reign** — Defeats the Kaška in the north and secures the northeastern frontier.
- **c. 1340s BC** — Launches the "One-Year War," conquering Mitanni's capital and most of its Syrian vassals.
- **c. 1330s BC** — Conducts the "Six-Year War," capturing Carchemish and installing his son as vassal king.
- **c. 1320s BC** — The Zannanza Affair: his son is murdered en route to marry the Egyptian queen.
- **c. 1322 BC** — Šuppiluliuma I dies, likely from plague; succeeded by his son Arnuwanda II.

## Body

### Origins and Accession
Šuppiluliuma's parentage was long debated, but seal impressions now identify him as the son of his predecessor, King Tudḫaliya III. He served as Tudḫaliya's chief military commander, helping recover Hittite lands from foreign occupation. When Tudḫaliya died, the throne was meant for his son, Tudḫaliya the Younger, to whom Šuppiluliuma had sworn loyalty. However, Tudḫaliya the Younger and his brothers were murdered, likely by Šuppiluliuma's supporters, allowing Šuppiluliuma to seize power. This act was later considered a primary cause of the plague that afflicted the Hittites.

### Military Campaigns in Anatolia
Before and during his reign, Šuppiluliuma waged extensive campaigns to secure Hittite borders. He defeated the Kaška people in the north and the Arzawa confederacy in the west, recovering lost territories. He also secured the northeast by arranging a marriage between his sister and the king of Azzi-Ḫayaša, establishing a vassal state. His western campaigns were prolonged, taking an estimated 20 years to fully settle.

### Conquest of Mitanni and Syria
Šuppiluliuma's greatest achievement was the defeat of Mitanni, a major rival in Upper Mesopotamia. After an initial failure, he allied with a Mitannian rival and launched the successful "One-Year War," capturing Mitanni's capital and its Syrian vassal states, including Aleppo and Nuḫašši. He later completed the conquest in the "Six-Year War," capturing Carchemish and installing his sons as viceroys. The weakened Mitanni was reduced to a Hittite dependency, with its former vassal, Assyria, now also under Hittite influence.

### The Zannanza Affair and Plague
Relations with Egypt turned hostile over the vassal kingdom of Amurru. The crisis peaked when the widowed Egyptian queen, likely Ankhesenamun, widow of Tutankhamun, proposed marriage to one of Šuppiluliuma's sons. After initial suspicion, Šuppiluliuma sent his son Zannanza, who was murdered en route. Believing the Egyptians responsible, Šuppiluliuma attacked Egyptian territory. The war's captives brought a plague to Hittite lands, causing a demographic catastrophe that lasted at least two decades and contributed to Šuppiluliuma's own death.

### Family and Succession
Šuppiluliuma's first wife was Ḫenti, possibly his half-sister. His sons included the heir Arnuwanda II, and viceroys Telipinu (Aleppo) and Piyaššili (Carchemish). His last queen was a Babylonian princess, possibly Malnigal. His daughter married the restored Mitannian king Šattiwaza, cementing a key alliance. After the plague, his second successor, Muršili II, blamed his father's ruthlessness and the Zannanza Affair for the gods' punishment.

## Terms
*   **Viceroy:** A ruler governing a subject territory on behalf of a sovereign king. Šuppiluliuma installed his sons as viceroys in Syria.
*   **Tawananna:** The title for the chief queen or queen mother in the Hittite court, a position with significant authority.
*   **Mitanni:** A major Hurrian-speaking kingdom in Upper Mesopotamia, the primary rival of the Hittites before its defeat by Šuppiluliuma.
*   **Kaška:** A people inhabiting the mountainous regions north of the Hittite heartland, frequently in conflict with them.
*   **Arzawa:** A confederation of states in western Anatolia, a persistent enemy of the Hittites.
*   **Vassal:** A state or ruler that owes allegiance and tribute to a more powerful sovereign in exchange for protection.
*   **Zannanza Affair:** The diplomatic incident where the Egyptian queen's proposed marriage to Šuppiluliuma's son ended in the prince's murder, triggering war and plague.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **The Identity of the Egyptian Queen:** While most scholars identify the widowed queen as Tutankhamun's widow Ankhesenamun, some propose she was Akhenaten's widow, Nefernefruaten-Nefertiti.
*   **The Murderer of Zannanza:** The Hittite texts are broken at the crucial point. While Šuppiluliuma blamed the Egyptians, the exact perpetrators and whether it was an official act or a factional plot remain unknown.
*   **The Cause of the Plague:** The Hittites attributed the plague to divine punishment for oath-breaking and murder. Modern scholars debate whether it was a specific epidemic like tularemia ("rabbit fever") and the precise sequence of events that introduced it.

# Cali

## Overview
Santiago de Cali, or Cali, is the capital of the Valle del Cauca department and the most populous city in southwest Colombia. It is a major economic and cultural hub, known as the "Salsa Capital of the World" and Colombia's "Sports Capital," having hosted numerous international events including the 1971 Pan American Games. Founded by the Spanish in 1536, its history spans pre-Columbian settlement, colonial rule, independence struggles, and modern industrial and social challenges.

## Timeline

- **1537** — City moved to its present location by Captain Miguel Muñoz.
- **3 July 1810** — Local junta established, refusing to recognize Spanish authority.
- **28 March 1811** — Battle of Bajo Palacé, first battle of Colombia's Independence.
- **1816** — Royalist rule restored by Pablo Morillo's army.
- **1819** — Royalist army defeated at Battle of Boyacá, leading to permanent Criollo control.
- **1956** — Cali explosion: army trucks carrying dynamite destroy eight city blocks, killing over 1,000.
- **1957** — First Cali Fair (La Feria de Cali) held.
- **1971** — Hosted the Pan American Games.
- **2021** — Major protests and riots occur; statue of founder Belalcázar is torn down.

## Body

### Pre-Columbian and Colonial Foundations
Before Spanish arrival, the region was inhabited by indigenous tribes, including the Gorrones and the Jamundíes, who resisted the conquistadors. Sebastián de Belalcázar founded Santiago de Cali on 25 July 1536, initially north of its current site. The city was moved to its present location in 1537. During the colonial period, Cali was part of the gobernación of Popayán. Its economy was based on haciendas (cattle farms and sugar cane plantations) worked by enslaved Africans, and it was strategically located for trade between mining regions and the Pacific port of Buenaventura.

### Independence and 19th Century
Cali's independence movement began early, with a local junta formed on 3 July 1810. The city was part of the Confederated Cities of the Cauca Valley, which declared independence from Popayán in 1811. After a period of conflict and the restoration of royalist rule in 1816, the decisive Battle of Boyacá in 1819 led to permanent control by independence forces. In the 19th century, Cali remained a small community of under 20,000 people, with an economy centered on livestock, sugar, and gold.

### 20th-Century Transformation and Crisis
The early 20th century saw a shift from an agricultural to an industrial and commercial economy, spurred by infrastructure like the railway to Buenaventura. Foreign investment in the 1940s-1960s established major factories, attracting immigration and making Cali a model of development. This growth collapsed in the late 1990s due to a national economic crisis and the costs of the internal conflict. The city also faced a severe public order crisis, with high homicide rates linked to conflicts between drug-trafficking groups like Los Rastrojos and Los Urabeños.

### Geography and Climate
Cali is located in the Cauca Valley, bordered by the Western Mountain Range to the east and the Cauca River. It sits at approximately 1,000 meters above sea level. The climate is a tropical monsoon (Köppen: Am), with an average annual temperature of 24°C (75°F). There are two rainy seasons (March-May and October-November), but the city is in a rain shadow, making it drier than the nearby coastal port of Buenaventura.

### Economy and Transport
Cali and Valle del Cauca form Colombia's third-largest economic center. The economy is driven by industry (food, beverages, pharmaceuticals), agriculture (sugar, fisheries), and trade. The city is served by Alfonso Bonilla Aragón International Airport, Colombia's third-busiest. Public transport includes the Masivo Integrado de Occidente (MIO) bus rapid transit system, which began operations in 2009.

### Culture and Sports
Cali is renowned for its salsa music culture, hosting events like the Summer Salsa Festival. The annual Cali Fair (La Feria de Cali) in December is the city's most important cultural event. As Colombia's "Sports Capital," it has hosted the Pan American Games (1971), World Games (2013), and numerous other championships. The city is home to major football clubs América de Cali and Deportivo Cali.

## Terms
- **Hacienda:** A large estate or plantation, common in colonial Latin America, often for agriculture or ranching.
- **Gobernación:** A colonial administrative division governed by a governor.
- **Criollo (Criollo):** A person of Spanish descent born in the Americas, often referring to the class that led independence movements.
- **Encomienda:** A colonial labor system where Spanish settlers were granted the right to the labor of indigenous people.
- **MIO (Masivo Integrado de Occidente):** Cali's bus rapid transit system, launched in 2009.
- **Köppen climate classification:** A system for classifying climates based on temperature and precipitation patterns.
- **Panela:** Unrefined whole cane sugar, a common food product in Colombia.
- **ESMAD:** Colombia's Mobile Anti-Disturbance Squadron, a riot police unit.

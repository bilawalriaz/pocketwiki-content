# Michiel de Ruyter

## Overview
Michiel de Ruyter (1607–1676) was a Dutch States Navy officer whose leadership during the Anglo-Dutch Wars established him as one of history's greatest naval commanders. Rising from a modest background, his strategic victories were crucial in defending the Dutch Republic's maritime power and commercial interests against England and France, cementing his status as a national folk hero.

## Timeline
- **24 March 1607** — Born in Vlissingen, Zeeland.
- **1641** — Serves as rear admiral in the Portuguese Restoration War.
- **1652** — Joins the Dutch Navy as a squadron commander in the First Anglo-Dutch War.
- **1655** — Promoted to vice admiral; participates in the Second Northern War.
- **1665** — Becomes lieutenant admiral and supreme commander of the Dutch fleet at the start of the Second Anglo-Dutch War.
- **1–4 June 1666** — Wins the Four Days' Battle.
- **1667** — Executes the Raid on the Medway, capturing the English flagship HMS *Royal Charles*.
- **1672** — Fights the Battle of Solebay in the Third Anglo-Dutch War.
- **1673** — Prevents an Anglo-French invasion at the Battles of Schooneveld and Texel.
- **8 January 1676** — Commands the Dutch fleet at the Battle of Stromboli.
- **22 April 1676** — Fatally wounded at the Battle of Augusta.
- **29 April 1676** — Dies in Syracuse, Sicily.

## Body

### Early Life and Merchant Career
De Ruyter was born to a seaman in Vlissingen and began seafaring at age 11. He fought as a cannoneer in 1622 during the Eighty Years' War before steadily rising through the merchant fleet to become a shipmaster by age 30. He married twice in the 1630s, experiencing personal tragedy with the death of his first wife and child. Between 1637 and 1640, he captained a privateer hunting Dunkirkers. After a brief, inconclusive naval action in 1641 during the Portuguese Restoration War, he returned to a prosperous merchant career for a decade, retiring to Vlissingen in 1651.

### First Anglo-Dutch War and Northern Wars
At the outbreak of the First Anglo-Dutch War in 1652, de Ruyter accepted a command in the Dutch Navy. He distinguished himself, notably at the Battle of Plymouth where he saved a large convoy, and was promoted to vice admiral by the war's end. He later participated in the Second Northern War, helping to relieve besieged Danish cities like Danzig and Nyborg, for which he was knighted by King Frederick III of Denmark.

### Second Anglo-Dutch War
In 1665, following a Dutch naval disaster at the Battle of Lowestoft, de Ruyter was appointed supreme commander of the fleet. In 1666, he won the hard-fought Four Days' Battle. His most celebrated achievement came in 1667 with the Raid on the Medway, where he destroyed much of the English fleet and captured its flagship, forcing a favorable peace for the Dutch. He later had a falling-out with his former ally, Cornelis Tromp, leading to Tromp's dismissal.

### Third Anglo-Dutch War
When England and France allied against the Dutch Republic in 1672, de Ruyter employed a strategy of using coastal shoals for protection and concentrating attacks on the English fleet while deterring the French. He fought the indecisive Battle of Solebay in 1672 and, in 1673, skillfully maneuvered his smaller fleet at the Battles of Schooneveld and Texel to prevent a combined Anglo-French invasion. A new rank, lieutenant-admiral general, was created for him in 1673.

### Final Campaign and Death
After the English withdrawal in 1674, de Ruyter fought against France. A 1674 amphibious assault on Martinique failed. In 1675, he was sent to the Mediterranean to aid Spain against France in Sicily. He fought the French fleet to a standstill at the Battle of Stromboli in January 1676. At the subsequent Battle of Augusta in April, he was fatally wounded by a cannonball. He died a week later in Syracuse and was buried with state honors in Amsterdam's Nieuwe Kerk.

## Terms
- **Lieutenant Admiral**: A senior naval rank; de Ruyter held this as supreme operational commander of the Dutch fleet.
- **Schout-bij-nacht**: A rank meaning "third in command," equivalent to a rear admiral in a squadron.
- **Commandeur**: A rank broadly comparable to a commodore, commanding a squadron.
- **Bestevaêr**: Early modern Dutch for "grandfather," de Ruyter's nickname reflecting his respect among sailors.
- **Rampjaar**: "Disaster year," the Dutch term for 1672, marked by military defeats and political upheaval.
- **Fireships**: Vessels set ablaze and sent into an enemy fleet to cause destruction.
- **Shoals**: Shallow waters that larger warships cannot navigate, used by de Ruyter as a tactical refuge.
- **Weather gauge**: The tactical advantage of having the wind at one's back, allowing control over the engagement.

## Debates and Open Questions
The source notes that many anecdotes about de Ruyter's early life are of "dubious historical value." The exact number of Dutch ships in the Battle of Plymouth is described as "unclear." There is also a scholarly debate about the origin of his surname "De Ruyter," with suggestions ranging from a nickname meaning "raider" to a commemoration of a cavalry ancestor.

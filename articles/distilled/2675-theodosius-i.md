# Theodosius I

## Overview
Theodosius I (347–395) was the last Roman emperor to rule both halves of the empire. His reign established Nicene Christianity as state orthodoxy and settled the Gothic crisis, but his death led to the empire's permanent division between his sons Arcadius and Honorius.

## Timeline
- **11 Jan 347** — Born in Hispania to general Theodosius the Elder.
- **374** — First command as *dux* of Moesia; repels Sarmatians.
- **378** — Appointed eastern emperor by Gratian after Valens' death at Adrianople.
- **380** — Issues Edict of Thessalonica, defining Nicene orthodoxy.
- **381** — Convenes First Council of Constantinople, confirming Nicene Creed.
- **3 Oct 382** — Ends Gothic War; Goths settle south of the Danube as *foederati*.
- **386** — Signs treaty with Sasanian Empire, partitioning Armenia.
- **388** — Defeats and executes usurper Magnus Maximus.
- **390** — Massacre of Thessalonica by Roman troops; Theodosius performs public penance.
- **392** — Western emperor Valentinian II dies; Eugenius proclaimed in the West.
- **6 Sep 394** — Defeats Eugenius at Battle of the Frigidus, becoming sole ruler.
- **17 Jan 395** — Dies in Milan; empire permanently divided between his sons.

## Body

### Early Life and Rise to Power
Born in Hispania, Theodosius first accompanied his father to Britain (368–369). He gained his first independent command in 374, repelling Sarmatians. His career was interrupted by his father's execution, forcing retirement. Rehabilitated after the responsible official's fall, he regained command by 377. Following the catastrophic defeat at Adrianople (378), where Emperor Valens was killed, Theodosius was appointed eastern emperor in 379 to manage the Gothic crisis.

### The Gothic War and Foreign Policy
Theodosius inherited a depleted army and a Gothic incursion he could not expel. After mixed military results, he negotiated a settlement in 382. The Goths settled south of the Danube as autonomous allies (*foederati*), serving Rome under their own leaders—a pragmatic but controversial arrangement creating a powerful semi-independent entity. In 386, he secured peace with the Sasanian Empire by partitioning Armenia.

### Civil Wars and Imperial Unity
Theodosius fought two civil wars to secure his dynasty. In 388, he defeated usurper Magnus Maximus, who had killed western emperor Gratian. After installing loyalists in the West, he returned east. Following the death of western emperor Valentinian II (392), he fought a second civil war against usurper Eugenius. His victory at the Frigidus (394) made him sole ruler, but he died months later, leaving the empire to his young sons.

### Religious Policy and the "End of Paganism"
A devout Nicene Christian, Theodosius opposed Arianism. He issued the Edict of Thessalonica (380), defining Nicene belief as orthodox and labeling other Christians heretics. He convened the First Council of Constantinople (381) to further define orthodoxy. While he issued laws against pagan sacrifices and heresy, modern scholars debate their practical impact. Archaeological evidence suggests paganism declined gradually; his policy was likely cautious and targeted, not a systematic destruction campaign. He did not prevent the demolition of some pagan temples, like the Serapeum of Alexandria, but also appointed non-Christians to high office.

### The Massacre of Thessalonica
In 390, Roman troops massacred thousands of civilians in Thessalonica, likely in response to a riot that killed a general. Theodosius, not present, was held responsible. Bishop Ambrose of Milan refused him communion until he performed public penance. While later tradition depicted this as a triumph of church over state, modern scholars see it as a negotiation between institutions, with Theodosius choosing a face-saving course of penitence.

## Terms
- **Consubstantiality (Homoousios):** Nicene doctrine that God the Father, Son, and Holy Spirit are of the same substance.
- **Arianism:** Heresy teaching the Son was created by and subordinate to the Father.
- **Foederati:** Barbarian groups settled within the Empire under treaty, providing military service while retaining autonomy.
- **Dux:** A Roman military commander or provincial general.
- **Edict of Thessalonica (380):** Decree establishing Nicene Christianity as orthodox for Constantinople.
- **First Council of Constantinople (381):** Ecumenical council reaffirming the Nicene Creed.
- **Damnatio memoriae:** Roman practice of erasing a person's name and images from public records.
- **Quinquennalia:** Celebration held every five years to mark an emperor's reign.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Theodosius's Role in the Massacre:** Did he directly order it, receive bad advice, or was it undisciplined military action he later covered for?
- **The "End of Paganism":** Was his reign a decisive turning point ending paganism through law, or did it decline gradually with his laws having limited effect?
- **The Gothic Settlement:** Was the 382 treaty a pragmatic necessity securing peace, or a catastrophic error creating a dangerous autonomous power?
- **Church vs. State:** Does Ambrose's penance demonstrate the church's new political power over the emperor, or was it a unique, negotiated event?

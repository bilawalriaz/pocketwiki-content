# Bolivia

## Overview
Bolivia is a landlocked, multiethnic country in central South America, named after independence leader Simón Bolívar. Its geography spans the high Andes mountains, the Altiplano plateau, and vast eastern lowlands, creating significant economic and cultural divides. Historically rich in silver and tin, Bolivia has experienced periods of political instability, indigenous empowerment, and economic reform, and remains a developing nation grappling with poverty and the effects of climate change.

## Timeline
- **c. 1500 BC – AD 1000** — Tiwanaku polity flourishes and then collapses.
- **1440s** — Inca Empire begins conquest of western Bolivia.
- **1533** — Spanish conquest of the Inca Empire is mostly completed.
- **1809** — Chuquisaca Revolution, the first cry for independence in Latin America.
- **6 August 1825** — Republic of Bolivia is proclaimed.
- **1879–1883** — War of the Pacific; Bolivia loses its coastal territory to Chile.
- **1903** — Treaty of Petrópolis; Bolivia loses the Acre territory to Brazil.
- **1932–1935** — Chaco War with Paraguay; Bolivia loses part of the Gran Chaco.
- **1952** — Bolivian National Revolution establishes universal suffrage and nationalizes tin mines.
- **1982** — Return to democratic civilian rule.
- **2006** — Evo Morales, the first indigenous president, takes office and begins nationalizations.
- **2009** — New constitution renames the country the Plurinational State of Bolivia.
- **2019** — President Evo Morales resigns amid political crisis.
- **2020** — Luis Arce of MAS party wins presidential election.
- **2025** — Rodrigo Paz wins election, ending MAS governance.

## Body

### Geography and Climate
Bolivia is the fifth-largest country in South America and the largest landlocked country in the Southern Hemisphere. Its geography is divided into three main physiographic regions: the Andean region (28% of territory) in the southwest, featuring the high Altiplano plateau and mountains like Nevado Sajama; the Sub-Andean region (13%), an intermediate zone of valleys; and the Llanos region (59%), a vast area of tropical lowlands and rainforests in the north and east. This diversity creates varied climates, from polar conditions in the high Andes to humid tropical in the lowlands. Bolivia is highly vulnerable to climate change, with its tropical glaciers receding rapidly, threatening water supplies for agriculture and cities.

### History
The region was home to the advanced Tiwanaku civilization, which collapsed around AD 1000. The Inca Empire conquered the western part in the 15th century before the Spanish arrived in the 16th century. Colonial rule was centered on the silver wealth of Potosí. Independence was declared in 1825 after a long war. The 19th and early 20th centuries were marked by territorial losses to neighbors, notably the coastal region to Chile in the War of the Pacific. The 1952 Revolution brought universal suffrage and nationalized mines. The late 20th century saw political instability, including military dictatorships, before a democratic transition began in 1982. The 21st century has been defined by the rise of indigenous political power under Evo Morales (2006–2019), who oversaw economic growth and a new constitution, followed by a brief interim government and a return to MAS rule.

### Government and Politics
Bolivia is a unitary state divided into nine departments. The 2009 Constitution established the Plurinational State, recognizing indigenous self-governance. The government has three branches: executive (led by a president), legislative (a bicameral Plurinational Legislative Assembly), and judicial. La Paz is the seat of government, while Sucre is the constitutional and judicial capital. Politics have been volatile, with three presidents resigning under pressure since 2003. Freedom House classifies Bolivia as a partly-free democracy.

### Economy
Bolivia's economy is driven by natural resources, including natural gas, minerals (silver, tin, zinc, lithium), and agriculture (soy, coca). It has the world's largest lithium reserves. Under Evo Morales, the state nationalized key hydrocarbon assets, and revenues funded social programs that significantly reduced poverty and inequality. However, the country remains one of South America's poorest. Agriculture is vital for rural employment, and the cultivation of coca is a major, though controversial, economic activity.

### Demographics and Culture
Bolivia is multiethnic, with a large indigenous population (including Aymara and Quechua peoples) and a majority mestizo population. The 2009 constitution recognizes 36 official indigenous languages alongside Spanish. The population is young and increasingly urban, though a significant rural minority remains. Bolivian culture blends indigenous and Spanish influences, evident in its festivals (like the Carnival of Oruro), music, and art. Roman Catholicism is the predominant religion, often syncretized with indigenous beliefs.

## Terms
- **Altiplano:** A high plateau in the Andes mountains, home to Lake Titicaca and the capital, La Paz.
- **Camba/Colla:** Demonyms referring to the cultural and economic divide between the eastern lowlands (Camba) and the western Andean highlands (Colla).
- **Mestizo:** A person of mixed European and Indigenous ancestry; the largest demographic group in Bolivia.
- **Plurinational State:** Bolivia's official name since 2009, emphasizing its multi-ethnic composition and indigenous rights.
- **Salar de Uyuni:** The world's largest salt flat, located in the Andes, and a major source of lithium.
- **Tiwanaku:** A pre-Inca civilization and polity that dominated the southern Andes until its collapse around AD 1000.
- **Mita:** A colonial labor draft system, particularly brutal in the silver mines of Potosí.
- **Latifundism:** The ownership of large landed estates, a system historically opposed by agrarian reform movements.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Territorial Losses:** Bolivia's history of losing over half its territory to neighbors, especially its access to the Pacific Ocean after the War of the Pacific, remains a central national grievance and a key issue in foreign policy.
- **Democratic Backsliding:** The political tenure of Evo Morales sparked debate over democratic backsliding, with his government accused of authoritarian tendencies despite popular support and economic growth.
- **Coca Cultivation:** The legal cultivation of coca for traditional and medicinal purposes versus its use in cocaine production is a persistent and contentious issue, involving domestic policy, indigenous rights, and international drug enforcement.
- **Lithium Development:** Bolivia holds the world's largest lithium reserves, but its extraction poses a dilemma between economic development and environmental protection of the unique Salar de Uyuni ecosystem.

# Zoroaster

## Overview
Zarathushtra Spitama, known as Zoroaster, was an ancient Iranian religious reformer and the spiritual founder of Zoroastrianism. His teachings, preserved in the Gathas, introduced a dualistic worldview centered on the conflict between truth (asha) and deception (druj), emphasizing personal morality and free will. Zoroastrianism became the dominant religion of Greater Iran for over a millennium, and Zoroaster's ideas are considered a possible influence on later Abrahamic faiths and Greek philosophy.

## Timeline
- **c. 1500–500 BC** — Most likely period for Zoroaster's life, based on linguistic and socio-cultural evidence.
- **c. 7th–6th century BC** — Traditional date for Zoroaster's life, based on later Zoroastrian sources.
- **c. 6th century BC** — Zoroastrianism becomes the prominent religion of Greater Iran.
- **3rd century BC – 7th century AD** — Period of official sanction for Zoroastrianism, especially under the Sassanid Empire.
- **7th century AD** — Zoroastrianism is persecuted following the Arab-Muslim conquest of Iran.
- **10th century AD** — Trend of historicizing legends and myths may include Zoroaster.
- **1885** — Friedrich Nietzsche publishes *Thus Spoke Zarathustra*, using Zoroaster's name for his central character.

## Body

### Name and Etymology
Zoroaster's name in his native Avestan language was likely *Zaraθuštra*. The common Greek form "Zoroaster" derives from a 5th-century BC transliteration. The Avestan name is generally accepted to derive from an Old Iranian form meaning "he who can manage camels," though alternative interpretations exist, such as "with old/aging camels" or "who is fostering camels." The name appears in various forms across later Iranian languages and classical sources.

### Date and Place
There is no consensus on Zoroaster's dating. Classical sources placed him thousands of years before their own time, while later Zoroastrian tradition dated him to "258 years before Alexander." Modern scholarship is divided between a late date (7th–6th century BC) and an early date (1500–1000 BC), with the early dating supported by linguistic similarities between the Old Avestan of the Gathas and the Sanskrit of the Rigveda. His birthplace is also unknown, but most scholars agree it was in eastern Greater Iran, possibly in regions like Bactria, Sistan, or the Bactria–Margiana Archaeological Complex.

### Life and Teachings
According to tradition, Zoroaster was trained as a priest from a young age. Around age 30, he experienced a divine revelation, encountering the being Vohu Manah (Good Purpose) and learning of Ahura Mazda (the Wise Lord) and the cosmic dualism of asha (order) versus druj (deception). He gained royal patronage under King Vishtaspa, established a community, and married three times. His teachings, recorded in the Gathas, emphasize that humans have free will to choose between good and evil through their thoughts, words, and deeds, thereby aligning with asha.

### Influences and Legacy
Zoroaster's ideas may have influenced later religions. Some scholars see his concepts of cosmic dualism and personal morality reflected in Judaism, Christianity, and Islam. In the Islamic world, Zoroaster was known as the founder of the Magians, and later Zoroastrian texts were sometimes modified to resemble Islamic monotheism. In the West, he was often depicted as an ancient sage, astrologer, or magician. His name was later adopted by Friedrich Nietzsche for his philosophical novel *Thus Spoke Zarathustra*.

## Terms
- **Gathas**: The oldest Zoroastrian scriptures, a collection of hymns traditionally attributed to Zoroaster.
- **Avestan**: The old Iranian language in which the Avesta, the Zoroastrian religious corpus, is written.
- **Asha**: A core Zoroastrian concept meaning truth, order, or righteousness, in opposition to druj.
- **Druj**: The principle of deception, falsehood, or disorder in Zoroastrianism.
- **Ahura Mazda**: The "Wise Lord," the supreme deity in Zoroastrianism.
- **Angra Mainyu**: The "Destructive Spirit," the evil counterpart to Ahura Mazda in Zoroastrian dualism.
- **Amesha Spenta**: A group of seven divine beings or "Holy Immortals" in Zoroastrianism.
- **Mazdayasna**: The Zoroastrian system of religious ethics, translated as "Worship of Wisdom/Mazda."

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Dating**: There is no consensus; scholarly estimates range from 1500 BC to 500 BC, with linguistic evidence favoring an earlier date and traditional sources a later one.
- **Historicity**: By modern historiographical standards, no evidence can place Zoroaster in a fixed period, and the historicization of his life may be a later development.
- **Birthplace**: While generally agreed to be in eastern Iran, specific locations like Bactria, Sistan, or the Bactria–Margiana Archaeological Complex remain tentative proposals.
- **Influence**: The extent of Zoroaster's direct influence on Greek philosophers (e.g., Heraclitus, Plato) and Abrahamic religions is a subject of ongoing scholarly controversy.

# Niue

## Overview
Niue is a raised coral atoll in the South Pacific, known as "The Rock," that exists in free association with New Zealand. It is one of the world's largest coral islands, with a small, predominantly Polynesian population that has significantly declined due to migration. Its political status as a self-governing state with a unique constitutional relationship with New Zealand, combined with its environmental initiatives and economic challenges, makes it a distinctive case study in Pacific island nationhood.

## Timeline
- **c. 900 CE** — Polynesians from Samoa settle on Niue.
- **16th century** — War party from Tonga arrives, according to tradition.
- **1774** — Captain James Cook sights Niue but is refused landing; names it "Savage Island."
- **1846** — Niuean missionary Nukai Peniamina returns to introduce Christianity.
- **1900** — Niuean chiefs consent to British annexation; it becomes a British Protectorate.
- **1901** — Niue is annexed by New Zealand.
- **1974** — Niue Constitution Act establishes self-government; Robert Rex becomes first Premier.
- **2004** — Cyclone Heta causes extensive damage, including to the capital Alofi.
- **2016** — Niue declares all national debt paid off.
- **2020** — Niue becomes the first entire country designated an International Dark Sky Sanctuary.
- **2022** — Niue's entire Exclusive Economic Zone is declared a marine park.
- **2023** — The United States formally establishes diplomatic relations with Niue.

## Body

### History and Political Status
Archaeological evidence indicates Polynesian settlement from Samoa around 900 CE. European contact began with James Cook in 1774, but sustained interaction started with whalers in the 19th century and Christian missionaries in the 1840s. In 1900, Niuean chiefs petitioned for British protection, leading to annexation by New Zealand in 1901. Self-government was restored in 1974 following a referendum, with Niueans choosing self-government in free association with New Zealand. The New Zealand monarch is Niue's head of state, and New Zealand handles most diplomatic relations, though Niue has developed its own foreign policy, establishing ties with countries like China, India, and the United States. It is not a UN member but participates in some UN agencies.

### Geography and Environment
Niue is a 261.46 km² raised coral atoll with a central plateau about 60 metres above sea level, surrounded by cliffs and a coral reef. Its soils are geochemically unusual, containing high levels of natural radioactivity from deep-sea origins. The climate is tropical rainforest. Niue has pursued "green growth," aiming to become fully organic and to use 80% renewable energy by 2025. In 2022, it declared its entire 317,500 km² Exclusive Economic Zone a marine park, though enforcement is a challenge. The Huvalu Forest Conservation Area protects primary forest and is an Important Bird Area.

### Economy and Infrastructure
Niue's economy is small, with a GDP of US$24.9 million in 2016, and relies heavily on aid from New Zealand, which provides about a third of government revenue. Key sectors are agriculture (notably taro and vanilla), fisheries, and tourism. The economy was severely set back by Cyclone Heta in 2004. Niue has no political parties, and its population of 1,681 (2022) is sparse, with most Niueans living in New Zealand. Infrastructure includes Niue International Airport and the .nu internet domain, a significant digital asset. In 2026, Starlink was licensed to operate as an internet provider.

### Demographics and Culture
The population is predominantly Niuean (73.7% in 2022), with English and Niuean as official languages. The majority are Christian, primarily affiliated with the Ekalesia Niue Church. Culture includes traditional arts like *hiapo* (barkcloth) and the *takalo* war dance. Rugby union is the most popular sport. Education is provided through two public schools, with scholarships available for study in New Zealand.

## Terms
*   **Free association:** A relationship where a smaller state (Niue) manages its own internal affairs while a larger state (New Zealand) handles defense and external relations, as defined by the 1974 Constitution.
*   **Raised coral atoll:** An island formed from a coral reef that has been lifted above sea level, creating a central plateau surrounded by cliffs.
*   **Exclusive Economic Zone (EEZ):** A maritime zone extending 200 nautical miles from a coast, where a state has special rights to explore and use marine resources.
*   **Patu-iki:** The traditional title for the kings of Niue before the modern era.
*   **Hiapo:** A traditional Niuean art form of decorated tapa cloth made from the inner bark of the paper mulberry tree.
*   **NISP (Niue Integrated Strategic Plan):** The national development plan setting priorities for Niue's governance and economy.
*   **.nu domain:** The internet country code top-level domain for Niue, a major source of revenue and a point of sovereignty discussion.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Sovereignty and Recognition:** While self-governing, Niue's full international sovereignty is debated. It is not a UN member, and its diplomatic recognition (e.g., by the U.S. in 2023) is an ongoing process.
*   **Economic Viability:** Niue's economy is heavily dependent on New Zealand aid and remittances. The sustainability of its development model, especially after debt repayment in 2016, is a key question.
*   **Digital Sovereignty:** The control and revenue from the .nu internet domain have been subjects of dispute between the Niuean government and the managing entity, IUS-N, reflecting broader issues of national control over digital assets.
*   **Environmental Enforcement:** The declaration of the entire EEZ as a marine park is ambitious, but the government's limited resources (e.g., only five staff in the Fisheries Division) raise questions about effective enforcement against illegal fishing.

# Itō Hirobumi

## Overview
Itō Hirobumi (1841–1909) was a foundational statesman of modern Japan, serving as its first Prime Minister and a key architect of its constitutional and governmental systems during the Meiji era. His career bridged Japan's transformation from a feudal society to a constitutional monarchy and imperial power, though his legacy is deeply contested due to his central role in the colonization of Korea.

## Timeline
- **16 October 1841** — Born Hayashi Risuke in Chōshū Domain.
- **1863** — Secretly travels to England as one of the Chōshū Five.
- **1868** — Appointed junior councilor for foreign affairs after the Meiji Restoration.
- **1871–1873** — Serves as deputy ambassador on the Iwakura Mission to the U.S. and Europe.
- **1881** — Emerges as de facto leader of the Meiji oligarchy; entrusted with drafting a constitution.
- **1885** — Becomes Japan's first Prime Minister, establishing the cabinet system.
- **11 February 1889** — Meiji Constitution is promulgated.
- **1892–1896** — Serves second term as Prime Minister during the First Sino-Japanese War.
- **1900** — Founds the Rikken Seiyūkai political party.
- **1906** — Becomes first Resident-General of Korea.
- **26 October 1909** — Assassinated in Harbin, Manchuria, by Korean nationalist An Jung-geun.

## Body

### Early Life and Education
Born into a poor farming family, Itō was adopted into a low-ranking samurai family. He studied at the Shōka Sonjuku academy under the activist Yoshida Shōin, whose execution in 1859 radicalized him into the *sonnō jōi* (revere the emperor, expel the barbarians) movement. A brief, secret study trip to England in 1863 (one of the Chōshū Five) profoundly shifted his views, making him a pragmatist who advocated for learning from the West rather than expelling it.

### Early Meiji Statesman and Financial Reforms
After the Meiji Restoration of 1868, Itō's Western knowledge propelled his career. He helped draft the "Principles for National Policy," advocating for a centralized monarchy, engagement with foreign countries, and scientific learning. In 1870, he studied U.S. finance, directly influencing Japan's adoption of the gold standard and modern banking system. His participation in the Iwakura Mission (1871–1873) further solidified his belief in gradual, systematic reform over radical change.

### Father of the Meiji Constitution
Itō became the central figure in drafting Japan's constitution. After a political crisis in 1881, he secured a mandate for a Prussian-style model. A year-long European study tour (1882–1883), especially his work with Lorenz von Stein in Vienna, provided the intellectual framework for a constitution that granted the emperor significant power while creating a national Diet. As Prime Minister (1885–1888), he established the cabinet system and the Privy Council. The Meiji Constitution, promulgated in 1889, was a "gift" from the emperor, not a contract with the people, though it did guarantee popular participation through the Diet.

### Later Career and the Rikken Seiyūkai
Itō's pragmatism evolved; he came to accept the necessity of party politics. He facilitated Japan's first party cabinet in 1898 and, in 1900, founded the Rikken Seiyūkai (Friends of Constitutional Government), Japan's first major political party. His fourth premiership (1900–1901) was short-lived. He later led constitutional reforms in 1907 that aimed to strengthen cabinet authority, though they inadvertently solidified some military independence.

### Resident-General of Korea and Assassination
Itō served as the first Resident-General of Korea (1906–1909), initially advocating for a protectorate and gradual modernization. Facing persistent Korean resistance and the failure of his "self-rule" policy, he ultimately supported the plan for outright annexation in 1909. He resigned as Resident-General in June 1909 and was assassinated four months later by Korean independence activist An Jung-geun in Harbin.

## Terms
*   **Genrō:** An informal group of senior statesmen who effectively dictated policy for the Empire of Japan during the Meiji era.
*   **Meiji Constitution:** The constitution of the Empire of Japan, promulgated in 1889, which established a constitutional monarchy with the emperor as sovereign.
*   **Privy Council (Genrōin):** A high-level advisory body to the emperor, created by Itō to review the constitution and advise on major political matters.
*   **Rikken Seiyūkai:** The first major political party in Japan, founded by Itō in 1900, which dominated pre-war politics.
*   **Kokutai:** A Japanese term for "national polity" or "national essence," often referring to the emperor-centered, unbroken imperial line as the core of the nation.
*   **Resident-General of Korea:** The chief administrator of the Japanese protectorate in Korea, a position held by Itō from 1906 to 1909.
*   **Sonnō jōi:** A political slogan and movement in the late Edo period meaning "Revere the Emperor, Expel the Barbarians."

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Gradualism vs. Opportunism:** Scholars debate whether Itō's political evolution—from anti-Western radical to constitutional monarchist to party founder—reflected a consistent, pragmatic gradualism or a flexible opportunism.
*   **Legacy in Korea:** Itō is viewed in Japan as a modernizer and statesman, but in Korea as a primary architect of imperialism and colonization. His role in the process leading to annexation remains deeply controversial.
*   **Constitutional Intent:** There is debate over the true nature of the Meiji Constitution: was it a sincere step toward limited government and popular sovereignty, or primarily a tool to legitimize oligarchic and imperial power?

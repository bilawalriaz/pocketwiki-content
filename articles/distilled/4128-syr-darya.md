# Syr Darya

## Overview
The Syr Darya, historically known as the Jaxartes, is one of the two main rivers feeding the Aral Sea in Central Asia. It is vital for irrigation in an arid region, but Soviet-era water diversion projects contributed to the catastrophic shrinkage of the Aral Sea, creating a major ecological disaster.

## Timeline
- **329 BC** — Alexander the Great reaches the Jaxartes, fights the Battle of Jaxartes against the Saka, and founds Alexandria Eschate (modern Khujand).
- **7th-8th centuries AD** — The Muslim conquest of Central Asia occurs; the city founded by Alexander is known as Khujand.
- **18th century** — The current name "Syr Darya" comes into use.
- **1847-1882** — The Russian Empire operates steam navigation on the river, with a key port at Kazalinsk.
- **Soviet era** — Extensive irrigation canals are built, diverting water for cotton and rice and causing the Aral Sea's disappearance.
- **1991** — The fall of the Soviet Union disrupts the regional water-sharing system.
- **2012** — The Syrdarya–Turkestan State Regional Natural Park is established in Kazakhstan.

## Body

### Name and Etymology
The river's earliest recorded name was Jaxartes (Ancient Greek: Ἰαξάρτης), possibly derived from the Old Persian *Yakhsha Arta* ("True Pearl"). Other historical names include the Turkic *Yinçü* ("Pearl river") and Chinese *Yaosha River*. The modern name "Syr Darya" dates from the 18th century. "Syr" may mean "secret" in Tajik, while "darya" means "river" in Tajik and "sea" in Persian. An alternative etymology from the Sogdian language suggests *Yaksart* means "flowing" or "streaming."

### History and Strategic Importance
The river was a significant frontier in antiquity. In 329 BC, Alexander the Great's army met native resistance at the Jaxartes, leading to the Battle of Jaxartes and the founding of a garrison city, Alexandria Eschate (now Khujand). In the 19th century, the Russian Empire introduced steam navigation on the river. Following the Soviet collapse in 1991, a cooperative water-sharing system between Central Asian nations disintegrated, leading to ongoing disputes over water management.

### Geography and Hydrology
The Syr Darya originates from the Naryn and Kara Darya headstreams in the Tian Shan mountains. It flows for approximately 2,256 km through Kyrgyzstan, Uzbekistan, Tajikistan, and Kazakhstan to the northern Aral Sea. Its drainage basin covers over 800,000 km², but only a fraction contributes significant flow. Its annual discharge is about 37 km³, half that of the Amu Darya. The river is crucial for irrigating Central Asia's most productive agricultural lands.

### Ecological Damage
Soviet-era expansion of irrigation canals for cotton and rice fields massively diverted the river's water. At times, no water reached the Aral Sea, contributing to its near-total disappearance. This has caused severe ecological damage. Additionally, uranium concentrations in the river water in Tajikistan sometimes exceed WHO drinking water guidelines, with pollution originating upstream in Uzbekistan and Kyrgyzstan.

## Terms
- **Endorheic basin:** A closed drainage basin that retains water and allows no outflow to other external bodies of water, like the Aral Sea basin.
- **Irrigation:** The artificial application of water to land to assist in the production of crops.
- **Headstreams:** The source streams that join to form a river.
- **Drainage basin:** An area of land where all water that falls in it drains to a common outlet.
- **Discharge:** The volume of water flowing through a river channel per unit of time.
- **Canal:** An artificial waterway constructed to allow the passage of boats or ships inland or to convey water for irrigation.

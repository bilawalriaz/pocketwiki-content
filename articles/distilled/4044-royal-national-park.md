# Royal National Park

## Overview
The Royal National Park is a protected area in New South Wales, Australia, established in 1879. It is the first national park declared in Australia and one of the oldest in the world, significant for its early conservation status and its preservation of diverse coastal and valley ecosystems near Sydney.

## Timeline
- **1879** — Formally proclaimed as "National Park" by Sir John Robertson, Acting Premier of New South Wales.
- **1955** — Renamed "Royal National Park" after Queen Elizabeth II passed by during her 1954 tour.
- **2006** — Added to the Australian National Heritage List.
- **2023** — Platypus relocated back to the park under a joint program.

## Body

### History and Access
The park was founded in 1879, just seven years after Yellowstone National Park in the United States. It was originally named "National Park" and renamed in 1955. The park covers 151 square kilometres and is located about 29 kilometres south of the Sydney central business district. It is situated on the traditional lands of the Dharawal people. Access is via road from Loftus, Waterfall, or Otford, and by ferry from Cronulla to Bundeena. A vehicle access fee applies.

### Landscape and Vegetation
The park's geography varies from coastal cliffs and beaches to an ancient plateau dissected by deep river valleys draining into Port Hacking. The geology is primarily Triassic Hawkesbury Sandstone. Vegetation communities are diverse and include:
*   **Coastal Heathland:** Hardy, salt-tolerant shrubs on rocky terrain with minimal soil.
*   **Littoral Rainforest:** Rare surviving coastal rainforest, such as the "Palm Jungle."
*   **Exposed Uplands:** Rocky ridges and plateaus with poor soil, significant for protecting species from cleared similar habitats.
*   **Valley Sides:** Sclerophyll open forest (dry and wet) with diverse eucalyptus species, shaped by bushfires and low-nutrient soils.
*   **Valley Floors:** Cooler, humid areas with rich soils supporting tall trees (up to 50m), temperate rainforest pockets, and groves of turpentine and cabbage palms.
*   **Riparian Forest:** Vegetation along streams, often containing rare species.
*   **Mangroves and Salt Marsh:** Found along the Port Hacking Estuary, serving as vital nursery grounds for fish and habitat for birds and crustaceans.
*   **Tidal Rockshelves and Rock Pools:** Fringing the coastline, home to molluscs, seaweed, and the toxic blue-lined octopus.

### Park Highlights and Activities
The park offers numerous walking and cycling trails, with the 30-kilometre Coast Walk being the most popular. Key highlights include:
*   **Audley:** A historic picnic area with a weir, boathouse, and dance hall.
*   **Wattamolla Beach:** A sheltered cove with a lagoon and waterfall.
*   **Lady Carrington Drive:** A former carriage road, now a popular walking and cycling track.
*   **Camping:** Permitted only at Bonnie Vale, North Era, and Uloola Falls, with a pre-booking system.
The park is heavily used for environmental education and has been burnt in bushfires, notably in 1939, 1994, and 2001.

## Terms
*   **Sclerophyll:** A type of vegetation (e.g., eucalyptus forest) adapted to low-nutrient soils and frequent fire.
*   **Littoral Rainforest:** A rare type of rainforest that grows on sand dunes along the coast.
*   **Riparian Forest:** The forested area along the banks of a river or stream.
*   **Estuary:** A partially enclosed coastal body of water where freshwater from rivers mixes with saltwater from the ocean (e.g., Port Hacking).
*   **Heathland:** A shrubland habitat found on thin, poor soils, often in coastal areas.
*   **Alluvium:** Soil, clay, or other material deposited by running water, typically found on valley floors.

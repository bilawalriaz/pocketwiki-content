# Uttar Pradesh

## Overview
Uttar Pradesh is India's most populous state, with over 241 million inhabitants, and a historically and politically significant region in the north. It is the heartland of several major empires and a major pilgrimage center for Hinduism, Buddhism, and Jainism, while its modern economy is dominated by the services sector.

## Timeline
- **c. 6000 BCE** — Neolithic settlements with early agriculture emerge at sites like Koldihwa.
- **c. 1000 BCE** — The Painted Grey Ware culture marks the beginning of the Iron Age in the region.
- **320–200 BCE** — The Maurya Empire controls the vital Gangetic plains.
- **590–647 CE** — The Kannauj empire under Harshavardhana reaches its zenith.
- **1206–1526** — The region is partially or entirely ruled by the Delhi Sultanate.
- **1526** — Babur founds the Mughal Empire; Uttar Pradesh becomes its heartland.
- **1803–04** — The British East India Company gains control after defeating the Maratha Empire.
- **1857** — The Indian Rebellion of 1857 begins at Meerut, playing a central role in the independence movement.
- **1902** — The North-Western Provinces and Oudh are renamed the United Provinces of Agra and Oudh.
- **1950** — The United Provinces are renamed Uttar Pradesh after India becomes a republic.
- **2000** — Northern districts are separated to form the new state of Uttarakhand.

## Body

### Geography and Climate
Uttar Pradesh is India's fourth-largest state by area, dominated by the fertile Indo-Gangetic plain. The Ganges and Yamuna rivers, which meet at Prayagraj, are central to its geography and agriculture. The state has a humid subtropical climate with four seasons, including hot summers with temperatures up to 50 °C and a monsoon season that brings most of the annual rainfall (650–1,000 mm). Floods and droughts are recurring phenomena.

### History
The region has been inhabited since prehistoric times, with evidence of early agriculture and urbanization. It was the core of ancient mahajanapadas (great realms) and later major empires like the Maurya, Gupta, and Mughal. The Delhi Sultanate ruled for 320 years, followed by the Mughals, for whom it was the heartland. After a period of Maratha influence, the British East India Company took control in the early 19th century. The area was central to the 1857 rebellion and the later independence movement. It was renamed Uttar Pradesh in 1950.

### Demographics and Society
Uttar Pradesh is the most populous Indian state, with a high population density. It has the largest scheduled caste population in the country. The state is home to the largest populations of both Hindus (approx. 80%) and Muslims (approx. 19%) in India. Hindi is the primary official language, with Urdu as an additional official language. Regional languages like Awadhi, Bhojpuri, and Braj Bhasha are widely spoken. The state has a low sex ratio and a literacy rate (67.7% in 2011) below the national average.

### Governance and Administration
The state has a bicameral legislature and sends the largest number of representatives (80 to Lok Sabha) to the national Parliament. It is divided into 18 divisions and 75 districts, each administered by an Indian Administrative Service officer. The Allahabad High Court is the state's highest judicial body. Politics is dominated by four major parties, often characterized by caste-based tensions and communal conflicts.

### Economy
The economy is the third-largest in India, with a GSDP of ₹39.8 trillion. The services sector is the largest contributor, followed by agriculture, where the state is a major producer of food grains and sugar (about 70% of India's sugar). Manufacturing, including leather and textiles, is significant. However, the state faces challenges like high poverty, regional economic disparities (western districts are more developed), and a large number of out-migrants seeking employment.

### Culture and Religion
The state is a major center for several religions. It hosts key Hindu pilgrimage sites like Varanasi, Mathura, and Ayodhya, and Buddhist sites like Sarnath (where Buddha gave his first sermon) and Kushinagar. It has a rich literary and artistic tradition, being the origin of the Kathak dance form and home to renowned writers. Mughlai and Awadhi cuisines are prominent. The Kumbh Mela at Prayagraj is one of the world's largest religious gatherings.

## Terms
- **Mahajanapada**: One of sixteen "great realms" or oligarchic republics in ancient India.
- **Indo-Gangetic plain**: A fertile alluvial plain formed by the Ganges River system, covering most of Uttar Pradesh.
- **Terai**: A marshy, low-lying belt of land at the base of the Himalayas in southern Nepal and northern India.
- **Lok Sabha**: The lower house of India's bicameral Parliament.
- **Rajya Sabha**: The upper house of India's bicameral Parliament.
- **Scheduled Castes**: Officially designated groups in India historically subject to social discrimination.
- **Zamindari**: A historical land tenure system where landlords collected taxes from peasants.
- **Gharana**: A lineage or school of a particular performing art, especially in Hindustani classical music or Kathak dance.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The proposal to split Uttar Pradesh into four smaller states (Purvanchal, Bundelkhand, Avadh Pradesh, and Paschim Pradesh) was announced in 2011 but later dropped, indicating ongoing debate about administrative reorganization.
- The state's poor record in economic development, governance, and human development indices (HDI) despite its political influence is a persistent issue.
- The demolition of the Babri Mosque in Ayodhya in 1992 and the subsequent legal and social conflicts highlight enduring communal tensions.

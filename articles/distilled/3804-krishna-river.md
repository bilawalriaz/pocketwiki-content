# Krishna River

## Overview
The Krishna River is the third-longest river in India, flowing 1,400 km from the Western Ghats to the Bay of Bengal. It is a vital water source for irrigation and drinking across four states—Maharashtra, Karnataka, Telangana, and Andhra Pradesh—and its basin supports significant agriculture, industry, and biodiversity.

## Timeline
- **c. 3000 BC** — Jain texts associate the region near the river with the Abhiras.
- **1779** — Stone structure with temples built across the river at Bhilawadi, Maharashtra.
- **1882–1884** — British-built railway bridge constructed at Ankali, Sangli.
- **1891** — British-built Kudachi–Ugar railway bridge completed.
- **1903** — Previous record river inflow at Prakasam Barrage set.
- **1933–1943** — Sirat-e-joodi bridge built near Raichur in memory of a Hyderabad prince.
- **1976** — Bachawat tribunal award for interstate water sharing issued.
- **2009** — Severe floods cause widespread damage; record inflow surpasses 1903 levels.
- **2013** — Brijesh Kumar tribunal award on water sharing given; later challenged in court.
- **2015** — Polavaram right bank canal links Godavari River to Krishna River.
- **Sep 2024** — Unprecedented floods hit Vijayawada; Prakasam Barrage records highest discharge in 70 years.
- **Aug 2025** — Flood threats emerge in Karnataka; Almatti Dam releases large volumes of water.

## Body

### Course and Basin
The Krishna originates near Mahabaleshwar in Maharashtra at about 1,300 m elevation and flows east to the Bay of Bengal. It traverses Maharashtra (305 km), Karnataka (483 km), and Andhra Pradesh (612 km). Its basin covers 258,948 km², making it India’s fifth-largest river basin. The basin’s average annual surface water potential is 78.1 km³, with 58.0 km³ utilizable. Due to inadequate water, the Godavari River was linked to the Krishna in 2015 via the Polavaram canal.

### Tributaries and Confluences
The river has 13 major tributaries. The longest is the Bhima River (861 km). Other principal ones are the Tungabhadra, Ghataprabha, Malaprabha, and Musi. The confluence of the Krishna and Malaprabha at Kudalasangama is a holy site for the Lingayat sect. Three tributaries—Panchganga, Warana, and Yerla—meet the Krishna near Sangli, a revered area.

### Ecology and Resources
The basin contains diverse soils and rich mineral deposits, including coal, iron, limestone, gold, and diamonds. The Krishna Wildlife Sanctuary in the estuary protects the last surviving mangrove forests and species like fishing cats, otters, and estuarine crocodiles. However, heavy pollution from cities and coal-fired power stations, combined with over-extraction for agriculture, has severely degraded the river’s ecology. In most years, the river no longer reaches the sea, leading to saline soils in its lower reaches.

### Water Management and Floods
Interstate water sharing is governed by the 1976 Bachawat tribunal award, though disputes continue, with Telangana seeking a fresh hearing. The river is prone to severe floods, notably in 2009 and 2024. The 2024 floods in Vijayawada were the worst in 70 years, with the Prakasam Barrage discharging 33,000 m³/s, overwhelming its capacity and causing major damage.

### Cultural Significance
The Krishna is sacred to Hindus, who believe bathing in it removes sins. The Krishna Pushkaram festival is held every 12 years on its banks. Numerous temples line its course, including the Kanaka Durga Temple in Vijayawada and the Mallikarjuna Jyotirlinga at Srisailam. Many historic bridges, like the British-era Irwin Bridge at Sangli, span the river.

## Terms
- **Basin**: The area of land drained by a river and its tributaries.
- **Catchment area**: The area from which a river receives its water from rainfall and tributaries.
- **Tributary**: A smaller river or stream that flows into a larger main river.
- **Confluence**: The point where two rivers meet and join.
- **Laterite**: A soil type rich in iron and aluminum, formed in hot and wet tropical areas.
- **Cusec**: A unit of flow rate, equal to one cubic foot per second.
- **Tribunal**: A formal judicial body established to resolve disputes, such as over water sharing.
- **Mangrove**: Salt-tolerant trees and shrubs growing in coastal saline or brackish water.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The Brijesh Kumar tribunal award (2013) on water sharing is challenged by Andhra Pradesh, and Telangana demands a fresh hearing, leaving the legal framework contested.
- The river’s ecological death is debated, as it rarely reaches the sea due to overuse, leading to saline soils and loss of aquatic ecosystems.
- The proposal to build a coastal reservoir on the Bay of Bengal to utilize floodwaters from Krishna and Godavari is a potential but unimplemented solution to water scarcity.

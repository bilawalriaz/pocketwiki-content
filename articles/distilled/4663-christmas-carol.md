# Christmas carol

## Overview
Christmas carols are songs or hymns on the theme of Christmas, traditionally sung during the Christmas season. They represent a subset of Christmas music and have evolved from early religious hymns to a global tradition encompassing both sacred and secular songs, often performed in churches, concerts, and community gatherings.

## Timeline
- **4th century** — First known Christmas hymns appear in Rome, such as "Veni redemptor gentium" by Ambrose.
- **13th century** — Popular Christmas songs develop in vernacular languages in France, Germany, and Italy, influenced by Francis of Assisi.
- **1426** — Earliest English carols recorded in a manuscript by John Awdlay.
- **1582** — "Piae Cantiones" collection published, containing early forms of many later popular carols.
- **1618** — "Silent Night" first performed in Oberndorf, Austria.
- **1833** — William Sandys publishes "Christmas Carols, Ancient and Modern," widening popular appeal.
- **1861** — "Hymns Ancient and Modern" includes several carols, helping integrate them into church services.
- **1880** — Singing of carols instituted on Christmas Eve in Truro Cathedral, Cornwall.
- **1928** — "The Oxford Book of Carols" published, becoming a widely used source.
- **1961** — "Carols for Choirs" series begins, becoming a bestselling collection in the English-speaking world.

## Body

### Origins and Early History
The first known Christmas hymns date to 4th-century Rome, with Latin works like "Veni redemptor gentium" by Ambrose. By the 13th century, popular songs in regional languages emerged, influenced by figures like Francis of Assisi. The earliest English carols appear in a 1426 manuscript. Many carols originated as communal festival songs for seasonal celebrations before being adopted for church use.

### Popularization and Church Adoption
Carols gained popularity after the Protestant Reformation, with reformers like Martin Luther promoting congregational singing. Despite a Puritan ban in England, carols were sung privately. The 19th century saw a revival through publications like William Sandys' 1833 collection and the inclusion of carols in hymnals such as "Hymns Ancient and Modern" (1861). Carols were formally instituted in church services in 1880 at Truro Cathedral.

### Musical and Lyrical Characteristics
Traditionally, carols are based on medieval chord patterns, giving them a distinctive sound. Some, like "Good King Wenceslas," trace directly to the Middle Ages. Lyrics often describe biblical episodes such as the Annunciation, the Nativity, or the Adoration of the Shepherds. Some carols, like "A Bone, God Wot!", have non-religious themes but are still classified as carols.

### Global Traditions
Caroling traditions vary worldwide. In Australia and New Zealand, "Carols by Candlelight" concerts are held outdoors. In Greece, children sing "Kalanta" carols on Christmas Eve, New Year's Eve, and Epiphany Eve. Polish carols are sung until February 2. In Ukraine, "kolyadka" were originally winter solstice songs later adapted for Christmas. The "Huron Carol" (c. 1642) is Canada's oldest Christmas song.

### Classical and Modern Composers
Composers have incorporated carols into classical works, such as Ralph Vaughan Williams' "Fantasia on Christmas Carols" (1912) and Benjamin Britten's "A Ceremony of Carols" (1942). Modern carols continue to be written, with composers like Alfred Burt contributing to the repertoire.

## Terms
- **Carole**: A song or hymn, possibly derived from French "carole" or Latin "carula," meaning a circular dance.
- **Villancico**: A poetic and musical form from the Iberian Peninsula and Latin America, now commonly meaning "Christmas carol."
- **Kalanta**: Greek folk carols sung during the Christmas season.
- **Kolyadka**: Ukrainian Christmas carols, originally for the winter solstice.
- **Nadala**: Catalan Christmas songs sung from Advent to Epiphany.
- **Wassailing**: The tradition of groups traveling house-to-house singing carols, often for rewards.
- **Macaronic**: A mixture of languages, such as English and Latin, in carol lyrics.
- **Piae Cantiones**: A 1582 collection of late-medieval Latin songs containing early carol forms.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The exact origin of the word "carol" is unclear, with possible roots in French or Latin terms for dance.
- The melody of "Adeste Fideles" ("O Come, All Ye Faithful") has a disputed origin.
- Sociological studies suggest carol singing is not solely related to Christian beliefs but also reinforces national customs and family traditions.

# Costa Rica

## Overview
Costa Rica is a Central American nation known for its stable democracy, abolition of its military in 1949, and strong emphasis on education and environmental protection. It transitioned from a poor, agrarian colony to a high-income economy with a diversified base in services, ecotourism, and technology, consistently ranking highly in human development and happiness indices.

## Timeline
- **c. 7000–10000 BC** — First human inhabitants arrive as hunter-gatherers.
- **1502** — Christopher Columbus sails to the eastern coast; the name "Costa Rica" ("Rich Coast") originates.
- **1821** — Costa Rica gains independence from Spain as part of the First Mexican Empire.
- **1823** — Joins the Federal Republic of Central America; a brief civil war (Battle of Ochomogo) moves the capital from Cartago to San José.
- **1838** — Formally declares independence from the Federal Republic of Central America.
- **1848** — Promulgates a liberal constitution and holds Central America's first free and fair national election.
- **1846** — Main road built, facilitating coffee export to the Pacific port of Puntarenas.
- **1890** — San José to Limón railroad completed, enabling banana exports and attracting Jamaican immigrant workers.
- **1917–1919** — Military dictatorship of Federico Tinoco Granados; its unpopularity leads to a decline in military influence.
- **1948** — 44-day Civil War following a disputed election; led by José Figueres Ferrer.
- **1949** — New constitution adopted, granting universal suffrage, social guarantees, and permanently abolishing the army.
- **2007** — Costa Rica breaks diplomatic ties with Taiwan, recognizing the People's Republic of China.

## Body

### Pre-Columbian and Colonial History
Human presence dates to between 7,000 and 10,000 BC. Indigenous peoples, classified within the Intermediate Area between Mesoamerican and Andean cultures, developed settled farming communities by the first millennium BCE. Spanish colonization began in the early 16th century. Costa Rica was a poor, isolated, and sparsely populated province of the Spanish Empire, described in 1719 as "the poorest and most miserable Spanish colony in all America."

### Independence and Nation-Building
Costa Rica gained independence in 1821 without fighting. After a brief civil war in 1823, the capital moved to San José. It formally left the Federal Republic of Central America in 1838. The 19th-century economy was transformed by coffee cultivation, which created a wealthy planter class and funded modernization. The construction of a railroad to the Caribbean in the 1870s-1890s, built with Jamaican labor, enabled banana exports, which later rivaled coffee. This led to the rise of foreign-owned corporations like the United Fruit Company, whose labor practices sparked major disputes, such as the Great Banana Strike.

### 20th Century to Present
The 20th century saw two periods of violence: a military dictatorship (1917–1919) and the 1948 Civil War. The latter led to a new constitution in 1949 that abolished the military, established universal suffrage, and guaranteed social rights. This cemented Costa Rica's path as a stable, unarmed democracy. The economy has since diversified from agriculture into finance, pharmaceuticals, software, and ecotourism. The country is a regional leader in human rights and environmentalism, having reversed deforestation and generating nearly all its electricity from renewable sources.

### Government and Society
Costa Rica is a presidential republic with a unicameral Legislative Assembly. The 1949 constitution established a robust democracy with an independent judiciary. The country is divided into seven provinces and 82 cantons. Its foreign policy focuses on human rights, sustainable development, and peace. It hosts international courts and has been a proponent of global governance initiatives. The population is predominantly Spanish-speaking and Christian, though secularism is rising. The education system is free and compulsory, with a high literacy rate, and the healthcare system provides universal coverage.

## Terms
- **Pura Vida**: A common Spanish phrase meaning "pure life," reflecting a national philosophy of a simple, positive, and relaxed existence.
- **Intermediate Area**: A cultural region where the peripheries of Mesoamerican and Andean native cultures overlapped, including pre-Columbian Costa Rica.
- **Coffee Barons**: The wealthy class of growers who emerged from the 19th-century coffee boom, which financed national modernization.
- **United Fruit Company**: A U.S. corporation that became a dominant economic and political force in Costa Rica through banana production, symbolizing foreign economic influence.
- **Great Banana Strike**: A major 1934 labor dispute against the United Fruit Company that led to the first collective agreement and was a step toward effective trade unions.
- **Social Insurance Administration (CCSS)**: The institution created in 1941 that administers Costa Rica's universal healthcare system.
- **Forest Law (1996)**: Legislation that provided financial incentives to landowners for environmental services, helping to reverse deforestation.
- **Beyond Oil and Gas Alliance (BOGA)**: An international initiative launched by Costa Rica and Denmark in 2021 to phase out fossil fuel use.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The timing of the transition from hunting and gathering to agriculture as the main livelihood in pre-Columbian Costa Rica is still unknown.
- The origin of the name "Costa Rica" is debated, with accounts attributing it to either Christopher Columbus or conquistador Gil González Dávila.
- Costa Rica's long-term disagreement with Nicaragua over navigation rights on the San Juan River and border issues (e.g., Isla Calero) has been subject to international court rulings but remains a point of contention.

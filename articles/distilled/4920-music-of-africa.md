# Music of Africa

## Overview
African music is a vast and diverse collection of musical traditions from across the continent, characterized by complex polyrhythms, call-and-response patterns, and deep social functions. It has profoundly influenced global genres like jazz, blues, and Latin music, while also evolving through cross-cultural exchange. Understanding African music is key to grasping the roots of many modern musical forms worldwide.

## Timeline
- **c. 3000 BC** — Ancient Egyptian and Carthaginian civilizations flourish, influencing later Mediterranean cultures.
- **7th-8th centuries** — Arab conquests begin integrating North Africa into the Maghreb, linking its music to Middle Eastern traditions.
- **1933** — Solomon Linda forms Solomon Linda's Original Evening Birds in South Africa.
- **1939** — Linda's group releases "Mbube," the first African record to sell over 100,000 copies.
- **1950s-1960s** — Miriam Makeba begins her career, later becoming a global ambassador for African music.
- **1960s-1970s** — Fela Kuti and Tony Allen develop Afrobeat in West Africa.
- **1972** — Manu Dibango releases "Soul Makossa," which becomes the most sampled African record in history.
- **1986** — Paul Simon's album *Graceland* popularizes South African music internationally.
- **1990s** — The genre Kalpop originates in Kenya.
- **2010** — "Waka Waka (This Time for Africa)," sampling a 1986 makossa song, becomes the FIFA World Cup theme.
- **2014** — The annual Visa for Music festival begins in Morocco.
- **2016** — Sony Music opens an office in Nigeria, expanding its African presence.

## Body

### Musical Characteristics and Social Role
African music is highly rhythmic, often employing polyrhythm—multiple contrasting rhythms played simultaneously, such as a three-against-two pattern. A common feature is call-and-response, where a melodic or rhythmic phrase is echoed by another voice or instrument. Music is integral to communal life, created for both enjoyment and participation, serving social bonding, commentary, and religious ritual. It is often functional, with specialized songs for work, marriage, and ceremonies, and is traditionally passed down orally.

### Regional Diversity
The continent's music is regionally diverse. North African music maintains ties to Middle Eastern maqamat (melodic modes). The music of the Horn of Africa, like Ethiopia's qenet modal system, is also distinct. Sub-Saharan Africa is often divided into four musicological regions: East, Southern, Central, and West. These share core Bantu rhythmic principles but show additional influences. West African music varies between Muslim regions, which incorporate Islamic vocal styles and favor string instruments, and non-Muslim regions, which emphasize drumming and indigenous traditions.

### Instruments and Language
Instruments are categorized as membranophones (drums), chordophones (strings like the kora), aerophones (winds), idiophones (rattles), and percussion. Unique designs, like the mbira's two-rank note layout, facilitate polyrhythmic playing. In many tonal African languages, vocal melody is constrained by linguistic tones, and instrumental music can imply text, forming the basis of "talking drums."

### Global Influence and Afro-Pop
African musical traditions, carried by enslaved people, were foundational to North American genres like blues, jazz, and Caribbean styles. These rhythms later influenced rock and soul. In a reciprocal exchange, African popular music (afro-pop) adopted Western instruments and studio techniques. Key 20th-century figures include Miriam Makeba and Fela Kuti. Genres like Congolese rumba evolved from Cuban son, and artists like Manu Dibango created widely sampled hits.

### Industry and Modern Challenges
The African music industry faces challenges like piracy and weak copyright enforcement, making live concerts a primary income source. Some countries, like Zimbabwe, mandate high percentages of local music on airwaves. Major labels like Sony have recently expanded into Africa, and platforms like MusikBi offer legal downloads, though limited by internet speeds. Festivals like Visa for Music aim to promote African artists globally.

## Terms
- **Polyrhythm**: The simultaneous use of two or more conflicting rhythms.
- **Call-and-response**: A musical texture where a phrase is answered by another voice or instrument.
- **Maqamat**: A system of melodic modes used in traditional Arabic and North African music.
- **Qenet**: A modal system in Ethiopian music comprising several main modes.
- **Membranophone**: A musical instrument that produces sound primarily by a vibrating stretched membrane, such as a drum.
- **Idiophone**: An instrument that creates sound by the vibration of the instrument itself, without strings or membranes, like rattles.
- **Lamellophone**: An instrument consisting of a set of tuned metal or bamboo tongues (lamellae) attached to a board or box, such as the mbira.
- **Afrobeat**: A music genre combining West African musical styles with American funk and jazz.
- **Makossa**: A Cameroonian urban popular music style.
- **Kalpop**: A Kenyan music genre blending Klassikan, African, and popular music elements.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The precise classification and boundaries of regional musicological areas within Sub-Saharan Africa are subject to scholarly debate.
- The extent to which specific contemporary genres are direct evolutions of traditional forms versus new creations influenced by global exchange is often discussed.

# Dire Dawa

## Overview
Dire Dawa is one of Ethiopia's two chartered cities, located in the east near the Oromia and Somali regions. Founded in 1902 due to a railway construction challenge, it grew into a major trade and industrial hub, second in population only to Addis Ababa. Its history reflects Ethiopia's broader political shifts, from imperial rule to Italian occupation, the Derg regime, and the current federal system.

## Timeline

- **1936** — Occupied by Italian troops during the Second Italo-Ethiopian War.
- **1941** — Liberated by Allied forces and placed under British Military Administration.
- **1947** — Handed back to the Ethiopian government.

- **2004** — Officially became a chartered city under federal administration.
- **2006** — Severe flooding from the Dechatu and Gorro rivers causes significant damage and casualties.

## Body

### Foundation and Early Growth
Dire Dawa's origin is tied to the Addis Ababa–Djibouti Railway. When the steep terrain near Harar proved too difficult, Emperor Menelik II agreed in 1896 to terminate the first section at a village at the foot of the mountains. The railway reached this location on 24 December 1902, the city's founding date. The railway company's engineers planned the northwestern part of the city, which attracted European and Arab residents, while the southeastern market area grew with Somali and Oromo traders. By the 1920s, the city's population reached 20,000.

### Italian Occupation and Post-War Expansion
Italian troops occupied Dire Dawa in May 1936, sparing it from bombardment due to international pressure. They improved infrastructure but left before completing their urban plans. After liberation in 1941, the city was under British administration until 1947. In the following decades, it expanded greatly, with its population reaching 160,000 and new industries established.

### Conflict and Administrative Changes
During the Derg regime (1974–1991), Dire Dawa saw worker unrest and nationalization of industries. A pivotal event was the 1977 Battle of Dire Dawa, where Ethiopian forces successfully repelled a Somali invasion, a decisive moment in the Ogaden War. After the EPRDF took control in 1991, territorial disputes between the Oromia and Somali regions led the federal government to administer Dire Dawa directly. It gained its current status as a chartered city in 2004.

### Demographics and Climate
The 2007 census recorded a population of 341,834, with the Oromo (46%), Somali (24%), and Amhara (20%) as the largest ethnic groups. Islam is the predominant religion (70.9%). The city has a tropical savanna climate with a mean annual temperature of 25.9°C and two rainy seasons.

## Terms
*   **Chartered City:** A city in Ethiopia with a special administrative status, directly governed by the federal government, like Addis Ababa.
*   **Woreda:** An administrative division in Ethiopia, similar to a district.
*   **Derg:** The military junta that ruled Ethiopia from 1974 to 1991.
*   **EPRDF:** The Ethiopian People's Revolutionary Democratic Front, the coalition that overthrew the Derg and has governed since 1991.
*   **Ogaden War:** A conflict (1977–1978) between Ethiopia and Somalia over the Ogaden region.
*   **Harla:** An extinct people believed to have inhabited the Dire Dawa area.
*   **Gurgura:** A clan sharing both Oromo and Somali identities, historically significant in the region.
*   **Dechatu River:** The river on which Dire Dawa is situated.

## Debates and Open Questions
The source notes that the area around Dire Dawa is believed to have been a settlement of the extinct Harla people, indicating archaeological and historical uncertainty about its earliest inhabitants. The city's administrative fate was a point of contention in 1993, with both the Somali and Oromia regions claiming it before federal intervention.

# Ugarit

## Overview
Ugarit was a major Levantine port city in northern Syria, flourishing during the Late Bronze Age (c. 1500–1185 BC). Its archaeological remains, rediscovered in 1928, are crucial for understanding the interconnected eastern Mediterranean world before its collapse. The site yielded the oldest known systematic alphabet and a rich archive of texts that illuminate the language, literature, politics, and daily life of the period.

## Timeline
- **c. 8000 BC** — First occupation of the site begins in the Neolithic period.
- **c. 1820 BC** — Ugarit becomes a vassal of the Kingdom of Yamhad (Aleppo).
- **c. 1620–1590 BC** — Hittite attacks lead to the collapse of Yamhad; Ugarit is relatively shielded.
- **c. 1500–1200 BC** — Ugarit's golden age as a major mercantile kingdom.
- **c. 1450–1185 BC** — Height of the Kingdom of Ugarit.
- **c. 1400 BC** — Development of the Ugaritic alphabetic script.
- **c. 1350–1315 BC** — Reign of Niqmaddu II; Ugarit becomes a vassal of the Hittite Empire.
- **c. 1215–1180 BC** — Reign of Ammurapi, the last king of Ugarit.
- **c. 1192–1190 BC** — Ugarit is destroyed and abandoned, part of the Late Bronze Age collapse.
- **1928** — Site rediscovered by a peasant plowing a field.
- **1929** — Systematic excavations begin under Claude Schaeffer.

## Body

### History and Politics
Ugarit's origins trace to the Neolithic, but it rose to prominence in the Late Bronze Age as a wealthy, independent kingdom. It maintained complex diplomatic and trade relations with major powers, including the Egyptian New Kingdom, the Hittite Empire, and Cyprus (Alashiya). In the mid-14th century BC, it became a Hittite vassal. The city's final decades were marked by severe regional crisis, including widespread food shortages and attacks by "Sea Peoples." Its last king, Ammurapi, sent desperate pleas for aid to the Hittites, but Ugarit was sacked and destroyed around 1190 BC, never to be reoccupied.

### Language and Literature
Ugarit is famed for its cuneiform archives, which used a dual scribal system. The diplomatic lingua franca was Akkadian, but the local Northwest Semitic Ugaritic language was also written in a unique 30-letter alphabetic cuneiform script, developed c. 1400 BC. This alphabet's letter order shows strong similarities to the later Phoenician alphabet. The literary texts found include mythological poems (e.g., the Ba'al Cycle, the Legend of Keret), legal documents, administrative lists, and royal correspondence that provides direct insight into international politics.

### Archaeology and Excavation
The site, known as Ras Shamra, covers about 28 hectares. Excavations since 1929 have revealed a city with a fortified acropolis, a royal palace, temples, and residential quarters. Key structures include the temples of Baal and Dagan on the acropolis, the Royal Palace complex, and the House of Alabaster Vessels, which contained Egyptian artifacts suggesting high-status foreign residents. The nearby port of Minet el-Beida served as Ugarit's harbor. Thousands of cuneiform tablets were discovered in libraries and archives throughout the city, providing the primary source for its history.

### Chronology and Destruction
The destruction of Ugarit is a key chronological marker for the end of the Bronze Age. Analysis of its destruction layer, which contains Late Helladic IIIB pottery but not the later IIIC style, helps date the transition in the Aegean. Cuneiform records and synchronisms with Egyptian pharaohs (like Merneptah and Ramesses III) and Hittite kings indicate the city fell between 1192 and 1190 BC. The cause was likely attacks during the widespread Late Bronze Age collapse, compounded by famine and the withdrawal of Hittite protection.

## Terms
- **Cuneiform:** A system of writing using wedge-shaped marks on clay tablets, used for several ancient languages.
- **Late Bronze Age Collapse:** A widespread societal crisis and decline across the Eastern Mediterranean and Near East around 1200 BC.
- **Vassal:** A state or ruler that is subordinate to a more powerful sovereign.
- **Alphabet (Ugaritic):** A writing system of 30 consonantal letters, the oldest known systematic alphabet.
- **Northwest Semitic:** A branch of the Semitic language family, including Ugaritic, Hebrew, and Phoenician.
- **Akkadian:** An East Semitic language that served as the lingua franca of the ancient Near East in the Bronze Age.
- **Tell:** An archaeological mound formed by the accumulated remains of ancient settlements.
- **Marzēah:** A Levantine communal feast or ritual, possibly associated with religious or social banquets.

## Debates and Open questions
- The exact cause and sequence of Ugarit's final destruction remain debated, with theories ranging from "Sea Peoples" attacks to internal rebellion or earthquakes.
- The precise logic behind the design and ordering of the Ugaritic alphabet's letters is still unknown.
- The relationship between the Ugaritic and Phoenician alphabets is not fully understood; they may share a common ancestor but are not direct derivatives.
- The identification of the "Temple of Rhyton" as a possible Temple of El is based on a single statue and is not conclusive.

# Kyoto

## Overview
Kyoto is a major city in Japan's Kansai region, serving as the country's cultural capital and a major tourist destination. It was Japan's imperial capital for over a millennium (794–1869), a history that has endowed it with an unparalleled concentration of temples, shrines, and traditional architecture, much of which was spared from World War II destruction.

## Timeline
- **794** — Emperor Kanmu establishes Heian-kyō (Kyoto) as the new imperial capital.
- **1467–1477** — The Ōnin War causes extensive destruction to the city.
- **Late 16th C.** — Toyotomi Hideyoshi reconstructs the city, adding new streets and earthwork walls.
- **1869** — The imperial court is relocated to Tokyo after the Meiji Restoration.
- **1889** — The modern municipality of Kyoto is officially formed.
- **1890** — The Lake Biwa Canal is completed, supplying water and hydroelectric power.
- **1956** — Kyoto becomes a city designated by government ordinance.
- **1994** — 17 historic monuments are inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
- **1997** — The Kyoto Protocol on greenhouse gas emissions is adopted in the city.

## Body

### History and Significance
Kyoto was chosen as the seat of Japan's imperial court in 794 by Emperor Kanmu, who wished to distance the government from the Buddhist clergy in Nara. The city, originally named Heian-kyō ("capital of peace and tranquility"), was modeled on the Chinese Tang dynasty capital of Chang'an. It remained Japan's official capital until the imperial court moved to Tokyo in 1869. Despite periods of severe destruction, notably during the Ōnin War (1467–1477), Kyoto was repeatedly rebuilt and flourished as a major cultural and economic center. Its survival largely intact through World War II is attributed to its removal from the atomic bomb target list at the insistence of U.S. Secretary of War Henry L. Stimson.

### Culture and Heritage
Kyoto is considered the cultural heart of Japan, housing approximately 20% of the nation's National Treasures. The city contains roughly 2,000 religious sites, including 1,600 Buddhist temples and 400 Shinto shrines. Prominent landmarks include Kiyomizu-dera, Kinkaku-ji (Golden Pavilion), and Ginkaku-ji (Silver Pavilion). The "Historic Monuments of Ancient Kyoto" are collectively a UNESCO World Heritage Site. The city is also renowned for its traditional festivals, such as the Gion Matsuri, and its unique cuisine, which features *kyō-yasai* (local vegetables). The Agency for Cultural Affairs of the Japanese government is headquartered in Kyoto.

### Economy and Industry
Kyoto's economy is driven by information technology, electronics, and tourism. It is the headquarters of major companies like Nintendo, Kyocera, and Omron. Traditional industries remain vital, with Kyoto being the premier center for kimono manufacturing and home to prominent sake breweries like Gekkeikan. Tourism is a significant contributor, though it faced a sharp decline during the COVID-19 pandemic.

### Geography and Climate
The city is located in the Yamashiro Basin, surrounded on three sides by mountains. This interior positioning results in a humid subtropical climate with hot summers and cold winters. The city sits atop a large natural water table, though urbanization is causing wells to dry up.

### Administration and Demographics
Kyoto is governed by a mayor and a 67-member city assembly. As of 2020, its population was 1.46 million, making it Japan's ninth-most populous city. Over half of Kyoto Prefecture's population resides in the city. While historically one of Japan's largest cities, its relative population rank has declined in the post-war era.

## Terms
- **Heian-kyō**: The original name for Kyoto, meaning "capital of peace and tranquility," used from its founding in 794.
- **Machiya**: Traditional wooden townhouses characteristic of Kyoto's historic architecture.
- **Kyō-yasai**: A variety of vegetables native to the Kyoto region, developed due to the city's inland location and Buddhist dietary influences.
- **Jidaigeki**: A genre of Japanese film and television set in the historical past, often featuring samurai; many are filmed at Kyoto's Toei Uzumasa Eigamura studio.
- **Lake Biwa Canal**: A 20 km waterway completed in 1890 that linked Lake Biwa to Kyoto, providing water, irrigation, and hydroelectric power.
- **Kuge**: The court nobility of imperial Japan, who were based in Kyoto.
- **Ōnin War**: A devastating civil war (1467–1477) that caused widespread destruction in Kyoto.
- **Kyoto Protocol**: An international treaty adopted in Kyoto in 1997, committing industrialized nations to reduce greenhouse gas emissions.

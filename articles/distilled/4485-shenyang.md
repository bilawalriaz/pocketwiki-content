# Shenyang

## Overview
Shenyang, historically known as Mukden, is a major sub-provincial city in Northeast China and the capital of Liaoning province. It is a critical industrial, transportation, and cultural hub for the region, with a history spanning over 8,000 years. The city's development has been shaped by its role as a strategic military and political center for various dynasties, including the Ming and Qing, and by significant foreign influence from Russia and Japan in the 20th century.

## Timeline
- **c. 300 BCE** — City established as Hou City during the Warring States period.
- **1625** — Jurchen leader Nurhaci captures Shenyang, making it the Later Jin capital.
- **1634** — City renamed Mukden (Shengjing).
- **1905** — Battle of Mukden (Russo-Japanese War) leads to increased Japanese influence.
- **1931** — Mukden Incident triggers Japanese invasion of Manchuria.
- **1932-1945** — City serves as a center of heavy industry under the Japanese puppet state Manchukuo.
- **1948** — Captured by the People's Liberation Army after the Liaoshen Campaign.
- **2005** — Awarded "national forest city" title.
- **2010** — First metro line opens.
- **2016** — Liaozhong County incorporated as a new district.

## Body

### Historical Significance
Shenyang's history includes early Neolithic settlement (Xinle culture). It was a key Ming dynasty military stronghold before being captured by the Jurchens in 1625, who made it their capital. After the Qing dynasty moved its capital to Beijing in 1644, Shenyang remained a secondary capital and spiritual home. In the 20th century, the city became a focal point for Russian and Japanese imperial ambitions, culminating in the 1931 Mukden Incident and subsequent Japanese occupation. It was a major battleground in the Chinese Civil War, captured by Communist forces in 1948.

### Economy and Industry
Shenyang is a core industrial center in China, historically focused on heavy industry like aerospace, machine tools, and defense. During the 1950s First Five-Year Plan, many factories were built in the Tiexi District. While the city experienced decline as a "rust belt" area after the 1980s, it has since revived through the central government's "Revitalize Northeast China" campaign. Key modern industries include software, automotive manufacturing, and electronics. The city hosts major companies like Brilliance Auto and Shenyang Aircraft Corporation.

### Urban Structure and Transportation
The city's administrative area includes 10 districts, one county-level city (Xinmin), and two counties. The old city was defined by inner and outer walls, most of which were demolished after 1949. Shenyang is the transportation hub of Northeast China, served by Taoxian International Airport, an extensive railway network including high-speed lines, and a growing metro system. The urban layout features a grid pattern with several ring roads.

### Culture and Education
Shenyang is a center for scientific research and education, home to universities like Northeastern University and Liaoning University. It is recognized for its scientific output and hosts numerous research institutes. The city's culture features Northeastern Chinese cuisine, folk dances like Errenzhuan, and a strong comedy tradition. Major tourist attractions include the UNESCO-listed Mukden Palace and the tombs of early Qing emperors.

## Terms
- **Mukden:** The historical Manchu name for Shenyang, used internationally until the mid-20th century.
- **Later Jin:** The Jurchen dynasty founded by Nurhaci, predecessor to the Qing dynasty.
- **Manchukuo:** The Japanese puppet state in Manchuria (1932–1945).
- **Liaoshen Campaign:** The 1948 Communist military offensive that captured Shenyang.
- **Rust Belt:** A term for regions experiencing industrial decline, applicable to Shenyang post-1980s.
- **Xiagang:** The term for the mass layoffs from state-owned factories during China's economic reforms.
- **Northeast Area Revitalization Plan:** A central government initiative to rejuvenate the economy of Northeast China.
- **Double First-Class Construction:** A Chinese government initiative to develop world-class universities and disciplines.

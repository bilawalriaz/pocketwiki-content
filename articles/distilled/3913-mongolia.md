# Mongolia

## Overview
Mongolia is a landlocked country in East Asia, bordered by Russia and China. It is the world's most sparsely populated sovereign state, with a population of about 3.5 million. Historically the heartland of nomadic empires, most famously the Mongol Empire founded by Genghis Khan in 1206, Mongolia transitioned from a Soviet satellite state to a multi-party democracy in 1990. Its modern identity blends a deep nomadic heritage with a growing, mining-driven economy.

## Timeline
- **209 BC** — The Xiongnu confederation is formed, the first major nomadic empire in the region.
- **1206** — Temüjin unites the Mongol tribes and takes the title Genghis Khan, founding the Mongol Empire.
- **1271** — Kublai Khan, grandson of Genghis Khan, establishes the Yuan dynasty in China.
- **1368** — The Yuan dynasty is overthrown by the Ming dynasty; Mongols retreat to their homeland.
- **1578** — Altan Khan meets the Dalai Lama, leading to the widespread adoption of Tibetan Buddhism in Mongolia.
- **1691** — The Khalkha Mongols submit to the Qing dynasty, bringing Mongolia under Manchu rule.
- **1911** — Following the collapse of the Qing dynasty, Mongolia declares independence.
- **1921** — With Soviet support, Mongolia re-establishes independence and becomes a satellite state of the Soviet Union.
- **1924** — The Mongolian People's Republic is founded as a socialist state.
- **1990** — A peaceful democratic revolution leads to the end of one-party rule.
- **1992** — A new constitution is adopted, establishing a multi-party system and a market economy.
- **1997** — Mongolia joins the World Trade Organization (WTO).

## Body

### Early History and Empires
The territory of modern Mongolia has been inhabited since the Paleolithic era. The region's history is defined by a succession of nomadic empires, including the Xiongnu, Xianbei, Rouran, and Turkic Khaganates. These states shared common institutions like the office of the Khan and a decimal military system. In 1206, Temüjin united the Mongol tribes and became Genghis Khan, creating the Mongol Empire—the largest contiguous land empire in history. After the Yuan dynasty (founded by his grandson Kublai Khan) fell in 1368, the Mongols retreated and experienced centuries of internal conflict.

### Buddhism and Qing Rule
In the 16th century, Tibetan Buddhism spread to Mongolia, profoundly shaping its culture. By the early 20th century, nearly one-third of adult males were monks. In the 17th century, the Qing dynasty of China absorbed Mongolia. The Qing maintained control through alliances, intermarriage, and a feudal system, while largely preserving Mongolian culture by restricting Chinese immigration.

### Independence and the Socialist Era
After the Qing dynasty collapsed in 1911, Mongolia declared independence. Following a brief period of turmoil involving Chinese and White Russian forces, the country became a Soviet satellite state in 1921. The Mongolian People's Republic was established in 1924. Under the socialist regime, particularly during the leadership of Khorloogiin Choibalsan, the country underwent forced collectivization and Stalinist purges. In the late 1930s, the regime destroyed most Buddhist monasteries and killed tens of thousands, including about 17,000 monks. Mongolia allied with the Soviet Union against Japan in 1939 and 1945. Its independence was internationally recognized after a 1945 referendum and it joined the United Nations in 1961.

### Democracy and Modern Economy
The dissolution of the Soviet Union prompted Mongolia's peaceful democratic revolution in 1990. A new constitution in 1992 established a multi-party democracy and a market economy. The transition was difficult, marked by inflation and shortages. Today, Mongolia's economy is heavily dependent on mining, with minerals representing over 80% of exports. It is a major producer of copper, gold, and coal, with the Oyu Tolgoi mine being a key project. The country maintains a "third neighbor" foreign policy to build ties beyond Russia and China.

## Terms
*   **Nomadic:** A lifestyle of people who do not settle permanently in one location, often moving with livestock for pasture.
*   **Khan:** A title for a sovereign or military ruler, used by Mongol and Turkic leaders.
*   **Kurultai:** A traditional Mongol and Turkic political council or assembly of nobles.
*   **Ger:** The traditional portable, round tent dwelling of Mongolian nomads, also known as a yurt.
*   **Zud:** A harsh Mongolian winter climate event where livestock die from extreme cold and starvation.
*   **Aimag:** A province, the primary administrative division of Mongolia.
*   **Naadam:** The main national festival, featuring the "Three Manly Games" of wrestling, horse racing, and archery.
*   **Morin khuur:** A traditional Mongolian horsehead fiddle, a two-stringed instrument.
*   **Soyombo:** A symbol of Mongolian independence and freedom, appearing on the national flag.

## Debates and open questions
*   The etymology of the word "Mongol" is uncertain, with scholarly proposals linking it to the founder of the Rouran Khaganate or other historical terms.
*   The ethnic origins and classification of the ancient Xiongnu empire, the first major nomadic state in the region, remain debated.
*   The economic and political challenges of balancing Mongolia's "third neighbor" policy with its deep economic dependence on China and strategic location between Russia and China are ongoing.

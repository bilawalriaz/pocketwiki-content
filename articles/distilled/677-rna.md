# RNA

## Overview
Ribonucleic acid (RNA) is a polymeric nucleic acid essential for all known life, functioning both as a carrier of genetic information (messenger RNA) and as a functional molecule that catalyzes reactions, regulates genes, and builds proteins (non-coding RNA). Unlike DNA, RNA contains a ribose sugar with a 2'-hydroxyl group and uses uracil instead of thymine, making it less stable but structurally versatile enough to fold into complex, protein-like shapes that enable catalysis (ribozymes). The "RNA world" hypothesis posits that early life relied on RNA for both genetic storage and catalysis before the evolution of DNA and protein enzymes. Modern biology retains this legacy in the ribosome—a ribozyme that synthesizes proteins—and in diverse regulatory RNAs that control gene expression.

## Body

### Chemical Structure and Distinction from DNA
RNA is a chain of nucleotides, each comprising a ribose sugar (carbons 1'–5'), a phosphate group linking the 3' and 5' carbons of adjacent sugars, and a nitrogenous base (adenine, guanine, cytosine, uracil). The phosphate backbone confers a negative charge (polyanion), requiring metal ions like Mg²⁺ for structural stability. Three key features distinguish RNA from DNA: (1) the 2'-hydroxyl group on ribose forces the A-form helix geometry (deep, narrow major groove; shallow, wide minor groove) and enables backbone cleavage in flexible regions; (2) uracil replaces thymine; (3) RNA folds into collections of short, packed helices rather than long double helices, achieving protein-like tertiary structures (hairpins, bulges, pseudoknots) that permit catalysis. The four-base alphabet is the minimum required to generate all necessary structural folds. Chemical modifications (e.g., pseudouridine, inosine, 2'-O-methylation) expand functional diversity; over 100 modified nucleosides exist, concentrated in functional regions of tRNA and rRNA.

### Types and Functions of RNA
**Protein synthesis:** Messenger RNA (mRNA) carries codons (three-nucleotide units) from DNA to ribosomes. In eukaryotes, pre-mRNA is processed (capped, polyadenylated, spliced) before export to cytoplasm; in prokaryotes, translation couples to transcription. Transfer RNA (tRNA, ~80 nt) delivers amino acids via anticodon-codon pairing. Ribosomal RNA (rRNA) forms the catalytic core of ribosomes (peptidyl transferase center); eukaryotes use 18S, 5.8S, 28S, and 5S rRNA. Transfer-messenger RNA (tmRNA) rescues stalled ribosomes in bacteria.

**Regulatory RNA (eukaryotes):** MicroRNAs (miRNAs) and small interfering RNAs (siRNAs) guide the RNA-induced silencing complex (RISC) to degrade or block translation of target mRNAs. Long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs, >200 nt) such as *Xist* recruit chromatin-modifying complexes (e.g., Polycomb) to silence chromosomal domains. Enhancer RNAs (eRNAs), transcribed from enhancer DNA elements, up-regulate target gene transcription.

**Prokaryotic regulatory RNA:** Bacterial small RNAs (sRNAs) typically base-pair antisense with mRNAs to down-regulate translation or stability, often mediating rapid stress responses. Riboswitches are *cis*-acting RNA elements that bind metabolites to allosterically control gene expression. CRISPR RNAs guide defense against viral invaders.

**Other classes:** Double-stranded RNA (dsRNA) forms genomes of some viruses and triggers immune responses (interferon, RNAi). Circular RNAs (circRNAs) arise from back-splicing; functions largely unknown, though some act as miRNA sponges. Viroids are infectious, non-coding RNA pathogens replicated by host polymerases.

### Synthesis, Processing, and Genetic Roles
RNA synthesis (transcription) is catalyzed by DNA-dependent RNA polymerase, reading the template strand 3'→5' to synthesize RNA 5'→3'. Initiation requires promoter binding; termination is sequence-encoded. RNA-dependent RNA polymerases replicate RNA virus genomes and function in RNAi pathways. Post-transcriptional processing includes 5' capping, 3' polyadenylation, and spliceosomal intron removal (mediated by snRNAs). Nucleotide modifications are guided by snoRNAs (60–300 nt) which base-pair target RNAs to recruit modifying enzymes.

RNA serves as genetic material in RNA viruses (replicated by viral polymerases) and viroids. Reverse transcribing viruses (retroviruses) and retrotransposons use reverse transcriptase to convert RNA into DNA, which integrates into host genomes. Telomerase, a ribonucleoprotein, uses an RNA template to synthesize chromosome ends.

### Medical Applications
RNA therapeutics overcame instability via chemical modifications (e.g., pseudouridine) and delivery systems. mRNA vaccines (COVID-19) deliver in vitro transcribed mRNA to produce antigenic proteins, offering rapid development versus traditional pathogen-based vaccines. Small interfering RNAs (siRNAs) silence disease genes; antisense oligonucleotides modulate splicing (e.g., nusinersen). Ribavirin, branaplam, and ataluren target RNA structures or splicing. IVT-mRNA shows promise for protein replacement therapies (bone, heart, pluripotency). Small molecules targeting RNA structures remain an emerging field.

## Terms
- ****Ribozyme**** — An RNA molecule with catalytic activity (e.g., ribosomal peptidyl transferase center, self-splicing introns).
- ****Polyanion**** — A polymer with multiple negative charges; RNA's phosphate backbone makes it a polyanion requiring cations (Mg²⁺) for folding.
- ****A-form helix**** — Helical geometry adopted by RNA (and DNA-RNA hybrids) due to the 2'-OH group; deep major groove, shallow minor groove.
- ****Wobble base pair**** — Non-Watson-Crick pairing (e.g., G–U) allowing tRNA anticodons to recognize multiple codons; inosine (deaminated adenine) expands wobble capacity.
- ****RNA interference (RNAi)**** — Post-transcriptional gene silencing pathway where small RNAs (miRNA/siRNA) guide RISC to degrade or block target mRNA.
- ****Long non-coding RNA (lncRNA)**** — RNA >200 nt with no protein-coding potential; functions in chromatin regulation, scaffolding, and transcriptional control (e.g., *Xist*).
- ****Riboswitch**** — *Cis*-regulatory RNA element in mRNA 5' UTR that binds a metabolite to allosterically control transcription, splicing, or translation.
- ****Reverse transcriptase**** — Enzyme that synthesizes DNA from an RNA template; used by retroviruses, retrotransposons, and telomerase.
- ****Circular RNA (circRNA)**** — Covalently closed single-stranded RNA formed by back-splicing (joining upstream 3' acceptor to downstream 5' donor); resistant to exonucleases.
- ****RNA world**** — Hypothesis that early life used RNA for both genetic information storage and catalysis before DNA and proteins evolved.

## Debates and open questions
- **Function of circRNAs:** Most circRNAs lack known functions; miRNA sponging is demonstrated for few examples.
- **Roles of RNA modifications:** Over 100 modifications exist, but specific functional roles for many remain unclear, though enrichment in ribosomal functional centers implies importance.
- **Origin of RNA world:** Mechanisms for prebiotic synthesis of activated nucleotides and polymerization remain under investigation; basalt glass and meteoritic chemistry are candidate pathways.
- **Small molecule targeting of RNA:** Drug development targeting RNA structures is nascent; few approved drugs exist despite therapeutic potential.
- **Classification of enhancer RNAs:** Unclear if eRNAs constitute a distinct class or a subset of lncRNAs.
- **L-RNA stability:** Synthetic L-RNA (mirror-image) resists RNase degradation, but biological relevance and therapeutic utility are unexplored in the source.

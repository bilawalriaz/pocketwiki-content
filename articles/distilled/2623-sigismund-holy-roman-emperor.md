# Sigismund, Holy Roman Emperor

## Overview
Sigismund of Luxembourg (1368–1437) was a pivotal late medieval ruler who united the crowns of Hungary, Germany, and Bohemia, and was crowned Holy Roman Emperor. His reign was defined by ambitious attempts to reform the Empire and Church, combat the Ottoman Empire, and manage the religious crisis of the Hussite Wars, though his vision was ultimately constrained by financial limits and political opposition.

## Timeline
- **1368** — Born, son of Emperor Charles IV.
- **1387** — Crowned King of Hungary after marrying Mary.
- **1396** — Led the disastrous Crusade of Nicopolis against the Ottomans.
- **1410** — Elected King of the Romans (Germany).
- **1414–1418** — Presided over the Council of Constance, ending the Western Schism.
- **1419** — Became King of Bohemia after Wenceslaus IV's death.
- **1420s** — Fought the Hussite Wars in Bohemia.
- **1433** — Crowned Holy Roman Emperor in Rome.
- **1437** — Died, leaving his domains to his son-in-law Albert II.

## Body

### King of Hungary
Sigismund's rule in Hungary began through his marriage to Queen Mary in 1385. He faced immediate challenges, including a rebellion by the House of Horvat and a prolonged struggle to secure his throne, which required pledging lands and forming alliances with powerful nobles like the Czillei-Garai League. After Mary's death in 1395, he became sole ruler. His authority was further tested by the defeat at the Crusade of Nicopolis (1396) and rival claims from Ladislaus of Naples. To govern during his frequent absences, he instituted the office of Palatine and introduced the *Placetum Regium* (1404), requiring royal consent for papal bulls in Hungary.

### Military Campaigns and the Order of the Dragon
Following the Nicopolis disaster, Sigismund focused on securing his borders. He formed a defensive alliance with Despot Stefan Lazarević of Serbia and personally led a large campaign against Bosnia, culminating in the Battle of Dobor (1408). After this victory, he founded the Order of the Dragon, a chivalric order dedicated to fighting the Ottoman Empire. Its members were key political allies, including Stefan Lazarević and Stibor of Stiboricz, Sigismund's most trusted advisor.

### King of the Romans and Imperial Politics
Elected King of the Romans in 1410, Sigismund's rule in Germany lacked a strong domestic power base (*Hausmacht*). He relied on associative politics and strategic alliances, notably with Frederick I of Brandenburg and Albert II of Austria. His major diplomatic achievement was organizing the Council of Constance (1414–1418), which resolved the Western Schism but also led to the execution of Jan Hus, fueling the Hussite rebellion in Bohemia. He later allied with England against France and awarded Brandenburg to the Hohenzollern family in 1415.

### The Hussite Wars and Final Years
After 1419, Sigismund's claim to the Bohemian throne was resisted by Hussites who saw him as Hus's betrayer. Multiple military campaigns against them failed, draining his resources. He was finally recognized as King of Bohemia in 1436, but with little real power. He was crowned Holy Roman Emperor in 1433. His later years saw further unsuccessful campaigns against the Ottomans. He died in 1437, having secured the succession through his daughter Elizabeth's marriage to Albert II of Austria, ending the Luxembourg male line.

## Terms
- **Jure uxoris**: Latin for "by right of his wife," denoting a king who rules through his marriage to a reigning queen.
- **King of the Romans**: The title for the elected monarch of the Holy Roman Empire before being crowned Emperor by the Pope.
- **Hausmacht**: German for "domestic power," referring to a ruler's personal hereditary lands and resources within the Empire.
- **Placetum Regium**: A decree by Sigismund requiring royal approval for papal bulls to be published in Hungary.
- **Order of the Dragon**: A chivalric order founded by Sigismund to unite nobles against the Ottoman Empire.
- **Council of Constance**: A major ecumenical council (1414–1418) that resolved the Western Schism and condemned Jan Hus.
- **Hussite Wars**: A series of religious wars in Bohemia sparked by the execution of reformer Jan Hus.
- **Palatine**: The chief administrative officer in Hungary, governing in the king's absence.

## Debates and Open Questions
- Sigismund's personal responsibility for the death of Jan Hus is debated. He had granted Hus safe conduct and protested his imprisonment, but Hus was executed by a Church court during Sigismund's absence.
- Recent scholarship, especially from East-Central Europe, re-evaluates his reign, viewing setbacks like the Hussite Wars as results of severe financial and political constraints rather than personal failure.
- The extent to which Sigismund's diplomatic maneuvering, particularly through allies like Stibor of Stiboricz, was a consistent strategy versus opportunistic alliance-building is analyzed.

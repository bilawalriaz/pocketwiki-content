# Ptolemy

## Overview
Claudius Ptolemy (c. 100 – 160s/170s AD) was a Greco-Roman scholar in Alexandria whose works became foundational texts for Byzantine, Islamic, and Western European science. He authored influential treatises in astronomy, geography, astrology, and music theory, most notably the *Almagest*, which presented a comprehensive geocentric model of the universe that dominated scientific thought for over a millennium.

- **12th century** — *Almagest* translated into Latin twice (in Sicily and Spain).
- **1977** — Robert R. Newton publishes *The Crime of Claudius Ptolemy*, alleging data fraud.
- **2022** — Discovery of Hipparchus's star catalog fragments challenges earlier accusations about Ptolemy's catalog.

## Body

### Astronomical Works
Ptolemy's most significant work is the *Almagest*, a comprehensive astronomical treatise. It merged Babylonian arithmetical techniques with Greek geometric models, building on the work of Hipparchus. The *Almagest* presented geocentric planetary models, a star catalogue of 48 constellations, and tables for computing celestial positions. It was the authoritative astronomical text in Europe, the Middle East, and North Africa for over a thousand years. Modern scholarship has identified patterns of errors in its observations, leading to debates about whether Ptolemy fabricated data to fit his theories. However, the 2022 discovery of fragments from Hipparchus's star catalog suggests Ptolemy combined multiple sources, including his own observations.

Other astronomical works include the *Handy Tables*, a set of computational tables for astronomical calculations; the *Planetary Hypotheses*, which described a physical model of nested spheres and estimated the universe's dimensions; and shorter treatises like the *Analemma* and *Planisphaerium*.

### Geography
Ptolemy's *Geography* is a handbook on map-making using geographical coordinates. It compiled a catalogue of about 8,000 localities from earlier sources like Marinus of Tyre, assigning coordinates to roughly 6,300 of them. Latitude was expressed as *climata* (length of the longest day) rather than degrees. The work provided instructions for mapping the known world (*oikoumenē*), which Ptolemy understood covered only about a quarter of the globe. The topographical tables likely evolved over centuries with new information.

### Astrology and Other Sciences
The *Tetrabiblos* ("Four Books") is a systematic astrological treatise that attempted to rationalize astrology within Aristotelian natural philosophy. It explained planetary influences through qualities like heating and cooling and was highly authoritative for over a millennium. A related work, the *Centiloquium*, is now considered pseudepigraphical.

Ptolemy's *Harmonics* is a treatise on music theory that argued for basing musical scales on mathematical ratios verified by experiment with a monochord. His *Optics*, surviving in a Latin translation, studied properties of sight, including reflection and refraction, and included an early table of refraction values.

### Philosophy
In his essay *On the Criterion and Hegemonikon*, Ptolemy discussed how humans obtain knowledge, arguing that truth is found by using both reason and sense perception. He also asserted the superiority of mathematical knowledge over theology or metaphysics for achieving certainty.

## Terms
- **Geocentric model**: A model of the universe with Earth at the center, which Ptolemy's *Almagest* mathematically formalized.
- **Almagest**: Ptolemy's masterwork on astronomy, originally titled *Mathēmatikē Syntaxis*.
- **Epicycle**: A small circle whose center moves around the circumference of a larger circle; used in Ptolemy's planetary models.
- **Geography**: Ptolemy's treatise on cartography and geographical coordinates.
- **Tetrabiblos**: Ptolemy's four-part astrological treatise, also known as *Quadripartitum*.
- **Climata**: In Ptolemy's *Geography*, a measure of latitude based on the length of the longest day of the year.
- **Monochord**: An experimental musical apparatus with a single string used to measure pitch ratios.
- **Oikoumenē**: The known, inhabited world as understood by ancient geographers.
- **Pseudepigraphical**: A work falsely attributed to a famous author, like the *Centiloquium* attributed to Ptolemy.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Data Integrity**: A major debate concerns whether Ptolemy fabricated astronomical observations. Robert R. Newton alleged systematic fraud, while others like Owen Gingerich and Bernard Goldstein argue the errors may stem from other factors like instrument warping or atmospheric refraction. The 2022 discovery of Hipparchus's star catalog fragments suggests Ptolemy used multiple sources, complicating the fraud accusation.
- **Authorship**: The identity of the actual author of the *Centiloquium*, attributed to Ptolemy, remains unknown.
- **Geographical Evolution**: The topographical tables in the *Geography* are believed to be cumulative texts altered over centuries, meaning information within the work is of different dates and contains scribal errors.

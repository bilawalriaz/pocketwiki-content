# Ecuador

## Overview
Ecuador is a South American country straddling the equator, known for its immense biodiversity, including the Galápagos Islands. Its history is marked by indigenous civilizations, Spanish colonization, a turbulent path to independence, and long-standing territorial disputes with its neighbors. Today, it is a developing, plurinational republic whose economy relies heavily on oil and agricultural exports, and it faces significant challenges with poverty, inequality, and security.

## Timeline
- **c. 16,500–13,000 years ago** — Paleo-Indians first disperse into the Americas, reaching the area of modern Ecuador.
- **15th century** — The Inca Empire gradually incorporates the highland regions of Ecuador.
- **1563** — Quito becomes the seat of a Spanish *real audiencia*.
- **10 August 1809** — Quito's criollos declare a junta for independence from Spain, the first in Spanish America.
- **9 October 1820** — Guayaquil declares independence from Spain.
- **24 May 1822** — Independence is secured after the Battle of Pichincha; Ecuador joins Gran Colombia.
- **13 May 1830** — Ecuador separates from Gran Colombia to become an independent republic.
- **1895** — The Liberal Revolution under Eloy Alfaro reduces the power of the clergy and conservative elites.
- **1941** — The Ecuadorian–Peruvian War ends with the Rio Protocol, awarding disputed territory to Peru.
- **13 April 2000** — Ecuador adopts the U.S. dollar as its official currency.
- **2008** — A new constitution recognizes the legal rights of nature.
- **26 October 1998** — The Brasilia Presidential Act ends the long-running territorial dispute with Peru.

## Body

### Pre-Inca and Inca Eras
Before the Inca arrival, diverse indigenous groups settled in the coastal, highland, and Amazonian regions, developing distinct cultures like the Valdivia, Quitus, and Cañari. The Inca Empire conquered the developed highland confederations over two generations in the 15th century, but coastal and Amazonian peoples largely resisted domination. A civil war between Atahualpa and Huáscar, triggered by the death of Emperor Huayna Capac from a European disease, weakened the empire just before the Spanish arrival.

### Spanish Colonization and Independence
Spanish colonization brought devastating diseases and forced labor systems. Quito became a key administrative center. The first major push for independence came from Quito in 1809. Full independence was achieved in 1822, and the territory joined Simón Bolívar's Gran Colombia. Ecuador separated from Gran Colombia in 1830. The 19th century was politically unstable, marked by rapid changes in leadership and the unifying influence of conservative Gabriel García Moreno. The Liberal Revolution of 1895 initiated a period of secular reform.

### Territorial Disputes and Modern Conflicts
Ecuador's borders were contested for much of its history, leading to significant territorial losses to Colombia, Brazil, and Peru. The most persistent dispute was with Peru, culminating in the 1941 war and the Cenepa War of 1995. The 1998 Brasilia Presidential Act finally settled the border. In the 21st century, political volatility continued, including the dollarization of the economy in 2000, the leftist reforms of Rafael Correa (2007-2017), and recent challenges with organized crime, including a 2024 declaration of an "internal armed conflict."

### Geography and Biodiversity
Ecuador is divided into four distinct regions: the coastal lowlands (La Costa), the Andean highlands (La Sierra), the Amazon rainforest (La Amazonía), and the Galápagos Islands. It is one of the world's 17 megadiverse countries, hosting a vast number of endemic species. The 2008 constitution was the first globally to recognize the rights of nature. However, ecosystems face threats from climate change, oil exploitation, and pollution.

### Government and Economy
Ecuador is a presidential republic with five branches of government. Its economy is classified as upper-middle-income and is highly dependent on petroleum and agricultural exports like bananas and flowers. The adoption of the U.S. dollar stabilized the economy but also tied it to U.S. monetary policy. Poverty and inequality have decreased in recent decades but remain significant issues.

## Terms
- **Mestizo**: A person of mixed European and Indigenous American ancestry; the largest ethnic group in Ecuador.
- **Criollo (Criollo)**: A person of full Spanish descent born in the Americas; a key group in early independence movements.
- **Real Audiencia**: A judicial district established by the Spanish crown in the Americas, also holding administrative powers.
- **Plurinational**: A state that recognizes the existence and rights of multiple indigenous nations within its borders.
- **Megadiverse country**: A country that harbors the majority of Earth's species and a high number of endemic species.
- **Encomienda**: A Spanish colonial labor system where colonists were granted the right to the labor of a group of indigenous people.
- **OPEC**: The Organization of the Petroleum Exporting Countries, which Ecuador joined in 1973 and left in 2020.
- **Cuy**: Guinea pig, a traditional food source in the Andean highlands.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The cause of President Jaime Roldós's 1981 plane crash is officially an accident, but many believe he was assassinated due to his reformist agenda.
- The long-term social and economic impacts of adopting the U.S. dollar remain a subject of debate.
- The effectiveness of the 2008 constitution's "rights of nature" in balancing environmental protection with economic development is continually tested.
- The government's strategy for combating powerful organized crime groups and reducing prison violence is a critical ongoing challenge.

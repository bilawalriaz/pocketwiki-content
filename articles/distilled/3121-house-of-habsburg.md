# House of Habsburg

## Overview
The House of Habsburg was one of the most powerful dynasties in Western history, ruling vast European and American realms from the Middle Ages until the 20th century. They held the Holy Roman Imperial throne almost continuously from 1440 to 1806 and controlled major kingdoms including Spain, Austria, Hungary, and Bohemia. Their power was built through strategic marriages and territorial expansion, but their influence waned due to political fragmentation, military defeats, and the eventual dissolution of their empires after World War I.

## Timeline
- **c. 1020s** — Habsburg Castle built by Radbot of Klettgau in present-day Switzerland.
- **1273** — Rudolph I elected King of the Romans.
- **1278** — Rudolph I defeats Ottokar II of Bohemia at the Battle on the Marchfeld, securing Austrian lands.
- **1379** — Treaty of Neuberg splits the dynasty into the Albertinian and Leopoldian lines.
- **1452** — Frederick III crowned Holy Roman Emperor, beginning near-continuous Habsburg control of the title.
- **1556** — Abdication of Charles V splits the dynasty into Spanish and Austrian branches.
- **1700** — Death of Charles II of Spain ends the Spanish Habsburg line, triggering the War of the Spanish Succession.
- **1740** — Death of Emperor Charles VI ends the direct Habsburg male line, leading to the War of the Austrian Succession.
- **1806** — Holy Roman Empire dissolved under pressure from Napoleon.
- **1867** — Austro-Hungarian Compromise creates the dual monarchy of Austria-Hungary.
- **1918** — Emperor Charles I renounces participation in state affairs, ending Habsburg rule.

## Body

### Origins and Rise to Power
The dynasty takes its name from Habsburg Castle, built in the 1020s. The family's political breakthrough came in 1273 when Rudolph I was elected King of the Romans. He consolidated power by defeating Ottokar II of Bohemia in 1278, acquiring the duchies of Austria and Styria for his family and moving their power base to Vienna. The Habsburgs expanded through arranged marriages and by inheriting titles from extinct noble families.

### Imperial Dominance and Territorial Expansion
From 1440 until 1740, the Holy Roman Imperial throne was continuously held by a Habsburg. The family split into Albertinian and Leopoldian lines in 1379 but was reunified under Maximilian I in 1490. Maximilian's marriage to Mary of Burgundy brought the wealthy Burgundian Netherlands into Habsburg control. His grandson, Charles V, inherited Spain, its colonies, and the Austrian lands, representing the dynasty's peak. After Charles V abdicated in 1556, the dynasty formally split into Spanish and Austrian branches, though they maintained close relations.

### Decline and End of Rule
The Spanish Habsburg line ended in 1700 with the death of the infertile Charles II. The direct Austrian male line ended in 1740 with Emperor Charles VI. His daughter, Maria Theresa, secured the succession through the War of the Austrian Succession and founded the House of Habsburg-Lorraine by marrying Francis Stephen of Lorraine. This new house ruled until the Holy Roman Empire's dissolution in 1806 and the Austrian Empire's collapse in 1918 following defeat in World War I. The last emperor, Charles I, renounced his role in November 1918.

### Legacy and Culture
The Habsburgs were known for frequent consanguineous marriages, which may have contributed to genetic health issues like mandibular prognathism ("Habsburg jaw") and the eventual extinction of their male lines. They developed a tradition of multilingualism to rule their diverse domains. After their deposition, the family opposed Nazism and Communism, with Otto von Habsburg playing a role in the fall of the Iron Curtain. The current head of the family is Karl von Habsburg.

## Terms
- **King of the Romans**: The title given to the elected ruler of the Holy Roman Empire before being crowned Emperor by the Pope.
- **Archduke**: A title of nobility above duke, officially recognized for Habsburg rulers of Austria in 1453.
- **Pragmatic Sanction**: A 1549 edict by Charles V that combined the Habsburg Netherlands into one indivisible unit for inheritance.
- **Consanguineous marriage**: Marriage between close relatives, practiced extensively by the Habsburgs to consolidate power.
- **Mandibular prognathism**: A facial deformity involving an extended lower jaw, colloquially called the "Habsburg jaw."
- **Habsburg-Lorraine**: The cadet branch of the dynasty formed by the 1736 marriage of Maria Theresa to Francis Stephen of Lorraine.
- **Austro-Hungarian Compromise of 1867**: The agreement that created the dual monarchy, granting Hungary co-equality with Austria.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The origin of the name "Habsburg" is uncertain, with theories deriving it from "hawk castle" (Habichtsburg) or a word for "ford."
- The extent to which inbreeding caused the extinction of the Habsburg male lines is debated; some studies find a correlation with health issues, while others dispute a link to fertility.
- The role of Habsburg multilingualism in governing their diverse empire is a subject of historical analysis.

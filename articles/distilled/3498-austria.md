# Austria

## Overview
Austria is a landlocked federal republic in Central Europe, historically a major imperial power and the heartland of the Habsburg monarchy. Its modern identity is shaped by its role in the Holy Roman Empire, its defeat in World War I, annexation by Nazi Germany, and post-war independence with a constitutionally enshrined neutrality.

## Body

### Early History and the Habsburg Rise
The area has been inhabited since the Paleolithic period. It was part of the Roman provinces of Noricum, Pannonia, and Raetia. After the Roman Empire's fall, the region was invaded by various Germanic tribes and later conquered by Charlemagne in 788. The core of modern Austria was given to Leopold I of the House of Babenberg in 976. The name "Austria" first appeared in 996 as *Ostarrîchi*. The Babenberg line ended in 1246, and after a brief period under Ottokar II of Bohemia, the Habsburg dynasty gained control following the Battle of Dürnkrut in 1278. The Habsburgs accumulated vast territories through marriage and inheritance, becoming Holy Roman Emperors from 1438 onward (with one exception).

### Empire, Dual Monarchy, and World War I
From the 16th century, Vienna served as the Holy Roman Empire's administrative capital. After defeating the Ottomans, Austria became a great power. The Holy Roman Empire was dissolved in 1806, two years after the Empire of Austria was established. Following defeat by Prussia in 1866, Austria left German politics. The Austro-Hungarian Compromise of 1867 created a dual monarchy under Franz Joseph I. The assassination of his heir, Archduke Franz Ferdinand, in 1914 triggered World War I. The empire's defeat led to its dissolution and the proclamation of the Republic of German-Austria in 1918.

### Interwar Period, Nazi Rule, and World War II
The First Austrian Republic faced economic crisis and political instability. In 1933, Chancellor Engelbert Dollfuss established an Austrofascist dictatorship. In 1938, Nazi Germany annexed Austria (the *Anschluss*), abolishing its independence. Austrians were disproportionately represented in the Nazi leadership and SS. The country was renamed the "Ostmark." After liberation in 1945, Austria was divided into Allied occupation zones.

### The Second Republic and Neutrality
A provisional government declared independence from Germany in 1945. After a decade of occupation, the Austrian State Treaty of 1955 restored full sovereignty. Austria then declared its perpetual neutrality. The political system is a semi-presidential representative democracy. Since 1945, governance has been dominated by grand coalitions between the Social Democratic Party (SPÖ) and the Austrian People's Party (ÖVP). Austria joined the EU in 1995 and adopted the euro in 1999.

## Terms
- **Anschluss**: The 1938 annexation of Austria into Nazi Germany.
- **Habsburg**: The European royal dynasty that ruled Austria and the Holy Roman Empire for centuries.
- **Holy Roman Empire**: A multi-ethnic complex of territories in Western and Central Europe that existed from the Early Middle Ages until 1806.
- **Ostarrîchi**: The Old High German term meaning "eastern realm," the origin of the name Austria.
- **Proporz**: The Austrian political system of proportionally splitting key posts and resources between major parties.
- **Austrofascism**: The authoritarian regime established in Austria from 1933 to 1938 under Engelbert Dollfuss.
- **Dual Monarchy**: The 1867 compromise that created the Austro-Hungarian Empire, uniting the Austrian Empire and the Kingdom of Hungary under one monarch.
- **Bundesheer**: The Austrian Armed Forces.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The extent of popular support for the 1938 *Anschluss* remains debated, with evidence of both genuine enthusiasm and coercion.
- Austria's post-war identity has been shaped by the tension between its German cultural character and its historical role as a multi-ethnic imperial center.
- The country's policy of neutrality is periodically debated, especially regarding deeper integration with EU security policies or potential NATO membership.

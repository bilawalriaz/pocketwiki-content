# Tutankhamun

## Overview
Tutankhamun was an ancient Egyptian pharaoh of the Eighteenth Dynasty who ruled c. 1332–1323 BC. He is historically significant for reversing the religious revolution of his predecessor Akhenaten, restoring traditional polytheism, and for the near-intact discovery of his tomb in 1922, which provided unprecedented insight into royal burial practices and sparked global fascination with ancient Egypt.

## Timeline
- **c. 1342 BC** — Born as Tutankhaten during the reign of Akhenaten.
- **c. 1332 BC** — Becomes pharaoh at age 8 or 9, succeeding Akhenaten's successors.
- **Regnal Year 3** — Reverses Akhenaten's religious reforms; restores the god Amun.
- **Regnal Year 4** — Erects the Restoration Stela documenting his religious and building programs.
- **c. 1323 BC** — Dies at about age 18; buried in a small, adapted tomb in the Valley of the Kings.
- **c. 1323 BC** — Succeeded by the vizier Ay, who performs his funeral rites.
- **c. 1319 BC** — Ay is succeeded by Horemheb, who completes the religious restoration.
- **4 November 1922** — Howard Carter discovers Tutankhamun's tomb (KV62).
- **17 February 1923** — The tomb is officially unsealed.
- **2005** — CT scans of the mummy are conducted, leading to new medical theories.
- **2012** — A study suggests his death resulted from a combination of malaria and a leg fracture.
- **2025** — His treasures are relocated to the Grand Egyptian Museum.

## Body

### Family and Parentage
Tutankhamun's parentage is debated. DNA testing identifies his father as the mummy from tomb KV55 (likely Akhenaten or Smenkhkare) and his mother as "The Younger Lady" from tomb KV35, who genetic research indicates was a full sibling of his father—both children of Amenhotep III and Tiye. He married Ankhesenamun, one of Akhenaten's daughters. They had two daughters who were stillborn or died shortly after birth and were buried with him. His death ended the royal bloodline of the Eighteenth Dynasty.

### Reign and Religious Restoration
Tutankhamun became pharaoh as a child, so his advisors likely instigated the dramatic reversal of Akhenaten's policies. In his third regnal year, he ended the worship of the Aten, restored the god Amun to supremacy, and changed his and his queen's names to remove the "-aten" suffix. He moved the capital from Amarna back to Memphis and reestablished diplomatic relations. His reign was a major restoration period, documented on the Restoration Stela, involving the repair of temples and the enrichment of traditional cults. He initiated building projects, such as a sphinx avenue at Karnak, though many were unfinished at his death and later usurped by Horemheb.

### Military Campaigns and Deification
Tutankhamun's military campaigns in Nubia and the Near East were victorious. The extent of his personal participation is debated; his tomb contained extensive weaponry and art depicting him in battle, but some scholars question this due to his youth and speculated physical frailty. He was one of the few pharaohs worshipped as a deity during his lifetime, with temples dedicated to his deified form in Nubia and evidence of a cult structure with its own priesthood.

### Health, Death, and Succession
Tutankhamun's health is heavily debated. Confirmed afflictions include malaria and a compound left leg fracture that showed no signs of healing, suggesting it occurred shortly before his death. Other diagnoses, like a clubfoot, are disputed, with evidence suggesting his many walking sticks were ceremonial regalia. Theories of murder or a chariot accident have been disproven. He was succeeded by his vizier Ay, who had a short reign, and then by Horemheb, the army commander. Horemheb completed the religious restoration and initiated a damnatio memoriae against Amarna-period rulers, though he left Tutankhamun's tomb untouched.

### Tomb and Discovery
Tutankhamun was buried in a small, non-royal tomb (KV62) adapted for his use after his unexpected death. Robbed in antiquity, it was rediscovered by Howard Carter in 1922, nearly intact with over 5,000 artifacts, including a solid gold coffin and mask. The discovery caused a worldwide sensation. The "curse of the pharaohs" legend arose from the early death of his patron, Lord Carnarvon, but studies show no statistical anomaly in the lifespans of those who entered the tomb.

## Terms
- **Atenism:** The religious system established by Akhenaten, centered on the worship of the Aten as the sole god, which Tutankhamun reversed.
- **Restoration Stela:** A monument erected by Tutankhamun in his fourth regnal year, recording his restoration of traditional temples and cults after the Amarna Period.
- **Damnatio memoriae:** A practice where a successor ruler systematically erases the memory of a predecessor, as Horemheb did to Ay and earlier Amarna rulers.
- **Mortuary cult:** A system of worship and offerings maintained for a deceased pharaoh, often with its own temple and priesthood.
- **Valley of the Kings:** The burial site for pharaohs of the New Kingdom, located near Thebes, where Tutankhamun's tomb was found.
- **KV55:** A tomb in the Valley of the Kings containing a mummy identified through DNA as Tutankhamun's father, likely Akhenaten or Smenkhkare.
- **The Younger Lady:** An anonymous mummy from tomb KV35, identified by genetic research as Tutankhamun's mother and a full sibling of his father.
- **Iry-pat:** A title meaning "heir presumptive," held by Horemheb before he became pharaoh.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Parentage:** The exact identities of Tutankhamun's father (Akhenaten or Smenkhkare) and mother (The Younger Lady's specific identity) remain debated despite genetic data.
- **Cause of Death:** While malaria and a leg fracture are confirmed, their role in his death is uncertain, and other theories (e.g., chariot accident) have been disproven.
- **Military Participation:** Scholars debate whether Tutankhamun personally led campaigns, given his youth and contested physical condition.
- **Physical Appearance and Health:** Reconstructions of his face and body are controversial, and diagnoses like clubfoot are disputed, with evidence suggesting he was more able-bodied than once thought.
- **Genetic Affinities:** The interpretation of Tutankhamun's genetic data regarding his ancestral affinities (e.g., with sub-Saharan African or Eurasian populations) is an ongoing scholarly discussion.

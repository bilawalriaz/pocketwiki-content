# Philippine Sea

## Overview
The Philippine Sea is the world's largest marginal sea, located in the Western Pacific Ocean east of the Philippine Archipelago. It is a geologically active and biologically rich region, forming a critical part of the Coral Triangle, the global epicenter of marine biodiversity. Its strategic location has made it a site of significant historical events, including a major World War II naval battle.

## Timeline
- **c. 1965** — A U.S. A-4 Skyhawk carrying a nuclear bomb is lost in the Philippine Sea.
- **1521** — Ferdinand Magellan becomes the first European to navigate the sea, naming it "Mar Filipinas."
- **1944 (June 19-20)** — The Battle of the Philippine Sea, the largest carrier-to-carrier battle in history, occurs near the Mariana Islands.
- **1944 (December)** — Typhoon Cobra strikes the U.S. Pacific Third Fleet in the Philippine Sea.
- **2011** — Philippine government agencies begin using "West Philippine Sea" for parts of the South China Sea.

## Body

### Geography and Geology
The Philippine Sea is bounded by the Philippines and Taiwan to the west, Japan to the north, the Mariana Islands to the east, and Palau and Indonesia to the south. Its floor is formed by the Philippine Sea Plate, which subducts beneath the Philippine Mobile Belt, creating the Philippine Trench. The sea's complex undersea relief includes island arcs and deep-sea trenches, with the Mariana Trench containing the planet's deepest point.

### Biodiversity and Ecology
The Philippine Sea is a core part of the Coral Triangle, a region with the world's highest marine biodiversity. It hosts over 3,200 fish species, 486 coral species, and numerous endangered animals like whale sharks and dugongs. The Verde Island Passage is noted as the "center of the center" of marine fish biodiversity. This rich ecosystem supports the livelihoods of 120 million people but faces threats from climate change, which causes coral bleaching and ocean acidification, and from unsustainable fishing practices.

### History and Economy
Spanish explorer Ferdinand Magellan first navigated the sea in 1521. Its most famous historical event is the 1944 Battle of the Philippine Sea, a decisive U.S. victory that crippled the Japanese navy and air force. The sea is vital to the Philippine economy, providing food and income through fisheries. The Benham Rise (Philippine Rise) is a particularly rich fishing ground for migratory species like tuna. In 2011, the term "West Philippine Sea" was adopted for parts of the adjacent South China Sea.

## Terms
- **Marginal sea:** A sea partially enclosed by islands, archipelagos, or peninsulas.
- **Subduction:** A geological process where one tectonic plate sinks beneath another.
- **Island arc:** A chain of volcanic islands formed by the subduction of an oceanic plate beneath another plate.
- **Coral Triangle:** A marine area in the western Pacific Ocean with the highest biodiversity of coral reefs on Earth.
- **EEZ (Exclusive Economic Zone):** A maritime zone where a state has special rights regarding the exploration and use of marine resources.
- **Vicariance:** The geographical separation of a group of organisms by a physical barrier, leading to speciation.
- **Scleractinian coral:** Stony, reef-building corals.

## Debates and open questions
- The exact causal explanation for the exceptionally high biodiversity of the Coral Triangle is debated, though geological factors like plate tectonics are a leading theory.
- The naming of adjacent waters is politically sensitive; while the Philippine government uses "West Philippine Sea" for parts of the South China Sea, the Philippine Sea proper remains distinct.

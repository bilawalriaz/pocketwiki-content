# Digital art

## Overview
Digital art is artistic work that uses digital technology as part of its creation or presentation. It encompasses a wide range of practices, from computer-generated imagery to art made with digital tools, and has become a significant field in contemporary art and culture since the 1960s.

## Timeline
- **Early 1960s** — John Whitney develops the first computer-generated art using mathematical operations.
- **1963** — Ivan Sutherland invents Sketchpad, the first interactive computer-graphics interface.
- **1974-1977** — Salvador Dalí creates works based on a computer-processed portrait of Abraham Lincoln.
- **July 1985** — Andy Warhol creates digital art using an Amiga computer at the Lincoln Center.
- **2014** — Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) are designed, later used by artists to create AI art.
- **2020-2021** — Non-Fungible Tokens (NFTs) boom, becoming a common tool for digital art ownership.
- **March 2024** — Sotheby's presents an auction highlighting significant contributions of digital artists.

## Body

### History and Early Development
Digital art emerged in the early 1960s with pioneers like John Whitney, who used computers for mathematical art, and Ivan Sutherland, whose Sketchpad interface was foundational. Early notable works include Salvador Dalí's computer-processed portraits in the 1970s and Andy Warhol's Amiga-based art in 1985. The field has been known by various names, including computer art, electronic art, and new media art.

### Definitions and Categories
Digital art is broadly defined as work using digital technology in its creative or presentational process. A key distinction exists between "art made for digital media," which is highly computational and engages explicitly with digital technologies, and "art that uses digital tools," which incorporates digital technology in creation but may exist outside the digital world. Art historian Christiane Paul notes the difficulty in classifying all art using digital technologies as "digital art."

### Forms and Subtypes
Digital art includes numerous forms. **Digital painting** mimics traditional painting using software. **Artificial intelligence art** has been created since the 1960s, with modern examples using GANs and text-to-image generators. **Digital installation art** creates immersive environments, often site-specific and scalable. **Internet art** uses the specific characteristics of the Internet as its medium. **Computer demos** are non-interactive, procedurally generated audiovisual presentations that emerged with the home computer revolution. **Computer-generated imagery (CGI)** is used extensively in film and animation.

### Scholarship, Education, and Analysis
Digital art education utilizes hardware like graphics tablets and software for 2D/3D creation. Scholarship involves analyzing digitized collections using computational methods: **close reading** (analyzing a single piece's visual aspects) and **distant viewing** (statistically analyzing features across large collections). A major debate concerns digital copyright, especially for 3D scans of historical artifacts.

### Blockchain and NFTs
Since the 2020-2021 boom, blockchain technology, particularly Non-Fungible Tokens (NFTs), has been used to establish provable ownership of digital art. Despite criticism for plagiarism and fraud due to its unregulated nature, major institutions like Sotheby's and museums have integrated NFTs, exhibiting digital art in both virtual and physical spaces.

## Terms
- **Digital Art**: Artistic work using digital technology as part of the creative or presentational process.
- **Generative Adversarial Network (GAN)**: A machine learning framework where two algorithms compete to generate new data, such as images, with visual effects similar to traditional art.
- **Non-Fungible Token (NFT)**: A unique digital asset recorded on a blockchain, used to establish ownership of digital art.
- **Close Reading**: A computational analysis method focusing on specific visual aspects of a single artwork.
- **Distant Viewing**: A computational analysis method that statistically visualizes similarities across an entire collection of artworks.
- **Computer-Generated Imagery (CGI)**: Digital graphics created using computer software, widely used in film and animation.
- **Procedurally Generated**: Content created algorithmically by a computer program rather than manually by an artist.
- **Augmented Reality (AR)**: A technology that superimposes computer-generated images on a user's view of the real world.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Classification**: There is ongoing debate about what qualifies as digital art, particularly whether art merely using digital tools in its creation should be included.
- **Ownership and Copyright**: The rise of NFTs has intensified questions about who owns digital copyrights, especially for 3D scans of historical artifacts.
- **Authenticity and Value**: The use of AI in art creation raises questions about authorship, originality, and the value of human skill versus algorithmic generation.
- **Institutional Integration**: The acceptance and integration of digital art, especially NFT-based work, into traditional art institutions like museums and auction houses is a developing and contested process.

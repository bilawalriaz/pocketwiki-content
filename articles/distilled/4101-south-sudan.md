# South Sudan

## Overview
South Sudan is a landlocked country in East Africa, the world's most recent sovereign state, having gained independence from Sudan in 2011. Its history is defined by decades of civil war, ethnic conflict, and profound underdevelopment, making it one of the poorest and most unstable nations on Earth. The country's future is shaped by its struggle to build a functional state while managing ongoing violence, a severe humanitarian crisis, and a fragile peace process.

## Timeline
- **1899–1956** — Sudan governed as an Anglo-Egyptian condominium.
- **1955–1972** — First Sudanese Civil War.
- **1972** — Southern Sudan Autonomous Region formed.
- **1983–2005** — Second Sudanese Civil War.
- **2005** — Comprehensive Peace Agreement signed; autonomy restored.
- **9 July 2011** — South Sudan becomes independent after a referendum.
- **Dec 2013** — South Sudanese Civil War begins.
- **Aug 2018** — A new power-sharing agreement is signed.
- **Feb 2020** — A national unity government is formed, officially ending the civil war.
- **Mar 2025** — Vice-President Riek Machar is arrested, escalating tensions.

## Body

### History and Independence
The region's Nilotic peoples, including the Dinka and Nuer, entered the area before the 10th century. British colonial policy largely neglected the south, preserving its distinct ethnic and religious institutions. After Sudan's independence in 1956, the south's marginalization led to two devastating civil wars. The Second Sudanese Civil War (1983–2005) killed over 2.5 million people. The 2005 Comprehensive Peace Agreement ended the war and granted autonomy, leading to a 2011 referendum where 98.8% voted for independence. South Sudan became an independent state on 9 July 2011.

### Civil War and Ongoing Conflict
Independence did not bring peace. In December 2013, a power struggle between President Salva Kiir and former deputy Riek Machar ignited the South Sudanese Civil War. The conflict, marked by ethnic targeting, mass atrocities, and the use of child soldiers, displaced over 4 million people and killed an estimated 400,000. A 2018 peace deal and a 2020 unity government officially ended the war, but violence between armed militias persists. Tensions escalated again in 2025 with ceasefire violations and the arrest of Vice-President Machar, prompting warnings of a potential collapse back into full-scale civil war.

### Geography and Economy
South Sudan's landscape is dominated by the Nile River system and the vast Sudd wetland. Its economy is one of the world's least developed, with the lowest nominal GDP per capita historically. Oil constitutes over 90% of state revenues, but production has been disrupted by conflict and disputes with Sudan over pipeline fees. The country faces chronic food insecurity, with millions requiring humanitarian aid, and a severe water crisis where half the population lacks access to improved sources.

### Demographics and Society
The population is predominantly young, with roughly half under 18, and is composed of over 60 ethnic groups, primarily Nilotic peoples. The Dinka are the largest group (~40%), followed by the Nuer (~20%). English is the official language, but Juba Arabic is a common lingua franca. The population is mostly Christian or adheres to traditional indigenous faiths, with a Muslim minority. The country has one of the world's weakest health systems and highest maternal mortality rates.

## Terms
- **Nilotic peoples**: An ethnolinguistic group native to the Nile Valley, including the Dinka, Nuer, and Shilluk, who form the majority of South Sudan's population.
- **Sudd**: A vast swamp and seasonally flooded grassland in central South Sudan, formed by the White Nile, which is a critical ecological area.
- **Comprehensive Peace Agreement (CPA)**: The 2005 agreement that ended the Second Sudanese Civil War, granting autonomy to southern Sudan and paving the way for the 2011 independence referendum.
- **SPLM/SPLA**: The Sudan People's Liberation Movement/Army, the main rebel group and political party that fought for South Sudan's independence and has dominated its government.
- **SPLM-IO**: The Sudan People's Liberation Movement-in-Opposition, a major opposition faction led by Riek Machar.
- **Intergovernmental Authority on Development (IGAD)**: A regional bloc in East Africa that has mediated peace processes in South Sudan.
- **Juba Arabic**: A creole language based on Arabic that serves as a common lingua franca in South Sudan.
- **Human Development Index (HDI)**: A composite statistic of life expectancy, education, and per capita income; South Sudan consistently ranks lowest.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **State Stability**: The arrest of Vice-President Machar in 2025 and ongoing militia violence have led the UN to warn that the 2018 peace agreement is "in shambles" and the country is on the "edge of a collapse into civil war."
- **Economic Viability**: South Sudan's extreme dependence on oil revenue, coupled with disputes with Sudan over transit fees and infrastructure, makes its economy highly vulnerable to conflict and price shocks.
- **Humanitarian Crisis**: The scale of need is immense, with over two-thirds of the population requiring aid in 2026, exacerbated by the influx of refugees from the war in neighboring Sudan. The capacity of the state and international community to respond is a critical question.
- **Governance and Democracy**: Elections have been repeatedly postponed, with the latest scheduled for December 2026. The country's transition from a coalition of former warring factions to a functional democracy remains untested.

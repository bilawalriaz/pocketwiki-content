# Santiago

## Overview
Santiago is the capital and largest city of Chile, anchoring the Santiago Metropolitan Region which concentrates over a third of the national population and about 45% of Chile's GDP. Located in the Chilean Central Valley between the Andes and the Coastal Range, it is the country's political, administrative, and economic heart. Its history spans from prehistoric settlement to its founding by Spanish conquistadors in 1541, through independence, rapid 20th-century growth, and its current status as a modern, though socioeconomically divided, metropolis.

## Body

### Founding and Colonial Era
The Santiago basin was inhabited since at least the 10th millennium BC, with agricultural villages established along the Mapocho River. The area was later incorporated into the Inca sphere of influence. Spanish conquistador Pedro de Valdivia founded the colonial city of Santiago del Nuevo Extremo on 12 February 1541, laying out a grid plan around the Plaza Mayor. The early settlement faced severe challenges, including food shortages, Indigenous attacks, floods, and earthquakes, most notably a devastating one in 1647. Despite these, it consolidated as the capital of the Captaincy General of Chile.

### Independence and 19th-Century Growth
Santiago was the political center during the Chilean War of Independence, beginning with the First Government Junta in 1810 and culminating in the patriot victory at the Battle of Maipú in 1818. After independence, the city expanded through 19th-century railway construction, state-building projects, and the creation of major educational and cultural institutions. It emerged as the central hub of the national railway system and the country's principal industrial and commercial center, surpassing the port of Valparaíso.

### 20th-Century Expansion and Modern Metropolis
The 20th century saw a massive population boom, driven by migration from rural areas and mining camps. This growth led to extensive suburbanization and the development of new communes. The city's urban planning evolved with the creation of development plans for Greater Santiago in the 1960s. After the 1973 military coup, a neoliberal economic model in the 1980s led to further urban sprawl. In the 21st century, Santiago has become a modern metropolis with a rising skyline, including the Gran Torre Santiago, the tallest building in South America. However, it continues to grapple with socioeconomic inequality, air pollution, and the impacts of natural disasters like earthquakes.

### Geography and Climate
Santiago lies in the Santiago Basin, a bowl-shaped valley flanked by the Andes to the east and the Chilean Coastal Range to the west. The city has a cool semi-arid climate with Mediterranean patterns, featuring warm, dry summers and cool, wetter winters. Its enclosed valley location contributes to scenic mountain views but also to chronic air pollution and winter smog. The city is located on the Pacific Ring of Fire, making it highly susceptible to earthquakes.

## Terms
*   **Captaincy General of Chile:** A Spanish colonial administrative division, of which Santiago was the capital.
*   **First Government Junta:** The governing body proclaimed on 18 September 1810, marking the start of Chile's independence process.
*   **Battle of Maipú:** The decisive 1818 battle that secured Chilean independence from Spain.
*   **Alameda:** The common name for Avenida Libertador General Bernardo O'Higgins, Santiago's main east-west avenue.
*   **Barrio Alto:** The eastern sector of Santiago, including communes like Las Condes and Vitacura, known for high-income residents and modern business districts.
*   **Sanhattan:** The colloquial name for the modern high-rise business district in the eastern sector of Santiago.
*   **Transantiago/RED:** The integrated public transportation system launched in 2007 (rebranded as RED in 2019), combining buses, metro, and commuter rail.
*   **Estallido Social:** The series of massive protests and riots that occurred in Chile, including Santiago, between 2019 and 2020.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   The source notes that socioeconomic inequality and geosocial fragmentation remain major problems in Santiago and were key factors leading to the "Estallido Social" protests.
*   The implementation and effectiveness of mandatory earthquake standards in modern architecture became a topic of heated discussion after the 2010 earthquake caused damage to some newer buildings.
*   The Transantiago public transportation system faced significant criticism and challenges after its 2007 launch, including complaints about lack of buses, excessive transfers, and high fare evasion.

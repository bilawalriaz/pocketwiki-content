# Trojan War

## Overview
The Trojan War is a legendary conflict in Greek mythology, traditionally fought between an alliance of Greek (Achaean) kingdoms and the city of Troy in the late Bronze Age. It is a foundational narrative of Western literature, primarily known through Homer's epic poems the *Iliad* and the *Odyssey*, and its historicity remains a subject of scholarly debate, blending myth with possible historical events.

## Timeline
- **c. 1250 BC** — Hittite texts mention conflicts involving the kingdom of Ahhiyawa (Greeks) and the Assuwa confederation, which included Wilusa (possibly Troy).
- **c. 1194–1184 BC** — Traditional date for the war, based on the chronology of Eratosthenes.
- **c. 1180 BC** — Archaeological evidence for the destruction of Troy VIIa, a possible historical core for the war.
- **9th–6th centuries BC** — Composition of Homer's *Iliad* and *Odyssey*.
- **7th–6th centuries BC** — Composition of the Epic Cycle poems, expanding on the war's narrative.
- **1868** — Heinrich Schliemann meets Frank Calvert, who identifies Hisarlık as the site of Troy.
- **1870s onward** — Schliemann's excavations at Hisarlık gain widespread attention.
- **2001** — Geologists Kraft and Luce present evidence aligning the geography of Hisarlık with Homer's descriptions.

## Body

### Sources and Literary Tradition
The story of the Trojan War is not found in a single authoritative text but is assembled from various, sometimes contradictory, sources. The most important are Homer's *Iliad*, which covers a few days in the tenth year of the siege, and the *Odyssey*, which follows Odysseus's journey home. The Epic Cycle, a series of now-lost poems, filled in other parts of the narrative. These works originated from oral tradition and were later adapted by Greek tragedians and Roman poets like Virgil, whose *Aeneid* recounts the war's fall from the Trojan perspective.

### The Legendary Narrative
The war's mythological cause begins with the "Judgement of Paris." The goddess Eris, uninvited to a wedding, threw a golden apple "to the fairest," sparking a quarrel among Hera, Athena, and Aphrodite. Paris, a Trojan prince, judged Aphrodite the winner, and she promised him the love of Helen, the most beautiful woman in the world. When Paris abducted Helen from her husband, Menelaus of Sparta, the oath sworn by Helen's suitors to defend her marriage bound the Greek kings to launch a war.

The Achaeans, led by Agamemnon, besieged Troy for ten years. The *Iliad* focuses on the final year, detailing the quarrel between Agamemnon and the warrior Achilles, the death of Achilles' companion Patroclus, and Achilles' subsequent revenge killing of the Trojan hero Hector. The war ended with the ruse of the Trojan Horse, which allowed Greek soldiers to enter and sack the city.

### Historical Basis and Archaeology
The historicity of the Trojan War is an open question. While ancient Greeks generally believed it was historical, by the 19th century it was widely considered mythical. The excavations of Heinrich Schliemann at Hisarlık in modern-day Turkey, identified as the site of Troy, revived interest in its historical basis. Most scholars now accept that the Homeric stories likely contain a historical core, possibly fusing various Mycenaean Greek expeditions during the Bronze Age collapse. Hittite texts from the period mention a kingdom of Ahhiyawa (Achaea) and conflicts in the region of the Assuwa confederation, which included a city named Wilusa, often identified with Troy (Ilium). The archaeological destruction of Troy VIIa around 1180 BC roughly corresponds to the traditional dating of the war.

## Terms
*   **Achaeans:** The collective name for the Greeks who fought in the Trojan War.
*   **Epic Cycle:** A collection of ancient Greek epic poems that together narrated the full story of the Trojan War, supplementing Homer's works.
*   **Judgement of Paris:** The mythological event where Paris awarded a golden apple to Aphrodite, leading to the abduction of Helen and the Trojan War.
*   **Pentekonter:** A type of ancient Greek ship with 50 oars, the primary vessel used in the Achaean fleet.
*   **Palladium:** A sacred statue in Troy, believed to protect the city; its theft by the Greeks was a key step in Troy's fall.
*   **Late Bronze Age collapse:** A widespread societal collapse in the Mediterranean region around 1200 BC, providing a historical context for the war's setting.
*   **Ahhiyawa:** The name for the Achaeans (Greeks) in Hittite texts from the Late Bronze Age.
*   **Wilusa:** The name for the city of Troy (Ilium) in Hittite texts.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Historicity:** Did a specific historical conflict inspire the myths, or are the stories a fusion of various Bronze Age events? Most scholars believe in a historical core but not a literal Homeric account.
*   **Location of Troy:** While Hisarlık is the accepted site, debates continue about which specific archaeological layer (e.g., Troy VI or VIIa) corresponds to the legendary city.
*   **Scale of the Conflict:** Was the war a massive pan-Hellenic expedition as described in the *Iliad*'s Catalogue of Ships, or a smaller-scale regional conflict?
*   **Nature of the Sources:** To what extent do the later Epic Cycle and other sources preserve early oral traditions versus later inventions?

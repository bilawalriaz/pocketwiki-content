# Seattle

## Overview
Seattle is the most populous city in Washington state and the Pacific Northwest, serving as a major economic, cultural, and transportation hub. Its history features boom-and-bust cycles tied to natural resources and technology, a vibrant cultural scene, and rapid 21st-century growth bringing prosperity and challenges like housing affordability and traffic.

## Timeline
- **c. 4000 BC** — Archaeological evidence shows Native American habitation in the Seattle area.
- **November 13, 1851** — The Denny Party pioneers land at Alki Point, the first permanent European-American settlement.
- **1852** — The settlement moves to Elliott Bay's eastern shore and is named "Seattle" after Chief Seattle.
- **1889** — The Great Seattle Fire destroys the central business district, leading to a rapid rebuild.
- **1897** — The S.S. Portland arrives with gold, launching Seattle's role as the Klondike Gold Rush gateway.
- **1909** — The Alaska–Yukon–Pacific Exposition is held, shaping the University of Washington campus.
- **1919** — The Seattle General Strike occurs, one of the first in the U.S.
- **1962** — The Century 21 Exposition (World's Fair) is held, featuring the Space Needle.
- **1971** — The first Starbucks coffee shop opens at Pike Place Market.
- **1999** — The World Trade Organization conference is met with large-scale protests.
- **2001** — Boeing moves its corporate headquarters from Seattle to Chicago.
- **2009** — Link light rail service begins, connecting downtown to Sea-Tac Airport.
- **2021** — The Seattle Kraken, an NHL hockey team, begins play.

## Body

### Early History and Founding
The Seattle area was inhabited by Native Americans, including the Duwamish, for at least 4,000 years. The first permanent American settlers, the Denny Party, arrived in 1851. After a difficult winter, they relocated across Elliott Bay in 1852, founding the settlement named for Chief Seattle, a leader of the local Duwamish and Suquamish tribes. The city was incorporated in 1869.

### Economic Cycles and Growth
Seattle's economy has experienced repeated boom-and-bust cycles. The first boom was driven by lumber. The Great Seattle Fire of 1889 destroyed the central business district but led to a grander rebuild. The Klondike Gold Rush (1897) ended a depression and established Seattle as a major transportation and supply center. The 20th century saw growth from shipbuilding (especially during World Wars I and II) and the rise of Boeing. A major downturn in the early 1970s, linked to Boeing's struggles, led to the famous "Will the last person leaving Seattle – Turn out the lights" billboard. Prosperity returned with technology companies like Microsoft (headquartered nearby in 1979) and Amazon, fueling a massive population and construction boom from the 1990s onward.

### Culture and Identity
Seattle's culture is heavily defined by its music history. The Jackson Street jazz scene (1918–1951) nurtured artists like Ray Charles and Quincy Jones. In the late 20th century, the city became the origin of grunge music (Nirvana, Pearl Jam) and other rock bands (Foo Fighters, Heart). It has a large LGBTQ community, with Capitol Hill as its historic center. The city is known for its coffee culture, pioneered by Starbucks, and its progressive, secular political environment.

### Geography and Climate
Seattle is located on an isthmus between Puget Sound and Lake Washington. Its topography is hilly, with seven principal hills, and has been significantly altered by regrading projects. The city lies in a major earthquake zone, with the Cascadia subduction zone posing a threat of a magnitude 9.0+ earthquake. Seattle has a warm-summer Mediterranean climate (Csb) with cool, wet winters and mild, relatively dry summers. It is one of the cloudiest cities in the contiguous U.S. but receives less total rainfall than many eastern cities.

### Demographics and Economy
Seattle's population has grown rapidly, surpassing 800,000 by 2025. The city is diverse, with significant Asian, Black, and Hispanic populations, and a high percentage of residents with mixed-race heritage. The economy is driven by technology (Amazon, Microsoft), aerospace (Boeing's manufacturing plants), retail (Starbucks, Nordstrom), and global health (Bill & Melinda Gates Foundation). The Port of Seattle is a major gateway for trade with Asia.

### Government and Politics
Seattle has a mayor–council form of government and is known for its liberal politics, consistently voting for Democratic candidates. It was the first major U.S. city to elect a female mayor (1926) and an openly gay mayor. In 2023, it became the first U.S. city to ban caste discrimination.

## Terms
- **Isthmus:** A narrow strip of land connecting two larger land areas, with water on either side.
- **Klondike Gold Rush:** A migration to the Yukon in northwestern Canada after gold was discovered in 1896; Seattle became its primary supply hub.
- **Grunge:** A subgenre of alternative rock that emerged in Seattle in the mid-1980s, characterized by distorted electric guitar and apathetic lyrics.
- **Regrading:** The large-scale reshaping of land by moving soil from high areas to fill low areas.
- **Cascadia Subduction Zone:** A fault line capable of producing a magnitude 9.0+ earthquake, located off the Pacific Northwest coast.
- **Mediterranean Climate (Csb):** A climate characterized by warm, dry summers and cool, wet winters.
- **At-Large Election:** An election where representatives are chosen by the entire voting population of a city, not by districts.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Housing Affordability:** Rapid growth has led to a severe housing crisis, with over 45,000 households spending more than half their income on housing and a significant homeless population.
- **Urban Growth Management:** The city grapples with balancing increased density (e.g., relaxing height limits downtown) with preserving single-family housing zones.
- **Transportation:** Seattle has the 8th-worst traffic congestion in the U.S. and is investing heavily in expanding its light rail system (Sound Transit 3) to address this.
- **Social Equity:** The city has addressed issues like caste discrimination (banning it in 2023) and continues to work on racial balance in its public schools following a 2007 Supreme Court ruling.

# Odesa

## Overview
Odesa is a major Ukrainian port city on the Black Sea, renowned for its multicultural history and 19th-century urban planning. Its strategic location and free-port status made it a vital trade hub and a cosmopolitan center, shaping a unique cultural identity. The city's historic center was designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2023, partly in response to damage from the Russian invasion of Ukraine.

- **1944** — Odesa is liberated by the Red Army.

## Body

### Early History and Founding
The site has been inhabited since at least the 6th century BC, with evidence of a Greek settlement possibly linked to Histria. In the Middle Ages, it was ruled by nomadic tribes, the Golden Horde, the Crimean Khanate, the Grand Duchy of Lithuania, and the Ottoman Empire. The fortress of Khadjibey was captured by Russian forces in 1789 during the Russo-Turkish War. In 1794, Empress Catherine II ordered the construction of a port and trading pier at the location, and in 1795, the settlement was renamed Odessa, using a Slavic feminine form of the ancient Greek city Odessos.

### Growth and Cosmopolitan Character
Odesa's rapid growth was driven by its status as a free port (1819–1858) and the leadership of early governors like the Duc de Richelieu. It became a major grain-exporting port and Russia's fourth-largest city by the 19th century. The city attracted a diverse population of Albanians, Armenians, Greeks, Italians, Jews, and many others, creating a multicultural environment. This cosmopolitan nature was noted by Alexander Pushkin during his exile there. The city's architecture reflects Mediterranean influences, with styles like Art Nouveau and Renaissance, and its iconic Potemkin Stairs were built between 1837 and 1841.

### 20th Century to Present
Odesa experienced significant turmoil in the 20th century. It was a center of revolutionary activity in 1905 and saw conflict during the Russian Civil War. During World War II, the city endured a brutal 73-day siege by Axis forces in 1941, followed by an occupation that included the massacre of approximately 25,000 people. It was awarded the title "Hero City" in 1945. After the war, it was an important Soviet trading port. Following Ukrainian independence in 1991, Odesa remained a key economic and cultural center. The city faced pro-Russian unrest in 2014 and has been repeatedly targeted by Russian airstrikes since the full-scale invasion of Ukraine in 2022.

## Terms
*   **Free port:** A port where goods can be landed, stored, and re-exported without payment of customs duties, a status Odesa held from 1819 to 1858.
*   **Raion:** An administrative district of a city or region in Ukraine.
*   **Hromada:** A basic-level administrative unit of Ukraine, equivalent to a municipality.
*   **Potemkin Stairs:** A giant stairway leading from the city center down to the harbor, built between 1837 and 1841.
*   **Catacombs:** A vast labyrinth of tunnels beneath the city, formed from abandoned limestone mines, used for smuggling and as partisan shelters in WWII.
*   **Odesan dialect:** A distinctive Russian dialect influenced by Yiddish and other languages, reflecting the city's multicultural history.
*   **Transnistria:** A region east of the Dniester River that was administered by Romania during WWII, with Odesa as its capital.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   The exact location of the ancient Greek settlement of Odessos, for which the modern city is named, is unknown and debated, with one proposed site near the village of Koshary.
*   The origin of the adjacent Moldavanka settlement is debated: some historians claim it predates Odesa, founded by Moldavians building the Ottoman fortress, while others argue it formed after Odesa's founding.
*   The claim that Secretary of State Adrian Gribovsky suggested the name "Odessa" is noted, though some express doubt about his memoirs.

# Leonardo da Vinci

## Overview
Leonardo da Vinci (1452–1519) was an Italian polymath of the High Renaissance whose expertise spanned painting, engineering, anatomy, and natural philosophy. Born illegitimate near Vinci, he trained in Florence under Andrea del Verrocchio before spending decades in Milan, Rome, and finally France under the patronage of Francis I. Though fewer than 25 major paintings survive—many unfinished—works such as the *Mona Lisa*, *The Last Supper*, and the *Vitruvian Man* define the Western artistic canon. His 13,000 pages of mirror-script notebooks reveal observational science and mechanical designs centuries ahead of their time, though few were built or published in his lifetime. He epitomizes the "Renaissance Man," integrating art and empirical inquiry through a method based on detailed depiction rather than experiment.

## Timeline
- **15 April 1452** — Born in or near Vinci, Tuscany, to notary Piero da Vinci and Caterina di Meo Lippi.
- **c. 1466–1472** — Apprenticed to Andrea del Verrocchio in Florence; learns painting, sculpture, and technical crafts.
- **1472** — Admitted to the Guild of Saint Luke as a master.
- **1481–1482** — Receives commissions for *Adoration of the Magi* and *St. Jerome*; leaves for Milan to serve Ludovico Sforza.
- **1482–1499** — First Milanese period: paints *Virgin of the Rocks* and *The Last Supper*; designs equestrian monument (Gran Cavallo).
- **1499–1500** — Flees Milan after French invasion; works as military engineer in Venice.
- **1500–1508** — Second Florentine period: serves Cesare Borgia as military architect; begins *Mona Lisa* (c. 1503); designs *Battle of Anghiari* mural.
- **1506–1513** — Second Milanese period under French governors; takes pupil Francesco Melzi; studies anatomy with Marcantonio della Torre.
- **1513–1516** — Resides in Rome under Pope Leo X; studies botany and hydraulics; dissects cadavers.
- **1516–1519** — Moves to France at invitation of Francis I; lives at Clos Lucé; designs palace at Romorantin.
- **2 May 1519** — Dies at Clos Lucé, aged 67; buried in the Collegiate Church of Saint Florentin at Château d'Amboise.

## Body

### Early Life and Training
Leonardo was born out of wedlock to a notary and a lower-class woman, Caterina. He received only basic informal education in vernacular writing and arithmetic. Around age 14, he entered the workshop of Andrea del Verrocchio, Florence’s leading artist, where he trained for seven years alongside Botticelli and Perugino. The workshop provided theoretical grounding and diverse technical skills: drafting, metallurgy, mechanics, and oil painting—a new technique Leonardo adopted early. By 1472 he was a master in the Guild of Saint Luke but continued collaborating with Verrocchio. His earliest dated work is a 1473 landscape drawing of the Arno Valley, notable for its geological detail.

### Professional Career: Milan, Florence, Rome, France
**First Milanese Period (1482–1499).** Leonardo wrote to Ludovico Sforza offering engineering and military skills, mentioning painting almost as an afterthought. He painted the *Virgin of the Rocks* (two versions) and *The Last Supper* in the refectory of Santa Maria delle Grazie. The latter deteriorated rapidly because Leonardo used tempera on gesso rather than true fresco. He also designed the *Gran Cavallo*, a colossal equestrian statue for Francesco Sforza; a clay model was completed but the bronze was requisitioned for cannons in 1494. He created the *Sala delle Asse* trompe-l'œil decoration in the Sforza Castle (c. 1498).

**Second Florentine Period (1500–1508).** After Ludovico’s fall, Leonardo fled to Venice, then Florence. He served Cesare Borgia as military architect (1502), producing accurate maps of Imola and the Chiana Valley. He rejoined the Guild of Saint Luke (1503) and began the *Mona Lisa* (portrait of Lisa del Giocondo). He was commissioned to paint the *Battle of Anghiari* in the Palazzo Vecchio, opposite Michelangelo’s *Battle of Cascina*; both murals were abandoned and lost.

**Second Milanese Period (1508–1513).** Recalled by French governor Charles II d’Amboise, Leonardo lived in Porta Orientale. He took on Francesco Melzi as a pupil and collaborated with anatomist Marcantonio della Torre. Plans for an equestrian monument for Gian Giacomo Trivulzio were halted by the 1512 Swiss/Spanish/Venetian invasion that expelled the French.

**Rome and France (1513–1519).** Under Pope Leo X, Leonardo lived in the Vatican’s Belvedere Courtyard with a monthly allowance. He studied botany, planned drainage of the Pontine Marshes, and dissected cadavers, but a painting commission was cancelled when he experimented with varnish. In 1516, Francis I invited him to France, providing the manor Clos Lucé near Amboise. Leonardo designed a palace at Romorantin and a mechanical lion for pageants. His right hand became paralytic c. 1517, likely causing unfinished works. He died at Clos Lucé on 2 May 1519, possibly of a stroke.

### Paintings and Artistic Innovation
Leonardo’s fame rested on fewer than 25 attributed major works. Key innovations include *sfumato* (subtle tonal gradation creating "smoke" effects), anatomical precision, geological and botanical accuracy, and psychological depth in physiognomy.
- **Early Works:** Collaboration on Verrocchio’s *Baptism of Christ* (c. 1472–75); two *Annunciations* showing a humanist Virgin Mary.
- **1480s:** *St. Jerome in the Wilderness* (unfinished, linking anatomy to penitence); *Adoration of the Magi* (unfinished, complex perspective); *Virgin of the Rocks* (two versions, apocryphal meeting in a wild landscape); *Lady with an Ermine* (Cecilia Gallerani, dynamic pose breaking profile convention).
- **1490s:** *The Last Supper* (moment of betrayal announcement; compositional mastery but technical failure).
- **1500s:** *Mona Lisa* (elusive smile via *sfumato*, atmospheric landscape, unadorned dress); *Virgin and Child with Saint Anne* (double oblique composition, influential on Raphael and Venetian painters). *Battle of Anghiari* known only through copies.

### Drawings and Notebooks
Leonardo was a prolific draughtsman. His journals (originally loose papers) fuse art and natural philosophy across ~13,000 pages. Famous drawings include the *Vitruvian Man* (ideal human proportions), *Head of an Angel*, botanical studies, and the large *Virgin and Child with Saint Anne and Saint John the Baptist* cartoon (National Gallery, London) using *sfumato*. He wrote in mirror-image cursive (right-to-left), used shorthand, and intended publication but never achieved it. After his death, heir Francesco Melzi attempted organization; the collection fragmented. Major codices survive in Windsor, the Louvre, Biblioteca Ambrosiana (Codex Atlanticus), British Library (Codex Arundel), and the privately owned Codex Leicester (Bill Gates).

### Science and Inventions
Leonardo’s science was observational: he described phenomena in utmost detail rather than experimenting or theorizing mathematically. Lacking formal Latin and mathematics, he was ignored by contemporary scholars.
- **Anatomy:** Dissected ~30 corpses (Santa Maria Nuova, Milan, Rome). Produced 240+ drawings and 13,000 words. Pioneered biomechanics (skeleton as levers), drew first scientific foetus *in utero*, defined atherosclerosis and cirrhosis, built wax cerebral ventricle models and a glass aorta to visualize blood flow. Abandoned humoral theory.
- **Engineering:** Created modern technical drawing (exploded views). Designed flying machines (ornithopter, helical rotor), armoured vehicle, hydraulic pumps, steam cannon, and moveable barricades for Venice. First stated laws of sliding friction (1493) while studying perpetual motion (which he deemed impossible); rediscovered by Amontons in 1699. Named first of 23 "Men of Tribology."
- **Precursors:** Research shows >100 inventions had Medieval, Classical, or Eastern prototypes; Leonardo’s novelty was combining functions into utility scenes.

### Personal Life and Relationships
Leonardo revealed little in his notebooks. Vasari noted his physical beauty, grace, and love of animals (reportedly vegetarian, buying caged birds to release). Close friends included mathematician Luca Pacioli (*Divina proportione* collaborator). His pupils Salaì (Gian Giacomo Caprotti, entered 1490) and Francesco Melzi were his most intimate companions. Salaì, called "thief, liar, stubborn, glutton," stayed 30 years; Melzi was heir and executor. In 1476, Leonardo and three others were charged with sodomy involving a male prostitute; charges dismissed for lack of evidence, possibly due to Medici influence. Scholarship (Freud, Isaacson) debates homosexuality, citing androgyny in *St. John the Baptist* and erotic drawings.

### Legacy and Remains
Vasari (1568) called him divinely endowed; 19th-century writers (Fuseli, Taine, Berenson) hailed his universal genius. The 2019 Louvre quincentenary exhibition displayed 100+ works. His remains were interred at Saint Florentin (1519), but the church was demolished in 1802. In 1863, Arsène Houssaye excavated a skeleton with a bronze ring and stone fragments reading "Leonardus Vinci"; a large skull was phrenologically linked to intelligence. Re-interred in the Chapel of Saint Hubert (1874) with a plaque stating "presumed" identity. DNA testing announced 2016; a 2021 genealogical study identified 14 living male relatives via half-brother Domenico’s line.

## Terms
- ****Polymath**** — A person of wide-ranging knowledge or learning; Leonardo exemplified this across art, science, and engineering.
- ****Sfumato**** — A painting technique of subtle tonal gradation without lines or borders, creating a "smoky" effect; Leonardo’s signature in the *Mona Lisa*.
- ****Chiaroscuro**** — The treatment of light and shade in drawing and painting to create volume; foundational to Leonardo’s modeling.
- ****Tempera**** — Paint using egg yolk as binder; Leonardo used it over gesso for *The Last Supper* instead of fresco, causing flaking.
- ****Fresco**** — Painting on wet plaster so pigment binds with the wall; the durable standard Leonardo avoided for *The Last Supper*.
- ****Vitruvian Man**** — Drawing (c. 1490) illustrating ideal human proportions inscribed in a circle and square, based on Roman architect Vitruvius.
- ****Codex Atlanticus**** — 12-volume collection of Leonardo’s drawings and writings (1478–1519), held at Biblioteca Ambrosiana, Milan.
- ****Mirror writing**** — Leonardo’s habitual right-to-left cursive, likely due to left-handedness; readable only in a mirror.
- ****Tribology**** — The study of friction, wear, and lubrication; Leonardo stated its first laws (1493) but unpublished until 1699.
- ****Gran Cavallo**** — Leonardo’s unbuilt 24-foot clay model for a bronze equestrian statue of Francesco Sforza; bronze used for cannons (1494).

## Debates and open questions
- **Attribution of *Salvator Mundi*:** Sold for $450.3M (2017) as autograph Leonardo; scholars debate extent of workshop participation vs. master’s hand.
- **Sexuality:** 1476 sodomy charge dismissed; Vasari silent. Freud (1910) and Isaacson (2017) argue for homosexuality based on pupil relationships and androgynous figures; others caution against anachronistic labels.
- **Mona Lisa’s identity:** Traditional view (Vasari) identifies Lisa del Giocondo; alternative theories propose Isabella d’Este, a self-portrait, or idealized composite.
- **Authenticity of remains:** 1863 excavation yielded "Leonardus Vinci" fragments, but skeleton height (1.73m), foot orientation, and large skull raise doubts. DNA tests (announced 2016) and 2021 genealogical study (14 living male relatives) aim to resolve.
- **Scientific influence:** Notebooks unpublished until 1651 (*Treatise on Painting*); Capra argues Leonardo’s integrated art-science method differs fundamentally from later experimental science (Galileo, Newton).
- **Chronology of *Virgin of the Rocks*:** Two versions (Louvre, London); debate over which was painted first and whether the London version is wholly autograph or workshop-assisted.

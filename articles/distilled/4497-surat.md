# Surat

## Overview
Surat is a major port city in Gujarat, India, historically one of the most important trading hubs in the Mughal Empire. Today, it is a thriving commercial and industrial center, globally renowned for its diamond cutting and polishing industry, which processes about 90% of the world's diamonds. The city's modern identity is shaped by its rapid economic growth, significant textile manufacturing, and a dramatic turnaround in public sanitation following a major plague outbreak in 1994.

## Timeline
- **c. 1500** — Traditional account of the city's founding by merchant Gopi; name "Surat" becomes established by 1520.
- **1512, 1530** — Surat is burned by the Portuguese.
- **1573** — Surat is conquered by the Mughal Emperor Akbar, becoming the empire's most prosperous port.
- **1612** — England establishes its first Indian trading factory in Surat.
- **1664** — The city is looted for the first time by Maratha king Shivaji.
- **1800** — The British take control of Surat.
- **1942** — Local cultivators fight police during the Quit India Movement.
- **1994** — A major plague outbreak leads to a city-wide sanitation overhaul.
- **2015** — Surat is selected for the IBM Smarter Cities Challenge and India's Smart Cities Mission.
- **2020** — Surat is ranked the second cleanest city in India by Swachh Survekshan.

## Body

### Historical Rise and Mughal Prominence
Before the 1500s, the nearby town of Rander was the main commercial center. Surat rose to importance as Rander declined due to Portuguese raids. By the 16th century, it was a key seaport. Its golden age came under the Mughal Empire after Akbar's conquest in 1573. Surat became the empire's wealthiest port, a bustling emporium exporting gold and cloth, and home to trading communities from across Asia and Europe, including Armenians and the English. Despite its prosperity, the city was sacked twice by the Maratha king Shivaji in the 17th century, which damaged its trade.

### Decline and Revival Under the British
Surat's importance waned with the rise of Bombay (Mumbai) in the 18th century. A devastating epidemic in 1790-91 killed 100,000 people. The British took control in 1800, and while Surat remained a textile port, it was less central to their administration. The city stagnated until the arrival of railways, which spurred a revival in its famous silk, cotton, and brocade industries.

### Modern Economic Hub and the 1994 Plague
Post-independence, Surat grew as a commercial center. A pivotal moment was the 1994 plague outbreak, attributed to poor sanitation. The crisis led to the election of a new administrator, Suryadevara Ramachandra Rao, who launched aggressive cleaning campaigns, road widening, and demolition of illegal constructions. This overhaul transformed Surat's sanitation, leading to its recognition as one of India's cleanest cities. Today, Surat's economy is dominated by its diamond industry, which emerged in the late 1950s, and its massive textile sector, which produces 60% of India's polyester cloth. The city is also a major petrochemical and IT hub.

## Terms
- **Emporium:** A center or place of trade, especially an important commercial city.
- **Mughal Empire:** The dominant Islamic empire in the Indian subcontinent from the 16th to the 19th century.
- **Maratha:** A Hindu warrior group that formed an empire in parts of India, often in conflict with the Mughals.
- **Plague:** An infectious disease that was a major cause of epidemics throughout history.
- **Swachh Survekshan:** An annual survey of cleanliness, hygiene, and sanitation in cities and towns across India.
- **Smart City Mission:** An urban renewal and retrofitting program by the Government of India to develop smart cities equipped with basic infrastructure and digital technology.
- **BRTS (Bus Rapid Transit System):** A high-capacity bus-based public transport system with dedicated lanes for faster service.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The exact origin and meaning of the name "Surat" is debated. While a traditional story links it to a Muslim king altering "Suryapur" (city of the sun), historical sources mention both names before the traditional founding date of c. 1500.
- The city's cleanliness ranking has fluctuated significantly in recent years (2nd in 2020, 12th in 2021, 25th in 2023), raising questions about the sustainability of its sanitation initiatives.

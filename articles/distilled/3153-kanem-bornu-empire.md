# Kanem–Bornu Empire

## Overview
The Kanem–Bornu Empire was a major African state centered around Lake Chad, existing from approximately 700 CE to 1902. Its longevity and control over trans-Saharan trade routes made it a pivotal political and economic power in the central Sahel region for over a millennium. The empire's history is defined by its adaptation to shifting environmental and economic conditions, its conversion to Islam, and its eventual fragmentation under internal strife and external colonial pressures.

## Timeline
- **c. 700** — Founding of the empire in the Kanem region, with the Duguwa dynasty ruling from Njimi.
- **11th century** — Conversion to Islam; the Duguwa are replaced by the Sayfawa dynasty.
- **13th century** — Zenith under Mai Dunama II Dibalemi, who commands a large cavalry and expands trade.
- **c. 1380** — The Bilala people oust the Sayfawa from Kanem; the empire re-centers in Bornu.
- **c. 1472** — Mai Ali I Gaji founds the new permanent capital, Ngazargamu, in Bornu.
- **Late 16th century** — Peak of power under Mai Idris IV Alooma, who secures Ottoman firearms and alliances.
- **17th century** — Slow decline begins due to changing trade patterns, environmental stress, and conflicts.
- **1807** — The Fula jihads reach Bornu; the capital Ngazargamu is captured and destroyed in 1808.
- **1814** — Muhammad al-Amin al-Kanemi becomes the de facto ruler, establishing the al-Kanemi dynasty.
- **1893** — Sudanese warlord Rabih az-Zubayr conquers Bornu, ending the al-Kanemi dynasty's rule.
- **1900** — Rabih is killed by French forces; the al-Kanemi dynasty is briefly restored.
- **1902** — Colonial powers (France, Britain, Germany) partition the empire's territories.

## Body

### Origins and Early History
The empire originated in the lands between Lake Chad and the Bahr el Ghazal River, with its core in the Kanem region. Its foundation is traditionally dated to c. 700, though dates vary. The early state was ruled by the Duguwa dynasty from the capital Njimi and grew wealthy through the export of slaves and control of trans-Saharan trade. The adoption of the camel by the 5th century CE facilitated this trade, leading to the rise of stratified societies and kingdoms.

### Conversion to Islam and Imperial Zenith
Islam was introduced to Kanem from the north, likely by traders or the Toubou people. The first Muslim ruler was Mai Hu in the 11th century, shortly before the Sayfawa dynasty came to power. Conversion was strategic, as Islam did not permit the enslavement of Muslims, which limited forced conversions to preserve the slave trade. The empire reached its peak in the 13th century under Mai Dunama II Dibalemi, who commanded a vast cavalry and extended influence to the Fezzan. Control of trade routes for salt, ivory, and slaves sustained the empire's power.

### Shift to Bornu and Later Apogee
Economic strain, conflict with the Bilala people, and environmental drying of Lake Chad led to the loss of Kanem in the 14th century. The Sayfawa dynasty re-centered the empire in the Bornu region. After a century of instability, Mai Ali I Gaji founded the new capital Ngazargamu c. 1472. The empire regained prominence under Mai Idris IV Alooma in the late 16th century, who modernized the army with Ottoman firearms and forged diplomatic ties, making Bornu a new center of Islamic learning after the collapse of the Songhai Empire.

### Decline and Fall
From the 17th century, the empire declined due to shifting Atlantic trade routes, Tuareg pressure on northern oases, and environmental stress from the Little Ice Age. The Fula jihads in the early 19th century shattered its western provinces and destroyed Ngazargamu. The scholar Muhammad al-Amin al-Kanemi rallied resistance, establishing the al-Kanemi dynasty and the new capital Kukawa. However, the empire was fatally weakened and was conquered in 1893 by the Sudanese warlord Rabih az-Zubayr. After Rabih's death in 1900, the remnants were partitioned by French, British, and German colonial forces in 1902.

## Terms
- **Mai**: The traditional title for the sovereign of the Kanem–Bornu Empire, equivalent to king or emperor.
- **Shehu**: The title (meaning "sheikh") adopted by the rulers of the al-Kanemi dynasty after the Fula jihads.
- **Girgam**: The empire's royal chronicle, preserved orally and later transcribed, used to reconstruct its political history.
- **Chima system**: The feudal administrative system for managing fiefs, where all land belonged to the sovereign and was granted to nobles and officials.
- **Kaigama**: The highest-ranking military commander and general of the empire's army.
- **Ngazargamu**: The capital city founded c. 1472 in Bornu, which served as the empire's center for over three centuries.
- **Trans-Saharan trade**: The network of trade routes across the Sahara Desert, which was the economic foundation of the empire.
- **Kanuri**: The dominant ethnic group and language associated with the empire, which spread due to its political influence.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The exact founding date of the empire is debated, with estimates ranging from c. 600 to c. 900.
- The nature and location of the original capital, Njimi, remain uncertain despite archaeological attempts.
- The historiographical name "Kanem–Bornu Empire" is considered anachronistic by scholars, as the state rarely controlled both regions simultaneously and was not known by this name contemporaneously.
- Historian Rémi Dewière has suggested the state should be called a "sultanate" rather than an "empire" to decolonize its history.

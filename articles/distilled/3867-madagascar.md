# Madagascar

## Overview
Madagascar is the world's fourth-largest island, located off the southeastern coast of Africa. Its long geological isolation has made it a global biodiversity hotspot, with over 90% of its wildlife found nowhere else on Earth. The island's human history is marked by successive waves of settlement from Southeast Asia and Africa, the rise and fall of kingdoms, French colonization, and a turbulent post-independence political journey.

## Timeline
- **c. 180 million years ago** — Madagascar splits from Africa during the breakup of Gondwana.
- **c. 90 million years ago** — Madagascar separates from the Indian subcontinent.
- **c. 500–700 CE** — First permanent settlement by Austronesian peoples from Indonesia.
- **c. 1000 CE** — Bantu-speaking migrants arrive from East Africa.
- **Early 19th century** — Merina Kingdom unites most of the island under King Radama I.
- **1896** — France annexes Madagascar, ending the Merina monarchy.
- **1960** — Madagascar gains independence from France.
- **1975–1991** — The socialist Second Republic under Didier Ratsiraka.
- **1992** — A new constitution establishes the Third Republic and multi-party democracy.
- **2009** — A political crisis and military coup lead to a transitional government.
- **2014** — Constitutional governance is restored, beginning the Fourth Republic.
- **2025** — Mass protests result in a military coup and installation of a junta.

## Body

### Geography and Biodiversity
Madagascar's separation from other landmasses allowed unique flora and fauna to evolve. Approximately 90% of its plant and animal species are endemic, including lemurs, the fossa, and baobab trees. The island's geography features a steep eastern escarpment with rainforests, a central highland plateau, and drier western and southern regions with deciduous and spiny forests. Human activity, particularly slash-and-burn agriculture (*tavy*), has led to the loss of over 90% of the original forest cover, threatening many species.

### Early Settlement and Kingdoms
The island was first settled by Austronesian peoples from Southeast Asia, followed by Bantu groups from Africa. For centuries, society was organized into shifting alliances. By the 16th century, centralized states began to form, such as the Sakalava kingdoms on the west coast and the Merina kingdom in the central highlands. European contact began around 1500, leading to trade and occasional conflict.

### Unification and Colonial Rule
In the early 19th century, King Radama I of the Merina kingdom expanded his rule to control most of the island. His successors, notably Queen Ranavalona I, resisted foreign influence. France gradually increased its control, leading to the First Franco-Hova War (1883–1885) and a full military invasion in 1894–1895. Madagascar was annexed as a colony in 1896, sparking the Menalamba rebellion. Colonial rule introduced plantation agriculture, infrastructure, and a French-style education system, while also fueling independence movements.

### Independence and Modern Politics
Madagascar gained independence in 1960. Its post-independence history has been marked by political instability, including four republics, coups, and contested elections. The First Republic maintained close ties to France. The Second Republic (1975–1991) was a socialist state aligned with the Eastern Bloc, which ended in economic crisis. The Third Republic (1992–2010) established a multi-party democracy but was marred by corruption and a violent 2009 political crisis. The current Fourth Republic was established in 2010, though it was interrupted by another military coup in 2025.

### Economy and Society
Madagascar is classified as a least-developed country. Its economy relies on agriculture (notably vanilla and cloves), mining, textiles, and ecotourism. Despite growth since the early 2000s, poverty remains widespread, with over 80% of the population living on less than $2.15 per day as of 2025. The population is predominantly Malagasy, with a blend of Southeast Asian and African ancestry. Malagasy and French are official languages, and Christianity is the predominant religion, often syncretized with traditional ancestor veneration.

## Terms
- **Biodiversity hotspot**: A region with a significant reservoir of biodiversity that is under threat from human activity.
- **Endemic**: A species native and restricted to a certain place.
- **Gondwana**: An ancient supercontinent that broke apart, forming the southern continents.
- **Hasina**: A sacred life force believed to legitimize authority in traditional Malagasy communities.
- **Tavy**: A traditional slash-and-burn agricultural practice.
- **Fady**: A system of taboos that are respected in Malagasy culture.
- **Famadihana**: A traditional funerary custom of exhuming and re-wrapping the remains of ancestors.
- **Merina**: The largest ethnic subgroup, historically dominant in the central highlands.
- **Sakalava**: An ethnic group and historical kingdom along the western coast.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The exact origin of the name "Madagascar" is uncertain, with hypotheses linking it to the Malay word for "Malay island" or a corruption of "Mogadishu."
- The precise timing and nature of the first human settlement are debated, with estimates ranging from 350 BCE to 550 CE.
- The origins and migration routes of early kingdoms like the Maroserana (Sakalava) are subjects of differing historical interpretations.
- The impact of the 2009 coup and subsequent political transitions on long-term democratic stability and economic development remains a critical question.

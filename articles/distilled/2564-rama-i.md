# Rama I

## Overview
Phutthayotfa Chulalok (Rama I, 1737–1809) was the founder of the Chakri dynasty and the Rattanakosin Kingdom, which is the direct predecessor of modern Thailand. He is pivotal for reunifying Siam after the collapse of Ayutthaya in 1767, establishing Bangkok as the new capital, and reviving the kingdom's political, religious, and cultural institutions, setting the stage for a new "Golden Age."

## Timeline
- **1737** — Born as Thongduang in the Ayutthaya Kingdom.
- **1767** — Ayutthaya falls to Burma; Thongduang serves under King Taksin of Thonburi.
- **1782** — Deposes and succeeds King Taksin, founding the Chakri dynasty and moving the capital to Rattanakosin (Bangkok).
- **1785–1786** — Repels the major Burmese "Nine Armies War" invasion.
- **1788** — Convenes a council to review and standardize the Buddhist canon (Tipiṭaka).
- **1805** — The court compiles the "Three Seals Law," a comprehensive legal code.
- **1809** — Dies in Bangkok; succeeded by his son, Rama II.

## Body

### Rise to Power
Thongduang was born into a noble Mon family and entered the royal court of Ayutthaya. After the capital's destruction by Burma in 1767, he became a key military commander under King Taksin of Thonburi. He rose to the highest noble rank, *Somdet Chao Phraya*, through successful campaigns that reunified Siam and expanded its influence into Cambodia and Laos. In 1782, following political instability in Thonburi, he seized power, crowned himself king, and established the Chakri dynasty.

### Consolidation and Cultural Revival
Rama I's reign focused on restoring Siamese power and institutions. He moved the capital to the east bank of the Chao Phraya River, founding Rattanakosin (Bangkok) for strategic and symbolic reasons. He built the Grand Palace and installed the Emerald Buddha, reinforcing the capital as a sacred and political center. To revive Theravāda Buddhism after the war's disruptions, he sponsored a council to standardize the Tipiṭaka (Buddhist canon) in 1788. His government also codified law in the 1805 "Three Seals Law" compilation.

### Foreign Policy and Warfare
Rama I consolidated and expanded Siam's influence across mainland Southeast Asia. He intervened in Vietnam, supporting Nguyễn Ánh against the Tây Sơn dynasty, though a joint fleet was defeated in 1785. In Cambodia, he deposed and later reinstalled a king, annexing the Siem Reap and Battambang regions. His most significant military challenge was repelling the massive Burmese invasion of 1785–1786, known as the "Nine Armies War," which was the last major Burmese assault on Siam. By his death in 1809, his kingdom's influence stretched from the Shan States to the northern Malay Peninsula.

## Terms
- **Chakri dynasty**: The royal dynasty of Thailand founded by Rama I in 1782, which still reigns today.
- **Rattanakosin**: The name of the new capital kingdom established by Rama I in 1782, now the historical center of Bangkok.
- **Somdet Chao Phraya**: The highest rank attainable by a noble in pre-modern Siam, equivalent in privilege to royalty.
- **Tipiṭaka**: The traditional term for the Buddhist canon; its standardization was a key religious project of Rama I's reign.
- **Three Seals Law**: A comprehensive legal code compiled in 1805 under Rama I, named for the official seals used to authenticate it.
- **Mandala**: A model of pre-modern Southeast Asian political organization, where a central kingdom exerted varying degrees of influence over surrounding tributary states.

## Debates and Open Questions
The exact circumstances of King Taksin's deposition and death in 1782 are debated. Later sources widely report that Rama I executed Taksin, but earlier sources contradict this, with some stating Taksin was consigned to a monastery. The extent to which Rama I's cultural and legal projects were true revivals versus new creations remains a point of scholarly interest.

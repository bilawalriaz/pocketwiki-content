# San José, Costa Rica

## Overview
San José is the capital and largest city of Costa Rica, serving as the nation's political, economic, and transportation hub. Located in the Central Valley, it is notable for its high quality of life, security, and cultural institutions, forming the core of the country's Greater Metropolitan Area.

## Body

### History and Founding
San José was founded in 1736 by order of Cabildo de León to concentrate the scattered population of the Aserrí Valley. A chapel was built near La Boca del Monte, and St. Joseph was chosen as parish patron, giving the city its name. Unlike neighboring Cartago, it lacked a formal city government until the Constitution of Cádiz in 1812. It was first defined as a city in 1813, lost the title in 1814, and regained it in 1820. San José became the capital of Costa Rica in 1823, making it one of the youngest capital cities in Latin America by conception. Economic growth was driven by the tobacco industry and later the coffee boom, which funded landmarks like the National Theater and the Barrio Amon neighborhood.

### Demographics and Government
As of 2022, the canton of San José had an estimated population of 352,381, with a high population density of 7,897.4/km². The population is mostly urban and distributed in peripheral districts, with only 18% living in the central downtown districts. The city is governed by a mayor and a Municipal Council, both elected every four years. The current mayor, elected in 2024, is Luis Diego Miranda Méndez.

### Climate and Education
San José has a tropical wet and dry climate (Köppen Aw) with distinct wet and dry seasons but little temperature variation. It is Costa Rica's educational hub, with high national literacy and school attendance rates. The city is home to the University of Costa Rica (UCR), re-established in 1940, and the UN-mandated University for Peace (1980). Its public and private education system ranges from pre-school to university level.

### Transport and Security
San José is a major transportation hub with internal and national road networks, private bus services, and an inter-urban railway system with three main routes. It is served by Juan Santamaría International Airport (SJO), one of Central America's busiest, and the smaller Tobías Bolaños International Airport. Public taxis are red, and the ride-sharing service Uber operates despite legal disputes. The city is noted for its security, with crime indices reduced significantly by 2012.

### Culture and Landmarks
The city is a cultural center with numerous theaters, including the historic National Theater, and museums like the National Museum of Costa Rica and the Museum of Pre-Columbian Gold. Major parks include La Sabana Metropolitan Park and the National Park. The San José Central Market is a hub for traditional cuisine like *gallo pinto* and *casados*. The city's major football club is Deportivo Saprissa.

## Terms
- **Canton:** An administrative division of Costa Rica, equivalent to a municipality.
- **Greater Metropolitan Area (GAM):** The urban region encompassing San José and neighboring cantons like Alajuela, Heredia, and Cartago.
- **Köppen climate classification Aw:** A tropical climate with distinct wet and dry seasons.
- **Barrio Amon:** A historic neighborhood in San José built in the late 19th century during the coffee golden age.
- **Gallo pinto:** The national dish of Costa Rica, consisting of black beans and white rice.
- **Casado:** A traditional Costa Rican meal of rice, beans, meat, salad, plantains, and tortillas.
- **Soda:** A small, informal restaurant in Costa Rica serving traditional, affordable food.
- **Regidor:** An elected member of a municipal council in Costa Rica.

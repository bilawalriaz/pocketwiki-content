# Surabaya

## Overview
Surabaya is the capital of East Java province and Indonesia's second-largest city, serving as a major historical trading port and economic hub. Its strategic location on the Madura Strait made it a key gateway for the Majapahit Empire and later the Dutch East Indies, shaping its role as a commercial and naval center. The city is also renowned for its pivotal role in the Indonesian National Revolution, earning it the title "City of Heroes."

## Timeline
- **1045** — Kingdom of Janggala formed after Airlangga's abdication; Surabaya area settled.
- **1293** — Victory of Majapahit over Mongols at Surabaya, commemorated as city's founding date.
- **1530** — Surabaya submits to the Demak Sultanate.
- **1625** — Surabaya conquered by the Mataram Sultanate under Sultan Agung.
- **1743** — Dutch East India Company (VOC) takes control from Mataram.
- **1905** — Surabaya receives municipality status.
- **1926** — Designated capital of East Java province.
- **1945** — Battle of Surabaya against British forces; November 10 becomes Heroes' Day.
- **2010-2020** — Tri Rismaharini serves as mayor, winning numerous environmental awards.
- **2018** — Suramadu Bridge completed; city wins Lee Kuan Yew World City Prize.

## Body

### Early History and Pre-Colonial Era
The area was settled in the 10th century by the Kingdom of Janggala, formed in 1045. By the 14th-15th centuries, it was a major port of the Majapahit Empire, documented by Chinese chroniclers. The city's name, derived from Javanese meaning "bravely facing danger," is linked to a prophecy and the 1293 victory over Mongol invaders. Islam took root in the late 15th century, and Surabaya grew into a powerful duchy. It resisted the Demak Sultanate until 1530, became independent after 1546, and was later conquered by the Mataram Sultanate in 1625.

### Colonial Era
The Dutch East India Company took control in 1743, collaborating with local elites. Surabaya became the largest city in the Dutch East Indies by the 18th-19th centuries, serving as a major trading hub, naval base, and center for the plantation economy. It was designated the capital of East Java in 1926. The city was an early center for leftist organizing, with the predecessor to the Communist Party founded there in the 1910s.

### Independence and Modern Era
During World War II, Surabaya was occupied by Japan (1942) and bombed by Allies (1944). After the war, it became a stronghold for Indonesian nationalists. The Battle of Surabaya (1945), triggered by the killing of a British brigadier, became a symbol of national resistance and is commemorated as Heroes' Day. Post-independence, rapid urbanization led to expansion and traffic congestion. The city has grown into a metropolis, known for its cleanliness and green spaces, though it faced terrorist attacks in 2018 and was an early COVID-19 epicenter in 2020.

### Economy and Infrastructure
As a historic port, Surabaya's economy is driven by trade, with principal exports including sugar, tobacco, and coffee. It hosts a large shipyard, naval schools, and is planned to become an Islamic financial center. The Port of Tanjung Perak is Indonesia's second-busiest. Infrastructure includes the Suramadu Bridge to Madura, a network of toll roads, and Juanda International Airport. The city has implemented innovative public transport like the Suroboyo Bus, which accepts plastic waste as payment.

### Demographics and Culture
The population is predominantly Javanese (83%), with significant Madurese (7%) and Chinese (7%) minorities. The local dialect, Suroboyoan, is a mix of Javanese, Indonesian, and Madurese. While Sunni Islam is the majority religion (85%), the city has a diverse religious landscape, including significant Christian communities and historical Hindu and Jewish presence. Culture features arts like Ludruk drama and Remo dance, and the city is a center for football, home to the Persebaya Surabaya club.

## Terms
- **Duchy**: A territory or domain ruled by a duke or duchess; in this context, a semi-autonomous political entity under a local lord.
- **VOC**: The Dutch East India Company (Vereenigde Oostindische Compagnie), a powerful trading company that colonized parts of Asia.
- **Mataram Sultanate**: A major Javanese Islamic kingdom that controlled much of Java before Dutch dominance.
- **Kecamatan**: An administrative district within an Indonesian city or regency.
- **Kelurahan**: An urban village, the lowest administrative division in Indonesian cities.
- **Suroboyoan**: The distinctive local dialect of East Javanese spoken in Surabaya, mixing Javanese, Indonesian, and Madurese.
- **Bonek**: The nickname for supporters of the Persebaya Surabaya football club, from "Bondo Nekat" (equipped by bravery).
- **Adipura**: An Indonesian environmental award for clean and green cities.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The exact origin of the name "Surabaya" is debated, with multiple folk etymologies and a prophecy linked to a battle between a shark and crocodile, which is interpreted by some as foretelling the 1293 Mongol invasion or the 1945 independence war.
- The definition of Surabaya's metropolitan area varies among sources (e.g., OECD, UN, Indonesian government), leading to differing population estimates ranging from approximately 6.8 million to over 12 million.

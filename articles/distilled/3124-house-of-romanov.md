# House of Romanov

## Overview
The House of Romanov was the imperial dynasty that ruled Russia from 1613 until the Russian Revolution in 1917. They transformed Russia from a tsardom into a major European empire and were the last imperial dynasty to rule the country. Their reign ended with the execution of Tsar Nicholas II and his family by Bolshevik revolutionaries.

## Timeline
- **1547** — Anastasia Romanovna marries Ivan IV ("the Terrible"), elevating the family's status.
- **1598** — Death of Feodor I ends the Rurik dynasty, leading to the Time of Troubles.
- **1613** — Michael Romanov is elected tsar by the Zemsky Sobor, establishing the Romanov dynasty.
- **1721** — Peter I proclaims the Russian Empire and transforms it into a great power.
- **1762** — Elizabeth dies childless; her nephew Peter III (House of Holstein-Gottorp) ascends the throne.
- **1796** — Paul I enacts the Pauline Laws, establishing strict succession rules.
- **1861** — Alexander II emancipates the serfs.
- **1881** — Alexander II is assassinated.
- **1917** — Nicholas II abdicates, ending Romanov rule after 304 years.
- **1918** — Nicholas II and his immediate family are executed by Bolsheviks in Yekaterinburg.

## Body

### Origins and Rise to Power
The Romanovs descended from the boyar (highest-ranking noble) class under the earlier Rurik dynasty. Their fortunes rose when Anastasia Romanovna married Ivan the Terrible in 1547. After the Rurik line ended in 1598, a succession crisis known as the Time of Troubles ensued. The family's leader, Feodor Nikitich Romanov (later Patriarch Filaret), was persecuted by Boris Godunov. Following political turmoil, his 16-year-old son Michael was elected tsar in 1613 as a compromise candidate, founding the new dynasty.

### Dynastic Crises and Peter the Great
Michael was succeeded by his son Alexei. After Alexei's death, a struggle ensued between his children from two marriages. This culminated in the reign of Peter I (the Great), who ruled from 1682 to 1725. Peter modernized Russia, expanded its territory, and proclaimed it an empire. His reforms and wars established Russia as a major power. The direct male Romanov line ended with Peter II in 1730, leading to a series of successions among female-line relatives.

### The Holstein-Gottorp-Romanov Era
After Empress Elizabeth died childless in 1762, her nephew Peter III, from the German House of Holstein-Gottorp, took the throne. Though he adopted the Romanov name, later rulers are sometimes called Holstein-Gottorp-Romanovs. His wife, Catherine the Great, overthrew him and ruled until 1796. Her son Paul I established strict succession laws (the Pauline Laws) requiring Orthodox faith and equal birth for spouses. The dynasty continued through Alexander I, Nicholas I, Alexander II, Alexander III, and finally Nicholas II.

### Downfall and Execution
The February Revolution of 1917 forced Nicholas II to abdicate. He and his family were imprisoned and later moved to Yekaterinburg. On the night of 16–17 July 1918, they were executed by Bolsheviks. Other extended family members were also killed in 1918. The remains of Nicholas, Alexandra, and three children were identified in 1991 via DNA analysis. The remains of two other children were found separately in 2007. The family was canonized as passion-bearers by the Russian Orthodox Church in 2000.

### Exiles and Contemporary Claims
After the revolution, 47 Romanov survivors went into exile. Dowager Empress Maria Feodorovna escaped to Denmark. The headship of the defunct imperial house was claimed in 1924 by Grand Duke Kirill Vladimirovich. Today, the succession is disputed between Maria Vladimirovna (who claims through her father, Vladimir Kirillovich) and other branches, such as the Romanov Family Association, which does not advance dynastic claims. Another claimant is Prince Karl Emich of Leiningen.

## Terms
- **Boyar**: The highest rank in the Russian nobility before the 18th century.
- **Zemsky Sobor**: A representative assembly of Russian society convened to advise the tsar or elect a new one.
- **Time of Troubles**: A period of political crisis and foreign intervention in Russia from 1598 to 1613.
- **Tsar**: The title for a Russian monarch, derived from "Caesar."
- **Tsesarevich**: The title for the heir apparent to the Russian throne.
- **Pauline Laws**: The strict succession laws enacted by Emperor Paul I in 1797, establishing semi-Salic primogeniture.
- **Morganatic marriage**: A marriage between people of unequal social rank, which prevents the passage of the higher spouse's titles and privileges to the lower spouse and any children.
- **Passion-bearer**: A saint in the Eastern Orthodox Church who dies in faith at the hand of murderers, not as a martyr for the faith itself.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Identity of the Missing Remains**: Experts debate which daughter's remains were missing from the main 1991 grave (Anastasia or Maria) until the 2007 discovery.
- **Succession Dispute**: The headship of the imperial house is contested between Maria Vladimirovna and other claimants, based on disagreements over the validity of marriages under the Pauline Laws.
- **Dynastic Legitimacy**: The Romanov Family Association, composed of most male-line descendants, officially does not advance dynastic claims, supporting instead whatever government the Russian people choose.

# San Marino

## Overview
San Marino is a microstate and the world's oldest extant sovereign republic, founded in AD 301. Located as an enclave within Italy, it is renowned for its unique constitutional system of dual heads of state and its long history of maintaining independence despite its small size.

## Timeline
- **c. AD 301** — Traditional founding by stonemason Saint Marinus on Monte Titano.
- **1291** — San Marino's tax exemption from Montefeltro is legally recognized.
- **1463** — Final territorial expansion; borders remain unchanged since.
- **1503** — Brief occupation by Cesare Borgia, later reversed.
- **1739** — Occupation by Papal legate Cardinal Alberoni; independence restored in 1740.
- **1862** — Convention of Friendship signed with the Kingdom of Italy.
- **1865** — First currently-existing state to abolish the death penalty.
- **1923-1943** — Rule by the Sammarinese Fascist Party (PFS).
- **1944** — Allied bombing and brief German occupation during World War II.
- **1945-1957** — Communist-Socialist coalition government in power.
- **1988** — Becomes a member of the Council of Europe.
- **1992** — Becomes a member of the United Nations.
- **2008** — Historic Centre and Mount Titano designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

## Body

### History and Government
San Marino claims to be the world's oldest republic, founded by Saint Marinus, a stonemason fleeing Diocletianic persecution. The earliest historical evidence for a community dates to the 5th or 6th century. The republic's borders were finalized in 1463. It has survived numerous threats, including occupations by Cesare Borgia (1503) and a Papal legate (1739), and navigated the Napoleonic era and Italian unification by maintaining its independence.

Its government is a parliamentary representative democratic republic with a rare constitutional structure. The legislature is the unicameral Grand and General Council (60 members). Every six months, the Council elects two Captains Regent from opposing parties to serve as joint heads of state with equal powers. This system, derived from Roman Republic customs, is mirrored only by Switzerland's Federal Council. The Statutes of 1600 form the core of its constitutional framework.

### Economy and Conventions
San Marino has a developed economy based on finance, industry, services, and tourism, ranking among the world's wealthiest by GDP (PPP) per capita. It uses the euro as its currency but is not an EU member. Key industries include banking, electronics, and ceramics. Tourism contributes over 22% of GDP.

Economic activity is governed by conventions with Italy. San Marino is forbidden from cultivating tobacco or producing goods subject to Italian state monopolies. All imports must transit through Italy. In exchange, Italy provides an annual stipend of salt, tobacco, and matches. San Marino has a competitive tax system with a 17% corporate rate, lower than Italy's, making it a business hub.

### Demographics and Culture
The population is approximately 33,559 (2026), with Italian as the official language. The Sammarinese dialect of Romagnol is also spoken. The country is predominantly Catholic. San Marino's culture is closely linked to Italy but distinct, symbolized by the Three Towers on Monte Titano. The historic centre and Monte Titano are a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

The country has a rich tradition in music and hosts museums dedicated to its history, art, and stamps—a significant revenue source. Football is the most popular sport. San Marino became the smallest country to win an Olympic medal at the 2020 Summer Olympics.

## Terms
*   **Enclave:** A territory entirely surrounded by the territory of another state.
*   **Captains Regent:** The two joint heads of state of San Marino, elected every six months by the Grand and General Council.
*   **Grand and General Council:** The unicameral legislature of San Marino, consisting of 60 members elected every five years.
*   **Castello:** The administrative division of San Marino, equivalent to a municipality.
*   **Statutes of 1600:** The core written governing documents of San Marino's constitutional framework.
*   **Sammarinese lira:** The former currency of San Marino, pegged to the Italian lira before the adoption of the euro.
*   **Euro:** The official currency of San Marino, used by arrangement with the EU despite non-membership.
*   **UNESCO World Heritage Site:** A landmark or area with legal protection for having cultural, historical, or other significance, designated by the United Nations.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   The traditional founding date of AD 301 is based on accounts recorded centuries later and is considered a mixture of "fables and miracles," though a monastic community is evidenced by the 5th or 6th century.
*   The exact nature and extent of Papal control after San Marino sought formal protection in 1631 is unclear, as it "never amounted to a de facto Papal control."
*   The future of San Marino's relationship with the European Union remains a topic, as it uses the euro but is not a member.

# Andaman Sea

## Overview
The Andaman Sea is a marginal sea of the northeastern Indian Ocean, bordered by Myanmar, Thailand, and Indonesia. It is a vital region for fisheries, transportation, and tourism, but its ecology and human activities are significantly shaped by its complex geology, monsoon-driven climate, and high seismic activity, including the devastating 2004 tsunami.

## Timeline
- **c. 3–4 million years ago** — Formation of the Andaman Sea basin begins as a back-arc basin.
- **1797, 1833, 1861** — Major earthquakes (magnitude ≥8.4) recorded in the region.
- **1980s** — Extensive removal of Thai mangrove forests for shrimp farming.
- **1991** — Barren Island volcano resumes activity after ~200 years.
- **1998–1999** — Fatal naval conflicts between Myanmar and Thailand over fishing rights.
- **26 December 2004** — A magnitude 9.3 megathrust earthquake triggers a massive tsunami, killing ~280,000 people and severely damaging coastal infrastructure.
- **13 May 2008** — Barren Island volcano begins an eruption that continues as of the source's writing.
- **2022–2024** — Approximately 33,000 Rohingya refugees flee by sea, with high mortality rates.

## Body

### Geography and Geology
The Andaman Sea is bounded by the Andaman and Nicobar Islands to the west, separating it from the Bay of Bengal. Its floor is defined by the boundary between the Burma and Sunda tectonic plates, which were once part of the Eurasian Plate. This plate boundary creates high seismic activity, including the 2004 earthquake where a 1,300–1,600 km section of the boundary shifted, uplifting the seafloor and generating a tsunami. The basin is shallow on its northern and eastern shelves due to silt from the Irrawaddy River, while the western and central areas are 900–3,000 meters deep. Water exchange with the Bay of Bengal occurs through key straits like the Preparis Channel, Ten Degree Channel, and Great Channel. Barren Island, east of Great Andaman, is the Indian subcontinent's only active volcano, formed by the subduction of the Indian Plate.

### Climate and Ocean Dynamics
The sea's climate is dominated by reversing monsoon winds. North-easterlies prevail from November to February, switching to strong south-westerlies from May to September. This drives seasonal changes in currents, precipitation (up to 3,000 mm/year in summer), and sea surface temperature (26–29°C). Ocean dynamics are influenced by remote forcing from equatorial Wyrtki jets, which generate downwelling Kelvin waves that propagate into the basin, causing sea level to rise between April and November. Water transport through the straits is a primary driver of the basin's sea surface height.

### Ecology
Coastal areas feature mangrove forests and seagrass meadows, which are critical habitats and nurseries for fish. Significant portions of Thai mangroves were lost to shrimp farming in the 1980s and to the 2004 tsunami. The sea hosts diverse fauna, including ~280 edible fish species, vulnerable dugongs (~150 individuals), dolphins, and four species of sea turtles. Coral reefs cover an estimated 117 km², but only 6.4% are in ideal condition.

### Human Activities
The sea is a major fishing ground; in 2000, Thailand harvested 710,000 tonnes of fish, with 19% of its total marine catch coming from the Andaman Sea. Overfishing is severe, with pelagic and demersal species overfished by 333% and 245% respectively in 1991. Fishing disputes have led to naval conflicts between Myanmar and Thailand. Tourism is a key industry, centered on coral reefs and islands, though damaged by the 2004 tsunami. The sea is also a dangerous migration route for Rohingya refugees, with hundreds dying or going missing in recent years.

## Terms
*   **Marginal Sea:** A sea partially enclosed by islands, archipelagos, or peninsulas, like the Andaman Sea.
*   **Back-arc Basin:** A geologic basin formed behind a volcanic arc at a convergent plate boundary, which describes the origin of the Andaman Sea.
*   **Megathrust Earthquake:** An earthquake occurring at subduction zones where one tectonic plate is being forced under another, capable of generating massive tsunamis.
*   **Monsoon:** A seasonal prevailing wind in the region of South and Southeast Asia, characterized by a reversal in wind direction.
*   **Kelvin Wave:** A wave in the ocean or atmosphere that is trapped near a boundary (like a coastline) and propagates along it.
*   **Exclusive Economic Zone (EEZ):** A maritime zone extending 200 nautical miles from a country's coast, where it has sovereign rights over resources.
*   **Isobath:** A line on a map or chart connecting points of equal underwater depth.

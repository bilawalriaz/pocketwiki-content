# Second Opium War

## Overview
The Second Opium War (1856–1860) was a conflict between the United Kingdom and France against the Qing dynasty of China. It was the second major conflict in the Opium Wars, fought over the right to import opium to China, and resulted in a second defeat for the Qing and the forced legalization of the opium trade. The war caused many Chinese officials to believe that conflicts with Western powers were part of a looming national crisis.

## Timeline
- **8 October 1856** — Qing officials seize the British-registered ship *Arrow* and arrest its Chinese crew.
- **23 October 1856** — British gunboats shell Canton (Guangzhou) after the Viceroy refuses to release all sailors.
- **December 1857** — Anglo-French forces storm and capture Canton.
- **May 1858** — Allied forces capture the Taku Forts and threaten Beijing, leading to the Treaty of Tientsin.
- **June 1859** — Qing forces repel a British attempt to re-take the Taku Forts.
- **Summer 1860** — A larger Anglo-French force captures the Taku Forts and advances on Beijing.
- **September 1860** — British diplomat Harry Parkes and others are captured and tortured by Qing authorities.
- **18 October 1856** — British troops burn the Old Summer Palace (Yuanmingyuan) in retaliation.
- **24 October 1860** — Prince Gong signs the Convention of Peking, ending the war.

## Body

### Causes and Outbreak
The war followed the First Opium War (1839–1842), whose treaty settlement failed to satisfy British goals for expanded trade and diplomatic relations. In the 1850s, Western powers sought to expand their privileges. Citing its most-favored-nation status, Britain demanded renegotiation of the Treaty of Nanking, seeking to open all of China to trade, legalize the opium trade, and establish a diplomatic presence in Beijing. The immediate trigger was the "Arrow Incident" of October 1856, when Qing officials seized the British-registered ship *Arrow* and arrested its crew. After the Viceroy of Liangguang, Ye Mingchen, refused to release all sailors or apologize, British forces bombarded Canton.

### The War and Foreign Intervention
France joined the British action, citing the execution of a French missionary, Auguste Chapdelaine, in Guangxi province. The United States and Russia sent envoys but did not join the military alliance; the U.S. was involved in a minor concurrent conflict but maintained official neutrality. After initial British attacks on Canton in 1856–57, a combined Anglo-French force captured the city in December 1857. They then sailed north, capturing the Taku Forts near Tianjin in May 1858 and threatening Beijing. This forced the Qing court to sign the Treaty of Tientsin, which opened 11 new ports to trade, allowed foreign embassies in Beijing, and granted indemnities.

### Second Phase and Conclusion
The Xianfeng Emperor refused to ratify the treaty, and Qing forces under General Sengge Rinchen successfully repelled a British attempt to re-take the Taku Forts in June 1859. In 1860, a larger Anglo-French force returned, captured the forts, and advanced on Beijing. The capture and torture of British diplomat Harry Parkes and other prisoners prompted Lord Elgin to order the burning of the Old Summer Palace as punishment directed at the emperor. The Xianfeng Emperor fled, and his brother, Prince Gong, signed the Convention of Peking in October 1860, ratifying the earlier treaty and making further concessions.

### Aftermath and Consequences
The Convention of Peking legalized the opium trade, ceded the Kowloon Peninsula to Britain, and increased indemnities. China also ceded over 1.5 million square kilometers of territory to Russia through the Treaty of Aigun (1858) and the Convention of Peking. The Qing government, humiliated and financially strained, was forced to focus on internal threats like the Taiping Rebellion. The defeat spurred the Self-Strengthening Movement, an early modernization effort. The opium trade was intensely criticized by figures like British MP William Ewart Gladstone, who called it "most infamous and atrocious."

## Terms
- **Opium Wars**: Two 19th-century conflicts (1839–42, 1856–60) between China and Western powers, primarily Britain, over the opium trade and diplomatic relations.
- **Treaty of Nanking (1842)**: The treaty ending the First Opium War, which ceded Hong Kong Island to Britain, opened five ports, and imposed an indemnity.
- **Treaty of Tientsin (1858)**: A set of treaties ending the first phase of the Second Opium War, opening new ports, allowing foreign embassies in Beijing, and legalizing opium trade.
- **Convention of Peking (1860)**: The treaty that ended the Second Opium War, ratifying the Treaty of Tientsin and ceding Kowloon to Britain.
- **Arrow Incident**: The seizure of the British-registered ship *Arrow* by Qing officials in 1856, which served as the immediate cause of the war.
- **Taku Forts**: A series of coastal forts near Tianjin guarding the approach to Beijing, the site of three major battles during the war.
- **Old Summer Palace (Yuanmingyuan)**: A vast complex of palaces and gardens near Beijing, looted and burned by Anglo-French forces in 1860.
- **Unequal Treaties**: A term for the 19th-century treaties imposed on China by Western powers, which granted extraterritoriality, indemnities, and trade concessions.
- **Self-Strengthening Movement**: A late Qing dynasty reform movement (c. 1861–1895) aimed at adopting Western technology and military methods to preserve Chinese institutions.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The primary justification for British intervention—the *Arrow* incident—is debated. The ship's British registration had expired, and its crew were Chinese, making the casus belli questionable.
- The morality of burning the Old Summer Palace remains a point of contention. Lord Elgin claimed it was a targeted punishment for the emperor, but it resulted in the destruction of irreplaceable cultural heritage.
- The role and motivations of the United States and Russia, who sent envoys but avoided direct military alliance, are analyzed in the context of their own imperial ambitions in the region.

# Brittany

## Overview
Brittany is a peninsula and historical region in northwest France with a distinct Celtic cultural identity. It was an independent kingdom and duchy before uniting with France in 1532, and its history of migration, political autonomy, and cultural revival makes it a key example of regional identity within a modern nation-state.

## Timeline
- **c. 5000 BC** — Construction of major megalithic monuments like the Cairn of Barnenez begins.
- **51 BC** — Region becomes part of the Roman Republic after the Gallic Wars.
- **5th–6th centuries** — Waves of Britons from Great Britain settle in Armorica, giving the region its name.
- **845–851** — Nominoe unifies Brittany into a single kingdom.
- **937** — Alan II expels the Vikings and establishes the Duchy of Brittany.
- **1066** — Breton lords participate in the Norman conquest of England.
- **1488** — Brittany loses the Mad War against France, leading to loss of independence.
- **1532** — Formal union of Brittany with the French Crown under Francis I.
- **1790** — French Revolution divides Brittany into five départements.
- **1956** — Modern Region of Brittany is legally reconstituted, excluding Loire-Atlantique.

## Body

### Prehistory to Roman Era
Brittany has been inhabited since the Lower Palaeolithic. The Neolithic period saw the creation of major megalithic sites, making it a "core area" of megalithic culture. During the Gallic era, it was home to five Celtic tribes, including the Veneti and Curiosolitae, who had strong trade ties with Insular Celts. After Roman conquest in 51 BC, towns were redeveloped and roads built, but the population remained largely rural. The region suffered attacks and economic collapse in the 3rd century AD.

### Migration and Early Medieval Kingdoms
From the late 4th century, Britons from modern-day Devon and Cornwall emigrated to Armorica, fleeing Anglo-Saxon invasions. This migration, possibly led by figures like Conan Meriadoc, established the Breton language and culture. By the early medieval era, three kingdoms (Domnonea, Cornouaille, Broërec) emerged. Nominoe unified them into the Kingdom of Brittany in the 9th century. The kingdom faced Viking attacks in the 10th century, losing eastern territories. Alan II later expelled the Vikings and established the Duchy of Brittany, paying homage to the French king.

### Duchy and Union with France
The Duchy of Brittany was often caught between English and French alliances, especially during the Hundred Years' War. Internal divisions weakened it, leading to defeat in the Mad War (1488). Duke Francis II's daughter, Anne, was forced to marry French kings, and the duchy was formally united with France in 1532, though it retained some privileges. Brittany experienced an economic golden age from the 15th to 18th centuries, benefiting from maritime trade, but later suffered from centralization and British blockades.

### Revolution to Modern Era
The French Revolution abolished the duchy in 1789, dividing Brittany into five départements and ending its special privileges. The 19th century saw economic recession, emigration, and the decline of the Breton language due to Francization policies. The 20th century brought World War II devastation and, in 1956, the reconstitution of the Brittany region—though without Loire-Atlantique. A cultural revival from the 1960s onward promoted Breton language and identity, alongside movements for greater autonomy or reunification.

## Terms
- **Armorica**: The Roman-era name for the region that included modern Brittany.
- **Breton**: A Celtic language closely related to Cornish and Welsh, brought to Armorica by migrants from Britain.
- **Gallo**: A Romance language of the Langues d'oïl branch, spoken in eastern Brittany.
- **Duchy of Brittany**: The feudal state that existed from 937 until its union with France in 1532.
- **Parish close**: A distinctive Breton architectural feature with a decorated church surrounded by a walled churchyard.
- **Fest-noz**: A traditional Breton popular feast with music and dance.
- **Gwenn ha Du**: The modern black-and-white flag of Brittany, designed in 1923.
- **Tro Breizh**: An old pilgrimage circuit visiting the graves of Brittany's seven founder saints.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Reunification**: There is ongoing debate about reincorporating Loire-Atlantique into the Brittany region. Polls show majority support (58% in Brittany, 62% in Loire-Atlantique), but obstacles include economic imbalance and the potential destabilization of the Pays de la Loire region.
- **Regional Identity**: Polls indicate a complex identity, with many inhabitants feeling both Breton and French. Support for greater political autonomy exists, but independence has minimal backing (around 4.6%).
- **Language Revival**: While Breton is classified as "severely endangered," immersion schools (Diwan) and bilingual education have increased the number of young speakers. Gallo, however, remains threatened and lacks widespread promotion.

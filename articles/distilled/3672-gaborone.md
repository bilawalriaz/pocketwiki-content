# Gaborone

## Overview
Gaborone is the capital and largest city of Botswana, serving as the nation's political, economic, and administrative center. Planned in the mid-1960s to be a neutral capital for the newly independent country, it has grown from a small town into one of the fastest-growing cities in the world, facing challenges related to rapid urbanization and infrastructure.

## Timeline
- **c. 1880** — Tlokwa people settle in the area, calling it Moshaweng.
- **1890** — Cecil John Rhodes selects Gaberones for a colonial fort.
- **1964** — Modern town planning and construction begins.
- **1965** — Capital of Bechuanaland Protectorate moves from Mafeking to Gaberones.
- **30 September 1966** — Bechuanaland becomes independent as Botswana.
- **1969** — City officially changes its name from Gaberones to Gaborone.
- **1975** — President Seretse Khama rezones the Naledi area to low-income housing.
- **Mid-1980s** — South African military raids on Gaborone and other border towns.
- **1994** — Post-election riots occur due to high unemployment.
- **2000** — International Law Enforcement Academy (ILEA) established.
- **2005** — Three Dikgosi Monument inaugurated.
- **2022** — Census records city population at 246,325.

## Body

### History and Founding
The area along the Notwane River has been inhabited for centuries. The Tlokwa people settled there around 1880, and the colonial name "Gaberones" derived from Chief Gaborone of the Tlokwa. The modern city was founded in 1964 to serve as the capital for the soon-to-be-independent Bechuanaland Protectorate. It was chosen over Lobatse due to its central location, proximity to fresh water and the railway, and lack of association with any specific indigenous group. Construction began in mid-1964, and the capital officially moved from Mafeking in 1965. The city was planned using Garden city principles, with a layout likened to a "brandy glass."

### Urban Growth and Challenges
Gaborone was initially designed for 20,000 people but experienced explosive growth, reaching 138,000 by 1992 and over 246,000 by 2022. This rapid expansion, fueled by migration, led to severe housing shortages and the proliferation of illegal settlements like Naledi. The government responded with initiatives such as building "New Naledi" and rezoning areas for housing. The city also faced external conflicts, including cross-border raids from South Africa in the 1980s and internal social unrest in the 1990s.

### Geography and Climate
Gaborone is located in southeastern Botswana, between Kgale and Oodi Hills near the Notwane River, about 15 km from the South African border. It has a hot semi-arid climate (Köppen BSh) with sunny conditions, hot summers, and cool winters. Rainfall is scanty and erratic, mostly occurring between October and April. The city has experienced periodic flooding, notably in 2000, 2001, and 2006.

### Economy and Infrastructure
As the national economic hub, Gaborone hosts the headquarters of major financial institutions, including the Bank of Botswana and the Botswana Stock Exchange, as well as the Southern African Development Community (SADC). The city is a center for the diamond industry, with Debswana's sorting house (Orapa House) and a new Diamond Technology Park. Infrastructure includes the Sir Seretse Khama International Airport, a railway line bisecting the city, and major highways. Water is supplied primarily by the Gaborone Dam, the second-largest in Botswana.

### Governance and Society
Gaborone is governed by the Gaborone City Council, though its authority is limited as Botswana is a unitary state. The city is the seat of the national government, with the Parliament and key ministries located in the Government Enclave. Demographically, it has a high population growth rate (3.4%) and a relatively low average household size. The city is religiously diverse, with Christian churches predominating, and is home to several universities and colleges, including the main campus of the University of Botswana.

### Culture and Recreation
Cultural institutions include the National Museum and Art Gallery (opened 1968) and the annual Maitisong Festival. The city is the setting for popular book series like *The No. 1 Ladies' Detective Agency*. Recreational areas include the Gaborone Game Reserve (600 hectares), the Gaborone Dam for water sports, and nearby Kgale Hill. The Mokolodi Nature Reserve, south of the city, is involved in wildlife conservation projects.

## Terms
- **Garden city principles**: An urban planning model emphasizing green spaces, pedestrian walkways, and a balance between urban and natural environments.
- **Government Enclave**: The area west of Gaborone's Main Mall containing key national government buildings like the National Assembly.
- **Main Mall**: A central, car-free commercial and administrative strip in Gaborone, running east-west.
- **Naledi**: An area originally an industrial zone that became a major low-income housing settlement due to rapid urban growth.
- **SADC**: Southern African Development Community, an inter-governmental organization headquartered in Gaborone to foster regional economic cooperation.
- **Setswana**: The principal language of the Tswana people and one of the main languages spoken in Gaborone.
- **Three Dikgosi Monument**: A landmark featuring statues of three chiefs who traveled to Britain in the 1890s to protect Bechuanaland's independence.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The original city planners are cited as a cause for Gaborone's development problems, particularly regarding housing and infrastructure not keeping pace with growth.
- There has been controversy over the high cost (P12,000,000) and an incorrect inscription date on the Three Dikgosi Monument, which was built by a North Korean company.
- The Gaborone City Council has been criticized for its limited authority and closed elections, with mayors advocating for greater local autonomy.

# Romania

## Overview
Romania is a developed, high-income country in Central and Southeastern Europe on the lower Danube and northwestern Black Sea coast. It is an EU and NATO member. The modern state formed in 1859 via the unification of Moldavia and Wallachia, with a history marked by Roman rule, medieval principalities, foreign domination, and a 20th-century transition from monarchy to communism and democracy.

## Timeline
- **c. 106 AD** — Roman Emperor Trajan conquers Dacia, creating Roman Dacia.
- **1330** — Basarab I secures Wallachia's independence.
- **1360** — Moldavia achieves autonomy under Bogdan I.
- **1859** — Moldavia and Wallachia unite under Alexandru Ioan Cuza.
- **1877** — Romania proclaims independence from the Ottoman Empire.
- **1881** — Romania becomes a kingdom under Carol I.
- **1918** — Transylvania, Bukovina, and Bessarabia unite with Romania, forming "Greater Romania."
- **1940** — Romania cedes territories to the USSR, Hungary, and Bulgaria.
- **1944** — King Michael I overthrows the pro-Axis government, switching Romania to the Allied side.
- **1947** — King Michael I abdicates; Romania becomes a socialist republic.
- **1989** — The Romanian Revolution ends communist rule.
- **2007** — Romania joins the European Union.

## Body

### Etymology and Early History
"Romania" derives from the local term for Romanian, from Latin *romanus*. The oldest Romanian document is the 1521 "Letter of Neacșu." Human remains in Peștera cu Oase date to c. 40,000 years ago. The region saw Neolithic agriculture, the Cucuteni–Trypillia culture, and the Dacian kingdom under Burebista. The Romans conquered Dacia in 106 AD, initiating Romanisation. Scholars debate whether modern Romanians descend from Dacians and Roman colonists (the Daco-Roman continuity theory) or from later migrations.

### Medieval and Early Modern Period
After Rome, the region saw migrations by Goths, Huns, and Slavs. The First Bulgarian Empire and Kingdom of Hungary exerted influence. Wallachia and Moldavia emerged in the 14th century, often defending against the Ottomans. Transylvania developed as a Hungarian province, later a semi-autonomous principality under Ottoman suzerainty. The 18th century saw increasing Habsburg and Russian influence, with the Phanariot era beginning in the Danubian Principalities.

### Unification and the Kingdom
The modern state was created by the 1859 union of Moldavia and Wallachia. After Cuza's abdication, Carol I became prince and later king, securing full independence in 1877. After WWI, the union of Transylvania, Bukovina, and Bessarabia created "Greater Romania." The interwar period saw democratic gains undermined by King Carol II's dictatorship. In WWII, Romania lost territories and allied with Nazi Germany before switching sides in 1944.

### Communist and Post-Communist Era
After WWII, a Soviet-backed communist regime was established, culminating in Nicolae Ceaușescu's authoritarian rule. The 1989 revolution ended communism. The post-1989 period has been characterized by a transition to democracy and a market economy, marked by economic growth, EU accession in 2007, and ongoing challenges with corruption and political instability.

### Geography and Climate
Romania is Europe's 12th-largest country, dominated by the Carpathian Mountains. It has a continental climate with four seasons. The country is rich in biodiversity, with significant forest cover and the Danube Delta, a major wetland and World Heritage Site.

### Government and Politics
Romania is a unitary semi-presidential republic. The President is head of state, and the Prime Minister leads the government. Legislative power is held by a bicameral Parliament. The country is divided into 41 counties and Bucharest. It is a member of the EU, NATO, and other international organizations.

### Economy
Romania has a high-income, service-based economy. It is a net exporter of automotive parts and has a significant IT sector. The country experienced rapid growth in the 2000s but was hit by the 2008 financial crisis. It uses the leu and plans to adopt the euro.

### Demographics and Culture
The population is predominantly Romanian (89.33%), with significant Hungarian and Roma minorities. Romanian is a Romance language. The majority religion is Eastern Orthodox Christianity. The culture features contributions in literature, music, and film, with notable traditions in cuisine and holidays.

## Terms
*   **Daco-Roman continuity theory:** The theory that modern Romanians descend from Dacians and Roman colonists in Roman Dacia.
*   **Phanariots:** Greek merchants from Istanbul's Phanar district appointed by the Ottomans to rule the Danubian Principalities in the 18th–19th centuries.
*   **Greater Romania:** The Kingdom of Romania after 1918, incorporating Transylvania, Bukovina, and Bessarabia.
*   **Act of 23 August:** The 1944 coup led by King Michael I that overthrew the pro-Axis government and aligned Romania with the Allies.
*   **National Salvation Front (FSN):** The political organization from the 1989 revolution that led Romania's initial transition to democracy.
*   **National Anticorruption Directorate (DNA):** An agency established in 2002 to prosecute high-level corruption.
*   **Autocephalous:** A term for an Eastern Orthodox Church that is self-governing, like the Romanian Orthodox Church.
*   **Flat tax:** A single-rate tax system adopted by Romania in 2005.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Origin of the Romanians:** The central debate is between the Daco-Roman continuity theory (an unbroken Latin-speaking presence north of the Danube) and theories of a later migration from the Balkans in the 11th century.
*   **Reliability of medieval sources:** The accuracy of chronicles like the *Gesta Hungarorum* regarding early medieval Transylvania's ethnic composition is debated.
*   **Democratic health:** International assessments rate Romania as a "flawed democracy," with debates about the effectiveness of anti-corruption efforts and political stability.

# Lombards

## Overview
The Lombards (Latin: Langobardi) were a Germanic people who migrated from Northern Europe to the Italian Peninsula, where they established a kingdom that lasted from 568 to 774 AD. Their conquest and subsequent rule significantly shaped the political, cultural, and linguistic landscape of medieval Italy, leaving a lasting legacy in the region of Lombardy.

## Timeline
- **c. 1st century AD** — First recorded by Roman historians (Strabo, Velleius Paterculus) living on the Lower Elbe.
- **487/488 AD** — Arrive in "Rugiland" (near Vienna) after the Rugii are defeated by Odoacer.
- **551/552 AD** — Lombard king Audoin defeats the Gepid leader Thurisind.
- **567 AD** — King Alboin destroys the Gepid kingdom with Avar help.
- **568 AD** — Alboin leads the Lombard migration into northeastern Italy.
- **572 AD** — Pavia falls, becoming the first capital of the Lombard Kingdom.
- **584 AD** — End of the "Rule of the Dukes"; Authari elected king.
- **636-652 AD** — King Rothari issues the Edictum Rothari, codifying Lombard law.
- **712-744 AD** — Reign of King Liutprand, the kingdom's zenith.
- **774 AD** — King Desiderius is defeated by Charlemagne; the Lombard Kingdom is annexed by the Franks.
- **1078 AD** — The Norman Robert Guiscard conquers the Principality of Salerno, ending Lombard rule in Italy.

## Body

### Origins and Migration
The Lombards originated as a Suebian tribe near the Lower Elbe (modern Germany). Roman sources from the 1st and 2nd centuries AD place them there. By the 5th century, they had migrated to the Middle Danube region. Legendary accounts, such as the *Origo Gentis Langobardorum*, describe their journey from a northern island (possibly Scandinavia) and a name change from "Winnili" to "Langobardi" ("Long-beards") after a divine trick involving the gods Odin and Frigg. In the Danube region, they fought neighboring tribes, culminating in the destruction of the Gepids in 567 AD.

### Conquest and Kingdom in Italy (568–774)
In 568, King Alboin led the Lombards, along with allied peoples like Saxons and Gepids, into Italy. The peninsula was depopulated after the Gothic War, and the invasion was largely unopposed. They conquered most of northern Italy, making Pavia the capital, and established duchies in central and southern Italy, including Spoleto and Benevento. The kingdom was initially divided into 36 duchies, leading to instability. After Alboin's murder in 572, a period of ducal rule ended with the election of King Authari in 584. The kingdom saw religious conflict between Arian Christians, pagans, and Catholics. Under King Liutprand (r. 712–744), the kingdom reached its peak, annexing the Byzantine Exarchate of Ravenna. In 774, King Desiderius was defeated by the Frankish king Charlemagne, who adopted the title "King of the Lombards."

### Later Lombard States in Southern Italy
After the northern kingdom fell, the Lombard Duchy of Benevento remained independent. It was later divided into the Principalities of Benevento, Salerno, and Capua. These states navigated complex alliances with the Byzantine Empire, the Papacy, and the Franks, and fought against Arab raiders. The last great Lombard prince, Guaimar IV of Salerno (d. 1052), ruled a vast territory. Lombard rule in the south ended with the Norman conquests of the 11th century, culminating in the fall of Salerno in 1078.

### Society and Culture
Lombard society was hierarchical, with a noble class, free warriors (*exercitales*), and unfree serfs. Their language, Lombardic, was an Upper German dialect that influenced Italian with words like *guerra* (war) and *guardia* (guard). Initially pagans worshipping gods like Odin, they gradually converted to Christianity, first to Arianism and later to Catholicism, a process completed by the 8th century. Lombard art evolved from portable Germanic metalwork to include architecture and frescoes influenced by Byzantine and Roman styles, as seen in the Church of Santa Sofia in Benevento.

## Terms
- **Edictum Rothari**: The first written compilation of Lombard law, issued in 643 AD, which applied only to Lombards, not their subjects.
- **Gastald**: A royal official or emissary appointed by the Lombard king to administer justice and manage estates in the duchies.
- **Fara**: The fundamental social and military unit of Lombard society, a clan or family group.
- **Langobardia Major**: The northern part of the Lombard Kingdom in Italy, centered on Pavia.
- **Langobardia Minor**: The semi-independent Lombard duchies in southern Italy, primarily Benevento.
- **Exarchate of Ravenna**: The Byzantine province in Italy that was a major rival to the Lombard Kingdom until its annexation by Liutprand.
- **Beneventan Rite**: A distinct Christian liturgical rite used in the Duchy of Benevento, related to the Ambrosian Rite.
- **Winnili**: The original name of the Lombard tribe before their legendary name change.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The exact route and timeline of the Lombard migration from the Elbe to the Danube are unclear, as the only accounts are legendary texts written centuries later.
- The historicity of the legendary name-change story involving Odin and Frigg is debated; it may be a later etiological myth.
- The precise nature and extent of Lombard religious practices during their pagan and Arian Christian periods are reconstructed from limited and often hostile sources.
- The degree of genetic continuity between the Lombards in Central Europe and those who settled in Italy is supported by recent studies but the scale of the migration population remains a subject of research.

# Kanpur

## Overview
Kanpur is a major city in Uttar Pradesh, India, founded by Rajput ruler Hindu Singh Chandel. It grew from a collection of villages into a critical British military and commercial hub on the Ganges, playing a pivotal role during the 1857 Indian Rebellion. Today, it is one of India's most populous metropolitan areas and a significant economic and educational center.

## Body

### History and Founding
Kanpur was founded by Raja Hindu Singh Chandel. Its original name, Kanhpur, may derive from "Kanhiyapur" or commemorate the Kanhaiya Ashtam festival. An alternative theory links it to the Mahabharata character Karna. The city began as a cluster of villages. British interest began after their 1765 victory near Jajmau, leading to a military camp in 1778. Formal British control came in 1801, after which they developed it into a major hub, leveraging its Ganges location for indigo trade and building key infrastructure like the Grand Trunk Road (1832).

### The 1857 Uprising
Cawnpore (Kanpur) was a major British garrison. During the 1857 Rebellion, 900 British were besieged for 22 days by rebels under Nana Sahib. They surrendered for safe passage. At Sati Chaura Ghat, the departing British were fired upon, leading to their death or capture. The exact cause is debated; some historians argue it resulted from confusion, not a premeditated plan by Nana Sahib. The 200 surviving women and children were held in the Bibighar and later killed, with bodies thrown into a well. The British retook the city on 18 July and committed severe retaliations. "Remember Cawnpore" became a British war cry.

### Geography and Climate
Located in the Indo-Gangetic Plains at an average elevation of 318 m, Kanpur lies on the Ganges. It has a monsoon-influenced humid subtropical climate (Cwa). Annual precipitation is 865–900 mm, mostly from the southwest monsoon (June–October). The city has been ranked the fifth-best National Clean Air City in India for large cities.

### Demographics and Administration
As per the 2011 census, Kanpur Nagar district had a population of 4,581,000. The city and suburbs formed the largest urban agglomeration in Uttar Pradesh with 3,015,645 people. Hindi and Urdu are predominant languages. Administratively, Kanpur is the headquarters of its division, range, and zone. The city is governed by the Kanpur Municipal Corporation (KMC), established in 1861 and upgraded in 1959. The Kanpur Development Authority (KDA) oversees infrastructure.

### Infrastructure and Economy
Kanpur is a major transport hub with a domestic airport, the busy Kanpur Central railway station, and key national highways including the Grand Trunk Road. The Kanpur Metro began operations in 2021. The city faces a severe water crisis, with supply falling short of the 600 mld requirement. It also deals with significant pollution, with large volumes of untreated sewage and industrial effluent flowing into the Ganges. Economically, Kanpur has a GDP of ~$9 billion.

### Education
Kanpur is a prominent education center, home to the Indian Institute of Technology Kanpur (IIT Kanpur), established in 1959 with US assistance. Other major institutions include Chhatrapati Shahu Ji Maharaj University, Chandra Shekhar Azad University of Agriculture and Technology, and Harcourt Butler Technical University (HBTU).

## Terms
- **Ghat**: A flight of steps leading down to a river, used for bathing and religious ceremonies.
- **Sepoy**: An Indian soldier serving under British or other European command.
- **Municipal Corporation**: A local government body for large urban areas in India, responsible for civic services.
- **Tehsil**: An administrative division of a district.
- **Lok Sabha**: The lower house of India's Parliament.
- **Vidhan Sabha**: The legislative assembly of an Indian state.
- **STP**: Sewage Treatment Plant, a facility for treating wastewater.
- **MLD**: Million Litres per Day, a unit for measuring water flow.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The exact events and responsibility for the 1857 Sati Chaura Ghat massacre are debated. While the British accused Nana Sahib of betrayal, no evidence proves he planned it, and some historians attribute it to confusion.
- The official limits of Kanpur's metropolitan area are pending government approval, leading to varying population projections.

# Henry IV of France

## Overview
Henry IV (1553–1610) was the first Bourbon king of France, renowned for pragmatically ending the devastating French Wars of Religion. His conversion from Protestantism to Catholicism and the subsequent Edict of Nantes (1598) established a fragile but lasting religious peace, allowing him to stabilize the kingdom and lay the foundations for France's future prominence.

- **1572** — Becomes King of Navarre; marries Margaret of Valois.
- **1572** — Survives the St. Bartholomew's Day massacre.
- **1589** — Inherits the French throne after the assassination of Henry III.
- **1593** — Converts to Catholicism to secure his claim to the throne.
- **1594** — Crowned King of France at Chartres.
- **1598** — Promulgates the Edict of Nantes, granting rights to Huguenots.

- **1610** — Assassinated in Paris by François Ravaillac.

## Body

### Early Life and Wars of Religion
Born a Catholic but raised a Protestant (Huguenot) by his mother, Henry inherited the throne of Navarre in 1572. He was a key figure in the French Wars of Religion, a series of conflicts between Catholics and Huguenots. He narrowly escaped death during the St. Bartholomew's Day massacre (1572) by temporarily converting to Catholicism. He later escaped court, abjured Catholicism, and led Huguenot forces. Upon the death of Henry III in 1589, Henry of Navarre became the legitimate heir to the French throne under Salic law, but his Protestant faith led the Catholic League, backed by Spain, to reject him.

### Conversion and the Edict of Nantes
After a military stalemate, Henry made the pragmatic decision to convert to Catholicism in 1593, reportedly stating "Paris is well worth a Mass." This secured his recognition by most of the kingdom. To formally end the civil wars and reconcile the nation, he issued the Edict of Nantes in 1598. This landmark decree granted significant religious liberties and political safeguards to the Huguenot minority, effectively establishing a state within a state and bringing religious peace to France.

### Domestic Policy and Economic Revival
Henry IV, working with his minister the Duke of Sully, focused on rebuilding France. He regularized state finances, promoted agriculture, and invested in public works like roads, bridges, and canals to improve communication and cohesion. He encouraged domestic manufacturing to reduce imports, established royal factories for luxury goods, and supported the arts, notably expanding the Louvre. His reign saw the beginning of successful French colonization in North America under explorers like Samuel de Champlain.

### Foreign Policy and Assassination
Henry's foreign policy aimed to contain the power of the Habsburgs (Spain and the Holy Roman Empire). He secured peace with Spain via the Peace of Vervins (1598) and allied with Protestant states like the Dutch Republic and England. He intervened in the War of the Jülich Succession to support Protestant princes against Habsburg influence. Despite his popularity, he faced opposition from hardline Catholics and some Protestants. He was assassinated in 1610 by François Ravaillac, a Catholic zealot, and was succeeded by his son, Louis XIII.

## Terms
*   **Huguenot:** A French Protestant, adhering to the Reformed tradition of Calvinism.
*   **Politique:** A member of a moderate faction during the French Wars of Religion who prioritized national unity and stability over religious uniformity.
*   **Edict of Nantes:** A 1598 decree issued by Henry IV that granted Huguenots substantial civil rights and religious freedoms, ending the French Wars of Religion.
*   **Salic Law:** An ancient French law barring inheritance of the throne through the female line, which Henry IV used to claim the throne over other Catholic candidates.
*   **Catholic League:** A powerful political and religious faction in France, supported by Spain, that opposed Henry IV's succession due to his Protestant faith.
*   **Peace of Vervins:** A 1598 treaty between France and Spain that ended their long-running conflict, allowing Henry to focus on domestic issues.
*   **Cadet branch:** A younger branch of a royal house, in this case the Bourbons, descended from a younger son of the senior Capetian line.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Sincerity of Conversion:** Historians debate whether Henry IV's conversion to Catholicism was a genuine change of faith or a purely political act to gain the throne. Evidence suggests he remained privately sympathetic to Calvinism.
*   **Legacy of the Edict of Nantes:** While it brought peace, the Edict was contested by parlements and hardliners on both sides. Its long-term success in integrating Huguenots is debated, as it was eventually revoked by Louis XIV in 1685.
*   **Economic Impact:** The extent of economic recovery under Henry IV and his minister Sully is questioned, as Sully's own memoirs may exaggerate their accomplishments.

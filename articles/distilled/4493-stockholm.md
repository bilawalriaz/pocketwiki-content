# Stockholm

## Overview
Stockholm is the capital and most populous city of Sweden, serving as the nation's cultural, political, and economic center. Situated across fourteen islands where Lake Mälaren meets the Baltic Sea, it is the largest urban area in the Nordic countries. Its strategic location has shaped its history from a medieval trading post to a modern, innovative global city.

## Body

### History and Founding
The area has been settled since the Stone Age. The city's name, first written in 1252, likely refers to a log or islet ("holm"). It was founded by Birger Jarl, possibly to protect against invasions after the 1187 pillage of Sigtuna. Stockholm rose to prominence through Baltic trade with the Hanseatic League. Its strategic importance made it central to the Kalmar Union's conflicts, culminating in the 1520 Stockholm Bloodbath, which sparked uprisings leading to Swedish independence under Gustav Vasa in 1523.

During the 17th-century Swedish Empire, the city's population multiplied, and it became the official capital in 1634, enjoying a trade monopoly. A renaissance in arts and sciences followed, with figures like René Descartes visiting. After a 1710 plague and the end of the Great Northern War, the city stagnated but regained momentum during the 18th-century Enlightenment under Gustav III. Industrialization in the late 19th century spurred dramatic population growth and the establishment of key institutions like the Karolinska Institutet. The 20th century saw modernization, controversial demolitions, and a shift to a high-tech and service-based economy.

### Geography and Climate
Stockholm is located on Sweden's east coast at the 59th parallel north, where Lake Mälaren flows into the Baltic Sea. Over 30% of the city area is water, and another 30% is parks and green spaces. It has a humid continental climate bordering on oceanic, with an average annual temperature of 7.9 °C (46 °F). Winters are cold but generally above freezing, while summers are pleasantly warm. The city experiences significant daylight variation, from over 18 hours in midsummer to about 6 hours in late December.

### Government and Economy
Stockholm is the seat of the Swedish government, including the Riksdag (parliament), the monarch's official residence at Stockholm Palace, and the prime minister's residence at Sager House. The city is Sweden's primary financial center and a major European hub for trade, finance, and technology. The Stockholm region's GDP is around $180 billion, with the highest GDP per capita of all Swedish counties. It hosts headquarters for major companies like Ericsson, H&M, and Spotify, and is home to Kista, Europe's largest information and technology cluster. The Stockholm Stock Exchange is the largest in the Nordic countries.

### Education and Culture
Stockholm is a major educational center, hosting top universities such as the Karolinska Institute, KTH Royal Institute of Technology, and the Stockholm School of Economics. It is the cultural heart of Sweden, home to the Nobel Prize ceremonies, the Vasa Museum (Scandinavia's most visited), and the world's first open-air museum, Skansen. The city's architecture ranges from the medieval Gamla Stan (Old Town) to modernist landmarks like the Stockholm City Hall. Its music scene is globally influential, from classical institutions to pop acts like ABBA and modern artists like Avicii.

### Transport and Environment
Stockholm has an extensive public transport system, including the Stockholm Metro, commuter rail, and ferries. It implemented a permanent congestion tax in 2007 to reduce traffic. The city is a leader in sustainability, winning the first European Green Capital Award in 2010. It aims to be fossil fuel-free by 2050 and is surrounded by the Royal National City Park, the world's first legally protected national urban park. Air quality has improved, though ozone remains a challenge.

## Terms
- **Hanseatic League:** A medieval commercial and defensive confederation of merchant guilds and market towns in Central and Northern Europe.
- **Kalmar Union:** A personal union that from 1397 to 1523 joined the three kingdoms of Denmark, Norway, and Sweden under a single monarch.
- **Riksdag:** The national legislature and supreme decision-making body of Sweden.
- **GDP per capita:** Gross Domestic Product per person, a measure of a region's economic output per resident.
- **Humid continental climate:** A climate characterized by large seasonal temperature differences, with warm to hot summers and cold winters.
- **Congestion tax:** A fee charged to vehicles entering a designated area to reduce traffic congestion and fund transport.
- **European Green Capital Award:** An annual award given by the European Commission to a city for its environmental performance and commitment to sustainable development.
- **National urban park:** A legally protected green area within or near a city, preserving natural and cultural heritage.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The origin of the name "Stockholm" is debated, with theories referring to logs, fortifications, or pile barrages.
- The city's rapid population growth has led to proposals for dense high-rise buildings in the city center, which face opposition from various groups.
- The implementation of the congestion tax was politically contentious, with different municipalities voting differently in a 2006 referendum.

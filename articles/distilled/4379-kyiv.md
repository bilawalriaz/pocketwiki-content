# Kyiv

## Overview
Kyiv is the capital and most populous city of Ukraine, located on both banks of the Dnieper River. As a major industrial, scientific, and cultural center, it has been a pivotal city in Eastern Europe for over a millennium, serving as the historic capital of Kievan Rus' and enduring periods of Mongol, Lithuanian, Polish, Russian, and Soviet rule before becoming the capital of independent Ukraine.

## Timeline
- **c. 5th century** — Probable existence as a commercial center.
- **Mid-9th century** — Captured by Varangians (Vikings), becoming the capital of Kievan Rus'.
- **1240** — Siege of Kiev by Mongols leaves the city in ruins.
- **1569** — Becomes part of the Polish-Lithuanian Commonwealth.
- **1667** — Formally becomes part of the Tsardom of Russia.
- **1918** — Capital of the newly declared Ukrainian People's Republic.
- **1934** — Becomes the capital of the Ukrainian Soviet Socialist Republic.
- **1941-1943** — Occupied by Nazi Germany; site of the Babi Yar massacre.
- **1986** — Chernobyl disaster occurs ~100 km north of the city.
- **1991** — Ukrainian independence declared; Kyiv remains the capital.
- **2013-2014** — Central Kyiv is the primary location of the Euromaidan protests.
- **2022** — Russian forces attempt to seize the city but are repelled.

## Body

### Origins and Early History
Kyiv's name is traditionally derived from its legendary founder, Kyi. Archaeological evidence suggests a Slavic settlement existed by the 6th or 7th centuries, though the traditional founding date is 482 CE. The city was a key point on the trade route between Scandinavia and Constantinople. It was likely a tributary of the Khazars before being captured by Varangians in the mid-9th century, which led to its rise as the capital of Kievan Rus', the first East Slavic state.

### Kievan Rus' and Mongol Destruction
Under Varangian rule, Kyiv flourished. By 1000 CE, it had a population of 45,000 and over 400 churches. Its significance was marked by large-scale construction like St. Sophia Cathedral. However, during a period of feudal division, the city changed hands 47 times between 1146 and 1246. It was sacked in 1169 and 1203 before being completely destroyed during the Mongol invasion led by Batu Khan in 1240, ending its era as a major political center.

### Lithuanian, Polish, and Russian Rule
After the Mongol period, Kyiv was contested by the Grand Duchy of Lithuania and the Golden Horde. Following the Battle of Blue Waters in 1362, it was incorporated into Lithuania. The 1569 Union of Lublin transferred it to the Polish Crown. In the 17th century, it became a center of Orthodox revival. After the Khmelnytsky Uprising, Kyiv was occupied by Russian troops (1654) and formally annexed by the Tsardom of Russia in 1667. It prospered during the late 19th-century Russian industrial revolution, becoming a major commercial and industrial hub.

### Soviet Era and World War II
Following the 1917 Russian Revolution, Kyiv was the capital of several short-lived Ukrainian states before becoming part of the Ukrainian SSR in 1921 and its capital in 1934. The city experienced rapid industrialization, the devastating Great Famine (1932–33), and Stalin's purges. During World War II, it was occupied by Nazi Germany from 1941 to 1943. The Babi Yar massacre in September 1941 resulted in the murder of over 100,000 people, mostly civilians. The city recovered post-war as the USSR's third-largest city.

### Independence and Modern Ukraine
Kyiv was the site of Ukraine's declaration of independence on 24 August 1991. It has since been the center of major political events, including the Orange Revolution (2004–05) and the Euromaidan protests (2013–14). During the 2022 Russian invasion, Ukrainian forces repelled an attempt to seize the city. Economically, Kyiv is Ukraine's wealthiest city, having transitioned from a Soviet industrial base to a modern economy with growing services and finance sectors.

## Terms
- **Kievan Rus'**: The first East Slavic state, with Kyiv as its capital, from the 9th to 13th centuries.
- **Varangians**: Norsemen (Vikings) who played a key role in the founding and early rule of Kievan Rus'.
- **Golden Horde**: The Mongol and later Turkicized khanate that ruled much of Eastern Europe after the Mongol invasion.
- **Union of Lublin (1569)**: The agreement that created the Polish-Lithuanian Commonwealth, transferring control of Kyiv from Lithuania to Poland.
- **Hetmanate**: The autonomous Cossack state in the 17th-18th centuries, of which Kyiv was a part under Russian suzerainty.
- **Ukrainization**: A 1920s Soviet policy promoting Ukrainian language and culture, which increased the Ukrainian character of Kyiv.
- **Euromaidan**: A wave of pro-European protests in Ukraine from 2013 to 2014, centered in Kyiv's Independence Square.
- **Babi Yar**: A ravine in Kyiv where Nazi forces massacred over 100,000 people, primarily Jews, in 1941.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Founding Date**: Scholars debate when Kyiv was founded, with the traditional date being 482 CE, while archaeological data suggests the 6th-9th centuries.
- **Early Rulers**: The Primary Chronicle states Varangians Askold and Dir ruled Kyiv in the late 9th century, but some historians argue Khazar rule continued into the 920s.
- **Ethnic Composition**: The historical ethnic and linguistic makeup of Kyiv is complex, with significant shifts between Ukrainian, Russian, Jewish, and Polish populations over centuries.

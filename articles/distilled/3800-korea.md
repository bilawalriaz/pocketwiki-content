# Korea

## Overview
Korea is a peninsular region in East Asia, historically unified but politically divided since 1945 into North Korea (DPRK) and South Korea (ROK). Its history spans from ancient kingdoms to modern states, marked by periods of unification, foreign invasion, and division. The division, stemming from the Cold War, has led to two starkly different societies and remains a source of global tension.

## Timeline
- **c. 2333 BC** — Legendary founding of Gojoseon by Dangun.
- **108 BC** — Han dynasty of China conquers Gojoseon.
- **668 AD** — Silla, allied with Tang China, unifies most of the peninsula.
- **918** — Goryeo dynasty founded, later giving its name to "Korea."
- **1392** — General Yi Seong-gye establishes the Joseon dynasty.
- **1895** — Japan defeats China in the First Sino-Japanese War, ending Chinese suzerainty.
- **1910** — Japan officially annexes the Korean peninsula.
- **1945** — Japan surrenders; Korea is divided at the 38th parallel into Soviet and US zones.
- **1948** — DPRK and ROK are established as separate governments.
- **1950-1953** — Korean War ends in stalemate and armistice, solidifying the division.

## Body

### Etymology and Names
The English name "Korea" derives from "Goryeo," the name of the dynasty that ruled from 918 to 1392. In South Korea, the whole region is called *Hanguk* ("country of the Han"). In North Korea, it is called *Chosŏn*, a name also used in Japan and China, which references the earlier Joseon dynasty and the legendary Gojoseon.

### Early History and Kingdoms
Human habitation dates to 40,000 BC. The first kingdom, Gojoseon, fell to China's Han dynasty in 108 BC. This led to the Three Kingdoms period (Goguryeo, Baekje, Silla). In 668 AD, Silla unified the peninsula with Tang Chinese aid. After a later collapse, the Goryeo dynasty unified the peninsula again in 918, becoming a vassal to the Mongol Yuan dynasty in the 13th century before regaining independence.

### Joseon Dynasty and Modernization
In 1392, General Yi Seong-gye founded the Joseon dynasty, which lasted over 500 years. Its early centuries were marked by peace, the creation of the Hangul alphabet, and the rise of Neo-Confucianism. Later, invasions by Japan (1592, 1597) and the Manchus (1627, 1637) caused devastation. In the late 19th century, Japan forced Joseon open, leading to turmoil and the end of Chinese suzerainty after the First Sino-Japanese War (1894-95). The Joseon dynasty became the Korean Empire in 1897.

### Japanese Rule and Division
After defeating Russia in 1905, Japan made Korea a protectorate and formally annexed it in 1910. Japanese rule involved industrialization, cultural suppression, and forced labor during World War II. Following Japan's 1945 surrender, the peninsula was divided at the 38th parallel into Soviet and US occupation zones. In 1948, these zones became the DPRK and ROK. The Korean War (1950-53) ended in a stalemate, cementing the division along a Demilitarized Zone (DMZ).

### Contemporary Division
The two Koreas have diverged dramatically. South Korea is a developed, capitalist democracy with a major global economy and cultural influence (the "Korean Wave"). North Korea is a totalitarian state following the "military first" (Songun) policy, possessing nuclear weapons and maintaining one of the world's largest militaries. The two states remain technically at war, though recent summits have aimed to reduce tensions.

## Terms
*   **Gojoseon:** The first Korean kingdom, traditionally founded in 2333 BC and conquered by China in 108 BC.
*   **Three Kingdoms:** The period (c. 57 BC – 668 AD) when Korea was divided between Goguryeo, Baekje, and Silla.
*   **Goryeo:** The dynasty (918–1392) from which the English name "Korea" is derived.
*   **Joseon:** The dynasty (1392–1897) that ruled Korea for over 500 years, known for creating Hangul.
*   **Hangul:** The Korean alphabet, invented in the 15th century by King Sejong the Great.
*   **38th Parallel:** The line of latitude that became the demarcation between the Soviet and US zones in 1945, and later the border between North and South Korea.
*   **DMZ (Demilitarized Zone):** The buffer zone established by the 1953 armistice, running roughly along the 38th parallel.
*   **Songun:** North Korea's "military first" policy, prioritizing the Korean People's Army in state affairs.
*   **Korean Wave (Hallyu):** The global spread of South Korean pop culture, especially music and cinema.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   The legality of the 1910 Japan–Korea Annexation Treaty is disputed, with claims it was signed under duress and without proper imperial ratification.
*   The ownership of the Dokdo islets is contested by both Korea and Japan.
*   The future of Korean reunification remains an open question, with ongoing diplomatic efforts and significant political, economic, and cultural barriers between the two states.

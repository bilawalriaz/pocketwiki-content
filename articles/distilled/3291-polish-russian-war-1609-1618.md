# Polish–Russian War (1609–1618)

## Overview
The Polish–Russian War (1609–1618) was a major conflict between the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth and the Tsardom of Russia, fought during Russia's "Time of Troubles." It was a pivotal struggle for control of the Russian throne, which saw Polish forces occupy Moscow and briefly install a Polish prince as tsar. The war ultimately failed to achieve a Polish-dominated union but concluded with territorial gains for the Commonwealth and, most significantly, paved the way for the establishment of the Romanov dynasty in Russia.

## Timeline
- **1598** — Death of Tsar Feodor I ends the Rurik dynasty; Boris Godunov becomes tsar.
- **1601–1603** — A major famine ravages Russia.
- **1604–1606** — First Polish intervention (First Dymitriad) supports False Dmitry I, who briefly becomes tsar.
- **1607–1609** — Second Polish intervention (Second Dymitriad) supports False Dmitry II.
- **1609** — King Sigismund III Vasa of Poland declares war on Russia.
- **1610** — Polish forces win the Battle of Klushino; enter Moscow.
- **1610** — Polish prince Władysław is elected tsar by the Seven Boyars, but Sigismund blocks his conversion to Orthodoxy.
- **1611** — Smolensk falls to Poland after a 20-month siege.
- **1612** — A Russian popular uprising, led by Kuzma Minin and Dmitry Pozharsky, recaptures Moscow from the Polish garrison.
- **1613** — The Zemsky Sobor elects Michael Romanov as tsar, ending the Time of Troubles.
- **1618** — The Truce of Deulino ends the war, granting Poland territorial concessions.

## Body

### Background and the Time of Troubles
Following the extinction of the Rurik dynasty in 1598, Russia entered a period of political crisis and civil war known as the Time of Troubles. A devastating famine from 1601 to 1603 worsened the instability. Polish nobles (*szlachta*) and Russian *boyars* (nobles) with competing interests sought to exploit the chaos. Some Russian factions proposed a union with the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth, while others supported pretenders to the Russian throne. Poland's King Sigismund III, a fervent supporter of Catholicism, saw an opportunity to expand his realm, weaken Sweden's ally, and potentially Catholicize Orthodox Russia.

### The Dymitriads and Polish Interventions
Polish magnates first backed False Dmitry I, who invaded in 1604, captured Moscow, and was crowned tsar in 1605. His rule, marked by Polish influence and his marriage to a Catholic Pole, sparked a backlash. He was overthrown and killed in 1606. A second pretender, False Dmitry II, emerged in 1607 with Polish support, leading to a second intervention. In 1609, Sigismund III formally declared war, aiming to secure the throne for himself or his son, Prince Władysław.

### Polish Victories and the Occupation of Moscow
Polish forces achieved key victories, most notably the 1610 Battle of Klushino, where elite Polish cavalry (*winged hussars*) defeated a larger Russian-Swedish army. This led to the deposition of Tsar Vasili Shuyski. In September 1610, Polish hetman Stanisław Żółkiewski entered Moscow, where the *Seven Boyars* (a council of nobles) elected Władysław as tsar, conditional on his conversion to Orthodoxy. King Sigismund, however, refused to allow the conversion, demanding the boyars accept him directly. This broken promise alienated Russian support and doomed the planned union.

### Russian Resistance and the End of the War
A popular Russian uprising, led by Kuzma Minin and Prince Dmitry Pozharsky, formed a Second Volunteer Army. After a brutal siege, they recaptured Moscow from the Polish garrison in November 1612. In 1613, the *Zemsky Sobor* (national assembly) elected Michael Romanov as tsar, founding a new dynasty. After a period of low-intensity conflict, a final Polish campaign in 1617–1618 failed to take Moscow. The war concluded with the Truce of Deulino (1618), which granted Poland control of Smolensk and other territories but preserved Russia's independence under the new Romanov tsar.

## Terms
*   **Time of Troubles:** A period of political crisis, famine, and civil war in Russia from 1598 to 1613, following the end of the Rurik dynasty.
*   **Szlachta:** The Polish nobility class, whose members held significant political power in the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth.
*   **Boyars:** The highest rank of the feudal Russian aristocracy, comparable to nobles.
*   **False Dmitry I & II:** Impostors who claimed to be Tsar Dmitry Ivanovich, son of Ivan the Terrible, and were supported by Polish factions.
*   **Winged Hussars:** The elite heavy cavalry of the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth, renowned for their devastating charges.
*   **Zemsky Sobor:** A national assembly in Russia, composed of representatives from various social classes, which elected Michael Romanov as tsar.
*   **Truce of Deulino:** The 1618 agreement that ended the Polish–Russian War, ceding Smolensk and other areas to Poland for a 15-year period.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Historiographical Framing:** The conflict is viewed differently in Polish and Russian historiography. In Poland, it is often seen as the "Dymitriads," a series of interventions. In Russia, it is part of the "Time of Troubles" and commonly called the "Polish Intervention," emphasizing foreign aggression.
*   **The Role of Religion:** The war's outcome hinged on King Sigismund's refusal to allow Prince Władysław to convert to Orthodoxy. Scholars debate whether this was a principled stand for Catholicism or a strategic miscalculation that cost Poland a potential personal union with Russia.
*   **Impact on Russian Identity:** The war fostered a long-lasting negative stereotype of Poland in Russia and was later used by the Romanov dynasty to promote a narrative of heroic national defense against Catholic aggression, a theme echoed in later Russian culture and politics.

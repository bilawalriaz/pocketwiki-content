# Milan

## Overview
Milan is the capital of Lombardy and Italy's economic powerhouse, serving as a global hub for finance, fashion, and industry. Its history spans from a Celtic settlement to a Roman imperial capital, a Renaissance duchy, and a modern metropolis that led Italy's unification and industrialization. Today, it is the second-most populous city in Italy and a major international center for culture, commerce, and innovation.

- **313 AD** — Edict of Milan issued, granting religious tolerance.
- **1395** — Gian Galeazzo Visconti becomes first Duke of Milan.
- **1450** — Francesco I Sforza conquers Milan, beginning a Renaissance golden age.
- **1535** — Sforza line extinct; Spanish Habsburg rule begins.
- **1713** — Austrian Habsburgs gain sovereignty after War of Spanish Succession.

- **1861** — Incorporated into the newly unified Kingdom of Italy.
- **1906 & 2015** — Hosted Universal Expositions.
- **2026** — Co-hosted the Winter Olympic and Paralympic Games.

## Body

### Etymology and Foundation
The city's name derives from the Celtic *Medhelanon* ("middle sanctuary"), later Latinized to *Mediolanum* ("middle plain"). It was founded around 590 BC by a Celtic tribe of the Insubres group, developing around an astronomically aligned sanctuary. The Romans captured it in 222 BC, making it the chief city of Cisalpine Gaul.

### Roman Imperial Capital
Mediolanum became a major Roman center, elevated to a *municipium* in 49 BC. In 286 AD, Emperor Diocletian made it the capital of the Western Roman Empire. It was from here that Emperor Constantine issued the Edict of Milan in 313 AD. The city's imperial role ended in 402 when the Visigoths besieged it, prompting the imperial residence to move to Ravenna.

### Medieval Duchy and Renaissance
After periods of Gothic and Lombard rule, Milan became a powerful commune. The Visconti family established a duchy in 1395, followed by the Sforza dynasty in 1450. Under the Sforzas, Milan became a leading Renaissance center, attracting figures like Leonardo da Vinci. The city's independence ended with French and then Spanish Habsburg domination.

### Early Modern and Unification
Spanish rule (1535–1700) was followed by Austrian control after the War of the Spanish Succession. Milan became a center of Enlightenment thought and resistance to Austrian domination. The "Five Days" revolt of 1848 was a key event in the Italian unification process, which was completed in 1861 with Milan incorporated into the Kingdom of Italy.

### Modern Industrial and Economic Capital
After unification, Milan led Italy's industrial and financial development, becoming the nation's economic heart. It was a base for Benito Mussolini's early political career. The city suffered heavy Allied bombing in WWII and was liberated by Italian partisans in 1945. The post-war "economic miracle" spurred massive growth and migration. After periods of social unrest in the 1970s and political scandal in the 1990s, Milan redeveloped former industrial areas into modern business districts like Porta Nuova and CityLife.

### Culture and Society
Milan is a global fashion capital and a major tourist destination. Its cultural institutions include the Pinacoteca di Brera, the Castello Sforzesco, and the Teatro alla Scala opera house. The city is home to a diverse population, with significant immigrant communities from Egypt, the Philippines, China, and elsewhere. The majority religion is Catholic, with the unique Ambrosian Rite.

### Economy and Infrastructure
Milan's economy is driven by finance, fashion, publishing, and services. It hosts the Borsa Italiana stock exchange and the headquarters of numerous national and multinational corporations. The city is a major transport hub with three airports (Malpensa, Linate, Bergamo), a dense rail network including high-speed lines, and an extensive metro system.

## Terms
- **Medhelanon/Mediolanum:** The Celtic and later Latin names for Milan, meaning "middle sanctuary" or "middle plain."
- **Duchy of Milan:** A state in northern Italy from the 14th to the 16th century, ruled successively by the Visconti and Sforza families.
- **Ambrosian Rite:** A Catholic liturgical rite used in the Archdiocese of Milan, distinct from the Roman Rite.
- **Quadrilatero della Moda:** Milan's upscale fashion district, bounded by four streets including Via Monte Napoleone.
- **Navigli:** A historic system of interconnected canals in Milan, now mostly covered.
- **Porta Nuova & CityLife:** Major 21st-century business and residential districts built on former industrial land.
- **Borsa Italiana:** Italy's main stock exchange, located in Milan.
- **San Siro:** The common name for the Stadio Giuseppe Meazza, shared by football clubs AC Milan and Inter Milan.

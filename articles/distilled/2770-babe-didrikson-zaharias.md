# Babe Didrikson Zaharias

## Overview
Mildred "Babe" Didrikson Zaharias was a pioneering American athlete who excelled in multiple sports, most notably track and field and golf. She won Olympic medals in 1932 and later became the dominant figure in women's golf, winning 10 major championships. Her career challenged contemporary gender norms in sports, and she is widely regarded as one of the greatest female athletes of the 20th century.

## Timeline
- **June 26, 1911** — Born in Port Arthur, Texas.
- **1932** — Wins two gold medals and a silver in track and field at the Los Angeles Olympics.
- **January 1938** — Competes in the Los Angeles Open, a PGA Tour event, becoming the first woman to do so in nearly six decades.
- **December 23, 1938** — Marries professional wrestler George Zaharias.
- **1946** — Wins the U.S. Women's Amateur and the British Ladies Amateur.
- **1947** — Turns professional and becomes a founding member of the LPGA (1950).
- **1950** — Completes the women's golf Grand Slam and is voted "Greatest Female Athlete of the First Half of the Century."
- **1953** — Diagnosed with colon cancer.
- **1954** — Wins the U.S. Women's Open one month after cancer surgery.
- **September 27, 1956** — Dies of colon cancer in Galveston, Texas, at age 45.

## Body

### Early Life and Multi-Sport Stardom
Born to Norwegian immigrants, Zaharias gained her nickname "Babe" as a child. After dropping out of high school, she worked for an insurance company to play on its basketball team, the Golden Cyclones. She first gained national attention at the 1932 AAU Championships, where, as the sole member of her team, she won five events outright and tied for first in a sixth to secure the team title. At the 1932 Los Angeles Olympics, she set four world records, winning gold in the 80-meter hurdles and javelin, and silver in the high jump. She remains the only track and field athlete to win individual Olympic medals in running, throwing, and jumping events. Following the Olympics, she performed in vaudeville and played other sports, including baseball, where she pitched in Major League spring training games.

### Golf Career
Zaharias began playing golf in 1935 and was denied amateur status, leading her to compete in men's PGA Tour events. In 1945, she made the cut in three PGA tournaments, a feat no other woman has matched. After regaining her amateur status in 1942, she won the 1946 U.S. Women's Amateur and the 1947 British Ladies Amateur. She turned professional in 1947 and was a founding member of the LPGA in 1950. Zaharias dominated the tour, winning 41 LPGA events and 10 majors. Her greatest year was 1950, when she won the Grand Slam of the three women's majors of the era. She continued to win after her 1953 cancer diagnosis, including the 1954 U.S. Women's Open, just one month after surgery.

### Personal Life and Legacy
Zaharias married George Zaharias in 1938. In her later years, she had a close, romantic relationship with fellow golfer Betty Dodd. Diagnosed with colon cancer in 1953, she became a public advocate for cancer awareness. She died in 1956 at age 45. Zaharias broke gender norms with her powerful athletic style and was both celebrated and criticized for her "manliness." She was voted the "Greatest Female Athlete of the First Half of the Century" in 1950 and the "Woman Athlete of the 20th Century" in 1999. She was inducted into numerous halls of fame, including the World Golf Hall of Fame and the National Women's Hall of Fame, and received the Presidential Medal of Freedom posthumously in 2021.

## Terms
- **AAU (Amateur Athletic Union):** The governing body for amateur sports in the U.S. at the time, which organized the championships where Zaharias first excelled.
- **Grand Slam:** Winning all major championships in a sport within a single season. Zaharias achieved this in women's golf in 1950.
- **LPGA (Ladies Professional Golf Association):** The primary organization for women's professional golf, which Zaharias helped found in 1950.
- **Major Championship:** The most prestigious tournaments in golf. During Zaharias's career, the three women's majors were the U.S. Women's Open, the Titleholders Championship, and the Women's Western Open.
- **PGA (Professional Golfers' Association):** The organization for male professional golfers in the U.S., whose tour events Zaharias competed in.
- **Vaudeville:** A type of entertainment popular in the early 20th century featuring a variety of acts, which Zaharias performed in after the Olympics.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Amateur Status:** Zaharias's career was marked by disputes over her amateur status, which was revoked when she turned professional and later regained after a three-year hiatus from other sports.
- **Racial Incident:** The source notes a racist incident before the 1932 Olympics where Zaharias threw water on Black athletes Tidye Pickett and Louise Stokes, who were subsequently removed from the team.
- **Sexual Orientation:** Zaharias's romantic relationship with Betty Dodd has led to her being described as the first lesbian gold medalist in Olympic athletics, though the term "lesbian" was not used by the couple.

# Graphic design

## Overview
Graphic design is an interdisciplinary applied art focused on creating visual communications to convey messages. It combines creativity, typography, and imagery to transform ideas into visual forms, influencing perception and memory. Its evolution from ancient techniques to digital tools reflects technological and cultural shifts, making it essential in advertising, branding, and information dissemination.

## Timeline
- **c. 3000 BCE** — Origins in Paleolithic cave paintings and early writing systems like cuneiform.
- **618–907** — Tang dynasty China sees woodblock printing on textiles and Buddhist texts.
- **868** — Earliest known printed book, a Buddhist scripture, produced in China.
- **11th century** — Movable type printing emerges in China during the Song dynasty.
- **Mid-15th century** — Johannes Gutenberg develops movable type in Mainz, Germany, revolutionizing printing.
- **1849** — Henry Cole promotes design education in Britain and organizes the Great Exhibition.
- **1891–1896** — William Morris' Kelmscott Press leads the Arts and Crafts movement in graphic design.
- **1908** — Term "graphic design" first appears in print in *Organized Labor*.
- **1917** — Frederick H. Meyer teaches "Graphic Design and Lettering" at California School of Arts and Crafts.
- **1927** — *Raffe's Graphic Design* becomes the first book with "Graphic Design" in its title.
- **1928** — Jan Tschichold publishes *New Typography*, codifying modern typography principles.
- **1936** — Leon Friend publishes *Graphic Design*, the first extensive literature on the topic.
- **1964** — Ken Garland launches the *First Things First* manifesto, critiquing consumerism.
- **Mid-1980s** — Desktop publishing and graphic software introduce digital tools, transforming design.
- **1999** — *First Things First 2000* manifesto re-published, advocating democratic communication.
- **2023** — Median pay for graphic designers in the U.S. is $58,910 per year. |

## Body

### Origins and Historical Development
Graphic design has roots in ancient visual communication, such as cave paintings and early writing. It emerged as a distinct profession in the West by the late 19th century, driven by the Industrial Revolution's technologies. Gutenberg's 15th-century movable type was a key catalyst. By the 19th century, advertising agencies and publishers hired art directors, leading to the profession's consolidation in the 20th century.

### Evolution of Terminology and Education
The term "graphic design" first appeared in a 1908 publication. In 1917, the California School of Arts & Crafts offered a course titled "Graphic Design and Lettering." The first book with "Graphic Design" in its title was published in 1927, and the first extensive literature on the topic appeared in 1936. These milestones marked its formal recognition as an academic and professional discipline.

### Key Movements and Influences
In the 1920s, Soviet constructivism applied utilitarian design principles. Jan Tschichold's 1928 book *New Typography* codified modern typography, influencing Bauhaus typographers. The *First Things First* manifesto, launched in 1964 and re-published in 1999, critiqued consumerism and called for more democratic communication. These movements shaped graphic design's aesthetic and ethical dimensions.

### Tools and Technology
The mid-1980s saw desktop publishing and graphic software replace many manual processes. Computers enabled instant experimentation with layouts and typography. Today, designers use a hybrid of traditional tools like pencils and digital software such as Adobe Photoshop, Illustrator, and InDesign, as well as open-source programs like Inkscape and GIMP.

### Applications and Related Fields
Graphic design applies to advertising, branding, information design, and user experience (UX) design. Print design involves creating visual content for physical reproduction. Interface design focuses on graphical user interfaces (GUIs). Experiential graphic design applies communication skills to built environments, such as wayfinding. These fields often overlap, requiring collaboration with other professionals.

### Occupations and Industry Trends
Careers include graphic designer, art director, and creative director. As of 2023, median pay was $58,910 per year in the U.S. Employment in online design is expected to grow by 35% by 2026, while traditional media design may decline by 22%. Crowdsourcing allows companies to source designs through contests. Designers must continually learn new techniques due to rapid technological changes.

### Ethics and Future Directions
Ethical considerations include accurately representing information and avoiding harmful stereotypes. Looking ahead, advancements in artificial intelligence, virtual reality, and automation may transform design processes. Social trends like sustainability and inclusivity are also likely to influence future practices.

## Terms
- **Graphic Design**: An interdisciplinary applied art using visual communication to convey messages, combining typography, imagery, and creativity.
- **Typography**: The art and technique of arranging type to enhance readability and aesthetic appeal.
- **Page Layout**: The arrangement of visual elements like text and images on a page for effective communication.
- **Grid**: A method of organizing space and information in design to establish order.
- **Desktop Publishing**: The use of computer software to create printed materials, revolutionizing design in the mid-1980s.
- **User Experience (UX) Design**: The process of creating products that provide meaningful experiences to users.
- **Experiential Graphic Design**: The application of communication skills to the built environment, such as wayfinding.
- **Crowdsourcing**: The practice of obtaining design services from a large, open group of people via online platforms.
- **Information Design**: The practice of presenting information in a clear and effective visual format.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Origins of the Profession**: There is no consensus on when graphic design began; some date it to the Interwar period, while others trace it to the late 19th century or ancient times.
- **Impact of Digital Tools**: Designers disagree whether computers enhance creativity or lead to paralysis through endless options.
- **Ethical Responsibilities**: Ongoing debates focus on the designer's duty to avoid misleading information and harmful stereotypes.
- **Future of the Profession**: Questions arise about how artificial intelligence and automation will affect design roles and creative control.

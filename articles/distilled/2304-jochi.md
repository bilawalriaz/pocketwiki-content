# Jochi

## Overview
Jochi (c. 1182–c. 1225) was the eldest son of Genghis Khan and a key military commander in the early Mongol Empire. His life was defined by a disputed birth and a resulting estrangement from his family, yet he established the lineage that ruled the Golden Horde, one of the empire's successor states.

## Timeline
- **c. 1180–1181** — Börte, Temüjin's wife, is captured and raped by the Merkit tribe.
- **c. 1182** — Jochi is born to Börte after her rescue, his paternity uncertain.
- **1206** — Temüjin is proclaimed Genghis Khan; Jochi receives the largest share of warriors and western territories.
- **1207–1208** — Jochi campaigns against and subjugates the Hoi-yin Irgen tribes.
- **1211** — Jochi commands the right wing in the invasion of the Jin dynasty.
- **1219–1221** — Jochi is a prominent commander during the invasion of the Khwarazmian Empire.
- **1221** — The siege of Gurganj occurs, leading to a major rift between Jochi and his family.
- **c. 1225** — Jochi dies of illness, estranged from Genghis Khan.

## Body

### Birth and Paternity
Jochi's mother, Börte, was captured by the Merkit tribe in 1180 or 1181 and was forcibly married to one of them. After being rescued by Temüjin (later Genghis Khan), she gave birth to Jochi c. 1182. Because she had been captive for nearly nine months, Jochi's biological father was uncertain. While Genghis Khan always treated him as his son, many Mongols, including Jochi's brother Chagatai, considered him illegitimate. This controversy shaped Jochi's life and succession prospects.

### Early Commands and Campaigns
In 1206, Genghis Khan granted Jochi the largest contingent of warriors (9,000) and territories in the west. Jochi successfully campaigned to subdue the Hoi-yin Irgen tribes (1207–1208) and played a key role in destroying the Merkit tribe over the following decade. He also commanded the right wing during the 1211 invasion of the Jin dynasty. His military successes secured trade routes and resources for the empire.

### Khwarazmian War and Succession
During the invasion of the Khwarazmian Empire (1219–1221), Jochi was tasked with conquering cities along the Syr Darya river. The subsequent siege of Gurganj was prolonged and destructive. A major quarrel reportedly erupted between Jochi and his brother Chagatai over tactics, leading Genghis Khan to appoint their brother Ögedei as overall commander. This event, along with Jochi's disputed birth, led to his exclusion from the line of succession in favor of Ögedei.

### Death and Legacy
Following the siege, Jochi became estranged from his father, possibly due to the siege's cost and his failure to send Genghis his share of the loot. He died of illness c. 1225. His son Batu Khan was confirmed as ruler of his territories, which later became the Golden Horde. Jochi's descendants, including Tokhtamysh, used their lineage to legitimize rule over this powerful khanate.

## Terms
- **Kurultai:** A traditional Mongol assembly to decide major policies and elect a leader.
- **Ulus:** The domain or territory assigned to a member of the Mongol ruling dynasty.
- **Golden Horde:** The Mongol and later Turkicized khanate established by Jochi's descendants, ruling over Russia and parts of Eastern Europe.
- **Merkit:** A tribe that feuded with Genghis Khan's family and was the source of the controversy over Jochi's birth.
- **Khwarazmian Empire:** A Persianate empire in Central Asia that was invaded by the Mongols under Genghis Khan.
- **Hoi-yin Irgen:** A collection of tribes on the Siberian taiga that Jochi subdued early in his career.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **The Siege of Gurganj:** Historians debate the accuracy of the account detailing a quarrel between Jochi and Chagatai. Some, like Christopher Atwood, argue this narrative was invented later to bolster Ögedei's legitimacy as khan.
- **Cause of Estrangement:** The exact reasons for the permanent rift between Jochi and Genghis Khan after the Khwarazmian war are unclear, with sources offering conflicting explanations.
- **Place of Burial:** A large mausoleum in Kazakhstan traditionally identified as Jochi's tomb has been shown by radiocarbon dating to be from a much later period.

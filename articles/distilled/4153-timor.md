# Timor

## Overview
Timor is an island in Maritime Southeast Asia, divided between the sovereign state of Timor-Leste (East Timor) and Indonesia (West Timor). Its history is defined by centuries of colonial division, a brutal Japanese occupation in World War II, and a long struggle for East Timorese independence, which was achieved in 2002. The island is geologically active, ecologically unique as part of Wallacea, and culturally diverse with over 11 ethno-linguistic groups.

## Timeline
- **13th century** — First historical record in Chinese text *Zhu Fan Zhi*, noted for sandalwood.
- **14th century** — Identified as part of the Majapahit realm in the Javanese *Nagarakretagama*.
- **Late 16th century** — Portuguese settlement begins.
- **Mid-17th century** — Dutch settlement begins at Kupang.
- **1859** — Treaty formally divides the island between Dutch and Portuguese control.
- **1912** — Final boundary between Dutch and Portuguese Timor is resolved.
- **Dec 1941** — Portuguese Timor occupied by Australian and Dutch forces to preempt Japanese invasion.
- **Feb 1942** — Japanese forces occupy Timor, leading to a guerrilla war and heavy civilian casualties (40,000–70,000 deaths).
- **1975** — Following Portugal's Carnation Revolution, Indonesia invades and annexes East Timor.
- **1999** — UN-sponsored referendum sees East Timorese reject autonomy within Indonesia, leading to independence.
- **2002** — Timor-Leste becomes an independent nation.

## Body

### Colonial Division and World War II
Timor was incorporated into Asian trading networks by the 14th century. Portuguese and Dutch colonizers arrived in the 16th and 17th centuries, respectively. Their rivalry led to a formal division of the island by treaty in 1859, with the final boundary set in 1912. West Timor became part of Indonesia in 1949, while East Timor remained a Portuguese colony. During World War II, despite Portuguese neutrality, Allied forces preemptively occupied East Timor in 1941. The subsequent Japanese invasion in 1942 sparked a fierce guerrilla campaign by Dutch-Australian forces and Timorese volunteers. The Japanese occupation was devastating, causing tens of thousands of civilian deaths.

### Independence Movement and Modern Division
After Portugal's 1974 revolution, its withdrawal from East Timor led to internal conflict. Fearing a communist government, Indonesia invaded in 1975 and annexed the territory, a move never recognized by the UN. A prolonged guerrilla resistance was led by Falintil, the military wing of the Fretilin party. A 1999 UN-sponsored referendum, in which the East Timorese rejected autonomy within Indonesia, paved the way for independence. Timor-Leste became a sovereign state in 2002, though it has faced ongoing political strife and poverty. The island remains divided, with a small exclave of Timor-Leste (Oecusse District) located within West Timor.

### Culture, Language, and Religion
The island is home to 11 distinct ethno-linguistic groups, with the Atoni in the west and Tetun in the east being the largest. Languages belong primarily to the Austronesian family. Christianity is the dominant religion (about 90% of the population), but its distribution is uneven: West Timor is majority Protestant (58%), while Timor-Leste is overwhelmingly Catholic (98%). Islam and animism account for the remainder.

### Geography and Geology
Timor is part of the Lesser Sunda Islands, lying north of Australia. It is geologically unique, formed by the collision of the Australian continental plate with the Eurasian oceanic plate, creating a complex accretionary wedge. The island is mountainous, with peaks near 3,000 meters, and has a long dry season. Its natural vegetation is tropical dry forest, part of the Wallacea ecoregion, which features a mix of Asian and Australasian species. Much of the original forest has been cleared for farming.

### Flora and Fauna
The Timor and Wetar deciduous forests ecoregion supports endemic species like the Timor shrew and Timor rat, alongside introduced marsupials. The island has 250 bird species, 24 of which are endemic. Historically, it was home to extinct giant monitor lizards and dwarf stegodonts. Late Cretaceous fossils include marine vertebrates like mosasaurs.

## Terms
- **Atoni**: The largest ethno-linguistic group in western Timor.
- **Tetun**: The largest ethno-linguistic group in central and eastern Timor; an official language of Timor-Leste.
- **Falintil**: The military wing of the Fretilin party, which led the guerrilla resistance against Indonesian occupation.
- **Fretilin**: The political party in East Timor whose feared rise prompted the Indonesian invasion.
- **Wallacea**: A biogeographical region between Asia and Australia, characterized by a mix of species from both continents.
- **Accretionary wedge**: A mass of sedimentary material scraped off a subducting tectonic plate and piled onto the overriding plate.
- **Oecusse District**: An exclave of Timor-Leste located within the Indonesian province of West Timor.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The geological deformation of Timor is debated: some researchers advocate for shallow, "thin-skinned" deformation, while others argue for deformation involving the deeper basement rock.
- There is no evidence of popular support in West Timor for a proposed "Great Timor" union with Timor-Leste, and the current Timor-Leste government recognizes the existing boundary.

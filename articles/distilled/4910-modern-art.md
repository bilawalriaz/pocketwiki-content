# Modern art

## Overview
Modern art refers to artistic work produced roughly from the 1860s to the 1970s, characterized by a break from traditional techniques and a spirit of experimentation. It marks a shift from narrative, representational art toward abstraction and new ideas about the nature and function of art itself. This period laid the groundwork for contemporary art and fundamentally changed how art is created and perceived.

## Timeline
- **1863** — Édouard Manet shows *Le déjeuner sur l'herbe*, a key early date for modern art.
- **c. 1905** — Fauvism emerges in Paris with artists like Matisse and Derain.
- **1907** — Pablo Picasso paints *Les Demoiselles d'Avignon*, a proto-Cubist work.
- **1908-1912** — Analytic Cubism is developed by Picasso and Braque.
- **1911** — Der Blaue Reiter group forms in Munich.
- **1913** — The Armory Show introduces modern art to the United States.
- **1916** — Dada movement begins in Zürich as an anti-art response to World War I.
- **1924** — André Breton publishes the first Surrealist manifesto.
- **1940s-50s** — Abstract Expressionism emerges, making New York a new art center.
- **1960s-70s** — Pop art, Minimalism, and conceptual art challenge traditional media.

## Body

### Roots in the 19th Century
Modern art's origins are traced to 19th-century movements that challenged academic tradition. Romanticism, Realism, and Impressionism laid the groundwork by focusing on emotion, everyday life, and the effects of light. Post-Impressionists like Van Gogh and Cézanne were essential precursors, experimenting with color and form. The Enlightenment's spirit of critique and the French Revolution's social upheaval fostered a new self-consciousness about artistic style.

### Early 20th Century: New Movements
The early 1900s saw an explosion of revolutionary styles. Fauvism used intense, non-naturalistic color for emotional effect. Cubism, pioneered by Picasso and Braque, fragmented objects into geometric forms, rejecting single-point perspective. German Expressionism, with groups like Die Brücke and Der Blaue Reiter, used distorted forms and vivid color to express inner experience. Other movements like Futurism celebrated technology and speed.

### World Wars and the Interwar Period
World War I disrupted the pre-war avant-garde and gave rise to anti-art movements like Dada, which embraced absurdity and chance. Surrealism followed, exploring the unconscious mind through dreamlike imagery. Between the wars, the School of Paris flourished, and movements like De Stijl and Bauhaus sought to unify art, craft, and design. Modern art was introduced to the U.S. via the 1913 Armory Show.

### Post-World War II
After WWII, the United States became the new focal point for art. Abstract Expressionism, with artists like Pollock and de Kooning, emphasized spontaneous, large-scale gesture. The 1960s and 70s saw a reaction against this, with movements like Pop art (using commercial imagery), Minimalism (reducing art to essential forms), and conceptual art (where the idea was paramount). New forms like video art, performance art, and land art expanded the definition of art beyond traditional media.

## Terms
- **Modernism:** The broader cultural and philosophical movement emphasizing innovation, experimentation, and a break from tradition, closely related to modern art.
- **Fauvism:** An early 20th-century style using strong, non-naturalistic colors for expressive, emotional effect.
- **Cubism:** A revolutionary style that depicts subjects from multiple viewpoints simultaneously, reducing forms to geometric shapes.
- **Expressionism:** A movement that seeks to express emotional experience rather than physical reality, often through distortion and vivid color.
- **Abstract Expressionism:** A post-WWII American movement characterized by large-scale, gestural, non-representational painting.
- **Conceptual Art:** Art where the idea or concept behind the work is more important than the finished physical object.
- **Dada:** An anti-art movement (c. 1916) that rejected logic and embraced chaos and absurdity as a protest against war and bourgeois culture.
- **Surrealism:** A movement that sought to unlock the creative potential of the unconscious mind, often through dreamlike and illogical imagery.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Defining Boundaries:** There is no single, universally agreed-upon date for the start of modern art; 1863, 1855, and 1784 are all proposed.
- **The "End" of Modern Art:** Scholars debate when modern art ended and contemporary or postmodern art began, with the late 1970s often cited.
- **The Role of the U.S.:** The shift of the art world's center from Paris to New York after WWII is a major historical development, but its implications for global art narratives are still discussed.

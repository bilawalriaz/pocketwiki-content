# Metaphysics

## Overview
Metaphysics is the branch of philosophy investigating the most fundamental structure of reality, addressing topics such as existence, categories of being, possibility and necessity, space and time, causality, and the mind-body relation. Historically regarded as the study of mind-independent features of the world ("first philosophy"), it has also been reconceived since Kant as an inquiry into the conceptual frameworks structuring human experience. Metaphysics differs from the individual sciences by examining the most general and abstract aspects of reality rather than specific empirical domains. Its methods range from *a priori* rational intuition and conceptual analysis to naturalized approaches continuous with empirical science. The field faces persistent criticisms regarding the reliability of its methods, the meaningfulness of its statements (logical positivism), and whether its disputes are merely verbal (deflationism).

## Timeline
- **7th–6th c. BCE** — Upanishads (India) and pre-Socratic philosophy (Greece) speculate on the origin and nature of the cosmos; Taoism emerges in China.
- **4th c. BCE** — Plato develops Theory of Forms; Aristotle writes *Metaphysics*, establishes categories, four causes, and the study of "being qua being."
- **3rd c. CE** — Neoplatonism (Plotinus) posits "the One" as source of reality; Madhyamaka (Nagarjuna) and Yogācāra schools develop Buddhist metaphysics of emptiness and consciousness-only.
- **9th–13th c. CE** — Medieval synthesis: Avicenna distinguishes existence/essence; Aquinas analyzes analogy of being; Ockham formulates Ockham's Razor; Advaita Vedanta (Shankara) asserts non-dualism.
- **17th–18th c.** — Early modern systems: Descartes (substance dualism), Spinoza (monism), Leibniz (monadology, possible worlds), Berkeley (idealism), Hume (regularity causation, empiricist critique).
- **1781** — Kant publishes *Critique of Pure Reason*, distinguishing transcendent metaphysics from critical inquiry into conditions of possible experience.
- **19th c.** — German Idealism (Hegel: reality is rational); Schopenhauer (will as ground); British Idealism (Bradley); Pragmatism (Peirce, Dewey) treats metaphysics as observational science.
- **Early 20th c.** — Analytic "revolt against idealism" (Russell, Moore); Logical atomism; Logical positivism (Carnap) declares metaphysical statements meaningless.
- **Mid–Late 20th c.** — Quine naturalizes metaphysics (ontological commitment); Lewis defends modal realism (possible worlds); Kripke revives essentialism; Heidegger develops fundamental ontology; Derrida deconstructs metaphysical binaries.

## Body

### Definition and Scope
Metaphysics examines the "most elementary features of reality," including existence, objects, properties, modality, spacetime, causation, and mind-matter relations. Its precise nature is disputed: some view it as a unified inquiry into fundamental reality or essences; others treat it as a disjunctive list of topics. Two classical definitions persist: the science of first causes, and the study of *being qua being* (what all beings share and their fundamental categories). Aristotle labeled it "first philosophy," implying it grounds other philosophical branches. A central divide concerns its object: **metaphysical realism** sees it as studying mind-independent reality, while **Kantian critical philosophy** and **Strawson’s descriptive metaphysics** reframe it as mapping the conceptual schemes (categories of quantity, quality, relation, modality) that structure human thought and experience. **Revisionary metaphysics** seeks to improve these schemes. Metaphysics differs from special sciences (physics, biology) by its generality—studying *all* entities rather than restricted classes—though the boundary may be a continuum rather than a dichotomy.

### Branches
*   **General Metaphysics (Ontology):** Studies the most fundamental aspects of being: categories (substance, property, relation, fact), their interdependence, and systems of classification.
*   **Special Metaphysics:** Narrower perspectives: **Metaphysical cosmology** (changeable things as a spatiotemporal totality); **Rational psychology** (mind, freedom); **Natural theology** (divine first cause). Overlaps with philosophy of mind and theology blur boundaries.
*   **Applied Metaphysics:** Uses metaphysical frameworks in ethics, religion, AI (ontologies for knowledge representation), economics, and psychiatry (status of diseases).
*   **Meta-metaphysics:** The metatheory investigating metaphysics' own nature, methods, and relation to other disciplines.

### Existence and Categories of Being
Existence (being part of reality) is a core concept. Theories differ on its logical form: **Frege/Russell view** treats existence as a second-order property (instantiation of properties); **Meinong** argues existence is a first-order property of individuals, allowing "nonexistent objects" (e.g., Pegasus). **Plato** posited degrees of existence: Forms (perfect, immutable) possess higher reality than imperfect matter. **Categories** provide the highest genera of being. **Aristotle** listed 10 categories, privileging *substance* (e.g., man, horse) as the primary subject on which others (quantity, quality, place) depend. **Kant** redefined categories as 12 a priori principles of understanding (quantity, quality, relation, modality). The **concrete/abstract** distinction is widely used: concrete objects (rocks, humans) exist in spacetime, change, and enter causal relations; abstract objects (numbers, sets) are spatiotemporally non-located, immutable, and causally inert.

### Particulars and Universals
**Particulars** are unique, non-repeatable individuals (concrete: Aristotle, Eiffel Tower; abstract: the number 2). **Universals** are repeatable entities (properties: redness; relations: taller-than) instantiated by multiple particulars. The **problem of universals** concerns their ontological status:
*   **Realism:** Universals are mind-independent. **Platonic realism** (ante rem): universals exist uninstantiated. **Aristotelian realism** (in re): universals exist only if instantiated.
*   **Nominalism:** Only particulars exist; universals are linguistic or mental constructs.
*   **Conceptualism:** Universals exist as mental concepts classifying experience.
**Particulars' structure:** **Substratum theory** (Locke): a bare particular + properties. **Bundle theory** (Hume): particulars are mere bundles of properties (sometimes plus a *haecceity* or individual essence). **Mereology** studies part-whole relations. **Universalism:** any collection composes a whole. **Moderatism:** composition requires conditions (e.g., contact). **Nihilism:** no composite wholes exist (only particles arranged table-wise). **Atomism** (simples exist) vs. **continuum theory** (infinite divisibility).

### Modality: Possibility and Necessity
Modal metaphysics analyzes statements about what *could* or *must* be the case. **Modal realism** (Lewis): possible worlds are concrete, spatiotemporally isolated realities; the actual world is just the one we inhabit. **Alternatives:** possible worlds as abstract objects or fictions. **Possible world semantics:** "Possibly *p*" = true in at least one world; "Necessarily *p*" = true in all worlds. Debate persists on whether modality is a fundamental, irreducible feature of reality or reducible to non-modal facts (linguistic compatibility, fictional discourse).

### Space, Time, and Change
**Spacetime realism** (substantivalism/absolutism): spacetime is a mind-independent container or object. **Relationism** (Leibniz): spacetime is a network of spatial/temporal relations between objects. **Idealism** (Kant): spacetime is a mental framework.
**Time's passage:** **A-theory**: time flows; events have dynamic properties (past, present, future). **B-theory**: time is static; events ordered by fixed "earlier-than/later-than" relations. **Eternalism**: past, present, future equally real. **Presentism**: only the present exists.
**Persistence:** **Endurantism**: 3D objects wholly present at each moment, gaining/losing properties. **Perdurantism**: 4D "worms" composed of temporal parts; change is qualitative difference between parts (e.g., unripe banana part vs. ripe part).

### Causality
Causality links cause and effect. **Deterministic** views: cause necessitates effect. **Probabilistic** theories (Anscombe): cause raises probability of effect. **Regularity theory** (Hume): causation = constant conjunction of events. **Nomic regularity**: regularities grounded in laws of nature. **Counterfactual theories**: effect depends on cause (would not occur without it). **Primitivism**: causation is unanalyzable (basic); some identify **causal powers** as the mechanism. **Eliminativism**: causation does not exist.

### Mind and Free Will
**Mind-body problem:** **Substance dualism** (Descartes): distinct mental/physical substances interacting. **Idealism** (Berkeley): only mental exists. **Materialism**: only physical exists; mind reduced to brain states, behavior, or functional roles. **Neutral monism**: fundamental reality is neither mental nor physical. **Hard problem of consciousness**: explaining how physical systems yield phenomenal experience.
**Free will vs. determinism:** **Incompatibilism**: free will requires indeterminism. **Hard determinism**: determinism true, no free will. **Libertarianism**: free will exists, so determinism false. **Compatibilism**: free will (acting on one's motivations) compatible with determinism.

### Identity and Truthmaking
**Numerical identity**: relation an entity bears to itself (Morning Star = Evening Star). **Qualitative identity**: exact similarity between distinct entities. **Leibniz's Law** (Identity of Indiscernibles): if *x* and *y* share all properties, they are identical (controversial). **Diachronic identity**: sameness over time (Ship of Theseus). **Personal identity**: criteria for personhood over time.
**Truthmaker theory**: Truth-bearers (propositions) are made true by truthmakers (entities/facts). Metaphysical inquiry becomes: "What entities make statements of type *T* true?" Modal metaphysics seeks truthmakers for modal claims; temporal metaphysics for tense claims. **Theories of truth**: correspondence, coherence, pragmatic, semantic, deflationary.

### Methodology
*   **A priori / Rationalist:** Relies on **intuitions** (non-inferential impressions) and **deductive reasoning** from **axioms** (self-evident principles). Uses **thought experiments** (e.g., philosophical zombies) to test intuitions.
*   **Common-sense philosophy:** Appeals to pre-theoretical beliefs to critique revisionary theories (e.g., against mereological nihilism).
*   **Conceptual analysis:** Decomposes concepts to clarify meaning (analytic tradition).
*   **Phenomenology:** **Eidetic variation** (Husserl): imaginatively varying features to find essences. **Transcendental method**: deducing conditions of possibility for entities.
*   **Naturalized metaphysics** (Quine): Continuous with science. Analyzes **ontological commitments** of best scientific theories (via existential quantification: "∃x Horse(x)" commits to horses). Rejects sharp analytic/synthetic distinction.

**Theoretical virtues** for theory choice: **Ockham's Razor** (parsimony), explanatory power, theoretical usefulness, proximity to established beliefs.

### Criticism
1.  **Cognitive limitation / Skepticism:** Humans lack access to ultimate reality (Hume: metaphysics beyond experience; Kant: knowledge limited to phenomena).
2.  **Meaninglessness (Logical Positivism):** Verification principle: statements meaningful only if empirically verifiable or analytic. Metaphysical statements fail → meaningless (Carnap).
3.  **Verbal disputes / Deflationism:** Disagreements are merely linguistic (e.g., "tables exist" vs. "particles arranged table-wise") with no substantive ontological import (Hirsch, Chalmers).
4.  **Lack of progress:** Deep, persistent disagreement suggests unreliable methods.
5.  **Pragmatic irrelevance:** Metaphysics lacks practical utility.
6.  **Heidegger/Derrida:** Traditional metaphysics forgets the "ontological difference" (Being vs. beings); relies on unstable binary oppositions (presence/absence) deconstructed by Derrida.
*Response:* "Serious metaphysicians" argue disputes track objective structure; some disputes may be verbal, others substantive.

### Relation to Other Disciplines
*   **Natural Sciences:** Science uses concepts (law, causation, spacetime) metaphysics analyzes generally. Science informs metaphysics (e.g., Relativity → spacetime unification). **Naturalized metaphysics** grounds theory in scientific ontology.
*   **Social Sciences:** Metaphysics examines social ontology: emergence, mind-independence of institutions, persistence of groups.
*   **Cosmology/Theology:** Shared topics (first causes, universe). Metaphysics uses reason; cosmology uses observation; theology uses revelation. Historically subfields of metaphysics.
*   **Computer Science:** **Ontologies** (formal specifications: classes, relations) structure data in AI, databases (upper ontologies: SUMO, BFO).
*   **Logic:** Metaphysics uses logic for precision. **Quinean criterion**: existential quantifiers (∃x) in formalized best theories carry ontological commitment.

## Terms
- ****Ontology**** — General metaphysics; the study of the most fundamental categories of being and their relations.
- ****Particular**** — A unique, non-repeatable individual entity (e.g., a specific apple, the number 2).
- ****Universal**** — A repeatable entity (property or relation) instantiated by multiple particulars (e.g., redness, taller-than).
- ****Substance**** — In Aristotle, the primary category of being; an entity that exists in itself and bears properties.
- ****Possible World**** — A complete, consistent way the totality of reality could have been; used to analyze modality.
- ****Modal Realism**** — The view that possible worlds exist as concrete realities on par with the actual world (David Lewis).
- ****Endurantism / Perdurantism**** — Theories of persistence: objects are wholly present at each moment (3D) vs. extended in time as temporal parts (4D).
- ****Truthmaker**** — The entity (fact, state of affairs) whose existence makes a proposition true.
- ****Ockham's Razor**** — Methodological principle preferring theories with fewer ontological commitments (parsimony).
- ****Naturalized Metaphysics**** — Approach (Quine) treating metaphysics as continuous with empirical science, reading ontology off best theories.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Nature of Metaphysics:** Is it a unified science of fundamental reality, a disparate list of topics, or an inquiry into human conceptual schemes?
*   **Universals:** Do they exist *ante rem* (Plato), *in re* (Aristotle), or not at all (Nominalism)?
*   **Modality:** Are possible worlds concrete (Lewis), abstract, or fictional? Is modality primitive or reducible?
*   **Time:** Does time flow (A-theory) or is it static (B-theory)? Is only the present real (Presentism) or all times equally real (Eternalism)?
*   **Composition:** Under what conditions do parts compose a whole? (Universalism vs. Moderatism vs. Nihilism).
*   **Mind-Body:** Is consciousness reducible to physical processes? Is substance dualism, materialism, or neutral monism correct?
*   **Free Will:** Is determinism true? If so, is free will compatible (Compatibilism) or impossible (Incompatibilism)?
*   **Methodology:** Can *a priori* intuition yield metaphysical knowledge, or must metaphysics be naturalized/empirical?
*   **Meaningfulness:** Are metaphysical disputes substantive or merely verbal (deflationism)? Do metaphysical statements have truth conditions (realism vs. anti-realism)?

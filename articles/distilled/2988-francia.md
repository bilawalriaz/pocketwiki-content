# Francia

## Overview
The Kingdom of the Franks was the largest post-Roman state in Western Europe, founded by the unification of Frankish tribes under King Clovis I in the late 5th century. It evolved from a tribal confederation into the Carolingian Empire, which at its height under Charlemagne (crowned Emperor in 800) united much of Western and Central Europe. The kingdom's legacy is foundational to the later nations of France and Germany, and its political and cultural structures profoundly shaped the medieval period.

## Timeline
- **c. 481–511** — Reign of Clovis I, founder of the Merovingian dynasty, who unifies Gaul.
- **486** — Clovis defeats Syagrius, conquering the last Roman enclave in northern Gaul.
- **507** — Clovis defeats the Visigoths at the Battle of Vouillé, conquering most of their territory.
- **687** — Pepin of Herstal becomes sole Mayor of the Palace, beginning Carolingian de facto rule.
- **732/737** — Charles Martel defeats an Umayyad army near Tours, halting their advance north of the Pyrenees.
- **751** — Pepin the Short is elected king, deposing the last Merovingian king and founding the Carolingian dynasty.
- **768–814** — Reign of Charlemagne, who expands the empire to its greatest extent.
- **800** — Charlemagne is crowned "Emperor of the Romans" by Pope Leo III.
- **843** — Treaty of Verdun divides the Carolingian Empire into three parts: West, Middle, and East Francia.
- **962** — Otto I is crowned Emperor, marking the beginning of the Holy Roman Empire from East Francia.

## Body

### Origins and Foundation
The Franks were Germanic tribes who settled near the Rhine and raided Roman Gaul during the 3rd-century crisis. By the 5th century, Frankish leaders like Chlodio had expanded into Roman territory as far as the Somme. Clovis I, son of the Roman-allied king Childeric I, unified the Frankish tribes by defeating rival kings. He then conquered the Roman enclave of Soissons (486) and the Visigothic Kingdom in Gaul (507), establishing Frankish dominance over most of modern France. His conversion to Catholicism allied the Franks with the powerful Gallo-Roman Church.

### Merovingian Dynasty and Partible Inheritance
The Merovingian dynasty, founded by Clovis, practiced partible inheritance, dividing the kingdom among a king's sons. This led to frequent partitions and reunifications, with sub-kingdoms like Austrasia (east) and Neustria (west) emerging. Despite internal conflicts, a sense of a unified "Kingdom of the Franks" persisted. Over time, real power shifted from the Merovingian kings (later called "do-nothing kings") to the Mayors of the Palace, chief ministers of the sub-kingdoms.

### Rise of the Carolingians
The Carolingian family, starting with Pepin of Herstal, held the office of Mayor of the Palace in Austrasia. Pepin's grandson, Charles Martel, became the de facto ruler after 687, defeating rivals and expanding Frankish territory, notably against the Umayyads in 732/737. Charles Martel's son, Pepin the Short, formally ended the Merovingian dynasty in 751 with papal approval, founding the Carolingian dynasty. Pepin secured papal support by returning conquered territories in Italy to the Pope, creating the Papal States.

### Charlemagne and the Carolingian Empire
Charlemagne (Charles the Great) expanded the empire through conquest, incorporating the Lombards in Italy, the Saxons in the east (after brutal wars from 772–804), and Bavaria. His realm stretched from the Pyrenees to the Elbe. On Christmas Day 800, Pope Leo III crowned him Emperor, reviving the Western Roman imperial title and creating a powerful alliance between Frankish kingship and the papacy. This event laid the groundwork for the Holy Roman Empire.

### Division and Legacy
After Charlemagne's death, his empire was divided among his grandsons by the Treaty of Verdun (843). West Francia evolved into the Kingdom of France under the Capetian dynasty. East Francia, under the Ottonian dynasty, became the Kingdom of Germany and the core of the Holy Roman Empire. The Carolingian Empire's administrative, legal, and cultural reforms, including the Carolingian Renaissance, had a lasting impact on European civilization.

## Terms
*   **Merovingian Dynasty:** The first ruling dynasty of the Franks, founded by Clovis I, known for long hair as a symbol of kingship and the practice of partible inheritance.
*   **Partible Inheritance:** The custom of dividing a kingdom or estate among all of a ruler's sons, leading to political fragmentation.
*   **Mayor of the Palace:** The chief minister and head of the royal household in Frankish kingdoms, who eventually became the de facto ruler.
*   **Carolingian Dynasty:** The dynasty of Frankish kings and emperors that succeeded the Merovingians, beginning with Pepin the Short.
*   **Capitulary:** A legislative or administrative ordinance issued by a Frankish king.
*   **Missi Dominici:** "Envoys of the lord," pairs of officials appointed by Charlemagne to oversee justice and administration in specific regions.
*   **Carolingian Renaissance:** The revival of art, culture, and learning in the late 8th and 9th centuries under Charlemagne's patronage.
*   **Treaty of Verdun (843):** The agreement that divided the Carolingian Empire into three parts, forming the basis for the future nations of France and Germany.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Unity vs. Duality:** Historians debate whether the Merovingian realm was a unified kingdom that was merely partitioned, or a "Frankish duality" of two competing entities (Neustria and Austrasia).
*   **Edict of Paris (614):** Scholars disagree on whether this edict was a major concession to the nobility (like a "Frankish Magna Carta") or primarily an anti-corruption measure.
*   **Coronation of Charlemagne:** The exact nature of the Byzantine recognition of Charlemagne's imperial title in 812 is debated—whether he was acknowledged as co-emperor or simply as "emperor" (*basileus*) without the Roman title.

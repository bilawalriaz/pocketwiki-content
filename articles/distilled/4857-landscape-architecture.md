# Landscape architecture

## Overview
Landscape architecture is the professional design of outdoor areas, landmarks, and structures to achieve environmental, social-behavioural, or aesthetic outcomes. It is a multi-disciplinary field integrating ecology, engineering, and design, crucial for creating sustainable and functional public and private spaces, from parks to stormwater systems.

## Timeline
- **1712** — Joseph Addison first writes of "making a landscape."
- **1828** — Gilbert Laing Meason invents the term "landscape architecture."
- **1840** — John Claudius Loudon popularizes the term in his book on Humphry Repton.
- **1841** — Andrew Jackson Downing publishes the first U.S. book on landscape gardening.
- **1863** — Frederick Law Olmsted uses "landscape architect" as a professional title in the U.S.
- **1899** — American Society of Landscape Architects (ASLA) is founded.
- **1948** — International Federation of Landscape Architects (IFLA) is founded in Cambridge, England.
- **1978** — IFLA headquarters are established in Versailles, France.

## Body
### Definition and Scope
Landscape architecture is a regulated profession involving the systematic design and engineering of outdoor spaces. Practitioners, often called landscape architects, work on a wide range of projects, from public parks and campuses to residential estates and civil infrastructure. They integrate "hard" (built) and "soft" (planted) materials while focusing on ecological sustainability. The profession's scope includes sub-categories like site planning, stormwater management, environmental restoration, and urban planning.

### History
Before 1800, the field focused on garden design for estates, exemplified by André Le Nôtre's work at Versailles. The modern profession emerged in the 19th century with the adoption of the term "landscape architecture." In the U.S., Frederick Law Olmsted, designer of Central Park, is considered the "father of American landscape architecture." The profession formalized with the founding of national societies like the ASLA (1899) and the international IFLA (1948). Key figures like Ian McHarg later introduced environmental analysis methods, laying the groundwork for Geographic Information Systems (GIS).

### Fields of Activity
Landscape architects collaborate on diverse projects, including parks, sustainable developments, stormwater management (e.g., rain gardens, green roofs), recreation facilities, and urban design. Specialized roles include landscape managers (long-term care), landscape scientists (soil, hydrology), and landscape planners (policy, master planning). The field continues to evolve, responding to contemporary design movements and environmental challenges.

### Relation to Urban Planning and Indigenous Practices
In the 19th century, landscape architecture merged with urban planning to address city needs, as seen in Olmsted's park systems. Urban planning later developed into a separate profession. The discipline has historically favored Western, geometric designs, often marginalizing Indigenous land management practices, which use vegetation and natural systems to create changing landscapes. These practices were sometimes misinterpreted by colonial settlers as evidence of unused land.

### Education and Profession
Landscape architects typically require an accredited university degree. The profession is regulated in many countries (e.g., U.S., Canada, Australia, UK) through licensure, which requires education, experience, and passing examinations like the Landscape Architect Registration Examination (L.A.R.E.). Professional bodies, such as the Landscape Institute (UK) and ASLA (U.S.), promote standards and interests.

## Terms
- **Landscape architect:** A licensed professional who designs outdoor areas, landmarks, and structures.
- **Stormwater management:** The control of rainwater runoff to prevent flooding and pollution, using methods like rain gardens and green roofs.
- **Green infrastructure:** A network of natural and semi-natural areas designed to manage water and provide environmental benefits.
- **Geographic Information Systems (GIS):** Software for analyzing and mapping spatial data, foundational to modern site analysis.
- **Landscape planner:** A specialist concerned with policy, strategy, and master planning for land use.
- **Accredited degree program:** An educational program in landscape architecture that meets professional standards.
- **L.A.R.E.:** Landscape Architect Registration Examination, a key licensing exam in the U.S.
- **IFLA:** International Federation of Landscape Architects, the global body for the profession.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The inclusion and recognition of Indigenous land management practices within the formal history and contemporary practice of landscape architecture.
- The evolving relationship and distinct roles between landscape architecture and the separate profession of urban planning.
- The impact of colonialism, where Western landscape ideals were used to justify the dispossession of Indigenous peoples, as seen in interpretations of Australian landscapes.

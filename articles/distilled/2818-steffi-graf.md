# Steffi Graf

## Overview
Steffi Graf is a German former professional tennis player widely regarded as one of the greatest athletes in the sport's history. She dominated women's tennis from the late 1980s through the 1990s, setting enduring records for weeks ranked world No. 1 and major titles. Her powerful, versatile game and athletic baseline play are credited with shaping the modern style of tennis.

## Timeline
- **14 June 1969** — Born in Mannheim, West Germany.
- **1983** — Becomes youngest player to appear in a major main draw at the French Open (age 13).
- **13 April 1986** — Wins first WTA tournament (Family Circle Cup), defeating Chris Evert.
- **17 August 1987** — Reaches world No. 1 ranking for the first time.
- **1988** — Achieves the "Golden Slam" (all four majors + Olympic gold).
- **1990** — Monica Seles ends Graf's 186-week consecutive No. 1 streak.
- **30 April 1993** — Monica Seles is stabbed during a tournament; Graf later reclaims dominance.
- **1995** — Wins French Open, Wimbledon, and US Open; Seles returns to tour.
- **1999** — Wins 22nd and final major at the French Open; announces retirement in August.
- **22 October 2001** — Marries Andre Agassi.
- **2004** — Inducted into the International Tennis Hall of Fame.

## Body

### Early Career and Breakthrough
Graf was introduced to tennis by her father, Peter Graf, at age three. She turned professional in 1982 and steadily climbed the rankings, emerging as a challenger to the dominance of Chris Evert and Martina Navratilova. Her breakthrough came in 1987, when she won her first major at the French Open and ascended to the world No. 1 ranking, a position she would hold for a record 186 consecutive weeks.

### The Golden Slam and Peak Dominance (1988–1989)
In 1988, Graf became the first tennis player to achieve the "Golden Slam," winning all four major titles and the Olympic gold medal in a single calendar year. This feat remains unique in tennis history. She continued her dominance in 1989, winning three majors and finishing with an 86–2 match record, the second-highest winning percentage in the Open Era.

### Rivalries, Challenges, and Second Dominance (1990–1996)
The early 1990s saw the rise of new rivals, most notably Monica Seles, who ended Graf's consecutive weeks at No. 1 in 1990. After Seles was stabbed in April 1993, Graf re-established her dominance, winning multiple majors. She battled significant injuries, including back and knee problems, but remained the top player, winning the US Open in 1995 and 1996. Her rivalry with Arantxa Sánchez Vicario produced several epic Grand Slam finals.

### Final Years and Retirement (1997–1999)
Injuries increasingly hampered Graf in the late 1990s, causing her to lose the No. 1 ranking to Martina Hingis. After missing much of 1997 due to knee surgery, she returned to win her 22nd and final major at the 1999 French Open, defeating the top three ranked players in the tournament. She retired in August 1999 at age 30, ranked No. 3.

### Playing Style and Legacy
Graf's game was defined by her powerful inside-out forehand, exceptional footwork, and versatility across all surfaces. She is the only player, male or female, to have won each Grand Slam singles tournament at least four times. Her aggressive baseline style is credited with developing the modern game. She was named the greatest female tennis player of the 20th century by the Associated Press and remains a benchmark for greatness in the sport.

## Terms
*   **Golden Slam:** Winning all four Grand Slam singles titles and an Olympic gold medal in the same calendar year. Graf achieved this in 1988.
*   **Grand Slam:** The four major annual tennis tournaments: Australian Open, French Open, Wimbledon, and US Open.
*   **Open Era:** The period in professional tennis beginning in 1968, when Grand Slam tournaments allowed professional players to compete.
*   **WTA:** The Women's Tennis Association, the principal organizing body of women's professional tennis.
*   **Year-end No. 1:** The player ranked as the world's top player at the conclusion of the season. Graf holds the record with eight.
*   **Inside-out forehand:** A forehand shot hit from the backhand corner across the body to the opponent's backhand side, a signature weapon of Graf's.
*   **Backhand slice:** A defensive or offensive shot hit with a chopping motion, causing the ball to skid low, which Graf used extensively.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Greatest of All Time (GOAT):** While widely considered the greatest female player of the 20th century, debate continues regarding her standing versus later champions like Serena Williams, who matched her record of 22 major titles.
*   **Impact of Seles's Absence:** The stabbing of Monica Seles in 1993, which sidelined her for over two years, is a central point of discussion. It is debated how Graf's career statistics and legacy would differ had Seles remained active during that period.
*   **Surface Versatility:** Graf's ability to win multiple majors on clay, grass, and hard courts is often cited as evidence of her unparalleled all-around skill, setting her apart from specialists.

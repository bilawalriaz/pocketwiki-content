# Marie Antoinette

## Overview
Marie Antoinette was the last Queen of France before the French Revolution, serving as the wife of King Louis XVI from 1774 until the monarchy's abolition in 1792. Her life and reputation became central symbols of the perceived excesses of the *Ancien Régime*, and her execution in 1793 marked a definitive break with the old order.

## Timeline
- **2 Nov 1755** — Born Maria Antonia in Vienna, youngest daughter of Empress Maria Theresa.
- **16 May 1770** — Marries the Dauphin Louis-Auguste at Versailles, becoming Dauphine of France.
- **10 May 1774** — Louis XVI ascends the throne; Marie Antoinette becomes Queen of France.
- **19 Dec 1778** — Birth of her first child, Marie-Thérèse Charlotte.
- **27 Mar 1785** — Birth of her second son, Louis Charles.
- **21 Jun 1791** — The royal family's failed Flight to Varennes destroys remaining public support.
- **10 Aug 1792** — Insurrection at the Tuileries; royal family takes refuge with the Legislative Assembly.
- **21 Sep 1792** — French monarchy is abolished; France is declared a republic.
- **21 Jan 1793** — Louis XVI is executed by guillotine.
- **16 Oct 1793** — Marie Antoinette is executed by guillotine at the Place de la Révolution.

## Body

### Early Life and Marriage
Born an Austrian archduchess, Maria Antonia was the fifteenth child of Empress Maria Theresa. Her marriage in 1770 to the French Dauphin, Louis-Auguste, was a political alliance to secure peace between Austria and France. The union was initially popular, but the couple's failure to consummate the marriage for seven years fueled damaging rumors. Upon becoming Queen in 1774, Marie Antoinette faced immediate criticism for her Austrian heritage and was embroiled in court politics, notably a conflict with Louis XV's mistress, Madame du Barry.

### Queen and Growing Unpopularity
As queen, Marie Antoinette's lavish spending on fashion, the Petit Trianon, and the "Hameau de la Reine" became public grievances during a national financial crisis. She was nicknamed "Madame Déficit." Her political influence grew, including support for the American Revolution, but she was blamed for blocking reforms by ministers like Turgot and Necker. The 1785 Affair of the Diamond Necklace, though she was innocent, permanently damaged her reputation. By the late 1780s, she was a primary target of revolutionary propaganda (*libelles*) that accused her of promiscuity and treason.

### Revolution and Fall
The financial crisis forced the convening of the Estates-General in 1789. Marie Antoinette urged Louis XVI to resist reforms, earning the nickname "Madame Veto." After the storming of the Bastille, the royal family was forced to move to Paris in October 1789. Secret negotiations with figures like Mirabeau failed to restore royal authority. The failed Flight to Varennes in June 1791 proved the monarchy's duplicity and radicalized the Revolution. Following the insurrection of 10 August 1792, the family was imprisoned, and the monarchy was abolished.

### Imprisonment and Execution
After Louis XVI's execution in January 1793, Marie Antoinette, now called "Widow Capet," was moved to the Conciergerie. Her trial in October 1793 included charges of treason and incest, the latter based on coerced testimony from her son. She was convicted and executed by guillotine on 16 October 1793. Her death was seen by revolutionaries as a necessary step in completing the Revolution.

## Terms
*   **Dauphine:** The title given to the wife of the Dauphin, the heir apparent to the French throne.
*   **Libelles:** Scandalous pamphlets, often pornographic, that circulated to damage the reputation of public figures like Marie Antoinette.
*   **Affair of the Diamond Necklace:** A 1785 scandal in which a fraudulent scheme to purchase an expensive necklace implicated the queen, though she was not involved.
*   **Madame Déficit:** A nickname given to Marie Antoinette by the public, blaming her for France's financial crisis.
*   **Estates-General:** The traditional representative assembly of France, convened in 1789 for the first time since 1614 to address the financial crisis.
*   **National Assembly:** The revolutionary body formed by the Third Estate in June 1789, which became the de facto government.
*   **Civil Constitution of the Clergy:** A 1790 law that subordinated the Catholic Church in France to the state, causing a major rift.
*   **Reign of Terror:** The period of the French Revolution (1793–1794) marked by mass executions of perceived enemies of the Revolution.
*   **Guillotine:** The device used for beheading, which became the symbol of the Reign of Terror.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Political Influence:** Historians debate the extent of Marie Antoinette's political power. Some see her as a decisive, reactionary force who blocked necessary reforms, while others argue her influence was limited and often ineffective.
*   **Personal Reputation:** The accuracy of the *libelles* portraying her as profligate and promiscuous is contested. While her spending was real, it was not the sole cause of the debt, and accusations of affairs (e.g., with Fersen) remain largely unsubstantiated by evidence.
*   **Role in the Revolution:** Some contemporaries, like Thomas Jefferson, blamed her directly for causing the Revolution. Modern historians generally view her as a symbol and a contributing factor through her unpopularity and political missteps, but not the primary cause of the systemic crisis.

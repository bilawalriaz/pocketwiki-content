# Lima

## Overview
Lima is the capital and largest city of Peru, serving as the nation's political, cultural, financial, and commercial center. Located on the country's central coast, it is one of the most populous metropolitan areas in the world and a major hub for regional diplomacy and international events.

## Timeline
- **15th century** — The Inca Empire incorporates the Lima valley, organized as the province of Pachacamac.
- **1535** — Francisco Pizarro founds the city on January 6, naming it "City of Kings."
- **1543** — Lima is designated the capital of the Viceroyalty of Peru and seat of a Real Audiencia.
- **1586–1687** — Violent earthquakes destroy much of the city, leading to major reconstruction.
- **1776** — Creation of the Viceroyalty of the Río de la Plata weakens Lima's economic control.
- **1821** — José de San Martín proclaims Peruvian independence in Lima.
- **1879–1883** — The Chilean army occupies Lima during the War of the Pacific.
- **1940s–1980s** — Massive migration from the Andean interior causes explosive population growth and urban expansion.
- **1988** — The Historic Centre of Lima is declared a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
- **2007** — The El Metropolitano bus rapid transit system begins operations.
- **2010** — Lima Metro Line 1 is completed.
- **2019** — Lima hosts the Pan American Games.
- **2024** — The Port of Chancay, a major new port, is inaugurated.

## Body

### Precolonial and Colonial Foundations
The valleys of the Chillón, Rímac, and Lurín rivers were inhabited by pre-Inca cultures like the Lima and Maranga, later conquered by the Wari and then the Inca Empire. The area was known as *Limaq*, meaning "talker," after a famous oracle. Spanish conquistador Francisco Pizarro founded the city in 1535 on the site of a local lord's settlement, choosing the location for its fertile land and strategic coastal access. It became the capital of the Viceroyalty of Peru, a wealthy center of trade connecting the Americas, Europe, and Asia. The city was severely damaged by earthquakes in 1586 and 1746, leading to extensive rebuilding.

### Independence and Republican Era
After the Peruvian War of Independence, Lima became the capital of the new republic. Its development stalled until the 1850s, when guano export revenues funded public works. The city was occupied by Chilean forces during the War of the Pacific (1879–1883). In the 20th century, massive migration from the Andean highlands transformed Lima, causing rapid, often unplanned, urban expansion into districts like San Juan de Lurigancho and Villa El Salvador. This period also saw the construction of major government buildings and infrastructure.

### Modern Metropolis and Economy
Lima is Peru's undisputed economic engine, generating over half of the national GDP and hosting most major corporations and financial institutions. The city is a key international port via Callao and the new Port of Chancay. It has hosted numerous global events, including APEC summits and the Pan American Games. The urban landscape is marked by stark socioeconomic segregation, with affluent districts like San Isidro and Miraflores contrasting with densely populated, lower-income peripheral areas. Transportation challenges include severe traffic congestion, addressed by systems like the Metropolitano BRT and the expanding Metro.

### Culture and Society
Lima is a cultural melting pot, blending Spanish, Andean, African, and Asian influences. It is renowned as the "Gastronomical Capital of the Americas," with internationally acclaimed restaurants like Central and Maido. The Historic Centre showcases colonial architecture, while modern districts feature contemporary design. The city is home to major museums, universities (including the 1551-founded San Marcos), and a vibrant arts scene. The dominant religion is Catholicism, with the Señor de los Milagros procession being a major annual event. Football is the most popular sport, with clubs like Alianza Lima and Universitario de Deportes being national institutions.

## Terms
- **Primate city**: A city that is disproportionately larger and more influential than other cities in its country.
- **Viceroyalty**: A territory administered by a viceroy, a governor acting as the representative of a monarch in a colony.
- **Huaca**: A sacred site or shrine in pre-Columbian Andean cultures.
- **Pueblo joven**: A shantytown or young town, typically formed by informal settlement.
- **Garúa**: A persistent winter drizzle or mist characteristic of Lima's climate.
- **BRT (Bus Rapid Transit)**: A high-capacity bus-based public transport system with dedicated lanes.
- **Serenazgo**: A community policing structure in Peruvian districts.
- **Mestizo**: A person of mixed European and Indigenous American ancestry.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The pre-Inca population of the Lima valley is debated. While colonial chronicles mention "hunu" (a term for 10,000 families) of inhabitants, scholars note the term can also mean "countless," and estimates vary, with one suggestion being around 150,000 people during Inca times.
- The origin of the name "Lima" is linked to the native word *Limaq*, but the exact linguistic transition and the reason the Spanish name prevailed over other local names like "Ychma" are subjects of scholarly discussion.

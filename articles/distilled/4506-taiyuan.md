# Taiyuan

## Overview
Taiyuan is the capital of Shanxi province in northern China, a major political, economic, and cultural center with over 2,500 years of urban history. Its strategic location on the Fen River made it a frequent capital or provisional capital for numerous dynasties, earning it the nickname "City of the Dragon." Today, it serves as a key industrial base focused on energy and heavy chemicals.

## Body

### Historical Significance
Taiyuan's history begins with the founding of Jinyang in 497 BC. It was a key city during the Spring and Autumn and Warring States periods, notably in the 453 BC Battle of Jinyang. The name "Taiyuan" was first officially applied to the area as a commandery under the Qin dynasty in 248 BC. Throughout subsequent eras, including the Sixteen Kingdoms, Southern and Northern Dynasties, and Sui, Taiyuan remained a vital administrative and military center.

During the Tang dynasty, Taiyuan's status was elevated to the Northern Capital (Beidu) in 690 AD, reflecting its importance as the military base of the founding emperors. This role continued into the Five Dynasties period, when it served as a provisional capital for Later Tang and Later Jin. The city was the last stronghold of the Northern Han kingdom, which was conquered by the Song dynasty in 979. The Song emperor dealt harshly with the city, flooding and burning it, after which it was downgraded and later rebuilt.

From the Yuan dynasty onward, Taiyuan was a key garrison city. During the Ming and Qing dynasties, Shanxi merchants rose to prominence, and Taiyuan became a significant trading and financial center. In the 20th century, warlord Yan Xishan controlled Shanxi from Taiyuan, fostering industrial development and resisting Japanese invasion until 1937. The city was the last in Shanxi to fall to Communist forces in 1949.

### Geography and Climate
Taiyuan is located in central Shanxi on the Fen River. It has a cold semi-arid climate (Köppen BSk) with long, dry winters and hot, humid summers. The annual mean temperature is 10.9 °C (51.6 °F). The city is rich in natural resources, particularly coal, iron, and marble. The western satellite city of Gujiao is China's largest production site for metallurgical coal.

### Economy and Transportation
Taiyuan's economy is driven by its energy and heavy chemical industries. In 2018, its GDP was 388.450 billion yuan. The China Taiyuan Coal Transaction Center, opened in 2012, is a major hub. The city is a transportation hub with highways, railways (including high-speed lines to Beijing), and Taiyuan Wusu International Airport. The Taiyuan Metro began operations in 2020.

### Culture and Tourism
Taiyuan's local cuisine includes specialties like dao xiao noodles and "brain" (tounao) soup. Key tourist sites include the Shanxi Museum, the twin towers of Yongzuo Temple, and the ancient Jinci Temple complex. The Tianlongshan Grottoes, a damaged Buddhist cave complex, and the Taoist Longshan Grottoes are located in the western hills.

## Terms
- **Commandery (jùn):** A historical administrative division in China, similar to a province.
- **Provisional Capital (péidū, biédū):** A secondary or temporary capital of a state or dynasty.
- **Warlord:** A military commander with personal control over a region, especially in early 20th-century China.
- **Piaohao:** A type of draft bank or remittance house that flourished in Qing-dynasty China.
- **Köppen climate classification BSk:** A cold semi-arid climate with low precipitation.
- **Grottoes:** Artificial caves, often carved into rock, used for religious purposes like Buddhist or Taoist worship.

## Debates and Open Questions
The source notes that the name "Taiyuan" appeared in historical records before 248 BC, potentially referring to different regions in present-day southern and central Shanxi, making the exact early geographical reference a point of scholarly discussion.

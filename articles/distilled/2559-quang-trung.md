# Quang Trung

## Overview
Emperor Quang Trung (1753–1792), born Nguyễn Huệ, was the second emperor of the Tây Sơn dynasty and one of Vietnam's most successful military commanders. He and his brothers led the Tây Sơn rebellion, overthrowing the ruling Nguyễn and Trịnh lords and briefly unifying Vietnam. His reign was marked by brilliant military campaigns, including a decisive victory over Qing China, and ambitious reforms, but his early death led to the dynasty's rapid collapse.

## Timeline
- **1753** — Born as Nguyễn Huệ in Tây Sơn village, Quy Nhơn Province.
- **1771** — Tây Sơn rebellion begins, led by his brother Nguyễn Nhạc.
- **1777** — Tây Sơn forces capture Saigon, destroying the Nguyễn lords' army; Nguyễn Ánh escapes.
- **1785** — Nguyễn Huệ defeats a large Siamese invasion force at the Battle of Rạch Gầm–Xoài Mút.
- **1786** — Tây Sơn army marches north, captures Thăng Long (Hanoi), and restores the Lê dynasty.
- **1788** — Civil war erupts between brothers Nguyễn Nhạc and Nguyễn Huệ, ending in a partition.
- **1788-89** — Qing China invades Vietnam to restore the Lê emperor.
- **1789** — Nguyễn Huệ proclaims himself Emperor Quang Trung; defeats the Qing army at the Battle of Ngọc Hồi–Đống Đa.
- **1789** — Re-establishes tributary relations with Qing China under the name Nguyễn Quang Bình.
- **1792** — Dies suddenly at age 40; succeeded by his young son Nguyễn Quang Toản.
- **1802** — Tây Sơn dynasty overthrown by Nguyễn Ánh, who establishes the Nguyễn dynasty.

## Body

### Rebellion and Unification
The Tây Sơn brothers—Nguyễn Nhạc, Nguyễn Lữ, and Nguyễn Huệ—rose from peasant origins to lead a popular rebellion against the corrupt Nguyễn lords in the south and the Trịnh lords in the north. Their forces grew by adopting a policy of fairness and aiding the poor. After initial setbacks, they used diplomacy to buy time, then launched successful military campaigns. By 1777, they had destroyed the main Nguyễn army, though the heir Nguyễn Ánh escaped. In 1786, Nguyễn Huệ led a swift northern campaign, capturing the Trịnh capital of Phú Xuân and then Thăng Long, effectively ending Trịnh rule and restoring the Lê dynasty.

### Civil War and Southern Campaigns
Internal conflict arose when Nguyễn Nhạc proclaimed himself emperor, leading to a civil war with Nguyễn Huệ. After a siege, the brothers agreed to a truce, dividing their territories. Meanwhile, Nguyễn Ánh, with Siamese support, attempted to reclaim the south. In 1785, Nguyễn Huệ brilliantly defeated a large Siamese fleet at the Battle of Rạch Gầm–Xoài Mút, securing Tây Sơn control over the south for a time.

### War with Qing China
After the Lê emperor Lê Chiêu Thống fled to China, the Qianlong Emperor sent a massive army to restore him in 1788. In response, Nguyễn Huệ proclaimed himself Emperor Quang Trung and marched north with a large force. In a surprise attack during the Tết holiday in January 1789, his army annihilated the Qing forces at Ngọc Hồi and Đống Đa, killing several Qing generals. This victory forced China to recognize Tây Sơn rule, and Quang Trung was enfeoffed as the "King of Annam" by the Qing emperor.

### Rule and Reforms
As emperor, Quang Trung implemented extensive reforms. He reorganized the administration, created a new tax system based on age and land, and introduced a census with an identity card system. He promoted the use of chữ Nôm (Vietnamese demotic script) as the official written language and reformed Buddhist institutions. He also planned to move the capital to Nghệ An and harbored ambitions to invade China, supporting anti-Qing groups and Chinese pirates.

### Death and Legacy
Quang Trung died suddenly in 1792, possibly from a stroke. His death left the dynasty vulnerable; his successor was young and unable to maintain control. The Tây Sơn dynasty was overthrown by Nguyễn Ánh in 1802. Quang Trung is remembered as a national hero and military genius, deified in some traditions, and celebrated in modern Vietnam.

## Terms
- **Tây Sơn rebellion**: A late 18th-century Vietnamese uprising led by three brothers from Tây Sơn village that overthrew the ruling Nguyễn and Trịnh lords.
- **Chữ Nôm**: A logographic writing system using modified Chinese characters to represent Vietnamese words, promoted by Quang Trung as the official script.
- **Tributary relationship**: A diplomatic system where a state acknowledges the supremacy of a larger power (like China) through periodic tribute missions in exchange for recognition and trade.
- **Corvée (sưu dịch)**: A system of compulsory labor service owed to the state by adult males.
- **Capitation tax (thuế thân)**: A poll tax levied on adult males.
- **Đạo, cơ, đội**: The hierarchical units of the Tây Sơn military organization, roughly equivalent to divisions, regiments, and companies.
- **Tín bài**: A wooden identity card issued to adult males during Quang Trung's census, recording their name, birthplace, and fingerprints.
- **Phượng Hoàng Trung Đô**: The planned new capital city at the foot of Kỳ Lân Hill in Nghệ An Province, which was never fully realized.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The exact cause of Quang Trung's death is uncertain; official records are silent, and theories range from a stroke to legendary punishments.
- The precise location of his secret burial site remains unclear and is a subject of research.
- The extent and feasibility of his plans to invade Qing China are debated, as they were never executed.
- The historical assessment of his religious reforms and their impact on Vietnamese society continues to be studied.

# Pedro II of Brazil

## Overview
Dom Pedro II was the second and final Emperor of Brazil, reigning from 1831 to 1889. His long, stable reign transformed Brazil from a fragile post-colonial state into a respected international power, marked by political stability, economic growth, and the eventual abolition of slavery. He was a patron of education and science, but was ultimately deposed in a military coup, spending his final years in exile.

## Timeline
- **2 Dec 1825** — Born in Rio de Janeiro, son of Emperor Pedro I.
- **7 Apr 1831** — Father abdicates; Pedro II becomes emperor at age 5, beginning a regency period.
- **23 Jul 1840** — Declared of age by Parliament at 14, ending the regency.
- **4 Sep 1843** — Marries Princess Teresa Cristina of the Two Sicilies.
- **4 Sep 1850** — Eusébio de Queirós Law gives Brazil authority to suppress the illegal slave trade.
- **Feb 1852** — Overthrow of Argentine dictator Rosas in the Platine War enhances Brazil's hemispheric power.
- **28 Sep 1871** — Law of Free Birth is enacted, freeing children born to enslaved women.
- **1 Mar 1870** — Paraguayan War ends with the death of dictator Francisco Solano López.
- **15 Nov 1889** — Deposed in a military coup; the Brazilian Republic is proclaimed.
- **5 Dec 1891** — Dies in exile in Paris, France.
- **1921** — Remains repatriated to Brazil with national honors.

## Body

### Early Life and Consolidation
Pedro II became emperor as a child after his father's abdication, leading to a regency period marked by instability. His rigorous, lonely upbringing instilled a strong sense of duty but also a personal ambivalence toward monarchy. He was declared of age in 1840, and his presence helped stabilize the government. He married Teresa Cristina in 1843, a union that began poorly but eventually produced four children, though only his daughter Isabel survived to adulthood. The early part of his reign saw the suppression of the illegal slave trade and military victories in the Platine War, establishing Brazil as a hemispheric power.

### Governance and Domestic Policy
Pedro II governed through cooperation with elected politicians, maintaining a reputation for impartiality and diligence. His reign was characterized by the *conciliação* (conciliation) and *melhoramentos* (material developments), which promoted infrastructure projects like railroads and telegraphs, uniting the nation. He was a dedicated patron of arts and sciences, founding institutions and corresponding with intellectuals worldwide. Domestically, he lived simply and held officials to high standards of merit and morality.

### The Paraguayan War and Abolition
The Paraguayan War (1864–1870) was a defining conflict. Pedro II personally traveled to the front, earning the title "First Fatherland Volunteer," and his resolve was pivotal in securing total victory, though at great cost. The war delayed but did not stop his long-term goal of ending slavery. He used his influence to support gradual abolition, facing significant political backlash. The Law of Free Birth (1871) was a major step, and he celebrated the final abolition of slavery in 1888 from his sickbed in Europe.

### Decline and Deposition
In his later years, Pedro II grew weary of office and doubtful of the monarchy's future without a male heir. A new generation of politicians, unfamiliar with the instability of the regency, saw him as less essential. The abolition of slavery alienated powerful coffee farmers, who shifted support to republicanism. On 15 November 1889, a military coup led by republican elites overthrew the monarchy with little popular resistance. Pedro II accepted deposition without protest, saying, "If it is so, it will be my retirement."

### Exile and Legacy
Pedro II spent his final years in modest exile in Europe, dying in Paris in 1891. His remains were returned to Brazil in 1921 with national honors, marking a reconciliation with the monarchical past. Historians consistently rank him among Brazil's greatest statesmen, praising his character and the stability of his reign, often in contrast to the turbulent republican era that followed.

## Terms
- **Regency**: A period of rule by appointed officials when the monarch is a minor.
- **Conciliação (Conciliation)**: A political policy of reducing partisan conflict and fostering cooperation.
- **Eusébio de Queirós Law**: 1850 law that gave Brazil authority to suppress the transatlantic slave trade.
- **Law of Free Birth**: 1871 law that freed all children born to enslaved women from that date forward.
- **Fatherland Volunteer**: Title given to citizens who volunteered for the Paraguayan War; Pedro II was called the "number-one volunteer."
- **Ultramontanism**: A Catholic movement emphasizing papal authority over national churches.
- **Positivism**: A philosophical system advocating rule by scientific experts, influential among Brazilian military officers.

## Debates and Open Questions
- Historians debate the primary cause of the monarchy's fall, attributing it variously to Pedro II's personal weariness, the rise of republicanism among elites, or the military's growing political ambitions.
- The degree to which Pedro II's support for abolition directly caused the loss of elite support for the monarchy is a subject of ongoing analysis.
- Scholars assess whether the monarchy's stability was due to Pedro II's personal character or to the strength of Brazil's political institutions during his reign.

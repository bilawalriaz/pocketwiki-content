# Artist

## Overview
An artist is a person who creates art or practices the arts, most commonly referring to visual artists like painters, but also used for performers and writers. The definition is indeterminate because there is no agreed-upon definition of art itself, leading to ongoing philosophical debates about who qualifies as an artist.

## Body

### Definition and Etymology
The word "artist" derives from Middle French *artiste*, from Medieval Latin *artista*, from Latin *ars* (art) + *-ista*. Its earliest known use as a noun is from the mid-1500s. Dictionaries define an artist variously as a creator of art, a skilled practitioner of visual arts, a performer, or a person very skilled at something. The related Greek term *technē* implies mastery of any craft. Despite common usage, the lack of a consensus definition of art makes the boundary of who is an artist indeterminate.

### Historical Concept
In ancient Greece, the nine Muses oversaw fields like epic poetry, history, and music, but none presided over visual arts. Sculptors and painters were considered manual laborers, often slaves. During the Middle Ages, "artist" in places like Italy meant a skilled craftsman, with excellence emphasized over the field of work. The division between major (fine) and minor (applied) arts began with Renaissance figures like Leon Battista Alberti, who stressed intellectual skill. European academies in the late 17th century solidified this gap. Contemporary definitions are often culturally contingent and resist standardization.

### Artists' Manifestos and Movements
Artists may create manifestos to explain their work or join movements with shared ideologies. The 1909 *Manifesto of Futurism* by Filippo Tommaso Marinetti glorified speed, machinery, and war, and called for the destruction of museums and libraries. Marinetti later allied with Benito Mussolini's Fascist regime in Italy from the 1920s to 1940s, hoping to make Futurism the official state art. Some modern artists, like singer Doechii, view their role as including civic responsibility, using their platform to advocate for social causes.

### Education and Employment
In the US, the Bureau of Labor Statistics classifies visual artists as craft artists (making functional works like pottery) or fine artists (making paintings, sculptures for aesthetic value). Skill is developed through long-term practice, often supplemented by university study. About half of US artists are self-employed, with others working in industries like manufacturing or publishing. The median income for craft and fine artists is approximately US$56,260 per year, lower than the US$61,000 median for all art-related fields. Job growth for artists is slower than in other fields.

## Terms
- **Artist**: A person who creates art or practices the arts, with definitions ranging from visual creators to skilled performers.
- **Technē**: A Greek term often translated as "art," implying mastery of any craft or skill.
- **Fine arts**: Arts pursued for aesthetic value, such as painting, sculpture, and music, historically distinguished from applied or mechanical arts.
- **Applied arts**: Arts or crafts where design is applied to functional objects, like pottery or textiles.
- **Manifesto**: A public declaration of aims and beliefs by an artist or movement, such as the Futurist Manifesto.
- **Atelier**: A workshop or studio, especially for artists; a royal manuscript atelier was an artist collective.
- **Craft artist**: An artist who makes handmade functional works, like pottery or clothing.
- **Fine artist**: An artist who creates works primarily for aesthetic value, such as paintings or sculptures.

## Debates and Open questions
The central debate is the lack of an agreed-upon definition of art, which makes the definition of who is an artist indeterminate. Artists and philosophers continue to debate what does and does not count as art. This metaphysical inquiry into the essence of a work of art remains unresolved.

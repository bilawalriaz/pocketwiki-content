# History of Turkey

## Overview
Turkey's history spans Anatolia and Eastern Thrace, a crossroads of civilizations from Neolithic settlements to the Byzantine and Ottoman Empires. The Republic of Turkey, established in 1923, transformed the Ottoman remnants into a secular, nationalist state.

## Body

### Prehistory and Ancient Anatolia
Inhabited since the late Paleolithic, Turkey contains early Neolithic sites like Göbekli Tepe and Çatalhöyük. Part of the Fertile Crescent, historical records begin c. 2000 BC from Kültepe. The region hosted diverse languages, including Hattian and Hittite (the oldest attested Indo-European language). The Hittite kingdom collapsed c. 1200 BC, followed by states like Phrygia and Lydia.

### Classical Antiquity and Roman/Byzantine Rule
After the Hittites, Anatolia was part of the Achaemenid Persian Empire. The Ionian Revolt (early 5th century BC) sparked the Greco-Persian Wars. Alexander's conquest (334–330 BC) began Hellenization. By the mid-1st century BC, it was incorporated into the Roman Republic. In 324, Constantine I made Constantinople the Roman capital. After 395, the Eastern Roman (Byzantine) Empire ruled Anatolia until the Late Middle Ages, serving as an early Christian center.

### Early Turkic Peoples and the Seljuks
Turkic peoples originated in Central Asia. The Göktürk Khanate (6th–8th centuries) was the first major Turkic state. The Seljuk Empire, founded in 1037, was highly Persianized and conquered eastern Anatolia. Their successor, the Seljuks of Rum, ruled until the Mongol invasion, leading to Anatolian beyliks.

### The Ottoman Empire and its Collapse
The Ottoman beylik, founded in the late 13th century, made Bursa its capital in 1326. After capturing Constantinople in 1453, it expanded into a vast empire, peaking under Suleiman the Magnificent. The Treaty of Karlowitz (1699) began its decline. After World War I defeat, the Treaty of Sèvres (1920) was imposed but never ratified, prompting the Turkish War of Independence.

### The Republic of Turkey
The Turkish Grand National Assembly declared itself the legitimate government in 1920. The Republic was proclaimed on 29 October 1923, with the Treaty of Lausanne recognizing its sovereignty. Mustafa Kemal Atatürk initiated secular, nationalist reforms, including a Latin alphabet, women's rights, and abolishing the caliphate. Turkey joined NATO in 1952. The modern era includes military coups (1960, 1971, 1980), the Kurdish–Turkish conflict (since 1984), economic liberalization, and the AKP's rise in 2002, followed by a 2017 referendum expanding presidential powers.

## Terms
- **Anatolia**: Asian Turkey, a peninsula between the Black Sea and Mediterranean.
- **Eastern Thrace**: European Turkey, east of the Maritsa River.
- **Hellenization**: Spread of Greek language and culture.
- **Satrapy**: Province of the Achaemenid Persian Empire.
- **Seljuks**: Medieval Turkic dynasty creating a vast empire.
- **Beylik**: Turkish princely state, especially post-Seljuk Anatolian principalities.
- **Ottomanism**: Pluralist Ottoman imperial identity, declining in the 19th century.
- **Turkification**: Policies promoting Turkish language and culture.
- **Atatürk's Reforms**: Secular, modernizing reforms establishing the Republic.

## Debates and Open Questions
- Indo-European language origins may be Anatolian or non-native.
- Historicity of the Trojan War is uncertain.
- Huns' ethnic origin is debated (Turkic vs. Mongolic).
- Extent of gender equality achieved by Atatürk's reforms is analyzed.

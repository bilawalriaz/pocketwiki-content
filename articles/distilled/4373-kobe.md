# Kobe

## Overview
Kobe is a major port city in Japan's Kansai region, serving as the capital of Hyōgo Prefecture. It is Japan's seventh-largest city and third-largest port, known for its cosmopolitan history, economic significance, and cultural landmarks like Kobe beef and Arima Onsen. Its development from an ancient anchorage to a modern metropolis reflects Japan's periods of isolation, opening, and resilience.

## Timeline
- **AD 201** — Founding of Ikuta Shrine by Empress Jingū, as recorded in the *Nihon Shoki*.
- **1180** — Briefly becomes Japan's capital when Taira no Kiyomori moves Emperor Antoku to Fukuhara-kyō.
- **1184** — Battle of Ichi-no-Tani between Taira and Minamoto clans occurs at the Taira fortress in Hyōgo-ku.
- **13th c.** — Port becomes known as Hyōgo Port during the Kamakura period, growing as a trade hub.
- **1868** — Hyōgo Port is opened to foreign trade by the Shogunal government.
- **1889** — Kobe, as a unified city, is founded on April 1.
- **1945** — City is heavily bombed on March 16-17, causing 8,841 deaths and destroying 21% of its urban area.
- **1956** — Kobe is designated a city by government ordinance.
- **1975** — City council passes an ordinance banning nuclear-armed vessels from the port (the "Kobe formula").
- **1995** — The Great Hanshin earthquake (magnitude 6.9) kills 6,434 people and devastates the city.

## Body

### History and Development
The area has been inhabited since the Jōmon period, with its natural geography leading to the development of a port at Wada Cape. The port, known as Ōwada Anchorage in the Nara and Heian periods, was a departure point for imperial embassies to China. During the Edo period, the region was politically fragmented until the modern prefecture system was established in 1871. Kobe's modern identity began with its 1868 opening to foreign trade and its formal founding in 1889. The city's name derives from *kanbe*, an archaic term for supporters of the Ikuta Shrine.

### 20th Century and the Great Hanshin Earthquake
Kobe was lightly bombed in the 1942 Doolittle Raid and heavily bombed in 1945 during World War II. Post-war, the city developed a distinctive nonproliferation policy in 1975, banning nuclear-armed vessels from its port. The defining modern event was the Great Hanshin earthquake on January 17, 1995. The magnitude 6.9 quake killed over 6,400 people, made 212,443 homeless, and destroyed large parts of the port and infrastructure, including the dramatic collapse of the Hanshin Expressway. The earthquake diminished Kobe's status as Japan's busiest port, though it remains the fourth-busiest. Recovery is commemorated annually by the Luminarie light festival.

### Geography and Urban Structure
Kobe is a long, narrow city wedged between the coast of Osaka Bay and mountains like Mount Rokkō (931 m). It is divided into nine wards. The commercial heart is Chūō-ku, containing Sannomiya, Motomachi, and Harborland. Hyōgo-ku is the historical port area. The city has expanded onto artificial islands, Port Island and Rokkō Island. The Akashi Kaikyō Bridge, the world's second-longest suspension bridge, connects Tarumi-ku to Awaji Island.

### Economy and Industry
The Port of Kobe is a major container port and the core of the Hanshin Industrial Region. As of 2004, the city's GDP was ¥6.3 trillion, with 78% of employment in the service sector. Major Japanese companies headquartered in Kobe include ASICS, Kawasaki Heavy Industries, and Kobe Steel. Over 100 international corporations have Asian or Japanese headquarters there, including Eli Lilly, Nestlé, and Procter & Gamble. The city is also a hub for research institutes like RIKEN and the Asian Disaster Reduction Center.

### Culture and Society
Kobe is famed for Kobe beef, raised in Hyōgo Prefecture, and the Arima Onsen hot spring resort. It has a strong cosmopolitan identity, reflected in its 19th-century Western-style residences (*ijinkan*) in Kitano and its status as a UNESCO "Design City." Cultural events include Kobe Fashion Week and the annual Kobe Jazz Street festival. The city is home to Japan's first golf course (1903) and first mosque (1935). The local dialect is Kobe-ben.

## Terms
- **Kansai region:** A region on the southern side of Honshū, containing major cities like Osaka, Kyoto, and Kobe.
- **Keihanshin:** The metropolitan area comprising Kobe, Osaka, and Kyoto.
- **Designated city:** A Japanese city status granted to large cities, giving them functions normally handled by the prefecture.
- **Hanshin Industrial Region:** An industrial area along the coast of Osaka Bay, including Kobe and Osaka.
- **Ikuta Shrine:** An ancient Shinto shrine in Kobe, whose founding is recorded in the *Nihon Shoki*.
- **Kobe formula:** A 1975 city ordinance banning vessels carrying nuclear weapons from Kobe Port.
- **Great Hanshin earthquake:** The 1995 earthquake that devastated Kobe, also known as the Hanshin-Awaji earthquake.
- **Luminarie:** An annual illumination festival in Kobe held to commemorate recovery from the 1995 earthquake.
- **Ijinkan:** Western-style residences from the Meiji era, primarily found in Kobe's Kitano area.

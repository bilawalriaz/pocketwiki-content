# Miyamoto Musashi

## Overview
Miyamoto Musashi (c. 1583–1645) was a legendary Japanese swordsman, strategist, and artist, renowned for his undefeated record in 62 duels and his creation of the Niten Ichi-ryū two-sword fighting style. He is considered a *kensei* (sword saint) of Japan. His enduring influence stems from his martial prowess and his philosophical writings, particularly *The Book of Five Rings* and *The Path of Aloneness*, which offer insights into strategy and a life of disciplined focus.

## Timeline
- **c. 1583** — Born in Miyamoto village, Harima Province.
- **c. 1595** — Wins first duel at age 12-13 against Arima Kihei.
- **1600** — Serves in Kuroda Yoshitaka's army (Eastern side) in the Sekigahara campaign.
- **1604** — Defeats Sasaki Kojirō in a famous duel on Ganryū Island.
- **1614** — Participates in the Siege of Osaka under the Tokugawa army.
- **1633** — Becomes a retainer of Hosokawa Tadatoshi in Kumamoto.
- **1638** — Allegedly participates in the suppression of the Shimabara Rebellion.
- **1643** — Retires to Reigandō cave to write *The Book of Five Rings*.
- **1645** — Dies around 13 June at age 62, bequeathing his works to his student Terao Magonojō.

## Biography
### Early Life and First Duels
Details of Musashi's early life are difficult to verify. He was born c. 1583 in Miyamoto village. His father, Shinmen Munisai, was an accomplished martial artist. Musashi's mother, Omasa, died giving birth to him. He developed eczema in infancy and, according to some accounts, never bathed or married. His first recorded duel was at age 12-13, and he left his village at 15 or 16. By 21, he had defeated several students of a famous Kyoto sword school.

### Military Campaigns and Famous Duels
In 1600, Musashi served in Kuroda Yoshitaka's army on the Eastern side, fighting in the Battle of Ishigakihara. While popular theory long placed him on the Western Army at Sekigahara, modern research suggests he and his father sided with the Tokugawa (Eastern Army). His most famous duel was in 1604 against Sasaki Kojirō on Ganryū Island. Musashi arrived late, having carved a wooden oar into a *bokken* (practice sword) during his boat journey, and defeated Kojirō with a fatal strike.

### Later Service and Final Years
Musashi served the Tokugawa shogunate in the Siege of Osaka (1614) and the Shimabara Rebellion (1638), often under his friend, General Mizuno Katsunari. In 1633, he became a retainer of Hosokawa Tadatoshi in Kumamoto, where he trained, painted, and wrote. In 1643, he retired to a cave to write *The Book of Five Rings*. He died c. 13 June 1645, reportedly from lung cancer, at the age of 62.

## Personal Life and Legacy
### Niten Ichi-ryū
Musashi founded the Niten Ichi-ryū ("two heavens as one") style of swordsmanship, which involves using a long sword (*katana*) and a short companion sword (*wakizashi*) simultaneously. The technique may have been inspired by temple drummers or his father's *jutte* (a parrying weapon) techniques. He was also an expert in throwing weapons like *shuriken*.

### Art and Philosophy
Musashi was a skilled calligrapher and ink painter, mastering the "broken ink" landscape style. He believed strategy could be applied to all arts and crafts. His philosophy, expressed in *The Book of Five Rings* and *The Path of Aloneness*, emphasized practical mastery and self-reliance, stating, "Respect Buddha and the gods without counting on their help."

## Terms
*   **Kensei**: "Sword saint," a title of honor for a master swordsman.
*   **Niten Ichi-ryū**: The two-sword fighting style founded by Musashi.
*   **Katana**: A long, curved Japanese sword.
*   **Wakizashi**: A shorter companion sword worn with the katana.
*   **Bokken**: A wooden practice sword.
*   **Jutte**: A metal truncheon used for parrying and disarming.
*   **Shuriken**: A concealed throwing weapon, such as a star or spike.
*   **Daimyō**: A powerful feudal lord in Japan.
*   **Koku**: A unit of rice yield, used to measure a fief's wealth and a retainer's stipend.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Sekigahara Allegiance**: While historians agree Musashi favored the Eastern Army, it is debated whether he fought at the main Battle of Sekigahara or in the concurrent Battle of Ishigakihara.
*   **Personal Life**: The extent of his relationships, including rumored connections to courtesans like Kumoi and Yoshino Tayu, and whether he fathered a daughter, remains uncertain.
*   **Death Location**: Sources disagree on whether he died in the Reigandō cave or at Chiba Castle in Kumamoto.

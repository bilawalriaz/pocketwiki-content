# São Tomé and Príncipe

## Overview
São Tomé and Príncipe is a small island nation in the Gulf of Guinea, formed by volcanic activity. Its history is defined by Portuguese colonization, a plantation economy reliant on enslaved Africans, and a peaceful transition to independence, making it one of Africa's most stable democracies.

## Timeline
- **1470** — Portuguese explorers João de Santarém and Pedro Escobar discover the uninhabited islands.
- **1493** — First successful settlement of São Tomé established by Álvaro Caminha.
- **1515** — Introduction of a water-powered sugar mill leads to a sugar boom and mass importation of enslaved Africans.
- **1595** — Largest slave revolt led by Amador destroys 60 of 85 sugar mills.
- **19th c.** — Sugar cultivation declines; coffee and cocoa are introduced as new cash crops.
- **1905** — São Tomé becomes the world's largest producer of cocoa.
- **1953** — "Batepá Massacre" occurs, a clash between African laborers and Portuguese rulers.
- **1975** — Peaceful independence achieved; Manuel Pinto da Costa becomes first president.
- **1990** — Democratic reforms begin; multiparty system established.
- **2012** — UNESCO establishes the Island of Príncipe Biosphere Reserve.
- **2025** — Biosphere reserve extended to include São Tomé, making the entire country a UNESCO site.

## Body

### Colonial History and Plantation Economy
The islands were uninhabited until their discovery by Portugal in 1470. Settlement began in the late 15th century, with the first successful colony on São Tomé in 1493. The volcanic soil and equatorial location made the islands ideal for sugar cultivation, which began in earnest after a water-powered mill was introduced in 1515. This created a plantation economy heavily dependent on enslaved Africans imported from the mainland. By the mid-16th century, São Tomé was Africa's foremost sugar exporter. The slave trade remained central to the economy until after 1600.

The plantation system, known as the *roças*, led to frequent slave rebellions and maroon settlements in the interior. The largest revolt occurred in 1595. Competition from the Americas and the difficulty of controlling the enslaved population led to a decline in sugar cultivation by the mid-17th century. In the 19th century, coffee and cocoa were introduced, and by 1905, São Tomé was the world's largest cocoa producer. Although Portugal officially abolished slavery in 1876, forced labor continued, leading to unrest and the 1953 Batepá Massacre.

### Independence and Modern Politics
A nationalist movement, the MLSTP, formed in the late 1950s. After Portugal's 1974 revolution, negotiations led to peaceful independence on 12 July 1975. The country began as a one-party communist state. In 1990, it became one of the first African countries to adopt democratic reforms, legalizing opposition parties and holding free elections in 1991. Since then, it has maintained a stable multiparty democracy.

The political system features a president elected for a five-year term and a prime minister appointed by the president. The National Assembly is the legislative body. The country has experienced a few attempted coups, notably in 2003 and 2022, but they were quickly resolved. São Tomé and Príncipe maintains close ties with Portugal and Angola and is a founding member of the Community of Portuguese Language Countries.

### Economy and Demographics
The economy is based on plantation agriculture, with cocoa as the main export crop. Following independence, plantations were nationalized, but economic difficulties in the 1980s and 1990s led to privatization reforms. The country imports most of its food and relies on foreign aid. Potential exists in tourism and offshore petroleum exploration, with a joint development zone agreed with Nigeria in 2001.

The population of about 201,800 (2018 estimate) is predominantly of African and mixed European-African (*mestiço*) descent. Portuguese is the official language, but several Portuguese-based creoles are spoken. The culture blends African and Portuguese influences, visible in music, dance, and cuisine. The country has a high literacy rate and good social indicators for its income level.

## Terms
*   **Roças:** The plantation system that dominated the islands' economy, characterized by large estates managed with a high degree of authority over workers.
*   **Mestiço:** A person of mixed European and African descent.
*   **Maroons:** Escaped enslaved Africans who formed independent communities in the islands' interior.
*   **Macambos:** The settlements established by maroon populations in the inhospitable mountain forests.
*   **Lusophone:** Relating to Portuguese-speaking countries and cultures.
*   **Biosphere Reserve:** A UNESCO designation recognizing areas of ecological and cultural importance for conservation and sustainable development.
*   **Joint Development Zone (JDZ):** An area of overlapping maritime claims between São Tomé and Príncipe and Nigeria, established for cooperative petroleum exploration.

## Debates and open questions
*   The exact dates of the initial European discovery are debated, with sources citing 1470, 1471, or 1472 for the different islands.
*   The long-term economic impact of potential petroleum revenues remains uncertain, with ongoing debates about how to manage and distribute the wealth.

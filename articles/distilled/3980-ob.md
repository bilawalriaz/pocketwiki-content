# Ob

## Overview
The Ob is a major river in western Siberia, Russia, which, with its tributary the Irtysh, forms the world's seventh-longest river system. It is one of three great Siberian rivers flowing into the Arctic Ocean and is vital for regional transport, irrigation, and energy, though it faces significant pollution challenges from industrial activity.

## Timeline
- **11th century** — Novgorodians aware of western Siberian lands (Yugra) and establish trade routes to the Ob.
- **Late 16th century** — Russian settlements of Beryozov, Obdorsk, and Surgut founded on the Ob.
- **1836** — First steamboat appears on the Ob at Tyumen.
- **1845** — Steamboats begin navigating the middle reaches of the Ob.
- **1870s** — Christian Dahl explores the Ob's navigability, opening a trade route from Siberia to Europe.
- **1885** — Railway reaches Tyumen, linking it to European Russia.
- **Late 19th century** — A canal system connecting the Ob and Yenisei via the Ket River is built but soon abandoned.
- **1916** — 49 steamers operate on the Ob.
- **1956** — A dam near Novosibirsk creates the Novosibirsk Reservoir.
- **1960s-1980s** — A proposed project to divert Ob and Irtysh waters to Central Asia is abandoned in 1986.

## Body

### Geography and Formation
The Ob forms at the confluence of the Biya and Katun rivers southwest of Biysk in the Altai Mountains. The Biya originates in Lake Teletskoye, while the Katun begins in a glacier on Mount Byelukha. The river flows generally northwest and then north, splitting into multiple arms after the Irtysh, its chief tributary, joins it. The Irtysh originates in China and is the furthest source of the Ob system. The combined Ob-Irtysh system is 5,410 km long with a basin of 2,990,000 km². The river empties into the Gulf of Ob, the world's longest estuary, which separates the Yamal and Gyda Peninsulas.

### Human Use and History
The Ob basin has been used for centuries for transport, fishing, and irrigation. Novgorodians established trade routes to the river by the 11th century. Russian settlements were founded in the late 16th century. Navigation was crucial before the Trans-Siberian Railway, with steamboats operating from the mid-19th century. The railway, which crosses the Ob at Novosibirsk, provided more direct east-west transport, but the river system remained important for connecting remote regions. A dam built in 1956 created the Novosibirsk Reservoir. A major Soviet-era project to divert water to replenish the Aral Sea was abandoned in 1986 for economic and environmental reasons.

### Pollution
The Ob's catchment area is part of the West Siberian petroleum basin, and oil and gas infrastructure are major pollution sources. As of 2019, anthropogenic loading is significantly increased. In the lower reaches, petroleum product concentrations exceed permissible limits by 9–10 times, and oxygen levels are four times lower than normal. Studies identify phenols, hydrocarbons, and heavy metals as key pollutants from both natural and human sources.

## Terms
- **Confluence**: The junction where two or more rivers meet and flow together.
- **Estuary**: A tidal mouth of a large river, where the tide meets the stream; the Gulf of Ob is the world's longest.
- **Drainage basin**: The area of land where precipitation collects and drains off into a common outlet, like a river.
- **Hydroelectric power**: Electricity generated from the energy of falling or flowing water, often using dams.
- **Tributary**: A river or stream that flows into a larger river or lake.
- **Taiga**: A biome characterized by coniferous forests, also known as boreal forest.
- **Steppe**: A large area of flat grassland with few trees, typical of semi-arid regions.
- **Tundra**: A biome where tree growth is hindered by low temperatures and short growing seasons.

## Debates and Open Questions
The etymology of the name "Ob" is debated. One theory derives it from a Proto-Indo-Iranian word for "river," while another suggests a Finno-Ugric origin. Scholarly analysis continues regarding the most probable linguistic source.

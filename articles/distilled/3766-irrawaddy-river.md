# Irrawaddy River

## Overview
The Irrawaddy River is Myanmar's principal waterway, running about 1,350 miles (2,170 km) from north to south through the country's center. It is the nation's most important commercial artery, supporting agriculture, fisheries, and transport, and its basin covers 61% of Myanmar's land area. The river's ecological and economic significance makes it a focal point for development projects and environmental concern.

## Timeline
- **c. 6th century** — River used for trade and transport; irrigation canals developed for agriculture.
- **12th century** — Well-developed canal network supports flourishing rice cultivation.
- **1934** — Ava (Innwa) Bridge, the first bridge across the Irrawaddy, is built.
- **1942** — Ava Bridge destroyed by retreating British Army in WWII.
- **1954** — Ava Bridge rebuilt after Burmese independence.
- **May 2007** — Myanmar's military junta signs agreement with China Power Investment Corporation for seven hydroelectric dams.
- **2008** — 5.3 magnitude earthquake near Myanmar-China border raises safety concerns about dam projects.
- **2011** — Construction of the controversial Myitsone Dam is suspended.

## Body

### Physiography and Course
The Irrawaddy originates from the confluence of the N'mai and Mali Rivers in Kachin State, with sources in Himalayan glaciers. The N'mai is larger but unnavigable due to strong currents, while the smaller Mali is navigable. The river flows through three defiles (narrow gorges) between Myitkyina and Mandalay before entering the central Dry Zone. It makes an abrupt westward turn after Mandalay, a geologically recent change from its probable original southward course. The river empties through the nine-armed Irrawaddy Delta into the Andaman Sea. The delta's fertile silt deposits cause it to extend into the sea at about 50 meters (160 ft) per year.

### Hydrology and Sediment
The river's volume varies greatly due to monsoonal rains (mid-May to mid-October) and summer snowmelt. Average discharge near the delta head ranges from a low of 2,300 to a high of 32,600 cubic meters per second. Water levels at Mandalay and Prome can vary by over 9.6 meters (31.7 ft) between flood and low-water stages. The Irrawaddy and Salween rivers collectively deliver over 600 million tons of sediment to the sea annually. Studies indicate complex, bi-directional sediment transport controlled by tides and monsoon winds.

### Ecology and Biodiversity
The river basin hosts distinct ecoregions. The Northern Mountains feature subtropical and temperate forests. The Central Basin, within the dry zone, is dominated by Irrawaddy moist deciduous forests, now largely altered for farming. The Delta area consists of mangroves and freshwater swamps, supporting diverse birdlife and mammals like the Malayan sambar deer and wild boar. The endangered Irrawaddy dolphin is a notable species found in the river's lower reaches. The basin is estimated to contain 119–195 endemic fish species.

### Economy and Transportation
The river has been a key trade route since the 6th century. Today, it remains Myanmar's largest commercial waterway, transporting rice, teak logs, petroleum, and other goods. Over 3,200 km of navigable waterways exist in the delta. Commercial navigation is maintained year-round for about 1,300 km from Hinthada to Bhamo. Unregulated gold mining during the 2021 civil war has led to severe mercury and cadmium contamination in parts of the river.

### Dams and Controversy
In 2007, the junta agreed to build seven hydroelectric dams totaling 15,160 MW, with most power destined for export to China. The largest, the 3,600 MW Myitsone Dam at the river's source, is highly controversial. Its reservoir would flood 766 km², displacing 47 villages and submerging cultural sites. Environmental concerns include blocking fish migration, reducing sediment flow to the fertile delta, and impacting a biodiversity hotspot. The dam's location near a major fault line also raises earthquake safety concerns. Construction was suspended in 2011.

## Terms
- **Defile**: A narrow gorge or pass through which a river flows, often flanked by steep cliffs.
- **Euryhaline**: An organism, like the Irrawaddy dolphin, that can tolerate a wide range of salinities in water.
- **Endemic**: Species found naturally in only one specific geographic area.
- **Alluvium**: Soil, silt, or sediment deposited by flowing water, especially in a river delta.
- **Distributary**: A stream that branches off and flows away from a main river channel, common in deltas.
- **Hydrography**: The study of the measurement and description of the physical features of bodies of water.
- **Girdling**: A forestry practice of cutting a ring through the bark and sapwood to kill a tree, used here to season teak so it floats.
- **Ecoregion**: An area of land or water that contains a distinct assemblage of natural communities and species.

## Debates and Open Questions
The primary debate centers on the proposed Myitsone Dam and similar hydroelectric projects. Key open questions involve balancing national energy needs and export revenue against irreversible ecological damage, displacement of communities, and threats to the agricultural productivity of the Irrawaddy Delta. The long-term impact of suspended dam projects and the effectiveness of mitigation for river pollution from mining remain critical issues.

# Ottawa

## Overview
Ottawa is the capital of Canada, located in southeastern Ontario at the confluence of the Ottawa and Rideau Rivers. It houses the federal government, Parliament, and national institutions. Founded as Bytown in 1826, it was selected as the capital in 1857 and renamed Ottawa in 1855. The city is a major economic, cultural, and educational hub with a population over one million and a metropolitan area of nearly 1.5 million.

## Timeline
- **c. 8000-8500 years ago** — First evidence of human presence in the Ottawa Valley via spearpoints.
- **1800** — Philemon Wright founds the first non-Indigenous settlement, a lumber town, on the north side of the river.
- **1826** — Bytown is established as a town for the Rideau Canal construction.
- **1832** — Rideau Canal is completed, connecting the Ottawa River to Lake Ontario.
- **1855** — Bytown is renamed Ottawa and obtains city status.
- **1857** — Queen Victoria selects Ottawa as the capital of the Province of Canada.
- **1859-1866** — Original Parliament buildings are constructed on Parliament Hill.
- **1900** — A major fire destroys much of Hull and spreads to Ottawa.
- **1916** — The Centre Block of Parliament is destroyed by fire.
- **1950** — The Gréber Plan is adopted, reshaping the National Capital Region's urban landscape.
- **2001** — Amalgamation creates the single-tier City of Ottawa from the former Regional Municipality of Ottawa–Carleton.
- **2019** — The Confederation Line, a light rail transit system, opens in downtown Ottawa.

## Body

### Early History and European Settlement
The Ottawa Valley was inhabited by Algonquin people for millennia before European contact. The first documented European to navigate the Ottawa River was Étienne Brûlé in 1610. Philemon Wright founded the first permanent non-Indigenous settlement in 1800, establishing a lumber trade. The town of Bytown was created in 1826 to support Rideau Canal construction, a military supply route. The canal's completion in 1832 spurred growth; the town was incorporated in 1850 and renamed Ottawa in 1855.

### Selection as Capital
The choice of Ottawa was contentious, following decades of debate and legislative relocations. In 1857, Queen Victoria, advised by Governor General Edmund Head, selected Ottawa for its defensible location, position on the border between Upper and Lower Canada, and existing transportation links. Parliament buildings were constructed between 1859 and 1866.

### Growth and Development
Ottawa's early economy was dominated by lumber. After Confederation, it solidified as the federal capital, though it faced challenges like the 1900 Hull-Ottawa fire and a 1911 typhoid outbreak. The federal government became the city's primary employer. The 1950 Gréber Plan altered the city's appearance, creating green spaces. From the 1980s, Ottawa grew into a technology centre, earning the nickname "Silicon Valley North." A major amalgamation in 2001 created the current single-tier city. Recent developments include light rail expansion and the 2022 Freedom Convoy occupation.

### Geography and Climate
Ottawa is on the south bank of the Ottawa River, with a mix of urban, suburban, and rural areas. The Rideau Canal, a UNESCO World Heritage Site, runs through the city. The climate is humid continental with four distinct seasons: cold, snowy winters and warm, humid summers. It lies within the Western Quebec Seismic Zone.

### Demographics and Culture
Ottawa is bilingual, with English and French as co-official languages. Its population is diverse, with significant immigrant communities. It attracts over 11 million visitors annually to landmarks like Parliament Hill, national museums, and festivals such as Winterlude and Canada Day. It hosts six of Canada's nine national museums.

### Economy and Education
The federal government is the largest employer. Technology, health, and tourism are major drivers. Ottawa has the highest proportion of university-educated residents in Canada. Key institutions include the University of Ottawa (the world's largest English-French bilingual university) and Carleton University. It hosts numerous national medical and research organizations.

### Government and Transportation
Ottawa is governed by a mayor and city council. As the national capital, it hosts about 150 embassies. Public transit is managed by OC Transpo, operating the O-Train light rail and bus rapid transit. The city is served by Ottawa Macdonald–Cartier International Airport and major highways.

## Terms
- **Amalgamation**: The 2001 merger creating the single-tier City of Ottawa.
- **Bytown**: Ottawa's original name, established in 1826.
- **Gréber Plan**: A 1950 urban plan that reshaped the National Capital Region.
- **National Capital Region (NCR)**: The federally defined area encompassing Ottawa, Ontario, and Gatineau, Quebec.
- **Rideau Canal**: A 202 km waterway opened in 1832, connecting the Ottawa River to Lake Ontario; a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
- **Silicon Valley North**: A nickname for Ottawa due to its technology sector.
- **Single-tier municipality**: A municipal structure with no upper level, established for Ottawa in 2001.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The selection of Ottawa as capital was historically contentious, with debate over other cities like Montreal and Toronto.
- The 2022 Freedom Convoy occupation raised questions about public order, protest rights, and the Emergencies Act.
- Negotiations regarding an Algonquin land claim for the unceded territory have been ongoing since 1991, with an agreement in principle in 2016 but no final treaty.

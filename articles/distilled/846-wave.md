# Wave

## Overview
A wave is a propagating disturbance in a physical field that transfers energy through space and time. Waves are fundamental to physics, underlying phenomena from sound and light to quantum mechanics and spacetime ripples. They are classified broadly into mechanical waves (requiring a medium, e.g., sound, seismic) and electromagnetic waves (propagating via coupled electric and magnetic fields, e.g., light, radio). Understanding wave behavior—propagation, interference, polarization—is essential across engineering, astronomy, and modern physics.

## Timeline
- **19th century** — James Clerk Maxwell showed light is an electromagnetic wave
- **1880s** — Heinrich Hertz experimentally confirmed electromagnetic waves
- **1842** — Christian Doppler described the Doppler effect
- **1900–1925** — Quantum mechanics developed; Schrödinger and Dirac equations formulated
- **1915** — Einstein's general relativity predicted gravitational waves
- **2016** — First direct observation of gravitational waves announced

## Body

### Definition and Nature
Waves are dynamic disturbances in a field—a physical quantity defined at every point in space—arising from delayed neighbor-to-neighbor interactions. No single definition suffices; they are described by common characteristics. Microscopically, a wave is a change in a property (pressure, temperature, displacement) at a point caused by changes in adjacent regions. Macroscopically, waves result from effects propagating at finite speed, such as rotating electric dipoles producing electromagnetic waves or binary stars emitting gravitational waves.

### Mathematical Description
A wave is described by a function F(x, t) mapping space and time to a field value (scalar, vector, or tensor). The domain D is the spatial region where the wave is defined. Waves obey superposition: simultaneous waves at a point sum their individual contributions. A wave spectrum statistically describes energy distribution across frequencies and directions, useful for complex fields like ocean surfaces.

### Wave Families and Equations
Wave families depend on parameters (e.g., amplitude, length, mode number). The sound pressure inside a recorder playing a "pure" note is typically a standing wave, modeled by F(A, L, n, c; x, t), where λ = 4L/(2n−1) and f = c/λ. Differential equations constrain wave evolution: the heat equation ∂F/∂t = α∇²F + βQ governs diffusion, while the wave equation ∂²F/∂t² = α∇²F + βP governs mechanical vibrations and electromagnetic fields. Solutions to these equations define the possible wave behaviors.

### Physical Properties
**Propagation**: Waves travel rectilinearly through media. Longitudinal waves oscillate parallel to propagation (sound); transverse waves oscillate perpendicularly (light, waves on strings). Media are classified as bounded/unbounded, linear/nonlinear, homogeneous/inhomogeneous, isotropic/anisotropic.

**Velocities**: Phase velocity v_p = ω/k describes crest motion; group velocity v_g = ∂ω/∂k describes envelope motion. In non-dispersive media (e.g., EM waves in vacuum), v_p = v_g = c. Dispersion occurs when velocity depends on frequency.

**Phenomena**: Reflection (angle of incidence equals reflection), refraction (speed change at media boundaries, governed by Snell's law), diffraction (bending around obstacles), interference (superposition creating patterns), polarization (orientation of transverse oscillations), absorption (energy loss in lossy media), and the Doppler effect (frequency shift due to relative motion).

### Special Waves
**Plane waves** have constant values on planes perpendicular to propagation direction. **Standing waves** arise from interference of counter-propagating waves, featuring nodes (zero amplitude) and antinodes (maximum amplitude), fundamental to musical instruments. **Solitons** are self-reinforcing wave packets maintaining shape via balance of nonlinearity and dispersion. **Sine waves** are pure periodic oscillations.

### Mechanical Waves
Require elasticity and inertia in a medium. Transverse waves (strings, v = √(T/μ)) and longitudinal waves (sound, v = √(B/ρ₀)) are primary types. Seismic waves include body waves (P-waves, S-waves) and surface waves (Rayleigh, Love). Shock waves form when waves exceed local sound speed, causing abrupt pressure/density changes.

### Electromagnetic Waves
Consist of perpendicular electric and magnetic field oscillations propagating at light speed in vacuum. Maxwell's equations unify them; Hertz confirmed experimentally. Classified by frequency: radio, microwave, infrared, visible, ultraviolet, X-ray, gamma ray.

### Quantum Mechanical Waves
The Schrödinger equation governs probability amplitude wavefunctions. De Broglie hypothesized λ = h/p for particles. Wave packets localize particles; Gaussian packets illustrate the uncertainty principle. The Dirac equation describes relativistic spin-½ particles, predicting antimatter.

## Terms
- **Field**: A physical quantity with values at every point in space.
- **Superposition**: The principle that wave amplitudes add at a point.
- **Dispersion**: Frequency-dependent wave speed causing waveform spreading.
- **Polarization**: Orientation of oscillation in transverse waves.
- **Node/Antinode**: Points of zero/maximum amplitude in standing waves.
- **Phase velocity**: Speed of constant phase propagation (v_p = ω/k).
- **Group velocity**: Speed of wave envelope/energy propagation (v_g = ∂ω/∂k).
- **Soliton**: Self-reinforcing wave maintaining shape via nonlinearity-dispersion balance.
- **De Broglie wavelength**: λ = h/p, linking particle momentum to wave behavior.

## Debates and Open Questions
The definition of a wave remains debated—no single definition captures all cases. Whether macroscopic phenomena like crowd waves or traffic patterns qualify as true physical waves is contested. The reconciliation of wave-particle duality in quantum mechanics continues to generate interpretive debates. The detection of gravitational waves opened new observational windows, but their interaction with quantum fields and role in early universe cosmology remain active research areas.

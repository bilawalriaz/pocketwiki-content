# Ulugh Beg

## Overview
Ulugh Beg (1394–1449) was a Timurid sultan and a pioneering astronomer-mathematician who transformed Samarkand into a major center of learning during the Timurid Renaissance. His scientific achievements, particularly in observational astronomy, were among the most significant of the 15th century, but his political failures led to his overthrow and assassination.

## Body

### Early Life and Rise to Power
Ulugh Beg was the grandson of Timur (Tamerlane) and the oldest son of Shah Rukh. Born in 1394, he traveled widely during his grandfather's conquests. After Timur's death, the capital moved to Herat. In 1409, the 16-year-old Ulugh Beg was made governor of Samarkand, the former capital. By 1411, he was the sovereign ruler of Mavarannahr. His moniker "Ulugh Beg" means "Great Ruler."

### Patronage of Science and the Observatory
Ulugh Beg's primary legacy is his scientific patronage. He built a madrasah in Samarkand (1417–1420) that attracted scholars like Jamshid al-Kashi and Qadi Zada al-Rumi. His greatest achievement was the Samarkand Observatory (built 1424–1429), one of the finest in the Islamic world. Its key instrument was the Fakhri sextant, with a 36-meter radius, used to measure stellar altitudes. With it, he compiled the *Zij-i-Sultani* (1437), a star catalogue of 994 stars, considered the most important between Ptolemy and Tycho Brahe. He also made highly accurate measurements of the sidereal year (error of +58 seconds) and the tropical year (error of +25 seconds), and determined Earth's axial tilt as 23°30'17". In mathematics, he produced trigonometric tables accurate to eight decimal places.

### Foreign Relations and Military Campaigns
Ulugh Beg maintained diplomatic ties with Ming China, exchanging envoys and gifts with the Yongle and Zhengtong emperors. Militarily, he was less successful. After a limited victory against Moghulistan in 1425, he was decisively defeated by Baraq of the Golden Horde in 1427. This humiliation ended his major military campaigns, and his territories were later raided by neighbors.

### Downfall and Death
Following his father Shah Rukh's death in 1447, Ulugh Beg entered a war of succession. He defeated his nephew Ala al-Dawla in 1448 but was then defeated by Ala al-Dawla's brother and forced to retreat. His own son, Abd al-Latif, rebelled, captured Samarkand, and forced Ulugh Beg to surrender. In 1449, while ostensibly traveling to Mecca, Ulugh Beg was assassinated on Abd al-Latif's orders. His remains were later placed in Timur's mausoleum.

## Terms
- **Timurid Renaissance**: A period of cultural and intellectual flourishing in the Timurid Empire, particularly in arts and sciences.
- **Mavarannahr**: The region of Transoxiana (modern Uzbekistan), the heartland of the Timurid Empire.
- **Madrasah**: An Islamic educational institution, often including a mosque.
- **Zij-i-Sultani**: A comprehensive astronomical star catalogue and set of tables compiled by Ulugh Beg.
- **Fakhri sextant**: A large, fixed astronomical instrument used to measure the altitudes of celestial objects.
- **Sidereal year**: The time taken for the Sun to return to the same position relative to the fixed stars.
- **Tropical year**: The time taken for the Sun to return to the same position in the cycle of seasons.
- **Sexagesimal system**: A numeral system with base 60, used for measuring angles (degrees, minutes, seconds).

## Debates and Open Questions
The source notes that Ulugh Beg's scientific expertise was not matched by his political skill, leading to his downfall. It also highlights his discovery of "multiple mistakes" in the long-accepted astronomical data of Ptolemy, which motivated his independent work. The relative accuracy of his tropical year measurement compared to later estimates by Copernicus is a point of scholarly interest.

# Prithviraj Chauhan

## Overview
Prithviraj III (1166–1192), also known as Prithviraj Chauhan, was a king of the Chauhan (Chahamana) dynasty who ruled from Ajmer in northwestern India. He is a pivotal figure in Indian history, remembered for his military campaigns against neighboring kingdoms and, most significantly, his defeat by the Ghurid ruler Muhammad of Ghor at the Second Battle of Tarain (1192). This defeat is traditionally seen as a landmark event facilitating the Islamic conquest of northern India and the establishment of the Delhi Sultanate.

## Timeline
- **1166 CE** — Prithviraj is born to King Someshvara and Queen Karpuradevi.
- **1177 CE** — Ascends the Chauhan throne as a minor; his mother acts as regent.
- **c. 1180 CE** — Prithviraj assumes direct control of the administration.
- **Before 1182 CE** — Defeats his cousin Nagarjuna and the Bhadanaka dynasty.
- **1182–83 CE** — Invades and sacks the Chandela territory of Jejakabhukti (Bundelkhand).
- **Before 1187 CE** — Fights a war with the Chaulukya (Solanki) king of Gujarat, Bhima II, leading to a peace treaty.
- **Before 1192 CE** — Elopement with Jayachandra's daughter Samyogita creates a rivalry with the Gahadavala king.
- **1190–91 CE** — **First Battle of Tarain:** Defeats Muhammad of Ghor near Taraori.
- **1192 CE** — **Second Battle of Tarain:** Is decisively defeated and captured by Muhammad of Ghor.
- **1192 CE** — Executed by the Ghurids; his son Govindaraja is installed as a vassal ruler in Ajmer.

## Body

### Sources and Historicity
Knowledge of Prithviraj comes from a mix of sources. His own inscriptions are few and not issued by him. Much information derives from later medieval literary texts like the *Prithviraja Vijaya* (a eulogy from his reign), the *Prithviraj Raso* (a legendary epic), and Jain chronicles such as the *Hammira Mahakavya*. These works contain exaggerations and anachronisms. Muslim accounts, like the *Taj-ul-Maasir*, provide details on the battles with the Ghurids. Historians must carefully evaluate these often-conflicting sources.

### Early Reign and Expansion
Prithviraj inherited a kingdom centered at Ajmer. After a regency period, he began military campaigns to expand his territory. He suppressed a revolt by his cousin Nagarjuna and defeated the Bhadanaka dynasty. His most significant early campaign was against the Chandela king Paramardi, sacking their capital Mahoba. While later legends describe a prolonged war, historical evidence suggests this was likely a raid rather than a permanent annexation.

### Regional Conflicts
Prithviraj engaged in conflicts with several neighboring powers. He fought a war with the Chaulukya (Solanki) king of Gujarat, Bhima II, which ended in a treaty around 1187 CE. He also had a rivalry with Jayachandra, the Gahadavala king of Kannauj, allegedly sparked by Prithviraj's elopement with Jayachandra's daughter, Samyogita. This conflict may have left Prithviraj without crucial allies during the later Ghurid invasions.

### The Ghurid Wars and the Battles of Tarain
The Ghurid dynasty, under Muhammad of Ghor, was expanding eastward into India. After initial raids, Muhammad invaded and captured the fortress of Tabarhindah (Bathinda) in 1190–91. Prithviraj led a coalition of Rajput rulers and decisively defeated the Ghurids at the **First Battle of Tarain** (1191), forcing Muhammad to retreat.

Muhammad returned in 1192 with a large army of mounted archers. Through a deceptive strategy of feigned retreats, he lured the Rajput army into a prolonged chase, exhausting them. Muhammad's reserve force then attacked, leading to a catastrophic defeat for Prithviraj at the **Second Battle of Tarain**. Prithviraj was captured and later executed, likely for rebelling against Ghurid suzerainty.

### Death and Succession
After his capture, Prithviraj was taken to Ajmer. Most sources agree he was executed sometime after the battle, possibly for plotting against Muhammad of Ghor. The Ghurids then installed his son, Govindaraja, as a vassal ruler. Prithviraj's brother, Hariraja, later briefly recaptured part of the kingdom before being defeated by the Ghurid general Qutb al-Din Aibak.

## Terms
- **Chauhan (Chahamana):** A Rajput dynasty that ruled parts of present-day Rajasthan and surrounding regions.
- **Sapadalaksha:** The core territory of the Chauhan dynasty, with its capital at Ajmer.
- **Ghurids:** A Muslim dynasty from present-day Afghanistan that expanded into northern India in the late 12th century.
- **Rajput:** A member of the traditional warrior ruling classes of northern and central India.
- **Vassal:** A ruler who holds land or a title in exchange for allegiance and service to a superior lord.
- **Kavya:** A genre of classical Sanskrit epic poetry.
- **Digvijaya:** A "conquest of all regions," a term for a king's campaign of universal conquest.
- **Swayamvara:** A ceremony where a princess chooses her husband from among assembled suitors.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Extent of Conquests:** Historians debate whether Prithviraj's campaigns, especially against the Chandelas, resulted in lasting territorial annexation or were primarily raids.
- **Number of Battles with Ghurids:** While Muslim sources mention two major battles, Hindu and Jain texts claim numerous engagements (up to 21 or 22). Scholars suggest these may refer to multiple raids and skirmishes before the final confrontation.
- **Cause of Defeat:** The traditional narrative emphasizes Prithviraj's neglect of state affairs and lack of allies. Modern analysis also highlights the tactical superiority of the Ghurid mounted archers and Muhammad's use of deception.
- **Circumstances of Death:** Accounts vary widely, from immediate execution on the battlefield to elaborate legends of blinding and revenge. The most historically plausible scenario is execution after a failed rebellion while held as a prisoner.

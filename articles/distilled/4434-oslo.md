# Oslo

## Overview
Oslo is the capital and most populous city of Norway, serving as the nation's economic, governmental, and cultural hub. Its history spans from a Viking Age trading post to a modern, rapidly growing metropolis known for its high quality of life, maritime industries, and ambitious environmental policies.

## Timeline
- **c. 1040** — Founded as a city named Ánslo.
- **1048** — Established as a *kaupstad* (trading place) by Harald Hardrada.
- **c. 1300** — Became the capital under Haakon V.
- **1624** — Destroyed by fire; rebuilt as Christiania by King Christian IV.
- **1814** — Became the capital of the newly independent Kingdom of Norway.
- **1877** — City name officially changed to Kristiania.
- **1925** — Name restored to Oslo.
- **1948** — Merged with the surrounding municipality of Aker.
- **2011** — Government Quarter bombed in a terrorist attack.
- **2019** — Selected as the European Green Capital.

## Body

### History and Name
The area was part of the Viking region of Viken. Archaeological evidence of Christian burials predates AD 1000, suggesting an earlier settlement. The city was elevated to a bishopric in 1070 and became the permanent royal residence under Haakon V (1299–1319). A devastating fire in 1624 led King Christian IV to rebuild the city closer to Akershus Fortress, naming it Christiania. The original site remained as a suburb called Oslo. After Norway's full independence in 1905, the name Christiania (memorializing a Danish king) was seen as inappropriate. In 1925, the name Oslo was transferred to the entire city, and the old suburb was renamed Gamlebyen ("Old Town"). The origin of the name "Oslo" is debated; it likely derives from Old Norse, meaning either "meadow at the foot of a hill" or "meadow consecrated to the Gods."

### Geography and Climate
Oslo occupies an arc at the northern end of the Oslofjord, surrounded by green hills and forests. The city's urban area sprawls in three corridors, giving it an upside-down "Y" shape. Two-thirds of its large land area are protected forests, hills, and lakes, including Maridalsvannet, a major drinking water source. The city has a humid continental climate with warm summers and fairly cold winters. Daylight varies dramatically, from over 18 hours in summer to about 6 in winter.

### Politics and Government
Oslo is unique in Norway as a combined municipality and county. It is the seat of the national government, with most offices in the Regjeringskvartalet. The city has a parliamentary system with a 59-seat City Council. The Mayor serves a ceremonial role, while the Governing Mayor heads the city government. The Conservative Party is the most represented party in the national parliament from Oslo.

### Economy
Oslo is a global city and a major European maritime hub, home to world-leading shipping, shipbroking, and insurance companies. Its economy is strong and varied, contributing about 20% of Norway's national GDP. The city is consistently ranked among the world's most expensive for living costs, particularly for goods and services. It hosts a high concentration of Norway's largest companies, including many in the oil and gas sector.

### Demographics and Society
Oslo is Norway's fastest-growing major city, with growth driven by international immigration and high birth rates. As of 2022, immigrants and their children made up about 35% of the population. The largest ethnic minority groups are Pakistanis, Poles, Somalis, and Swedes. The city is religiously diverse, with 48.7% affiliated with the Church of Norway and significant Muslim (9.5%) and unaffiliated (28.9%) populations.

### Culture and Education
Oslo is a cultural center with numerous museums, including the Munch Museum (housing *The Scream*) and the Viking Ship Museum. It hosts the annual Nobel Peace Prize ceremony. The city has a vibrant arts scene, from the Oslo Opera House to major music festivals. It is home to the University of Oslo and several other higher education institutions, with a highly educated workforce.

### Environment and Transport
Oslo aims to become a low-carbon city, targeting a 95% reduction in greenhouse gas emissions by 2030. It was the first city to implement a climate budget in 2017 and is transitioning to an all-electric public transport system. The city has an extensive public transport network, including the world's most extensive metro per resident. It actively promotes electric vehicles, which made up 41% of registered cars in the municipality as of the source date.

## Terms
- **Kaupstad**: A Norse term for a trading place or market town.
- **Formannskapsdistrikt**: The system of local government municipalities established in Norway in 1838.
- **Marka**: The forested hills surrounding Oslo, a popular recreational area.
- **Regjeringskvartalet**: The government quarter in Oslo, housing most national government offices.
- **Storting**: The Norwegian parliament.
- **Ruter**: The public transport authority for Oslo and Akershus.
- **Climate budget**: A tool making government departments responsible for tracking and reducing their greenhouse gas emissions.
- **Gamlebyen**: Literally "the Old Town," the area of the original medieval Oslo settlement.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The etymology of the name "Oslo" is disputed, with linguists considering "meadow at the foot of a hill" and "meadow consecrated to the Gods" as equally likely interpretations.
- The exact founding date of Oslo is debated; while Norse sagas cite c. 1049, archaeological evidence suggests an earlier urban settlement, leading to the celebration of Oslo's millennium in 2000 rather than 2049.

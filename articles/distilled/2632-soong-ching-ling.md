# Soong Ching-ling

## Overview
Soong Ching-ling (1893–1981) was a pivotal Chinese political figure, known as Madame Sun Yat-sen and the "Mother of Modern China." As the wife of the Republic's founder and a lifelong leftist, she bridged the Kuomintang (KMT) and Chinese Communist Party (CCP), ultimately becoming a symbolic head of state in the People's Republic of China (PRC). Her life reflects the turbulent political shifts of 20th-century China, from the fall of the Qing dynasty to the Communist revolution.

## Timeline
- **27 Jan 1893** — Born in Shanghai to Charlie Soong and Ni Kwei-tseng.
- **1907–1913** — Educated in the United States at Wesleyan College.
- **25 Oct 1915** — Marries Sun Yat-sen in Tokyo, Japan.
- **16 Jun 1922** — Suffers a miscarriage during the Chen Jiongming uprising in Guangzhou.
- **12 Mar 1925** — Sun Yat-sen dies in Beijing; she becomes a key KMT leftist leader.
- **14 Jul 1927** — Condemns the KMT-CCP split, leading to her exile.
- **1928–1931** — Lives in Berlin, Germany, engaging with European leftist movements.
- **1931–1937** — Returns to Shanghai; secretly works as a Comintern agent aiding the CCP.
- **1938** — Founds the China Defence League in Hong Kong to support the war effort.
- **1 Oct 1949** — Attends the proclamation of the People's Republic of China in Beijing.
- **1949–1954** — Serves as one of six Vice Chairmen of the Central People's Government.
- **1959–1975** — Elected Vice President of the PRC alongside Dong Biwu.
- **1966–1976** — Survives the Cultural Revolution, protected by Zhou Enlai but harassed by Red Guards.
- **16 May 1981** — Admitted to the CCP and named Honorary Chairman of the PRC.
- **29 May 1981** — Dies in Beijing from chronic lymphocytic leukaemia.

## Body

### Early Life and Marriage to Sun Yat-sen
Born into a prominent Christian family, Soong Ching-ling was educated in the U.S., where she absorbed Sun Yat-sen's revolutionary ideals. She married Sun, the founder of the Republic of China and the KMT, in 1915, becoming his political partner. During the First United Front (1923–1927), she supported Sun's alliance with the Soviet Union and the CCP. After Sun's death in 1925, she emerged as a leading leftist within the KMT, advocating for the continuation of his policies.

### Exile and Secret CCP Work
Following the violent KMT-CCP split in 1927, Soong condemned the purge and went into exile in the Soviet Union and Germany. Though she never publicly embraced communism, she maintained ties with leftist movements. Upon returning to Shanghai in 1931, she secretly worked for the Communist International (Comintern), using her status to rescue CCP members and funnel aid to the Communist base in Yan'an during the Second Sino-Japanese War (1937–1945). Her public work included founding the China Defence League to rally international support.

### Role in the People's Republic of China
After the Communist victory in 1949, Soong joined the new government in Beijing, holding high-ranking ceremonial positions, including Vice President (1959–1975). She represented China abroad and championed social welfare through the China Welfare Institute. During the Cultural Revolution (1966–1976), she was officially protected by Premier Zhou Enlai but still suffered personal losses, including the desecration of her parents' graves. In her final years, she criticized the excesses of past political movements. Shortly before her death, she was admitted to the CCP and given the unique title of Honorary Chairman.

## Terms
- **Kuomintang (KMT):** The Nationalist Party of China, founded by Sun Yat-sen, which governed the Republic of China until 1949.
- **Chinese Communist Party (CCP):** The ruling party of the People's Republic of China, which came to power in 1949.
- **First United Front (1923–1927):** An alliance between the KMT and CCP, supported by the Soviet Union, to unify China.
- **Second United Front (1937–1945):** A fragile alliance between the KMT and CCP to resist the Japanese invasion.
- **Comintern (Communist International):** An international organization founded in 1919 to advance world communism.
- **Cultural Revolution (1966–1976):** A sociopolitical upheaval launched by Mao Zedong to purge capitalist and traditional elements from Chinese society.
- **Honorary Chairman:** A ceremonial, non-executive title bestowed upon Soong Ching-ling in 1981, unique in PRC history.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Degree of Communist Sympathy:** Scholars debate whether Soong was a committed communist ideologue or a pragmatic nationalist who aligned with the CCP for Sun Yat-sen's legacy. Her refusal to formally join the party until 1981 suggests complexity.
- **Role in the Comintern:** The extent of her secret work for the Comintern and the CCP during the 1930s remains a subject of historical research, with some details still emerging from archives.
- **Family Relations:** Her political choices caused a permanent rift with her sisters, especially Soong Mei-ling (wife of Chiang Kai-shek), highlighting the personal cost of China's political divisions.

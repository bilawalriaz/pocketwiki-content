# Hayreddin Barbarossa

## Overview
Hayreddin Barbarossa (c. 1466/1483–1546) was an Ottoman corsair and admiral whose naval victories secured Ottoman dominance over the Mediterranean during the mid-16th century. Rising from a privateer to the Grand Admiral (Kapudan Pasha) of the Ottoman Navy, he was instrumental in establishing Ottoman supremacy in the region until the Battle of Lepanto in 1571.

## Timeline
- **c. 1466/1483** — Born on Lesbos as Khizr, son of an Ottoman sipahi.
- **1503** — Joins his brother Oruç Reis as a corsair at Djerba.
- **1516** — Captures Algiers from Spain with Oruç; Oruç declares himself Sultan.
- **1518** — Oruç is killed; Khizr inherits his nickname "Barbarossa" and Ottoman allegiance.
- **1533** — Appointed Kapudan Pasha (Grand Admiral) by Sultan Suleiman the Magnificent.
- **1534** — Captures Tunis from the Hafsid Sultan.
- **1538** — Decisive victory over the Holy League at the Battle of Preveza.
- **1543** — Leads a Franco-Ottoman alliance, capturing Nice for France.
- **1545** — Retires to Constantinople.
- **1546** — Dies in his palace on the Bosphorus.

## Body

### Early Life and Corsair Career
Born on Lesbos to an Ottoman father and Greek mother, Khizr and his three brothers all became seamen. Initially engaged in trade, they turned to privateering to counter the Knights Hospitaller. After his brother Ilyas was killed and Oruç was captured, Oruç later became a successful corsair with Ottoman backing. Khizr joined Oruç in the Western Mediterranean around 1503, operating from bases like Djerba and La Goulette (Tunis). The brothers gained fame raiding Christian shipping and coasts, and for transporting Muslims (Mudéjars) from Spain to North Africa, which earned Oruç the nickname "Barbarossa" (Redbeard).

### Rulers of Algiers and Ottoman Service
In 1516, the brothers captured Algiers from Spain. To secure protection against Spanish retaliation, Oruç offered Algiers to the Ottoman Sultan Selim I in 1517, becoming its governor. After Oruç and their brother Ishak were killed by Spanish forces in 1518, Khizr contacted Selim I, received Ottoman support, and inherited his brother's position, name, and mission. He recaptured Tlemcen in 1518 and defeated a Spanish-Italian army in 1519, consolidating Ottoman control in the region.

### Grand Admiral of the Ottoman Navy
Summoned to Constantinople in 1532, Khizr was appointed Kapudan Pasha by Suleiman the Magnificent in 1533. He led major campaigns, including the capture of Tunis in 1534 and a devastating raid on Italian coasts in 1537. His victory over the Holy League fleet at Preveza in 1538 secured Ottoman naval dominance for decades. He later formed a strategic alliance with France against the Habsburgs, assisting King Francis I in capturing Nice in 1543 and using Toulon as a base. He repulsed a major Spanish siege of Algiers in 1541 led by Emperor Charles V.

### Retirement and Legacy
Barbarossa retired to Constantinople in 1545, where he dictated his memoirs. He died in 1546 and was buried in a mausoleum designed by the architect Mimar Sinan. His legacy is that of the architect of Ottoman naval supremacy in the Mediterranean. His tomb in Beşiktaş, Istanbul, became a site of naval tradition, with ships saluting it as they passed.

## Terms
- **Kapudan Pasha**: Grand Admiral of the Ottoman Navy.
- **Beylerbey**: A high-ranking governor of a province (sanjak) in the Ottoman Empire.
- **Sipahi**: A cavalryman in the Ottoman army.
- **Mudéjars**: Muslims living under Christian rule in medieval Spain and Portugal.
- **Holy League**: A coalition of Christian states (including the Papacy, Spain, and Venice) formed against the Ottoman Empire.
- **Galiot**: A small, fast galley used for raiding and coastal warfare.
- **Sanjak**: A province or district in the Ottoman Empire.
- **Janissaries**: Elite infantry units of the Ottoman army, often of Christian origin.

## Debates and Open Questions
- His exact birth year is uncertain, with sources giving either 1466 or 1483.
- The origin of the name "Barbarossa" is debated; it evolved from "Baba Oruç" (Father Oruç) due to a similarity in sound in European languages.
- The precise number of captives and slaves taken during his various raids is often estimated and varies by source.

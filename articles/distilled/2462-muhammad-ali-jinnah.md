# Muhammad Ali Jinnah

## Overview
Muhammad Ali Jinnah was a barrister and statesman who became the founder of Pakistan. As leader of the All-India Muslim League, he championed the creation of a separate Muslim state, leading to the partition of British India in 1947. He served as Pakistan's first governor-general until his death in 1948, shaping the new nation's early institutions amid immense challenges.

## Timeline
- **25 Dec 1876** — Born in Karachi, Bombay Presidency, British India.
- **1892–1896** — Studies law at Lincoln's Inn, London; called to the bar.
- **1906** — Opposes the formation of the All-India Muslim League.
- **1913** — Joins the All-India Muslim League.
- **1916** — Helps broker the Lucknow Pact between Congress and the Muslim League.
- **1920** — Resigns from the Indian National Congress over Gandhi's satyagraha campaign.
- **1930–1935** — Practices law in London; largely absent from Indian politics.
- **1937** — After provincial elections, begins campaign for Muslim political rights.
- **23 Mar 1940** — Muslim League passes the Lahore Resolution demanding separate Muslim states.
- **14 Aug 1947** — Pakistan becomes independent; Jinnah becomes its first governor-general.
- **11 Sep 1948** — Dies in Karachi at age 71.

## Body

### Early Life and Legal Career
Born to a wealthy merchant family, Jinnah trained as a barrister in London, becoming the youngest British Indian called to the bar in 1895. He established a successful legal practice in Bombay, gaining fame for his skill in cases like the 1908 "Caucus Case." His early political life was marked by advocacy for Hindu-Muslim unity and constitutional methods within the Indian National Congress.

### Shift from Congress to Muslim League
Jinnah initially opposed the All-India Muslim League and separate electorates for Muslims. However, after the 1920 Congress endorsed Gandhi's satyagraha—which Jinnah saw as political anarchy—he resigned. He gradually became the League's leader, working to protect Muslim political rights. A key turning point was the 1937 provincial elections, where the Congress formed governments without the League, convincing Jinnah that Muslims needed a separate state to avoid marginalization.

### The Pakistan Movement
Influenced by poet-philosopher Muhammad Iqbal, Jinnah championed the idea of a Muslim homeland. The Muslim League's 1940 Lahore Resolution formally demanded independent Muslim-majority states. During World War II, the League gained strength while Congress leaders were imprisoned. Post-war elections in 1945–46 gave the League a mandate, winning all Muslim-reserved seats. Negotiations with the British and Congress failed, leading to the 1947 partition plan and the creation of Pakistan.

### Governor-General and Final Years
As Pakistan's first governor-general, Jinnah oversaw the massive migration of millions and the violent aftermath of partition. He worked to establish state institutions and personally supervised refugee aid. His health, long affected by tuberculosis, declined rapidly. He died on 11 September 1948, just over a year after independence.

## Terms
- **Satyagraha**: A form of non-violent civil resistance advocated by Mahatma Gandhi.
- **Two-Nation Theory**: The belief that Hindus and Muslims are distinct nations, which cannot coexist in a single state.
- **Lahure Resolution**: The 1940 Muslim League resolution calling for independent Muslim-majority states in northwestern and eastern India.
- **Radcliffe Line**: The boundary demarcation line drawn in 1947 to partition British India into India and Pakistan.
- **Quaid-e-Azam**: Urdu for "Great Leader," a title bestowed on Jinnah.
- **Governor-General**: The representative of the British monarch and head of state in a Dominion like Pakistan.
- **Constituent Assembly**: The body tasked with drafting a constitution for the new nation.
- **Direct Action Day**: A 1946 day of protest called by the Muslim League, which led to major communal riots in Calcutta.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Secular vs. Islamic State**: Scholars debate whether Jinnah envisioned a secular state (citing his 1947 speech on religious freedom) or an Islamic state (citing his later references to Islamic principles in governance).
- **Religious Affiliation**: Controversy persists over whether Jinnah was a Shia Muslim, a Sunni Muslim, or simply a non-sectarian Muslim, with legal cases and conflicting testimonies.
- **Intent for Partition**: Some historians argue Jinnah used the Pakistan demand as a bargaining chip for Muslim rights within a united India, while others see it as a genuine, non-negotiable goal.
- **Legacy in India**: Jinnah is viewed negatively by many in India as the architect of partition, though some Indian politicians have praised his statesmanship.

# Hannibal

## Overview
Hannibal Barca (247–c. 181 BC) was a Carthaginian general and statesman who commanded Carthage against the Roman Republic in the Second Punic War. He is renowned for his tactical genius, particularly his invasion of Italy by crossing the Alps and his decisive victories at battles like Cannae. His campaign nearly brought Rome to its knees but ultimately ended in defeat, cementing his legacy as one of antiquity's greatest military minds.

## Timeline
- **247 BC** — Hannibal is born in Carthage.
- **218 BC** — Hannibal attacks Saguntum, a Roman ally, sparking the Second Punic War.
- **218 BC** — Hannibal crosses the Alps with his army and war elephants to invade Italy.
- **218 BC** — Hannibal wins the Battle of Ticinus and the Battle of Trebia.
- **217 BC** — Hannibal destroys a Roman army at the Battle of Lake Trasimene.
- **216 BC** — Hannibal inflicts a catastrophic defeat on Rome at the Battle of Cannae.
- **202 BC** — Hannibal is defeated by Scipio Africanus at the Battle of Zama in North Africa.
- **201 BC** — The Second Punic War ends with a Roman victory.
- **c. 195 BC** — Hannibal flees Carthage into voluntary exile.
- **183–181 BC** — Hannibal dies by suicide in Bithynia to avoid capture by Rome.

## Body

### Early Life and the Path to War
Hannibal was the son of Hamilcar Barca, a Carthaginian general. After Carthage's defeat in the First Punic War, Hamilcar led the conquest of the Iberian Peninsula. According to tradition, a young Hannibal swore an oath to his father to "never be a friend of Rome." Upon Hamilcar's death and the subsequent assassination of his brother-in-law Hasdrubal, Hannibal was proclaimed commander-in-chief of the Carthaginian army in Iberia at age 26. He completed the conquest of Iberia south of the Ebro River. Rome then allied with the city of Saguntum, which Hannibal saw as a breach of a prior treaty. He besieged and captured Saguntum, an act that led the Roman Senate to declare war.

### The Invasion of Italy and Early Victories
In 218 BC, Hannibal launched his famous overland invasion of Italy. He marched his army from Hispania, crossed the Pyrenees and the Alps, and arrived in the Po Valley with a reduced but still formidable force. His arrival encouraged Gallic tribes to join his cause. He then won a series of tactical victories against Roman armies. At the Trebia and Lake Trasimene, he used ambushes and terrain to devastating effect. His most famous victory came at Cannae in 216 BC, where he executed a double envelopment, surrounding and destroying a much larger Roman army. This battle is considered a masterpiece of tactics.

### Stalemate and Retreat from Italy
Despite his victories, Hannibal could not force a final surrender from Rome. The Romans, guided by the Fabian strategy of attrition, avoided pitched battles and wore down his forces. Hannibal's position in Italy grew difficult due to a lack of reinforcements from Carthage, which was politically divided. He held southern Italy for 15 years but was gradually pushed back. The turning point came when his brother Hasdrubal was defeated and killed at the Metaurus River in 207 BC. Eventually, Hannibal was recalled to Carthage to defend against a Roman invasion led by Scipio Africanus.

### Final Battle and Later Life
Hannibal met Scipio at the Battle of Zama in 202 BC. Unlike his earlier battles, Hannibal was defeated, ending the war. After the war, Hannibal was elected *sufet* (chief magistrate) in Carthage and enacted financial reforms to pay the war indemnity to Rome. These reforms angered the Carthaginian aristocracy, and facing Roman pressure, he went into exile. He served as a military advisor to Antiochus III of the Seleucid Empire against Rome. After Antiochus's defeat, Hannibal fled again, eventually taking refuge with Prusias I of Bithynia. Cornered by Roman agents, he committed suicide between 183 and 181 BC.

## Terms
*   **Sufet:** The chief magistrate of the Carthaginian state, a position Hannibal held after the Second Punic War.
*   **Barcid:** A term for the family of Hannibal, derived from the cognomen "Barca" (meaning "lightning") of his father, Hamilcar.
*   **Fabian strategy:** A Roman military strategy of avoiding decisive battles and using attrition to wear down an enemy, named after the dictator Fabius Maximus.
*   **Double envelopment:** A military tactic where both flanks of an enemy's formation are attacked simultaneously, as Hannibal executed at Cannae.
*   **War indemnity:** A sum of money Carthage was forced to pay Rome as compensation after losing the Second Punic War.
*   **Punic:** An adjective relating to ancient Carthage or its people, derived from the Latin word for Phoenician.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **The Alpine Route:** The exact pass Hannibal used to cross the Alps is heavily debated. Theories favor routes like the Col de Montgenèvre, Col de Mont Cenis, or the Col de Clapier. Recent archaeological evidence supports the Col de la Traversette.
*   **Troop Numbers:** The precise size of Hannibal's army at various stages, especially after crossing the Alps, is uncertain and disputed by historians.
*   **Hannibal's Son:** The existence of a son named Haspar or Aspar, mentioned by the poet Silius Italicus, is not confirmed by other major ancient sources like Livy or Polybius.
*   **Cause of Death:** Ancient sources give conflicting accounts of Hannibal's death, with stories ranging from suicide by poison to death from a fever after a sword wound.

# Shenzhen

## Overview
Shenzhen is a major prefecture-level city in Guangdong, China, and the country's first Special Economic Zone (SEZ). Its transformation from a rural village area in 1979 into a global technology, finance, and trade hub exemplifies China's rapid economic development under the "reform and opening up" policy. The city's proximity to Hong Kong was instrumental in its growth, attracting massive foreign investment and migration, and it now boasts one of the world's largest economies.

## Timeline
- **214 BC** — Area incorporated into Qin dynasty's Nanhai Commandery.
- **1911** — Kowloon–Canton Railway opens, with Shenzhen station as the last mainland stop.
- **1979** — Bao'an County renamed Shenzhen; Shekou Industrial Zone established as the first export processing zone.
- **1980** — Shenzhen designated as China's first Special Economic Zone (SEZ).
- **1990** — Shenzhen Stock Exchange established.
- **1996** — Shenzhen High-tech Industrial Development Zone approved by the State Council.
- **2010** — SEZ expanded to cover the entire city; "second line" border dissolved.
- **2011** — Hosts the 26th Summer Universiade.
- **2015** — Shekou and Qianhai zones integrated into the Guangdong Free-Trade Zone.
- **2019** — Central government unveils reform plans, labeling Shenzhen a "pilot demonstration zone."

## Body

### History and Development
The area has been inhabited for over 6,700 years and was historically part of Bao'an County. Its modern development began in 1979 when the county was renamed Shenzhen. In 1980, it became China's first SEZ under Deng Xiaoping's "reform and opening up" policy, designed as an experimental ground for market capitalism. The city's population and economy boomed, growing from 30,000 in 1980 to over 17.5 million by 2020. A key administrative feature was the "second line" border, a barbed-wire checkpoint system separating the SEZ from the rest of China, which was dismantled in 2010 when the SEZ expanded citywide.

### Economy and Industry
Shenzhen's economy is driven by four pillar industries: high-tech, finance, logistics, and culture. As of 2022, its nominal GDP surpassed that of Hong Kong and Guangzhou. The city is a global technology hub, home to corporations like Huawei, Tencent, DJI, and BYD, the latter making Shenzhen China's largest automobile manufacturing city. The Shenzhen Stock Exchange is one of the world's largest by market capitalization. The Port of Shenzhen is the world's fourth-busiest container port. The city is also a major financial center, ranked 8th globally in 2021.

### Demographics and Society
Shenzhen's population is overwhelmingly composed of migrants from across China, giving it a young demographic profile with only 5.36% of residents over 60. The city has a high male-to-female ratio (130:100) and a high birth rate. Linguistically, it has shifted from local Cantonese and Hakka dialects to Mandarin as the dominant language due to migration. The city has a notable Korean minority and is religiously diverse, with Buddhism and Taoism as the main faiths.

### Geography and Climate
Located in the Pearl River Delta, Shenzhen borders Hong Kong, Dongguan, and Huizhou. It has a humid subtropical climate with mild, dry winters and hot, humid summers. The city is prone to typhoons and torrential rain. Its landscape includes over 160 rivers and 24 reservoirs.

### Politics and Administration
Shenzhen has a parallel party-government system and, as a former SEZ, historically wielded significant legislative autonomy from the central government. It is divided into nine administrative districts. The city has faced notable corruption cases among high-ranking officials.

### Culture and Cityscape
Shenzhen promotes a competitive "Shenzhen speed" culture. It was named a "United Nations Design Capital" by UNESCO in 2008. The cityscape is defined by its numerous skyscrapers, including the 599-meter Ping An Finance Centre, and features historical Hakka walled villages and traditional architecture alongside modern development.

## Terms
*   **Special Economic Zone (SEZ):** A designated area in China where market-oriented economic policies and foreign investment are encouraged, established as an experimental ground for reform.
*   **Reform and Opening Up:** The program of economic reforms introduced in 1978 by Deng Xiaoping that led to the creation of SEZs and China's market-oriented economy.
*   **Hukou:** A system of household registration in China that determines access to social services and benefits in a particular locality.
*   **Second Line (二线关):** The barbed-wire border and checkpoint system that separated the Shenzhen SEZ from the rest of mainland China until its dissolution in 2010.
*   **Guannei/Guanwai:** Colloquial terms for areas "within the border" (the original SEZ) and "outside the border" (areas added later), reflecting historical differences in economic privileges.
*   **Pearl River Delta:** A major manufacturing and economic region in Guangdong province, including cities like Guangzhou, Shenzhen, and Hong Kong.
*   **Qianhai Shenzhen-Hong Kong Modern Service Industry Cooperation Zone:** A free-trade zone in Shenzhen designed to deepen economic cooperation between the city and Hong Kong.

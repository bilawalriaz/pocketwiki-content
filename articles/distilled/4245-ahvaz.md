# Ahvaz

## Overview
Ahvaz is the capital city of Khuzestan province in southwestern Iran, situated along the Karun River near the Iraqi border. It is a major industrial, transportation, and cultural hub with a long history dating back to the Achaemenid period, known today as the "Oil Capital of Iran" and the "City of Bridges."

## Timeline
- **c. 3000 BC** — Ancient settlement known as Taryana, located on the Royal Road.
- **3rd century CE** — Renamed "Hormazd-Ardeshir" by Sasanian king Ardashir I; becomes capital of Susiana.
- **7th century CE** — Arab conquest destroys part of the city; the remaining market area is named "Sūq al-Ahvāz."
- **19th century** — Small borough with 700–2000 inhabitants.
- **1880s** — Karun River dredged for commerce; railway built, reviving trade.
- **Early 20th century** — Oil discovered nearby, spurring growth.
- **1926** — Provincial capital transferred from Shushtar to Ahvaz.
- **1929** — Trans-Iranian Railway reaches Ahvaz.
- **1935** — City officially renamed from Nāseri back to Ahvaz.
- **1942** — Polish refugee camp established during World War II.
- **1980–1988** — Iran–Iraq War; Ahvaz is near front lines and suffers severe damage.
- **2005** — Series of bombings attributed to Arab separatist groups.
- **2018** — Military parade attack kills at least 29 people.
- **2025–2026** — City witnesses protests and clashes during nationwide unrest.

## Body

### Etymology and Ancient History
The city's earliest name was Taryana, reflecting a connection to the legendary Persian king Darius. Under the Sasanian Empire, King Ardashir I (r. 224–242 CE) expanded and renamed it Hormazd-Ardeshir, later abbreviated to Darawashir. The modern name "Ahvaz" is the Arabicized plural of "Huzi," derived from the ancient Uzi (or Huzi) people, likely descendants of the Elamites who inhabited the region. The province name Khuzestan shares this origin. The city was a key center of the Academy of Gondishapur, an ancient center of learning.

### 20th Century to Present
Ahvaz grew from a small 19th-century town into a major city after oil was discovered in the early 20th century. Its strategic location made it a commercial crossroads linking river and rail traffic. During World War II, it hosted a Polish refugee camp. The city was severely damaged during the Iran–Iraq War (1980–1988), as Iraq attempted to annex Khuzestan. Post-war, industrial development continued with projects like the Foolad steel facility (1989). In recent decades, Ahvaz has experienced ethnic tensions, bombings (2005), and a deadly attack on a military parade (2018). It was also a site of significant protests and clashes during the 2025–2026 Iranian unrest.

### Geography, Climate, and Infrastructure
Ahvaz is bisected by the Karun River, Iran's largest by volume and one of only two navigable rivers. The city is divided into an administrative/industrial right bank and a residential left bank. It has a subtropical hot desert climate with extremely hot summers (regularly over 45°C/113°F) and mild winters. The city is a major transportation hub with an international airport, railway station, and freeway connections. It is famously called the "City of Bridges," with nine major bridges spanning the Karun, including the historic Pol Sefid (White Bridge, 1936) and the Pol Hashtom, the largest cable bridge in the Middle East.

### Demographics and Culture
Ahvaz has a diverse population of over 1.3 million, including Persians, Arabs, Qashqai, and Kurds. Persian is the official language, but many are bilingual, speaking Khuzestani Arabic, Bakhtiari dialect, or Neo-Mandaic. Football is a major part of the city's culture, hosting several professional clubs like Foolad and Esteghlal Khuzestan. The city is also an educational center with universities such as Shahid Chamran University and Ahvaz Jundishapur University of Medical Sciences.

## Terms
- **Karun River**: The largest river in Iran by volume, flowing through Ahvaz; one of Iran's two navigable rivers.
- **Khuzestan**: The province of which Ahvaz is the capital, historically known as Susiana.
- **Academy of Gondishapur**: An ancient center of learning and medicine in the region, active during the Sasanian period.
- **Sasanian Empire**: The last Persian imperial dynasty before the Muslim conquest (224–651 CE).
- **Trans-Iranian Railway**: A major railway line connecting Tehran to the Persian Gulf, reaching Ahvaz in 1929.
- **Pol Sefid (White Bridge)**: A historic arch bridge completed in 1936, a symbol of the city.
- **Neo-Mandaic**: A Neo-Aramaic language spoken by the Mandaeans of Ahvaz.
- **Foolad F.C.**: A major football club in Ahvaz, champion of Iran's Premier League in 2005 and 2014.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The exact timing of the city's name change from Hormazd-Ardeshir to Darawashir is debated, with sources attributing it to either Ardashir I or his grandson Hormizd I.
- The political integration of Ahvaz's diverse ethnic groups, particularly its Arab population, remains a sensitive issue, with some groups advocating for greater cultural and provincial autonomy.

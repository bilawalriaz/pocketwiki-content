# History of Portugal

## Overview
Portugal's history spans from prehistoric settlements to its current status as a European democracy. Its identity was shaped by Roman rule, Germanic kingdoms, the Islamic conquest, the Christian Reconquista, and a pioneering Age of Discovery that created a global empire. The nation experienced periods of independence, union with Spain, dictatorship, and finally a democratic revolution in 1974, leading to its integration into the European Union.

- **218 BC** — Roman conquest of the Iberian Peninsula begins.
- **711–716** — Islamic Umayyad Caliphate invades, establishing Al-Andalus.
- **1095** — Portugal breaks away from the Kingdom of Galicia.
- **1139** — Afonso Henriques proclaims himself King of Portugal.
- **1249** — Conquest of the Algarve from the Moors completes Portugal's territory.

- **1415** — Conquest of Ceuta marks the beginning of the Portuguese Empire.
- **1494** — Treaty of Tordesillas divides the New World with Spain.
- **1580–1640** — Iberian Union: Portugal under the Spanish crown.
- **1640** — Portuguese Restoration War begins, ending Spanish rule.
- **1755** — Great Lisbon earthquake devastates the capital.
- **1822** — Independence of Brazil, Portugal's largest colony.
- **1910** — Monarchy abolished; First Republic established.
- **1926** — Military coup ends the First Republic, leading to the Estado Novo dictatorship.
- **1974** — Carnation Revolution ends dictatorship, beginning the Third Republic.
- **1986** — Portugal joins the European Economic Community (now EU).

## Body

### Prehistory and Roman Rule
The region has been inhabited since c. 400,000 years ago. Pre-Celtic and Celtic tribes, such as the Lusitanians and Gallaeci, settled the land. The Roman conquest, beginning in 218 BC, led to the provinces of Lusitania and Gallaecia. Romanization introduced Latin (the basis of Portuguese), Christianity, and urban infrastructure.

### Germanic Kingdoms and Islamic Conquest
After Rome's fall, Germanic tribes controlled the territory. The Kingdom of the Suebi (5th–8th centuries) and the Visigothic Kingdom were the major powers. In 711, the Islamic Umayyad Caliphate invaded, conquering most of the peninsula and establishing Al-Andalus. Muslim rule lasted for centuries, though the northern region remained largely Christian.

### The Reconquista and Foundation of the Kingdom
The Christian Reconquista began in 722. The County of Portugal was established in the 9th century. In 1139, Afonso Henriques declared himself king, and the Kingdom of Portugal was recognized in 1143. The final territory, the Algarve, was conquered in 1249, stabilizing Portugal's borders. The 1385 victory at Aljubarrota secured independence from Castile and led to the Treaty of Windsor (1386) with England.

### Age of Discovery and Empire
In the 15th and 16th centuries, Portugal pioneered European maritime exploration. Beginning with the capture of Ceuta (1415), it built a global empire with trading posts and colonies in Africa, Asia, and South America (including Brazil, claimed in 1500). The Treaty of Tordesillas (1494) divided newly discovered lands with Spain. This era made Portugal a world power but also involved the Atlantic slave trade.

### Iberian Union and Restoration
The death of King Sebastian in 1578 triggered a succession crisis. In 1580, Philip II of Spain claimed the Portuguese throne, beginning the 60-year Iberian Union. Portuguese nobles restored independence in 1640 under John IV of the House of Braganza, following the Portuguese Restoration War.

### 18th and 19th Centuries
The 18th century saw the reign of John V and the reforms of the Marquis of Pombal after the 1755 Lisbon earthquake. The 19th century was marked by the Napoleonic invasions, the transfer of the royal court to Brazil, and the loss of Brazilian independence in 1822. Political instability followed, ending the monarchy in 1910.

### The Estado Novo and the Third Republic
The First Republic (1910–1926) was unstable. A 1926 coup led to the authoritarian Estado Novo regime under António de Oliveira Salazar, which lasted until 1974. The Carnation Revolution, a military coup on April 25, 1974, overthrew the dictatorship, granted independence to African colonies, and established the democratic Third Republic. Portugal joined the European Economic Community in 1986.

## Terms
- **Reconquista**: The centuries-long period of Christian reconquest of the Iberian Peninsula from Muslim rule.
- **Al-Andalus**: The name given to the parts of the Iberian Peninsula under Muslim rule from 711 to 1492.
- **Treaty of Tordesillas (1494)**: Agreement between Portugal and Spain dividing newly discovered lands outside Europe between them.
- **Iberian Union (1580–1640)**: Period when the Spanish crown also ruled Portugal.
- **Estado Novo (1933–1974)**: The authoritarian corporatist regime led by António de Oliveira Salazar.
- **Carnation Revolution (1974)**: The largely bloodless military coup that overthrew the Estado Novo regime.
- **Retornados**: Portuguese citizens who returned from former African colonies after their independence in 1975.
- **Marquis of Pombal**: Sebastião de Melo, the chief minister who rebuilt Lisbon after the 1755 earthquake and implemented Enlightenment reforms.

## Debates and Open Questions
- Historians debate the nature of the First Republic (1910–1926): was it a progressive, democratic experiment or a continuation of elitist, unstable liberal regimes?
- The extent to which the Portuguese Age of Discovery was driven by economic motives versus religious or geopolitical ones is discussed.
- The legacy of the Estado Novo is contested, with some emphasizing its role in maintaining stability and others highlighting its repression and economic stagnation.

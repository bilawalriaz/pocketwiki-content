# Austrian Empire

## Overview
The Austrian Empire (1804–1867) was a multinational European great power formed from the Habsburg Monarchy's diverse territories. Created in response to Napoleon's rise, it was a key player in post-Napoleonic European politics but was ultimately weakened by nationalist pressures and military defeats, leading to its transformation into the dual monarchy of Austria-Hungary.

## Timeline
- **1804** — Francis II proclaims the Austrian Empire, uniting Habsburg lands.
- **1806** — Francis II abdicates as Holy Roman Emperor; the Holy Roman Empire dissolves.
- **1809** — Klemens von Metternich becomes Foreign Minister, beginning his era of influence.
- **1815** — Congress of Vienna reaffirms Austria as a great power and leader of the German Confederation.
- **1848** — Revolutions sweep the empire; Metternich resigns; serfdom is abolished.
- **1852** — Alexander von Bach's neo-absolutist policies centralize authority.
- **1859** — Austria loses Lombardy to France and Sardinia.
- **1866** — Austria is defeated by Prussia in the Austro-Prussian War.
- **1867** — Austro-Hungarian Compromise creates the dual monarchy of Austria-Hungary.

## Body

### Foundation and Napoleonic Wars
The Austrian Empire was proclaimed in 1804 by Holy Roman Emperor Francis II to safeguard his dynasty's imperial status against Napoleon's ambitions. It legally unified the Habsburg composite monarchy's separate realms, though the Kingdom of Hungary maintained its own institutions as a *Regnum Independens*. The empire remained part of the Holy Roman Empire until its dissolution in 1806. Austria fought Napoleon throughout the Napoleonic Wars, suffering major defeats at battles like Austerlitz (1805) and Wagram (1809), which led to territorial losses via treaties like Pressburg (1806). However, Austria played a decisive role in Napoleon's final defeat in 1813–14.

### The Metternich Era (1815–1848)
Foreign Minister Klemens von Metternich dominated Austrian and European politics after 1815. A staunch conservative, he used balance-of-power diplomacy and the "Metternich system" of international congresses (e.g., Aix-la-Chapelle, Troppau) to suppress liberalism and nationalism, preserving the Habsburg status quo. Domestically, he employed censorship and a spy network to stifle dissent. This era was marked by political stability, economic growth, and a population rise to 37.5 million by 1843, but it was also seen as reactionary, opposing national aspirations in Italy and Germany.

### Revolutions and Neo-Absolutism
The Revolutions of 1848 threatened the empire with nationalist, liberal, and socialist uprisings. Though most failed, they forced Metternich's resignation and led to lasting reforms like the abolition of serfdom and a promised constitution. After the revolutions, Minister Alexander von Bach implemented neo-absolutism (1852–1859), centralizing administration and allied with the Catholic Church via the 1855 Concordat. This period saw economic liberalization, such as the abolition of internal customs duties, but also political repression.

### Decline and Transformation
Military defeats accelerated the empire's decline. It lost Lombardy in 1859 and was decisively beaten by Prussia in 1866, dissolving the German Confederation. Diplomatically isolated after the Crimean War (1854–56), Austria could not prevent these losses. The Austro-Hungarian Compromise of 1867, following the 1866 defeat, created the dual monarchy of Austria-Hungary, joining the Empire of Austria and the Kingdom of Hungary as equal entities.

## Terms
- **Composite Monarchy**: A state formed by the personal union of several distinct kingdoms or territories under one monarch, each retaining its own laws and institutions.
- **Regnum Independens**: Latin for "independent kingdom," referring to Hungary's special status within the Habsburg realms, administered by its own Diet and monarch.
- **Congress of Vienna (1815)**: A conference of European powers that redrew the map of Europe after Napoleon's defeat, restoring conservative monarchies and establishing a balance of power.
- **German Confederation**: A loose association of 39 German states created in 1815, with Austria as its leading power.
- **Neo-absolutism**: A political system, associated with Alexander von Bach, that rejected constitutionalism and centralized state power under the monarch, often with Church support.
- **Dual Monarchy**: The political entity formed in 1867 by the Austro-Hungarian Compromise, uniting the Empire of Austria and the Kingdom of Hungary under a single monarch with separate governments.

## Debates and Open Questions
- Historians debate whether the Metternich era was primarily a period of effective stability and prosperity or of oppressive stagnation that delayed necessary reforms.
- The extent to which Metternich's resignation in 1848 directly caused the spread of revolutions throughout the monarchy is uncertain and stipulated but not confirmed.
- The long-term impact of Bach's neo-absolutism is contested: it centralized the state and modernized the economy but also fostered nationalist resentment, particularly among Czechs.

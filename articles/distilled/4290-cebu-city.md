# Cebu City

## Overview
Cebu City is a highly urbanized city in the Central Visayas region of the Philippines and its regional center. It is the country's sixth-most populous city and the most populous in the Visayas. Serving as the prime trading center of the southern Philippines, it is a major hub for domestic shipping, commerce, and tourism. Its historical significance as the site of the first Spanish settlement and the "cradle of Christianity in the Far East" underpins its modern cultural and economic influence.

## Timeline
- **1225** — The kingdom of Suwu (Cebu) is mentioned in the Chinese work *Zhu Fan Zhi*.
- **April 7, 1521** — Ferdinand Magellan lands in Cebu and is welcomed by Rajah Humabon.
- **April 15, 1565** — Spanish conquistadors led by Miguel López de Legazpi arrive, founding the settlement.
- **July 3, 1565** — The Treaty of Cebu is formalized; the city is named Villa de San Miguel de Cebú.
- **August 14, 1595** — Pope Clement VIII creates the Diocese of Cebu.
- **February 24, 1937** — Cebu City's charter is restored, merging several neighboring municipalities.
- **March–April 1945** — Cebu City is liberated from Japanese occupation in the Battle for Cebu City.
- **1990s** — The "Ceboom" period of rapid economic growth begins following Typhoon Ruping.
- **October 15, 2013** — A massive earthquake causes significant damage to the city and neighboring Bohol.
- **December 2021** — Super Typhoon Rai (Odette) devastates the city, causing widespread calamity.
- **2022** — The Cebu–Cordova Link Expressway (CCLEX) bridge opens.

## Body

### Etymology and Pre-Hispanic Period
The name "Cebu" derives from the native word *Sugbo*, meaning "to wade into water." Archaeological evidence suggests settlement from at least the 10th century CE, with artifacts dating to the 14th-15th centuries. Chinese records from the 13th century reference a kingdom called Suwu. Pre-colonial Cebu was a small polity with Indo-Malay cultural influences, ruled by nobility with Sanskrit-derived titles like "Rajah." The popular myth of a founding by "Sri Lumay" is considered a modern invention unsupported by historical documents.

### Spanish Period and Founding
Portuguese explorer Ferdinand Magellan landed in Cebu in 1521, initiating contact with local ruler Rajah Humabon. Spanish colonization began in 1565 under Miguel López de Legazpi, who established the first permanent settlement. The city became a crucial port for ships from Mexico and a base for exploring the archipelago. A diocese was established in 1595. The Spanish period saw the construction of Fort San Pedro and the introduction of Latin American and Spanish cultural influences. The city's status, granted by Spain in 1594, was later invalidated by a change in colonial administration.

### American Period, World War II, and Cityhood
Following the Spanish-American War, Cebu was transferred to American control in 1899. Resistance continued until 1901. In 1937, the city's charter was restored, merging the municipalities of El Pardo, Mabolo, Talamban, Banilad, and San Nicolás. During World War II, Cebu was occupied by the Japanese, who established "comfort stations." The city was liberated in 1945 after being virtually razed to the ground.

### Post-War and Economic "Ceboom"
Post-war reconstruction was rapid, with the business district expanding inland. A key catalyst for modern growth was Typhoon Ruping in 1990, which forced local authorities to rethink development priorities. This led to the "Ceboom" economic boom of the 1990s, shifting activity from the old downtown to new business districts like the Cebu Business Park and Cebu IT Park. The city became a major hub for the business process outsourcing (BPO) industry.

### Government and Administration
As a highly urbanized city, Cebu City is administratively independent from Cebu province. It is governed by a mayor, vice mayor, and sixteen councilors representing two congressional districts. The city is politically subdivided into 80 barangays.

### Economy and Infrastructure
Cebu City is the prime trading center of the southern Philippines and hosts about 80% of the country's domestic shipping companies. Key economic sectors include tourism, BPO, real estate, and shipbuilding. Major infrastructure includes the Port of Cebu, the South Road Properties (SRP) development, and the Cebu–Cordova Link Expressway (CCLEX). A Bus Rapid Transit (BRT) system, TransCebu, began partial operations in 2026.

### Culture and Tourism
The city is a significant cultural center, known for landmarks like Magellan's Cross and the Basilica Minore del Santo Niño. The annual Sinulog festival is a major religious and cultural event honoring the Santo Niño. Cebu City is a UNESCO City of Design and a gateway to the region's diving and coastal destinations. It is also the birthplace of the BisRock music genre.

### Demographics and Religion
The population was 965,332 in the 2024 census. The vast majority speak Cebuano. Roman Catholicism is the predominant religion, practiced by about 95% of the population, making the Roman Catholic Archdiocese of Cebu the largest in Asia.

## Terms
- **Highly Urbanized City (HUC):** A city in the Philippines administratively independent from its province.
- **Barangay:** The smallest administrative division in the Philippines, similar to a village or district.
- **Ceboom:** A portmanteau of "Cebu" and "boom," referring to the city's rapid economic growth starting in the 1990s.
- **Sinulog:** A dance prayer ritual of pre-Hispanic origin, now the central event of Cebu's annual festival honoring the Santo Niño.
- **BisRock:** A genre of rock music originating in Cebu, sung primarily in the Cebuano language.
- **Encomienda:** A Spanish colonial system where a grantee was given authority over indigenous people for tribute and labor.
- **Sandugo:** A blood compact ritual performed to seal a bond or treaty, as between Legazpi and Datu Sikatuna in 1565.
- **BPO:** Business Process Outsourcing, an industry where companies contract external providers for services like customer support and IT.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The authenticity of the "Sri Lumay" founding myth and the historical significance of the supposed capital "Singhapala" are highly debated and considered by scholars to be modern inventions.
- The exact date of the initial settlement and naming of the village "Sugbu" remains unknown due to the lack of pre-colonial written records among the Visayans.
- The historical interpretation of the "Pisheye" territories mentioned in Chinese records and their precise relationship to pre-colonial Cebu is a subject of scholarly analysis.

# Padmasambhava

## Overview
Padmasambhava, also known as Guru Rinpoche ("Precious Guru"), is a semi-legendary tantric Buddhist master from medieval India, revered as the central figure in the transmission of Buddhism to Tibet in the 8th century. He is considered an emanation of Shakyamuni Buddha and is the founding figure of the Nyingma school of Tibetan Buddhism. His legacy is defined by the founding of Tibet's first monastery, the subjugation of local spirits, and the concealment of secret teachings (terma) for future discovery.

## Timeline
- **c. 8th century** — Invited to Tibet by King Trisong Detsen to help found Samye Monastery.
- **c. 8th century** — Designed Samye, the first Buddhist monastery in Tibet.
- **c. 8th century** — With King Trisong Detsen and Śāntarakṣita, helped create the Tibetan Canon by translating Buddhist teachings.
- **c. 12th century** — First full biography, *The Copper Palace*, revealed by tertön Nyangral Nyima Özer.
- **c. 14th century** — Orgyen Lingpa expands the hagiography, introducing the "11 deeds" of Padmasambhava.

## Body

### Historical Figure and Early Sources
Early Tibetan sources, such as the *Testament of Ba* (c. 9th–12th centuries), record Padmasambhava as a tantric master who helped found Samye Monastery during King Trisong Detsen's reign. He was invited by the abbot Śāntarakṣita to tame local spirits and gods whose opposition was hindering construction. After subduing them, he taught tantric yoga and was later asked to leave when the court suspected he sought power. While his historical authenticity was once questioned, it is now "cautiously accepted." Texts like the *Man ngag lta ba'i phreng ba* are attributed to him in sources from the 9th–10th centuries.

### Development of the Mythos
From the 12th century onward, hagiographies expanded Padmasambhava's profile, portraying him as a "second Buddha" who tamed all Tibetan spirits and concealed terma texts. Nyangral Nyima Özer (1124–1192) authored the influential *Zangling-ma* (Jeweled Rosary), the earliest full biography. Later, Orgyen Lingpa (1323–c. 1360) further expanded the narrative in works like the *Padma bka' thang* (Lotus Testament). This growing legend eventually overshadowed other founding figures of Tibetan Buddhism.

### Hagiographical Life
Traditional accounts depict Padmasambhava's life in three realms: Dharmakaya (truth body), Sambhogakaya (enjoyment body), and Nirmanakaya (emanation body). Key episodes include his miraculous birth from a lotus in Oddiyana, his exile and practice as a mahasiddha in Indian charnel grounds, and his conversion of Princess Mandarava in India. He traveled to Nepal for long-life rituals and to Tibet, where he subdued deities, founded Samye, and initiated key disciples like Yeshe Tsogyal. He is also associated with Bhutan, where he meditated at Paro Taktsang ("Tiger's Nest").

### Teachings and Legacy
Padmasambhava's teachings are preserved in two lineages: the oral lineage (kama) and the hidden treasure lineage (terma). The Nyingma school traces its Dzogchen lineage to him. He is visualized in guru yoga practice and is the subject of the widely recited Vajra Guru mantra and Seven Line Prayer. His life is depicted in Cham dances, and terma cycles attributed to him, such as the *Bardo Thodol* (Tibetan Book of the Dead), are practiced across Tibetan Buddhist schools.

### Iconography and Manifestations
Padmasambhava is iconographically depicted with one face and two hands, wearing a lotus hat and holding a vajra and skull-cup. His eight manifestations represent different aspects of his activity, such as Guru Pema Gyalpo (Lotus King) and the wrathful Guru Dorje Drolo. His five main consorts, or wisdom dakinis, are emanations of Vajravārāhī and include Yeshe Tsogyal and Mandarava. He is said to reside in the pureland paradise of Zangdok Palri (Copper-Coloured Mountain).

## Terms
*   **Padmasambhava**: "Born from a Lotus"; a tantric Buddhist master central to Tibetan Buddhism.
*   **Guru Rinpoche**: "Precious Guru"; a title for Padmasambhava.
*   **Nyingma**: The "Ancient" school of Tibetan Buddhism, which considers Padmasambhava its founder.
*   **Terma**: "Treasure texts" hidden by Padmasambhava for future discovery by tertöns.
*   **Tertön**: A "treasure finder" who discovers hidden terma teachings.
*   **Dzogchen**: "Great Perfection"; a tradition of teachings in the Nyingma school, traced to Padmasambhava.
*   **Samye**: The first Buddhist monastery in Tibet, designed by Padmasambhava.
*   **Vajrayana**: "Diamond Vehicle"; a form of Buddhism emphasizing tantric practices.
*   **Dakini**: A female tantric practitioner or enlightened being, often a consort of a master.
*   **Kama**: The oral lineage of teachings passed down through an unbroken chain.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **Historical Authenticity**: While earlier Tibetologists questioned his historicity, modern scholarship "cautiously accepts" him as a historical figure based on early sources and physical evidence like rock inscriptions.
*   **Evolution of the Mythos**: Scholars debate the extent to which the later, elaborate hagiographies (from the 12th century onward) reflect historical events versus theological and cultural developments.
*   **Authorship of Texts**: The attribution of specific tantric texts to Padmasambhava is a subject of scholarly analysis, with some works being more likely than others based on early citations.

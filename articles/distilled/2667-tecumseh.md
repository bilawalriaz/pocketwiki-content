# Tecumseh

## Overview
Tecumseh (c. 1768–1813) was a Shawnee chief and warrior who became a pivotal figure in Native American resistance to U.S. expansion. He is renowned for his oratory and for attempting to forge a vast, multi-tribal confederacy to halt the loss of Indigenous lands. His alliance with the British during the War of 1812 and his death in battle cemented his legacy as a folk hero in American, Canadian, and Indigenous histories.

- **1812** — Tecumseh allies with the British and helps capture Detroit.
- **1813** — Killed at the Battle of the Thames in Upper Canada.

## Body

### Early Life and Warrior Training
Tecumseh was born into the Shawnee Panther clan during a period when his tribe was reuniting in the Ohio Country after decades of displacement. His childhood was marked by violent conflict as American colonies expanded. His father was killed in the 1774 Battle of Point Pleasant, and Tecumseh was subsequently mentored by his older brother, Cheeseekau, a noted war chief. As a young warrior, he participated in the armed struggle against American encroachment, culminating in the defeat at the Battle of Fallen Timbers (1794) and the loss of most Ohio lands via the Treaty of Greenville (1795).

### The Prophet's Movement and Prophetstown
In 1805, Tecumseh's younger brother, Tenskwatawa, launched a religious revival calling for a rejection of European cultural ways and a return to traditional practices. The brothers established Prophetstown in 1808, which grew into a large, multi-tribal community. Tecumseh's political message complemented this spiritual movement: he proclaimed that Native lands were owned in common by all tribes and that no territory should be ceded without unanimous consent. This stance alarmed U.S. leaders and accommodationist Native chiefs. In 1811, while Tecumseh was recruiting allies in the South, U.S. forces under William Henry Harrison attacked and destroyed Prophetstown at the Battle of Tippecanoe.

### Building a Confederacy
Following the controversial Treaty of Fort Wayne (1809), Tecumseh emerged as the primary diplomat and military strategist for a proposed pan-tribal confederacy stretching from the Great Lakes to the Gulf of Mexico. He envisioned a permanent, sovereign Native nation with defined borders. In 1810 and 1811, he met with Governor Harrison at Vincennes, presenting a powerful indictment of U.S. land seizures and alleged biological warfare. Tecumseh then traveled thousands of miles to recruit allies among tribes in the Northwest and South. While he found receptive listeners among some Muscogee (Creek) factions, many southern tribes, like the Choctaws and Chickasaws, rejected his overtures.

### War of 1812 and Death
Tecumseh allied with the British in the War of 1812, seeing it as a chance to secure Native land rights. He played a key role in the capture of Detroit in August 1812. After the American naval victory on Lake Erie in 1813, the British retreated into Canada. Tecumseh reluctantly followed, and his forces were engaged at the Battle of the Thames on October 5, 1813. The British line collapsed, and Tecumseh was killed in the fighting. His death led to the immediate collapse of his confederacy. The British attempt to create a Native barrier state in post-war negotiations was rejected by the United States.

## Terms
*   **Confederacy:** A union of sovereign states or tribes for a common purpose. Tecumseh's goal was a multi-tribal alliance to resist U.S. expansion.
*   **Pan-Indianism:** A movement advocating for political and cultural unity among diverse Native American tribes, a concept Tecumseh pioneered.
*   **Treaty of Greenville (1795):** A treaty that ended the Northwest Indian War, forcing Native tribes to cede most of present-day Ohio to the United States.
*   **Treaty of Fort Wayne (1809):** A controversial agreement in which the U.S. purchased 2.5–3 million acres of land in present-day Indiana and Illinois, which Tecumseh argued was illegitimate.
*   **Prophetstown:** A multi-tribal village established by Tecumseh and his brother Tenskwatawa in 1808, serving as the spiritual and political center of their movement.
*   **Shawnee:** An Algonquian-speaking Native American tribe originally from the Ohio Valley, central to Tecumseh's life and resistance.
*   **War of 1812:** A conflict between the United States and Great Britain (1812–1815), which Tecumseh joined to further Native American interests.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **The Battle of Tippecanoe's Impact:** Historians debate whether the 1811 battle was a devastating blow to Tecumseh's confederacy or a setback from which it could have recovered had he been present.
*   **Tenskwatawa's Role:** Traditional accounts claim Tecumseh disowned his brother after Tippecanoe, but modern scholarship suggests Tenskwatawa retained his spiritual leadership role.
*   **Killer of Tecumseh:** The identity of the soldier who fired the fatal shot at the Battle of the Thames is disputed. Colonel Richard Mentor Johnson's claim, which helped him become U.S. Vice President, is the most famous but not definitively proven.
*   **Burial Site:** The location of Tecumseh's remains is unknown, with competing traditions placing them at the battlefield, in Canada, or on Walpole Island.

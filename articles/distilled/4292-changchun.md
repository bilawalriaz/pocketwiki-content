# Changchun

## Overview
Changchun is the capital and largest city of Jilin Province in Northeast China. Its modern identity is defined by its role as the historic capital of the Japanese puppet state Manchukuo and its current status as a major industrial hub, particularly for automobile manufacturing, earning it the nickname "China's Detroit."

## Body

### Early History and Railway Era
Changchun began as a Qing dynasty trading post named in 1800. Its strategic location was transformed by the arrival of railways in 1898, built by Russia. After the Russo-Japanese War (1904-05), Japan gained control of the southern railway line. A 1907 agreement made Changchun a critical junction and break-of-gauge point between the Russian and Japanese railway systems, spurring rapid development.

### Japanese Planning and Manchukuo (1906-1945)
From 1906, Japan developed Changchun as a colonial railway hub, purchasing land without Chinese consent and investing heavily in urban planning. In 1932, Japan established the puppet state of Manchukuo and renamed the city Hsinking ("New Capital"). A grand metropolitan plan, influenced by Paris and garden city movements, was implemented. The city became an administrative and military center, with population growing from ~142,000 in 1934 to over 863,000 by 1944. Architecture featured a "Pan-Asianist" style. During this period, the Japanese military established Unit 100, a biological warfare facility in the city.

### Civil War and People's Republic
After Soviet capture in 1945, the city was contested during the Chinese Civil War. It fell to the Communists in 1948 after a devastating five-month siege that caused mass starvation. Under the People's Republic, Changchun became Jilin's capital in 1954. It was designated a center for China's automotive industry, with the state-owned FAW Group beginning production in 1956. The city later faced economic challenges due to layoffs from struggling state-owned enterprises in the 1990s.

### Economy and Industry
Changchun's economy is dominated by automobile manufacturing, led by the FAW Group, which produces brands like Hongqi and has joint ventures with Volkswagen and Audi. Other key industries include agricultural processing, biopharmaceuticals, photo-electronics, and railway vehicle manufacturing (CRRC). The city hosts several development zones, including a major automotive trade center and a high-tech zone focused on bio-engineering and information technology.

### Infrastructure and Education
The city has a compact, planned layout with a comprehensive transport network, including multiple railway stations, an international airport, and a rail transit system that opened in 2002. It is a major educational center, home to top universities like Jilin University and Northeast Normal University, and is ranked among the world's top 30 cities for scientific research.

## Terms
- **Manchukuo:** A Japanese-controlled puppet state in Northeast China (1932-1945).
- **South Manchuria Railway (SMR):** A Japanese-owned railway company that controlled key infrastructure in Manchuria.
- **Break of gauge:** A point where railway lines of different track widths meet, requiring transfer of cargo or passengers.
- **Pan-Asianist style:** An architectural style promoted by Japan during the Manchukuo era, blending modern concrete structures with traditional Asian ornamental roofs.
- **FAW Group:** China's "First Automotive Works," a major state-owned automobile manufacturer headquartered in Changchun.
- **Xiagang:** A term referring to the mass layoffs from state-owned enterprises in China during the 1990s economic restructuring.
- **Sub-provincial city:** An administrative division in China with a budget and planning authority above a prefecture but below a province.

## Debates and Open Questions

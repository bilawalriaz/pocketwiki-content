# Karachi

## Overview
Karachi is the capital of Pakistan's Sindh province and the country's largest city, with a population exceeding 20 million. It is the nation's primary industrial, financial, and commercial hub, generating about 25% of Pakistan's GDP and handling 95% of its foreign trade. Founded as a fortified village in 1729, its strategic coastal location led to rapid growth under British rule as a major seaport. After Pakistan's independence in 1947, Karachi became the national capital and experienced massive demographic transformation due to migration, evolving into one of the world's most populous and ethnically diverse metropolises.

## Body

### Early History and Foundation
The Karachi region has been inhabited for millennia, with ancient Greek texts possibly referring to it as Barbarikon or Ramya. The modern city was formally founded in 1729 as the fortified village of Kolachi-jo-Goth, named after a resident, Mai Kolachi. It was a small fishing settlement until it came under the control of the Talpur dynasty in 1795.

### British Colonial Development
The British East India Company captured Karachi in 1839. Recognizing its strategic value, they developed it into a major seaport and transportation hub, connecting it to the Indian subcontinent's railway network. The city's population grew from an estimated 8,000-14,000 in 1839 to 117,000 by 1901. It became the largest wheat-exporting port in the British Empire by 1914.

### Post-Independence Transformation
At independence in 1947, Karachi had a population of over 400,000 with a slight Hindu majority. Partition caused a massive demographic shift: hundreds of thousands of Muslim refugees (Muhajirs) arrived from India, while most of the Hindu population left. By 1951, Muslims constituted 96% of the population. Karachi served as Pakistan's capital until 1959 and remained the capital of Sindh province. The city experienced rapid economic growth and continued to attract migrants from across Pakistan and South Asia.

### Modern Challenges and Economy
From the 1980s, Karachi faced sharp ethnic, sectarian, and political conflict, exacerbated by the influx of weapons from the Soviet–Afghan War. Crime rates soared, but a controversial crackdown operation initiated by the Pakistan Rangers in 2013 led to a significant decrease in violent crime. Today, Karachi is Pakistan's economic powerhouse, with an estimated GDP (PPP) of over $190 billion (2021). It is home to the country's two largest seaports, busiest airport, and the headquarters of most multinational corporations and banks.

## Terms
- **Muhajir**: An Arabic term meaning "migrant," used to describe the Urdu-speaking Muslim refugees who migrated to Pakistan from India after the 1947 partition.
- **Talpur dynasty**: A Sindhi dynasty that ruled Sindh and parts of Punjab from 1783 until 1843, when they were defeated by the British.
- **Sindh**: A province in southern Pakistan, with Karachi as its capital.
- **One Unit programme**: A 1955 political initiative that merged all provinces of West Pakistan into a single unit, which lasted until 1970.
- **MQM (Muttahida Qaumi Movement)**: A political party in Pakistan, founded in 1984, that primarily represents the Muhajir community.
- **Pakistan Rangers**: A paramilitary force under the Ministry of Interior of Pakistan, tasked with maintaining law and order.
- **GDP (PPP)**: Gross Domestic Product at Purchasing Power Parity, an economic metric that adjusts for differences in price levels between countries.
- **Orangi Pilot Project (OPP)**: A community-based organization founded in 1980 that pioneered low-cost sanitation and housing solutions in Karachi's Orangi Town.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Population Figures**: The official 2017 census figure of 14.9 million for Karachi is disputed by political parties and some analysts, who argue it undercounts the population due to methodological issues and the city's complex administrative boundaries. Estimates have ranged from 18 to over 25 million.
- **Impact of the Rangers Operation**: While credited with drastically reducing crime, the 2013 Karachi operation is described as "controversial," with concerns about extrajudicial actions and its impact on political dynamics.
- **Governance and Infrastructure**: The city's fragmented civic administration, with multiple authorities (KMC, DMCs, cantonments, provincial agencies) controlling different services, is frequently cited as a root cause of its chronic infrastructure and service delivery problems.

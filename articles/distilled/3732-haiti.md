# Haiti

## Overview
Haiti is a Caribbean nation occupying the western third of Hispaniola, sharing the island with the Dominican Republic. It holds profound historical significance as the first sovereign state in the Caribbean, the second republic in the Americas, and the only country in history established by a successful slave revolt. Despite this revolutionary legacy, Haiti has endured a tumultuous history of political instability, foreign intervention, and economic hardship, culminating in a severe ongoing crisis marked by gang violence and state collapse.

## Timeline
- **1492** — Christopher Columbus lands on Hispaniola, establishing the first European settlement, La Navidad.
- **1697** — Treaty of Ryswick cedes the western third of Hispaniola to France, which names it Saint-Domingue.
- **1791–1804** — The Haitian Revolution, a slave revolt, leads to independence from France.
- **1804** — Haiti declares independence; Jean-Jacques Dessalines becomes Emperor.
- **1825** — France recognizes Haitian independence in exchange for a crippling indemnity of 150 million francs.
- **1915–1934** — The United States occupies Haiti.
- **1957–1986** — The Duvalier dynasty (François "Papa Doc" and Jean-Claude "Baby Doc") rules as a dictatorship.
- **2010** — A magnitude-7.0 earthquake devastates the country, followed by a cholera outbreak.
- **2021** — President Jovenel Moïse is assassinated, deepening a political crisis.
- **2024** — Gangs force Prime Minister Ariel Henry to resign; a Transitional Presidential Council is formed.

## Body

### Colonial Era and Revolution
Originally inhabited by the Taíno people, Hispaniola was claimed by Spain in 1492. The western portion was ceded to France in 1697, becoming the extremely profitable colony of Saint-Domingue, built on a brutal plantation economy reliant on enslaved Africans. Inspired by the French Revolution, a massive slave revolt began in 1791. After a prolonged struggle led by figures like Toussaint Louverture and Jean-Jacques Dessalines, the enslaved population defeated French forces. Haiti declared independence on January 1, 1804, becoming the first free black republic.

### Post-Independence Struggles and Foreign Intervention
The new nation faced immediate challenges, including a massacre of most remaining whites, political fragmentation, and international isolation. A crippling indemnity demanded by France in 1825 for recognition of independence burdened Haiti's economy for over a century. The 19th century was marked by political instability, failed attempts to reunify Hispaniola, and a second empire. In 1915, the United States occupied Haiti, imposing a new constitution and infrastructure projects but also suppressing dissent, until withdrawing in 1934.

### The Duvalier Era and Modern Instability
After decades of political turmoil, François "Papa Doc" Duvalier established a dictatorship in 1957, using a private militia, the Tontons Macoutes, to terrorize the population. He was succeeded by his son, Jean-Claude "Baby Doc," who ruled until 1986. The post-Duvalier era has been characterized by coups, contested elections, and foreign interventions. A catastrophic earthquake in 2010 and a subsequent cholera outbreak, introduced by UN peacekeepers, caused immense devastation. Following the 2021 assassination of President Jovenel Moïse, the state has largely collapsed, with gangs controlling much of the capital and no elected officials in power.

### Economy and Society
Haiti is one of the poorest countries in the Western Hemisphere, with an economy reliant on agriculture (notably vetiver), remittances, and foreign aid. Widespread poverty, corruption, and unemployment are endemic. Society is stratified, with a historical legacy of racial hierarchy. The official languages are French and Haitian Creole, and while Catholicism is the largest religion, Vodou is widely practiced, often syncretized with Christianity. The country faces severe challenges in healthcare, education, and infrastructure.

## Terms
*   **Taíno:** The Indigenous Arawakan-speaking people who inhabited Hispaniola before European contact.
*   **Saint-Domingue:** The name of the French colony on the western third of Hispaniola, renowned for its sugar production and brutal slave system.
*   **Maroons:** Enslaved Africans who escaped to remote, autonomous communities in the mountains.
*   **Indemnity:** The 150 million franc debt imposed by France in 1825 in exchange for recognizing Haitian independence, which crippled the nation's economy.
*   **Tontons Macoutes:** The paramilitary militia and secret police force created by François Duvalier to enforce his rule through terror.
*   **Vodou:** A religion with West African roots, officially recognized in Haiti in 2003, often syncretized with Catholicism.
*   **Haitian Creole (Kreyòl):** A French-based creole language spoken by virtually the entire population, with grammar influenced by West African languages.
*   **Restavek:** A system where impoverished families send children to live with wealthier households as domestic servants, a practice controversially likened to child slavery.

## Debates and Open Questions
*   **The 2004 Ouster of Aristide:** The circumstances of President Jean-Bertrand Aristide's removal from power are disputed. Aristide and his supporters claim he was the victim of a U.S.-orchestrated "coup d'état or modern kidnapping," while the U.S. government denies this.
*   **Impact of the French Indemnity:** Scholars debate the precise long-term economic impact of the 1825 debt, with estimates of lost economic growth ranging from $21 billion to $115 billion.
*   **U.S. Occupation Legacy:** The U.S. occupation (1915–1934) is viewed by some as having built necessary infrastructure, while others emphasize its violent suppression of dissent and the deep resentment it fostered among Haitians.
*   **Cholera Responsibility:** While the UN apologized in 2016 for introducing cholera via a peacekeeping station, it has refused to acknowledge legal fault, avoiding financial responsibility for the epidemic that killed thousands.

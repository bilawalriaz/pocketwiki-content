# Mahathir Mohamad

## Overview
Mahathir bin Mohamad is a Malaysian politician and physician who served as the country's fourth and seventh prime minister (1981–2003, 2018–2020). He is Malaysia's longest-serving prime minister, with a cumulative 24 years in office, and is credited with transforming the nation into a modern, industrialized economy. His political career, spanning over 75 years, has been marked by significant economic development, controversial authoritarian measures, and a complex legacy of Malay nationalism and modernization.

## Timeline
- **10 July 1925** — Born in Alor Setar, Kedah.
- **1953** — Graduates in medicine from the University of Malaya.
- **1964** — Enters parliament as MP for Kota Setar Selatan.
- **1970** — Publishes *The Malay Dilemma*; expelled from UMNO.
- **1976** — Appointed Deputy Prime Minister.
- **16 July 1981** — Sworn in as Prime Minister for the first time.
- **1987** — Orders Operation Lalang detentions; survives UMNO leadership challenge.
- **1991** — Announces Vision 2020 for Malaysia to become a developed nation.
- **1998** — Dismisses Deputy PM Anwar Ibrahim, sparking Reformasi movement.
- **31 October 2003** — Retires as Prime Minister after 22 years.
- **10 May 2018** — Wins general election, becoming Prime Minister for a second time.
- **24 February 2020** — Resigns amid political crisis, ending his second term.

## Body

### Early Life and Political Rise
Born to a school principal, Mahathir excelled academically and became a physician. He joined the United Malays National Organisation (UMNO) and entered parliament in 1964. After losing his seat in 1969 and being expelled from UMNO for criticizing Prime Minister Tunku Abdul Rahman, he wrote *The Malay Dilemma*, which argued for affirmative action for Malays. He rejoined UMNO in 1972, rose rapidly through ministerial posts (Education, Trade and Industry), and became Deputy Prime Minister in 1976.

### First Premiership (1981–2003)
Mahathir's first tenure was defined by aggressive economic modernization and infrastructure development. He promoted privatization, launched major projects like the North–South Expressway and the Kuala Lumpur City Centre, and established the national carmaker Proton. His government achieved high economic growth, transforming Malaysia into a dynamic emerging economy. Politically, he was dominant, winning five consecutive elections. He navigated the 1997 Asian financial crisis by imposing capital controls, defying IMF advice, and securing a swift recovery. His rule was also marked by centralization of power, including the 1988 constitutional crisis that raised concerns about judicial independence, and the 1998 dismissal and imprisonment of Deputy Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim.

### Foreign Policy
Mahathir was a prominent voice for the developing world. He championed "Asian values" and alternative development models. His "Look East" policy prioritized economic and cultural ties with Japan. He maintained a pragmatic but sometimes contentious relationship with the West, criticizing neoliberalism and the Iraq War while attracting foreign investment. He strengthened ties with China, navigated disputes in the South China Sea, and was a vocal supporter of the Palestinian cause. He also played a key role in ASEAN, supporting Myanmar's inclusion and later criticizing its junta.

### Second Premiership and Later Career (2018–Present)
At 92, Mahathir returned as Prime Minister in 2018, leading the Pakatan Harapan coalition to a historic victory. His second term focused on combating corruption (notably the 1MDB scandal), reviewing Chinese-funded infrastructure projects, and implementing fiscal reforms. He secured a pardon for Anwar Ibrahim but resigned in 2020 amid a political crisis over the planned power handover. He remains politically active, forming new parties and continuing to influence national discourse, though he lost his parliamentary seat in 2022.

## Terms
- **Bumiputera**: A term for the indigenous Malay and other native peoples of Malaysia, who are afforded special economic and educational privileges under policies like the New Economic Policy.
- **UMNO (United Malays National Organisation)**: The dominant political party in Malaysia's ruling coalition for decades, which Mahathir led and later left and rejoined multiple times.
- **Vision 2020**: A development roadmap announced by Mahathir in 1991, aiming for Malaysia to achieve fully developed nation status by 2020.
- **Reformasi**: A political reform movement that emerged in 1998 following the dismissal and arrest of Deputy Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim.
- **1MDB Scandal**: A massive corruption and money laundering scandal involving the state investment fund 1Malaysia Development Berhad, which became a central issue in Malaysian politics.
- **Pakatan Harapan**: The opposition coalition that Mahathir led to victory in the 2018 general election.
- **Look East Policy**: Mahathir's initiative to emulate the economic development models and work ethic of Japan and South Korea.
- **Asian Financial Crisis (1997-98)**: A regional economic downturn that Mahathir addressed with controversial capital controls and a fixed exchange rate, diverging from IMF prescriptions.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Authoritarianism vs. Stability**: Scholars debate whether Mahathir's centralization of power and suppression of dissent were necessary for national stability and economic growth or represented a damaging erosion of democratic institutions.
- **Economic Legacy**: While credited with modernization, his heavy-industrialization projects like Perwaja Steel resulted in massive losses, raising questions about the efficacy of state-led industrial policy.
- **Ethnic Politics**: His lifelong advocacy for Malay affirmative action is seen by some as essential for equity and by others as fostering dependency and racial division.
- **Political Longevity**: His repeated political comebacks and shifting party allegiances prompt analysis of his enduring influence and its impact on Malaysia's political system.

# Harare

## Overview
Harare is the capital and largest city of Zimbabwe, serving as the nation's primary commercial, financial, and governmental hub. Founded in 1890 as a colonial fort, it has evolved through periods of rapid growth, political turmoil, and economic crisis, reflecting the broader history of Zimbabwe itself. Its development and current challenges, including urban sprawl and infrastructure strain, are central to understanding modern Zimbabwe.

- **1935** — Granted city status.

- **April 2022** — National parliament moves to the New Parliament Building in Mount Hampden.

## Body

### Colonial Foundation and Early Growth
Harare was founded on 12 September 1890 by the Pioneer Column, a settler force organized by Cecil Rhodes, and named Fort Salisbury after the British Prime Minister. The British South Africa Company administered the city until Southern Rhodesia achieved responsible government in 1923, with Salisbury as its capital. Early development occurred on sloping ground, with the Causeway area later becoming the site of key government buildings.

### Post-War Boom and Federation
After World War II, the city expanded rapidly as the capital of the Federation of Rhodesia and Nyasaland (1953–1963). This period brought significant immigration, investment, and suburban sprawl, transforming the skyline. The federation's collapse in 1963 ended this era of optimism and prosperity.

### Independence and Renaming
Following Zimbabwe's independence in 1980, Harare experienced a boom fueled by optimism and investment. The city was renamed Harare on 18 April 1982, from the Shona name for a local chief. This period saw growth in education, healthcare, and a middle class, symbolized by landmarks like the New Reserve Bank Tower. The city hosted major international summits, including the 1986 Non-Aligned Movement summit.

### Economic Crisis and Hyperinflation (1999–2008)
Harare was severely impacted by Zimbabwe's political and economic crises. Essential services deteriorated, and in 2005, the government demolished shanties in Operation Murambatsvina, allegedly to suppress opposition support. The city was voted the world's toughest place to live in 2009 and 2011 by the Economist Intelligence Unit.

### Contemporary Challenges and Growth
Since 2000, Harare has seen periods of decline but also stabilization and uneven growth. Significant real estate development has occurred in northern suburbs, fueled by diaspora investment and speculation, leading to high property prices. However, the city faces challenges like unchecked urban sprawl, a persistent brain drain of skilled professionals, and high unemployment. The national parliament relocated to Mount Hampden in 2022.

## Terms
- **Pioneer Column**: A military volunteer force of settlers organized by Cecil Rhodes that founded Salisbury in 1890.
- **Federation of Rhodesia and Nyasaland**: A federal state (1953–1963) comprising Southern Rhodesia (Zimbabwe), Northern Rhodesia (Zambia), and Nyasaland (Malawi).
- **Operation Murambatsvina**: A 2005 government campaign to demolish informal settlements and illegal vending sites in Harare and other cities.
- **Highveld**: The high-altitude plateau region of Zimbabwe where Harare is situated.
- **Subtropical highland climate (Köppen Cwb)**: A climate type characterized by mild temperatures and distinct wet and dry seasons, moderated by high elevation.
- **Brain drain**: The emigration of highly educated or skilled people from a country, a significant issue for Harare.
- **Gentrification**: The process where wealthier individuals move into a deteriorating neighborhood, renovating housing and often displacing poorer residents.
- **Gamma World City**: A ranking by the Globalization and World Cities Research Network indicating a city with some international influence, which Harare was classified as in 2018 and 2020.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The true purpose of Operation Murambatsvina is debated; the government cited crime and disease, while it was widely alleged to be a political move to weaken opposition support in urban areas.
- The effectiveness of the Mnangagwa government's reforms to improve Harare's business environment and attract diaspora investment remains uncertain, with progress described as limited.
- The future of Harare's urban development is a concern, with predictions of a metropolitan population reaching 4–5 million by 2025 sparking worries about unregulated sprawl and infrastructure strain.

# George Eastman

## Overview
George Eastman (1854–1932) was an American entrepreneur and innovator who founded the Eastman Kodak Company and revolutionized photography by making it accessible to the general public through roll film and simple cameras. His innovations and business strategies helped establish the modern film industry, and he became one of the most significant philanthropists of his era, funding major educational and cultural institutions.

- **1932** — Died by suicide on March 14, leaving his entire estate to the University of Rochester.

## Body

### Founding of Kodak and Innovation
While working as a bank clerk, Eastman became interested in photography and developed a machine for coating dry plates in 1879. In 1881, he co-founded the Eastman Dry Plate Company. His key innovation was creating flexible roll film to replace glass plates. In 1888, he patented and released the Kodak camera, which was sold pre-loaded with film for 100 exposures. After use, the entire camera was mailed to Kodak for processing, printing, and reloading. This separation of picture-taking from development made amateur photography accessible to the public for the first time.

### Growth of the Film Industry
Eastman focused on film production as the primary revenue source, supplying affordable film to all camera manufacturers, which turned competitors into customers. He patented the first nitrocellulose film in 1889. Kodak pursued a monopoly through patents and acquisitions, becoming the leading film supplier by 1896 and the largest employer in Rochester by 1915. The company became the sole film supplier to the motion picture industry in 1908. This monopolistic behavior led to a federal antitrust lawsuit filed in 1913, resulting in a 1921 judgment that forced Kodak to stop price-fixing and sell some interests. Innovations continued, including the Brownie camera for children and early color film experiments leading to Kodachrome.

### Personal Life and Later Years
Eastman never married and was close to his mother and sister. After his mother's death in 1907, he became especially close to family friend Josephine Dickman. He was an avid traveler and pianist. In his later years, he was involved in civic reform, supporting Rochester's change to a city manager system in 1925. He also championed the 13-month International Fixed Calendar, funding its promoter and implementing it in Kodak's bookkeeping from 1928 to 1989.

### Philanthropy and Views on Race
Eastman donated $100 million (over $2 billion in 2022 value) during his lifetime, often anonymously as "Mr. Smith." Major gifts established the Eastman School of Music, schools of medicine and dentistry at the University of Rochester, and the Rochester Philharmonic Orchestra. He funded dental clinics in Rochester, London, and Stockholm, and made large donations to MIT, RIT, and historically black universities like Tuskegee and Hampton. However, his views on race were paternalistic; he upheld segregation, and Kodak hired virtually no Black employees during his lifetime. He also donated annually to the American Eugenics Society.

### Infirmity and Death
In his final two years, Eastman suffered intense, debilitating pain from a spinal disorder, which caused depression. On March 14, 1932, he died by suicide, leaving a note: "To my friends, my work is done. Why wait?" His entire estate was bequeathed to the University of Rochester.

## Terms
*   **Roll film:** Flexible photographic film in a roll, which replaced fragile glass plates and enabled simpler cameras.
*   **Nitrocellulose film:** An early type of flexible, transparent film base used for both still photography and motion pictures.
*   **Kodachrome:** A brand name for two different color film processes developed by Kodak; the second, released in 1935, was the first widely marketed integral tripack color film.
*   **Antitrust lawsuit:** A legal action taken by a government to prevent unfair business monopolies and practices.
*   **Profit-sharing:** A system where a company distributes a portion of its profits to its employees.
*   **International Fixed Calendar:** A proposed 13-month calendar where each month has 28 days, designed to simplify scheduling.
*   **Eugenics:** A discredited pseudoscientific belief in improving the genetic quality of the human population through selective breeding and social control.

## Debates and Open questions
*   The exact spinal disorder causing Eastman's final years of pain is unknown; modern speculation includes degenerative disc disease or spinal stenosis.
*   The nature of his long-term, platonic relationship with Josephine Dickman is a subject of biographical interest.
*   His legacy is complex, juxtaposing transformative innovation and massive philanthropy with the reinforcement of racial segregation and support for eugenics.

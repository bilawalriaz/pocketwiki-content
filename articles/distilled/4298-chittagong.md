# Chittagong

## Overview
Chittagong (officially Chattogram) is Bangladesh's second-largest city and its primary seaport, serving as the nation's commercial capital. Its strategic location on the Bay of Bengal has made it a vital trade hub for millennia, shaping its diverse culture and economy. The city's history as a gateway for commerce and conquest has left a lasting legacy on its architecture, demographics, and regional importance.

## Timeline
- **c. 3000 BC** — Neolithic habitation indicated by fossils and tools.
- **2nd century AD** — Port appears on Ptolemy's world map.
- **9th century** — Merchants from the Abbasid Caliphate establish a trading post.
- **1340** — Sultan Fakhruddin Mubarak Shah conquers Chittagong for the Bengal Sultanate.
- **1528** — Portuguese establish the first European colonial enclave in Bengal.
- **1666** — Mughal Empire expels the Portuguese and Arakanese, renaming the city Islamabad.
- **1793** — Nawab of Bengal cedes the port to the British East India Company.
- **1928** — Declared a "Major Port" of British India.
- **1930** — Chittagong armoury raid by Bengali revolutionaries.
- **1947** — Partition makes Chittagong the chief port of East Pakistan.
- **1971** — Site of the Bangladeshi declaration of independence during the Liberation War.
- **2018** — Official name changed to Chattogram by the government.

## Body

### Historical Significance
Chittagong is one of the world's oldest ports with a functional natural harbor. It was a key node on the southern Silk Road and a major medieval maritime trade hub connecting Bengal to China, Southeast Asia, the Middle East, and East Africa. The city saw successive rule by the Bengal Sultanate, the Kingdom of Mrauk U (Arakan), and the Mughal Empire, each leaving architectural and cultural imprints. Under British rule, its port was reorganized and linked by rail to the tea and jute regions of Assam and Bengal. During World War II, it was a critical Allied base for the Burma Campaign. The city was the site of the 1971 declaration of independence that began the Bangladesh Liberation War.

### Economy and Infrastructure
Chittagong is the engine of Bangladesh's economy, contributing 12% of the national GDP, 40% of industrial output, 80% of international trade, and 50% of tax revenue. The Port of Chittagong is the busiest in the country and among the busiest in South Asia. The city hosts major industrial zones, including the Chittagong and Karnaphuli Export Processing Zones, and is home to the headquarters of many of Bangladesh's oldest and largest conglomerates. Key industries include petroleum, steel, shipbuilding, textiles, and pharmaceuticals. The Karnaphuli Tunnel is South Asia's first underwater road tunnel. The city is served by Shah Amanat International Airport and is a major hub for the Bangladesh Railway.

### Culture and Demographics
Chittagong is a historic melting pot, resulting in high religious and ethnic diversity. While Muslims form a great majority, the city has significant Hindu, Buddhist, and Christian communities, as well as indigenous groups like the Chakmas and Baruas. The Chittagonian language, with many Arabic, Persian, and Portuguese loanwords, is considered by many linguists to be a separate language from Bengali. The city is known as the "Land of the Twelve Saints" due to its many Sufi shrines. It is also regarded as the birthplace of Bangladeshi rock music. The 2022 census recorded a city population of over 3.2 million.

## Terms
- **Port of Chittagong**: Bangladesh's busiest seaport and a major South Asian hub for international trade.
- **Chittagonian**: The language spoken in Chittagong, often identified as a nonstandard dialect of Bengali but considered separate by many linguists.
- **Mukti Bahini**: The Bengali guerrilla resistance force during the 1971 Bangladesh Liberation War.
- **Export Processing Zone (EPZ)**: A designated area where goods may be landed, handled, manufactured, or reconfigured and re-exported without the intervention of the customs authorities.
- **Karnaphuli Tunnel**: The first and only underwater road tunnel in South Asia, located in Chittagong.
- **Chittagong Hill Tracts**: A hilly region to the east of the city, home to Bangladesh's highest mountains and diverse indigenous communities.
- **Sufism**: A mystical branch of Islam that played a key role in the historical spread of the religion in the region.
- **Baruas**: Bengali-speaking Theravada Buddhists, one of the oldest communities in Chittagong.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The etymology of "Chittagong" is uncertain, with multiple theories involving Arabic, Arakanese, and Buddhist origins.
- The linguistic status of the Chittagonian language is debated, with some classifying it as a dialect of Bengali and others as a distinct language.
- The 2018 official name change to "Chattogram" was criticized in Bangladeshi media, reflecting ongoing debate about the city's identity.

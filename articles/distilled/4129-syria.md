# Syria

## Overview
Syria is a country in West Asia with a rich history as a crossroads of civilizations, from ancient Ebla and the Umayyad Caliphate to the modern Ba'athist state. Its strategic location has made it a focal point of regional and global conflict, culminating in a devastating civil war that began in 2011 and led to the fall of the Assad regime in 2024. The country is now in a transitional period, facing immense challenges in reconstruction and governance.

## Body

### Ancient and Classical Antiquity
The region of modern Syria is a cradle of civilization. The Natufian culture (c. 11th millennium BC) was among the first sedentary societies. The earliest recorded indigenous civilization was the Kingdom of Ebla (c. 3500 BC). The area was subsequently a battleground for empires including the Hittites, Egyptians, Assyrians, and Babylonians. Following the conquest of Alexander the Great (c. 330 BC), the region became part of the Seleucid Empire, which introduced the Greek name "Syria." It was later a wealthy and strategic province of the Roman and Byzantine Empires, with cities like Palmyra rising to prominence.

### Islamic and Ottoman Periods
Arab Muslim armies conquered Syria by 640. The Umayyad dynasty (661–750) made Damascus the capital of their caliphate, after which the Abbasids moved the capital to Baghdad. Syria was later ruled by various dynasties, including the Ayyubids and Mamluks, before being incorporated into the Ottoman Empire in 1516. Ottoman rule lasted for four centuries, administering the region through a system of religious communities (millets). During World War I, the Ottoman Empire's defeat led to the Sykes-Picot Agreement (1916), which secretly divided the region into British and French spheres of influence.

### French Mandate and Independence
Following the war, France established the Syrian Mandate in 1920, suppressing a short-lived independent kingdom. A major nationalist revolt occurred in 1925. Syria negotiated a treaty of independence in 1936, but it was never ratified. After the Allied occupation of Syria in 1941, persistent nationalist pressure led to the withdrawal of French troops in April 1946, granting full independence.

### Ba'athist Rule and Civil War
Post-independence Syria experienced political instability with multiple coups between 1949 and 1971. The Ba'ath Party seized power in 1963, establishing a one-party state. Hafez al-Assad consolidated power in 1970, creating a hereditary dictatorship centered on his family and the Alawite minority. His son, Bashar al-Assad, succeeded him in 2000. In 2011, protests during the Arab Spring evolved into a multifaceted civil war. The conflict involved numerous factions, including the Free Syrian Army, the Islamic State (which at its peak controlled two-thirds of the country), and foreign powers. The war caused over 600,000 deaths, displaced millions, and devastated the economy.

### Fall of the Assad Regime and Transition
In late 2024, a rebel offensive led by Hay'at Tahrir al-Sham (HTS) rapidly advanced, capturing Damascus on 8 December and ending the Assad family's 53-year rule. A transitional government was formed, and in March 2025, Ahmed al-Sharaa was appointed president for a five-year transitional period. The new government faces the monumental tasks of rebuilding infrastructure, reviving the economy, and navigating complex relations with regional powers like Turkey and Israel.

## Terms
- **Ba'ath Party:** The Arab Socialist Ba'ath Party, a secular, pan-Arab nationalist party that ruled Syria as a one-party state from 1963 to 2024.
- **Millet:** A system used in the Ottoman Empire where each ethno-religious minority community was governed by its own religious leaders under Ottoman sovereignty.
- **Hay'at Tahrir al-Sham (HTS):** An Islamist militant organization that led the 2024 rebel offensive which toppled the Assad regime.
- **Free Syrian Army (FSA):** A loose coalition of rebel groups formed in 2011 to fight the Assad government.
- **Islamic State (ISIS):** A jihadist group that seized large parts of Syria and Iraq during the civil war before being territorially defeated in 2017.
- **Syrian Democratic Forces (SDF):** A Kurdish-led, U.S.-backed coalition that fought ISIS and controlled parts of northeastern Syria.
- **Captagon:** An addictive amphetamine that became Syria's primary illegal export under the Assad regime, generating billions in revenue.
- **Golan Heights:** A strategic plateau captured by Israel from Syria in the 1967 Six-Day War and annexed in 1981, a move not recognized internationally.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The long-term political structure of Syria remains uncertain, with debates over whether a decentralized or centralized system will be adopted.
- The future of Kurdish autonomy in the northeast is a major point of contention, as the SDF has agreed to merge with the new Syrian army but details are unresolved.
- The transitional government's ability to maintain security, prevent sectarian violence, and gain broad international legitimacy is untested.
- The scale of reconstruction needed, estimated in the tens of billions of dollars, presents a profound economic and logistical challenge.

# Run-DMC

## Overview
Run-DMC was an American hip-hop group formed in Hollis, Queens, New York City in 1983 by Joseph "Run" Simmons, Darryl "DMC" McDaniels, and Jason "Jam Master Jay" Mizell. They are widely regarded as the most influential act in hip-hop history, pioneering the "new school" sound that replaced disco-influenced old-school rap with sparse, aggressive beats and rap-rock fusion. The group achieved a historic string of firsts: the first hip-hop act to earn a Gold album (*Run-D.M.C.*, 1984), a Platinum album (*King of Rock*, 1985), and a multi-platinum album (*Raising Hell*, 1986); the first to appear on MTV, *American Bandstand*, the cover of *Rolling Stone*, and at Live Aid; and the first to secure a major endorsement deal (Adidas). Their 1986 collaboration with Aerosmith on "Walk This Way" became a crossover landmark, peaking at No. 4 on the Billboard Hot 100 and revitalizing both careers. Inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 2009 and awarded a Grammy Lifetime Achievement Award in 2016, the group disbanded following Mizell’s 2002 murder, which was solved in 2020 with the conviction of two men.

## Timeline
- **1983** — Group forms in Hollis, Queens; signs with Profile Records; releases debut single "It's Like That/Sucker MCs."
- **1984** — Releases debut album *Run-D.M.C.*; first hip-hop group to achieve Gold certification; "Rock Box" becomes first rap video on MTV.
- **1985** — Releases *King of Rock*; first hip-hop group to go Platinum; appears in film *Krush Groove*; performs at Live Aid.
- **1986** — Releases *Raising Hell* (produced by Rick Rubin); first multi-platinum rap album; "Walk This Way" (feat. Aerosmith) hits No. 4 on Hot 100; signs $1.6M Adidas endorsement. |
- **1988** — Releases *Tougher Than Leather*; stars in eponymous film directed by Rick Rubin.
- **1990** — Releases *Back from Hell*; critical and commercial disappointment; members face severe personal crises (alcoholism, shooting, legal charges).
- **1993** — Releases *Down with the King*; debuts at No. 1 on R&B charts; Simmons becomes ordained minister.
- **2001** — Releases final album *Crown Royal*; McDaniels largely absent due to creative friction with Simmons.

- **2009** — Inducted into Rock and Roll Hall of Fame (second rap act after Grandmaster Flash & the Furious Five).
- **Aug 2020 / Feb 2024** — Ronald Washington and Karl Jordan Jr. arrested (2020) and convicted (2024) for Mizell's murder.
- **Aug 11, 2023** — Simmons and McDaniels perform stated "final performance" as Run-DMC at "Hip Hop 50" concert at Yankee Stadium.

## Body

### Formation and Early Sound (1983–1984)
Joseph Simmons entered hip-hop via his older brother Russell, a promoter who managed Kurtis Blow; Simmons performed as Blow’s DJ ("DJ Run, Son of Kurtis Blow"). Darryl McDaniels, initially focused on athletics, began writing rhymes as "Easy D" after Simmons encouraged him. They met Jason Mizell ("Jazzy Jase"), a popular local DJ with a flashy "d-boy" style, at Two-Fifths Park in the late 1970s. After Russell Simmons initially refused to record the duo, they graduated high school in 1982 and convinced him to let them record with Mizell as their official DJ, now named Jam Master Jay. Russell marketed them as "Run-D.M.C."—a name the group initially hated—combining Simmons's DJ name with letters from McDaniels's name (DMC also stood for "Devastating Mic Controller"). Their 1983 debut single "It's Like That/Sucker MCs" peaked at No. 15 on the R&B charts. The 1984 self-titled debut album introduced a revolutionary sound: sparse, drum-machine-driven beats and aggressive delivery, epitomized by "Rock Box," a groundbreaking fusion of raw hip-hop and hard rock that birthed the rap-rock subgenre.

### Aesthetic Revolution and Mainstream Breakthrough (1984–1986)
Run-DMC deliberately discarded the "old school" glam aesthetic—tight leather, rhinestones, capes—worn by acts like Afrika Bambaataa and Grandmaster Flash & the Furious Five. Adopting Mizell’s street style (Kangol hats, Cazal glasses, leather jackets, unlaced Adidas sneakers), they established the "classic hip-hop stage setup": two MCs front, one DJ on turntables behind. Simmons later explained the laceless shoes originated from prison regulations (inmates couldn't have laces), transforming a restriction into a fashion statement that defined hip-hop style for 25 years. *King of Rock* (1985) deepened the rap-rock fusion; its title track video was the first rap video broadcast on MTV. The group performed at Live Aid and starred in *Krush Groove*, a fictionalization of Russell Simmons’s rise. For *Raising Hell* (1986), producer Rick Rubin pushed for a rock crossover, resulting in "Walk This Way," a full cover collaboration with Aerosmith (Steven Tyler, Joe Perry). The single hit No. 4 on the Hot 100, resurrected Aerosmith’s career, and cemented Run-DMC’s crossover status. "My Adidas" spawned a $1.6 million endorsement deal, described as the beginning of modern hip-hop fashion. *Raising Hell*’s success is credited with kick-starting hip-hop’s "golden age," though the subsequent tour was marred by gang violence, prompting the group to call for a day of peace in Los Angeles.

### Creative Shifts, Personal Crises, and Resurgence (1988–1993)
*Tougher Than Leather* (1988) abandoned rap-rock for a grittier, sample-heavy sound. Though initially viewed as a commercial disappointment compared to its predecessor, it later gained critical reappraisal (Chuck D called it "a spectacular performance against all odds"). The group starred in a Rubin-directed crime caper of the same name, which bombed but reinforced their association with Def Jam (they were managed by Russell Simmons and produced by Rubin, but signed to Profile). *Back from Hell* (1990) was a critical failure; the group chased the "new jack swing" trend (a fusion of hip-hop and contemporary R&B) and adopted preachy lyrics. Personal chaos followed: McDaniels spiraled into alcoholism; Mizell survived a car crash and two gunshot wounds; Simmons faced rape charges in Ohio (later dropped). The members turned to faith—Simmons became a reverend ("Rev Run"), McDaniels joined the church. After a three-year hiatus, *Down with the King* (1993) returned to a gritty sound with subtle religious themes and high-profile guests (Pete Rock, CL Smooth, Q-Tip). It debuted at No. 1 on the R&B chart and went platinum, yielding their last hit single. Mizell found independent success founding JMJ Records and discovering Onyx and 50 Cent.

### Final Album, Friction, and Murder (2001–2002)
Tensions between Simmons and McDaniels defined the *Crown Royal* (2001) sessions. Simmons wanted a return to aggressive rap-rock (mirroring Korn, Limp Bizkit, Kid Rock); McDaniels, influenced by singer-songwriters like John Lennon and Sarah McLachlan, wanted introspection. McDaniels sat out most sessions; Simmons recorded with guests including Kid Rock, Nas, Method Man, and Stephan Jenkins. The album featured only three DMC appearances and drew criticism for his absence. A successful 2001 world tour with Aerosmith revitalized McDaniels, who had developed an inoperable vocal disorder, but Simmons abruptly quit, citing family priorities and fatigue with hip-hop. On October 30, 2002, Mizell was shot and killed in his Queens studio. The murder remained unsolved until August 2020, when Ronald Washington and Karl Jordan Jr. were arrested; they were convicted in February 2024. A third suspect, Jay Bryant, was charged in May 2023 (DNA on a hat found at the scene; witness testimony of a prior fight) and awaits separate trial. Prosecutors revealed Mizell had secretly become a high-volume cocaine dealer in 1996 to cover expenses, and the murder stemmed from a drug dispute, complicating the group’s public anti-drug legacy.

### Post-Breakup Activities
Simmons and McDaniels pursued solo careers: Simmons starred in reality show *Run's House*; McDaniels released solo albums, authored children's books (*Daryl's Dream*, 2022), and created preschool content for Nickelodeon. They reunited sporadically for performances (Made in America Festival 2012, Fun Fun Fun Fest 2012, Atlanta 2013/2014) but recorded no new music. A planned biopic by *Notorious* writer Cheo Hodari Coker never materialized. Their final performance was August 11, 2023, at Yankee Stadium for "Hip Hop 50."

### Legacy
Run-DMC transformed hip-hop musically, aesthetically, and commercially. Musically, they shifted rap from funk/disco grooves ("Rapper's Delight") to sparse, hard-hitting beats, originating **hardcore hip-hop**—a style dominating two decades (Public Enemy, Boogie Down Productions, Wu-Tang Clan, Nas, N.W.A). Their rap-rock fusion influenced Faith No More, Red Hot Chili Peppers, and the late-90s nu-metal wave (Rage Against the Machine, Korn, Linkin Park). Aesthetically, they replaced glam costumes with streetwear (jeans, Adidas, fedoras), establishing the visual template for LL Cool J, Beastie Boys, and generations of rappers. Commercially, they shattered barriers: first rap act on MTV, *Rolling Stone* cover, Live Aid, major arena, major endorsement (Adidas), and Grammy nomination. *Rolling Stone* ranked them No. 48 on "100 Greatest Artists" (2004); MTV/VH1 named them Greatest Hip-Hop Group/Artist (2007); *Raising Hell* entered the National Recording Registry (2018).

## Terms
- ****New-school hip-hop**** — The style pioneered by Run-DMC (mid-80s onward), characterized by drum-machine minimalism, aggressive delivery, and rock influences, replacing the funk/disco-based "old school."
- ****Hardcore hip-hop**** — A subgenre originated by Run-DMC defined by sparse, hard-hitting beats and confrontational lyrics; dominated 1990s–2000s rap (Public Enemy, Wu-Tang Clan, Nas).
- ****Rap-rock fusion**** — The blending of hip-hop vocals with hard rock instrumentation/guitars; Run-DMC's "Rock Box" (1984) and "Walk This Way" (1986) were foundational texts.
- ****Golden age hip-hop**** — Period (mid-80s to mid-90s) of peak lyrical innovation, stylistic diversity, and commercial viability; *Raising Hell* is credited with kick-starting this era.
- ****New jack swing**** — A late-80s/early-90s production style merging hip-hop rhythms with contemporary R&B melodies; Run-DMC’s ill-fated *Back from Hell* (1990) attempted this sound.
- ****Jam Master Jay (Jason Mizell)**** — Run-DMC’s DJ; credited with crafting the group’s sonic identity ("created almost every sound"), discovering 50 Cent, and founding JMJ Records. Murdered 2002.
- ****Russell Simmons**** — Older brother of Joseph Simmons; hip-hop promoter, manager of Kurtis Blow and Run-DMC, co-founder of Def Jam Recordings.
- ****Rick Rubin**** — Producer of *Raising Hell* and *Tougher Than Leather*; co-founder of Def Jam; pushed for the Aerosmith collaboration on "Walk This Way."
- ****Adidas endorsement**** — $1.6 million deal (1986) sparked by "My Adidas"; cited as the birth of hip-hop fashion as a commercial industry. |
- ****Profile Records**** — The independent label Run-DMC was signed to for their entire career (not Def Jam, a common misconception).

## Debates and open questions
- **Jam Master Jay’s drug involvement vs. public image:** Prosecutors in the 2020–2024 trials alleged Mizell became a high-kilo cocaine dealer in 1996 to fund expenses, and his murder resulted from a drug dispute. This contradicts the group’s longstanding, public anti-drug stance (PSAs, lyrics in "It's Tricky"). His family maintains he did not use drugs.
- **Creative direction conflict (Simmons vs. McDaniels):** The friction during *Crown Royal* (2001) reflected a fundamental split: Simmons favored the group’s signature aggressive rap-rock; McDaniels sought introspective, singer-songwriter-inspired material. This tension arguably prevented further group recordings after 2001.
- **Def Jam association misconception:** Despite being managed by Russell Simmons, produced by Rick Rubin, and touring with Def Jam acts, Run-DMC remained signed to Profile Records throughout their career. The source notes this is a "common misconception."
- **Biopic development:** A film written by Cheo Hodari Coker was announced in 2009 but never entered production; reasons are unspecified in the source.

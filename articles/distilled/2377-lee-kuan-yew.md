# Lee Kuan Yew

## Overview
Lee Kuan Yew (1923–2015) was the first Prime Minister of Singapore, serving from 1959 to 1990. He is the founding father of modern Singapore, credited with transforming it from a developing port city into a highly developed, high-income economy. His governance was characterized by pragmatic economic policies, strict social controls, and an emphasis on meritocracy and multiracialism, often described as an "illiberal democracy" or "nanny state."

## Timeline
- **16 Sep 1923** — Born in Singapore to a Peranakan Chinese family.
- **1950** — Called to the bar at the Middle Temple; returned to Singapore to practice law.
- **21 Nov 1954** — Co-founded the People's Action Party (PAP).
- **2 Apr 1955** — Won his first elected seat in the Tanjong Pagar division.
- **5 Jun 1959** — Sworn in as Singapore's first Prime Minister after PAP's electoral victory.
- **16 Sep 1963** — Singapore officially became part of Malaysia.
- **9 Aug 1965** — Singapore was expelled from Malaysia, leading to its independence.
- **28 Nov 1990** — Stepped down as Prime Minister, succeeded by Goh Chok Tong.
- **23 Mar 2015** — Died of pneumonia at age 91.

## Body

### Early Life and Political Rise
Born during British colonial rule, Lee studied law at Cambridge University. The Japanese occupation of Singapore (1942–1945) profoundly shaped his views on power and self-governance. After the war, he returned to Singapore, practiced law, and became involved in anti-colonial and labor movements. In 1954, he co-founded the PAP, which won seats in the 1955 election, making him the de facto opposition leader. He led the PAP to victory in 1959, becoming Prime Minister.

### Struggle for Independence and Merger
Lee believed Singapore's survival required merger with Malaya. He led negotiations that resulted in Singapore joining the Malaysian federation in 1963. However, ideological differences and racial tensions led to Singapore's expulsion in 1965. Lee famously broke the news of independence in a televised press conference, expressing deep anguish over the separation.

### Governance and Economic Transformation
As Prime Minister, Lee oversaw major economic reforms, attracting foreign investment and establishing Singapore as a global financial and manufacturing hub. His government implemented strict anti-corruption measures, promoted public housing through the Housing and Development Board (HDB), and launched the "Garden City" initiative. Social policies included campaigns for family planning and later, incentives for graduate mothers to have more children. His administration was known for restricting press freedoms, public assembly, and using defamation laws against political opponents.

### Foreign Policy and Later Years
Lee cultivated strong relationships with world leaders, including those from the US, UK, China, and Indonesia. He was a key proponent of ASEAN. After stepping down as Prime Minister in 1990, he served as Senior Minister (1990–2004) and Minister Mentor (2004–2011), remaining influential in Singapore's governance until his retirement.

## Terms
- **Peranakan Chinese**: A distinct ethnic group in Southeast Asia descended from Chinese immigrants who married local Malays, often adopting Malay language and customs while retaining Chinese cultural elements.
- **People's Action Party (PAP)**: The political party co-founded by Lee Kuan Yew in 1954, which has governed Singapore continuously since 1959.
- **Merdeka**: A Malay word meaning "independence," used in the context of Singapore's negotiations for self-governance from Britain.
- **Operation Coldstore**: A 1963 security operation in which over 100 left-wing politicians and activists, including Lim Chin Siong, were detained without trial.
- **Asian Values**: A concept promoted by Lee, emphasizing communitarianism, respect for authority, and prioritizing social harmony and economic development over individual political rights.
- **Housing and Development Board (HDB)**: A statutory board responsible for Singapore's public housing program, which houses the majority of the population.
- **Garden City**: An urban planning vision initiated by Lee to transform Singapore into a city with abundant greenery and clean environment.
- **National Service (NS)**: Compulsory military conscription for all able-bodied male Singaporean citizens, introduced in 1967.
- **Corrupt Practices Investigation Bureau (CPIB)**: An independent agency established to combat corruption in Singapore, given significant powers under Lee's government.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Authoritarianism vs. Pragmatism**: Scholars debate whether Lee's restrictions on civil liberties and political opposition were necessary for Singapore's stability and economic growth, or an overreach that stifled democratic development.
- **Legacy of "Asian Values"**: The concept is contested as either a valid cultural framework for development or a justification for authoritarian rule used to suppress dissent.
- **Eugenics and Social Engineering**: Lee's policies encouraging educated women to have more children and offering sterilization grants to lower-income women are criticized as discriminatory and based on questionable genetic assumptions.
- **Use of Defamation Laws**: The practice of suing political opponents and foreign media for defamation is seen by critics as a tool to silence dissent, while supporters argue it protects reputations and maintains political standards.

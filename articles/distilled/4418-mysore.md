# Mysore

## Overview
Mysore (officially Mysuru) is a historic city in Karnataka, India, famed as the traditional seat of the Wadiyar dynasty and the capital of the Kingdom of Mysore for nearly six centuries. It is renowned for its palaces, cultural heritage, and the annual Dasara festival, earning it titles like the "City of Palaces" and the "Cultural capital of Karnataka." The city's development has been shaped by royal patronage, colonial rule, and modern economic growth in tourism and information technology.

## Timeline
- **1524** — Mahishūru Fort constructed by Chamaraja Wodeyar III.
- **1610** — Dasara festivities first introduced by King Raja Wodeyar I; Seringapatam becomes the kingdom's capital.
- **1799** — Death of Tipu Sultan in the Fourth Anglo-Mysore War; capital moved back to Mysore.
- **1831** — British take direct control of Mysore; capital shifted to Bangalore.
- **1881** — Maharaja Chamaraja Wadiyar X given control (rendition); Mysore regains capital status.
- **1888** — Mysore municipality established.
- **1903** — City Improvement Trust Board (CITB) established, initiating planned urban development.
- **1916** — University of Mysore founded, the first in Karnataka.
- **1947** — Indian independence; Mysore remains a princely state until integration.
- **1977** — Mysore municipality converted into a city corporation.
- **2014** — City officially renamed from Mysore to Mysuru.

## Body

### Etymology and History
The name Mysore derives from "Mahiṣūru," meaning "abode of Mahiṣa," referring to the demon Mahishasura from Hindu mythology. The city's recorded history begins with the construction of a fort in 1524. It was the capital of the Kingdom of Mysore, ruled by the Wadiyar dynasty, which expanded significantly in the 17th century. The kingdom reached its peak under the de facto rulers Hyder Ali and Tipu Sultan in the 18th century, engaging in four Anglo-Mysore Wars with the British. After Tipu Sultan's defeat in 1799, the Wadiyar dynasty was restored under British suzerainty. The British directly administered Mysore from 1831 to 1881, during which the capital was moved to Bangalore. Mysore was returned to the Wadiyars in 1881 and remained a princely state until Indian independence in 1947.

### Geography and Climate
Mysore is located at the foothills of the Chamundi Hills, at an altitude of 770 m (2,530 ft). It lies between the Kaveri and Kabini rivers. The city has a tropical savanna climate with distinct summer, monsoon, and winter seasons. Recorded temperatures range from a high of 39.9 °C (104 °F) to a low of 7.7 °C (46 °F). Average annual rainfall is 798.6 mm (31.44 in).

### Demographics and Administration
As of the 2011 Census, Mysore city had a population of 920,550, with a literacy rate of 86.84%. Kannada is the predominant language. The city is administered by the Mysore City Corporation, established in 1888. It is divided into 65 wards, and citizens elect representatives to the state legislature and national parliament. The city is known for its cleanliness and has won national awards for municipal governance.

### Economy
Tourism and information technology are major industries. Mysore is a hub for traditional industries like sandalwood carving, silk weaving (Mysore silk), and incense production. It hosts large IT companies and central government research institutes. The city is part of the Bidar-Mysore Industrial Corridor and has several industrial areas on its outskirts.

### Culture and Tourism
Mysore is celebrated for its cultural heritage, epitomized by the ten-day Dasara festival, which features a grand procession. The city is home to numerous palaces, most famously the Amba Vilas (Mysore Palace). It is the origin of Mysore painting, Mysore pak (a sweet), and Mysore peta (a traditional turban). Key tourist attractions include the Mysore Zoo, Chamundeshwari Temple, and nearby Brindavan Gardens. The city is also a center for yoga and wellness tourism.

### Transport and Education
Mysore is connected by national highways and a railway junction with lines to Bangalore, Mangaluru, and Chamarajanagar. It has a domestic airport. The city has a long history of modern education, starting with schools in the 19th century and the establishment of the University of Mysore in 1916. It hosts several national research and educational institutes.

## Terms
- **Wadiyar dynasty:** The Hindu royal dynasty that ruled the Kingdom of Mysore for centuries.
- **Dasara:** A ten-day annual festival in Mysore celebrating the victory of good over evil, culminating in a grand procession.
- **Anglo-Mysore Wars:** A series of four conflicts in the late 18th century between the Kingdom of Mysore and the British East India Company.
- **Rendition:** The 1881 act of restoring administrative control of Mysore to the Wadiyar dynasty after a period of direct British rule.
- **Princely state:** A semi-autonomous region in British India under the suzerainty of the British Crown, ruled by a local monarch.
- **Mysore Palace (Amba Vilas):** The principal residence of the Wadiyar dynasty, known for its Indo-Saracenic architecture.
- **Mysore silk:** A famous type of sari made from pure silk and gold zari, originating in Mysore.
- **Mysore pak:** A traditional sweet delicacy from Karnataka, originating in the Mysore Palace kitchen.
- **Mysore peta:** A traditional silk turban worn by men in ceremonial contexts.

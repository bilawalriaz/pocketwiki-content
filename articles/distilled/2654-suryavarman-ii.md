# Suryavarman II

## Overview
Suryavarman II (r. 1113–1150) was a powerful Khmer king who reunified the empire and is most famous for commissioning Angkor Wat, the world's largest Hindu temple. His reign was marked by ambitious military campaigns, diplomatic outreach, and a flourishing of monumental architecture, though his eastern wars were largely unsuccessful. He is considered one of the Khmer Empire's greatest rulers.

## Timeline
- **c. 1094/1098** — Suryavarman II is born into a provincial estate during a period of weakening central control.
- **1113** — Enthroned after defeating rivals; formal coronation follows in 1119.
- **1114** — Sends a diplomatic mission to the Chola dynasty in South India.
- **1116** — Resumes formal diplomatic relations with China.
- **1128** — Leads a major, unsuccessful invasion of Đại Việt (Vietnam) with 20,000 soldiers.
- **1132** — Launches another combined Khmer-Cham invasion of Đại Việt.
- **1145** — Invades and sacks the Cham capital of Vijaya, installing a new king.
- **c. 1150** — Dies, possibly during a military campaign; succeeded by his cousin Dharanindravarman II.

## Body

### Rise to Power and Coronation
Suryavarman II grew up during a time of weak central authority. He claimed a legitimate right to the throne and maneuvered against rivals, including a southern claimant and his own great-uncle, King Dharanindravarman I. Inscriptions describe a violent battle where Suryavarman killed an enemy king on an elephant. He was first enthroned in 1113 and formally crowned in 1119, with the aged Brahmin sage Divakarapandita officiating both ceremonies.

### Military Campaigns and Diplomacy
Suryavarman II reunited the empire and received tribute from vassals. His major military efforts were directed east against Đại Việt and Champa, but they were largely unsuccessful. He led several large-scale invasions of Đại Việt between 1128 and 1137, all of which failed. In 1145, he successfully invaded Champa, sacked its capital, and placed a relative on its throne, but Cham forces soon recaptured it. A final planned invasion in 1150 was aborted due to disease among the troops. Diplomatically, he sent embassies to the Chola dynasty and China, where he was recognized as a "great vassal of the empire."

### Architecture and Religious Patronage
Suryavarman II's reign saw a major architectural innovation, possibly influenced by the Cholas. His greatest project was Angkor Wat, a vast temple complex dedicated to Vishnu, whose five towers represent Mount Meru. The temple is adorned with over 1,860 carved apsaras (heavenly nymphs) and extensive bas-reliefs. Other temples from his era include Banteay Samre and Beng Mealea. Unusually for a Khmer king, he focused court religious life on Vishnu rather than Shiva. He was the first Khmer king to be depicted in art, shown in a detailed bas-relief at Angkor Wat in a serene, regal pose, and later in a military scene with war elephants.

### Death and Legacy
Suryavarman II died around 1150, possibly during a campaign against Champa. He was succeeded by his cousin, leading to a period of instability. He was posthumously named Paramavishnuloka ("He Who Has Entered the Heavenly World of Vishnu"). Angkor Wat was completed after his death, and his tomb was placed within the complex. His image continues to be a symbol of Khmer heritage.

## Terms
- **Khmer Empire**: The powerful Indianized state in Southeast Asia that Suryavarman II ruled.
- **Angkor Wat**: The largest Hindu temple in the world, built by Suryavarman II and dedicated to Vishnu.
- **Vishnu**: A principal Hindu deity; Suryavarman II unusually made him the focus of court worship over Shiva.
- **Champa**: An Indianized kingdom in present-day central Vietnam, a frequent rival and target of Khmer military campaigns.
- **Đại Việt**: A Vietnamese precursor state that successfully resisted Khmer invasions.
- **Bas-relief**: A type of sculpture where figures are carved to project from a flat background, as seen at Angkor Wat.
- **Apsara**: A female spirit of the clouds and waters in Hindu and Buddhist culture, depicted as a celestial dancer.
- **Mount Meru**: A sacred five-peaked mountain in Hindu cosmology, symbolized by the central towers of Angkor Wat.
- **Paramavishnuloka**: Suryavarman II's posthumous name, meaning "He Who Has Entered the Heavenly World of Vishnu."

## Debates and Open Questions
- Scholars disagree on whether an inscription describing Suryavarman killing an enemy king on an elephant refers to the death of a southern rival or of his great-uncle, King Dharanindravarman I.
- The reason Suryavarman II chose to focus court religious life on Vishnu, rather than the more traditional Shiva, is unknown.
- There is debate over whether his association with Vishnu explains why Angkor Wat faces west (Vishnu's direction) instead of the more common eastward orientation for Khmer temples.

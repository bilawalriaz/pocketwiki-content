# Pierre-Joseph Proudhon

## Overview
Pierre-Joseph Proudhon (1809–1865) was a French philosopher, economist, and the first person to self-identify as an "anarchist," earning recognition as the "father of anarchism." Born into poverty in Besançon, he rose from a printer's apprenticeship to become a leading socialist theorist and a member of the French Parliament after the 1848 Revolution. His seminal work, *What Is Property?* (1840), introduced the famous declaration "property is theft," distinguishing between exploitative capitalist property (propriété) and legitimate possession based on use. Proudhon developed **mutualism**, a socio-economic system advocating for a stateless society organized as a federation of free communes and worker associations, where credit is free, exchange is equitable, and the means of production are controlled by those who use them. He opposed both capitalism and state socialism (authoritarian communism), arguing for peaceful revolution via monetary reform and dual power institutions. His intellectual rivalry with Karl Marx—sparked by Marx’s *The Poverty of Philosophy* (1847) critiquing Proudhon’s *The Philosophy of Poverty*—cemented the historic split between anarchist and Marxist wings of the socialist movement. Proudhon’s legacy is complicated by posthumously published notebooks revealing virulent antisemitism and anti-feminism, which scholars debate as either contradictions to or extensions of his authoritarian tendencies in later life.

## Body

### Early Life and Intellectual Formation
Proudhon was born in Besançon to a brewer/cooper father and a mother who taught him to read. Denied formal education by poverty, he entered a printing apprenticeship in 1827. The trade proved formative: proofreading ecclesiastical works led him to reject Christianity, while printing Charles Fourier’s *Le Nouveau Monde Industriel et Sociétaire* (1829) introduced him to socialist ideas. His friendship with Gustave Fallot, a wealthy scholar, granted him access to Enlightenment authors (Rousseau, Voltaire, Diderot) and financial support to study philosophy in Paris (1830–1831). Though uncomfortable in metropolitan salons, this period solidified his autodidactic resolve. After a failed printing venture (1838), he won the Suard Pension, enabling full-time scholarship.

### The "Property is Theft" Breakthrough and Marxist Rupture
Proudhon’s 1840 debut, *What Is Property?*, used a dialectical method to reject both private property (capitalist appropriation) and state communism, synthesizing them into "liberty" defined as **possession**—use-based ownership of land and tools. The work drew Karl Marx’s correspondence and initial praise (*The Holy Family* called it a "scientific manifesto of the French proletariat"). However, Marx’s 1847 *The Poverty of Philosophy*, a book-length rebuttal of Proudhon’s *The Philosophy of Poverty*, accused Proudhon of misunderstanding labor-value theory and remaining trapped in bourgeois ethics. This dispute became the theoretical fountainhead of the split between anarchists (later Bakuninists) and Marxists in the International Working Men's Association.

### Mutualism: Federalism, Workers' Associations, and Free Credit
Proudhon’s mature system, **mutualism**, envisioned a stateless "agro-industrial federation" of free communes. Key pillars included:
*   **Workers' Self-Management (Autogestion):** Factories and large workplaces run by democratic "labor associations"; peasants and artisans retain individual possession. "Every worker employed in the association [must have] an undivided share in the property of the company."
*   **Socialization of Means of Production:** Land and capital are "collective property" (inalienable), while products of labor remain individual property. He demanded mines, canals, and railways be handed to worker associations.
*   **The People's Bank:** A national credit institution funded by a tax on capital, issuing interest-free loans to workers' cooperatives to break the cycle of debt and wage slavery. He attempted this in 1848 as a deputy; the income tax failed, and the bank collapsed.
*   **Equitable Exchange:** Commerce based on labor-time equivalence, eliminating profit, rent, and interest—not by law, but by the "universalization of the principle of reciprocity."

### Evolution of Political Strategy: From Anti-State to Federalism
Proudhon’s stance on the state shifted. In *The General Idea of the Revolution* (1851), he urged a "society without authority," defining government as a machinery of oppression. By *The Principle of Federation* (1863), he argued the state could not be immediately abolished; instead, **federalism**—a decentralized "balancing of authority by liberty"—would counter state power. He redefined anarchy as "the government of each by himself," where political functions reduce to industrial contracts. In the posthumous *Theory of Property*, he controversially argued "property is the only power that can act as a counterweight to the State," legitimizing widespread, egalitarian small-scale property as a bulwark against centralization.

### Revolution, Socialism, and the 1848 Experience
Proudhon identified as a revolutionary socialist but rejected violent insurrection. He viewed revolution as a moral transformation of society via dual power: building alternative institutions (clubs, credit banks, associations) to supplant the state. In the 1848 Constituent Assembly, he criticized the National Workshops as charity and the June Days Uprising as premature violence, famously declaring the February Revolution "without an idea." His parliamentary tenure reinforced his federalist pivot: he proposed militia service over conscription and a centralization "in which no one obeys, no one is dependent."

### Controversial Positions: Antisemitism, Anti-Feminism, and Proto-Fascism Claims
Proudhon’s legacy is heavily contested.
*   **Antisemitism:** Private notebooks (*Carnets*, pub. 1960s) reveal "paranoid feelings of hatred," including a draft 1847 article calling Jews "the enemy of the human race" demanding expulsion or extermination. Scholars (Graham, McKay) agree this was personal bigotry, not part of his revolutionary programme, which explicitly rejected nationality and race.
*   **Anti-Feminism:** *La Pornocratie* (posth. 1875) and notebooks frame women as "courtesan or housekeeper," justifying patriarchal mastery via male physical strength. Contemporary anarchist Joseph Déjacque denounced this as a contradiction of anarchist principles.
*   **Proto-Fascism:** Right-wing groups (Cercle Proudhon, Action Française) claimed him for his anti-republicanism, nationalism, and monarchy praise. Historians (Zeev Sternhell) cite this lineage; others (Vincent, McKay, Krier) reject it, noting his federalism, anti-militarism, and explicit rejection of nationalism ("citizenship is everywhere an acquired right") as incompatible with fascism.

## Terms
- ****Mutualism**** — Proudhon’s anarchist socialism: a stateless federation of free communes and worker associations using interest-free credit and equitable exchange (labor-for-labor).
- ****Possession (Possession)**** — Legitimate, use-based ownership (dwelling, tools, land under cultivation); distinct from *propriété*. "Possession is a matter of fact, not of right."
- ****Propriété (Private Property)**** — Capitalist/landlord property: exclusive legal title enabling rent, interest, and profit without labor; backed by state force. "Property is theft."
- ****Agro-Industrial Federation**** — Proudhon’s 1863 term for the socio-economic structure replacing the state: decentralized federations of agricultural and industrial worker associations.
- ****People's Bank (Banque du Peuple)**** — Proposed national credit union funded by capital tax, issuing interest-free loans to cooperatives to abolish wage labor and usury.
- ****Dual Power**** — Concept (later named by Lenin) of building revolutionary counter-institutions (clubs, associations) alongside the state to prepare social agenda.
- ****Autogestion (Workers' Self-Management)**** — Democratic control of enterprises by workers; Proudhon is a primary 19th-century theorist of this practice.
- ****Federalism**** — Political decentralization via free contract between sovereign communes/associations; Proudhon’s later mechanism for "balancing authority by liberty."
- ****Collective Force**** — The productive power arising from association/cooperation, which Proudhon argued is appropriated by capitalists as "surplus value."
- ****Reciprocity**** — The ethical-economic principle of equivalent exchange (labor for labor), replacing exploitation; the "universalization" of which renders interest/rent obsolete.

## Debates and open questions
*   **Continuity vs. Rupture in Proudhon’s Thought:** Does the 1863 *Principle of Federation* (advocating federal government, property as state counterweight) betray his 1840 anti-statism, or adapt it to practical realities? Scholars (Guérin vs. McKay) dispute whether this is a "conservative turn" or a tactical shift.
*   **Proudhon vs. Marx on Value Theory:** Marx claimed Proudhon misunderstood the labor theory of value and sought to realize "bourgeois equality" rather than abolish the value-form. Anarchist scholars (Rocker, McKay) counter that Proudhon anticipated surplus value theory and that Marx distorted his critique of property.
*   **Status of "Property is Freedom":** In *Theory of Property*, Proudhon redefines property as a liberty-guaranteeing bulwark against the state. Is this a dialectical synthesis of "theft" and "possession," a pragmatic concession, or a theoretical contradiction?
*   **Bigotry vs. Theory:** To what extent do Proudhon’s antisemitic and anti-feminist writings (largely private) invalidate his libertarian framework? Most scholars (Graham, McKay, Gemie) argue they are "asides" contradicting his core egalitarianism, but feminists (Nochlin) and critics (Schapiro) view them as structural to his patriarchal "justice."
*   **Classification of Mutualism:** Is mutualism "individualist anarchism" (market-based, possession-focused) or "social anarchism" (federated, collective means of production)? The source notes it is widely considered a synthesis or "middle way" between the two.

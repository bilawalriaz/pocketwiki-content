# Francis of Assisi

## Overview  
Francis of Assisi (c. 1181–1226) was an Italian mystic and Catholic friar who founded the Franciscan Order, renouncing wealth to live in radical poverty and preach the Gospels. His emphasis on simplicity, devotion to Christ’s suffering, and reverence for nature made him one of Christianity’s most venerated saints, influencing theology, art, and environmental ethics across centuries.

## Timeline

- **1202** — Captured at Collestrada during war with Perugia; begins spiritual re-evaluation
- **1205** — Vision at San Damiano chapel; hears Christ call to rebuild His church
- **1206** — Renounces inheritance before Bishop of Assisi; begins life as a beggar
- **Feb 1208** — Receives calling to live the apostolic life; begins preaching and gathering followers
- **Apr 16, 1210** — Pope Innocent III approves the Franciscan Order (per tradition)
- **Mar 28, 1212** — Clare of Assisi joins Francis on Palm Sunday; establishes the Poor Clares
- **1219** — Travels to Egypt during the Fifth Crusade; meets Sultan Al-Kamil
- **Sep 17, 1224** — Receives stigmata on Mount La Verna
- **Oct 3, 1226** — Dies in Assisi on Saturday evening, singing Psalm 141 ("Voce mea ad Dominum")
- **Jul 16, 1228** — Canonized by Pope Gregory IX

## Body  

### Early Life and Conversion  
Born Giovanni di Pietro di Bernardone, Francis was the son of a prosperous silk merchant and a noblewoman from Provence. Indulged in youth, he embraced a life of revelry, fine clothes, and troubadour culture. A turning point came around 1202 when he was captured in battle and held prisoner for a year, prompting introspection. Returning to Assisi, he briefly resumed worldly pleasures before a mystical vision led him to renounce his father’s wealth and embrace radical poverty.

### Founding the Franciscan Orders  
In 1208, while attending Mass, Francis heard the Gospel commissioning the Twelve Apostles and felt called to imitate Christ’s poverty. He adopted the habit of a peasant—brown tunic tied with a rope—and began preaching penance. His message attracted followers, and by 1210, Pope Innocent III approved the Order of Friars Minor (OFM). Francis also founded the Order of Poor Clares for women, led by Clare of Assisi, and later established the Third Order for laypeople seeking to live Franciscan values without monastic vows.

### Missionary Work and the Fifth Crusade  
In 1219, during the Fifth Crusade, Francis journeyed to Egypt hoping to convert Sultan Al-Kamil or attain martyrdom. Though no Arab sources record the meeting, Christian tradition holds that the sultan received him graciously. Francis returned unharmed and continued promoting peace and missionary work. The Franciscans maintained a presence in the Holy Land thereafter, becoming known as Custodians of the Holy Land.

### Stigmata, Final Days, and Canonization  
In September 1224, while fasting on Mount La Verna, Francis experienced a vision of a seraph and received the stigmata—wounds mirroring Christ’s crucifixion. Suffering from trachoma and likely malaria, he was cared for in various cities before returning to a hut near the Porziuncola. He died on October 3, 1226, dictating his spiritual testament. Pope Gregory IX canonized him just two years later, on July 16, 1228.

### Character, Legacy, and Writings  
Francis saw all creation as reflecting God’s glory, calling animals and elements his “brothers” and “sisters.” He composed the *Canticle of the Sun*, praising divine beauty in nature. His devotion to the Eucharist and service to lepers shaped his legacy. He is credited with instituting the first nativity scene in Greccio (1223). Modern popes have honored him as patron of ecology, especially through Pope Francis’ encyclical *Laudato si’*. His feast day, October 4, includes blessings of animals worldwide.

## Terms  
- **Stigmata**: Supernatural wounds corresponding to Christ’s crucifixion marks.  
- **Evangelical Counsels**: Vows of poverty, chastity, and obedience practiced by religious orders.  
- **Poor Clares**: Female branch of the Franciscan Order founded by Clare of Assisi.  
- **Third Order**: Lay branch of the Franciscans observing vows within secular life.  
- **Porziuncola**: Small chapel near Assisi that became central to the Franciscan Order.  
- **Canticle of the Sun**: Poetic hymn by Francis celebrating creation’s praise of God.  
- **Custody of the Holy Land**: Role granted to Franciscans to care for Christian holy sites.  
- **Regula non bullata**: The "First Rule" or "Rule Without a Papal Bull" composed by Francis in 1221, never officially endorsed by the pope.  
- **Regula Bullata**: The "Second Rule" or "Rule With a Bull" approved by Pope Honorius III on 29 November 1223, becoming the official rule of the Franciscan Order.  
- **Laudato si’**: 2015 papal encyclical on environmental stewardship, named after Francis’ canticle.  
- **World Animal Day**: Annual celebration on October 4 honoring Francis’ love for animals.

## Debates and Open Questions  
- **Stigmata authenticity**: Some scholars suggest the wounds may have been purpura from malaria rather than supernatural.  
- **Egyptian encounter**: No contemporary Arab records confirm Francis’ meeting with Sultan Al-Kamil; later accounts may embellish the event.  
- **Dietary habits**: Claims that Francis was vegetarian are disputed; early biographies mention he ate meat, including shrimp pie.  
- **Naked renunciation**: Whether Francis literally stripped naked before the bishop remains debated among historians.  
- **Orthodox recognition**: Francis is not officially canonized by any Orthodox Church, and some Orthodox theologians criticize hagiographic portrayals as heretical.

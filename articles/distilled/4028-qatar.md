# Qatar

## Overview
Qatar is a small, wealthy nation on the Arabian Peninsula, ruled by the Al Thani dynasty since 1825. Its modern identity is defined by its vast natural gas reserves, which have fueled rapid economic growth and geopolitical influence, while its political system remains an autocratic monarchy with significant restrictions on civil liberties.

## Body
### History and Politics
Qatar's history includes periods of rule by the Ottomans and British. The Al Thani family has ruled since 1825, with the current emir, Tamim bin Hamad Al Thani, holding nearly all executive, legislative, and judicial authority. The country is officially a constitutional monarchy but functions as an autocracy. A 2005 constitution provided for a partially elected Shura Council, but after a single election in 2021, the system reverted to a fully appointed body in 2024.

### Economy and Energy
Qatar's economy was transformed by the discovery of oil in 1939 and, more significantly, the development of the North Field, the world's third-largest natural gas reserve. It is a leading exporter of liquefied natural gas (LNG) and has one of the world's highest GDPs per capita. The government has invested heavily in infrastructure and sovereign wealth funds, while pursuing economic diversification through its Qatar National Vision 2030.

### Foreign Relations
Qatar pursues an independent foreign policy, acting as a mediator in regional conflicts and maintaining ties with diverse parties, including the US, Iran, and various political movements. It is a founding member of the Gulf Cooperation Council (GCC) and was a member of OPEC until 2019. A major diplomatic crisis with several neighboring states began in 2017 and was resolved in 2021.

### Society and Human Rights
The population is overwhelmingly composed of foreign expatriates and migrant workers. Islam is the state religion. Qatar's human rights record is criticized for restrictions on freedom of expression, press, and assembly. The kafala system, which ties workers to their employers, has been a major point of contention, though reforms have been announced. Women face legal discrimination under a male guardianship system, and LGBTQ+ rights are severely restricted.

## Terms
- **Al Thani**: The ruling dynasty of Qatar since 1825.
- **Kafala system**: A sponsorship system that ties migrant workers to their employers, limiting their labor mobility and rights.
- **Sharia**: Islamic law, which serves as the main source of Qatari legislation.
- **Shura Council**: The consultative assembly of Qatar, whose members are appointed by the emir.
- **Liquefied Natural Gas (LNG)**: Natural gas that has been cooled to a liquid state for easier storage and transport; Qatar is a leading global exporter.
- **North Field**: The world's largest natural gas field, located offshore in the Persian Gulf, shared with Iran.
- **Gulf Cooperation Council (GCC)**: A regional intergovernmental political and economic union of Arab states bordering the Persian Gulf.

## Debates and Open Questions
Scholars debate the sustainability of Qatar's economic model and its long-term geopolitical strategy. There is ongoing scrutiny of the effectiveness of labor reforms and the gap between announced changes and the lived reality for migrant workers. The extent to which Qatar's hosting of major events like the World Cup has led to lasting social or political liberalization is also contested.

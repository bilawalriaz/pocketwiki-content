# Funj Sultanate

## Overview
The Funj Sultanate (c. 1504–1821) was an Islamic kingdom in the Nile Valley, succeeding the medieval Christian Nubian states. It played a crucial role in spreading Islam and Arabic culture in the region, shaping the foundations of modern Sudanese society, before its decline and conquest by the Turco-Egyptian Empire.

## Timeline
- **c. 1504** — Sultan Amara Dunqas founds the Funj Sultanate, with its capital at Sennar.
- **1525** — Ottoman admiral Selman Reis notes the kingdom's weakness and its tribute to Ethiopia.
- **1585** — The Funj defeat the Ottomans at the Battle of Hannik, setting a border.
- **1603/1604** — Abdallabi Sheikh Ajib I revolts, temporarily seizing control of the kingdom.
- **1618–1619** — A full-scale war with the Ethiopian Empire devastates Funj eastern provinces.
- **Mid-17th century** — The Sultanate reaches its peak, expanding into Kordofan and the Fazughli gold region.
- **1762** — Hamaj general Muhammad Abu Likaylik seizes Sennar, establishing the Hamaj Regency.
- **1785/1786** — The Fur Sultanate conquers Kordofan from the Funj.
- **1821** — The last Funj sultan, Badi VII, surrenders to the Turco-Egyptian conquest.

## Body

### Origins and Founding
The Funj Sultanate emerged from the decline of the Christian Nubian kingdoms of Makuria and Alodia. By the 15th century, the region was overrun by Bedouin tribes who introduced Islam. The Funj, a nomadic-pastoralist people of disputed ethnic origin, conquered the remnants of these states. In 1504, their leader Amara Dunqas established the sultanate with its capital at Sennar, quickly adopting Islam to rule over its diverse population.

### Conflict and Expansion (16th–17th Centuries)
The early sultanate faced external threats, notably from the Ottomans, who were halted at the Third Cataract of the Nile after the Battle of Hannik in 1585. Internally, the powerful Abdallabi sheikh Ajib I revolted, temporarily pushing the Funj south before being defeated. The 17th century was the sultanate's peak, marked by prosperity, urbanization at Sennar, and territorial expansion. It defeated the Kingdom of Taqali, conquered the gold-rich Fazughli region, and fought a major war with Ethiopia in 1618–1619, after which relations stabilized for over a century.

### Decline and Fall (18th Century–1821)
The 18th century saw the erosion of royal power due to internal strife, the rise of a merchant class, and the growing influence of Islamic scholars (Ulama). A pivotal moment came in 1762 when the Hamaj, a non-Arabized group, seized power in a coup, reducing the Funj sultans to puppets. The state then rapidly disintegrated, losing provinces like Kordofan to the Fur Sultanate and Dongola to the Shaykiyya. By 1800, the sultanate's authority was limited to the Gezira region. In 1821, the last sultan surrendered to the invading forces of Muhammad Ali of Egypt.

### Government and Society
The Funj state was a feudal monarchy where the sultan's power was checked by a council of elders. Succession was matrilineal, with the sultan chosen from sons of previous rulers and Funj noblewomen. Nobles gained status by marrying princesses, and the sultan maintained control through a network of spies and forced marriages. The army was feudal, relying on noble levies and a core of slave warriors captured in annual raids (salatiya).

### Religion and Culture
Although the Funj elite converted to Islam early, their practice was initially nominal, blending with traditional African beliefs. The sultan was a divine figure, secluded and veiled. Despite this, the state sponsored Sufi holy men, which accelerated Islamization and Arabization among the populace. Pre-Arabic languages like Nubian and Hamaj gradually gave way to Arabic, which became the language of administration and trade by the 18th century. Christianity persisted in some regions until the 19th century.

## Terms
- **Funj**: The ruling elite of the sultanate, a people of disputed ethnic origin who were originally nomadic pastoralists.
- **Sennar**: The capital city of the Funj Sultanate, founded c. 1504 on the Blue Nile.
- **Hamaj**: A generic term for pre-Funj, non-Arabized or semi-Arabized peoples of the Gezira region; they seized power in 1762.
- **Abdallabi**: A powerful Bedouin tribal federation that was subordinate to the Funj but later rivaled them for control.
- **Ulama**: Islamic scholars and jurists whose growing influence in the 18th century challenged royal authority.
- **Salatiya**: Annual slave raids conducted by the Funj army, primarily targeting non-Muslim groups in the Nuba Mountains.
- **Manjil**: A provincial governor in the Funj administrative system.
- **Mek**: The title held by the sultans of Sennar.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The ethnic origins of the Funj people remain disputed, with theories suggesting they were Nubian, Shilluk, or from regions like Bornu or Eritrea.
- The exact timing and cause of the collapse of the Christian kingdom of Alodia are unclear, with evidence suggesting it may have occurred as early as the 12th century.
- The nature and extent of the Funj language, which some sources mention, is poorly understood, with surviving records being fragmentary and possibly inaccurate.

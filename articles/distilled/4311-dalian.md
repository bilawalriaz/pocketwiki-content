# Dalian

## Overview
Dalian is a major port city and financial center in Northeast China, located at the southern tip of the Liaodong Peninsula. Its strategic, ice-free harbor has made it a focal point of foreign interest and conflict, leading to successive occupations by Russia and Japan before returning to Chinese control. Today, it is a key economic hub for shipping, logistics, and industry in East Asia.

## Timeline
- **1898** — Russian Empire leases the area from Qing China and builds the port city of Dal'niy.
- **1905** — After the Russo-Japanese War, Japan gains control and renames the city Dairen.
- **1945** — Soviet forces liberate the city; it is administered by the Soviets and Chinese Communists.
- **1950** — Dalian and Lüshun merge to form the city of Lüda.
- **1981** — The city is officially renamed Dalian.
- **1984** — Dalian is designated a Special Economic Zone by the Chinese government.
- **1994** — Dalian is upgraded to a sub-provincial city.
- **2007** — Dalian begins hosting the World Economic Forum's "Summer Davos" in alternating years.
- **2010** — A major oil spill occurs in the Yellow Sea near Dalian.
- **2014** — The Dalian Jinpu New Area, a state-level new area, is established.

## Body

### Etymology and Historical Names
The city originated from the fishing village of Qingniwa. The Russian name "Dal'niy" (meaning "remote") was used from 1898. After the Russo-Japanese War, Japan renamed it Dairen. In 1950, it merged with Lüshun (formerly Port Arthur) to form Lüda, before being renamed Dalian in 1981.

### Foreign Occupation and Development
The area was leased by Russia in 1898, which built a modern port city. Following the Russo-Japanese War (1904–05), Japan took control, establishing the Kwantung Leased Territory and developing the region as a major trade port. After World War II, the city was under Soviet administration until 1955, after which it became a major Chinese shipbuilding center.

### Modern Economy and Industry
Dalian is a financial, shipping, and logistics center. Its port is among the busiest in the world by cargo tonnage. Key industries include machine manufacturing, petrochemicals, electronics, and IT. The city hosts major industrial zones, including a Development Zone with companies like Intel, and is a hub for the Dalian Commodity Exchange. It is also a leading center for software and IT services.

### Geography and Climate
Located on the Liaodong Peninsula, Dalian has a 1,906 km coastline. It has a monsoon-influenced humid continental climate with warm, wet summers and cold, dry winters. The annual mean temperature is 11.8 °C.

### Culture and Demographics
Dalian's culture reflects its history of migration from Shandong and foreign occupation, with a distinct dialect and seafood-focused cuisine. The city is known as China's "Football City" due to its successful football history. As of the 2020 census, its total population was 7,450,785.

## Terms
- **Prefecture-level city**: An administrative division of China, ranking above a county-level city and below a province.
- **Sub-provincial city**: A city in China with a budget independent of the province, administered by both the province and the central government.
- **Special Economic Zone (SEZ)**: A designated area where business and investment laws differ from the rest of the country to encourage economic growth.
- **Kwantung Leased Territory**: The territory in the Liaodong Peninsula leased to and administered by the Empire of Japan from 1905 to 1945.
- **Dalian Commodity Exchange (DCE)**: A futures exchange in Dalian, the only one in Northeast China, trading agricultural and industrial commodities.
- **Jiaoliao Mandarin**: A subgroup of Mandarin Chinese spoken in parts of Shandong and Liaoning provinces, including Dalian.
- **Chuang Guandong**: A historical migration of people from Shandong province to Northeast China (Manchuria).

## Debates and Open Questions
- The exact scale of the 2010 Dalian oil spill is debated, with official figures at 11,000 barrels and independent estimates suggesting it could be much higher.
- The environmental and long-term health impacts of the oil spill and the 2011 petrochemical plant breach remain a concern.

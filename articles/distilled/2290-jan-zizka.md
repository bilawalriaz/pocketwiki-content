# Jan Žižka

## Overview
Jan Žižka (c. 1360–1424) was a Czech military commander and knight who became a national hero for leading the Hussite forces, specifically the Taborite faction, during the Hussite Wars. He is renowned for his innovative tactics, including the use of wagon forts and early gunpowder weapons, and remained undefeated in battle against multiple crusades. His strategies are studied as early examples of asymmetric warfare.

## Timeline
- **c. 1360** — Jan Žižka is born in Trocnov, Kingdom of Bohemia.
- **15 July 1410** — Fights for the Polish-Lithuanian side at the Battle of Grunwald.
- **30 July 1419** — Participates in the First Defenestration of Prague, sparking the Hussite revolution.
- **25 March 1420** — Wins the first pitched battle of the Hussite Wars at Sudoměř.
- **14 July 1420** — Defends Vítkov Hill against the first anti-Hussite crusade.
- **6 January 1422** — Defeats Sigismund's army at Nebovidy after a mobile artillery maneuver.
- **1423** — Becomes totally blind after being wounded at Rabí Castle but continues to command.
- **20 April 1423** — Defeats Utraquist nobles at Hořice during a civil war.
- **7 June 1424** — Wins his last battle at Malešov against the "Praguers" and Utraquists.
- **11 October 1424** — Dies during the siege of Přibyslav on the Moravian frontier.

## Body

### Early Life and Outlaw Years
Žižka was born into lower Czech nobility. A 1378 document mentions a "Jan called Zizka of Trocnov," leading to an estimated birth year of c. 1360, though this identification is debated. After 1384, he disappears from records for about 20 years, likely serving as a mercenary. By 1406, he appears in Rosenberg estate records as an accused bandit, possibly due to social tensions and land disputes. He led a group of outlaws in raids and robberies until he was pardoned by King Wenceslaus IV in 1409.

### Military Innovation and Tactics
Žižka's tactics were unorthodox and are considered early modern guerrilla warfare. He pioneered the use of **wagon forts** (*vozová hradba* or *Wagenburg*), where carts were chained together to form mobile fortifications. Crews of 16–22 soldiers, including crossbowmen, handgunners, and pikemen, defended these forts. Battles typically involved a defensive stage using artillery and firearms from the carts to weaken enemy cavalry, followed by a counterattack. He was an innovator in using gunpowder weapons, including field guns and handguns (the Czech *píšťala*, ancestor of "pistol"), and was the first European commander to maneuver with medium-caliber cannon on carts. His highly disciplined troops, recruited from all social classes, used terrain and ambushes to overcome numerically superior foes.

### Hussite Wars and Crusades
The Hussite Wars began in 1419 after the First Defenestration of Prague, following the execution of reformer Jan Hus. Žižka joined the Hussites and became a captain of the Taborite faction. He successfully defended Prague against the first anti-Hussite crusade in 1420, notably at the Battle of Vítkov Hill. He later defeated crusader armies at Kutná Hora (1421) and Nebovidy (1422), demonstrating the effectiveness of his mobile artillery. He lost his remaining eye in 1423 but continued to command. He fought in internal Hussite civil wars, defeating Utraquist nobles and the "Praguers" at battles like Hořice (1423) and Malešov (1424).

### Death and Legacy
Žižka died on 11 October 1424 during the siege of Přibyslav. The cause is now believed to be a purulent disease (carbuncle), not plague or poison. His dying wish was for his skin to be made into drums so he could continue leading his troops. His soldiers called themselves "Orphans" after his death. He was succeeded by Prokop the Great. A monument on Vítkov Hill in Prague honors his 1420 victory.

## Terms
- **Hussites**: Followers of the religious reformer Jan Hus, whose teachings sparked the Hussite Wars.
- **Taborites**: A radical, democratic faction of the Hussites, named after their stronghold Tábor.
- **Utraquists**: A moderate Hussite faction that emphasized receiving communion in both kinds (under the species of bread and wine).
- **Wagon Fort (Wagenburg)**: A tactical formation of chained carts used as a mobile fortification, central to Hussite warfare.
- **Píšťala**: A Czech term for a handgun, considered an ancestor of the English word "pistol."
- **Houfnice**: A Czech term for an anti-infantry field gun, considered an ancestor of the English word "howitzer."
- **Defenestration**: The act of throwing someone out of a window, a key event in starting the Hussite revolution.
- **Crusade**: A military campaign authorized by the Pope against those deemed heretics, in this case, the Hussites.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Birth Year**: The exact date is unknown. The c. 1360 estimate comes from a 1378 document, but some historians argue this could refer to his father, as it would make Žižka unusually old for his later military career.
- **Cause of Death**: Traditional accounts cited plague, but modern historians rule this out. Arsenic poisoning was also disproven by examining skeletal remains; a carbuncle is now the most likely cause.
- **Early Career**: The 20-year gap in records (c. 1384–1406) is assumed to be spent as a mercenary, but concrete evidence is lacking.

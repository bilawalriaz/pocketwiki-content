# Bursa

## Overview
Bursa is a major city in northwestern Turkey, historically significant as the first capital of the Ottoman Empire and a key center of the silk trade. Today, it is Turkey's fourth-largest city and a major industrial hub, particularly for automotive production, while also being a center for tourism due to its historical monuments, thermal baths, and proximity to the ski resort on Mount Uludağ.

- **September 1922** — Turkish Nationalist Army recaptures Bursa.

## Body

### Antiquity and Early History
The area has been settled since c. 6000 BC. The Bithynian city of Prusa, known for its hot springs, was built by King Prusias I. After the last Bithynian king bequeathed his kingdom to Rome in 75/74 BC, Prusa became part of the Roman Empire. Under Byzantine rule, Emperor Justinian I built a palace there, and by the mid-6th century, it was a famous silk textile center.

### Ottoman Capital and Commercial Hub
Bursa became the first capital of the Ottoman Empire after its capture in 1326. The city saw major construction, including the first official Ottoman mint, hospitals, and caravanserais. Although the capital moved to Edirne in the 1360s, Bursa retained spiritual and commercial importance. It was a major hub for the Ottoman silk trade, distributing silk from the East to Mediterranean markets. The city was damaged during the Ottoman Interregnum after the 1402 Battle of Ankara but was later rebuilt.

### Modern Era and Demographics
In the 19th and 20th centuries, Bursa's industry shifted from foreign-owned silk factories to broader manufacturing. The city received waves of immigrants, notably from the Balkans after 1877 and from Bulgaria in the 1950s and 1989, shaping its modern demographic. During World War I and the Greco-Turkish War, the city's Christian populations (Greek and Armenian) faced deportations and violence. Greek forces held the city from 1920 until its recapture by Turkish nationalists in 1922.

### Jewish Community
A small Romaniote Jewish community existed before the Ottoman conquest. It was joined by Sephardic Jews after the 1492 expulsion from Spain. The community, centered in the Kuruçeşme quarter, was involved in trade and crafts like silk and goldsmithing. Despite periods of prosperity, it faced economic struggles and antisemitic incidents like blood libels. The population peaked at 3,500 before World War I and has since dwindled to about 60 as of 2021.

### Economy and Geography
Bursa is Turkey's largest automotive production center, hosting factories for Fiat, Renault, and parts suppliers. The textile and food industries are also strong. Historically, it was the empire's largest silk trade center. The city is located on the slopes of Mount Uludağ (ancient Mysian Olympus) and has a Mediterranean climate. It is a major tourism destination for its historical sites, thermal baths, and the Uludağ ski resort.

## Terms
- **Beylik**: An early Ottoman principality or state.
- **Külliye**: A complex of buildings centered around a mosque, typically including schools, hospitals, and baths.
- **Devshirme**: The Ottoman practice of levying Christian boys for conversion and service in the state or military.
- **Vilayet**: An administrative division of the Ottoman Empire, similar to a province.
- **Bedesten**: A covered market or bazaar, often for textiles and valuables.
- **Caravanserai**: A roadside inn for travelers, providing shelter and supplies.
- **Hammam**: A traditional Turkish bath.
- **Süper Lig**: The top professional football league in Turkey.

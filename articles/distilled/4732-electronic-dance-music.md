# Electronic dance music

## Overview
Electronic dance music (EDM) is a broad range of percussive electronic music genres originally created for nightclubs, raves, and festivals. It is typically produced for playback by DJs, who create seamless mixes, or performed live by producers. EDM has evolved from underground scenes into a global mainstream industry, influencing popular music and spawning numerous subgenres.

## Timeline
- **Late 1960s-1970s** — Precursors emerge, including dub music, synthesizer-based disco, and electropop.
- **1980** — Ryuichi Sakamoto's "Riot in Lagos" introduces the Roland TR-808 drum machine to clubs.
- **1982** — Afrika Bambaataa's "Planet Rock" popularizes the TR-808 and informs electro and techno.
- **Early 1980s** — House music develops in Chicago; techno emerges in Detroit.
- **1988** — Acid house becomes a trend in the UK; the term "techno" is popularized by a compilation album.
- **Late 1980s** — Rave culture grows in the UK, fueled by warehouse parties and MDMA.
- **Early 1990s** — Trance develops in Europe; jungle and drum and bass emerge from the UK rave scene.
- **Late 1990s** — Dubstep originates in South London as an offshoot of UK garage.
- **Early 2010s** — The term "EDM" is pushed by the American music industry to rebrand rave culture.
- **2012** — Corporate consolidation begins with SFX Entertainment's $1 billion investment plan. |
- **2013** — Billboard launches the Dance/Electronic Songs chart.
- **2020** — Major EDM festivals are canceled or postponed due to the COVID-19 pandemic.

## Body

### Precursors and Early Genres
EDM's roots lie in late 1960s and 1970s electronic music, including Jamaican dub, which pioneered remix culture and studio effects; disco, which used drum machines and synthesizers; and synth-pop, which featured synthesizers as the dominant instrument. Hip-hop contributed turntablism and breakbeats. These influences converged in the early 1980s with the development of affordable electronic instruments like the Roland TR-808 and TB-303.

### The 1980s: House, Techno, and Rave
The 1980s saw the birth of core EDM genres. In Chicago, DJs like Frankie Knuckles created house music by editing disco records with drum machines and synthesizers. In Detroit, DJs Juan Atkins, Derrick May, and Kevin Saunderson fused house with funk to create techno. By the late 1980s, acid house (characterized by the squelchy sound of the TB-303) and rave culture exploded in the UK, centered on warehouse parties and the drug MDMA.

### The 1990s: Diversification and Mainstream Growth
The 1990s saw EDM diversify into subgenres. Trance emerged as a melodic offshoot of techno and house. In the UK, breakbeat hardcore evolved into jungle and then drum and bass, emphasizing fast, syncopated breakbeats and heavy bass. While rave culture faced legal and media hostility in the US, it achieved mainstream popularity in Europe and Japan.

### The 21st Century: Global Mainstream and New Genres
In the 2000s, EDM's popularity surged globally, especially in the US and Australia. The American music industry adopted the term "EDM" to rebrand the culture. New subgenres emerged, including dubstep (from South London), electro house, and trap music. Corporate investment grew, with companies like SFX Entertainment acquiring festivals and promoters. However, this commercialization led to criticism of over-commercialization and a perceived homogenization of sound.

### Production and Terminology
EDM is produced using digital audio workstations (DAWs), synthesizers, samplers, and MIDI controllers. The term "EDM" was used as early as 1980 by producer Richard James Burgess but was widely adopted in the late 2000s by the US industry. The City of Chicago defines it as music created by DJs using specialized equipment.

### Social Impact and Festivals
EDM culture is centered on festivals and clubs, which evolved from illegal raves into large-scale, commercially sponsored events with elaborate visual productions. These festivals have significant economic impacts but have also faced criticism for over-commercialization and safety issues, including drug-related incidents and deaths. The COVID-19 pandemic in 2020 caused widespread cancellations, severely impacting the industry.

## Terms
- **DJ Mix**: A seamless selection of tracks created by segueing from one recording to another.
- **Live PA**: A live performance of electronic music by a producer in a concert or festival setting.
- **Four-on-the-floor**: A rhythm pattern with a steady bass drum hit on every beat, common in house and techno.
- **Breakbeat**: A syncopated drum pattern sampled from a "break" in a song, foundational to genres like jungle and drum and bass.
- **Sub-bass**: Very low-frequency bass tones, a key characteristic of genres like dubstep.
- **DAW (Digital Audio Workstation)**: Software used for recording, editing, and producing audio files.
- **MIDI (Musical Instrument Digital Interface)**: A protocol that allows electronic instruments, computers, and other devices to communicate.
- **Ghost Producer**: A hired music producer who creates a song for another artist, who releases it as their own.
- **Bedroom Producer**: An independent musician who creates electronic music in a home studio using low-cost software and equipment.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Terminology**: The term "EDM" is debated; it is seen by some as an American industry rebranding of rave culture, while in the UK, "dance music" is more common.
- **Commercialization vs. Artistry**: There is criticism that the over-commercialization of EDM has led to a homogenized "big room" sound and a decline in DJing skill, with some artists arguing it prioritizes entertainment over artistry.
- **Safety and Drug Culture**: The association of EDM with recreational drug use, particularly MDMA, has led to drug-related deaths at festivals, prompting legal bans and increased regulation in some regions.
- **Economic Bubble**: Following corporate consolidation, there have been concerns about an EDM "bubble," with questions about the sustainability of high festival costs and market oversaturation.

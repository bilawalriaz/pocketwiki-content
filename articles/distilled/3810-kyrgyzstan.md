# Kyrgyzstan

## Overview
Kyrgyzstan is a landlocked, mountainous country in Central Asia, historically part of the Silk Road. Its modern identity is shaped by a nomadic heritage, a complex history of foreign domination, and a turbulent post-Soviet transition marked by revolutions, ethnic tensions, and ongoing political and economic challenges.

## Timeline
- **840 AD** — Kyrgyz state reaches greatest expansion after defeating the Uyghur Khaganate.
- **1207** — Kyrgyz tribes peacefully become part of the Mongol Empire.
- **1876** — Territory is formally incorporated into the Russian Empire.
- **5 Dec 1936** — Kirghiz Soviet Socialist Republic established as a constituent republic of the USSR.
- **31 Aug 1991** — Kyrgyzstan declares independence from the Soviet Union.
- **2005** — "Tulip Revolution" protests oust President Askar Akayev.
- **2010** — President Kurmanbek Bakiyev is ousted; violent ethnic clashes occur in Osh and Jalal-Abad.
- **2015** — Kyrgyzstan joins the Eurasian Economic Union (EAEU).
- **2021** — Sadyr Japarov elected president; constitutional referendum approves a shift to a presidential system.
- **Sep 2022** — Violent border clashes with Tajikistan occur.
- **Mar 2025** — Border demarcation agreement signed with Tajikistan.

## Body

### Early History and Etymology
The name "Kyrgyz" possibly means "forty tribes," referencing the legendary hero Manas who united forty clans. The Kyrgyz state peaked after defeating the Uyghur Khaganate in 840 AD. Over centuries, the region was overrun by Mongols, the Qing dynasty of China, and the Khanate of Kokand. The Epic of Manas, a UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage, reflects this nomadic past.

### Russian and Soviet Rule
The eastern part of modern Kyrgyzstan was ceded by Qing China to the Russian Empire in the late 19th century, formally incorporated in 1876. Soviet power was established in 1919, and the Kirghiz Soviet Socialist Republic was formed in 1936. The Soviet period brought forced collectivization of agriculture, which caused significant hardship and unrest among nomads, and a policy of nation-building that promoted a standardized Kyrgyz identity. By the late 1980s, glasnost allowed limited political openness.

### Independence and Political Turbulence
Following the failed 1991 coup in Moscow, Kyrgyzstan declared independence on 31 August 1991. Askar Akayev was elected president in October 1991. The post-Soviet era has been marked by political instability. The 2005 "Tulip Revolution" protests over fraudulent elections led to Akayev's ouster. His successor, Kurmanbek Bakiyev, was himself ousted in 2010 after protests over corruption and energy prices, which were followed by deadly ethnic clashes between Kyrgyz and Uzbeks in the south. A 2010 referendum approved a constitution to reduce presidential powers, but the system has oscillated between parliamentary and presidential models. In 2021, Sadyr Japarov was elected president after protests, and a referendum approved a new constitution granting the president expanded powers, raising concerns about authoritarianism.

### Geography and Economy
Kyrgyzstan is dominated by the Tian Shan mountains, which cover over 80% of its territory. Its economy, one of the poorest in Central Asia, was severely impacted by the collapse of the Soviet Union. It relies on agriculture (livestock, wool, meat), gold mining, and the re-export of Chinese goods. Remittances from Kyrgyz migrants in Russia are a significant economic contributor. The country is a member of the Eurasian Economic Union and the World Trade Organization. Major infrastructure projects, like the China-Kyrgyzstan-Uzbekistan railway, are planned.

### Demographics and Culture
The population is over 7.4 million, with ethnic Kyrgyz (77.8%) forming the majority, followed by Uzbeks (14.3%) and Russians (3.7%). Kyrgyz is the state language, with Russian also official. Islam is the dominant religion, though the state is secular. Traditional nomadic culture is reflected in the use of yurts, the Epic of Manas, and sports like Ulak Tartysh (a horseback game). The flag's 40-ray sun symbolizes the forty tribes.

## Terms
- **Tian Shan**: A major mountain system in Central Asia, covering over 80% of Kyrgyzstan.
- **Silk Road**: Historic network of trade routes connecting the East and West, passing through Kyrgyzstan.
- **Collectivization**: Soviet policy of consolidating individual farms into state-controlled collective farms (kolkhoz).
- **Glasnost**: Policy of increased openness and transparency in Soviet institutions during the 1980s.
- **Tulip Revolution**: The 2005 protests that led to the ouster of President Askar Akayev.
- **Eurasian Economic Union (EAEU)**: A regional economic integration organization of former Soviet states, which Kyrgyzstan joined in 2015.
- **Yurt**: A portable, round tent covered with skins or felt, traditionally used by nomadic groups in Central Asia.
- **Manas**: A legendary Kyrgyz hero and the central figure of an epic poem, a symbol of national unity.

## Debates and Open Questions
- The origin of the name "Kyrgyz" (e.g., "forty tribes") is a subject of multiple theories.
- The traditional nature of "bride kidnapping" (ala kachuu) is debated; some view it as a distortion of arranged marriage practices.
- The political system continues to be contested, with shifts between parliamentary and presidential models reflecting a struggle over the concentration of power.
- The impact of the 2021 constitutional changes on democracy and human rights, including press freedom, is a significant concern.

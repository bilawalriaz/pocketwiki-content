# Liechtenstein

## Overview
Liechtenstein is a doubly landlocked Alpine microstate between Austria and Switzerland. Formed in 1719 and fully independent since 1866, it is a constitutional monarchy with a prince who holds significant power. Despite its small size, it has a highly developed economy, a strong financial sector, and a high standard of living.

## Timeline
- **c. 5300 BC** — Neolithic farming settlements appear in the valleys.
- **58 BC** — Julius Caesar defeats Alpine tribes, bringing the region under Roman control.
- **1396** — Vaduz gains imperial immediacy, subject only to the Holy Roman Emperor.
- **1699-1712** — The Liechtenstein family purchases the Lordships of Schellenberg and Vaduz.
- **23 January 1719** — Emperor Charles VI unites Vaduz and Schellenberg, creating the Principality of Liechtenstein.
- **1806** — The Holy Roman Empire dissolves; Liechtenstein joins the Confederation of the Rhine.
- **1866** — Liechtenstein becomes fully independent after the dissolution of the German Confederation.
- **1918** — Economic devastation from WWI leads to a customs and monetary union with Switzerland.
- **1921** — A new constitution establishes a constitutional monarchy.
- **1984** — Liechtenstein becomes the last European country to grant women the right to vote.
- **1990** — Liechtenstein is admitted to the United Nations.
- **2003** — A constitutional referendum grants the monarch greater powers, including a veto and the ability to dismiss the government.

## Body

### History and Foundation
Inhabited since the Middle Paleolithic era, the area was part of Roman Raetia before Alemannic settlement and incorporation into the Frankish Empire. The ruling Liechtenstein family acquired Schellenberg (1699) and Vaduz (1712) to gain a seat in the Holy Roman Imperial Diet. In 1719, Emperor Charles VI united these lands into the Principality. Full independence came in 1866 after the German Confederation dissolved; the army was abolished for financial reasons. Close ties to Austria-Hungary ended after WWI, leading to a customs and monetary union with Switzerland in 1923. The country remained neutral in both World Wars, though its ruling family's properties in Czechoslovakia and Poland were expropriated after WWII.

### Government and Politics
Liechtenstein combines absolute monarchy, representative democracy, and direct democracy. The Prince is head of state with extensive powers, including a legislative veto and the ability to dismiss the government, reaffirmed by 2003 and 2012 referendums. The unicameral Landtag has 25 members elected by proportional representation. The government, led by a head of government, is appointed by the Prince from parliamentary proposals. Citizens can propose laws and constitutional changes via referendum. The country is a member of the UN, EFTA, Council of Europe, and participates in the Schengen Area and European Economic Area, but is not an EU member. Its foreign policy focuses on international legal cooperation to safeguard sovereignty.

### Economy and Finance
Liechtenstein has a prosperous, highly industrialized free-enterprise economy with a high standard of living. It participates in a customs and monetary union with Switzerland, using the Swiss franc. Key industries include electronics, pharmaceuticals, and precision instruments, with the Hilti Group as its largest employer. The financial sector is a major part of the economy. Historically known as a tax haven, Liechtenstein has reformed its financial system following the 2008 tax affair, signing tax transparency agreements with Germany, the UK, and the EU. The country has one of the world's lowest crime rates and no national debt.

### Geography and Demographics
A mountainous Alpine country, about half its territory lies above 2,000 meters. Its highest point is the Grauspitz (2,599 m). The Rhine River forms its western border with Switzerland. The climate is continental, influenced by warm foehn winds. The population is approximately 41,000, with about one-third foreign-born. The official language is German, with an Alemannic dialect widely spoken at home. Catholicism is the state religion, practiced by about 76% of the population. The literacy rate is 100%.

### Culture and Sports
Liechtenstein's culture reflects its German-speaking neighbors. Cultural institutions include the Kunstmuseum Liechtenstein and the National Museum. The national holiday is celebrated at Vaduz Castle. In sports, Liechtenstein excels in winter sports, particularly alpine skiing, having won more Olympic medals per capita than any other nation. Its football teams play in the Swiss league system, and the national team has achieved notable upsets against larger nations.

## Terms
- **Doubly landlocked**: A country surrounded only by other landlocked countries. Liechtenstein and Uzbekistan are the world's two such countries.
- **Imperial immediacy (Reichsunmittelbarkeit)**: A status within the Holy Roman Empire where a territory was subject only to the Emperor, not to any intermediate feudal lord.
- **Constitutional monarchy**: A system where a monarch acts as head of state within the parameters of a constitution.
- **Customs and monetary union**: An agreement between countries to eliminate tariffs on trade and use a common currency. Liechtenstein's union is with Switzerland.
- **European Economic Area (EEA)**: An agreement extending the EU's single market to non-EU member states, including Liechtenstein.
- **Schengen Area**: An area of 27 European countries that have abolished internal border controls. Liechtenstein participates via its agreement with Switzerland.
- **Stiftungen**: Foundations used in Liechtenstein's financial sector, historically used to obscure asset ownership for non-residents.
- **Alemannic German**: A group of German dialects spoken in Switzerland, western Austria, Liechtenstein, and southwestern Germany.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Monarchical Powers**: The 2003 and 2012 referendums expanding the Prince's powers were criticized by the Council of Europe's Venice Commission as incompatible with European democratic standards, raising ongoing questions about the balance of power.
- **Tax Haven Legacy**: While Liechtenstein has reformed its financial sector, the 2008 tax affair and historical use of foundations for tax evasion continue to influence its international reputation and relations.
- **Post-WWII Property Claims**: The expropriation of the Liechtenstein dynasty's properties in Czechoslovakia and Poland after WWII remains a subject of legal dispute at the International Court of Justice.

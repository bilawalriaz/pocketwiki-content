# Vlad the Impaler

## Overview
Vlad III (c. 1428–1476/77), known as Vlad the Impaler or Vlad Dracula, was a Voivode (ruler) of Wallachia who fought to centralize power, crush the nobility (boyars), and resist Ottoman expansion. His brutal methods, especially impalement, made him a legendary figure of terror in European folklore and likely inspired the name of Bram Stoker's fictional vampire, Count Dracula.

## Timeline
- **c. 1428–1431** — Born in the House of Drăculești, son of Vlad II Dracul.
- **1442** — Held as a hostage in the Ottoman Empire with his brother Radu.
- **1447** — His father and elder brother Mircea are murdered; Vladislav II becomes voivode.
- **1448** — First, brief reign after invading with Ottoman support; forced to flee by year's end.
- **1456** — Second reign begins with Hungarian support; purges boyars and conflicts with Transylvanian Saxons.
- **1462** — Launches a major campaign against the Ottomans, culminating in the Night Attack at Târgoviște.
- **1462** — Imprisoned by King Matthias Corvinus of Hungary, possibly due to forged letters.
- **1475** — Released from prison, reportedly after converting to Roman Catholicism.
- **1476** — Third reign begins with Hungarian and Moldavian support.
- **Late 1476/early 1477** — Killed in battle near Snagov while fighting Ottoman-backed forces.

## Body

### Early Life and Exile
Vlad was the second son of Vlad II Dracul, a member of the Order of the Dragon (a militant Christian fraternity). In 1442, Vlad and his younger brother Radu were held as hostages in the Ottoman Empire to secure their father's loyalty. After their father and elder brother were murdered in 1447, Vlad's second cousin, Vladislav II, took the throne. Vlad's first attempt to seize power in 1448, with Ottoman backing, was short-lived. He spent the following years in exile in the Ottoman Empire, Moldavia, and Hungary.

### Second Reign and Consolidation of Power
Vlad returned to Wallachia in 1456 with Hungarian support, killing Vladislav II. He immediately began a purge of the boyars he suspected of disloyalty, redistributing their wealth to his retainers to strengthen central authority. He came into conflict with the Transylvanian Saxons, who supported his rivals. In response, Vlad plundered Saxon villages and impaled their inhabitants, earning his infamous epithet. Peace was restored in 1460.

### War with the Ottoman Empire
In 1462, Vlad defied Ottoman Sultan Mehmed II by refusing to pay homage and executing his envoys. He then launched a devastating raid into Ottoman territory. In retaliation, Mehmed led a massive army into Wallachia. During the famous Night Attack at Târgoviște (June 16–17, 1462), Vlad attempted to capture the sultan but failed. The Ottomans eventually installed Vlad's brother, Radu, as ruler. Deserted by many of his own men, Vlad fled to Hungary, where King Matthias Corvinus had him imprisoned.

### Imprisonment and Final Reign
Vlad was held captive in Hungary from 1463 to 1475. During this time, stories of his cruelty spread across Europe. He was released in 1475, reportedly after converting to Catholicism, and fought for Corvinus in Bosnia. With Hungarian and Moldavian help, he briefly regained the Wallachian throne in late 1476 but was killed in battle against Ottoman-supported forces before January 10, 1477.

### Legacy and Reputation
Vlad's reputation for extreme cruelty was cemented by German pamphlets and Slavic chronicles published after his death, which detailed his use of impalement and other tortures. In Romania, he is often viewed as a just ruler who used terror to enforce order and defend against foreign domination. His patronymic, "Dracula" (meaning "son of the dragon" or "son of the devil"), was borrowed by Bram Stoker for his 1897 novel, though Stoker's notes show no direct reference to the historical figure.

## Terms
- **Voivode**: A historical title for a military commander or ruler in parts of Central and Eastern Europe.
- **Boyars**: A member of the old aristocracy in parts of the Balkans and Eastern Europe.
- **Order of the Dragon**: A chivalric order founded in 1408 to defend Christianity against the Ottoman Empire.
- **Impalement**: A method of execution where a person is pierced with a long stake.
- **Scorched earth**: A military strategy of destroying anything useful to the enemy while advancing or retreating.
- **Suzerainty**: A relationship in which one state controls the foreign affairs of another, while the latter retains internal autonomy.
- **Hemolacria**: A rare medical condition where tears are partially composed of blood.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Extent of Cruelty**: Scholars debate whether the stories of Vlad's atrocities are accurate historical accounts or exaggerated propaganda created by his enemies, particularly the Transylvanian Saxons and the Hungarian court.
- **Reason for Imprisonment**: The exact cause of his 14-year imprisonment by Matthias Corvinus is unclear. The three letters allegedly proving Vlad's treachery to the Ottomans are widely considered forgeries.
- **Connection to Dracula**: While Vlad's name inspired Stoker's character, historians note that Stoker had limited knowledge of the historical figure and that the fictional vampire is not a direct portrayal.
- **Cause of Death**: The circumstances of his death are uncertain, with accounts ranging from being killed in battle to being assassinated by a Turkish agent or even mistakenly by his own troops.

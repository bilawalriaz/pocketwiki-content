# Theodore Roosevelt

## Overview
Theodore Roosevelt was the 26th U.S. President (1901–1909), a progressive leader who expanded federal power to regulate business, conserve natural resources, and assert American influence abroad. His "Square Deal" domestic policies and "Big Stick" foreign policy reshaped the modern presidency and established the U.S. as a global power. He remains one of the most historically ranked presidents, celebrated for his energetic leadership and controversial for his imperialist and interventionist views.

- **Oct 27, 1858** — Born in New York City.

- **1898** — Led the Rough Riders in the Spanish-American War.

- **1908** — Left office; handpicked William Howard Taft as successor.
- **1912** — Lost Republican nomination; ran as Progressive ("Bull Moose") candidate; shot during campaign.
- **Jan 6, 1919** — Died at Sagamore Hill, New York.

## Body

### Early Life and Political Rise
Roosevelt overcame childhood asthma through a "strenuous life" of physical exertion. After Harvard, he entered New York politics, gaining fame as an anti-corruption assemblyman and later as a Civil Service Commissioner. His national profile rose as a historian (*The Naval War of 1812*) and as a rancher in the Dakotas, where he developed his conservationist ethos. He served as Assistant Secretary of the Navy, helping plan the war with Spain, then resigned to lead the Rough Riders, becoming a war hero. Elected New York Governor in 1898, his progressive agenda alarmed party bosses, who engineered his nomination for Vice President in 1900 to remove him from state politics.

### Presidency (1901–1909)
Roosevelt became president after McKinley's assassination in 1901. He championed the "Square Deal," a domestic program focused on fairness, corporate regulation, and conservation. He aggressively used the Sherman Antitrust Act to break up "bad trusts" like the Northern Securities Company, earning the nickname "Trust Buster." He mediated the 1902 anthracite coal strike, the first time a president intervened in a labor dispute. Key legislation included the Hepburn Act (regulating railroad rates) and the Pure Food and Drug Act.

Roosevelt prioritized conservation, establishing the U.S. Forest Service and protecting approximately 230 million acres of public land through national parks, monuments, and executive orders. In foreign policy, he pursued "Big Stick" diplomacy, asserting U.S. dominance in the Western Hemisphere. He supported Panamanian independence from Colombia to secure the Panama Canal and issued the Roosevelt Corollary to the Monroe Doctrine, claiming a U.S. right to intervene in Latin American nations. He expanded the Navy and sent the Great White Fleet on a world tour. His mediation of the Russo-Japanese War won him the Nobel Peace Prize.

### Post-Presidency and Later Years
Roosevelt honored his pledge not to seek a third term in 1908, supporting Taft. He later grew dissatisfied with Taft's conservatism, seeking and failing to win the 1912 Republican nomination. He formed the Progressive ("Bull Moose") Party, splitting the Republican vote and allowing Democrat Woodrow Wilson to win. During the campaign, Roosevelt was shot but delivered his speech with the bullet lodged in his chest.

He led a dangerous 1913–1914 expedition in the Amazon, nearly dying from tropical disease. During World War I, he vehemently criticized Wilson's neutrality and offered to lead volunteers in France, but was rejected. His son Quentin was killed in the war. Roosevelt died in 1919, his health broken by illness and exertion.

## Terms
- **Square Deal**: Roosevelt's domestic policy platform, emphasizing fairness, corporate regulation, and conservation.
- **Trust Buster**: Nickname for Roosevelt due to his aggressive use of antitrust laws to break up monopolies.
- **Big Stick Diplomacy**: Roosevelt's foreign policy ideology, summarized as "speak softly and carry a big stick," emphasizing military strength and assertive diplomacy.
- **Roosevelt Corollary**: An addition to the Monroe Doctrine asserting the U.S. right to exercise "international police power" in the Western Hemisphere.
- **Bull Moose Party**: The informal name for the Progressive Party, which Roosevelt founded in 1912.
- **Strenuous Life**: Roosevelt's personal philosophy advocating vigorous physical and moral exertion.
- **Muckraker**: A term Roosevelt coined for journalists who exposed corporate and political corruption.
- **New Nationalism**: Roosevelt's later progressive platform, calling for stronger federal regulation of corporations and labor protections.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Imperialism vs. Internationalism**: Historians debate whether Roosevelt's foreign policy was a necessary assertion of U.S. power or an aggressive imperialism. His Philippine policy, in particular, draws criticism.
- **Conservation Legacy**: While celebrated for protecting vast lands, some scholars note his actions sometimes conflicted with Native American land rights.
- **Racial Politics**: Roosevelt's record is mixed; he invited Booker T. Washington to the White House but also dismissed the Brownsville battalion of Black soldiers and ran a "lily-white" campaign in the South in 1912.
- **Panama Canal Corruption**: Controversy persists over whether Roosevelt's actions in Panama involved corruption or undue influence from business interests.
- **Medal of Honor**: His 2001 posthumous Medal of Honor for San Juan Hill remains debated, with critics calling it a politically motivated award.

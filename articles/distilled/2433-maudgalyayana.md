# Maudgalyayana

## Overview
Maudgalyāyana (Pali: Moggallāna) was one of the Buddha's two foremost male disciples, renowned for his psychic powers. His story, especially rescuing his mother from hell, became foundational for the East Asian Ghost Festival, illustrating karma, merit transfer, and filial piety.

## Timeline
- **c. 5th century BCE** — Born as Kolita in Kolita village, on the same day as his friend Śāriputra.
- **Youth** — With Śāriputra, studies under the skeptic Sañjaya Vairatiputra.
- **Youth** — After hearing the "Ye Dharma Hetu" verse from Aśvajit, both seek the Buddha.
- **Youth** — Ordains at Veṇuvana with Śāriputra and Sañjaya's 500 students.
- **Shortly after ordination** — Attains enlightenment (arhatship) in Kallavala.
- **Same day as ordination** — The Buddha appoints him and Śāriputra as chief male disciples.
- **During Buddha's ministry** — Uses psychic powers to reunite the community after Devadatta's schism.
- **c. 483 BCE** — Killed at age 84 by robbers hired by a rival sect at Kālasilā Cave.
- **19th century** — Relics inscribed with his and Śāriputra's names found at Sanchi and Satdhāra.
- **1952** — Relics formally re-installed at Sanchi, India, after return from Britain.

## Body

### Meeting the Buddha and Enlightenment
Maudgalyāyana and his friend Upatiṣya (Śāriputra) began as spiritual wanderers under Sañjaya. Dissatisfied, they separated, agreeing to share any discovery. Upatiṣya first heard the "Ye Dharma Hetu" verse from Aśvajit, summarizing dependent origination. This verse, widely inscribed in Buddhism, led both to the first stage of the path. They ordained under the Buddha, bringing Sañjaya's students. While others attained enlightenment quickly, Maudgalyāyana and Śāriputra, likened to kings needing preparation, attained arhatship one to two weeks later.

### Role as Chief Disciple and Teacher
The Buddha appointed them as foremost male disciples, a role depicted in art. Their specializations were complementary: Śāriputra for wisdom, Maudgalyāyana for psychic powers (*iddhi*). He was a key teacher, often instructing in the Buddha's place and training disciples like Rahula. Using his powers, he communicated with gods and spirits in hell realms, providing cosmological and karmic maps of rebirth. A famous demonstration was his victory over the dragon Nandopananda.

### Schism, Death, and Karma
Maudgalyāyana played a crucial role in resolving Devadatta's schism. Using his powers to learn of Devadatta's alliance with Prince Ajātasattu, he informed the Buddha. He and Śāriputra later persuaded Devadatta's followers to return. He died violently at age 84, beaten by robbers hired by a rival sect. The Buddha explained this was the fruition of heavy karma from a past life where he killed his own parents, underscoring that even enlightenment cannot avert severe past actions.

### Legacy: Filial Piety and the Ghost Festival
The post-canonical account of Maudgalyāyana rescuing his mother from hell became immensely influential. Using his powers, he found her as a hungry ghost. The Buddha instructed him to make offerings to the monastic community and transfer the merit to her, freeing her. This story, in the *Ullambana Sutra*, emphasized filial piety and merit transfer, leading to the Ghost Festival (e.g., *Yulan Hui* in China, *Obon* in Japan). Celebrated in the seventh lunar month, it involves ceremonies transferring merit to ancestors, helping Buddhism integrate into Chinese society by aligning with Confucian values.

### Relics and Textual Associations
In the 19th century, bone fragments inscribed with his and Śāriputra's names were found at Sanchi and Satdhāra, dated to the 2nd century BCE. These relics were returned from Britain to India in 1952 and are venerated annually. Traditionally, Maudgalyāyana is associated with meditation and psychic powers, and some texts link him to the *Dharmaguptaka* school and Abhidharma lists, though scholarly attribution is debated.

## Terms
- **Arhat:** A fully enlightened being who has attained nirvana and will not be reborn.
- **Psychic powers (iddhi/ṛddhi):** Supernatural abilities like flight and mind-reading, attained through deep meditation.
- **Karma:** The law of moral cause and effect, where intentional actions determine future rebirths.
- **Merit transfer:** Dedicating positive karma from virtuous actions to benefit another being.
- **Ghost Festival:** An annual Buddhist festival in East Asia transferring merit to ancestors and hungry ghosts.
- **Ye Dharma Hetu stanza:** A verse summarizing dependent origination, widely inscribed in Buddhist art.
- **Schism:** A formal division in the monastic community, here caused by Devadatta.
- **Abhidharma:** The Buddhist canon section analyzing teachings and phenomena philosophically.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Historicity of the schism account:** Some Vinaya versions state the effort to reunite Devadatta's followers failed, which André Bareau considers historically authentic, citing the later persistence of Devadatta's sect.
- **Authorship of Abhidharma texts:** While traditions attribute works like the *Dharmaskandha* to Maudgalyāyana, scholars doubt this, though he likely compiled early mnemonic lists (*mātṛkā*) that formed their basis.
- **The "Ye Dharma Hetu" verse:** Its exact origin and impact are not fully understood; it is not directly attributed to the Buddha in canonical form, suggesting it may be Aśvajit's summary.
- **Nature of Maudgalyāyana's skin color:** The Pali Canon describes it as blue like a lotus, but some scholars interpret this as originally meaning dark skin.

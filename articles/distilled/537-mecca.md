# Mecca

## Overview
Mecca (officially Makkah al-Mukarramah) is a city in western Saudi Arabia regarded as the birthplace of the Prophet Muhammad and the holiest city in Islam. Its religious centrality derives from the Kaaba, a cuboid structure in the Great Mosque (Masjid al-Haram) believed to have been built by Abraham and Ishmael, which serves as the *qibla* (direction of prayer) for Muslims worldwide. The city’s history is defined by its role as a pilgrimage destination: the annual *Hajj* (obligatory once in a lifetime for able Muslims) and the year-round *Umrah* swell the population dramatically, driving an economy historically reliant on pilgrim services and trade. Since the Saudi conquest in 1925, massive infrastructure expansion—including the Abraj al-Bait clock tower complex and the Haramain High Speed Railway—has modernized the city, though this has entailed the demolition of an estimated 95% of historic structures. Non-Muslims are prohibited from entering the city.

## Body

### Etymology and Names
The name "Mecca" is an Anglicization; the Saudi government adopted **Makkah al-Mukarramah** ("Makkah the Honored") as the official transliteration in the 1980s. The Quran refers to the city as **Bakkah** (Surah 3:96), identified as the site of the first house of worship, and **Umm al-Qurā** ("Mother of all Settlements," 6:92). **Macoraba**, listed by Ptolemy (2nd century CE), is debated as a reference to Mecca; Glen Bowersock argues it derives from *Makkah* + Aramaic *rabb* (great), while Patricia Crone rejects the link. Islamic tradition associates **Fārān** with the Desert of Paran (Genesis 21:21), where Ishmael settled.

### Pre-Islamic History
Mecca’s early history is obscure due to sparse textual and archaeological sources. The valley was barren but strategically located at caravan crossroads with water from the Zamzam Well. By the 5th century, the Quraysh controlled the city, profiting from the 6th-century shift of spice trade from dangerous sea routes (Byzantine piracy, Sasanian disruption) to secure overland routes. Mecca surpassed Petra and Palmyra as a trading hub, dealing in leather, clarified butter, wool, and camels—often destined for the Roman army. The **Year of the Elephant** (c. 570–572) marks Abraha’s failed attempt to destroy the Kaaba and divert pilgrimage to his cathedral in San'aa; Islamic tradition holds the lead elephant refused to enter the city, and birds sent by God destroyed the army (Quran 105: Al-Fil).

### Muhammad and the Conquest of Mecca
Muhammad was born in Mecca c. 570 into the Banu Hashim clan of Quraysh. In 610, he received his first revelation in the Cave of Hira. After 13 years of persecution, he performed the *Hijrah* (emigration) to Medina in 622. Conflict ensued; the 627 Battle of the Trench saw Meccan confederates fail to defeat Muhammad. The 628 Treaty of al-Hudaybiya permitted Muslim pilgrimage the following year, but Quraysh allies violated it in 630. Muhammad marched on Mecca with 10,000 followers, conquered the city, destroyed pagan imagery, and rededicated the Kaaba to Allah, establishing *Hajj* as a Pillar of Islam. He returned to Medina, appointing a governor; Mecca became the spiritual center while political capitals shifted to Kufa, Damascus, and Baghdad.

### Medieval and Ottoman Eras
Mecca was never an imperial capital except briefly under the Zubayrids (683–692), who were besieged twice by Umayyads. In 930, Qarmatians sacked the city. The Ottomans gained suzerainty in 1517 via the Sharif of Mecca, who retained autonomy. The First Saudi State held Mecca 1803–1813, destroying tombs; Ottomans retook it via Muhammad Ali Pasha. Cholera struck pilgrims 27 times between 1830–1930. Ibn Battuta (c. 1327–1330) described a clean, elegant city sustained by alms, where the Kaaba had been rebuilt smaller than its pre-Islamic form.

### Modern Saudi Era
**Hashemite Revolt (1916):** Sharif Hussein, aided by T.E. Lawrence, captured Mecca, declaring the Kingdom of Hejaz. **Saudi Conquest (1924–25):** The Saud family overthrew the Sharifate, incorporating Mecca into Saudi Arabia. The Saudi government demolished historic sites (e.g., Ajyad Fortress, Khadijah’s house, Muhammad’s birthplace) citing prevention of *shirk* (associationism) and need for pilgrim infrastructure. **Grand Mosque Seizure (1979):** Juhayman al-Otaybi’s militants seized the mosque for two weeks; hundreds died in the retaking. **Infrastructure:** The city lacks an airport (served by Jeddah, 70 km away, and Taif). Highway 40 (Jeddah–Riyadh–Dammam) and Highway 15 (Medina–Jordan/Abha) intersect here. The Haramain High Speed Railway (2018) connects Mecca and Medina in under two hours at 300 km/h. The Al Masha'er Metro (2010) serves Hajj sites (Arafat, Muzdalifah, Mina).

### Significance in Islam
**Masjid al-Haram:** Encloses the Kaaba, the *qibla*. The **Mataf** is the circumambulation area. **Hajr-e-Aswad (Black Stone):** Set in the Kaaba’s eastern corner; believed a meteorite by scientists, divine by Muslims; turned black from humanity’s sins. **Maqam Ibrahim:** Stone bearing Abraham’s footprints (40x40x20 cm), housed in a golden enclosure. **Safa and Marwa:** Two hills; walking between them seven times (*sa'ee*) is a *rukn* (pillar) of Umrah. **Jabal al-Nour:** Mountain (642 m) containing the **Cave of Hira**, site of the first revelation. **Hajj/Umrah:** Hajj (12th month, Dhu al-Hijjah) drew 2.49 million in 2019; Umrah is non-obligatory but recommended. Pilgrims also visit Mina, Muzdalifah, and Mount Arafat.

### Geography and Climate
Mecca lies in a narrow valley (277 m / 909 ft elevation) 70 km inland from Jeddah, within the Hejaz mountain corridor ("Hollow of Mecca"). The metropolitan area exceeds 1,200 km². Climate is hot desert (BWh): winter 19–30°C, summer consistently >40°C, low humidity (30–40%). Rain falls Nov–Jan; flash floods historically common (89 recorded by 1965), now mitigated by dams.

### Water Management
Water scarcity defined pre-modern Mecca. Sources: brackish wells (Zamzam), rainwater cisterns, and the **Ayn Hunayn** aqueduct system. Zubayda (wife of Harun al-Rashid) funded a massive 1.75M dinar project (Abbasid era) building aqueducts from Ayn Hunayn and **Ayn Zubayda** on Mount Arafat. The system deteriorated; major repairs occurred in 1245, 1361, 1400, 1474, 1510. Suleiman the Magnificent rebuilt it entirely 1525–1571. Modern supply relies on desalination and treatment plants.

### Economy
Historically dependent on pilgrimage: taxes on pilgrims (increased during Great Depression, lasted until 1972), services (transport, lodging), and trade. Saudia airline derives 12% revenue from Hajj. City revenue >$100M; Saudi spending ~$50M on Hajj services. Modern economy is service-oriented; industries include textiles, furniture, utensils, and a new industrial city in Al Akashiya (45 km²). Jet travel accessibility caused 20th/21st-century population and infrastructure boom (freeways, malls, skyscrapers).

### Governance and Demographics
Governed by Mecca Regional Municipality (14 elected members, mayor appointed by Saudi government; Saleh Al-Turki as of Jan 2022). Province governed by Prince Khalid Al-Faisal since 2007. 2022 metro population: 2.4M (44.5% Saudi, 55.5% Muslim foreigners). Non-Muslims prohibited (loosely enforced; Ludovico di Varthema entered 1503, Burton 1853). Ahmadis considered non-Muslims. Mecca Province is the only Saudi province where expatriates outnumber citizens. Diverse population includes South/Southeast Asians, Africans, Europeans; Burmese community ~250,000.

### Human Resources
**Education:** Formal system began late Ottoman period. Madrasat al-Falah founded 1911–12. 2005: 532 male schools, 681 female schools. Medium: Arabic (English second language); some international schools use English. Sole university: Umm Al-Qura (college 1949, university 1981). **Healthcare:** Free for pilgrims. Ten main hospitals (e.g., King Faisal, King Abdullah Medical City, Ajyad) plus walk-in and temporary Hajj clinics.

### Culture and Communications
Culture shaped by global pilgrim influx; most diverse city in Muslim world. Sports: traditional wrestling/foot races; football now dominant (Al-Wehda FC, 1945; King Abdulaziz Stadium, 38k capacity). Landmarks: **Abraj al-Bait** (7 towers, central clock tower 601 m, 4th tallest globally, 5th largest floor area); **Mecca Gate** (Quran Gate, 1979, book-on-rehal design). **Press:** First press 1885 (Osman Nuri Pasha); *Al Qibla* (Hashemite gazette), *Umm al-Qurā* (Saudi gazette), *Al Nadwa* (city paper). **Telecom:** 20 phones (Hashemite) → 450 (1936, half the kingdom). Modern TV/radio since 1985. **Radio:** Network functional 1932; Hajj broadcasts from 1950 (Day of Arafah); Radio Makkah reached 450 kW.

### Transportation
**Air:** No active city airport. Served by King Abdulaziz International (Jeddah, Hajj Terminal: 47 planes, 3,800 pilgrims/hr) and Taif Regional. **Road:** Junction of Highway 40 and 15. Four congested ring roads; numerous tunnels. **Rail:** **Al Masha'er Metro** (18.1 km, 9 stations, Hajj-only, opened 2010). **Haramain HSR** (450 km, 35 trains, 60M pax/yr capacity, 300 km/h, operational 2018). **Planned:** Makkah Mass Rail Transit (4-line metro).

## Terms
- ****Hajj**** — The major Islamic pilgrimage to Mecca, obligatory once in a lifetime for able Muslims, performed in Dhu al-Hijjah.
- ****Umrah**** — The minor pilgrimage to Mecca, performable year-round; recommended but not obligatory.
- ****Kaaba**** — Cuboid structure in Masjid al-Haram believed built by Abraham/Ishmael; the *qibla* (prayer direction) for all Muslims.
- ****Qibla**** — The direction faced during Islamic prayer, oriented toward the Kaaba in Mecca.
- ****Hijrah**** — Muhammad’s emigration from Mecca to Medina in 622 CE, marking the start of the Islamic calendar.
- ****Zamzam Well**** — Sacred well in Mecca providing water since Abrahamic times; central to pre-Islamic and Islamic pilgrimage rites.
- ****Mataf**** — The open area surrounding the Kaaba where pilgrims perform *tawaf* (circumambulation).
- ****Hajr-e-Aswad**** — The Black Stone, set in the Kaaba’s eastern corner; believed by Muslims to be of divine origin, by scientists a meteorite.
- ****Maqam Ibrahim**** — Stone bearing the footprints of Abraham, used by him to build the upper Kaaba; housed in a golden enclosure.
- ****Shirk**** — The sin of associating partners with Allah; cited by Saudi authorities as justification for demolishing historic shrines/tombs.

## Debates and open questions
*   **Pre-Islamic Mecca’s prominence:** Patricia Crone and Tom Holland argue Mecca was not a major trading outpost; Glen Bowersock asserts it was a major hub. Crone later revised some views, suggesting trade focused on leather/hides for the Roman army.
*   **Ptolemy’s Macoraba:** Long identified as Mecca (Bowersock: *Makkah* + *rabb*); recently questioned (Crone: "nothing to do with Mecca"; suggests Ptolemy’s "Moka" in Arabia Petraea is the actual reference).
*   **Diodorus Siculus’s shrine (1st c. BCE):** Described as revered by all Arabians; location (Leuke Kome/Nabataea) disputes identification with the Kaaba.
*   **Early population:** Estimates for Muhammad’s era range from ~550 (Robinson, genealogical) to 10,000 (traditional scholars).
*   **Heritage destruction vs. capacity:** Saudi demolition of ~95% of historic buildings (post-1985) for hotels/infrastructure is contested by historians and heritage advocates.
*   **Non-Muslim entry ban:** Saudi law prohibits entry; enforcement is described as loose (notable historical violations: Varthema 1503, Burton 1853).

# Richard Nixon

## Overview
Richard Nixon (1913-1994) was the 37th U.S. President, serving from 1969 until his resignation in 1974. His presidency was marked by significant foreign policy achievements, including opening relations with China and pursuing détente with the Soviet Union, alongside major domestic initiatives. However, his legacy is permanently defined by the Watergate scandal, which led to his becoming the only U.S. president to resign from office.

## Body

### Early Life and Political Rise
Born into a poor Quaker family, Nixon graduated from Whittier College and Duke University Law School. After Navy service in WWII, he was elected to Congress in 1946, gaining national attention for his role in the Alger Hiss spy case. He won Senate and vice-presidential elections, serving two terms under Eisenhower. After losing the 1960 presidential race and a 1962 California gubernatorial race, he staged a political comeback to win the presidency in 1968.

### Foreign Policy: China, Vietnam, and Détente
Nixon's presidency fundamentally reshaped U.S. foreign policy. He pursued a strategy of "triangular diplomacy," using improved relations with the People's Republic of China to gain leverage over the Soviet Union. His 1972 visit to China ended decades of isolation. With the USSR, he achieved the Anti-Ballistic Missile Treaty and SALT I arms control agreements, inaugurating an era of "détente." In Vietnam, he implemented "Vietnamization" to withdraw U.S. troops while expanding the war into Cambodia and Laos. The Paris Peace Accords were signed in 1973, ending direct U.S. combat, though North Vietnam conquered the South in 1975.

### Domestic Policy and the Environment
Domestically, Nixon enacted a range of policies. He created the Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) and signed laws like the Clean Air Act and Endangered Species Act. He ended the military draft, oversaw the Apollo 11 Moon landing, and implemented the Twenty-sixth Amendment, lowering the voting age to 18. In 1971, he ended the dollar's direct convertibility to gold, effectively ending the Bretton Woods system, and imposed temporary wage and price controls to combat inflation.

### Watergate and Resignation
The Watergate scandal began with a 1972 break-in at the Democratic National Committee headquarters, which was linked to Nixon's re-election campaign. A cover-up ensued, involving the administration and the President. Revelations, including the existence of secret White House tapes, led to a constitutional crisis. Facing certain impeachment and removal from office after the release of the "Smoking Gun" tape, Nixon resigned on August 9, 1974. His successor, Gerald Ford, granted him a full pardon.

### Post-Presidency
After his resignation, Nixon worked to rehabilitate his image as an elder statesman. He wrote nine books, traveled extensively, and offered foreign policy advice to subsequent administrations. He died in 1994, with his funeral attended by five living presidents.

## Terms
- **Détente**: The easing of strained relations, especially in a political situation, notably between the U.S. and Soviet Union during the Cold War.
- **Vietnamization**: A U.S. policy during the Vietnam War aimed at "expand[ing], equip[ping], and train[ing] South Vietnamese forces and assign[ing] to them an ever-increasing combat role."
- **Triangular Diplomacy**: Nixon's foreign policy strategy of using improved relations with China to gain leverage over the Soviet Union.
- **Watergate**: A major political scandal involving the Nixon administration's attempts to cover up its involvement in the 1972 break-in at the Democratic National Committee headquarters.
- **Smoking Gun Tape**: A White House tape recording from June 23, 1972, which proved President Nixon had personally approved the cover-up of the Watergate break-in just days after it occurred.
- **Checkers Speech**: A nationally televised address by Nixon in 1952 to defend himself against allegations of financial improprieties, which saved his vice-presidential candidacy.
- **New Federalism**: Nixon's domestic policy philosophy aimed at devolving certain powers and responsibilities from the federal government to state and local governments.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Legacy Evaluation**: Historians debate whether Nixon's foreign policy achievements (China, détente) outweigh the domestic damage of Watergate and the Vietnam War's prolongation.
- **Southern Strategy**: Scholars argue over whether Nixon's political strategy to win over Southern white voters was primarily driven by racial politics or economic conservatism.
- **Environmental Record**: While Nixon created the EPA and signed major environmental laws, some historians contend his actions were driven more by political expediency than genuine environmental conviction.
- **Watergate Motivations**: The full extent of Nixon's personal involvement in and knowledge of the Watergate cover-up from its inception remains a subject of analysis, though the "Smoking Gun" tape provided conclusive evidence of his early role.

# Tiger Woods

## Overview
Tiger Woods is an American professional golfer widely regarded as one of the greatest of all time and one of the most famous athletes in modern history. His career is defined by unprecedented dominance, a series of historic comebacks from personal and physical adversity, and a massive impact on the sport's popularity and economics.

## Timeline
- **December 30, 1975** — Born in Cypress, California.
- **August 1996** — Turns professional at age 20.
- **April 13, 1997** — Wins first major, the Masters, by a record 12 strokes.
- **June 1997** — Reaches world No. 1 for the first time.
- **2000** — Wins U.S. Open by a record 15 strokes; completes the "Tiger Slam" (four consecutive major wins).
- **November 2009** — Announces hiatus from golf following a public infidelity scandal.
- **February 2021** — Hospitalized after a severe single-car crash in California.
- **April 14, 2019** — Wins the Masters, his first major in 11 years.
- **March 27, 2026** — Arrested for DUI following a car crash in Florida.

## Body

### Early Life and Amateur Career
Born Eldrick Tont Woods, he was introduced to golf before age two by his father, Earl, a retired U.S. Army officer and accomplished amateur golfer. A child prodigy, Woods won the Junior World Golf Championships six times and became the youngest U.S. Junior Amateur champion at age 15. He won three consecutive U.S. Junior Amateur titles and three consecutive U.S. Amateur titles, becoming the youngest winner of the latter. He attended Stanford University on a golf scholarship, winning the NCAA individual championship in 1996 before turning professional.

### Professional Dominance and the "Tiger Slam"
Woods turned professional in 1996 and quickly signed record-breaking endorsement deals. He won his first major, the 1997 Masters, by 12 strokes and became the fastest player to reach world No. 1. His peak performance came in 2000, when he won nine PGA Tour events, including the U.S. Open by a record 15 strokes. By winning the 2001 Masters, he became the only player to win four consecutive major professional golf titles (the "Tiger Slam"). He held the world No. 1 ranking for a record 264 consecutive weeks from 1999 to 2004.

### Comebacks, Injuries, and Later Career
Woods's career was marked by significant comebacks. After a slump in 2003-04, he returned to No. 1 in 2005. Following a self-imposed hiatus in 2009-10 due to personal issues, he fell to No. 58 in the rankings before returning to No. 1 in 2013. A series of four back surgeries between 2014 and 2017 caused him to drop out of the top 1,000. He staged a remarkable comeback, winning the 2018 Tour Championship and the 2019 Masters—his first major in 11 years. A severe 2021 car crash caused compound leg fractures, and he later stated his full-time career was over. In 2026, he was involved in another car crash and arrested for DUI.

### Records, Endorsements, and Impact
Woods holds numerous records, including the most weeks at world No. 1, 82 PGA Tour wins (tied with Sam Snead), and 15 major championships (second only to Jack Nicklaus's 18). He is one of six men to complete the career Grand Slam. His marketability made him the world's highest-paid athlete for a record 11 years. His dominance led to the strategy of "Tiger-proofing" golf courses by adding yardage to reduce the advantage of long hitters. He has been honored with the Presidential Medal of Freedom and induction into the World Golf Hall of Fame.

## Terms
- **Major Championships:** The four most prestigious annual tournaments in men's professional golf: The Masters, U.S. Open, The Open Championship, and PGA Championship.
- **Career Grand Slam:** Winning all four major championships at least once during a player's career.
- **Tiger Slam:** The feat of winning four consecutive major championships, achieved by Woods across the 2000 U.S. Open and 2001 Masters.
- **Official World Golf Ranking (OWGR):** A system for ranking the competitive performance of male professional golfers.
- **PGA Tour:** The main organizer of professional golf tours in the United States and North America.
- **Tiger-proofing:** The practice of lengthening golf courses to reduce the advantage of exceptionally long hitters like Woods.
- **DUI:** Driving Under the Influence, a criminal offense for operating a vehicle while impaired by alcohol or drugs.

## Debates and Open Questions
- **Greatest of All Time:** While widely considered one of the greatest, debates persist comparing his career to Jack Nicklaus's, particularly regarding major championship totals (Woods: 15, Nicklaus: 18).
- **Impact on Competitors:** Studies suggest other top golfers scored worse when competing directly against Woods, a psychological effect attributed to his "superstar" status.
- **Future of Career:** Following his 2026 arrest and statement about seeking treatment, Woods's future participation in professional golf is uncertain.

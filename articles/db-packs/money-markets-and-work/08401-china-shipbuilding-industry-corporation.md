# China Shipbuilding Industry Corporation

China Shipbuilding Industry Corporation (CSIC) was a Chinese state-owned shipbuilding conglomerate that existed from 1999 to 2019. Three facts frame it: CSIC was one half of a deliberate north–south split of Chinese shipbuilding, it served both military and civilian markets, and its short independent life ended in a merger driven largely by corruption.

## Origins and structure

CSIC was created on 1 July 1999 by the Government of the People's Republic of China, from companies spun off from the China State Shipbuilding Corporation (CSSC). It was 100% owned by the State-owned Assets Supervision and Administration Commission (SASAC) of the State Council. The same State Council initiative split China's top five Defence and Technology Corporations into ten new enterprises, all large state-owned enterprises under direct State Council supervision.

CSIC and CSSC are easily confused. CSIC (中国船舶重工股份有限公司) is distinct from the older China Shipbuilding Corporation and from the contemporary CSSC.

The 1999 split divided Chinese shipbuilding by region: CSSC retained the east and south, while CSIC took the north and west. CSIC's footprint included 96 enterprises across cities such as Dalian, Tianjin, Qingdao, Wuhan, Xi'an, Chongqing, and Kunming, plus 30 research institutes and ten laboratories. It employed over 300,000 people. Its major shipbuilding arm was the Dalian Shipbuilding Industry Company, and the group was headquartered in Beijing's Haidian District.

## Business and products

CSIC operated 10 main product areas: shipbuilding, marine engineering, diesel engines, storage batteries, large steel structure fabrications, port machinery, turbochargers, tobacco machinery, gas meters, and automation distribution systems. Beyond ships, its scope covered managing state-owned assets, domestic and overseas investment and financing, military product research and production (mainly warships), design, production, and repair of civil vessels, marine equipment, non-ship products, overseas turnkey project contracting, labour export, and various forms of economic and technological cooperation. A subsidiary, China Shipbuilding Industry Company Limited (CSICL), was listed on the Shanghai Stock Exchange in 2008 under ticker SSE: 601989 and was a CSI 300 component. The group's trade arm was China Shipbuilding & Offshore International Co. Ltd (CSOC). Hu Wenming (胡问鸣) served as Chairman.

## Merger with CSSC

On 26 November 2019, CSIC merged back with CSSC to form a new China State Shipbuilding Corporation. The combined entity became the world's largest shipbuilder, holding roughly 20% of the global market and around US$110 billion in assets. The merger was driven substantially by corruption within CSIC's structure, notably the investigation of its former chairman. After the merger, CSIC ceased to exist as an independent entity; its successor is China State Shipbuilding Corporation Limited.

## US restrictions

In November 2020, US President Donald Trump issued an executive order prohibiting any American company or individual from owning shares in companies the United States Department of Defense had listed as linked to the People's Liberation Army, a list that included CSIC. In December 2020, the United States Department of Commerce added 25 research institutes affiliated with CSIC to the Bureau of Industry and Security's Entity List, citing their role in territorial disputes in the South China Sea.

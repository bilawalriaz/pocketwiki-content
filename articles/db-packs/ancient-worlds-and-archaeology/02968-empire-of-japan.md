# Empire of Japan

The Empire of Japan (大日本帝国) was the period of Japanese history from the Meiji Restoration in 1868 to the enactment of the post-war constitution in 1947. In less than eighty years, Japan moved from a feudal society to a modern industrial and military power, waged aggressive expansion across Asia and the Pacific, and was defeated in World War II.

## The Modernizing Phase (1868–1912)

The Empire began with the Boshin War (1868–1869), a civil war that destroyed the Tokugawa shogunate and restored direct imperial rule under Emperor Meiji. The new government modernized under the slogan "Enrich the Country, Strengthen the Armed Forces" (*fukoku kyōhei*): it built industries, copied Western laws and government, issued the 1889 Meiji Constitution, and abolished the feudal class system. The First Sino-Japanese War (1894–1895) ended with the Treaty of Shimonoseki, which gave Japan Taiwan and confirmed it as a regional power. The Russo-Japanese War (1904–1905) then delivered influence in Korea and Manchuria, and Japan formally annexed Korea in August 1910.

The Tokugawa shogunate was the hereditary military government that had ruled Japan in the emperor's name since 1603. Its overthrow returned real political power to the Imperial Court.

## The Drift Toward Militarism (1912–1937)

The Taishō era (1912–1926) allowed a brief flowering of party politics, often called "Taishō Democracy," but the military kept an independent power base. After World War I, Japan seized former German territories in the Pacific. Economic shocks in the 1920s, including the Great Depression, fed ultranationalism and militarism. From 1931, the military, acting without civilian control, invaded Manchuria and set up the puppet state of Manchukuo, which caused Japan to leave the League of Nations in 1933. A full-scale invasion of China began with the Marco Polo Bridge Incident in July 1937.

Militarism here means political choices driven by military leaders rather than elected civilians; the civilian government still existed, but lost the ability to restrain the armed forces.

## World War II and Defeat (1937–1945)

Faced with a United States oil embargo, Japan attacked Pearl Harbor on 7 December 1941, drawing the U.S. into the war. Rapid conquests in Southeast Asia and the Pacific followed. The tide turned at the Battle of Midway in June 1942 and the Guadalcanal campaign of 1942–1943; from 1944, Allied forces advanced through an island-hopping campaign. In 1945, firebombing of Japanese cities, the atomic bombings of Hiroshima and Nagasaki on 6 and 9 August, and the Soviet invasion of Manchuria forced surrender on 2 September 1945 under the terms of the Potsdam Declaration, the Allied ultimatum demanding unconditional surrender.

## The End of the Empire

After surrender, Allied forces, led by U.S. General Douglas MacArthur, occupied Japan. They dissolved the Imperial Japanese Armed Forces and enacted a pacifist constitution that came into force on 3 May 1947, formally ending the Empire and leaving the emperor as a symbolic figurehead. The Treaty of San Francisco (1951) officially ended the occupation and the state of war.

## Open Debates

Historians still disagree about how to classify the Empire in the 1930s and 1940s. Because Japan had no single dictator and no mass fascist party, some prefer "militarism," "authoritarianism," "police state," or "para-fascism" to "fascist" or "totalitarian." The role of Emperor Hirohito, whether figurehead or active war leader, remains unresolved.

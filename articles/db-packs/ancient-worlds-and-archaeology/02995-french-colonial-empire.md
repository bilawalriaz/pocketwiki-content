# French colonial empire

The French colonial empire was a global system of overseas territories, protectorates, and mandates under French rule from the sixteenth century to the mid-twentieth century. It had two phases: a first empire destroyed by the Napoleonic Wars, and a second empire built from 1830 that became the world's second-largest colonial empire by 1914. Its stated purpose was the *mission civilisatrice*, the belief that France had a duty to spread its language, culture, and republican values. In practice, the empire ran on economic exploitation, forced assimilation, and large-scale violence.

## Two empires, two shapes

The first empire grew from the early 1600s. Its North American centre was New France, built on the fur trade and alliances with Indigenous peoples, with Port Royal (1605) in Acadia and Quebec (1608) as founding settlements. The Caribbean colony of Saint-Domingue (later Haiti) became the world's richest sugar colony, powered by enslaved African labour. France also ran trading posts in Senegal, Madagascar, and India, tied to the slave trade and plantation agriculture. The empire was thinly populated compared to British America and was destroyed by repeated wars with Britain. The Seven Years' War (1756–1763) cost France nearly all of New France and its Indian outposts, the Haitian Revolution (1791–1804) cost it its most profitable colony, the 1803 sale of Louisiana to the United States erased the North American centre, and the Napoleonic Wars left only a few remnants.

The second empire began with the invasion of Algiers in 1830, a brutal conquest whose Algerian death toll historians estimate at 500,000 to 1,000,000. Under Napoleon III (1852–1870), France expanded into Indochina (Vietnam, Cambodia, Laos) and parts of Africa. After the Franco-Prussian War (1870–1871), the Third Republic joined the Scramble for Africa, creating federations such as French West Africa and French Equatorial Africa. By 1914, the empire stretched across Africa, the Middle East (Syria, Lebanon), the Pacific, and Southeast Asia, with French Indochina formally established in 1887 and Morocco a protectorate from 1912.

## How the empire worked on the ground

The official *mission civilisatrice* concealed a rigid hierarchy. A small European settler population, the *pieds-noirs*, largest in Algeria, held political and economic power, while the majority of colonised people were legal subjects with limited rights. A small Westernised elite, the *évolués*, received French-style education intended to assimilate them but which often produced the intellectuals who later led independence movements. A *protectorate* was a softer form of rule: a local ruler stayed in place, but France controlled foreign policy and key administration, as in Morocco and much of Indochina. Whether French policy aimed at full assimilation or at ruling through local structures is debated; both approaches appear in different colonies and periods.

## Decolonisation and what survived

Defeat in World War II weakened the empire's hold. France fought and lost two long wars: in Indochina (1946–1954), ending at Điện Biên Phủ, and in Algeria (1954–1962), a war so traumatic it toppled the Fourth Republic. Most other African colonies followed in 1960, the Year of Africa. The 1946 French Union, a framework meant to replace empire, was quietly abandoned. Critics argue the empire never fully closed: through the CFA franc (a currency shared by fourteen African countries and long pegged to the French franc, then the euro), military interventions, and the network of political and economic ties called *Françafrique*, France continued to exert neocolonial influence. A few territories, including New Caledonia and Mayotte, remained French as overseas departments or collectivities, and the Francophonie, the worldwide community of French-speaking states, remains the most visible institutional legacy of four centuries of rule.

## Timeline

- 1605 — Port Royal founded in Acadia, the seed of the first empire in North America.
- 1763 — Seven Years' War ends; France loses most of New France and Indian outposts to Britain.
- 1804 — Haiti becomes independent after a slave revolt, costing France its richest colony.
- 1830 — Invasion of Algiers begins the second empire.
- 1887 — French Indochina formally established.
- 1912 — France establishes a protectorate over Morocco.
- 1946 — The French Union nominally replaces the colonial empire.
- 1954 — French defeat at Điện Biên Phủ forces withdrawal from Indochina.
- 1960 — Most remaining African colonies gain independence in the Year of Africa.
- 1962 — Algeria gains independence, effectively ending the French empire.

# Western Roman Empire

The Western Roman Empire was the western half of the Roman Empire when it was administered from a separate imperial court, conventionally from AD 395 until 476. Its collapse marked the political transition from the classical world to the Middle Ages in Western Europe, though contemporaries saw it as one empire with two courts rather than two states.

## How the Split Happened

The empire was too large for one ruler to govern effectively. In 286, Emperor Diocletian responded by creating the **Tetrarchy**: two senior emperors (*Augusti*) and two junior heirs (*Caesars*), each controlling a portion of the territory. The Tetrarchy later broke down, but the east-west division of administration stuck.

The split became permanent in 395, when Emperor Theodosius I died and left the Western Empire to his young son Honorius and the Eastern Empire to his son Arcadius. A *Praetorian Prefecture* was a major administrative region governed by a *Praetorian Prefect*, the emperor's chief minister for that region. Although two courts now existed, Romans on both sides still considered the whole a single polity.

## Why the West Fell

The Western Empire was undone by external invasions, loss of revenue, and internal instability.

**Barbarian pressure.** Many foreign groups, especially Germanic peoples, were settled inside the empire as *foederati*, allied peoples granted land in exchange for military service. Over time they grew semi-independent and destabilising. The most damaging blows were:

- 410: Visigoths under Alaric sacked Rome, the first time the city had fallen to a foreign enemy in roughly 800 years.
- 439: Vandals seized the wealthy province of Africa, capturing Carthage and cutting off grain shipments and tax income that had long supported the Western government and the city of Rome itself.

**Internal decay.** The Western court suffered a run of weak emperors, while powerful Germanic generals behind the throne made and unmade rulers. Ricimer dominated the court from 461 to 472, installing and deposing puppet emperors. A *magister militum* was a high-ranking military commander, often of barbarian origin, who became the de facto power while the emperor was a figurehead.

**The final act.** In 476, the Germanic chieftain Odoacer deposed the last Western emperor, Romulus Augustulus. The Eastern emperor Zeno refused to treat this as the end of the Western court, recognising the exiled Julius Nepos as legitimate until Nepos was murdered in 480. Whether 476 or 480 marks the "true" end remains debated, because the East never formally accepted that the Western throne had simply ceased to exist.

## The Aftermath

After 476, Germanic kingdoms such as the Ostrogoths in Italy and the Visigoths in Hispania preserved Roman administrative systems and law rather than destroying them. Roman institutions therefore continued under new rulers.

The Eastern Empire briefly reversed some of these losses. Between 533 and 554, Justinian I's generals reconquered North Africa, Italy, and parts of Spain. Most of these gains were later lost to the Lombard invasion of Italy and the Islamic conquests. The Exarchate of Ravenna, a Byzantine province combining civil and military authority, was one of the few Western footholds the East held for a time.

The idea of a Roman Empire in the West was revived on Christmas Day 800, when Pope Leo III crowned Charlemagne "Roman Emperor." This coronation established the precedent of *translatio imperii*, the transfer of imperial authority to a new realm, which eventually evolved into the Holy Roman Empire.

## What Lasted

The Western Empire's deepest legacy is institutional and linguistic. Latin spread across the western provinces and evolved into the Romance languages. Roman law remained the foundation of European legal tradition. The Catholic Church, headquartered in Rome, outlived the empire and preserved much of its administrative culture. Historians still debate whether the transition to the Middle Ages was a gradual transformation or a sharp break, but the political structure of imperial Rome in the West did not survive past 476.

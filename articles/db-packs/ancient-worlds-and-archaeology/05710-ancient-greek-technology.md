# Ancient Greek technology

Ancient Greek technology developed during the 5th century BC and continued through the Roman period and beyond. Greek innovations included the gear, screw, rotary mills, bronze casting techniques, water clock, water organ, torsion catapult, steam-driven experimental devices, and a chart for finding prime numbers. Many of these inventions appeared late in the period, often driven by the need to improve weapons and tactics in war. Peaceful uses are evident in the early development of the watermill, which the Romans later scaled up. Their technical work rested on advanced mathematics and surveying, and much of it survives because philosophers such as Archimedes and Heron published the results.

## Water and public health

Greek water engineering covered groundwater extraction, aqueducts, stormwater and wastewater sewers, flood control, drainage, fountains, baths, sanitary facilities, and recreational water features. A well-preserved example is the drainage system on the Anatolian west coast, whose masonry outlet was shaped to make the structure self-cleaning. This shows the Greeks understood the link between hygienic conditions and public health, and that the outlet was one element in a wider network of underground drainage and water supply.

## Mining at Laurium

The silver mines at Laurium were a major source of wealth that helped Athens grow as a city-state. Ore was cut from underground galleries, washed, and smelted into metal. Washing tables at the site used rainwater stored in cisterns during the winter. Metal from the mines was converted into coinage, helping to create a monetary economy. Tunnels reached depths of about 330 feet and were worked by slaves using picks and iron hammers. Ore was lifted in small skips hauled by a rope, sometimes guided by a wheel set against the rim of the shaft.

## War as a driver of invention

A torsion catapult is a siege weapon that stores energy in twisted ropes, and it shows how Greek engineering absorbed military pressure. The same culture that built self-cleaning drains and quiet watermills also produced the catapult and other weapons, and many of the most notable inventions appeared late in the period, after military pressure had already pushed engineers toward better tools. Mathematics and surveying sat behind both halves of this record.

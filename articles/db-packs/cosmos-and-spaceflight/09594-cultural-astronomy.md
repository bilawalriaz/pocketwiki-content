# Cultural astronomy

Cultural astronomy investigates the many ways human cultures, ancient and modern, perceive celestial objects and integrate them into their view of the world. It is an interdisciplinary field that studies the astronomies of current and past societies rather than the physical nature of celestial objects.

## Origins

Cultural astronomy developed from the merger of two earlier disciplines:

- **Archaeoastronomy** studies the use of astronomy and its role in ancient cultures and civilizations.
- **Ethnoastronomy** merges astronomy, textual scholarship, ethnology, and the interpretation of ancient iconography to reconstruct lifeways, astronomical techniques, and rituals.

Both fields remain closely allied within cultural astronomy.

## Related fields

Cultural astronomy overlaps with several neighbouring disciplines:

- **Historical astronomy** analyses historical astronomical data.
- **History of astronomy** traces the evolution of astronomy as a discipline.
- **History of astrology** investigates the relationship between astrology and astronomy.

Because the field is interdisciplinary, projects often draw on archaeology, linguistics, anthropology, and the history of science alongside astronomy.

## Examples

The traditions studied under this umbrella include Ancient Greek, medieval Islamic, Australian Aboriginal, Babylonian, Chinese, Egyptian, Hebrew, Indian, Maya, Inuit, Persian, Serbian folk, and Tibetan astronomy, as well as the cosmology and creation traditions of the Nakh peoples.

## Publication

Research appears in the *Journal of Astronomy in Culture*, established in 2016 by the International Society for Archaeoastronomy and Astronomy in Culture (ISAAC).

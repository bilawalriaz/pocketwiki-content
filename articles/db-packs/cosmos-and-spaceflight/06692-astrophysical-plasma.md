# Astrophysical plasma

Astrophysical plasma is plasma outside the Solar System. It is the partially ionized, low-density gas that fills the space between stars and between galaxies, and by current accounts it accounts for the great majority of the ordinary (baryonic) matter in the universe. It is distinguished from "space plasma," a term usually reserved for the Sun, the solar wind, and the ionospheres and magnetospheres of planets.

A plasma forms when matter is heated enough to ionize, splitting neutral atoms into negatively charged electrons and positively charged ions. Because the particles carry charge, they couple to local electric and magnetic fields. In astrophysical settings those fields range from the strong fields generated by stars to the weak fields threading star-forming regions, the interstellar medium, and the intergalactic medium.

## Gas versus plasma in astronomy

Gas and plasma are distinct states of matter, but astronomers often use "gas" loosely for any astrophysical fluid, neutral or ionized. The term "ionized gas" specifically refers to plasma.

## How it is observed

Plasmas inside stars can both generate and interact with magnetic fields, producing dynamic behaviour sometimes detected in spectra through the Zeeman effect, in which a magnetic field splits a spectral line into components. In cooler, diffuse plasmas such as the interstellar and intergalactic media and the solar wind, any pre-existing magnetic field is weak and must be inferred indirectly, through polarimetry of background starlight or radio sources. Electric fields are also reported in some stellar phenomena.

## Common environments

Four diffuse-plasma settings dominate discussion: the intergalactic medium between galaxies, the interstellar medium within galaxies, the interplanetary medium between planets, and the solar wind streaming from the Sun.

## Related phenomena and laboratory links

Active galactic nuclei are of interest because the plasmas there may be directly related to those studied in laboratories. Many observed behaviours, including turbulence and plasma instabilities, are described by magnetohydrodynamics, the theory that treats a plasma as a conducting fluid coupled to a magnetic field. In Big Bang cosmology the entire universe was a plasma before recombination, when electrons and ions combined into neutral atoms and the plasma became transparent to light.

## An early prediction

The Norwegian physicist and Arctic explorer Kristian Birkeland argued in 1913 that space must be permeated by charged particles. He suggested that each stellar system continually ejects ions and electrons into space, so that "the whole of space is filled with electrons and flying electric ions of all kinds," and that most of the mass of the universe resides in this intervening medium rather than in the stars themselves, a conclusion consistent with the modern view that galaxies are embedded in a vast, low-density plasma.

A 2023 American Astronomical Society white paper titled "Just Say Plasma" notes that in popular accounts the Sun is still often called a "mass of incandescent gas." A 1959 children's song and its later cover by They Might Be Giants repeated the phrase, but the same group's later album paired it with a corrected sequel describing the Sun as a miasma of incandescent plasma, reflecting the current view that the Sun, like most visible matter in the cosmos, is a plasma.

# Moon landing

A Moon landing is the arrival of a crewed or robotic spacecraft on the lunar surface. It is among the hardest routine destinations in spaceflight because a vehicle must first escape Earth's gravity, then slow from orbital speed to a gentle touchdown, and often return. The feat was first accomplished in 1969 and remains rare: in more than half a century, only a handful of nations and a few private firms have done it.

## The central idea: a controlled fall from orbit

Landing on the Moon is, mechanically, a controlled cancellation of orbital speed. A spacecraft in lunar orbit is moving fast enough that gravity bends its path into a circle. To reach the surface it must lose most of that speed, and a "soft" landing means arriving at near-zero vertical speed. This is done with retrorockets, rocket engines fired against the direction of travel that push the craft downward and backward and so bleed off velocity. A "hard" landing is simply an impact at orbital speeds, used for early probes that carried no need to survive.

The second constraint is mass. A crewed mission must carry not only astronauts and life support but also the fuel to lift off the Moon and return home. That return requirement shaped the Apollo architecture: rather than sending one large vehicle from Earth to the surface and back, NASA split the job between a command ship that stayed in lunar orbit and a separate Lunar Module that carried two astronauts down and back up. Rendezvousing in lunar orbit proved dramatically more fuel-efficient than carrying the whole ship to the surface, and lunar orbit rendezvous became the standard profile.

## Cold War origins

The first lunar attempts were political products. The Space Race, the Cold War competition between the United States and the Soviet Union, treated each space first as a measure of technological and ideological strength. The Soviet Union opened the contest with Sputnik in 1957, struck the Moon with Luna 2 in 1959, and soft-landed Luna 9 in 1966. These Soviet firsts jolted the United States into creating NASA in 1958 and committing, under President Kennedy in 1961, to land a crew on the Moon before the decade was out.

The U.S. program that resulted, Apollo, was a strategic project as much as a scientific one. Between July 1969 and December 1972, six Apollo missions carried twelve astronauts to the lunar surface, each leaving a third crew member in lunar orbit. Apollo 8 (1968) and Apollo 10 (1969) had already rehearsed the orbital mechanics and landing approach without touching down, which is why Apollo 11's 20 July 1969 landing worked on its first descent attempt. Apollo 17, on 14 December 1972, remains the last time humans have walked on the Moon.

The Soviet Union never flew a crewed lunar landing. Its parallel Zond program, intended for crewed circumlunar loops, suffered repeated failures and was cancelled. Soviet secrecy compounded the picture: missions were numbered publicly only after they succeeded, which made their early lead look smoother in retrospect than it was.

## A long pause and a new wave

After Apollo, crewed lunar flight stopped. The 37-year gap that followed is usually attributed to the program's cost and to the absence of any political motive comparable to beating the Soviets.

Robotic landings resumed and accelerated. China ended the soft-landing hiatus with Chang'e 3 in 2013, became the first to land on the lunar far side with Chang'e 4 in 2019, and has since conducted two sample-return flights. India touched down near the south pole with Chandrayaan-3 in 2023, and Japan followed with SLIM in January 2024, becoming the fifth nation to soft-land on the Moon. In February 2024, Intuitive Machines' Odysseus became the first successful commercial lunar lander, and in March 2025 Firefly Aerospace's Blue Ghost completed the first fully successful commercial landing, marking the transition of lunar access from superpowers to private companies.

The far side and the south pole are now the priority targets. The lunar far side, which never faces Earth, requires relay satellites for communications and offers radio quietness useful for astronomy. The south pole, where Chandrayaan-3 and Blue Ghost have landed, contains permanently shadowed craters whose floors may hold water ice, the most valuable resource for any future long-duration presence because ice can be split into hydrogen and oxygen for breathing air and rocket propellant.

## Lunar orbit and the surprises it held

Operating in lunar orbit was not a simple step along the way. In the mid-1960s, lunar orbiters from both superpowers discovered mascons, regions of denser rock beneath the lunar maria whose extra gravity tug pulls low orbits forward and causes them to decay unpredictably. Mascons complicate any mission that needs to loiter in orbit and were a real engineering worry for Apollo, which is why modern uncrewed missions in low lunar orbit plan for frequent station-keeping maneuvers.

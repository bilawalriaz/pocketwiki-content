# Extremal black hole

An extremal black hole is a black hole carrying the minimum mass compatible with its electric charge and angular momentum. For an ordinary black hole, mass, charge, and spin are independent; lowering the mass while holding charge and spin fixed eventually reaches a floor where further reduction would violate energy conditions. At that floor, surface gravity vanishes, Hawking temperature drops to zero, and the hole radiates nothing.

## Defining properties

Three features distinguish an extremal black hole from a non-extremal one. First, zero Hawking temperature: because Hawking radiation is thermal emission tied to surface gravity, vanishing surface gravity means no thermal emission at all. Second, in supersymmetric theories (theories that pair every particle with a heavier "superpartner"), extremal black holes are themselves supersymmetric, meaning they remain invariant under several supercharges. This follows from the BPS bound, an inequality that limits the energy of a configuration in terms of its charges and is saturated when supersymmetry is preserved. Third, their entropy can be computed using string theory, a framework that replaces point particles with tiny vibrating strings. No extremal black hole has been observed in nature, so these remain theoretical constructs.

## The third-law debate

A long-standing question is whether any physical process can produce an extremal black hole. One proposal, the "third law of black hole thermodynamics," says no: forming one would require a process with a finite number of steps that nevertheless drives surface gravity to zero, which is thought to violate the weak energy condition (the requirement that energy density, as measured by any observer, never be negative). Werner Israel published a proof of this in 1986. More recent work, including a 2024 result by Kehle and Unger, argues that Israel's proof contains an error and that extremal black holes can in fact form. The third law in this setting has always been controversial, and the question remains open.

## Near-extremal black holes

A near-extremal black hole has a mass only slightly above the extremal minimum for its charge and spin. Its horizon structure is simple, which makes it a convenient testing ground for black-hole calculations. Theoretical work is usually done by treating the non-extremal contribution as a small perturbation, with an expansion parameter called non-extremality. In supersymmetric theories, a near-extremal black hole is a small perturbation of a supersymmetric one, carries a correspondingly small Hawking temperature, and emits only a tiny amount of radiation. Its entropy can be calculated in string theory much as for the extremal case, at least to first order in non-extremality. The simplicity of near-extremal geometry, combined with tractable thermodynamics, is why so much of black-hole research, including recent work on the statistical mechanics of near-extremal black holes by Iliesiu and Turiaci (2021), uses these objects as a starting point.

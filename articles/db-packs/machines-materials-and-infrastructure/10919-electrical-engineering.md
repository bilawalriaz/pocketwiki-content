# Electrical engineering

Electrical engineering is the discipline that studies, designs, and applies equipment and systems using electricity, electronics, and electromagnetism. Its two original purposes still organise the field: power engineering moves electrical energy from generators to users, while telecommunications, signal processing, and electronics move information.

The discipline emerged as a profession in the second half of the 19th century, once the electric telegraph, telephone, and electrical power systems began to be commercialised. Universities created dedicated departments: Technische Universität Darmstadt founded the first in 1882 and the first degree course in 1883, Cornell produced the first US graduates in 1885, and University College London created the first British chair in 1885. The 1893 Chicago conference standardised the volt, ampere, ohm, coulomb, farad, and henry, giving the new industry shared units.

## How the technology developed

| Year | Advance | Why it mattered |
|---|---|---|
| 1820 | Ørsted shows current deflects a compass | Electricity and magnetism are linked |
| 1831 | Faraday discovers electromagnetic induction | Moving magnets can generate current, enabling generators and transformers |
| 1827 | Ohm's law | Quantifies current, voltage, and resistance |
| 1873 | Maxwell's *Electricity and Magnetism* | Unifies electricity, magnetism, and light as electromagnetic waves |
| 1882 | Edison's 110 V DC network in Manhattan | First large-scale public power system |
| 1880s–90s | AC transformers and induction motors (Tesla, Ferraris, Dolivo-Dobrovolsky) | Efficient long-distance power transmission |
| 1888 | Hertz transmits and detects radio waves | Confirms Maxwell, enables wireless |
| 1901 | Marconi's transatlantic signal | Wireless communication across the ocean |
| 1947 | Point-contact transistor at Bell Labs | Semiconductor replaces vacuum tubes |
| 1958–59 | Integrated circuit (Kilby, Noyce) | Many components on one chip |
| 1959 | MOSFET (Atalla, Kahng) | Compact, mass-producible transistor |
| 1971 | Intel 4004 microprocessor | A computer CPU on a single chip |

The shift from vacuum tubes to transistors, and then to integrated circuits built around the MOSFET, is the main reason modern electronics is small, cheap, and ubiquitous. Gordon Moore observed in 1965 that transistor counts on a chip were doubling roughly every two years, and that scaling has driven the entire digital era.

## Subfields

Power and energy engineering covers generation, transmission, and distribution. The power grid connects many generators to users through transformers, high-voltage lines, and power electronics. Off-grid systems serve remote loads.

Telecommunications engineering moves information across channels such as coax, optical fibre, or free space. Information is shifted onto a carrier frequency through modulation, most often amplitude or frequency modulation, then recovered at the receiver. Designers balance signal power against noise, since a weak signal is corrupted by static before it can be decoded.

Control engineering models dynamic systems and designs controllers to make them behave as desired. Feedback is the central idea: in cruise control, the vehicle's speed is measured and the result fed back to adjust the engine. The same approach runs industrial automation, aircraft, and robots.

Electronic engineering designs circuits from components such as resistors, capacitors, inductors, diodes, and transistors. Before integrated circuits, circuits were built from discrete parts; afterwards, billions of transistors fit on a chip the size of a coin. The field grew out of radio engineering after the Second World War.

Microelectronics pushes fabrication toward smaller scales. Silicon is the standard material, with compound semiconductors such as gallium arsenide used at higher frequencies. Sub-100 nm features became standard around 2002.

Signal processing analyses and manipulates signals. Analog processing amplifies and filters; digital processing compresses, detects, and corrects errors. Digital signal processors are now embedded in televisions, phones, audio gear, and radar.

Instrumentation engineering designs sensors and measurement devices for quantities like pressure, flow, and temperature, often serving as the input stage of a larger control system.

Computer engineering covers the design of computing hardware and its integration with software, including the embedded processors now found in cars, appliances, and consumer devices.

Photonics and optics deal with electromagnetic radiation at optical frequencies. Fibre-optic communication, the dominant backbone of long-distance information transfer, uses infrared light in the 1530–1565 nm C band.

## Education, licensing, and professional bodies

Most countries require a four- or five-year bachelor's degree covering physics, mathematics, and the breadth of electrical engineering. A master's or doctorate follows for research or specialist work. After graduation, engineers in many jurisdictions must satisfy experience requirements to become licensed as a Professional Engineer, Chartered Engineer, or European Engineer.

The two largest professional bodies are the IEEE, with over 360,000 members, and the IET, with over 150,000. The International Electrotechnical Commission (IEC) sets global standards, while the National Society of Professional Engineers (NSPE) represents the profession in the United States.

## Tools and working life

The basic instruments are the voltmeter, which measures voltage, and the oscilloscope, which displays changing signals. Spectrum and network analysers serve high-frequency work. Most design today uses computer-aided design software.

Electrical engineers work in power stations, fabrication plants, consulting offices, mines, and ships, and the work combines technical analysis with project management, budgeting, and client communication. Around 0.25% of the labour force in the United States, Australia, and Canada are electrical engineers.

## Relationship with other fields

Electrical engineering overlaps with electronic engineering, computer engineering, and mechanical engineering through mechatronics, the design of combined electrical and mechanical systems, and through microelectromechanical systems (MEMS), which sense, actuate, and communicate at microscopic scales. Machine learning is now applied across smart grids, adaptive control, and electronic design automation, drawing on techniques first developed for signal processing.

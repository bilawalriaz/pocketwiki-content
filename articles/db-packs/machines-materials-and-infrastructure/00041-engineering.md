# Engineering

Engineering is the systematic application of natural science and mathematics to design and improve systems, devices, and processes that solve problems under constraints. Driven by human needs, the field produces creations ranging from bridges and engines to pacemakers, spacecraft, and the internet. Engineers balance competing demands (safety, performance, cost, regulations, time) within fundamental limits such as the laws of physics. The traditional disciplines are civil, mechanical, electrical, and chemical, and the field has evolved from ancient simple machines into a scientific profession.

## Etymology and early history

The word "engineering" derives from Latin *ingenium*, "innate quality, especially mental power" (c. 1250). "Engineer" dates to the 14th century as *engine'er*, a constructor of military engines such as catapults; this obsolete usage survives in bodies like the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers. When civilian construction matured as a technical discipline, "civil engineering" distinguished non-military work from military engineering.

The six classic simple machines were known in the ancient Near East: wedge and inclined plane since prehistoric times; the wheel in Mesopotamia during the 5th millennium BC; the lever roughly 5,000 years ago; the pulley in early 2nd-millennium Mesopotamia and Twelfth Dynasty Egypt; and the screw, last invented, in Neo-Assyrian Mesopotamia (911–609 BC). Imhotep, official of Pharaoh Djosèr, is the earliest named civil engineer, credited with the Step Pyramid at Saqqara (c. 2630–2611 BC). Greek contributions include the Antikythera mechanism, an early mechanical analog computer, and Archimedes' inventions; both relied on differential or epicyclic gearing, principles central to modern gear trains, robotics, and automotive engineering.

## Industrial revolution

Classical Newtonian mechanics formed the scientific basis of much modern engineering. John Smeaton, the first self-proclaimed civil engineer, designed bridges, canals, harbors, and lighthouses; his water-wheel experiments introduced iron axles and gears, and his third Eddystone Lighthouse (1755–59) pioneered hydraulic lime. Steam power developed through Papin's piston experiments (1707), Savery's "Miner's Friend" (1698), and Newcomen's commercial piston engine (1712). Cort's puddling process (1784) mass-produced wrought iron, and Neilson's hot blast (1828) cut smelting fuel needs. High-pressure steam engines enabled steamboats and locomotives. Wilkinson's boring machine, the first machine tool, enabled precision cylinder boring; interchangeable parts led to large-scale factory production by the late 19th century.

## New disciplines

The 1850 US Census first listed "engineer" with 2,000 counted; fewer than 50 US engineering graduates existed before 1865. Electrical engineering grew from Volta, Faraday, Ohm, the telegraph (1816), the electric motor (1872), and Maxwell/Hertz theory. Chemical engineering emerged by 1880 around large-scale chemical plant design. Materials science evolved from metallurgy, advanced by Space Race alloys and semiconductors. Aeronautical engineering traces to Sir George Cayley (work dated to the 1790s); World War I military aircraft drove extensive development shortly after the Wright brothers.

## Methodology and practice

Engineers identify constraints (laws of physics and chemistry, resources, cost, safety, marketability) to derive specifications. Designs are validated through prototypes, scale models, simulations, and stress tests, typically including a factor of safety. Engineers bear responsibility for public safety, embodied in Cicero's Creed: *salus populi suprema lex esto* ("the health of the people shall be the supreme law"). Forensic engineering studies failed products to prevent recurrence. Computers pervade practice through CAD, CAE (including finite element analysis), CAM, and EDA, collectively known as product lifecycle management (PLM).

## Social context and ethics

Projects depend on funding from companies, investors, or governments. Codes of ethics, such as the NSPE's, demand honesty, impartiality, and dedication to public welfare; Canadian engineers wear the Iron Ring as a reminder of this duty. NGOs like Engineers Without Borders apply engineering to disaster relief and development.

## Relationships with other disciplines

Scientists study the world as it is; engineers create what has never been. Engineering applies science but uses semi-empirical methods like parameter variation when problems are too complex for exact solution. Medicine and engineering intersect through artificial organs, brain implants, and pacemakers; viewing the body as a biological machine inspired AI, neural networks, and robotics, creating biomedical engineering. Business engineering combines engineering, IT, and change management.

## Key terms

- **Simple machines**: Wedge, inclined plane, lever, wheel and axle, pulley, screw.
- **Factor of safety**: Margin in designs to reduce unexpected failure.
- **Forensic engineering**: Study of failed products to find causes and prevent recurrence.
- **Puddling process**: Henry Cort's 1784 method for large-scale wrought iron.

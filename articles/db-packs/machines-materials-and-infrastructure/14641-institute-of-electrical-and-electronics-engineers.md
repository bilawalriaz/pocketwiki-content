# Institute of Electrical and Electronics Engineers

The Institute of Electrical and Electronics Engineers (IEEE, pronounced "I-triple-E") is an American 501(c)(3) charitable professional organization for electrical engineering, electronics engineering, and related disciplines. It is a global network of more than 486,000 STEM professionals whose stated purpose is to foster technological innovation for the benefit of humanity. Its corporate office is at 3 Park Avenue, New York City, and its operations center is in Piscataway, New Jersey, opened in 1975. Revenue in 2024 was US$646.4 million.

## Origins and merger

IEEE traces its lineage to 1884 and the American Institute of Electrical Engineers (AIEE). A rival body, the Institute of Radio Engineers (IRE), formed in 1912. Although AIEE was initially larger, IRE attracted more students and overtook it by the mid-1950s. The two merged on January 1, 1963 to form IEEE. AIEE and IRE are unrelated to the British Institution of Electrical Engineers (now the IET) or to Industrial Electronic Engineers.

## What IEEE does

IEEE works through three levers: industry standards, conferences, and publications. It claims to produce over 30% of the world's literature in its fields, publishing roughly 200 peer-reviewed journals and more than 1,700 conference proceedings each year. Subscribers access this content through IEEE Xplore, the organization's digital library platform. IEEE also maintains its own paper format and an editorial style guide based on The Chicago Manual of Style.

Work is organized through 39 technical societies, each focused on a knowledge area such as power and energy, signal processing, robotics, computer engineering, photonics, and communications. The societies run specialized publications, conferences, and networking. Members are grouped into 10 geographic regions divided into local sections. A planned realignment takes effect January 1, 2028: Regions 1 and 2 merge into a single Region 2, and a new Region 11 is carved out of Region 10.

## Support bodies

The IEEE Foundation, established in 1973 and incorporated separately, supports education, innovation, and recognition. Its board must consist of active IEEE members, with one third drawn from current or former IEEE directors. By the end of 2014 the Foundation held about $45 million in assets, split between unrestricted and donor-designated funds. The IEEE Global History Network, founded in 2008, now redirects to the Engineering and Technology History Wiki. Membership Geographic Activities (MGA) handles recruitment, retention, and member services through tools like vTools.

## Controversies

In May 2019 IEEE restricted Huawei employees from peer reviewing papers or serving as editors, citing "severe legal implications" of U.S. sanctions. Chinese researchers protested, with some canceling memberships. After U.S. government clearance, IEEE lifted the restrictions on June 3, 2019. Huawei employees had remained free to vote in standards work and attend standards meetings throughout.

In 2022 IEEE Spectrum published a Q&A titled "A Russian Perspective on the War in Ukraine." An open letter argued the piece echoed Russian propaganda, and on April 6, 2022 the editors appended an apology for "not providing adequate context at the time of publication" without revising the article itself.

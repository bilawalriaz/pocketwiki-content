# Lordosis behavior

Lordosis behavior, also called mammalian lordosis or presenting, is the natural female mating posture seen in most mammals, including rodents, elephants, and cats. The name comes from Greek *lordōsis*, "bent backward." During lordosis the forelimbs lower, the rear limbs extend, the hips rise, the spine arches ventrally (toward the abdomen), and the tail lifts or moves sideways. The posture appears during estrus ("heat," the fertile window), during copulation itself, and in some species including the cat during pre-copulatory courtship. By tilting the pelvis forward and raising the hips, lordosis makes penile penetration easier.

## Spinal reflex and sensory trigger

The movement is a spinal reflex hardwired in the lumbar and sacral segments (L1, L2, L5, L6, and S1). When a male mounts, tactile input from the female's flanks, perineum, and rump travels through sensory nerves into the spinal cord and lower brainstem, where it combines with descending signals. The integrated output travels through motor nerves to the longissimus and transverso-spinalis muscles, whose contraction produces the ventral arching of the spine.

## Brain modulation and balance

The vestibular nuclei and cerebellum, via the vestibular tract, supply balance information so the posture stays coordinated with overall body position. More importantly, the ventromedial nucleus of the hypothalamus (VMN) normally inhibits the reflex; without that brake the reflex would fire constantly. The VMN signal is relayed through the periaqueductal gray and the medullary reticular formation, descending the reticulospinal tract to the spinal lordosis circuits at L1–L6.

## Hormonal gating

Sexual behavior is timed to reproductive readiness through estrogen. During the breeding season, when an ovum is available, estradiol simultaneously induces ovulation and estrus. Acting on neurons of the VMN, the periaqueductal gray, and other regions, estradiol lifts the inhibition and induces sexual receptivity. With the reflex disinhibited, flank and rump touch from a mounting male is enough to elicit lordosis.

The control has been mapped through several experimental manipulations. Lesioning the VMN abolishes lordosis, confirming its essential role. Ovariectomy removes the estrogen source and reduces the behavior; injections of estradiol benzoate and progesterone restore it. Stress during puberty suppresses the hypothalamic-pituitary-gonadal (HPG) axis, the hormone-signaling chain linking the hypothalamus, pituitary, and gonads, lowering gonadal hormone levels during a sensitive window and reducing adult lordosis responsiveness.

## In humans

Reflexive lordosis has not been observed in humans, and there is no equivalent obligatory posture for receptivity; arching seen in people being mounted from behind is a conscious choice to expose the vulva or anus. A 2017 study using 3D models and eye-tracking found that a slight forward tilt of a woman's hips increases perceived attractiveness and draws the gaze of both men and women, and the authors proposed that residual lumbar curvature may be a vestigial signal of proceptivity or receptivity, even though human receptivity is neither reflexive nor passive. Anthropologist Helen Fisher suggested that high-heeled footwear mimics lordosis by tilting the pelvis and arching the back, which may help explain why such shoes are rated sexually attractive. Subsequent studies have supported the perception of receptivity when women arch the back in supine (lying on the back) or quadruped (on hands and knees) positions, and have shown that other women can read that posture as a possible threat to a romantic relationship.

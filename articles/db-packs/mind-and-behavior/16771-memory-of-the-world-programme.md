# Memory of the World Programme

UNESCO's Memory of the World (MoW) Programme is an international initiative that recognises documentary heritage of global importance. It aims to safeguard manuscripts, recordings, films, and other archival items against collective amnesia, neglect, physical decay, climate damage, and deliberate destruction, while improving public access and awareness. The programme began in 1992, with first inscriptions on its International Register in 1997 and 570 items inscribed by April 2025.

## Governance and inscription process

The International Advisory Committee (IAC), a 14-member body appointed by UNESCO's Director-General, reviews nominations and recommends inscriptions to UNESCO's Executive Board. Any organisation or individual can nominate an item, but only through a UNESCO Member State's National Commission (or the equivalent government body). Up to two nominations per state are considered per cycle; joint nominations from multiple states have no limit. The IAC meets in odd-numbered years and runs four sub-committees covering Bureau operations, preservation, register assessment, and education and research, supported by a UNESCO Secretariat.

## Regional distributionThe 570 inscriptions (April 2025) break down by region as follows: Europe and North America 315, Asia and the Pacific 180, Latin America and the Caribbean 83, Africa 41, Arab States 24, and International Organizations 10. The leading nominating countries are Germany (33), the United Kingdom (27), the Netherlands and France (26 each), South Korea (21), Poland (19), and China (18). Autonomous national and regional committees maintain parallel registers for heritage of national or regional importance, with established registers in Australia, Austria, Brazil, Canada, Finland, Mexico, the Netherlands, New Zealand, Norway, the Philippines, and the UK, plus regional registers for Asia-Pacific and Latin America–Caribbean; an African regional register is being established.

## The Jikji Prize

Established in 2004 with South Korea, the biannual US$30,000 Jikji Prize recognises institutions that advance preservation and access. It commemorates the Jikji, the earliest known book printed with movable metal type (1377), inscribed on the Register in 2001. Recipients have included national archives, libraries, and archive-development programmes in the Czech Republic, Austria, Malaysia, Australia, Mexico, Mali, Cambodia, Egypt, and Indonesia.

## Audiovisual heritage

UNESCO's concern for film loss led to the 1980 Recommendation for the Safeguarding and Preservation of Moving Images, which documented that by the mid-1990s more than three-quarters of the earliest film production had been lost. Three periods of large-scale loss are linked to the destruction of early cinema, the silent-to-sound transition, and the shift from nitrate to acetate stock. This produced the programme's first list, *Memory of the World: National Cinematic Heritage* (1995), a survey of representative films from 50 countries.

The programme is not without controversy. In the 2015 cycle, inscription became contested in East Asia, where states viewed registry as implicit approval of disputed historical narratives, particularly regarding the Nanjing Massacre and "comfort women."

# Brain-specific homeobox

Brain-specific homeobox (Bsx) is a protein encoded in humans by the **BSX** gene. It is a transcription factor, meaning a protein that binds DNA and switches other genes on or off, and it acts mainly in the brain.

## What kind of protein it is

Bsx belongs to the **ANTP class of homeodomain proteins**. A *homeodomain* is a folded stretch of about 60 amino acids that recognises and binds specific DNA sequences. The ANTP class is named after the *Antennapedia* gene in fruit flies, the founding member of the homeobox family. Bsx is evolutionarily highly conserved, so its sequence has been preserved across distant species, which suggests an essential, ancient function. As a transcription factor, it localises to the nucleus and controls which genes a cell reads.

## Where it is found

In mouse, Bsx is expressed in four brain regions:

- the **septum of the telencephalon**, a forebrain relay for emotion and memory,
- the **pineal gland**, a small endocrine organ that releases melatonin,
- the **mammillary bodies**, hypothalamic structures linked to memory, and
- the **arcuate nucleus** of the hypothalamus, a hub for appetite control.

Human RNA data match this pattern, with expression in the hypothalamus, epithalamus (which contains the pineal gland), and septal regions, plus low-level signal in gonadal and kidney tissues.

## What it does

**Feeding behaviour.** In the arcuate nucleus, Bsx keeps two *orexigenic* (appetite-stimulating) neuropeptides, **agouti-related peptide (AgRP)** and **neuropeptide Y (NPY)**, at normal levels. Mice lacking Bsx develop hyperphagia (overeating) and altered locomotion, tying the factor to energy-balance circuits.

**Pineal photoreceptors and the clock.** In the clawed frog *Xenopus*, Bsx expression in the pineal gland follows the **circadian rhythm** and drives differentiation of photoreceptor cells. A daily timing signal switches Bsx on, and Bsx in turn helps the pineal become a light-sensing organ.

**Pineal development and brain asymmetry in zebrafish.** Zebrafish need Bsx to produce all major cell types of the pineal complex, including melatonin-releasing **pinealocytes**, photoreceptors, and a small group of **parapineal cells** that migrate to the left side of the brain. These left-migrating cells establish left–right differences in the zebrafish brain, so Bsx indirectly contributes to **brain asymmetry**.

Together, these findings show Bsx as a conserved transcription factor acting in small, specialised neuronal and neuroendocrine populations, where it helps establish their identity and, in the hypothalamus, their output onto appetite and movement circuits.

## Gene and protein data

The BSX gene sits on human chromosome 11 (band 11q24.1) and on mouse chromosome 9 (band 9 A5.1). The protein shuttles between cytoplasm and nucleus and is part of a transcription-regulator complex. Database entries are UniProt Q3C1V8 (human) and Q810B3 (mouse).

# Behavior

Behavior is the range of actions an organism, individual, system, or artificial entity performs in response to internal or external stimuli. Responses can be conscious or subconscious, overt or covert, voluntary or involuntary. Some behavior is driven by the environment (extrinsic motivation); other behavior arises from within the organism, sometimes described as agency or free will.

## Biological behavior

A broad biological definition treats behavior as a response to an event or environmental change across an individual's lifetime, similar to phenotypic plasticity. Unlike biochemical or physiological changes, which happen quickly, behavior unfolds over longer periods and excludes purely developmental change (ontogeny). Behavior is any action that changes the organism's relationship to its environment, producing outputs that feed back into the surroundings.

Behaviors are shaped by genetics, learning, or both. The more complex the nervous system, the larger the role of learning, though even in mammals genetics determines a substantial fraction of behavior. Prairie voles are monogamous while meadow voles are promiscuous, a difference largely controlled by the gene *Avpr1a*, which encodes a receptor for vasopressin.

Animal behavior is studied in two overlapping traditions. Ethology examines behavior under natural conditions, treating it as an evolutionarily adaptive trait. Behaviorism studies measured responses to stimuli or trained behaviors in the laboratory, with less emphasis on evolutionary function. Human behavior is influenced by the nervous system and the endocrine system, and greater neural complexity tends to correlate with greater behavioral complexity and learning capacity.

## Social and organizational behavior

Social behavior occurs when members of the same species affect one another through interaction, often modeled as an exchange in which giving carries the expectation of return. It emerges from the interaction of individual traits and situational factors, so any act reflects both the organism and its environment.

In management, behavior follows an antecedents–behavior–consequences chain. Antecedents are the stimuli that trigger an action; the behavior follows; consequences, either rewards or punishments, reinforce or suppress it. Managers specify a desired outcome, but recurring behavioral patterns often dictate what actually happens.

## Behavior informatics

Behavior informatics, also called behavior computing, builds computational theories and tools to model, represent, analyze, and manage the behavior of individuals, groups, and organizations. It represents behavior as a vector of actor, operation, interactions, and their properties. It differs from applied behavior analysis, which approaches behavior from psychology, by treating behavior as data that can be mined and reasoned about quantitatively and qualitatively.

## Health behavior

Health behavior is the set of beliefs and actions that affect a person's well-being, shaped by individual choices and by social, cultural, and physical environments. It is an early indicator of population health because disease often lags behind behavior by years.

Seven lifestyle features are linked to lower long-term morbidity and higher subsequent survival: avoiding snacks, eating breakfast regularly, exercising regularly, maintaining a desirable body weight, moderate alcohol intake, not smoking, and sleeping 7–8 hours per night. Smoking, heavy alcohol use, drug use, poor diet, gaps in primary care, and low screening uptake are significant drivers of poor health.

Two intervention models target these behaviors. The health belief model raises perceived susceptibility to negative outcomes, highlights their severity, and emphasizes benefits while minimizing perceived barriers. The theory of planned behavior uses persuasive messages to shape behavioral, normative, and control beliefs, building intentions that, combined with self-efficacy from past mastery, translate into action.

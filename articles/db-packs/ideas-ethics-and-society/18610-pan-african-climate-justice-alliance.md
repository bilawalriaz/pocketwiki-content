# Pan African Climate Justice Alliance

The Pan African Climate Justice Alliance (PACJA) is a coalition of more than 1,000 civil society organisations across 48 African countries, headquartered in Nairobi, Kenya. Co-founded in 2008 by climate activists Augustine B. Njamnshi and Mithika Mwenda, it is a people-centred consortium of NGOs, grassroots groups, trusts, foundations, indigenous communities, farmers' associations, community-based organisations, and religious bodies.

A short working definition: climate justice frames global warming as an ethical and political problem, not only an environmental one. It asks who is most harmed, who is most responsible, and who should pay and decide.

## Mission and structure

PACJA's objectives run along a single throughline: poverty reduction and equity for Africa in international climate negotiations. It works by making information available, sharing strategy, engaging African governments and stakeholders, and defending fairness in the global climate dialogue. Six practical focus areas are climate justice, environmental justice, sustainable development, community empowerment, policy influence, and research, development, and education.

In June 2023, Executive Director Mithika Mwenda summarised climate justice around three dimensions that structure much of PACJA's advocacy: distributive justice (a fair sharing of climate burdens and benefits), retributive justice (accountability for harm), and procedural justice (meaningful inclusion of affected people in decisions).

A 2017 episode captures PACJA's operating method. Dozens of national and community groups, including Greenpeace Mauritius, the Somali Climate Change Network, the Human Rights and Legal Aid Network of Sudan, Journalists for Climate Change in Nigeria, and Young Volunteers for Environment Zambia, signed a PACJA-organised petition against EU and French interference in the Africa Renewable Energy Initiative, whose secretariat is housed at the African Development Bank. The action reflects a consistent stance: climate and energy decisions in Africa should be made by African civil society in partnership with African governments, with external funders supporting rather than steering the process.

## Influence and external position

PACJA urges African organisations and the international community to implement the Paris Agreement and advance the 2030 Agenda, and presses governments to take responsibility for environmental and social justice. It carries African development expectations into high-level international summits such as COP28 and COP29, calling for financial and technical assistance rather than externally imposed solutions. Within Africa it plays a coordinating role, helping the continent move toward fair climate and environmental policies.

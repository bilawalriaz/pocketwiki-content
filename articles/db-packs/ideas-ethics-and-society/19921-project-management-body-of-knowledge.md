# Project Management Body of Knowledge

The Project Management Body of Knowledge (PMBOK) is a curated set of standard terminology and guidelines for managing projects. It is published as *A Guide to the Project Management Body of Knowledge* (the "PMBOK Guide") by the Project Management Institute (PMI), the organisation that administers the Certified Associate in Project Management (CAPM) and Project Management Professional (PMP) certifications. The eighth edition was released in 2025.

The PMBOK Guide overlaps with general management (planning, organising, staffing, executing, controlling) and draws supporting ideas from financial forecasting, organisational behaviour, budgeting, and management science. Techniques unique to project management sit at its core, including the **critical path method** (a scheduling technique that identifies the longest chain of dependent tasks and therefore the shortest possible project duration) and the **work breakdown structure** (a hierarchical decomposition of total project scope into smaller work packages). Earlier editions were recognised as standards by the American National Standards Institute (ANSI/PMI 99-001-2008) and the Institute of Electrical and Electronics Engineers (IEEE 1490–2011).

## Editions and evolution

| Year | Edition | Notable change |
|------|---------|----------------|
| 1996 | First | Evolved from PMI's 1983 "Ethics, Standards, and Accreditation Committee Final Report" white paper. |
| 2000 | Second | Added material reflecting practice growth. |
| 2004 | Third | Shifted inclusion criteria from "generally accepted" to "generally recognized as good practice." |
| 2008 | Fourth | Routine update. |
| 2013 | Fifth | Routine update. |
| 2017 | Sixth | First inclusion of agile practices (alongside an Agile Practice Guide). |
| 2021 | Seventh | Replaced the ten knowledge areas with twelve principles of project management; broadened agile coverage. |
| 2025 | Eighth | Refreshed terminology; introduced six principles for effective project management and seven performance domains integrating technical ways of working; redefined a project as a "temporary initiative in a unique context undertaken to create value." |

The eighth edition states that "all projects are investments" and adds three investment-related techniques to its Glossary: the **value breakdown structure (VBS)**, **critical path drag**, and **critical path drag cost**. Drag is the duration by which a critical-path activity delays the project end date, and drag cost translates that delay into monetary impact.

## Purpose and scope

The PMBOK Guide presents itself as a subset of the broader project management body of knowledge, restricted to practices "generally recognized" as applying to most projects most of the time and treated as "good practice" because applying the described skills, tools, and techniques tends to raise the chance of success. Some currently fashionable practices therefore do not appear in the latest edition, and the guide deliberately lags consultant-driven trends.

## Process-based structure

The PMBOK Guide describes work as accomplished by processes that take inputs (documents, plans, designs), apply tools and techniques, and produce outputs. Processes overlap and interact throughout a project or its phases, in a manner consistent with ISO 9000 and CMMI. The guide recognises 49 processes, organised as a matrix of five process groups and ten knowledge areas.

### Five process groups

- **Initiating**: define a new project or phase and obtain authorisation to start.
- **Planning**: establish scope, refine objectives, and define the course of action.
- **Executing**: complete the work defined in the project management plan to satisfy specifications.
- **Monitoring and Controlling**: track, review, and regulate progress and performance; identify needed changes and initiate them.
- **Closing**: finalise all activities across process groups to formally close the project or phase.

### Ten knowledge areas

1. **Project Integration Management** — identify, define, combine, unify, and coordinate processes across groups.
2. **Project Scope Management** — ensure the project includes all and only the work required.
3. **Project Schedule Management** — manage timely completion (renamed from "Project Time Management" in the sixth edition).
4. **Project Cost Management** — plan, estimate, budget, finance, fund, manage, and control costs.
5. **Project Quality Management** — determine quality policies, objectives, and responsibilities.
6. **Project Resource Management** — organise, manage, and lead the project team (renamed from "Project Human Resource Management" in the sixth edition).
7. **Project Communications Management** — plan, create, distribute, store, retrieve, manage, and dispose of project information.
8. **Project Risk Management** — plan, identify, analyse, plan responses, and control risk.
9. **Project Procurement Management** — purchase or acquire products, services, or results from outside the team.
10. **Project Stakeholder Engagement** — identify impacted people and organisations, analyse expectations, and develop engagement strategies.

Each process belongs to exactly one knowledge area and exactly one process group, forming a 10-by-5 matrix.

## Extensions and criticism

Three official extensions tailor the core guide to particular sectors: the **Software Extension**, the **Construction Extension**, and the **Government Extension**.

The **critical chain** school (Eliyahu M. Goldratt, Lawrence P. Leach) focuses on resource constraints and buffer management rather than the deterministic activity sequencing of the critical path method; the PMBOK does list critical chain as an alternative within Project Time Management. **Lean Construction** (Koskela, Howell) argues that the PMBOK model lacks two-way communication and proposes a language/action perspective combined with continual improvement of the planning process.

Process groups run across the project life cycle while knowledge areas supply the discipline-specific content that populates them.

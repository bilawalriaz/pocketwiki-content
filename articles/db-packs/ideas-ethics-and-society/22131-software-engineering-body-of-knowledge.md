# Software Engineering Body of Knowledge

The Software Engineering Body of Knowledge (SWEBOK, pronounced SWEE-bok) is the collective knowledge, skills, techniques, methodologies, best practices, and experiences accumulated within software engineering over decades. It is published as the *Guide to the Software Engineering Body of Knowledge* (the SWEBOK Guide) by the IEEE Computer Society, which makes it freely available. The guide is a joint standard of the International Organization for Standardization (ISO) and the International Electrotechnical Commission (IEC), originally recognised as ISO/IEC TR 19759:2005 and revised as ISO/IEC TR 19759:2015.

The guide organises the field into numbered *knowledge areas* (KAs), the core topics every practitioner is expected to master. A separate list of *related disciplines* tracks subjects that overlap with software engineering but are not defined inside SWEBOK itself.

## Evolution of the knowledge areas

Each edition reflects the discipline's expansion by adding knowledge areas to the prior set.

| Edition | Year | Knowledge areas |
|---|---|---|
| SWEBOK 2004 | 2004 | 10 |
| SWEBOK V3 | 2013 | 15 |
| SWEBOK V4 | 2024 | 18 |

The 2004 edition defined ten knowledge areas: software requirements, software design, software construction, software testing, software maintenance, software configuration management, software engineering management, software engineering process, software engineering tools and methods, and software quality. Its related disciplines were computer engineering, computer science, management, mathematics, project management, quality management, software ergonomics, and systems engineering.

SWEBOK V3 was approved for publication in late 2013. It added five new knowledge areas to the 2004 set: software architecture, software engineering operations, software engineering models and methods, software engineering professional practice, and software security. It also added computing foundations, mathematical foundations, and engineering foundations. Its recognised related disciplines were computer engineering, systems engineering, project management, quality management, general management, computer science, and mathematics.

SWEBOK V4 was published in October 2024 after an evolution effort the IEEE Computer Society started in 2016. It adds software engineering economics to the V3 set, bringing the total to eighteen knowledge areas. The related disciplines expanded to business analysis, computer engineering, computer science, cybersecurity, data science, general management, information systems and information technology, mathematics, project management, quality management, and systems engineering.

## Relationship to related disciplines

SWEBOK defines the knowledge a practitioner should have after roughly four years of practice and lists adjacent disciplines it acknowledges but does not cover. The Computing Curriculum Software Engineering (CCSE), officially Software Engineering 2004 (SE2004), is a similar effort aimed at academia. CCSE drew on SWEBOK 2004 as a source but defines what an undergraduate software engineering student should know at graduation, including the mathematics and general engineering principles SWEBOK leaves to other disciplines. SWEBOK V3 was designed in part to bridge these intersections.

Other professional bodies maintain their own bodies of knowledge: the Project Management Body of Knowledge (PMBOK), the Enterprise Architecture Body of Knowledge (EABOK), the Systems Engineering Body of Knowledge (SEBOK), the Automation Body of Knowledge (ABOK), and the Data Management Body of Knowledge (DMBOK). The standard is maintained through ISO/IEC JTC 1/SC 7, the subcommittee responsible for software and systems engineering standards.

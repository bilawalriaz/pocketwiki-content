# Rejection of evolution by religious groups

For roughly 150 years, a minority of religious believers have rejected evolution by natural selection, which the scientific community accepts as fact. The conflict is universally treated as religious rather than scientific, and creationist alternatives are classified as pseudoscience. The dispute now centers almost entirely on classroom policy in the United States, with smaller parallel conflicts in parts of Judaism and Islam.

## Core positions

Evolution means descent with modification by natural selection. It became the explanatory framework of biology in the late 19th century, replacing the older view that species are fixed products of divine creation. Creationism is the belief that a divine creator produced kinds of organisms, usually rejecting or restricting evolutionary descent.

Scientific organizations worldwide treat creation science and intelligent design as pseudoscience because they invoke supernatural causes and cannot be falsified by observation. Mainline Christian churches, including the Catholic, Anglican, and most Lutheran bodies, have long accepted evolution. The Catholic Church holds no official rejection; Pope Francis stated in 2014 that evolution is "not inconsistent with the notion of creation." Gregor Mendel, an Augustinian friar, founded genetics, supplying the inheritance mechanism that later merged with Darwinism into the modern evolutionary synthesis.

## Main creationist viewpoints

Young-Earth creationism holds that Earth and universe were created roughly 6,000 years ago, the Ussher chronology derived from biblical genealogies, in six literal 24-hour days, with humans coexisting with dinosaurs. It is often built on flood geology, the claim that a global flood explains most sedimentary rock and the fossil order.

Old-Earth creationism accepts the scientific age of Earth and universe but rejects key evolutionary mechanisms, reading Genesis non-literally through day-age, gap, or progressive creation frameworks.

Neo-creationism, or intelligent design, repackages the claim as a "scientific" argument from an unnamed intelligent designer, avoiding explicit biblical language. In Kitzmiller v. Dover (2005), a U.S. federal court ruled that intelligent design is not science but a religious view in disguise.

Theistic evolution holds that God works through evolution. It is the most common accommodation among mainstream clergy and many evangelical scientists, and treats Genesis as literary framework rather than literal history. Agnostic and materialistic evolution accept the science while remaining neutral or skeptical about any divine role.

## Key historical milestones

In 1859 Darwin's *On the Origin of Species* made evolution scientifically credible, and most scientists had accepted it by around 1875. In 1925 Tennessee's Butler Act banned teaching evolution, producing the Scopes "Monkey Trial." The U.S. Supreme Court struck down anti-evolution statutes in 1968 (Epperson v. Arkansas), ruling them an unconstitutional establishment of religion. Subsequent rulings in 1975, 1982, 1987, and 2005 each rejected rebranded forms of creationism, equal-time laws, and intelligent design. Each legal loss prompted creationists to strip explicit biblical language and reframe the claim, from creation science in the 1960s to intelligent design in the 1990s, without producing new scientific evidence.

## Disputes over scientific evidence

Common descent is universally accepted in biology. Human and chimpanzee coding DNA are about 99.4 percent identical, and hominid fossils document human ancestry back at least 3.2 million years. Creationists often accept variation within "kinds," called baramin, usually at the family level, while rejecting trans-kind common ancestry. They dispute genetic and fossil evidence by treating similarities as common design rather than common descent.

Transitional fossils document stepwise changes such as whale evolution, including *Ambulocetus*, a three-metre land-and-water predator. Because fossilization is rare, the record always retains gaps, but each discovered transition narrows them.

Radiometric dating relies on the constancy of decay rates. Only three technical exceptions to constant decay exist, and none affect the methods used on rocks. Earth is about 4.5 billion years old; the universe about 13.8 billion years old.

Falsifiability, the principle that scientific claims must be testable, was articulated by philosopher Karl Popper. A Precambrian rabbit fossil would refute evolution; no equivalent test exists for creation science. Courts have ruled creation science "simply not science" on this basis.

## Distinguishing science from religion

Science operates by methodological naturalism, the convention that observable events are explained by natural causes without asserting or denying the supernatural. Science never claims evolution disproves God, only that it does not need supernatural explanations. Some scientists and clergy frame science and religion as separate domains of inquiry, sometimes called non-overlapping magisteria, while creationist groups reject this separation and treat evolution as an attack on faith.

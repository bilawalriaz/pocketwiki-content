# Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy

The Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy (SEP) is a free online reference work in philosophy, published and maintained by Stanford University. It serves as an encyclopedia of philosophical topics and as a venue for peer-reviewed original publication, with entries that are revised over time rather than fixed in a single edition.

## What it is and who runs it

The SEP is produced by the Metaphysics Research Lab, part of Stanford's Center for the Study of Language and Information. Philosopher Edward N. Zalta created it in 1995 and serves as principal editor alongside Uri Nodelman. The site is hosted at plato.stanford.edu and is identified by ISSN 1095-5054. As of August 5, 2022, it contained 1,774 published entries.

## How entries are produced and kept current

Each entry is written and maintained by a specialist in the field, most often a professor from an academic institution worldwide. Authors are selected by an editor or editorial committee competent in the subject area, and the encyclopedia applies the peer-review standards used by academic journals. Authors grant Stanford the right to publish their articles but retain the copyright themselves, which allows the SEP to recruit working scholars while keeping every article openly accessible.

## Dynamic updates and rival views

Zalta designed the SEP from the start as a dynamic reference work: the original author revises an entry over time rather than letting it freeze at publication. The charter also permits rival articles on a single topic, so reasoned disagreements among philosophers appear side by side instead of being collapsed into one voice.

## Funding and open access

The SEP was initially developed with U.S. public funding from the National Endowment for the Humanities (NEH) and the National Science Foundation. To preserve free access after that startup phase, a long-term fundraising plan is run in collaboration with the Scholarly Publishing and Academic Resources Coalition (SPARC), the International Coalition of Library Consortia (ICOLC), and the Southeastern Library Network (SOLINET). Many university libraries and library consortia contribute under this plan, with matching funds from the NEH.

## The logo

The SEP logo renders the letters "SEP" in the shape of Rodin's sculpture *The Thinker*. An original cast of the sculpture is co-owned by Stanford and appears across the university's wider iconography.

# Migration

Migration denotes the systematic relocation of entities—organisms, people, data, celestial bodies, or physical particles—from one environment, system, or state to another. The term appears across disciplines with a shared core: directed movement that alters the entity's context, often requiring adaptation or integration.

## Human and social migration

Human migration involves physical movement across regions or political boundaries. International migration specifically crosses state borders with a minimum duration of stay, reshaping demographics, economies, and cultures. Internal migration moves people within a country, often from rural to urban areas. Forced migration includes refugees fleeing conflict, persecution, or environmental disaster; voluntary migration seeks economic opportunity, education, or family reunification. Remittances from migrants often exceed formal aid flows to developing nations. Migration policies—visas, asylum systems, border controls—mediate who moves, where, and under what conditions.

## Biological migration

Biological migration operates at multiple scales. Animal migration entails seasonal, large-scale movement between habitats driven by resource availability, breeding requirements, or climate. Birds navigate using solar cues, star patterns, magnetic fields, and olfactory maps. Wildebeest follow rainfall gradients across the Serengeti. Salmon return to natal streams via chemical imprinting. Plant migration occurs via seed dispersal—wind, water, animals, or explosive mechanisms—shifting populations across generations. Forest migration describes gradual range shifts of tree species tracking climate change over centuries. At the cellular level, cell migration drives embryonic development, wound healing, and immune response; collective cell migration coordinates groups during tissue formation. Gene migration (gene flow) transfers alleles between populations through interbreeding, a key evolutionary force that counters genetic drift and local adaptation.

## Physical and chemical migration

Physical and chemical migration covers phenomena driven by external forces rather than spontaneous diffusion. In organic chemistry, migration reactions rearrange molecular structures—hydride shifts, alkyl shifts, or sigmatropic rearrangements—moving atoms or groups within a molecule. Seismic migration reprocesses radar or sonar data to correct subsurface imaging, repositioning reflected signals to their true geological locations. Microscopic motion of material caused by external fields includes drift current (charge carriers in an electric field), electrophoresis (charged particles in a fluid under an electric field), electromigration (atoms in a conductor driven by momentum transfer from electrons), thermodiffusion (particles along a temperature gradient, the Soret effect), and sedimentation (particles settling under gravity or centrifugation). Planetary migration alters a satellite's orbital parameters over time through gravitational interactions with a protoplanetary disk or other bodies, explaining hot Jupiters and resonant moon systems.

## Computational migration

Computational migration transfers active or stored assets between systems. Virtualization migration moves running virtual machines between physical hosts—live migration preserves memory state and network connections without downtime. Data migration transfers data across storage types, formats, or systems, requiring validation, transformation, and rollback plans. Schema migration manages incremental, reversible changes to relational database schemas through versioned scripts. Software migration converts, rewrites, or ports legacy systems to modern platforms, often involving refactoring, containerization, or API layering. System migration moves data and applications from current hardware to new infrastructure, coordinating cutover, testing, and user transition.

## Cultural and other uses

"Migration" serves as a title for numerous albums, films, books, and a play, reflecting the concept's metaphorical resonance for transition and displacement. In body modification, piercing migration describes a piercing's gradual shift from its initial placement due to tissue pressure, jewelry weight, or immune response.

Across all domains, migration implies a transition that redefines the relationship between the moving entity and its new surroundings.

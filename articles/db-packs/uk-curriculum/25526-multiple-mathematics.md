# Multiple (mathematics)

In mathematics, a **multiple** is the product of a quantity and an integer. For quantities *a* and *b*, we say *b* is a multiple of *a* if there exists an integer *n* (the **multiplier**) such that *b* = *n*·*a*. When *a* ≠ 0, this is equivalent to *b*/*a* being an integer.

When *a* and *b* are both integers and *b* is a multiple of *a*, then *a* is a **divisor** of *b*; one says *a* divides *b*. For non-integer quantities, mathematicians often specify "integer multiple" to avoid ambiguity, since "multiple" can refer to other products—for example, a polynomial *p* is a multiple of *q* if *p* = *q*·*r* for some polynomial *r*.

**Examples.** 14, 49, −21, and 0 are multiples of 7 because each equals 7 times an integer: 14 = 7×2, 49 = 7×7, −21 = 7×(−3), 0 = 7×0. By contrast, 3 and −6 are not multiples of 7; although 3 = 7×(3/7) and −6 = 7×(−6/7), the factors 3/7 and −6/7 are not integers.

**Properties.**
- **Zero.** 0 is a multiple of every quantity, since 0 = 0·*a*.
- **Integer multiples.** For any integer *n* and any integer *k*, the product *n*·*k* is a multiple of *n*. In particular, *n* = *n*×1 is a multiple of itself.
- **Closure under addition and subtraction.** If *a* and *b* are multiples of *x*, then *a* + *b* and *a* − *b* are also multiples of *x*. Proof: *a* = *m*·*x*, *b* = *n*·*x* ⇒ *a* ± *b* = (*m* ± *n*)·*x*, and *m* ± *n* is an integer.

**Submultiples.** In some texts, *a* is a **submultiple** of *b* when *a* = *b*/*n* for an integer *n* (equivalently, *b* = *n*·*a*). This terminology is also used in metrology. A **unit submultiple** is the quotient of a main unit by an integer, usually a power of 10³. For example, a millimetre is the 1000-fold submultiple of a metre (1 mm = 1 m / 1000). An inch is a 12-fold submultiple of a foot and a 36-fold submultiple of a yard.

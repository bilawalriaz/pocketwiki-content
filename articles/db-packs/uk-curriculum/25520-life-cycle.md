# Life cycle

A life cycle is the series of stages an entity passes through from inception to termination. The concept applies across biology, computing, business, economics, and culture, each domain adapting a common structure: a defined beginning, progression through characteristic phases, and a defined end.

## Biology

The biological life cycle is the sequence of stages an organism undergoes from conception to reproduction. In multicellular organisms, this typically includes fertilization, embryonic development, growth to maturity, reproduction, and death. Plants and algae often exhibit alternation of generations, cycling between haploid (gametophyte) and diploid (sporophyte) phases. Parasites may require multiple hosts to complete their cycle. The cycle completes when a new generation reaches reproductive maturity.

## Computing

In computing, several distinct life cycles govern software and systems.

A **program lifecycle phase** describes the state of a single program: source code creation, compilation, linking, loading, execution, and termination.

The **systems development life cycle (SDLC)** structures the entire process of planning, creating, testing, deploying, maintaining, and ultimately disposing of an information system. Common models include waterfall (sequential phases), iterative (repeated cycles), and agile (incremental delivery with continuous feedback).

The **software release life cycle** governs version progression: pre-alpha (early development), alpha (internal testing), beta (external testing), release candidate (final testing), stable release, and eventual end-of-life when support ceases.

## Business and Economics

The **life-cycle hypothesis**, developed by Franco Modigliani, models how individuals plan consumption and saving over their lifetime: borrowing when young, saving during peak earning years, and dissaving in retirement.

A **product lifecycle** tracks a commercial product through introduction, growth, maturity, and decline. Marketing strategy, pricing, and investment shift at each stage.

**Project life cycle** defines phases from initiation through planning, execution, monitoring, and closure. **Enterprise life cycle** describes the evolution of an entire organization: startup, growth, maturity, and potential renewal or dissolution.

**Records life-cycle** covers creation, active use, semi-active storage, and final disposition (archival preservation or destruction).

**Life-cycle assessment (LCA)** quantifies environmental impacts—resource extraction, manufacturing, distribution, use, and disposal—associated with a product or service.

**Technology life cycle** describes the commercial trajectory of a technology: research and development, ascent (growing adoption), maturity (market saturation), and decline (obsolescence).

## Culture

The **Jewish life cycle** comprises rituals marking major transitions: birth (brit milah, naming), coming of age (bar/bat mitzvah), marriage (chuppah), and death (burial, mourning periods). Each ritual structures communal recognition of the individual's changing status.

A **life cycle ritual** in any culture marks events such as birth, puberty, marriage, and death, reinforcing social bonds and transmitting values across generations.

## Unifying pattern

Across domains, a life cycle provides a framework for managing change over time. It identifies predictable phases, clarifies transitions, and defines criteria for completion. Whether applied to an organism, a software system, a product, or a human life, the model enables anticipation of needs, allocation of resources, and planning for termination or renewal.

# Physical education

Physical education (PE) is a school subject that develops physical fitness, motor skills, health awareness, and social interaction through sports, exercise, and movement. It engages students across psychomotor (physical), cognitive (mental), affective (emotional), social, and cultural domains. The International Charter of Physical Education establishes access to PE, physical activity, and sport as a fundamental human right for all.

## Five Learning Bodies

Recent scholarship reframes the traditional three domains into Five Learning Bodies:

- **Moving Body**: Physical competencies and motor skills—control, coordination, efficiency.
- **Thinking Body**: Cognitive engagement—rules, strategies, concepts, decision-making, reflection.
- **Social Body**: Social interactions—collaboration, communication, teamwork, empathy, community.
- **Emotional Body**: Emotional experiences—joy, frustration, confidence, anxiety; role in motivation and well-being.
- **Cultural Body**: Cultural contexts and identities—how backgrounds, values, and norms shape participation.

Integrating these domains lets teachers move beyond physical performance toward inclusive, meaningful development.

## Curriculum Approaches

Ennis identifies three main curriculum models, plus a critical approach:

| Model | Focus | Example |
|-------|-------|---------|
| **Factory** | Standardized skill mastery, efficiency, discipline, outcome-based | Basketball drills with timed assessments |
| **Garden** | Individual nurturing, emotional wellbeing, inclusivity, student choice | Yoga, dance, cooperative games; personal health goals |
| **Journey** | Exploration, curiosity, reflection, co-creation, personal meaning | Students design games, research cultural sports |
| **Critical** | Question norms, address inequity, transform PE into space for social change | Revise game rules to improve fairness and participation |

Schools typically blend these; skill mastery aligns with Factory, critical thinking with Journey.

## Pedagogical Goals

Core teaching goals:
- Expose children to diverse exercise and healthy activities.
- Teach skills for lifetime fitness and health.
- Encourage self-reporting and monitoring of exercise.
- Individualize duration, intensity, and activity type.
- Focus feedback on effort, not outcome.
- Provide active role models.

Foundational pioneers (Delsarte, Diem, Laban) emphasized self-expression through movement. Modern PE also addresses moral development, social inclusion, and social justice, particularly for disabled students.

## Technology Integration

- **Video recording**: Students review playback to correct form; studies show this outperforms verbal correction alone.
- **Pedometers & heart rate monitors**: Set step and heart rate goals; improve participation, motivation, enjoyment.
- **Projectors & GPS**: Demonstrate form, enable outdoor activity.
- **Exergames** (Wii, Kinect, dance/sport simulations): Encourage activity in and out of classroom.
- **AI adaptive coaching**: Emerging personalization for adolescents, boosting motivation and program effectiveness.

Teacher technology competence determines integration success.

## Global Policy and Practice

- **WHO** recommends 60 minutes daily exercise ≥3×/week for youth; PE programs contribute.
- **79% of countries** legally mandate PE in schools.
- **Singapore**: Biennial NAPFA fitness exam (gold/silver/bronze/fail); affects national service.
- **Poland**: ≥3 hours/week primary/secondary; universities require 60 hours.
- **Sweden**: Graded minutes: 55 min/week (years 0–2), 110 (3–6), 220 (7–9); upper secondary 100-hour course.
- **UK**: England 2 hrs/week (years 7–9), 1 hr (10–11); Wales 2 hrs; Scotland ≥2 hrs; Northern Ireland 2 hrs (years 8–9).
- **Canada**: British Columbia mandates daily 30-min DPA; Ontario 20-min sustained moderate-vigorous daily.
- **US**: Patchwork—only 6 states require PE every grade; >50% allow substitutions/exemptions. Illinois “Zero Hour” links cardiovascular exercise to academic alertness.
- **Brazil**: PE compulsory in basic education; National Common Curricular Base frames bodily practices broadly (games, sports, gymnastics, dances, martial arts, adventure).

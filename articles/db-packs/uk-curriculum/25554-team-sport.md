# Team sport

A team sport requires multiple individuals to cooperate as a single unit; it cannot be played by one person. Team sports exclude individual or individual-to-team events within a sport. The objective typically involves teammates moving a ball or similar object under shared rules to score against an opponent. Success depends on continuous coordination, strategy, and role specialization throughout the match.

## Core categories

**Pure team sports** fix the number of players on the field and require a larger roster for substitutions due to fatigue, injury, or tactics. Examples include basketball, volleyball, rugby union, rugby league, water polo, handball, lacrosse, cricket, baseball, and the various codes of football and hockey. Two-person variants such as beach volleyball and dancesport operate without substitutes; each partner is indispensable.

**Formations** adapt individual sports into cooperative pairs or crews. Doubles in tennis, badminton, and table tennis demand shared tactics. Rowing uses crews of two, four, or eight; sailing uses multi-person boat classes; cycling’s team time trial assigns specialized roles (pacemaker, climber, sprinter) to optimize a single finishing time.

**Relays** chain individual efforts within a team framework. Each athlete covers a segment independently in running, swimming, cross-country skiing, biathlon, or short-track speed skating. Victory hinges on both raw speed and the precision of baton or tag exchanges.

**Team ratings** aggregate individual results into a collective score. In cycling, domestiques (support riders) sacrifice personal placement to shepherd a leader. In Formula One, two drivers earn constructor points while competing against each other; team orders—directives to favor one driver—were banned from 2002 to 2010, then permitted again after the 2010 German Grand Prix controversy.

## Historical development

Team ball games appear in the earliest recorded societies. Neolithic cave paintings in Mongolia (7000 BCE) and Libya (6000 BCE) depict wrestling and swimming with spectators. Mesoamerican *ollamaliztli* (Aztec ball game) fused sport, ritual, and cosmology. Ancient Greece formalized competition: the Olympic Games began in 776 BCE with foot races, boxing, wrestling, pankration (combat sport), discus, and javelin. Spartans played *episkyros*, a football-like game; Romans adapted it as *harpastum* and staged team chariot races (*ludi circenses*) for civic unity and social control. In China, *cuju*—kicking a ball into a net—flourished during the Han dynasty (206 BCE – 220 CE) as both recreation and military training. Across cultures, team sports reinforced communal identity, religious practice, and political cohesion.

## Olympic institutionalization

The modern Olympics codified team sports into a global framework. As of the 2024 Summer Olympics, 16 team sports hold medal status: 14 summer (cricket, football, water polo, lacrosse, field hockey, basketball, 3×3 basketball, handball, volleyball, beach volleyball, baseball, softball, rugby sevens, flag football) and 2 winter (ice hockey, curling). Ice hockey debuted at the 1920 Summer Games before moving permanently to the 1924 Winter Games. Bobsleigh was reclassified after women’s monobob debuted in 2022. Three sports were discontinued: polo (five editions, 1900–1936), rugby union (four editions, 1900–1924), and tug of war (five editions, 1900–1920). Nine others appeared only as demonstrations, including American football (1904, 1932), korfball (1920, 1928), and Australian football (1956). Baseball/softball, cricket (T20), flag football, and lacrosse (sixes) return for Los Angeles 2028.

## Modern scale

Professional leagues draw massive live audiences. In the 2024–25 season, American football led with university teams averaging over 100,000 spectators (Michigan, Penn State, Texas A&M, Ohio State, Texas). Association football clubs in Europe and South America averaged 73,000–85,000 (River Plate, Borussia Dortmund, Bayern München, Manchester United, Real Madrid). Australian football, baseball (MLB and NPB), basketball (NBA and NCAA), Canadian football, cricket (IPL), ice hockey (NHL), rugby league (NRL), and rugby union (URC, Top 14) all sustained multiple clubs above 20,000 average attendance, confirming team sport as the dominant form of mass spectator athletics worldwide.

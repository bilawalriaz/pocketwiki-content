# Design and Technology

Design and Technology (D&T) is a school subject taught in the United Kingdom at primary and secondary levels. It first appeared as a titled subject in the first National Curriculum for England in 1990 and has undergone several reviews, most recently in 2013. The subject involves students designing in a practical context using a range of materials and media. D&T is also taught internationally in countries including India, the United States, Australia, New Zealand, Ireland, Malta, China, South Africa, Latvia, France, Finland and Singapore.

Beyond schools, D&T is a university course in Australia, Canada, the US, Singapore, South Africa, the Netherlands and New Zealand, both for teacher preparation and for general education in areas such as industrial design. UK universities offering courses include Brighton, Sheffield Hallam, Goldsmiths, University of London, and Greenwich.

## Purpose and aims

The National Curriculum for England frames D&T as an inspiring, rigorous and practical subject in which pupils use creativity and imagination to design and make products that solve real problems within varied contexts, considering their own and others' needs. Pupils acquire broad subject knowledge and draw on mathematics, physics, mechanics, electronics, engineering, computing and art. They learn to take risks, becoming resourceful, innovative, enterprising and capable citizens. Through evaluating past and present design and technology, they develop a critical understanding of its impact on daily life and the wider world.

The curriculum gives four aims: pupils should develop the creative, technical and practical expertise to perform everyday tasks confidently and participate successfully in a technological world; build and apply knowledge, understanding and skills to design and make high-quality prototypes and products for a wide range of users; critique, evaluate and test their own ideas and products and the work of others; and understand and apply the principles of nutrition and learn how to cook. The second aim is known as "design and technology capability": the interaction of responding to a design context while drawing on knowledge and experience, giving pupils the power to produce change and improvement in the made world.

## Qualifications in England

**GCSE D&T (2017 onwards).** Taught to pupils in upper secondary school (years 10–11, ages 14–16), the GCSE incorporates all material areas except food, which is now a separate GCSE. The qualification has two assessed components: an exam and a non-examined assessment (NEA), with a 50:50 split of marks.

**A level.** A and AS level examinations prepare students for individualised learning and problem solving essential in business and industry. Time management is a key factor in success within coursework elements. The subject covers activities from control technology to aesthetic product design. Students use computer-aided design and manufacture, spreadsheets and computer presentations, and outputs are often sent to CNC (computer numerical control) machines for manufacture.

**Previous qualifications.** When the first National Curriculum introduced new GCSE qualifications for D&T in England, Wales and Northern Ireland, earlier subject titles were retained alongside new additions. These specifications had two components: 50% coursework and 50% examination on general subject knowledge (materials, processes, techniques, sustainability). GCSE titles offered included Electronic Products, Food Technology, Graphic Products, Resistant Materials, Systems and Control, Textiles Technology and Product Design.

## International Baccalaureate

IB Design Technology is an elective subject offered in many International Baccalaureate schools globally and is one of the Group 4 sciences. Design is compulsory in the IB Middle Years Programme (MYP) for grades 6–10 and available at Diploma Programme level (grades 11–12). MYP Design focuses on the design cycle: students complete projects solving real problems, document their progress, create solutions and evaluate them through testing.

The Diploma Programme is a two-year introduction to designing, fundamentals of technology and global technological issues. It gives students knowledge to design and make in school workshops while developing informed literacy about technology in general. Because it is an international curriculum, it has a particular focus on global environmental issues. Core topics cover human factors and ergonomics, resource management and sustainable production, modeling, raw materials to final production, innovation and design, and classic design. Higher-level topics include user-centred design, sustainability, innovation and markets, and commercial production. The diploma is accepted for university entrance in many countries and prepares students for careers in engineering, architecture, product design, interior design, design and education.

## Scotland

Technological education is part of the Scottish secondary school curriculum, segregated into subjects available at National 4, National 5, Higher and Advanced Higher. Standard subjects include Graphic Communication, Design and Manufacture, Engineering Science, Practical Electronics (N5 only), Practical Woodwork (N5 only) and Practical Metalwork (N5 only). Specialist subjects include Architectural technology, Automotive engineering, Civil engineering, Building services, Construction, Electrical engineering, Mechanical engineering and Mechatronics.

## Wales

In 2022, the new Welsh Curriculum combined distinct subjects into Areas of Learning Experience (AoLE). What was D&T now comes under the "Science and Technology" AoLE, though not all schools had fully transitioned as of 2025 and some still practice D&T as a standalone subject.

## Awards

In the UK, the Arkwright Scholarships Trust awards two-year scholarships to students taking GCSE or Scottish Standard Grade in design and technology. The Arkwright Engineering Scholarships support students through their A levels or Scottish Highers and encourage them to study engineering or a related design area at a top university or through a high-quality industrial apprenticeship.

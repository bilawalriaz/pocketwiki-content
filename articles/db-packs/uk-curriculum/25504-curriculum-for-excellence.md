# Curriculum for Excellence

Curriculum for Excellence (CfE) is Scotland's national curriculum for learners aged 3 to 18. It was implemented in schools during 2010–11 and is overseen by Education Scotland, the executive agency responsible for Scotland's education system. Councils and schools share responsibility for what is taught, within national guidelines.

CfE's development began with the National Debate on Education in 2002, a consultation by the Scottish Executive. A Curriculum Review Group formed in 2003 published *A Curriculum for Excellence* in 2004, identifying four purposes of education: to enable young people to become successful learners, confident individuals, responsible citizens, and effective contributors.

## Curriculum Structure

CfE is organised into eight curriculum areas: Expressive arts, Health and wellbeing, Languages (English, Gàidhlig, Gaelic Learners, modern and classical languages), Mathematics, Religious and moral education (including provision for Roman Catholic schools), Sciences, Social studies, and Technologies. The curriculum also operates through interdisciplinary learning, the ethos and life of the school, and wider opportunities for personal achievement.

Three curricula are cross-cutting responsibilities: literacy, numeracy, and health and wellbeing. Education Scotland and the Scottish Government regard these as "the responsibility of all staff" across every subject.

## Experiences, Outcomes and Benchmarks

CfE describes learning expectations through Experiences and Outcomes, which set out what learners should know and be able to do at each level. In August 2016, Education Scotland published Benchmarks to clarify the national standards within these Experiences and Outcomes. Benchmarks cover literacy and numeracy in detail, and all other curriculum areas from Early Level through Fourth Level (Modern Languages use First to Fourth Level). They define lines of progression and support consistency in teachers' professional judgements.

Achievement of a level is based on informed teacher judgement drawing on multiple sources: classroom observation, outdoor or workplace learning, tests, learning conversations, and planned holistic assessment. A learner has achieved a level when they show breadth across the experiences and outcomes, respond consistently well to the level's challenge, and apply learning in unfamiliar situations.

## Qualifications

The Scottish Qualifications Authority introduced new qualifications in 2014 aligned with CfE. National 1 through National 4 are assessed internally by teachers. National 5, Higher, and Advanced Higher are externally assessed. These replaced the previous Standard Grade system: National 3 replaced Foundation-level, National 4 replaced General-level, and National 5 replaced Credit-level. Higher replaced older Higher Grade courses, and Advanced Higher replaced the Certificate of Sixth Year Studies.

## Criticism

Many Scottish teachers raised concerns about vagueness, particularly around the Experiences and Outcomes framework. The Educational Institute of Scotland, the main teachers' union, feared this imprecision would create confusion about classroom expectations and assessment.

These concerns had practical consequences. East Renfrewshire, one of Scotland's highest-performing education authorities, delayed secondary implementation by a year. Several independent schools, including St Aloysius' College in Glasgow, did the same.

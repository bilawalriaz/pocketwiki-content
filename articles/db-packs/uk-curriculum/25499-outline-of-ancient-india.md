# Outline of ancient India

Ancient India spans the Indian subcontinent from prehistoric times to around 500 CE, when the Gupta Empire fell and medieval India began.

**Pre-history (c. 8000–3500 BCE).** Early settled cultures include Bhirrana (7570–6200 BCE) and Mehrgarh (c. 7000–2500 BCE), among the earliest farming communities in South Asia.

**Bronze Age / Proto-history (c. 3300–1300 BCE).** The Indus Valley Civilisation represents the "first urbanisation" in the region, with planned cities like Harappa and Mohenjo-daro. Contemporary cultures include the Ahar–Banas and Ochre Coloured Pottery cultures. Cemetery H culture (c. 1900–1300 BCE) overlaps with the late Indus phase.

**Iron Age (c. 1800–200 BCE).** The Vedic civilisation (c. 1700–600 BCE) brought Indo-Aryan peoples and the ritual religion codified in the Vedas. Polities of this era include the Kuru Kingdom (c. 1200–345 BCE), Gandhara Kingdom (c. 1500–545 BCE), and Brihadratha dynasty. The Northern Black Polished Ware (c. 1200–500 BCE) and Painted Grey Ware cultures mark this transition. By c. 600–345 BCE, numerous Iron Age kingdoms competed in the Ganges plain and south (Pandyan Kingdom from c. 600 BCE).

**Early Historic (c. 500 BCE–300 CE).** The Nanda Empire (c. 345–322 BCE) briefly unified north India before the Maurya Empire (c. 322–185 BCE) arose. In the south, the Sangam period (c. 300 BCE–300 CE) saw the Pandyan, Chera, and Chola kingdoms flourish. After Maurya, the Shunga, Kanva, and Satavahana empires held power. Indo-Greek, Indo-Scythian, and Kushan kingdoms entered from the northwest, bringing Central Asian and Hellenistic influences.

**Classical Period (c. 300–550 CE).** The Gupta Empire (c. 320–579 CE) was a major power. Concurrent powers included the Vakataka, Pallava, and Kadamba dynasties in the south.

**Religion and language.** The historical Vedic religion evolved into Hinduism over centuries, but scholars disagree on when. Some place the classical period at 100–1000 CE, others at 200 BCE–1100 CE, still others at 800–200 BCE, when karma, reincarnation, and personal enlightenment emerged. These concepts were absent in early Vedic thought. Jainism and Buddhism also arose as distinct traditions. Languages included Vedic Sanskrit and Old Tamil, written in scripts such as Brahmi, Tamil-Brahmi, and later Pallava and Gupta scripts. Science and technology produced advances in mathematics, astronomy, Ayurveda, and martial arts like Kalaripayattu.

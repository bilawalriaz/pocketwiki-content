# Biologist

A biologist is a scientist who studies life, from a single cell to a multicellular organism to communities of interacting populations. Most specialize in one branch of biology, such as molecular biology, zoology, or evolutionary biology, and narrow further to a research focus like malaria or cancer.

## How biology is practiced

Biologists follow the scientific method, an empirical cycle of testing hypotheses, in two modes. Basic research advances knowledge of the natural world. Applied research solves specific problems, such as developing a drug. Modern biologists usually hold at least a bachelor's degree, and those in research typically hold a master's or doctorate, with graduate training structured as an apprenticeship under a research head, a model in use since the 1800s.

Undergraduate biology programs typically require coursework in molecular and cellular biology, genetics, ecology, microbiology, anatomy, physiology, botany, zoology, and paleontology, alongside physics, chemistry (general, organic, and biochemistry), calculus, and statistics.

Biologists work in universities, nonprofits, private industry, and government. Many depend on grant funding, which creates pressure to meet deadlines and conform to strict proposal formats. Those in private industry often must explain their work to non-scientists who can approve or veto projects. Field biologists may face strenuous work in any climate, while laboratory workers handling dangerous organisms or toxic substances follow strict safety procedures.

## A short history of the field

Biology took shape through a chain of unifying discoveries. Francesco Redi, the founder of experimental biology, is recognized as one of the greatest biologists of all time. Robert Hooke, an English natural philosopher, coined the term *cell* in 1665, noting that plant structures resembled honeycomb cells. Charles Darwin and Alfred Wallace independently formulated evolution by natural selection, with Darwin laying out the case in *On the Origin of Species* (1859). Gregor Mendel formulated the principles of inheritance in 1866, founding modern genetics. James D. Watson and Francis Crick described the double-helix structure of DNA in 1953, building on work by Maurice Wilkins and Rosalind Franklin. Ian Wilmut led the team that in 1996 cloned Dolly, a Finnish Dorset lamb and the first mammal cloned from an adult somatic cell.

## Major specializations

Most biologists focus on a particular type of organism or a particular activity. Recent advances, especially in molecular tools, have blurred some traditional boundaries.

- **Genetics**: genes, heredity, and variation of organisms.
- **Neuroscience**: the nervous system.
- **Developmental biology**: how organisms grow and develop.
- **Biochemistry**: the chemical composition and reactions of life, including metabolism, reproduction, and growth.
- **Molecular biology**: interactions between biomolecules.
- **Microbiology**: microscopic organisms such as bacteria, algae, and fungi.
- **Physiology**: life functions of plants and animals, from whole organism down to cellular and molecular levels.
- **Biophysics**: applying physics-based experimental methods to biology.
- **Computational biology**: mathematical modeling and computer simulation of biological systems, often without a lab.
- **Zoology and wildlife biology**: animals, their origin, behavior, diseases, and life processes, with subfields such as ornithology (birds), mammalogy (mammals), herpetology (reptiles and amphibians), ichthyology (fish), and entomology (insects).
- **Botany**: plants and their environments, ranging from algae and mosses to flowering plants.
- **Mycology**: fungi such as yeasts, molds, and mushrooms, a separate kingdom from plants.
- **Marine and freshwater biology**: saltwater and freshwater organisms, often within oceanography, the integrated study of the ocean's biological, chemical, geological, and physical characteristics.
- **Paleontology**: the history of life on Earth through fossils, combining biology with geology to reconstruct ancient organisms, ecosystems, evolution, and extinction.
- **Evolutionary biology**: the processes, including natural selection, common descent, and speciation, that produced life's diversity from a single common ancestor.
- **Ecology**: relationships among organisms and between organisms and their environment.

## Basic versus applied research

Basic research expands knowledge of life itself, from evolution and biochemistry to cell biology and neuroscience. It is mostly laboratory work on animals, plants, microorganisms, or biomolecules, though some biology happens in the field through natural observation, such as a botanist cataloging species in a given habitat or an ecologist watching a forest recover after fire.

Applied research translates those findings into pharmaceutical drugs, treatments, and diagnostic tests. Advances in genetics and organic chemistry drove biotechnology, the manipulation of the genetic material of animals, plants, and other organisms. Recombinant DNA techniques now produce substances including human insulin and growth hormone that were once available only in tiny quantities. Genome projects isolate genes and link them to diseases such as sickle cell anemia, with commercial applications in medicine, agriculture, and environmental remediation.

## Recognition

The highest honor a biologist can receive is the Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine, awarded since 1901 by the Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences. Other significant awards include the Crafoord Prize in Biosciences (since 1980) and the Maxwell/Hanrahan Award in Field Biology (since 2020).

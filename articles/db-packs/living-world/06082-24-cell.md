# 24-cell

The 24-cell is a convex regular 4-polytope, the four-dimensional analogue of a Platonic solid, bounded by 24 octahedral cells with 96 triangular faces, 96 edges, and 24 vertices. Six octahedral cells meet at each vertex, and the vertex figure is a cube. Its Schläfli symbol {3,4,3} encodes these facts: triangular faces, octahedral cells, cube vertex figure. The 24-cell and the tesseract are the only convex regular 4-polytopes whose edge length equals their radius.

The 24 vertices are the 24 coordinate permutations of (±1, ±1, 0, 0) in R⁴, so the 24-cell is the convex hull of those points. It can also be built by rectifying the 16-cell, the cross-polytope of four dimensions. Cutting each vertex of a 16-cell at the midpoint of its incident edges turns its 8 vertices into 8 new octahedra and reshapes its 16 tetrahedral cells into 16 more octahedra, giving 24 octahedra total. The 24-cell is self-dual.

Among the six convex regular 4-polytopes, the 24-cell is fourth in size and complexity, nesting between the 8-cell (tesseract) and the 120-cell, and it can be decomposed into 3 overlapping tesseracts. Each successive regular 4-polytope is rounder than its predecessor, enclosing more 4-dimensional content at the same radius.

The 24 vertices form a D₄ root system, the 24 root vectors of the Lie group SO(8). Adding the 24 vertices of the dual gives 48 vectors forming the F₄ root system, whose Weyl group of order 1152 is the full symmetry group of the 24-cell. The rotational subgroup has order 576, and every rotation equals two reflections through non-parallel mirror planes.

Interpreted as unit Hurwitz quaternions, the 24 vertices form a closed multiplicative ring, the group of units of the Hurwitz integer quaternions, also called the binary tetrahedral group. The elements are 1, −1, six quaternion units ±i, ±j, ±k of order 4, eight of order 6, and eight of order 3. In antipodal pairs they represent the 12 rotations of a regular tetrahedron.

A 4-dimensional rotation is a double rotation: the composition of two rotations in completely orthogonal 2-planes. When the two angles are equal, the rotation is isoclinic, and every Clifford-parallel great circle plane through the polytope also rotates through that angle. A 60° isoclinic rotation in a hexagonal plane moves every vertex to a vertex two edge lengths away and rotates all 16 hexagons by 60° simultaneously. A 360° isoclinic rotation reverses the chirality of the 24-cell.

The 24-cell tiles 4-dimensional Euclidean space by translation to form the 24-cell honeycomb {3,4,3,3}, one of only three regular tessellations of R⁴. The other two are the dual 16-cell honeycomb {3,3,4,3} and the tesseractic honeycomb {4,3,3,4}. Each 24-cell has 24 neighbors sharing an octahedron and 24 more sharing only a single vertex, with eight 24-cells meeting at every vertex. Because it tiles by translation, the 24-cell is a parallelotope, the simplest one that is not a zonotope, and the Voronoi cells of the D₄ root lattice are themselves regular 24-cells.

The 24 octahedral cells can be arranged on a 3-sphere. Pick one cell as the North Pole; eight great-circle meridians of two cells each radiate outward, meeting at a South Pole cell, with six equatorial cells between. The 24 cells also form four disjoint rings of six cells, a discrete Hopf fibration of the 3-sphere.

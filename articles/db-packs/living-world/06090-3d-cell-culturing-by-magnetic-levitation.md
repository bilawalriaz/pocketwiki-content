# 3D cell culturing by magnetic levitation

The Magnetic Levitation Method (MLM) grows three-dimensional cell cultures without any artificial scaffold. Cells are loaded with magnetic nanoparticles and placed in a standard petri dish beneath a neodymium magnetic driver. The applied field lifts the magnetised cells to the air–liquid interface, where they collide, adhere, and self-assemble into multicellular tissue within about four hours. Cultures can range from 500 cells to millions, and the same hardware fits single dishes or high-throughput, low-volume plates.

The reagent that confers magnetic responsiveness is a composite assembly called the nanoshuttle, consisting of iron oxide (magnetite) nanoparticles that carry a magnetic moment, gold nanoparticles that provide biocompatibility and a chemical surface, and cell-adhesive peptide sequences that bind the assembly to the cell membrane so the whole cell moves with it. These polymer-based reagents attach to individual cells during a brief incubation.

The protocol runs in five steps. A nanoshuttle suspension is added to a cell culture and incubated. Cells are then detached, transferred to a petri dish, and a magnetic driver is placed on the lid. The vertical field gradient pulls the magnetised cells upward to the air–medium interface, where they concentrate and make immediate cell–cell contact. As the cluster spreads and relaxes, 3D structures become visible after about four hours. The same magnetised cells can also serve as building blocks for magnetic 3D bioprinting.

Because no matrix or scaffold is required, the constructs still form extracellular matrix proteins and respond to exogenous agents in patterns that resemble in vivo tissue. N-cadherin, a transmembrane junction protein used as a marker of in vivo–like assembly, concentrates at the cell membrane in human glioblastoma cells grown as mouse xenografts and in cells levitated for 48 hours. The same cells on a 2D glass coverslip show N-cadherin only in the cytoplasm and nucleus, with no membrane localisation, so the levitated pattern matches tumour tissue while the 2D pattern does not.

Because magnetised cells can be steered, different populations can be brought together after separate culture. Human glioblastoma cells labelled with GFP and normal human astrocytes labelled with mCherry are cultured apart, then magnetically guided into contact to form a 3D invasion assay in which tumour cells migrate into the astrocyte population over 12 to 252 hours. Such assays are used for cancer biology and drug screening.

MLM also supports organoids called adipospheres that mimic white adipose tissue. Co-culturing 3T3-L1 preadipocytes with murine bEND.3 endothelial cells in 3D produces a vascular-like network together with lipogenesis in perivascular cells. The stromal vascular fraction of mouse white adipose tissue, containing adipose stromal cells, endothelial cells, and infiltrating leukocytes, organises into adipospheres with distinct capsules and internal vessel-like structures lined by endothelium. After adipogenesis induction, the cells form large lipid droplets characteristic of in vivo white adipocytes, whereas 2D culture yields only small droplets. The platform enables functional screens for molecules acting on specific adipose cell populations and supports white adipose tissue transplantation and cell-therapy development.

Sequential magnetic layering of endothelial, smooth muscle, fibroblast, and epithelial cells creates bronchiole-like structures that retain their phenotypes and deposit extracellular matrix, showing that magnetic guidance can impose native-like architecture on co-cultures. The method has been applied to a wide range of cells, including murine and human endothelial cells, adipocytes, chondrocytes, neural stem cells, and mesenchymal stem cells; primary human pulmonary fibroblasts, small airway and bronchial epithelial cells, type II alveolar cells, and tracheal smooth muscle; the A549 alveolar adenocarcinoma line, HEK293 kidney cells, B16 melanoma, NHA astrocytes, LN229 glioblastoma, MCF10A mammary epithelial cells, MDA231 breast cancer, and MG63 osteosarcoma; T cells with antigen-presenting cells; and porcine heart valve endothelium.

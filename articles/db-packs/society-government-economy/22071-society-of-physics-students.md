# Society of Physics Students

The Society of Physics Students (SPS) is a professional association that combines a student membership body with a physics honor society under one organizational roof. It exists to help undergraduates interested in physics and astronomy move from coursework into the professional scientific community, an aim that goes beyond what physics classes alone usually teach.

## Origins and Structure

SPS was formed in 1968 through a union of three pre-existing groups: the student sections of the American Institute of Physics (AIP), the chapters of the honor society Sigma Pi Sigma, and AIP itself. Sigma Pi Sigma is the associated honor society for physics and astronomy, and the two organizations share governance.

Membership is open to any college student interested in physics or astronomy, with no academic prerequisite beyond that interest. Most members come from natural science, engineering, and medicine majors. Membership is granted through college chapters, which together form a national network.

The national body is governed by an elected council and an executive committee. The council is organized into 18 geographic zones, primarily covering North America. Each zone elects one faculty councilor and one student associate councilor; both lead local zone activities, support their chapters, and meet annually to set policy. The executive committee includes the SPS and Sigma Pi Sigma presidents, the SPS office director, the historian, an at-large member, an elected student representative, and the AIP CEO. AIP provides staff support, including the salaried director who serves as Executive Administrative Officer.

## Programs and Activities

SPS runs programs designed to build professional skills beyond the classroom. Members can join professional membership societies, subscribe to peer-reviewed journals, present and publish original research, apply for scholarships, and earn awards for excellent work. The quarterly magazine *The SPS Observer* keeps members and alumni connected, and members participate in science policy, education, and public outreach.

The Internship Program places selected SPS members in Washington, D.C. for 9.5-week summer placements with organizations working in science policy, science communication, physics outreach, and research. SPS and Sigma Pi Sigma also participate in the AIP Career Network, a set of online job sites for scientists, engineers, and computing professionals.

Alongside *The SPS Observer*, SPS and Sigma Pi Sigma publish the online *Journal of Undergraduate Research in Physics*, and Sigma Pi Sigma publishes *Radiations Magazine* twice a year in print.

## SPS Congress

SPS holds a national undergraduate physics meeting. The congress was originally scheduled every four years, and by 2025 the 18th meeting had taken place, with the interval having shifted to every three years. The next congress is planned for Minneapolis, Minnesota in 2028.

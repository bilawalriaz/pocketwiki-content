# IEEE Computer Society

The IEEE Computer Society is the computing-focused technical society of the Institute of Electrical and Electronics Engineers (IEEE), one of 39 such societies under the IEEE Technical Activities Board, the IEEE's umbrella for its specialized fields. Founded in 1946, it is the largest of those 39 societies, with more than 375,000 members in 150 countries, over 100,000 of them in the United States. It operates as a global, non-governmental, not-for-profit professional body headquartered in Washington, DC, with additional offices in California, China, and Japan. Its stated purpose is "advancing the theory, practice, and application of computer and information processing science and technology," with work divided across four areas: hardware, software, standards, and the people who build them.

## Origins

The society grew out of two predecessor committees from organizations that no longer exist as separate entities. In 1946, the American Institute of Electrical Engineers (AIEE) formed the Subcommittee on Large-Scale Computing. In 1951, the Institute of Radio Engineers (IRE) formed the Professional Group on Electronic Computers. When the AIEE and IRE merged in 1963 to form the IEEE, the two committees were combined into the IEEE Computer Group. The group adopted its own constitution and bylaws in 1971, at which point it became the IEEE Computer Society. Its 1946 founding date reflects the earlier of the two predecessor committees, not the 1963 merger.

## Activities and structure

The society runs its work through five program areas, each overseen by a volunteer board: membership, professional and educational activities, publications, standards, and technical and conference activities. Twelve standing boards and committees handle elections and awards. Each year it sponsors around 200 technical conferences worldwide and supports 634 local chapters through its Member and Geographic Activities Board, which also runs the Distinguished Visitor Program, a speakers' bureau for chapter events.

## Publications

The Computer Society is one of the largest publishers of peer-reviewed computing material. Its output includes 12 technical magazines, 23 scholarly journals called *Transactions*, conference proceedings, books, and digital products. The Computer Society Digital Library (CSDL) is the central repository, holding more than 810,000 papers, articles, and proceedings available to subscribers. Since 2014, the society has also published *Computing Edge*, a monthly digest that curates articles from its magazines.

## Technical communities and standards

Twenty-nine active technical communities and task forces (TCs) organize specialists around subfields of computing, from VLSI design (very-large-scale integration, the chip-design discipline) to software engineering. A technical community is an international network of professionals focused on one computing discipline; it serves as the focal point for that discipline's standards work, conferences, publications, and educational programs within the society. The Technical Community on VLSI (TCVLSI) is a representative example, covering VLSI design along with the computer-aided design (CAD) and electronic design automation (EDA) tools used to create it, for digital, analog, and mixed-signal circuits. The TCVLSI chair is elected by its voting members and appoints the other executive members.

Twelve standards committees develop IEEE computing standards. Examples include the Design Automation Standards Committee and the IEEE 802 LAN/MAN Standards Committee, which oversees the IEEE 802 family of networking standards.

## Education and awards

Alongside the Association for Computing Machinery (ACM), the society helps shape college computing curricula, including the joint CS2013 report. It also runs software-development certification programs and provides online access to e-learning courseware and books.

Awards recognize contributions in three categories: Technical, Education, and Service. Examples include the Harry H. Goode Memorial Award and the W. Wallace McDowell Award in the technical category, the Taylor L. Booth Education Award in the education category, and the Richard E. Merwin Award for Distinguished Service in the service category. In 2018, the society itself won a Southern California Journalism Award for "Untold Stories: Setting the Record Straight on Tech's Racial History."

The society's day-to-day effect on computing comes from three channels: the standards its committees publish, the conferences and journals where new research appears, and the curriculum guidance that shapes how university programs train new practitioners.

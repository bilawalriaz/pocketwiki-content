# International Linear Algebra Society

The International Linear Algebra Society (ILAS) is a professional mathematical society that promotes research and education in linear algebra, matrix theory, and matrix computation. It operates as a 501(c)(3) nonprofit and serves mathematicians and scientists worldwide through conferences, publications, prizes, and lectures. Membership is open to anyone interested in furthering its aims.

## Origins

ILAS grew out of the Combinatorial Matrix Analysis Conference held at the University of Victoria in British Columbia, Canada, May 20–23, 1987, hosted by Dale Olesky and Pauline van den Driessche. The group formed there became the International Matrix Group in 1987 and was renamed ILAS in 1989, the year treated as its official founding. Its first officers were Hans Schneider (President), Robert C. Thompson (Vice President), Daniel Hershkowitz (Secretary), and James R. Weaver (Treasurer).

## Conferences

The inaugural ILAS conference met at Brigham Young University in Provo, Utah, August 12–15, 1989, with one day at the Sundance Mountain Resort. Eighty-five attendees from 15 countries participated, including Olga Taussky-Todd, a leading figure in matrix theory. Brigham Young University, the National Security Agency, and the National Science Foundation supported the meeting. The second conference was held in Lisbon, Portugal, August 3–7, 1992, drawing 150 participants from 27 countries.

For its first four meetings ILAS alternated between the United States and Europe. It then adopted the current pattern of holding a conference in two of every three years. Under a 1999 agreement with SIAM, the two societies avoid holding conferences in the same year, so ILAS meets in years where the SIAM Activity Group on Linear Algebra does not. The geographic reach widened steadily: Haifa hosted the first conference outside the US and Europe in 2001, Shanghai the first Far East meeting in 2007, and Cancun, Mexico, the first Latin American meeting in 2008.

## Prizes

ILAS awards three prizes named after influential figures in linear algebra.

The Hans Schneider Prize, instituted at the 1993 ILAS meeting in Pensacola, Florida, recognizes a distinguished career in linear algebra and is typically awarded every three years. It was funded by a donation from Schneider himself, ILAS's first president and a founding editor of *Linear Algebra and Its Applications*.

The ILAS Olga Taussky and John Todd Prize honors two researchers whose work shaped theoretical and numerical linear algebra for over half a century. Originally a lecture series, it was established at the same 1993 Pensacola meeting and is awarded every three to four years to a mid-career researcher.

The ILAS Richard A. Brualdi Early Career Prize, instituted in 2021, is awarded every three years to an outstanding early-career researcher. Brualdi has been a major contributor to combinatorial matrix theory and an active ILAS supporter since its inception.

## Publications

ILAS publishes the *Electronic Journal of Linear Algebra* (ELA), founded in 1996 with Volker Mehrmann and Daniel Hershkowitz as its first Editors-in-Chief. ELA is a platinum open access journal, charging neither subscription fees nor article processing fees, and uses electronic peer review.

ILAS also distributes *IMAGE*, a semiannual electronic bulletin founded in 1988 with Robert C. Thompson as its first editor. *IMAGE* carries essays, feature articles, expert interviews, book reviews, conference reports, society notices, problem sets, and member news.

## Leadership

Presidents of ILAS serve six-year terms. Schneider led the society from 1987 to 1996, followed by Richard A. Brualdi (1996–2002), Daniel Hershkowitz (2002–2008), Stephen Kirkland (2008–2014), Peter Šemrl (2014–2020), and Daniel B. Szyld (2020–2026). Shaun Fallat became president in 2026.

## Collaborations

ILAS works with three partner organizations. Its 1999 agreement with SIAM exchanges speakers between ILAS conferences and the triennial SIAM Applied Linear Algebra meeting. The partnership with the American Mathematical Society began in late 2020 and took effect at the 2022 Joint Mathematics Meetings, where ILAS sponsors a named lecture and at least four special sessions. The collaboration with the International Workshop on Operator Theory and its Applications (IWOTA) began in 2017 with the Israel Gohberg ILAS-IWOTA Lecture, a biennial lecture funded by donations, honoring Gohberg as the founding president of IWOTA and an active ILAS member.

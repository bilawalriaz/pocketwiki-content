# Migration Period

The Migration Period (c. 300–600 AD) was the era in which Germanic and other peoples moved into and within the Western Roman Empire, contributing to its collapse and the rise of post-Roman kingdoms. Whether the migrations caused the empire's fall or were a consequence of its internal breakdown remains a central scholarly debate.

## Timeline

- c. 375: Huns enter Europe from Asia, displacing the Goths.
- 376: Tervingi (Visigoths) cross the Danube into Roman territory.
- 406: Vandals, Alans, and Suebi cross the Rhine into Gaul.
- 410: Visigoths sack Rome.
- 476: Odoacer deposes the last Western Roman Emperor, Romulus Augustulus.
- 486: Clovis I defeats Syagrius, consolidating Frankish power in Gaul.
- 489: Ostrogoths under Theodoric invade Italy.
- 568: Lombards conquer much of Italy, the period's traditional endpoint.
- 711: Moors invade the Iberian Peninsula.
- c. 895: Hungarians occupy the Carpathian Basin.

## Causes and Scale

Three pressures drove the migrations: Huns pushing from the east, internal Roman instability, and economic incentives inside the empire. Migrations ranged from small war bands to entire tribes. Modern estimates put the Gothic crossing of the Danube (376) at 90,000–200,000 people, the Rhine crossing (406) at up to 200,000, and the Lombard invasion of Italy (568) at roughly 150,000. Total migration into Roman territory may have reached 500,000 to over 1,000,000.

## First Wave: Germanic Settlements (c. 300–500)

Goths, Vandals, and Franks moved into Roman provinces. Visigoths settled first in Gaul, then Iberia. Franks gradually entered Gaul and built a kingdom under Clovis I, the nucleus of later France and Germany. Anglo-Saxons filled Britain after Roman withdrawal. Burgundians took parts of modern France and Switzerland. Ostrogoths under Theodoric invaded Italy in 489 and ruled it from Ravenna.

## Second Wave: Slavic and Steppe Expansions (c. 500–700)

Slavic tribes expanded across central, southern, and eastern Europe, making the eastern half of the continent predominantly Slavic-speaking. Turkic Avars and later Magyars (Hungarians) migrated into the Danube basin. Bulgars settled the Balkans permanently. Arab armies probed southeastern Europe but were repelled, while Moors conquered most of Iberia from the Visigoths in 711.

## How Tribal Identities Formed

Older scholarship treated barbarian tribes as coherent biological groups. Modern constructionist views, especially the Austrian school's concept of *ethnogenesis*, argue that tribal identities were social constructs. A small core of military and aristocratic elites, called the *Traditionskern* ("kernel of tradition"), forged group identity using metaphors of kinship. Identity was fluid and political, not fixed by race.

## Why the West Fell and the East Held

The Western Empire fragmented into successor kingdoms because its internal collapse pulled tribes into its territories, where they filled administrative vacuums. The takeover varied by region: in Aquitaine it resembled an administrative handover, in Gaul a power vacuum. The Eastern Roman Empire adapted to similar pressures and survived, while the West did not. Historian Guy Halsall argues the migrations were a result of Roman collapse, not its cause.

## Key Terms

- *Völkerwanderung*: German term for the period, literally "wandering of peoples."
- *Ethnogenesis*: The process by which a group becomes recognized as a distinct ethnic group.
- *Traditionskern*: Core elite that provided the political kernel of a tribal polity.
- *Foederati*: Barbarian tribes settled inside the empire under treaty.
- *Hospitalitas*: Roman practice of billeting soldiers on civilians, later used to settle allied barbarian forces.
- *Limes*: The Roman Empire's fortified frontier.
- *Barbaricum*: The lands beyond Roman frontiers, inhabited by non-Roman peoples.

## Open Questions

- Did migrations cause the Western Empire's fall, or did its collapse draw migrants in?
- How large were the migrating groups, and what was the total demographic impact?
- Were barbarian tribes coherent ethnic groups or fluid political constructs?
- Is the period best framed as a destructive invasion or a transformative migration and settlement?

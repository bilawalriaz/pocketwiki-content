# Maritime sociology

Maritime sociology is a sub-discipline of sociology studying how human societies and cultures relate to the oceans and the marine environment, and the social processes that flow from those relations. It treats the oceans as a social space, not a purely natural one.

Its subject matter includes human activity at and with the sea: seafaring, fisheries, maritime and coastal tourism, offshore extraction, deep-sea mining, and marine environmental conservation, together with the institutions and discourses surrounding those activities. It also studies societal–natural relations in the marine realm, such as over-fishing and the social consequences of climate change.

## Relation to neighbouring disciplines

Maritime sociology shares theories, methods, and collaborators with the sociology of work and environmental sociology. Despite being nearly as old as sociology itself, it has not been heavily institutionalised and is currently practised by several largely independent national schools.

| Region | Institutional milestones |
| --- | --- |
| Poland (Szczecin, Gdańsk, Poznań) | After the Second World War, an expanded coastline made maritime matters politically and scientifically important; maritime sociology was established as a national research area focused on maritime professions. |
| Germany | A working group at the Institute of Sociology, Christian-Albrechts-University, Kiel ran from 1985 to 1992 to establish maritime sociology there. |
| China | Ocean University and Shanghai Ocean University run institutes; a professional committee was founded in the Chinese Sociological Association in 2010. |
| Europe (field-wide) | Since 2009, research streams have met at the biannual European Sociological Association conference. |
| Canada (Memorial Newfoundland, Dalhousie, St. Francis Xavier, British Columbia, Toronto) | Focused on the environmental dimensions of human–ocean relations and on fisheries research. |

## Theoretical positions

No single framework unites the field. Maritime sociologists share the topic of the sea rather than a common paradigm, and most borrow theory from elsewhere.

Marinization. From the 1970s, Polish sociologist Ludwik Janiszewski developed "marinization" (*Marynizacja*). Modelled on industrialisation, urbanisation, and digitalisation, it names a long-run historical tendency for land-based societies to become more entangled with the maritime realm, with the sea growing in importance.

Critical political economy of oceans. In the Marxist tradition, this approach examines how the capitalist mode of production shapes the ocean. One strand uses social-ecological tools such as social metabolic analysis for environmental and sustainability issues; John Hannigan argues its proponents retain a terrestrial bias and fail to conceptualise the ocean as a distinct social space. A second strand analyses the sea's role in international trade, migration, maritime labour, and piracy; an early landmark is Philip Steinberg's *The Social Construction of the Ocean*. Campling and Colás frame the wider aim as showing how capitalism has made land and sea more interdependent and more sharply differentiated.

Posthumanist and postmodern theories. Drawing on Actor-Network-Theory and Donna Haraway, recent maritime sociologists argue for dissolving the nature/society divide and treating the ocean as a hybrid rather than as two separate domains.

## Fields of research

Major empirical strands include Ferdinand Tönnies' study of dockworkers and seafarers in North German seaports, Norbert Elias's work on the origins of the naval profession in England, and the academic debate over whether a ship is a "total institution." Most maritime scholars studying seafaring accept that label; critics argue that a merchant ship is not a disciplinary institution and cannot be adequately described in those terms. The Seafarers International Research Centre (SIRC) at Cardiff University, whose members study the social conditions of global seafarers in the world's merchant fleets, anchors this applied strand.

No journal is dedicated exclusively to maritime sociology. Relevant interdisciplinary venues include *Maritime Studies*, *Maritime Policy & Management*, *Marine Policy*, the *WMU Journal of Maritime Affairs*, *Asia-Pacific Journal of Marine Science & Education*, *Constanta Maritime University Annals*, *Pomorstvo*, and *Roczniki Socjologii Morskiej* (1986–2016).

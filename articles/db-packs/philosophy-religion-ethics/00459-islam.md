# Islam

Islam is an Abrahamic, monotheistic religion built on the Quran and the teachings of Prophet Muhammad, with roughly 2 billion adherents, making it the world's second-largest religion after Christianity and its fastest-growing major faith. Muslims hold Islam is the final, complete expression of a primordial faith revealed through earlier prophets (Adam, Noah, Abraham, Moses, Jesus), with Muhammad as the last.

## Doctrine

The creed (*aqidah*) rests on six articles: belief in God, angels, revealed scriptures, prophets, the Day of Resurrection, and divine predestination. The central concept is *tawhid*, God's absolute oneness; associating anything with God is *shirk*, the gravest sin. God is addressed by names such as *Ar-Rahman* and *Ar-Rahim*; *Allah* is grammatically singular and genderless.

The Quran, 114 chapters (*sūrah*), 6,236 verses (*āyāt*), was revealed to Muhammad by the angel Gabriel between 610 and 632 CE. Meccan chapters stress spiritual themes; later Medinan chapters cover law and society. Earlier scriptures (the Torah/*Tawrat*, Gospel/*Injil*) are respected but held to have been distorted. Prophets are human, not divine; messengers (*rasūl*) deliver new scriptures. Muhammad is the "Seal of the prophets." His normative practice (*sunnah*), preserved in *hadith* collections graded by chain of narrators (*sanad*) and text (*matn*), guides Quran interpretation. The Day of Resurrection (*Yawm al-Qiyāmah*) brings bodily resurrection and judgment, ending in paradise (*Jannah*) or hell (*Jahannam*). Most Muslims accept predestination (*al-qadāʾ wa l-qadar*); the Muʿtazila and some Shi'as reject it.

## The Five Pillars

Worship rests on five obligations:

1. **Shahada**, the declaration that there is no god but God and Muhammad is His messenger, required for conversion.
2. **Salah**, five daily prayers in Arabic facing the Kaaba in Mecca, preceded by ritual washing (*wudu* or *ghusl*), performed at a mosque (*masjid*).
3. **Zakat**, 2.5% annual almsgiving on wealth, functioning as welfare. Optional charity is *sadaqah*; a perpetual charitable trust is a *waqf*.
4. **Sawm**, fasting from dawn to sunset during Ramadan.
5. **Hajj**, pilgrimage to Mecca during *Dhu al-Hijjah*, required once for those able. Rituals reenact Abraham's family: circumambulating the Kaaba, walking between Safa and Marwa, standing at Arafat, stoning the Devil. Men wear seamless white *ihram*. *Umrah* is a shorter, optional year-round pilgrimage.

Other acts include Quran memorization (a *hafiz* learns all 6,236 verses), supplication (*duʿāʾ*), and remembrance phrases (*dhikr*).

## History

Muhammad, born in Mecca around 570 CE, began preaching monotheism in 610 against Meccan polytheism, drawing followers from the poor, women, foreigners, and slaves. After 12 years of persecution, the 622 *Hijra* to Medina founded the first Islamic state under the Constitution of Medina. The 624 victory at Badr and disruption of Meccan trade enabled the near-bloodless conquest of Mecca by 629; Arabia was united by Muhammad's death in 632.

Succession passed to the Rashidun ("Rightly Guided") caliphs: Abu Bakr, who crushed the *Ridda* revolts and began compiling the Quran; Umar; Uthman, assassinated in 656; and Ali. Civil wars followed, including Aisha's defeat at the Camel, Mu'awiya's challenge at Siffin, and Ali's assassination (656–661). Husayn's killing at Karbala in 680 became the defining martyrdom of Shia Islam, formalizing the Sunni–Shia split. The Umayyad dynasty (661–750) expanded into Persia, Byzantine lands, North Africa, Iberia, and Sindh, but their refusal to grant equal status to non-Arab converts (*mawali*) fueled the 750 Abbasid Revolution.

The Abbasid era (750–1258), the Islamic Golden Age, saw Bukhari and Muslim (Sunni) and al-Kulayni (Shia) compile authoritative hadith, four Sunni legal schools form, al-Tabari write the foundational Quran commentary, and Sufism emerge from ascetics like Hasan al-Basri. The Muʿtazila, who held the Quran was created and affirmed free will, clashed with traditionalists like Ahmad ibn Hanbal, tortured for rejecting this view. Philosophers (Al-Farabi, Avicenna, Averroes) reconciled Aristotle with Islam before Al-Ghazali's critique redirected energy toward mysticism. Advances included Rhazes distinguishing smallpox from measles, Ibn al-Haytham's optics, and al-Khwarizmi founding algebra. Trade and Sufi missions carried Islam to Volga Bulgaria, India, and China.

Mongol forces destroyed Baghdad in 1258. The caliphal title passed to the Ottoman sultans from 1517 until abolition in 1924, while Shia Safavids (1501) entrenched Twelver dominance in Iran. The 1979 Iranian Revolution established the first major modern Islamic state. Recent currents include Islamist parties (Muslim Brotherhood, Turkey's AK Party), the Saudi-Wahhabi alliance, pan-Islamic institutions like the Organisation of Islamic Cooperation (founded 1969), persecution of Muslim minorities (Rohingya, Uyghurs), and decentralized authority via televangelists and online interpretation.

## Branches

Sunnis (about 87–90% of Muslims) accept the first four caliphs as rightful successors and follow one of four legal schools (Hanafi, Hanbali, Maliki, Shafi'i). Currents include Traditionalism (the Quran is uncreated), Maturidism, Ash'arism, and Salafism.

Shias hold leadership must stay within Muhammad's family (*Ahl al-Bayt*) through divinely appointed Imams, citing Ghadir Khumm, where they believe Muhammad named Ali; Sunnis hold no appointment was made. Twelvers, the largest Shia group, await the hidden twelfth Imam's return from *occultation*. Zaydis reject Imams' infallibility; Isma'ilis split over the seventh Imam (the Nizaris include the Aga Khan).

Ibadism, mostly in Oman, descends from the Kharijite secession but does not excommunicate sinful Muslims. Smaller movements include Ahmadiyya (founded 1889, rejected by most Muslims), Alevism, Quranism, and growing non-denominational Islam.

## Law and daily life

*Sharia* derives from the Quran and Sunnah using *qiyas* (analogy), *ijma* (consensus), and *ijtihad* (independent reasoning); rulings span five categories from obligatory (*fard*) to forbidden (*haram*). There is no clerical hierarchy: imams lead prayer, *ulama* are scholars, muftis issue non-binding legal opinions (*fatwas*), and *qadis* judge. Islamic banking prohibits interest (*riba*). *Jihad* means "striving in God's way," spiritual effort or, qualified, armed struggle; most modern Muslims interpret it as defensive only.

Daily life follows dietary rules (no pork, blood, carrion, or intoxicants; meat requires *halal* slaughter), modesty (*haya*, *hijab*), marriage (a *mahr* bridal gift, polygyny up to four wives, no polyandry), birth rites (*adhan*, *aqiqah*, *khitan* circumcision), and burial within about 24 hours. The lunar calendar begins with the 622 Hijra. Major festivals are *Eid al-Fitr* (end of Ramadan) and *Eid al-Adha* (end of Hajj).

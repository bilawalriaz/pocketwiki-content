# Hindu views on evolution

Hindu traditions span both a creationist rejection of biological evolution and the claim that Hindu texts anticipated Darwinian ideas. A 2009 Pew Research Center report found American Hindus supported evolution at 80%, second only to Buddhists at 81%; a 2014 survey produced similar results. Evolution is taught at Indian universities as a scientific fact, and the creation–evolution controversy that dominates in the United States, the Middle East, and parts of Africa is largely insignificant in India.

## Reception in India

In nineteenth-century India, Darwinism met little hostility because Hindu traditions already included a notion of common ancestry between humans and animals. The creation–evolution controversy has seen much debate in the US, Middle East, and parts of Africa, but it is an insignificant issue in India, where the population is Hindu-majority.

Tensions emerged in the 2020s. In 2023, India removed references to evolution from textbooks and stopped teaching it at the 10th-grade level and below, moving it to 12th grade. NCERT books further excluded Darwin's theory in 2025. The 2023 change prompted 4,500 Indian scientists, teachers, and educators to sign a petition warning that the decision threatened the technology sector by discouraging science study. A 2018 episode had prompted the Indian National Science Academy (INSA), the Indian Academy of Science (IASc), and the National Academy of Sciences India (NASI) to issue a joint statement affirming that Darwinian theory is well established.

## Hindu creationism

Hindu creationism, also called Vedic creationism, is a form of old-Earth creationism: it accepts an immense age for the planet but rejects biological evolution. Its modern form is linked to Dayananda Saraswati, founder of Arya Samaj, whose *Satyarth Prakash* argued that God designed the physical bodies of every species 1.96 billion years ago on Earth and other planets at the start of the present cosmic cycle, then conjoined those bodies with pre-existing souls, assigning species to souls according to karma from the previous cycle. Saraswati publicly rejected Darwinian evolution but misunderstood common descent by asking why, if humans evolved from apes, monkeys no longer turn into men. Vedic creationism draws on the Vedas and the Bhagavad Gita, and was later promoted by A. C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada, founder of ISKCON, who called Darwin and his followers "rascals," and by ISKCON devotees Michael Cremo and Richard L. Thompson, authors of the 1993 book *Forbidden Archaeology*. They argue humans are a distinct species billions of years old, search the fossil record for anomalies, and reinterpret it to fit their metaphysical assumptions. Historian of science Ronald Numbers wrote that "Hindu Creationists have insisted on the antiquity of humans, who they believe appeared fully formed as long, perhaps, as trillions of years ago."

## Dashavatara as evolution

Some Hindus read the *Dashavatara*, the ten principal avatars of Vishnu, as a sequential image of evolutionary ascent. British geneticist J. B. S. Haldane endorsed this reading. The avatars begin with Matsya (a fish, aquatic life), then Kurma (a turtle, aquatic reptiles moving to land), then Varaha (a boar, mammals), then Narasimha (a man-lion, sometimes linked to transitional forms such as the okapi or *Archaeopteryx*), then Vamana (a dwarf, early hominids), then Parashurama (humans in a caveman stage), and Rama (the rise of kingdoms and civilization). When Balarama is included, he represents the growth of agriculture; Krishna represents the growth of art and crafts. The tenth avatar, Kalki, is prophesied to appear at the end of the present age, the Kali Yuga.

## Vanaras and the ape-man claim

The Sanskrit epic *Ramayana* describes *vanaras*, an ape-like species with human intelligence that lived millions of years ago alongside modern humans. Michael Cremo treats this as evidence that the ape-man idea predates Darwin and as support for coexistence rather than evolution: humans and ape-men lived at the same time as separate kinds.

## Spiritual evolution

A separate current treats evolution as a spiritual rather than biological process. Reformers have linked the Samkhya term *parinama* (evolute) to Darwinian evolution, and David Gosling has argued that Swami Vivekananda's cosmological and biological ideas drew heavily on Samkhya. Influenced by Western esoteric thought, Vivekananda and Sri Aurobindo described an *involution* of the Divine into matter, followed by the soul's evolution over many lives until the Divine recognises its true nature and liberation (*moksha*) is attained.

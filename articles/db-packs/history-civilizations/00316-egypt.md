# Egypt

Egypt is a transcontinental country of over 107 million people, spanning northeast Africa and, via the Sinai Peninsula, southwest Asia. It is the most populous Arab country and Africa's third-most populous, with the continent's second-largest economy. One of humanity's oldest continuous civilisations developed along the Nile, and Egypt remains a regional power in the Middle East, North Africa, and the Muslim world.

## Land and People

Egypt covers 1,001,450 km² but functions as a river civilisation. About 99% of the population lives on roughly 5.5% of the land, concentrated in the Nile Valley and Delta. The climate is hot and arid; the seasonal Khamaseen wind can push temperatures above 50 °C. The Aswan High Dam, completed in 1971, ended the annual Nile floods that had shaped agriculture for millennia.

Ethnic Egyptians make up 99.7% of the population, with over 9 million migrants. Muslims are85–90% and Coptic Christians 10–15%, though the Christian share is disputed up to 15–20%. Arabic is official, with Egyptian Arabic the main spoken dialect. When Muslims became a majority is unknown, with estimates from around 1000 CE to the 14th century.

## Ancient Foundations

Neolithic farming took root in the Nile Valley by about 6000 BCE. The earliest hieroglyphic inscriptions appear on Naqada III pottery around 3200 BCE, and King Menes unified Egypt around 3150 BCE, beginning dynastic rule. The Old Kingdom (c. 2700–2200 BCE) built the Giza pyramids. The New Kingdom (c. 1550–1070 BCE), founded by Ahmose I after expelling the Hyksos, a West Asian people who had occupied the Delta, was Egypt's imperial height under pharaohs including Hatshepsut, Thutmose III, Akhenaten, Tutankhamun, and Ramesses II. Akhenaten's worship of Aten was an early form of monotheism.

Ancient Egyptians called their land *km.t* ("black"), for the dark fertile floodplain soils, contrasted with *deshret*, the "red land" of the desert. "Egypt" comes from Greek *Aígyptos*, thought to derive from *Hutkaptah* ("Temple of the Ka of Ptah"), a name for Memphis. The Arabic *Miṣr* traces to a Semitic root meaning "civilisation" or "metropolis"; the related Akkadian *miṣru* means "border," reflecting Egypt's position at the edge of the Mesopotamian world.

## Conquests and Cosmopolitan Capitals

Persia conquered Egypt in 525 BCE, followed by Alexander the Great in 332 BCE. His general Ptolemy I Soter founded a Hellenistic dynasty whose capital, Alexandria, became a centre of Greek learning. After Cleopatra VII's suicide, Rome annexed Egypt. Christianity arrived in the 1st century through Saint Mark, and the Coptic Church was established by 451 CE. Arab Muslim forces conquered Egypt in 639–642, founding Fustat, later replaced by Cairo in 969. Cairo then served as capital of the Fatimid, Ayyubid, and Mamluk (from 1250) caliphates. The Mamluks were a Turco-Circassian military caste that ruled until 1517. The Black Death killed about 40% of Egypt's population in the mid-14th century.

## Ottoman Rule and Muhammad Ali

The Ottomans conquered Egypt in 1517, though Mamluks kept semi-autonomous influence. Napoleon's 1798 invasion destabilised the region, allowing Muhammad Ali Pasha to seize power in 1805. He massacred the Mamluks, reformed the army along European lines, promoted cotton agriculture, and expanded into Sudan, Syria, Arabia, and Anatolia before European powers forced him to retreat in 1841. His dynasty ruled until 1952.

## Monarchy, British Occupation, and Republic

The Suez Canal opened in 1869. Debt forced Isma'il Pasha to sell Egypt's canal shares to Britain in 1875, and Britain occupied Egypt in 1882 after the Urabi revolt. After World War I, nationalism surged, producing nominal independence in 1922 under King Fuad I and a parliamentary constitution in 1923. Defeat in the 1948 Arab–Israeli War, continued British presence, and the killing of 43 Egyptian policemen at Ismailia in 1952 fuelled the Free Officers coup of July 1952, which abolished the monarchy. Egypt became a Khedivate (autonomous Ottoman vassal) in 1867 and an autonomous state with the Arab world's oldest continuous parliamentary tradition, dating to an 1866 assembly.

## Nasser's Republic and the Struggle with Israel

Gamal Abdel Nasser consolidated power in 1956, nationalised the Suez Canal (triggering the Suez Crisis), and formed the United Arab Republic with Syria (1958–1961). In the 1967 Six-Day War, Israel captured Sinai and the Gaza Strip, after which an Emergency Law expanded police powers and restricted rights until 2012. His successor, Anwar Sadat, shifted Egypt toward the United States, fought the 1973 war, then signed the Camp David Accords (1978) and a 1979 peace treaty with Israel. The treaty cost Egypt its Arab League membership but made Sadat domestically popular. He was assassinated in 1981 and succeeded by Hosni Mubarak, whose rule included the 1997 Luxor massacre (62 dead) and restricted political freedoms.

## Contemporary Egypt

The 2011 revolution ousted Mubarak after 18 days of protests. Mohamed Morsi of the Muslim Brotherhood won the 2012 election but was removed by the military on 3 July 2013 after mass protests; crackdowns killed hundreds, including 595 protesters on 14 August 2013. Abdel Fattah el-Sisi won the presidency in 2014 with 96.1% and secured a third six-year term in December 2023. Freedom House rated Egypt "Not Free" in 2024, and the Economist Democracy Index scored it 2.93 (authoritarian). Concerns include an estimated 60,000 political prisoners since 2013, mass death sentences, de facto criminalisation of homosexuality, and severe press restrictions.

Egypt is a semi-presidential republic with a House of Representatives elected to five-year terms. Law blends Islamic and Napoleonic civil law, with Sharia governing personal status.

## Economy

Egypt ranks 44th in the world (2025). The economy depends on remittances ($36.5 billion in fiscal year 2024/25), trade (40% of GDP), manufacturing (about 22% of Africa's manufacturing value), hydrocarbons (including the Zohr gas field, the Mediterranean's largest reserve), agriculture, tourism (a record 15.7 million visitors and $14.1 billion in revenue in 2024), and the Suez Canal, which generated a record $9.4 billion in 2023. US aid has averaged $1.5 billion annually since 1979.

## Culture

Egypt sets cultural trends across the Arab world. Its cinema, often called "Arab Hollywood," has produced more than 4,000 films, and Naguib Mahfouz was the first Arabic-language Nobel laureate in literature. Al-Azhar University is a leading centre of Islamic learning. Football dominates sport: Al Ahly holds 20 international trophies, and the national team has won seven Africa Cup of Nations titles.

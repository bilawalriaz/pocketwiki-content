# 1911 Revolution

The 1911 Revolution, also called the Xinhai Revolution, overthrew the Qing dynasty, China's last imperial house, and on 1 January 1912 established the Republic of China in Nanjing. It ended more than two thousand years of monarchy and opened a republican era whose politics soon collapsed into warlord rule.

## Why the Qing collapsed

The Qing had been failing for a generation. The Opium Wars and the First Sino-Japanese War of 1895 exposed military weakness to foreign powers, and reform efforts deepened the crisis. The Hundred Days' Reform of 1898 was crushed before it could take hold, and the Boxer Rebellion of 1900 drew foreign intervention that further humiliated the court. By 1911 the dynasty still ruled in name but had lost the capacity to command obedience.

Opposition crystallised outside the system. In 1905 Sun Yat-sen founded the Tongmenghui, or United League, a clandestine alliance that called for a republic rather than a reformed monarchy. It drew recruits from overseas Chinese communities, students, and secret societies, and between 1895 and 1911 it launched a series of failed uprisings. These attempts failed militarily but kept the idea of revolution alive in provincial cities and exile networks. The reformers of 1898 had wanted to save the monarchy by modernising it; the revolutionaries concluded the monarchy itself was the obstacle. That disagreement shaped the next decade.

## The revolution in 1911

On 10 October 1911 soldiers of the New Army, modernised Qing units whose officers had absorbed revolutionary ideas, seized the city of Wuchang. Their success was unexpected and contagious: within weeks, most provinces declared independence from the Qing and formed their own military governments. Provincial officials and garrison commanders, sensing the court could no longer punish them, switched sides.

The movement was not unified. Some participants wanted to expel the Manchu ruling house and restore Han Chinese rule, others wanted regional autonomy, and only a minority, organised around the Tongmenghui, pushed for a full republic. That mixture of motives left the post-revolution settlement unstable from the start.

## From empire to republic, and back to dictatorship

On 29 December 1911 revolutionary delegates elected Sun Yat-sen Provisional President, and the Republic of China was formally proclaimed in Nanjing on 1 January 1912. The new state controlled little real power. The Qing court, hoping to preserve itself, had appointed Yuan Shikai, a senior general, as Prime Minister on 1 November 1911.

A compromise followed. Sun Yat-sen resigned the presidency on the condition that Yuan pressure the boy emperor to abdicate. The Xuantong Emperor, Puyi, aged six, formally stepped down on 12 February 1912, ending the imperial line. Yuan Shikai was inaugurated President in Beijing on 10 March 1912, and the capital effectively shifted north.

Yuan's republicanism was thin. In December 1915 he declared himself Emperor, provoking nationwide opposition that forced him to abandon the title by March 1916. He died in June 1916, and with no accepted successor the central government fractured. The next decade was the warlord era, in which regional militarists, not presidents, held power.

## What actually changed

The revolution changed the symbol at the top of the state from emperor to president, and it did so permanently: no one seriously tried to restore the dynasty afterward. It made republican language, constitutions, and elections part of Chinese political vocabulary, and both the Republic of China, on Taiwan, and the People's Republic of China, on the mainland, still claim Sun Yat-sen as a founding figure.

It did not transform the underlying social order. Landholding, the gentry, and the military-bureaucratic elite survived largely intact, and real power passed from one set of strongmen to another. That gap between political form and social reality is why historians disagree about whether the revolution succeeded: by 1916 the country had a republican flag, a constitutional vocabulary, and no republican government.

# Aftermath of World War II

World War II's end in 1945 produced a radically reorganized world. Two powers, the United States and the Soviet Union, emerged with the military reach, industrial base, and political weight to dominate global affairs. Their wartime alliance collapsed into a decades-long confrontation known as the Cold War, fought through espionage, political subversion, and proxy wars rather than direct combat. Both sides possessed nuclear weapons capable of destroying each other even after a first strike, a condition called mutually assured destruction that discouraged direct war.

## A Divided Europe

The continent was physically and economically shattered. Roughly 70% of European industrial infrastructure was destroyed, tens of millions had been killed, and tens of millions more were displaced. Recovery was shaped by which occupying power controlled which territory.

In the West, U.S. Secretary of State George C. Marshall launched the European Recovery Program in 1947, commonly called the Marshall Plan. From 1948 to 1952 it delivered about $13 billion (roughly $140 billion in 2024 dollars) to rebuild Western Europe. In the East, the Soviet Union installed Communist governments in Poland, Czechoslovakia, Hungary, Romania, Bulgaria, Albania, and East Germany, creating satellite states that exported machinery and raw materials back to the USSR. Yugoslavia under Josip Broz Tito was an exception: Communist, but independent of Moscow, because Tito's partisans had liberated their own country.

Germany was split into four occupation zones, consolidated in 1949 into the Federal Republic of Germany (West) and the German Democratic Republic (East). About 10 million ethnic Germans were expelled from territories in Eastern Europe. The Saar was detached and placed under French economic administration in 1947. West Germany initially faced de-industrialization, with over 700 manufacturing plants dismantled, but by 1950 Western policy shifted toward reconstruction as American officials recognized that European recovery required German industry. By the late 1950s, West German output had doubled pre-war levels.

Winston Churchill's March 1946 speech in Fulton, Missouri publicly named the divide as an "Iron Curtain." The United States built institutions to contain Soviet influence: the Truman Doctrine of 1947 extended military and economic aid to Greece and Turkey to block Communist expansion; NATO formed in 1949; and the European Coal and Steel Community, created in 1951, pooled French and German heavy industry to make another war between them materially impossible. The Coal and Steel Community evolved into the European Economic Community and eventually the European Union.

## The Cold War in Asia

The same superpower rivalry reshaped Asia. Korea, divided at the 38th parallel for Soviet and American occupation in 1945, became two separate states in 1948 and a battlefield in 1950 when North Korea invaded the South. In China, the wartime truce between the Kuomintang (KMT) and the Chinese Communist Party (CCP) collapsed in 1946; by 1949 Mao Zedong's CCP controlled the mainland, founding the People's Republic of China, while Chiang Kai-shek's KMT retreated to Taiwan. The Soviets dismantled Japanese industrial assets in Manchuria, turning the region into a staging ground for Communist forces.

In French Indochina, the Viet Minh seized power during the August Revolution of 1945, and the First Indochina War began in 1946, ending in 1954 with French withdrawal and a temporarily partitioned Vietnam. The Dutch tried to reconquer Indonesia after 1945, using some Marshall Plan funds for the effort, but recognized Indonesian sovereignty in December 1949. British Malaya faced a Communist insurgency from 1948 to 1960.

## Decolonization and the United Nations

The war accelerated the collapse of European and Japanese empires. India and Pakistan became independent in 1947 after partition along religious lines, producing one of history's largest mass migrations. The State of Israel was established in 1948 from the former British Mandate of Palestine after the 1948 Palestine War. Sub-Saharan African colonies gained independence through the 1950s and 1960s. Indonesia became independent in 1949 and the Philippines in 1946.

The United Nations, founded on 24 October 1945, replaced the failed League of Nations. Its Security Council gave permanent seats with veto power to the United States, United Kingdom, France, Soviet Union, and Republic of China (later transferred to the People's Republic of China in 1971). The 1948 Universal Declaration of Human Rights set a common standard, though the Soviet Union abstained and the United States did not ratify its economic and social provisions.

## Human Cost and Retribution

Soviet losses were catastrophic: the population fell by about 27 million, including 8.7 million military dead, and roughly a quarter of the USSR's capital resources were destroyed. After the war, the Allies repatriated about 4.2 million Soviet citizens from Western zones, including prisoners of war and forced laborers, with force used against those who resisted. Declassified records show 58% of returnees were cleared, 33.5% were mobilized into military or labor service, and 6.5% were sent to NKVD (Soviet secret police) special camps; many faced social stigma for decades.

Rape was widespread during the Soviet advance. Historians estimate around 1.9 million German women were raped by Red Army soldiers, about one-third of all women in Berlin, with Berlin hospital records indicating 95,000 to 130,000 rapes and roughly 10,000 deaths, many by suicide. Smaller numbers of rapes by U.S. and British troops were documented in Germany, France, and Britain. In Japan, especially in port cities and Okinawa, American occupation forces committed documented rapes in the first months of occupation; the British Commonwealth Occupation Force in Kure faced similar accusations.

## Occupation, Scientists, and the Arms Race

Both superpowers recruited German expertise to fuel the rivalry. The American Operation Paperclip brought about 1,600 German scientists and roughly $10 billion in patents and industrial processes to the United States. The Soviet Operation Osoaviakhim in 1946 used 92 trains to transport an estimated 10,000 to 15,000 German specialists and their families to the USSR, transplanting research centers such as Mittelwerk.

Nuclear weapons defined the new strategic reality. The U.S. held a monopoly until the Soviet Union's first test in 1949, after which an arms race followed. Eisenhower's "massive retaliation" doctrine threatened nuclear response to any Soviet non-nuclear incursion; the Soviets called this "atomic blackmail." No peace treaty was ever signed between Japan and Russia because the Kuril Islands, seized by the USSR in 1945, remain disputed.

Denazification in West Germany was scaled back by 1951, and former Nazi Party members soon occupied senior government posts. Unexploded ordnance from the war still forces evacuations in Europe, with thousands of bombs recovered each year. An estimated 1 million metric tons of chemical weapons were dumped into the world's oceans between 1945 and the late 1940s, with poorly recorded locations, and they continue to corrode on the seafloor.

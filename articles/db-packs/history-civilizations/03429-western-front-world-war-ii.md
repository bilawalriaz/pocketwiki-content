# Western Front (World War II)

The Western Front was the Western European theatre of World War II, fought between the Allied powers and Nazi Germany. It ran in two distinct phases: a rapid German victory in 1940, and an Allied advance from Normandy in June 1944 to the Elbe in May 1945.

## Axis victory in 1940

After war was declared in September 1939, a **Phoney War** of little ground combat lasted until April 1940. Germany then invaded Denmark and Norway, and in May launched the **Battle of France**. German forces used **blitzkrieg**, concentrated combined-arms thrusts of tanks, infantry, and air support that broke Allied lines, encircled large formations, and prevented organised defence. Within roughly six weeks, Germany occupied Luxembourg, the Netherlands, Belgium, and most of France, and the British Expeditionary Force was evacuated by sea from Dunkirk.

France signed an armistice on 22 June 1940, splitting the country into an occupied north and a collaborationist **Vichy** regime in the south. Britain, undefeated, including after the Battle of Britain, became the sole Western opponent of Germany.

## Build-up to the second front

Germany turned its main effort eastward against the Soviet Union. Along the Atlantic coast, German engineers built the **Atlantic Wall**, coastal fortifications intended to repel an amphibious landing. Inside Germany, the **Siegfried Line** (Westwall) defended the western border.

While the Western Front was quiet, Allied ground action shifted to the Mediterranean, and a parallel air campaign tried to break German industry: RAF Bomber Command struck cities by night, while the US Eighth Air Force hit military and industrial targets by day. An August 1942 raid on the French port of Dieppe was a disaster, but lessons about beach exits, armoured support, and air cover shaped the cross-Channel invasion. By 1944, German forces in the west were thin but spread along the entire coast because the landing site could not be predicted.

## Liberation and collapse, 1944–1945

Operation Overlord on 6 June 1944 opened the long-promised second front. After a slow breakout from the Normandy beachhead, Allied forces advanced so quickly that supply lines could not keep up, a problem eased by the **Red Ball Express**, a truck convoy system running from the beaches to the front. Paris was liberated on 25 August, and Operation Dragoon cleared southern France.

The advance then stalled against the Siegfried Line and the limits of Allied logistics. Operation Market Garden, an airborne attempt to seize Rhine crossings ahead of the ground troops, failed at Arnhem. In December 1944, Germany committed its last strategic reserve in the Ardennes, the Battle of the Bulge, creating a temporary dent in the Allied line before being repulsed.

In early 1945, Allied forces fought through the Rhineland, taking roughly 280,000 German prisoners, and crossed the Rhine at several points, most famously the Remagen bridge on 7 March. The US 12th Army Group then encircled about 300,000 German troops in the Ruhr Pocket.

## How the war ended in the West

As Allied forces closed on the Rhine, the Red Army was approaching Berlin from the east, raising the question of whether the Western Allies should race for the capital. Supreme Commander Eisenhower judged that capturing Berlin would be costly, that German forces in the south and west still had to be destroyed, and that the post-war division of Germany would be settled politically. He halted the Western advance along the Elbe and Mulde rivers, leaving the capital to the Soviets; Churchill and others had urged a dash for Berlin, and the choice remains debated between military prudence and political opportunity. Germany signed unconditional surrender on 7 May 1945, with operations ceasing on 8 May.

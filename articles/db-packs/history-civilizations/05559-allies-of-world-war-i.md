# Allies of World War I

The Allies of the First World War were a coalition that began as the Triple Entente, the loose counter-bloc to the Central Powers, and grew into a 20-plus nation alliance by 1918. Their central problem was binding together states with clashing empires, competing colonies, and secret treaties, then directing them to win a war few had expected to last long.

## Origins of the Entente (1902–1907)

By 1900, Britain had abandoned its policy of **splendid isolation**, the refusal of formal alliances, in response to German naval expansion and the costs of the Second Boer War. It negotiated three agreements that formed the Entente:

- **Anglo-Japanese Alliance (1902)**: mutual defence in East Asia, letting Britain concentrate the Royal Navy in the North Sea.
- **Entente Cordiale with France (1904)**: settled colonial disputes, then evolved into military staff talks. By 1911, Britain secretly committed a 100,000-strong British Expeditionary Force to France within two weeks of war breaking out.
- **Anglo-Russian Convention (1907)**: settled disputes in Persia, Afghanistan, and Tibet, completing the Triple Entente.

These were not formal alliances. They settled colonial disputes to allow informal cooperation. France and Russia had a deeper 1894 Franco-Russian Alliance, a formal military pact. Russia's defeat by Japan in 1905 and France's loss of Alsace-Lorraine in 1871 gave both a powerful motive to coordinate against Germany.

## How the Coalition Triggered in 1914

The assassination of Archduke Franz Ferdinand on 28 June 1914 gave Austria-Hungary a pretext to attack Serbia. Russia mobilised to defend fellow Slavs on 30 July; Germany declared war on Russia on 1 August and on France on 3 August. Germany's plan required sweeping through Belgium to knock France out before Russia could fully mobilise, an inevitable trigger for British entry, since the 1839 Treaty of London guaranteed Belgian neutrality. Germany demanded free passage on 3 August, was refused, and invaded on the morning of 4 August. Britain declared war on Germany the same day, and Japan declared war on 23 August.

## Principal Powers and Their Stakes

**Britain and the British Empire** entered automatically with all Dominions (Canada, Australia, New Zealand, Newfoundland, South Africa) and colonies, including the British Raj, whose army of over one million served from France to the Middle East. Cabinet oversight shifted from the British War Cabinet to the Imperial War Cabinet in 1917.

**France** was motivated by revanchism, the desire to recover Alsace-Lorraine lost to Germany in 1871. Its lower birth rate (39.6 million people versus Germany's 64.9 million in 1911) made it depend on Russian pressure in the East and on colonial troops; over 500,000 colonial soldiers served on the Western Front, though front-line combat remained with metropolitan French units.

**Russia** was militarily stronger than in 1905 but the 1917 October Revolution pulled it out. Lenin signed the Treaty of Brest-Litovsk (3 March 1918), ceding huge eastern territories.

**Italy** was bound to Germany and Austria-Hungary by the 1882 Triple Alliance but stayed neutral in 1914, claiming Austria's aggression against Serbia voided the pact. The April 1915 Treaty of London traded Italian entry for promises of Austrian-held "lost territories" (Trieste, Trentino, Dalmatia). Italy declared war on Austria in May 1915 but not on Germany until 1916, and felt cheated by the 1919 settlement, a grievance that fed Mussolini's rise.

**Japan** sought to seize German Pacific colonies. Within weeks it captured the German treaty port of Tsingtao and occupied the Mariana, Caroline, and Marshall islands. Japan's 1915 Twenty-One Demands on China poisoned relations with the other Allies, who came to see it as a threat rather than a partner.

**United States**: declared war on 6 April 1917 after German unrestricted submarine warfare and the Zimmermann Telegram, a German offer to help Mexico recover territory lost in the 1848 war. It entered as an "associated power" rather than a formal ally, avoiding the "foreign entanglements" its Senate had long resisted.

## Smaller Allies and Their Costs

Smaller states joined for specific territorial or strategic reasons, with devastating results.

- **Belgium** fought on throughout, holding the Yser Front even after Germany occupied 95% of the country, and provided 25,000 Congolese troops and 260,000 porters in East Africa.
- **Serbia**, exhausted by the Balkan Wars of 1912–1913, suffered the worst proportional losses of any combatant: over 25% of mobilised Serbs became casualties, and including civilians and disease, over 1.2 million died, around 30% of the entire pre-war population. Occupation in late 1915 forced the Serbian army to retreat across Albania.
- **Romania** joined in August 1916 hoping for equal status and was overwhelmed on a 1,600-km front with only 50,000 Russian allies, losing Bucharest by December. It re-entered the war on 10 November 1918, the day before the armistice.
- **Greece** was split by the "National Schism" between the pro-Entente Prime Minister Venizelos and the pro-German King Constantine, a division that forced Constantine into exile in June 1917.
- **Portugal**, **Brazil** (1917, after German submarines sank its merchant ships), **China**, **Siam**, and several Latin American states entered as the US did, often as "associated" rather than formal allies, with limited but real contributions: medical missions, naval patrols, a few divisions.

The 1919 Paris Peace Conference classified the British Empire, France, Italy, Japan, and the US as the "Principal Allied and Associated Powers." Britain, France, Italy, and Japan became the four permanent members of the League of Nations Executive Council; the US Senate refused to ratify the treaty, excluding the United States from the League entirely. Romania, Serbia, and Belgium, despite their sacrifices, were relegated to secondary status at the bargaining table.

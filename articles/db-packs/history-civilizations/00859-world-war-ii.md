# World War II

World War II (1939–1945) was a global war between the Axis powers (Germany, Italy, Japan) and the Allies (principally Britain, France, the Soviet Union, the United States, and China). It killed 60–75 million people, the majority of them civilians, and remains the deadliest conflict in history.

## Origins

Three forces converged to make another major war likely by the late 1930s. First, the Treaty of Versailles (1919) humiliated Germany with territorial losses, military restrictions, and reparations, feeding revanchism (a desire to reverse the defeat). Second, fascist and militarist governments pursued territorial expansion: Adolf Hitler's Germany in Europe, Benito Mussolini's Italy in the Mediterranean and Africa, and Imperial Japan in East Asia. Third, the League of Nations, the body created after World War I to prevent aggression, lacked enforcement power and failed to respond effectively to Japan's 1931 invasion of Manchuria or Italy's 1935 invasion of Abyssinia (Ethiopia). Britain and France also pursued a policy of appeasement, conceding to some German demands in hopes of avoiding war, which failed to halt Hitler.

## Escalation (1939–1941)

On 1 September 1939, Germany invaded Poland. Britain and France declared war two days later. The Soviet Union seized eastern Poland under its August 1939 non-aggression pact with Germany (the Molotov–Ribbentrop Pact, a treaty of neutrality between the two). Germany then conquered Denmark, Norway, the Low Countries (Netherlands, Belgium, Luxembourg), and France by mid-1940. Italy, having entered the war in 1940, suffered setbacks in North Africa and the Balkans. In Asia, Japan deepened its war against China. On 7 December 1941, Japan attacked Pearl Harbor, drawing the United States into the war.

The Axis strategy of **Blitzkrieg** ("lightning war") used fast-moving tanks, infantry, and air support to bypass fortified lines and encircle opposing armies. This produced rapid victories in 1939–1941 but overextended German supply lines.

## Turning Points (1942–1943)

The tide turned in three decisive theaters. The Battle of Midway (June 1942) halted Japanese expansion across the Pacific. In North Africa, Allied forces pushed Axis troops back. On the Eastern Front, the Soviet encirclement of the German Sixth Army at Stalingrad (February 1943) marked the beginning of Germany's long retreat. The Allies also invaded Sicily and mainland Italy, leading to Mussolini's fall.

## Allied Victory (1944–1945)

On 6 June 1944, the D-Day landings opened a Western Front in France. The Soviets advanced from the east, capturing Berlin in late April 1945. Adolf Hitler committed suicide on 30 April, and Germany surrendered on 8 May (VE Day). In the Pacific, U.S. forces captured key islands, leapfrogging Japanese positions. After warnings to surrender were ignored, the United States dropped atomic bombs on Hiroshima (6 August) and Nagasaki (9 August). Japan formally surrendered on 2 September 1945 (VJ Day).

## Aftermath

The war ended European global dominance. Europe divided into a Western bloc led by the United States and an Eastern bloc dominated by the Soviet Union, a boundary later called the **Iron Curtain** and the start of the **Cold War** (a decades-long rivalry without direct fighting between the two superpowers). The United Nations was founded in 1945 to replace the failed League of Nations. Decolonization accelerated in Asia and Africa, and Germany and Japan were rebuilt under Allied occupation. The **Nuremberg Trials** prosecuted surviving German leaders for war crimes, crimes against humanity, and aggressive war, establishing that individuals, not just states, could be held accountable under international law.

The **Holocaust**, the Nazi regime's systematic murder of roughly six million Jews and millions of other victims (including Roma, disabled people, and Soviet prisoners of war), occurred during the war and is central to its moral legacy.

## Open Questions

Historians still debate the war's precise start, citing Japan's 1931 invasion of Manchuria or the 1936 Spanish Civil War. Some scholars argue that resistance to Japanese expansion in China could have been far more effective had the major Western powers acted earlier and more forcefully. The end date is also contested: V-J Day (15 August 1945) versus Japan's formal surrender on 2 September. Whether the atomic bombings were necessary to end the war, and how far economic motives versus ideology drove Axis aggression, remain active debates.

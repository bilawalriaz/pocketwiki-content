# Force

A force is a vector, an arrow with magnitude and direction, that pushes or pulls an object. Forces change an object's velocity, deform it, or compress it. The SI unit is the newton (N): the force that accelerates one kilogram by one metre per second squared.

Newton's second law is **F = dp/dt** — net force equals the rate of change of momentum. When mass is constant this reduces to **F = m·a**. Because force is a vector, forces in different directions add by the parallelogram rule, and only the vector sum, the net force, determines acceleration.

## Newton's three laws

In an **inertial frame**, a reference frame that is not itself accelerating, three rules hold:

1. **Inertia.** An object keeps its velocity unless a net force acts on it. Galileo reached this by rolling balls down inclines, showing that a force is needed to *change* motion, not to sustain it, and that gravitational acceleration is independent of mass.
2. **F = dp/dt.** Net force equals the rate of momentum change.
3. **Action–reaction.** If body 1 pushes body 2 with force F, body 2 pushes body 1 with the equal and opposite force –F. Internal forces inside a closed system cancel, so total linear momentum is conserved.

**Equilibrium** means zero net force. It is **static** when the object is at rest (gravity balanced by a normal force, for example) or **dynamic** when the object moves at constant velocity (applied force balances friction). Galileo's principle that constant velocity is physically equivalent to rest rules out any absolute rest frame.

## Common classical forces

| Force | Key law |
| :--- | :--- |
| Gravity (near Earth) | F = m·g, g ≈ 9.81 m/s² |
| Gravity (universal) | F = –G·m₁·m₂ / r² (toward the other mass) |
| Lorentz (electromagnetic) | F = q(E + v × B) |
| Spring (Hooke) | F = –k·Δx |
| Static friction | F_sf ≤ μ_sf·F_N (matches applied force up to a limit) |
| Kinetic friction | F_kf = μ_kf·F_N (μ_kf < μ_sf) |
| Centripetal (uniform circular motion) | F = m·v²/r, directed toward the centre |

Normal force, tension, and friction are **contact forces**, transmitted where surfaces or strings touch. They are not fundamental; they emerge from electromagnetic interactions between atoms.

## Quantities built from force

**Impulse** ∫F dt equals the change in momentum. **Work** ∫F·dx equals the change in kinetic energy. **Power** dW/dt equals F·v. A force that can be written as the negative gradient of a potential energy U, **F = –∇U**, is **conservative** and conserves mechanical energy; gravity, springs, and electrostatic force are conservative, while friction and drag are not. For rotation, **torque** τ = r × F replaces force, with τ = I·α and dL/dt = τ, where L is angular momentum.

## Modern revisions

**Special relativity** keeps F = dp/dt but redefines momentum as p = γ·m₀·v, where γ = 1/√(1 – v²/c²). As v approaches c, producing the same acceleration requires ever larger force: F_x = γ³·m·a_x, and transverse components scale as γ·m·a.

**Quantum field theory** reframes forces as momentum exchange. A force between two particles is the *exchange* of a **gauge boson** (photon, gluon, or W/Z), required by a symmetry of space. Feynman diagrams track this: straight lines for matter, wavy lines for bosons.

**General relativity** removes gravity from the list of forces. What we feel as Earth's pull is the curvature of spacetime; free objects follow **geodesics**, the straightest possible paths through that geometry. A falling apple follows a geodesic, and the "force" we attribute to gravity is the push of the ground preventing it from doing so.

## The four fundamental interactions

All known interactions reduce to four:

1. **Strong nuclear**, carried by gluons, binds quarks into protons and neutrons; a residual version binds nucleons into nuclei. **Colour confinement** prevents isolated quarks.
2. **Electromagnetic**, carried by photons, acts between electric charges and unifies electricity, magnetism, and light.
3. **Weak nuclear**, carried by the massive W± and Z⁰ bosons, drives radioactive beta decay and forms no bound states.
4. **Gravitational**, described by general relativity, couples to mass–energy and has no accepted quantum version.

The weak and electromagnetic forces merge into one **electroweak** interaction above roughly 10¹⁵ K, a temperature reached in the early universe. Grand Unified Theories extend this to the strong force at still higher energies but remain untested.

Everyday contact forces (friction, spring, tension, normal push) are not on this list. They are large-scale expressions of electromagnetism, constrained by quantum rules such as the Pauli exclusion principle, which produces **degeneracy pressure** that holds matter up against its own gravity.

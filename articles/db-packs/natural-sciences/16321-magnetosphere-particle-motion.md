# Magnetosphere particle motion

Earth's magnetic field is generated by electrical currents in the liquid outer core and extends into space as the magnetosphere. Field lines trace the force a north magnetic pole would feel, and they channel nearly everything inside: charged particles spiral along them, Birkeland currents flow along them, and heat moves along them far more easily than across them. The underlying reason is the Lorentz force, the magnetic force $q\mathbf{v}\times\mathbf{B}$ that bends particle motion without doing any work.

In the closed configuration, the magnetopause follows the outermost field lines and lets little plasma cross, except through the two polar cusps, funnel-shaped weak points where the field intensity on the boundary drops to zero. By the fixed-point theorem, any closed field topology must have such cusps even without noon–midnight symmetry. How much solar-wind plasma actually enters depends on how far the interplanetary magnetic field tilts southward and threads across this boundary.

## Gyration and the guiding centre

In a uniform magnetic field, a charged particle entering perpendicular to $\mathbf{B}$ moves in a circle, because the magnetic force supplies the centripetal force:

$$R_g = \frac{mv}{qB}$$

This is the gyration radius, sometimes called the Larmor radius. If the velocity has a component $v_\parallel$ along the field as well as $v_\perp$ across it, only $v_\perp$ enters the formula, and the particle spirals around a central guiding field line. The parallel component is unaffected, because the magnetic force has no component along $\mathbf{B}$. When the field curves or varies, the motion keeps the same character of spiralling around a guiding centre.

Using $W_\perp = \tfrac{1}{2}mv_\perp^2$ in electron-volts and $B$ in nanotesla, the gyration radius in kilometres is

- Protons: $R_g = (144/B)\sqrt{W_\perp}$
- Electrons: $R_g = (3.37/B)\sqrt{W_\perp}$

Because the magnetic force is always perpendicular to velocity, it performs no work and changes particle trajectories without any energy input.

## Magnetic mirroring

Where field lines converge, the field grows stronger; where they diverge, it weakens. The magnetic moment $\mu = W_\perp / B$ is an adiabatic invariant, a quantity that stays nearly constant as the field changes slowly, even though it is not an exact constant of motion. Its conservation, pointed out by Hannes Alfvén about fifty years after the phenomenon was first derived, governs what happens when a spiralling particle enters a stronger region: $W_\perp$ must grow to keep $\mu$ fixed. Since the magnetic field does no work, total energy is conserved, and the extra $W_\perp$ comes at the expense of $v_\parallel$. The pitch angle between velocity and field grows until it reaches 90°, at which point the particle has no forward motion and cannot penetrate further. It briefly gyrates perpendicular to the field line, then reverses and unwinds back into the weaker region. This is magnetic mirroring, first derived by Henri Poincaré in 1895 for a charged particle in the field of a magnetic monopole.

In the dipole-like field near Earth, each line is strongest at the ends where it enters the atmosphere and weakest where it crosses the equator. A particle drifting along such a line is mirrored at each end and bounces between hemispheres; this is how the radiation belts and the ring current trap their particles. Particles whose pitch angle is very small never mirror and instead plunge into the atmosphere and are lost. The bundle of directions around the field line emptied this way is the loss cone.

## Gradient drift and the ring current

Trapped particles also drift slowly around Earth, switching guiding field lines while staying at roughly the same distance, governed by a second adiabatic invariant. The drift arises because the magnetic field grows stronger as Earth is approached, so the gyration circle curves more tightly on the inner side. The asymmetry shifts the centre of the circle sideways. Ions and electrons gyrate in opposite senses and drift in opposite directions, and this separation of charge produces the ring current.

## The plasma fountain

A flow of hydrogen, helium, and oxygen ions, the plasma fountain, streams out of the polar regions. Some of this material returns along field lines as the aurora, and the rest escapes to space, both outcomes shaped by the same Lorentz-force dynamics that govern trapped-particle motion closer to Earth.

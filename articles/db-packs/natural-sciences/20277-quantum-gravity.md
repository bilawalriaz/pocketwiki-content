# Quantum gravity

Quantum gravity is the unfinished branch of theoretical physics that merges general relativity, Einstein's description of gravity as curved spacetime, with quantum mechanics, the rules governing matter at subatomic scales. General relativity treats spacetime geometry as a dynamic field reshaped by matter ("spacetime tells matter how to move; matter tells spacetime how to curve"). Quantum field theory is typically built on a fixed, flat background. Combining them requires treating the geometry itself as quantum.

Both effects become strong in the same places: inside black holes, in the first moments after the Big Bang, and near the Planck length, roughly 10⁻³⁵ meters. The energies needed to probe these scales directly far exceed anything achievable in particle accelerators, so physicists lack direct experimental data to judge between competing theories. Indirect motivation comes from failures of general relativity alone: black hole singularities, the inferred presence of dark matter and dark energy, and a vacuum energy discrepancy of 60 to 120 orders of magnitude between prediction and observation.

## Why straightforward quantisation fails

Treating gravity as an ordinary quantum field, the way electromagnetism is treated, produces a perturbatively nonrenormalizable theory. A renormalizable quantum field theory needs only a finite number of parameters fixed by experiment; quantum electrodynamics, for instance, needs only the electron's charge and mass. Quantised gravity, in perturbation theory, needs infinitely many independent counterterm coefficients. At low energies the renormalization group suppresses the extras and predictions reduce to ordinary Einstein gravity. At Planck-scale energies all the unknowns matter and the theory makes no predictions. Three workarounds are pursued. The effective field theory approach applies quantum methods at low energy, yielding testable corrections such as the first quantum correction to the Newtonian potential and corrections to the Bekenstein–Hawking black hole entropy formula. Asymptotic safety seeks a non-perturbative ultraviolet fixed point, a special energy scale where the theory's behaviour becomes well-defined and predictive again. String theory instead introduces new symmetries that constrain the parameters to a finite set.

## Two leading approaches

String theory replaces point particles with one-dimensional strings whose vibrational modes correspond to different particles. One mode always matches the graviton, the hypothetical massless messenger particle that general relativity's structure seems to demand. The cost is six extra spatial dimensions beyond the familiar three, plus a "string landscape" of roughly 10⁵⁰⁰ consistent vacuum states. M-theory extends this by unifying five consistent string theories and eleven-dimensional supergravity into one framework.

Loop quantum gravity takes the opposite path: it quantises gravity alone, without trying to unify all forces. Its central result is that area and volume have discrete spectra, so space comes in indivisible chunks at the Planck scale. The quantum state of spacetime is encoded in spin networks, graphs labelled with quantum numbers, originally introduced by Roger Penrose and shown by Carlo Rovelli and Lee Smolin to arise naturally from a non-perturbative quantisation of general relativity. Spinfoam models describe how these networks evolve.

## Conceptual mismatches and experimental tests

Deeper conflicts remain beyond renormalisability. Quantum mechanics treats time as a fixed backdrop against which states evolve; general relativity treats time as a dynamical variable tied to matter and geometry, producing the "problem of time" when the two are combined. Locality is also strained: in quantum gravity the metric is dynamic, so whether two points are spacelike separated can itself exist in superposition.

Because direct experiments are out of reach, researchers pursue phenomenology: indirect signatures such as violations of Lorentz invariance, imprints in cosmic microwave background polarization, gravitationally mediated entanglement, and decoherence from spacetime foam. ESA's INTEGRAL satellite constrained the granularity of space to below 10⁻⁴⁸ m, thirteen orders of magnitude beneath the Planck scale, and IceCube and gamma-ray burst observations have placed limits on Lorentz-violating effects.

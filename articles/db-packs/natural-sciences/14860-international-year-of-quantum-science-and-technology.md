# International Year of Quantum Science and Technology

The International Year of Quantum Science and Technology (IYQ) was a United Nations observance running 1 January to 31 December 2025. It aimed to raise public awareness of quantum science and its practical applications, and it was timed to mark the hundredth anniversary of Werner Heisenberg's 1925 *Umdeutung* paper, the work that reformulated quantum mechanics into its modern form.

IYQ began as a grassroots movement among physicists and moved through a formal diplomatic process. In May 2023 the UNESCO Executive Board endorsed the proposal in a resolution co-sponsored by 59 countries. UNESCO's 42nd General Conference passed a follow-up resolution in November 2023, recommending that the UN General Assembly act. Ghana's delegation submitted the resolution to the 78th session of the UN General Assembly in May 2024, and on 7 June 2024 the full Assembly adopted it without objection, by acclamation. The resolution was co-sponsored by more than 70 countries representing over 5 billion people.

Coordination was handled by an IYQ Secretariat convened jointly by UNESCO and the American Physical Society. Two guiding principles shaped the year: "No One Owns Quantum Science" and "Everyone is Invited." UNESCO also stressed equitable development, warning against a widening gap between the global north and the global south as quantum technology matures.

Activities spanned the calendar. The opening ceremony took place at UNESCO Headquarters in Paris on 4–5 February 2025. Over 500 events were registered through the IYQ website, ranging from local school talks to Global Events such as the closing ceremony, held in Ghana in February 2026.

Helgoland 2025 was a conference held 9–14 June on Heligoland, the small North Sea island where Heisenberg retreated to write his 1925 paper. Roughly 300 scientists attended, including Nobel laureates Alain Aspect, John Clauser, Serge Haroche, David J. Wineland and Anton Zeilinger. The programme offered 31 lectures, five panel debates and more than 100 posters, with one panel looking ahead to the next century of quantum technologies.

The Quantum Century Exhibition, launched on 7 November 2025 at Cochin University of Science and Technology in Kochi, Kerala, India, was a statewide travelling outreach campaign. It was jointly run by the Kerala Sasthra Sahithya Parishad through its science-communication portal LUCA, the Centre for Science in Society at CUSAT, and the Curiefy Science Lab, and it was designed to translate quantum history and breakthroughs for the general public, students and educators. To preserve its materials, the Wikimedians of Kerala User Group ran a Wikimedia Commons documentation campaign so that images and resources remain openly accessible.

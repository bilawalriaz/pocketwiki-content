# Black star (semiclassical gravity)

A black star is a hypothetical compact object composed of matter that mimics a black hole from the outside but has no event horizon and no central singularity. It is built with semiclassical gravity, a framework in which matter is treated quantum-mechanically while spacetime stays a classical geometry. Analogous structures are predicted for the Einstein–Maxwell–Dirac and Einstein–Yang–Mills–Dirac systems, the classical limits of quantum electrodynamics and of the Standard Model.

## Formation

In general relativity, a sufficiently massive collapsing star forms a black hole whose event horizon hides a singularity of infinite density. A black star is the alternative that appears when quantum effects inside the matter refuse to let that collapse complete.

A black star forms when infalling matter compresses at a rate significantly slower than the free-fall speed of a hypothetical particle falling toward the object's centre. Vacuum polarization drives the resistance: quantum fields cannot occupy the same point at the same time, so as matter is crushed the vacuum polarises and generates a degeneracy pressure that opposes further compression. This vacuum energy is, in principle, unlimited, so the outward pressure can build faster than gravity can squeeze.

Collapse therefore stalls before reaching zero radius. The result is one of two limiting behaviours: an ever-decreasing collapse rate driving the total collapse time to infinity, or an approach to a finite, non-zero radius that the object asymptotically nears. A black star may be a transitional phase between a collapsing star and a singularity, or it may not occur at all.

## External appearance

A black star whose radius is only slightly larger than the event-horizon radius of an equivalent-mass black hole is almost indistinguishable from one. Nearly every photon emitted near the surface curves back and is recaptured. Photons that escape lose energy climbing out of the deep gravitational well and emerge as severely redshifted light. The surface also produces Hawking radiation together with thermal Planckian radiation that closely resembles the Hawking radiation expected from a true black hole of the same mass.

## Interior

Inside, spacetime is not the smooth vacuum of general relativity but a polarised quantum state. Each radial depth looks the same as a black star of equivalent mass and radius with its outer layers stripped away, and temperature rises toward the centre.

# Philippine Sea

The Philippine Sea is the largest **marginal sea** in the world, a body of water partially enclosed by islands, archipelagos, or peninsulas. It lies in the western Pacific, east of the Philippine archipelago, bordered by the Philippines and Taiwan to the west, Japan to the north, the Mariana Islands to the east, and Palau and Indonesia to the south.

## Geological Foundation

The floor of the Philippine Sea is a single tectonic plate. Along its western edge, the Philippine Sea Plate slides beneath the Philippine Mobile Belt through **subduction**, where one plate sinks beneath another. This collision carved the Philippine Trench and lifted the island arcs—chains of volcanic islands formed by subduction—that rim the basin. The Mariana Trench, on the eastern side, contains the deepest point on Earth.

## Biological Richness

The Philippine Sea sits at the heart of the Coral Triangle, the western Pacific region with the highest coral-reef biodiversity on Earth. Its waters hold more than 3,200 fish species, 486 coral species, and endangered megafauna such as whale sharks and dugongs. Within this region, the Verde Island Passage is called the "center of the center" of marine fish biodiversity. The cause of this exceptional concentration is debated, but geological drivers such as plate tectonics are a leading explanation. These ecosystems sustain roughly 120 million people through fisheries, but face pressure from coral bleaching and ocean acidification driven by climate change, and from unsustainable fishing.

## Economy and Naming

Fisheries are a major economic resource for the Philippines, with the Benham Rise (also called the Philippine Rise) an especially productive ground for migratory species such as tuna. In 2011, Philippine agencies began using "West Philippine Sea" for parts of the adjacent South China Sea. The Philippine Sea proper remains a distinct body of water to the east.

## Human History

The sea's recorded history begins in 1521, when Ferdinand Magellan became the first European to navigate it and named it *Mar Filipinas*. Its most consequential event came in June 1944, when the Battle of the Philippine Sea—the largest carrier-to-carrier battle in history—was fought near the Mariana Islands, and the United States crippled the Japanese navy and air force. In December 1944, Typhoon Cobra struck the U.S. Pacific Third Fleet operating in the sea. In 1965, a U.S. A-4 Skyhawk carrying a nuclear weapon was lost over the Philippine Sea.

# Earth

Earth is the third planet from the Sun and the only astronomical object known to harbor life. That status comes from being the only Solar System planet with stable liquid surface water, sustained by a dynamic atmosphere, an active magnetic field, and ongoing plate tectonics. The biosphere has reshaped the planet for billions of years, and human activity is now altering the climate and biosphere at planetary scale.

## Formation and early history

Earth formed about 4.54 billion years ago (Ga = giga-annum, billion years ago) from gas and dust in the solar nebula. The nebula collapsed into a disk, and planetesimals accreted over roughly 70–100 million years. The leading explanation for the Moon is the giant-impact hypothesis: a Mars-sized body called Theia struck Earth, and ejected material re-accumulated into the Moon. Between 4.0 and 3.8 Ga, the Late Heavy Bombardment reshaped the surface.

The atmosphere and oceans formed by volcanic outgassing, with additional water from asteroids, protoplanets, and comets. Greenhouse gases kept the oceans from freezing under a faint young Sun, when luminosity was only 70% of today's. The magnetic field became established by about 3.5 Ga.

## Geological evolution and life

The first crust was mafic (magnesium- and iron-rich); the first continental crust was felsic (silica-rich), produced by partial melting. Two models describe continental growth: steady growth, supported by radiometric dating, and rapid early growth in the Archean, supported by hafnium and neodymium isotopes. Plate tectonics, driven by heat loss from the interior, assembled and broke supercontinents: Rodinia (~750 Ma), Pannotia (600–540 Ma), and Pangaea (broke apart 180 Ma). Ice ages intensified about 3 Ma, with glaciation cycles of roughly 21,000, 41,000, and 100,000 years.

Self-replicating molecules appeared by about 4 Ga, with the last common ancestor by ~3.5 Ga. Oxygenic photosynthesis evolved around 2.7 Ga, and the resulting oxygen transformed the planet during the Great Oxidation Event (~2 Ga) while also forming the protective ozone layer. Eukaryotes developed when larger cells incorporated smaller ones. The Neoproterozoic "Snowball Earth" hypothesis proposes extensive ice cover before the Cambrian explosion at 535 Ma, when multicellular life diversified rapidly.

## Internal structure

Earth is the densest planetary-mass object and the most massive rocky Solar System body, at 81 lunar masses (about 5.97×10²⁴ kg). Composition is dominated by iron (32.1%), oxygen (30.1%), silicon (15.1%), and magnesium (13.9%). The interior divides into a silicate crust (6 km beneath oceans, 30–50 km beneath continents), a viscous mantle, a liquid outer core, and a solid inner core roughly one-fifth of Earth's radius. The lithosphere (crust plus rigid upper mantle) rests on the low-viscosity asthenosphere. Internal heat comes from primordial heat and radioactive decay of potassium-40, uranium-238, and thorium-232; mean heat loss is 87 mW/m². The seven major plates (Pacific, North American, Eurasian, African, Antarctic, Indo-Australian, South American) move at convergent, divergent, and transform boundaries, producing earthquakes, volcanoes, and mountain ranges.

## Surface, atmosphere, and water

Earth's 510 million km² surface is 70.8% ocean and 29.2% land, ranging from −418 m (Dead Sea) to 8,848 m (Mount Everest). The atmosphere is 78.084% nitrogen, 20.946% oxygen, and 0.934% argon, with trace CO₂ and 0.01–4% water vapor; three-quarters of its mass lies below 11 km. The greenhouse effect, driven by water vapor, CO₂, methane, nitrous oxide, and ozone, raises average surface temperature from −18 °C to +15 °C. The hydrosphere holds ~1.35×10¹⁸ metric tons of water at mean ocean depth 3,682 m and salinity ~35 g/kg; 97.5% is saline, and 68.7% of fresh water is locked in ice.

## Magnetic field, Moon, and orbit

The magnetic field is generated by a dynamo in the liquid outer core, with surface strength 3.05×10⁻⁵ T at the equator; the poles drift and reverse, most recently ~700,000 years ago. The magnetosphere deflects solar wind, with a compressed dayside of ~10 Earth radii and an extended nightside tail. The Moon is about one-quarter Earth's diameter, the largest moon relative to its planet in the Solar System, and its apparent size nearly matches the Sun's, enabling total and annular eclipses. Tidal interactions cause the Moon to recede ~38 mm/year and lengthen Earth's day by ~23 μs/year. Earth's axial tilt of ~23.44° produces the seasons, and the slightly elliptical orbit (perihelion ~3 January, aphelion ~4 July) changes incoming solar energy by about 6.8%.

## Humans and the future

Humans emerged in Eastern Africa 300,000 years ago, with agriculture beginning in the 10th millennium BCE. Population reached eight billion in the 2020s and may peak near 10 billion late this century. Human activity has crossed five of nine planetary boundaries: biosphere integrity, climate change, chemical pollution, habitat destruction, and the nitrogen cycle. Long-term, rising solar luminosity will render Earth uninhabitable: CO₂ may fall lethally low in 100–900 million years, and oceans could evaporate within 1.6–3 billion years. The Sun will become a red giant in ~5 billion years; Earth's fate, vaporization or survival, is unresolved, though a 2026 study suggests survival.

# The arts

The arts are human practices of creative expression, storytelling, and cultural participation. They shape social, cultural, and individual identities and transmit values, ideas, and experiences across time and space. The arts divide into three main branches: visual arts, literature, and performing arts. They remain open to continual redefinition, since modern art shifts its own boundaries through experimentation and self-criticism.

## Definition and scope

The arts include practices and objects made with skill, creativity, and imagination, such as painting, sculpting, music, theatre, and literature. Whether something counts as art has been repeatedly contested, with cases including Cubist and Impressionist paintings, Marcel Duchamp's *Fountain*, J. S. G. Boggs' banknote imitations, conceptual art, and video games.

## History and classifications

In Ancient Greece, art and craft were united under the term *techne* (the root of "technique"), with veneration of the animal form and anatomically correct proportions. Roman art depicted gods as idealized humans with distinguishing attributes, such as Zeus' thunderbolt. Byzantine and Gothic art expressed Christian themes under church dominance, while medieval universities taught the seven liberal arts: the trivium (grammar, rhetoric, logic) and quadrivium (arithmetic, geometry, music, astronomy). Asian traditions in India, Tibet, and Japan emphasize surface patterning and outline-based local colour. Islamic art avoids depicting living beings in religious contexts, using calligraphy and geometric designs instead.

The traditional "Seven Arts" are painting, architecture, sculpture, literature, music, theatre, and filmmaking. Television is sometimes called the "eighth" and comics the "ninth art" in Francophone scholarship. Some arts derive from others: drama is literature performed with acting, dance is music expressed through motion, and songs fuse music, literature, and voice.

## Visual arts

Visual arts include architecture, ceramics, drawing, filmmaking, painting, photography, printmaking, sculpture, and video, plus applied arts such as industrial, graphic, fashion, and interior design. Architecture balances feasibility and cost for builders against function and aesthetics for users, and works often serve as cultural and political symbols. Ceramic art covers pottery, tiles, figurines, and sculptures, but excludes glass and glass-tesserae mosaics. Conceptual art prioritizes ideas over aesthetic or material concerns. Drawing uses tools like graphite, ink, charcoal, or digital equivalents, employing techniques such as hatching, stippling, and blending. Painting serves self-expression through gesture, composition, narration, or abstraction; some painters, including Jean Dubuffet and Anselm Kiefer, mix in sand, cement, straw, wood, or hair. Photography as art reflects the photographer's creative vision, distinct from photojournalism. Sculpture, one of the plastic arts (three-dimensional works such as sculpture and ceramics), operates through carving (removal), modelling (addition), welding, moulding, or casting, with near-complete material freedom after modernism. Applied arts apply design and decoration to functional objects, often overlapping with fine art in practice.

## Literary arts

Literature in Western culture comprises mainly prose (fiction and non-fiction), drama, and poetry. Oral forms worldwide include epic, legend, myth, ballad, oral poetry, and folktales. Comics combine visual art with narrative literature and are called the "ninth art."

## Performing arts

Performing arts, including dance, music, theatre, opera, and mime, are defined by human performance as the principal product and exist in time rather than as fixed objects. Products are repeatable (scripts or scores) or improvised. Dance ranges from functional folk movement to codified virtuoso techniques such as ballet, and also appears in gymnastics, figure skating, synchronized swimming, and martial arts *kata*. Music combines sounds organized by pitch (melody and harmony), duration (rhythm and tempo), intensity (dynamics), and timbre (tone colour), dividing into composition and performance with improvisation as intermediary. Theatre acts out stories using speech, gesture, music, dance, sound, and spectacle, with forms including opera, ballet, mime, kabuki, and Indian classical dance.

## Multidisciplinary works

Opera (Italian for "works") fuses sets, costumes, acting, libretto, singers, and orchestra. Richard Wagner called such fusions *Gesamtkunstwerk*, meaning "synthesis of the arts," exemplified by *Der Ring des Nibelungen*. Performance art combines instruments, objects, and art over time, scripted or improvised, sometimes with audiences; John Cage's 1940 *Living Room Music* is a quartet for non-melodic household objects. Video games combine visuals, sound, and emergent interactive experience. Their artistic status remains debated: Hideo Kojima argued in 2006 that they are a service rather than an art form, while the US National Endowment for the Arts included them among "works of art" in 2011 and the Smithsonian exhibited *The Art of the Video Game* that year.

## Criticism, education, politics, and morality

Art criticism evaluates art within aesthetics or theories of beauty, dividing into historical criticism (art history) and contemporary criticism of living artists. Movements once dismissed by critics, including Impressionism and Cubism, were later adopted as badges of honour. Multiple theories guide appreciation: aesthetic, pragmatic, expressive, formalist, relativist, mimetic, and postmodern. Arts education researches learning through dance, drama, music, literature, storytelling, film, design, digital art, media, and photography. The arts connect strongly to politics, from Alexander Pushkin's independent verse irritating Emperor Alexander I to Banksy's anonymous dissent; "artivism" names art used to promote positive social or political change. In 1963 the Catholic Church declared that the arts are "not exempt" from "the absolute primacy of the objective moral order."

## Open debates

Whether contested works such as Duchamp's *Fountain*, conceptual art, and video games qualify as art remains unsettled, as do the exact definitions of music's core elements and whether art criticism can transcend its sociopolitical circumstances. Judgement of art varies by aesthetics, perception, personal preference, and cultural acceptance, raising persistent questions of subjectivity.

# Music

Music is the deliberate arrangement of sound to produce form, harmony, melody, or rhythm, and it appears in every known human society. Sound is organised into pitch (how high or low), rhythm (when sounds occur and stop), and timbre (the tone colour that lets you tell a flute from a violin on the same note). These combine into melody (pitches in succession), harmony (pitches sounded together as chords within a key), texture (how layers interact), and form (the large shape, such as verse–chorus). Music is created by composing, improvising, and performing, and serves social, religious, and entertainment roles in every culture.

## How music is built

Rhythm places sounds and silences in time, usually grouped into a recurring metre. Texture names how layers relate: *monophony* (one line alone), *heterophony* (one line with ornamental variants), *polyphony* (independent lines woven together), and *homophony* (one melody supported by chords). Expression covers vibrato, loudness changes, tempo fluctuation, and articulation. Form is the large-scale shape: AABA (a 32-bar jazz/pop pattern), verse–chorus, strophic (repeating verses), sonata-allegro, fugue, or rondo.

National curricula disagree on which elements are essential. Australia lists pitch, timbre, texture, dynamics, rhythm, and form; the UK adds duration and tempo (renamed "inter-related dimensions" in 2013); the USA adds harmony and style. The disagreement itself is a clue: no element is universally accepted as the defining one.

## A compressed timeline

Around 43,000 years ago, the oldest widely accepted instruments appear: bone flutes from Geissenklösterle cave in Germany's Swabian Jura. By about 1400 BCE the Hurrian Hymn to Nikkal (Ugarit) is the oldest surviving notated work, and the Seikilos epitaph the oldest complete composition. The Medieval era (500–1400) brought Gregorian chant, notated by the Catholic Church so melodies could travel its empire. The Renaissance (c. 1400–1600) favoured secular themes and rich polyphony; the printing press (c. 1450) spread sheet music rapidly. The Baroque (1600–1750) produced opera, fugue, sonata, and concerto (Bach, Handel, Vivaldi). The Classical period (1730–1820) standardised instrumentation, pursued balanced homophonic texture, and developed public concerts, freeing Haydn, Mozart, and the young Beethoven from aristocratic dependence. Romanticism (c. 1820–1900) expanded orchestras with Industrial Revolution instruments and pushed emotion and nationalism.

In the late 19th and early 20th centuries jazz emerged in African American communities of the Southern US, built on blue notes, improvisation, polyrhythms, syncopation, and swung notes. Rock developed in the 1950s from rock and roll, rockabilly, blues, and country, and revolves around guitars and a strong back beat. In the 20th century radio and gramophone records replaced sheet music as the main way people encountered music; multitrack recording reshaped rock, and later digital synthesis (1980s), computer tools (1990s), and inexpensive soft synths and bedroom production (2020s) progressively lowered the cost of making it.

## Creation and performance

Composition ranges from fully written scores to lead sheets (melody, lyrics, and chords) to memory-based traditions. *Aleatoric* music (Cage, Feldman, Lutosławski) introduces controlled chance. Performance always involves *interpretation*: the performer's choices of tempo, phrasing, and style, even when the score is precise. Improvisation was central in Baroque and Classical practice (ornaments, the basso continuo of improvised chords over a bass line, cadenzas), declined as common-practice performance was institutionalised, and remains central in jazz, blues, and Indian classical music, where a *raga* (melodic framework) and *tala* (rhythmic cycle) organise performance.

## Some non-Western traditions

Indian classical music (*marga*) is monophonic, organised through raga and tala; Carnatic music is largely devotional. Indonesian traditional music centres on percussion, including gamelan orchestras, gong chimes, angklung, and sasando. Chinese classical music spans about 3,000 years and uses twelve notes per octave. Arabic distinguishes *musiqi* (usually instrumental and metric) from *khandan* (vocal and improvised); Hindi *sangita* properly refers to art music. Many languages lack a single word matching the broad Western scope, and the Greek root *mousiké technē* ("art of the Muses") shows the category itself is cultural.

## Live questions

The definition of music is open: scholars agree it has elements but disagree on which are necessary, and some cultures lack a matching word. Origins are debated, whether music arose before, with, or after language, and whether it is an adaptation or, as one phrase has it, "auditory cheesecake," a pleasurable byproduct of other mental faculties. Darwin's 1871 sexual-selection theory was long dismissed but has been revived by 21st-century scholars; alternatives tie music to labour coordination, long-distance communication, divine contact, group cohesion, or predator defence. The Divje Babe "flute," a perforated cave bear femur at least 40,000 years old, is disputed as an instrument. Hanslick and Wagner disagreed on whether music can express meaning at all. Music therapy draws on the physical, emotional, mental, social, aesthetic, and spiritual facets of listening and playing to improve health, with roots at least as far back as Al-Farabi in the 10th century.

The mental model to keep: music is organised sound, built from pitch, rhythm, and timbre into melody, harmony, texture, and form; created by composition, improvisation, and performance; universal across cultures yet defined differently in each; its history is one of expanding access, from chanted liturgy to printing, recording, streaming, and now generative AI.

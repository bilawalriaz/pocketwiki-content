# Climate change in Indonesia

Indonesia is both a major emitter of greenhouse gases and one of the countries most exposed to their effects. It is the world's largest archipelago, with more than 17,000 islands and a long coastline, and its forests, peatlands, and coal-based energy make it the fifth-largest emitter globally. Emissions rose 2.2% between 2014 and 2019.

## Why Indonesia is highly exposed

Indonesia sits almost entirely in the humid tropics, with average temperatures near 28°C and humidity up to 90%. Under a high-emissions scenario, average temperatures are projected to rise 1.6°C by 2050 and 3.9°C by 2100, and heatwaves are expected to last about 8% longer and occur 98% more often, intensifying droughts, runoff, and floods.

Around 42 million people live below 10 metres elevation, and Jakarta is widely ranked the world's most climate-vulnerable city. Oceanic warming and acidification bleach coral reefs and reduce plankton at the base of the food web, which reduces fish stocks. Indonesia holds 24% of the world's mangroves, but 40% have been degraded or lost in three decades, removing nurseries for fish and natural barriers against storms.

## Emissions: deforestation, peat, and coal

Two mechanisms dominate. Clearing tropical forests releases the carbon stored in trees. Draining and burning peatlands releases far more. Peatlands are waterlogged soils where plant matter accumulates faster than it decays, locking away large carbon stores; once drained, the peat dries, oxidises, and burns. Indonesia has 160,000–270,000 km² of peatlands, mostly on coastal lowlands, and peat fires have been responsible for up to 5% of global annual CO₂ emissions in some years, with haze causing major respiratory illness. Expansion of palm-oil, logging, and mining plantations drives most forest loss. Coal still supplied about 85% of the energy mix in 2023, and Indonesia is a major producer of coal, gold, and nickel.

## Sea level rise and the sinking of Jakarta

The global sea rises about 3–10 mm per year, but Jakarta sinks 75–100 mm per year because of groundwater extraction, so the relative rise is nearly 10 cm annually. Continued emissions and unchecked pumping are projected to submerge 95% of northern Jakarta by 2050, a primary reason the government is moving the capital to East Kalimantan on Borneo. Inherited Dutch colonial water infrastructure left most residents without piped water (about 60% coverage in 2019), pushing them to drill illegal wells.

## Impacts on people and ecosystems

Agriculture employs millions and supplies exports such as palm oil, cocoa, coffee, rice, spices, tea, coconuts, fruit, and tobacco. Higher heat stress and shifting rainfall reduce yields, worsen pests, and degrade soil. Fishing contributed 2.77% of GDP in 2021 and supports roughly 12 million jobs; catch potential could fall about 20% at 1.5°C of warming. From 1998 to 2018, about 80% of climate-related disasters were floods, windstorms, landslides, and drought. Health effects include heat illness, respiratory disease from haze, vector- and water-borne disease, and malnutrition. Indonesia holds about 12% of the world's mammal species, and 25% of its native mammals are endangered, including Sumatran elephants, tigers, and orangutans.

## Policy and the gap with results

Under the Copenhagen Accord and the Paris Agreement, Indonesia pledged to cut emissions 29% by 2030 (41% with international support), restore 12 million hectares of degraded peatland and forest, and raise renewables to 23% by 2025 and 31% by 2030. The REDD+ programme pays developing countries to protect forests, but progress is slow: the renewable share moved only from 9.8% in 2015 to 11.2% in 2020, and emissions from land use remain high. A 2019 YouGov–Cambridge survey found Indonesia had the highest share of climate-change deniers surveyed, at 18%, with climate education absent from the school curriculum. Between 2011 and 2023, the country mobilised US$78 billion for energy transition and forest conservation, but subsidies to polluting sectors, land-tenure conflicts, and weak governance limit the results.

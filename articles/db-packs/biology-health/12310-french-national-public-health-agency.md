# French National Public Health Agency

The French National Public Health Agency (*Santé publique France*, or ANSP) is the single national body responsible for watching over the health of the French population. It is a public administrative establishment under the Ministry of Health, headquartered in Saint-Maurice, Val-de-Marne. Its work concentrates on epidemiological surveillance, prevention, health promotion, and crisis response, functions France previously distributed across separate bodies.

## Origin

Santé publique France was created on 1 May 2016 under the Health System Modernization Law (Law No. 2016-41 of 26 January 2016) and Ordinance No. 2016-462 of 14 April 2016. The agency's founding director general, François Bourdillon, had led two of the predecessor agencies and submitted a preliminary report on 2 June 2015 to Marisol Touraine, then Minister of Social Affairs, Health, and Women's Rights. The new agency merged three health agencies and absorbed one addiction-information service:

- **InVS** (*Institut de veille sanitaire*, 1998): continuous health surveillance, monitoring, alerts, and crisis management.
- **Inpes** (*Institut national de prévention et d'éducation pour la santé*, 2002): prevention programs, health promotion expertise, and management of urgent health situations. Inpes had itself replaced the French Committee for Health Education (CFES).
- **Éprus** (*Établissement de préparation et de réponse aux urgences sanitaires*, 2007): the health reserve and strategic pharmaceutical stocks.
- **Adalis** (*Addictions Drogues Alcool Info Service*): drug, alcohol, and addiction information.

The merger reflected a deliberate design choice. Sibling agencies such as the National Agency for the Safety of Medicines and Health Products (ANSM), the National Agency for Food, Environmental, and Occupational Health Safety (Anses), and the High Authority of Health (HAS) each cover a specific slice of risk or practice quality. Santé publique France instead takes a population-wide perspective, modeled in part on the US Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) and Public Health England.

## Mission and priorities

The agency's core task is continuous epidemiological surveillance, the ongoing collection and analysis of health data across a population. This is used to monitor population health, tailor health policy, detect emerging risks early, and reduce health inequalities through prevention and health promotion. The agency also pilots experimental programs across French territories and aligns its work with the first pillar of the 2018–2022 National Health Strategy: promoting health and prevention across all settings.

The Ministry of Health sets five-year objectives through a Contract of Objectives and Performance (COP). The first COP, signed by Agnès Buzyn in 2018, outlined six strategic and 22 operational goals covering surveillance, prevention, crisis preparedness, policy expertise, and partnerships. Strategic priorities for 2022 included climate change (especially vector-borne diseases and heatwaves), evidence-based data expertise, health impacts of pollutants, robust health monitoring, public health social marketing such as the "Tobacco-Free Month" campaign, and regionalization through 15 regional intervention units (*Cellules d'intervention en région*, Cire) and Géodes, an interactive regional health-data platform.

## Governance

The agency's governance layers several bodies with distinct remits. The board of directors (32 members, four-year renewable terms) sets strategic direction, programs, and resource allocation, drawing on state representatives, health-insurance bodies, institutional partners, health professionals, associations, local elected officials, qualified experts, agency staff, and parliamentarians. The president, appointed by decree of the President of the French Republic, has been Marie-Caroline Bonnet-Galzy since 18 December 2017.

The scientific council (27 members, four-year terms) ensures quality and coherence of scientific policies, advising on research, expertise, and partnerships. The Ethics and Deontology Committee (seven members) enforces ethical standards and prevents conflicts of interest. The Orientation and Dialogue Committee (10–20 members) prioritizes public health issues and steers crisis communication.

Leadership rests with a Director General supported by ten cross-disciplinary scientific directorates, five support directorates, and an activity-support directorate. Directors general have been François Bourdillon (2016–June 2019), Martial Mettendorff (interim, June–October 2019), Geneviève Chêne (November 2019–October 2022), and Caroline Semaille (since 23 February 2023).

## Resources and regional presence

In 2019 the agency employed 625 staff with an annual budget of around €200 million. In 2020 it received a state allocation of €150,155,356, plus an exceptional €860 million for epidemic prevention and strategic stockpiling tied to the COVID-19 response.

The 15 Cire units conduct regional epidemiological surveillance, monitor population health, issue local alerts, and manage crises under the agency's regional directorate. Headquarters occupies three buildings in Saint-Maurice: the 19th-century Château de Vacassy (originally a World War I hospital, founded under an 1876 decree from a bequest by Jean-Joseph Vacassy), the Villermé building designed by AIA Architecte in 2012, and the Simone Veil building, a wooden structure completed in 2019 by Atelier du Pont Architecte.

The health reserve, established in 2007 under Éprus, mobilises volunteer health professionals during crises exceeding normal capacity, and has been deployed during chikungunya outbreaks and the COVID-19 pandemic in France.

## European and international role

At the European level, Santé publique France collaborates with the European Centre for Disease Prevention and Control (ECDC) on disease surveillance and alerts, with the European Food Safety Authority (EFSA) on food safety, and with the European Agency for Safety and Health at Work (EU-OSHA), for which it runs France's national focal point. It also contributes to European Commission projects on infectious diseases, chronic diseases, injuries, work, environment, and training.

Internationally, it supports the World Health Organization's European Bureau, co-funds WHO–European Commission projects, serves as secretariat for the International Association of National Public Health Institutes (established 2006), and is a member of the International Union for Health Promotion and Education (IUHPE).

## Documentary output and prevention tools

The agency's documentary portal gathers its scientific output: the *Bulletin épidémiologique hebdomadaire* (BEH), a free weekly epidemiology journal; Géodes for regional and national health indicators; health barometer surveys tracking vaccination, smoking, alcohol, and cancer screening, running since 1992 under CFES; the registry of validated interventions cataloguing evidence-based prevention programs; and *La santé en action*, a quarterly magazine on health promotion. Thematic prevention websites and remote health assistance programs, including the "Tabac Info Service" helpline launched in 1998, extend the agency's reach, with the 2014 "Health Assistance Quality Label" setting standards for these services.
